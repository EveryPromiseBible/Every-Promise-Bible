Twelve men are sent out with an inventory of what they may not take.

## Two by two

*Began sending them out **in pairs**.*

Nobody goes alone. The law required two witnesses, and the practice runs right
through Acts — Peter and John, Paul and Barnabas, Barnabas and Mark, Paul and Silas.

## What He gave them

*He gave them **authority over unclean spirits**.*

That is the whole equipment list on the credit side. One item, and it is not
material.

## What they may take

*Take **nothing for the road but a walking stick** — no bread, no traveling bag, no
coins tucked in your belt. **Wear your sandals**, and **do not carry a second
shirt**.*

A stick and the sandals on their feet.

Four things forbidden: food, luggage, money, and a change of clothes.

Every one of them is what a sensible traveller packs. The instruction removes any
possibility of self-sufficiency for the length of the journey.

## The stick and the sandals

They are allowed to keep what they are already holding and already wearing. Nothing
is to be acquired for the trip.

## The house

*"Whatever house you go into, **stay there until you leave that town**."*

No moving to better accommodation. Whoever takes you in first, you stay with.

It rules out working up through a village's households, and it puts them at the mercy
of the first person who says yes.

## The dust

*"If there are any who will not welcome you… **shake the dust off your feet** as a
witness against them."*

A Jewish traveller shook off Gentile dust on re-entering the land. The gesture said:
your ground is not ours.

They are told to do it to Jewish towns.

## What they preached

*They went out and **called people to change their minds and turn to God**.*

The Greek is **metanoeo** — to think differently afterwards.

## What they did

*They **drove out many demons**, and they **rubbed oil on many who were sick and
healed them**.*

The only place in the Gospels where the disciples use oil.

The Greek verb is **aleipho** — the everyday word for rubbing oil on, as you would
after a bath or onto a wound. Not the ceremonial anointing word.

James gives the same instruction to elders: *let them pray over him and put oil on
him in the name of the Lord.* Same verb. See the note on James 5:13–15.

## What happened when they got back

*The apostles gathered to Jesus and told Him all things, both what they had done and
what they had taught.*

And His response: *come aside by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a while* —
*for there were many coming and going, and they did not even have time to eat.*

The debrief is a rest.
