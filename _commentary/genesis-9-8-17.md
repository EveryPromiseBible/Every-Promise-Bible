God makes a promise and then puts up something to look at, and the thing He puts
up is a weapon.

## The word

*"I have set **my rainbow** in the clouds."*

The Hebrew is **qeshet** — and it is simply the word for a bow. The war bow, the
hunting bow, the thing David sang about, the weapon Jonathan gave away.

There is no separate Hebrew word for a rainbow. God says *I have set My bow in the
cloud*, and the picture is of a warrior hanging up his weapon.

And it is hung pointing away — the arc bends toward heaven, with the string toward
earth. Whatever is left in it is aimed at Himself.

## Who has to remember

*"Whenever I bring clouds over the earth and the rainbow appears, **I will remember
my covenant**."*

Said three times in the passage: *I will remember*, *I will see it and remember*,
*this is the sign of the covenant.*

Not *when you see it, remember*. The sign is placed where He looks.

The Hebrew **zakar** is the same verb as *God remembered Noah* a chapter earlier —
it means acting on a commitment, not recovering a lost thought. See the note on
Genesis 8:1–5.

## Who the covenant is with

*"With you and your descendants after you, and **with every living creature** that
was with you — the birds, the livestock, and all the wild animals."*

He says it four times over. The animals are named as parties to it.

It is the first covenant in Scripture and it is made with the whole earth, with no
conditions attached to anybody.

## What is not required

Nothing. Read the whole passage and there is no *if*, no clause about behaviour, no
term for either side to keep.

Which is the only reason it is still standing — the generation it was made with
included Ham, and Noah is drunk and naked in his tent within a few verses.

## Who brings the clouds

*"**Whenever I bring clouds** over the earth."*

He does not promise there will be no more clouds. He says He is the one bringing
them, and that when they come, the bow shows up in them.

The sign only appears when there is rain and sun at once. You do not get the
rainbow on a clear day.

## What Isaiah does with it

*"To Me this is like the waters of Noah; for as I have sworn that the waters of
Noah would no more cover the earth, so have I sworn that **I would not be angry
with you, nor rebuke you**."*

Isaiah 54, which follows Isaiah 53 — the servant *wounded for our transgressions*.

God stakes the certainty of one promise on the certainty of the other, and invites
the comparison Himself. See the note on Isaiah 54:9–10.

## The last line

*"This is the sign of the covenant I have established between me and **all life on
the earth**."*

> **The other place the bow turns up.** Ezekiel 1, and Revelation 4: *there was a
> rainbow around the throne, in appearance like an emerald.* The sign hung in the
> sky over a wet world is described as encircling the throne itself.
