A prophecy about a city under siege, and in the middle of it the speaker says something
about Himself that does not fit.

## The setting

*"On that day I will set out to **destroy all the nations that come against
Jerusalem**."*

Zechariah 12 to 14 is one long passage about a final assault on the city and what happens
to it.

## The pouring

*"And I will **pour out on the house of David and on the people of Jerusalem a spirit of
grace and of pleading**."*

The Hebrew is **ruach chen v'tachanunim** — a spirit of grace and of supplications.

**Chen** is favour, grace — the word used over Noah, over Esther, over Joseph in
Potiphar's house. See the note on Genesis 39:1–6.

**Tachanunim** is entreaty, pleading for mercy. The same root as the word for the
Israelite begging in the cities of refuge.

Both are poured out. Neither is worked up.

## The sentence that does not fit

*"They will look on **Me, the one they have pierced**."*

The Hebrew is *they shall look upon **Me** whom they have pierced*.

The speaker through the whole chapter is the LORD. And the pronoun changes mid-clause in
the Hebrew — *upon Me*, and then *they shall mourn for **Him***.

The oddity is in the text and translators have wrestled with it for centuries. Some
ancient copies smooth it to *upon him*.

## The verb

**Daqar** — to pierce, to thrust through. It is used of a spear or sword going into a
body.

## The mourning

*"And they will **mourn for him as one mourns for an only child**, and **grieve bitterly
for him as one grieves for a firstborn son**."*

Two comparisons, and both are the loss of a son.

**An only child** — the Hebrew is **yachid**, the word used of Isaac on the mountain:
*your son, your only son*. See the note on Genesis 22:11–12.

## Where John quotes it

At the cross, after the spear: *and again another Scripture says, "**They shall look on Him
whom they pierced**."* See the note on John 19:31–34.

John cites it as the second of two fulfilled Scriptures in that scene — the unbroken bones
and this.

## Where Revelation quotes it

*Behold, He is coming with clouds, and every eye will see Him, **even they who pierced
Him**. And all the tribes of the earth will mourn because of Him.*

The book's opening page, joining this to Daniel 7.

## What Zechariah says next

*"**In that day a fountain shall be opened** for the house of David and for the
inhabitants of Jerusalem, **for sin and for uncleanness**."*

That is the first verse of the next chapter, and it follows directly. The looking and the
mourning are followed by a fountain being opened.

## What is poured and what is opened

A spirit of grace and pleading, poured out. Then a fountain, opened.

Neither is described as obtained.

> **The clause everything turns on.** *They will look on Me, the one they have pierced.*
> The speaker is God, the wound is His, and what follows the looking is a fountain rather
> than a judgment.
