The bluntest paragraph in the New Testament, and it is addressed to a specific
group.

## Who is being spoken to

*Now you. **The rich.***

Not the church. James is addressing a class of people from outside it — the next
verse names what they did: *you kept back by fraud the wages of the labourers who
mowed your fields.*

Unpaid wages. That is the charge, and it is the same charge Egypt had four hundred
years of.

## The three corrosions

*Your **wealth has gone rotten**. Your good clothes are **full of moth holes**.
Your gold and your silver have **gone green with corrosion**.*

Three kinds of stored wealth in that world — grain, garments, and metal — and each
is described decaying in its own way.

Gold does not corrode. James says it anyway; the point is not metallurgy.

## The corrosion as a witness

*That corrosion is going to **stand up and testify against you**, and eat its way
through your flesh like fire.*

The rust becomes a witness in court. The evidence against them is produced by the
hoard itself, sitting in the dark, doing what unused things do.

## The line

*You piled the stuff up — and **the last days are what you piled it up for**.*

The Greek is *you have heaped up treasure in the last days*, and it carries both
senses: they did the heaping in the last days, and the last days are what the heap
is for.

The pile has a destination that is not theirs. Ecclesiastes said the same:
*to the one who misses the mark, He gives the task of gathering and piling up, only
to hand it over to the one who pleases God.* See the note on Ecclesiastes 2:24–26.

## What James tells the believers to do about it

Nothing aggressive. Four verses later:

*Be patient, brethren, until the coming of the Lord. See how the farmer waits for
the precious fruit of the earth, **waiting patiently for it until it receives the
early and latter rain**. You also be patient. **Establish your hearts**, for the
coming of the Lord is at hand.*

A farmer, two rains, and a heart established. No scheming, no grasping, no hurry.

Proverbs: *the stingy man hurries after wealth and never knows that want is coming
for him.* See the note on Proverbs 28:21–28.

## The wages that cried out

*The cries of the reapers have reached the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth.*

**Sabaoth** — the LORD of armies. James reaches for the military title in a
paragraph about farm wages.

The same thing happened in Egypt: *their cry came up to God because of the bondage,
and God heard their groaning.* And on the way out, the back pay was settled. See
the note on Exodus 12:33–36.

> **Who James was.** The brother of Jesus, and the leader of the Jerusalem church —
> writing to Jewish believers scattered by persecution, most of them poor. He does
> not tell them to organise. He tells them to wait for the rain.
