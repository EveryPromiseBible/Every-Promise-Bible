Four verses about the arrival of news, and **Paul** quotes them to explain why anybody preaches.

## The feet

*How beautiful on the mountains are **the feet of the one who brings good news**.*

The Hebrew is **mevasser** — the herald, the one who runs ahead with a report. The Greek Old
Testament renders the verb **euangelizomenou**, which is the verb behind **gospel**.

Feet are the least beautiful part of a man who has been running over hills. Isaiah says they are
the beautiful part, because of what is arriving on them.

*And how shall they preach unless they are sent? As it is written: "**How beautiful are the feet
of those who preach the gospel of peace, who bring glad tidings of good things!**"*

**Romans** 10, and **Paul** builds his whole argument for sending people on this verse. The chain
he gives runs backwards — how shall they call on Him in whom they have not believed, and how
shall they believe in Him of whom they have not heard, and how shall they hear without a
preacher.

*And having **shod your feet with the preparation of the gospel of peace**.*

## The four things announced

Good news. Peace. Salvation. And *your God reigns*.

The herald is not bringing instructions. Every item is a report of something already settled
somewhere else.

## The arm

*The LORD has **bared His holy arm** before the eyes of all the nations, and **all the ends of the
earth will see the salvation of our God**.*

An arm is bared to work or to fight. The image is of a sleeve pushed back.

And then the very next paragraph:

*Who has believed our report? And **to whom has the arm of the LORD been revealed**? For He shall
grow up before Him as a tender plant… He is despised and rejected by men.*

Isaiah 53 opens by asking who recognised the arm, and the answer the chapter gives is a Man with
no form or comeliness. The bared arm of chapter 52 is the Servant of chapter 53, and the two are
separated only by a chapter division somebody added later.

*But **although He had done so many signs before them, they did not believe in Him**, that the
word of Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled, which he spoke: "Lord, who has believed our
report? And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?"*

**John** 12 quotes it of **Jesus** directly.

## The ruins told to sing

*Break into song together, **sing for joy, you ruins of Jerusalem**.*

Not the citizens. The rubble.

*"**I say to you that if these should keep silent, the stones would immediately cry out.**"*

> **What the sleeve is pushed back for.** Isaiah 52 has the arm bared in front of all the
> nations, and Isaiah 53 asks who recognised it — and describes somebody with nothing about His
> appearance that anyone would desire.
