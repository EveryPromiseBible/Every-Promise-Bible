key: Hosea 14:4–5
title: "I will love them freely"

The last chapter, and the first thing God says is the whole book in one sentence.

## What they were told to say

*"Return, Israel, to the LORD your God… **Take words with you** and return to the LORD. Say to Him,
'Take away all iniquity; receive us graciously, for we will offer the sacrifices of our lips.'"*

They have nothing else. The temple is in the other kingdom, the calves are at Bethel, and the
prophet tells them to bring sentences.

*Therefore by Him let us continually offer **the sacrifice of praise to God**, that is, **the fruit
of our lips**, giving thanks to His name.*

**Hebrews** 13 quotes the phrase.

## The answer

*"**I will heal their waywardness.**"*

The Hebrew is **erpa meshuvatam** — I will heal their turning-away.

Not punish it, not forgive it, not overlook it. **Heal** it. The word for the backsliding is treated
as a condition rather than a crime, and the verb applied to it is a physician's.

## The two words

*"**I will love them freely.**"*

**Ohavem nedavah.**

**Nedavah** is a noun, not an adverb — it is the word for a **freewill offering**, the sacrifice
nobody was required to bring. Leviticus lists it among the offerings a person could make for no
reason except that they wanted to.

God says He will love them *as a freewill offering*. There is no obligation on Him and no
inducement from them.

*Being justified **freely by His grace** through the redemption that is in **Christ Jesus**.*

**Paul**'s word is **dorean** — as a gift, gratis, for nothing. It is the same claim, and it is the
one adverb the New Testament attaches to justification.

*"**Freely you have received, freely give.**"*

*And let him who thirsts come. Whoever desires, let him **take the water of life freely**.*

The last invitation in the Bible ends on the word Hosea's last chapter opens with.

## The reason given

*"For **My anger has turned away from them**."*

The Hebrew is **ki shav appi mimmennu** — for My anger has turned from him.

The verb is **shuv**, the same one used all through the chapter for Israel returning. They are told
to turn, and the thing that actually turns is the anger.

## The dew

*"**I will be like the dew to Israel.**"*

Dew, in that country, is what keeps things alive through the dry season. It arrives at night,
without noise, without being asked, and it is the one form of water that does not depend on the
weather.

Earlier in the book, Israel's own love was the thing compared to moisture: *your love is like the
morning cloud, and like the early dew it goes away.*

The same image, transferred. Theirs evaporated; His is what they are watered by.

## What grows

*"He will **blossom like a lily** and **sink his roots deep like the cedars of Lebanon**."*

A lily has no root worth the name and a cedar is nothing but root. Hosea puts both on the same
plant.

*"I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in Me, and I in him, **bears much fruit**."*

## The last verse of the book

*"**Ephraim, what have you to do with idols anymore? It is I who answer him and watch over him.**"*

And then: *the ways of the LORD are right; the righteous walk in them, but transgressors stumble
in them.*

The book that opened with three children named as sentences closes with God asking a rhetorical
question about idols and calling Himself an evergreen tree.

> **The noun.** *Nedavah* — a freewill offering, the sacrifice nobody had to bring. God says that
> is how He will love them, and Romans 3 uses the one Greek adverb that means the same thing:
> **freely**.
