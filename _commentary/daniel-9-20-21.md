The answer arrives before the prayer is finished.

## The phrase, twice

*"**While I was still speaking** and praying… **while I was still in prayer**, Gabriel…
came to me."*

Daniel says it at the start of the sentence and again at the end. He wants the timing
on record.

He had done the same thing three verses earlier — *while I was speaking, praying, and
confessing.*

## What he was praying

*Confessing **my sin and the sin of my people Israel**.*

He includes himself. Nothing in the book is charged against Daniel — even his enemies
had to invent a law about prayer to get at him — and he says *my sin*.

## What he was asking for

*Laying **my plea** before the LORD my God **for His holy mountain**.*

Not for himself, not for release, not for his position under the new Persian
administration. For the temple hill, seven hundred miles away, in ruins.

## Who came

***Gabriel**, the one I had seen in the vision before.*

The name means *man of God* or *God is my strength*.

He appears four times in Scripture and every appearance is a message: twice to Daniel
here, then to Zechariah in the temple about John, then to Mary in Nazareth.

The angel who explains Israel's timetable to Daniel is the one sent to announce its
fulfilment five hundred years later.

## "Swift flight"

The Hebrew is *being caused to fly swiftly*, or *in weariness of flight* — the
commentators divide over it. Either way it is a word about speed.

## The hour

*About the time of the **evening offering**.*

Three in the afternoon. The hour of the lamb on the altar.

There has been no temple, no altar and no evening offering for sixty-eight years. There
is nothing in Jerusalem to make it at.

Daniel keeps the hours anyway, and the angel arrives on one of them.

It is also the hour the veil tore.

## What he is told first

Before the message, the reason: *at the beginning of your supplications the command went
out, and I have come to tell you, **for you are greatly beloved**.*

*At the beginning* — the reply was issued when he started, and the flight is what took
the twenty-one days in the following chapter.

## What the message was

The seventy weeks — seventy sevens decreed on his people, with the Messiah cut off in
the middle of it, and a date attached.

He asked about seventy years and was given seventy sevens.

## What he had been reading

Jeremiah, and the seventy years of desolation. See the note on Daniel 9:1–2.

> **What was said about him.** *Greatly beloved* — the Hebrew is *precious, treasured*.
> Gabriel says it to him three times across two chapters, to a civil servant in a pagan
> administration praying toward a demolished city.
