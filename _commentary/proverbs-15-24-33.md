Ten proverbs, and one of them was quoted back at the Pharisees by a man who had just been given
his sight.

## The proverb that gets quoted

*The LORD is far from the wicked, **but He hears the prayer of the righteous**.*

*"Now we know that **God does not hear sinners**; but if anyone is a worshipper of God and does
His will, He hears him."*

That is the man born blind, in **John** 9, standing in front of the men who are interrogating
him. He is quoting this verse, and he is using it as an argument that the Man who opened his
eyes cannot be a sinner.

They throw him out for it. And then **Jesus** finds him, and he worships.

## What the proverb does not say

Where a man gets the righteousness that gets him heard.

*For **He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of
God in Him**.*

*Therefore, having been justified by faith, **we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus
Christ, through whom also we have access by faith into this grace** in which we stand.*

**Paul**'s word is **prosagoge** — the introduction, being brought in and presented. Proverbs
says the LORD is far from one man and hears the other; **Romans** says how the distance was
closed, and it was not closed from this side.

## The good news

*Bright eyes gladden the heart. **Good news puts strength in the bones**.*

The Hebrew is **shemuah tovah** — a good report, good tidings. The Greek Old Testament renders
this family of phrases with the verb behind **euangelion**.

*"Behold, I bring you **good tidings of great joy** which will be to all people."*

**Luke**'s angel uses the word, and the proverb has already said what it does to a body:
**tedashen-atzem** — makes the bone fat, puts marrow in it. Bones are where blood is made.

The proverb is not describing encouragement. It is describing news arriving from outside and
changing something structural.

## The widow's boundary

*The LORD tears down the house of the proud, but **He keeps the widow's boundary safe**.*

A boundary stone was the whole of a poor household's security, and a widow had nobody to argue
for hers.

*"**Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you devour widows' houses**, and for a
pretence make long prayers."*

**Matthew** records **Jesus** saying it in the temple courts, in the same chapter as *the house
of the proud*. And a few verses later He sits down opposite the treasury and watches a widow put
in two coins.

## The last line

*The fear of the LORD is the teaching of wisdom, and **humility comes before honour**.*

*And being found in appearance as a man, **He humbled Himself and became obedient to the point
of death, even the death of the cross**. Therefore God also has highly exalted Him.*

**Paul**'s sentence has the proverb's exact order, with a *therefore* in the middle of it.

> **Who quoted this passage first.** A man who had been blind that morning, using verse 29
> against the men questioning him — and the argument he built on it was that the One who healed
> him had to be from God.
