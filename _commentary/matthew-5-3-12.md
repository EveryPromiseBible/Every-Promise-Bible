key: Matthew 5:3–12
title: "Blessing falls on the unlikely"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Nine sentences, and not one of them is an instruction.

## The word

*"**Blessed** are the ones who have nothing to bring."*

The Greek is **makarioi** — an adjective, not a verb. There is no *shall be* in the Greek of the
first clause and no imperative anywhere in the list.

**Jesus** is not telling the crowd to become these things. He is announcing who is already in a
good position, and the list is a surprise to everybody hearing it.

## What the qualifications are

Poverty of spirit. Grief. Gentleness. Hunger. Mercy. A single heart. Peacemaking. Being hunted.

Six of the eight are conditions of **lack**. Nobody arranges to be poor in spirit, and nobody
achieves bereavement.

*"**Blessed are the ones who have nothing to bring, who come with empty hands and know it.**"*

**Hoi ptochoi to pneumati** — the destitute in spirit. **Ptochos** is not the working poor; it is
the beggar, the one crouched with a hand out.

## The tenses

*The kingdom of heaven **already belongs** to them.*

Present. The first and the eighth beatitudes both have it, bracketing the list, and everything
between them is future — *they will be comforted, they will inherit, they will be filled*.

The possession is now and the payment is later, which is the New Testament's ordinary shape.

*In Him also, having believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise, **who is the
guarantee of our inheritance** until the redemption of the purchased possession.*

## The one that names the mechanism

*"Blessed are the ones who are **hungry for things to be made right**… they will be **filled full and
satisfied**."*

**Chortasthesontai** — the verb for feeding animals until they can eat no more.

The qualification is the appetite. Not the righteousness — the hunger for it.

*But now **the righteousness of God apart from the law is revealed**… even the righteousness of God,
through faith in **Jesus Christ**, to all and on all who believe.*

*For **He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us**, that we might become the righteousness of God
in Him.*

## The one about mercy

*"Blessed are the merciful… **mercy will find its way back to them**."*

It is the only beatitude whose reward is the same word as its condition, and **James** picks it up:
*mercy triumphs over judgment.*

*"Should you not also have had compassion on your fellow servant, **just as I had pity on you**?"*

**Matthew** 18, and the parable puts the order beyond argument: the mercy is received first and by
an amount nobody could repay.

## The last one

*"Blessed are you when people insult you and chase you off and pile up lies against you **because of
Me**."*

The first eight describe a condition. The ninth changes to the second person and names a cause,
and the cause is a Person.

*If you are reproached **for the name of Christ**, blessed are you, for the Spirit of glory and of
God rests upon you.*

**Peter** uses **makarioi** and quotes the beatitude at people being persecuted, thirty years on.

## Where the sermon goes from here

Straight into standards nobody can keep — anger counted as murder, a look counted as adultery, and
*be perfect, as your Father in heaven is perfect.* See the note on Matthew 5:43–48.

The list of blessings comes first, and it is addressed to people with nothing.

> **What is missing from the list.** Any imperative. Nine sentences, no verbs of command, and six
> of the eight conditions are things nobody could arrange for themselves — starting with having
> nothing to bring and knowing it.
