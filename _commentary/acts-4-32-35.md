Luke's second summary of the young church, and every clause in it is worth
slowing down over.

## One heart and soul

*The whole company of believers was **one in heart and soul**.*

The Greek is literally *of one heart and one soul* — a phrase used in Greek
literature of the closest friendships, applied here to thousands of people.

## What they said about property

*Not one of them said that anything he owned was **his alone**.*

Luke is precise. He does not say they had no possessions. He says nobody used the
word *mine* about what he had.

The change is in the pronoun before it is in the ledger.

## Great power, great grace

*And with **great power** the apostles kept giving their witness to the
resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and **great grace** was on them all.*

The two adjectives are the same Greek word, and Luke uses it twice in one
sentence — great power on the preaching, great grace on everybody.

The power was on the apostles. The grace was on all of them.

## "Not a needy person among them"

The phrase is an echo. Deuteronomy 15 promised Israel *there shall be no poor
among you* if they kept the covenant.

Luke reports it as an accomplished fact in Jerusalem, and the mechanism he
describes is not a law. It is people selling things.

## As he had need

*It was distributed to each one as he had need.*

Not equally. According to need — which requires somebody to know each person's
situation. The distribution assumes a community small enough to be known in.

> **What this paragraph is doing where it is.** It follows the prayer of chapter 4,
> where the place was shaken and they spoke the word with boldness. Luke's order
> is: they pray, the room shakes, and then nobody owns anything alone. He puts the
> economics downstream of the prayer meeting.
