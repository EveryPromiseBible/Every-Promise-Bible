The only rule attached to free food is that it cannot be stored.

## The instruction

*Moses told them, "**Don't keep any of it until morning**."*

The Hebrew is **al-yoter mimennu** — let no one leave any of it over.

They are a month out of Egypt, in a wilderness, with no supply line. And the rule about the food
is that they may not put any aside.

## What it was

*In the morning there was **a layer of dew** around the camp, and when it lifted there was **a
fine flake-like thing, fine as frost**.*

*So they said to one another, "**What is it?**" — for they did not know what it was.* See the note
on Exodus 16:13–15.

The Hebrew is **man hu**, and the name stuck: **manna**. The food is called *what is it*.

## The disobedience

*But some didn't listen. **They held a little back**.*

Which is the reasonable thing to do. Anyone who has been hungry would.

## What happened to it

*And by morning it was **crawling with maggots and reeking**.*

The Hebrew is **vayarum tola'im** — it bred worms. And **vayivash** — it stank.

*Moses was angry with them.*

## What the manna would not do

Keep.

It could feed a nation of hundreds of thousands every morning for forty years, and it could not
survive one night in a jar.

The failure was designed in.

## The rhythm

*So **each morning they gathered it fresh, every person as much as they needed**.*

The Hebrew is **ish ke'fi ochlo** — each according to his eating.

And Paul's note on it: *he who gathered much had nothing left over, and he who gathered little
had no lack*. See the note on 2 Corinthians 8:13–15.

## The deadline

*And **when the sun grew hot, whatever was left simply melted away**.*

There was a window. Gather early, or it is gone.

## The exception

On the sixth day they gathered twice as much, and Moses said *bake what you will bake today, and
boil what you will boil, and lay up for yourselves all that remains*.

*So they laid it up till morning, as Moses commanded; **and it did not stink, nor were there any
worms in it***.

The same food, kept overnight, with the opposite result — because that time it was commanded.

The manna was not obeying chemistry.

## The one jar

*Fill an omer with it, to be kept for your generations… **so Aaron laid it up before the Testimony,
to be kept***.

One jar of the stuff that would not keep, kept forever in front of the ark.

## What the prayer says

*Give us **this day our daily bread**.* See the note on Matthew 6:9–13.

The Greek is **epiousion** — a word found almost nowhere else in ancient literature. Its likeliest
sense is *for the coming day*.

Today's portion, asked for today.

## What Jesus says about it

*"Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and are dead… **I am the living bread which came
down from heaven**."* See the note on John 6:47–51.

## The forty years

*And the manna ceased on the day after they had eaten the produce of the land.*

Every morning for forty years, and then the day they ate from Canaan, it stopped.

> **What it would not do.** Keep. The same food fed a nation for forty years and could not last
> one night in a jar — except on the sixth day, when it was told to.
