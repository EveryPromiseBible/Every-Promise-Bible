One verse, and it is a report on what pain did not accomplish.

## Where it sits

The fifth bowl. The bowl judgments are the last series in the book — seven angels, seven
bowls, poured on the earth, the sea, the rivers, the sun, the throne of the beast, the
Euphrates, and the air.

## What has happened

The fourth bowl scorched men with fire; the fifth brought darkness on the beast's
kingdom, *and they gnawed their tongues because of the pain*.

## The response

*And they **cursed the God of heaven** over their pains and their sores.*

The Greek is **eblasphemesan** — blasphemed.

They know who is doing it. The verse says they cursed **the God of heaven**, which means
the source is not in doubt.

## The clause

*And they **did not turn away from the things they were doing**.*

The Greek is *they did not repent of their deeds* — **metanoeo**, to change the mind.

## The refrain

It is not the only time. The chapter says it three times, and the sixth trumpet in
chapter 9 says it too:

- *They **did not repent** to give Him glory.*
- *And they **did not repent** of their deeds.*
- *And men blasphemed God because of the plague of the hail… and they **did not repent**.*
- Chapter 9: *the rest of mankind, who were not killed by these plagues, **did not
  repent** of the works of their hands.*

Four times, after four different judgments.

## What the book is reporting

The judgments land, the pain is real, the source is correctly identified, and nobody
changes their mind.

Revelation states it as an observation each time, without comment.

## What Paul says produces repentance

*Do you despise the riches of His goodness, forbearance and longsuffering, **not knowing
that the goodness of God leads you to repentance?**"*

The Greek is **agei** — leads, draws along.

Paul names a different agent from the one operating in Revelation 16, and Revelation
records what the other one achieved.

## The one exception in the book

Chapter 11, after the two witnesses are raised and an earthquake kills seven thousand:
*and the rest were afraid and **gave glory to the God of heaven**.*

The only time in the judgment sequences that anyone does.

## What the bowls are

*Seven golden bowls full of the wrath of God.*

The same word for bowl — **phiale** — as the golden bowls full of incense held by the
elders in chapter 5, which John says are the prayers of God's people. See the note on
Revelation 5:7–8.

Same vessel, two contents.

## The two invitations still standing in the same book

The bowls fall on people who will not turn — and the same book, written by the same hand, keeps the
door open the whole way through.

*Behold, **I stand at the door and knock**. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come
in to him.*

*And whoever desires, let him **take the water of life freely**.*

That second sentence is in the last chapter, after everything in this one. Revelation does not
finish with the bowls; it finishes with an invitation and the word **dorean** — without charge.

So the refusal reported here is not a shortage of mercy. It is a refusal of the specific mercy that
is still, at the very end of the book, being offered by Jesus Christ to *whoever desires*.

> **What the verse records.** Pain, correctly attributed, and no change. Revelation says
> it four times across two judgment cycles and never once presents it as a surprise.
