Two men are sent to prepare a meal and find that most of it is done.

## The day

*The day the **Passover lamb had to be killed**.*

Nisan 14. Thousands of lambs were slaughtered in the temple that afternoon.

Luke notes the date in a chapter that will end with a trial.

## Who is sent

*Jesus sent **Peter and John** ahead.*

The two who will run to the tomb together on Sunday morning.

## The question

*"**Where do You want us to prepare it?**"*

A reasonable question in a city packed with pilgrims. Rooms in Jerusalem at Passover
were impossible to find, and by custom nobody charged for them.

## The sign

*"As you go into the city, **a man carrying a jar of water** will meet you."*

Carrying water was women's work. A man with a water jar would stand out in a crowded
street.

It is a marker chosen so that two men could pick him out without asking anybody
anything.

## The password

*"Say to the owner of the house, '**The Teacher asks: where is the guest room** where
I may eat the Passover with My disciples?'"*

No name is given. *The Teacher* is enough.

Whatever arrangement had been made, it had been made without the disciples' knowledge,
and Judas is not in the room when the location is given.

## What they find

*"He will take you upstairs and show you **a large room, already set out**."*

The Greek is *furnished* — spread with couches, laid out.

They were sent to prepare, and the preparing had been done.

*Get everything ready there* — the lamb, the bread, the wine. The room was not theirs
to arrange.

## The verification

*The two of them went, and **found it just as He had told them**.*

Luke says it flatly. He does the same with the colt on Palm Sunday — sent, described,
found exactly so.

## What happens in that room

The Passover, the bread and the cup, the argument about who is greatest, the warning
to Peter, the washing of feet in John's account, and the four chapters of the upper
room discourse.

*"I have longed to eat this Passover with you before I suffer."* See the note on
Luke 22:14–18.

## The other room in Luke

*There was no room for them in the inn.* Chapter 2.

The Gospel opens with no space available and closes its last week in a large upper
room, furnished and ready.

> **What the man with the jar never knew.** He is not named, the householder is not
> named, and neither appears again. Somebody in Jerusalem gave up the best room in
> his house that week and is not recorded.
