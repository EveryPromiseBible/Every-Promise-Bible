A list, at the end of the book, of men nobody would otherwise have heard of.

## Where the chapter sits

Immediately after David's last words. He has just said that his covenant is *ordered in all
things and sure*. See the note on 2 Samuel 23:5–7.

Then a roll of names.

## The heading

*These are **the names** of David's mighty warriors.*

The Hebrew is **shemot** — names. The chapter is a list of them, and it runs to thirty-seven.

Most are otherwise unknown. Several are foreigners — a Hittite, an Ammonite, men from Gath.
The last name on the list is **Uriah the Hittite**, and the writer puts him there without
comment.

## Who they had been

*Everyone who was **in distress**, everyone who was **in debt**, and everyone who was
**discontented** gathered to him. So he became captain over them.*

Four hundred men in a cave at Adullam, described by three disqualifications. That is where
this list comes from.

Distressed, in debt, and bitter of soul — and the chapter at the other end of the book calls
the same men *mighty*.

*And such were some of you. **But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were
justified** in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of our God.*

## The first name

*Josheb-Basshebeth… **he raised his spear against eight hundred men and struck them down in a
single battle**.*

The number is not the point of the sentence. The point is that somebody wrote it down.

## What the list is doing

Recording, individually and by name, what each man did — one against eight hundred, one who
held a field of lentils, one who went down into a pit on a snowy day and killed a lion, three
who broke through an army for a drink of water their king then poured out on the ground.

None of it changed the outcome of the kingdom. All of it is in the book.

*For **God is not unjust to forget your work and labor of love** which you have shown toward
His name.*

*And I heard a voice from heaven saying to me, "Write: 'Blessed are the dead who die in the
Lord from now on.'" "Yes," says the Spirit, "that they may rest from their labors, and **their
works follow them**."*

## Who made them mighty

Not the cave.

*The LORD brought about a great victory that day* — said twice in this chapter, once of
Eleazar and once of Shammah, at the end of paragraphs about what each man personally did. See
the note on 2 Samuel 23:9–10.

The chapter gives them the names and gives Him the victories, and does not treat the two as
being in competition.

> **Where the list starts.** A cave, four hundred men, and three words for who they were:
> distressed, in debt, discontented. The register at the end of the book calls the same men
> by name and says what each of them did.
