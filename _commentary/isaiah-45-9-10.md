title: "The clay and the potter"

A warning against arguing with the Maker, and **Paul** picks it up and turns it toward mercy.

## The image

*Does the clay ask the potter, "**What are You making?**" Does the finished pot say, "**You have
no skilled hands**"?*

The Hebrew of the second is *your work has no hands* — an idiom for incompetence.

Two objections, and both are absurd because of who is holding whom.

## The context

Isaiah 45 is about **Cyrus** — a Persian king, named a century and a half before he was born,
called *My shepherd* and *His anointed*, and used to send the exiles home.

*I have raised him up in righteousness… he shall build My city and let My exiles go free, **not
for price nor reward**.*

The clay is objecting to God's choice of instrument. The complaint underneath the potter image is
that God is using the wrong man.

## Who quotes it

*But indeed, O man, who are you to reply against God? **Will the thing formed say to him who
formed it, "Why have you made me like this?"** Does not the potter have power over the clay?*

**Romans** 9, quoting this passage almost word for word.

## What Paul does with it next

He does not stop at the potter's authority. Two verses later he says what the vessels are for:

*That He might make known **the riches of His glory on the vessels of mercy**, which He had
prepared beforehand for glory, even us whom He called.*

**Skeue eleous** — vessels of mercy. The potter's answer in **Romans** is not *because I can*. It
is a display of mercy, and the pots are the exhibit.

## And what the clay turned out to hold

*But **we have this treasure in earthen vessels**, that the excellence of the power may be of God
and not of us.*

**Paul**'s word is **ostrakinois** — made of baked clay, the cheapest pottery there was. He is
still in Isaiah's image, and the point has moved from the potter's rights to what got put inside.

## The one who was formed

*But **made Himself of no reputation**, taking the form of a bondservant, and coming in the
likeness of men.*

The one Person in Scripture who could have argued with the Maker about the shape He was given did
not.

*"Nevertheless, **not My will, but Yours, be done**."*

> **Where Romans takes it.** Isaiah's potter has the right to make what He likes, and Paul agrees
> — and then names what He decided to make. *Vessels of mercy.* The clay's objection is answered
> by what is poured into it.
