title: "No stumbling block"

**Paul** rules on a dispute about food by refusing to rule on the food.

## The turn

*"**Let us stop judging one another, then. Decide this instead: never to put a stumbling block or a
snare in a brother's way.**"*

The Greek repeats the verb — **krinomen** and **krinate** — *let us not judge; rather judge this*.
The energy spent on the verdict is redirected to a decision about oneself.

## What he grants

*"**I know, and I am persuaded in the Lord Jesus, that nothing is unclean of itself.**"*

**Paul** takes a side on the theology, and it is the strong side. He is not neutral about whether the
food is clean.

*"**Do you not perceive that whatever enters a man from outside cannot defile him?**"… thus He
purified all foods.*

*"**What God has cleansed you must not call common.**"* See the note on Acts 10:9–16.

## And what he does with being right

*"**But to him who considers anything to be unclean, to him it is unclean.**"*

And then the sentence that costs something:

*"**If your brother is grieved because of your food, you are no longer walking in love. Do not destroy
with your food the one for whom Christ died.**"*

**Hyper hou Christos apethanen** — for whom **Christ** died.

The argument is not that the strong are wrong. It is that they are weighing a meal against a death.

*For **you were bought at a price**.*

## The measure

*Let each of us please his neighbour for his good, leading to edification. **For even Christ did not
please Himself**.*

**Romans** 15:2–3, and **Paul** applies the same standard to himself: *though I am free from all men, I
have made myself a servant to all.*

## What is at stake

*"**Do not let your good be spoken of as evil.**"*

**Blasphemeistho** — the verb behind *blasphemed*. A liberty that damages somebody turns into a
reproach on the thing that granted it.

> **What Paul does with being right.** Concedes the theology to the strong in one sentence and then
> asks them to weigh the meal against a death: *do not destroy with your food the one for whom
> Christ died.*
