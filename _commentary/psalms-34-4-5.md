Two verses, and the second one changes from *I* to *they*.

## The setting

*A Psalm of David when he pretended madness before Abimelech, who drove him away.*

He had fled to Gath carrying Goliath's sword, been recognised, and *changed his behaviour before
them, feigned madness, scratched on the doors of the gate, and let his saliva fall down on his
beard*.

The psalm that opens *I will bless the LORD at all times* came out of that week.

## The seeking

*I **went looking for the LORD**, and He answered.*

The Hebrew is **darashti et-YHWH ve'anani**.

**Darash** — to seek, to enquire of, to consult.

## The result

*He **pulled me out of all my fears**.*

The Hebrew is **umikkol-megurotai hitzilani** — from all my terrors He delivered me.

**Magor** — dread, terror. Not the general word for fear.

## What is not said

That the danger ended.

He was a fugitive before Gath and remained one afterwards. The verse says the terrors were removed
from him, not the situation.

## The shift

*Those who **look to Him light up**.*

The Hebrew is **hibbitu elav venaharu**.

**Nabat** — to look, to gaze at. **Nahar** — to shine, to be radiant, and also to flow.

## The change of person

Verse 4 is *I*. Verse 5 is *they*.

He starts with his own case and generalises it before the sentence ends.

## The faces

*Their faces **never have to hang in shame**.*

The Hebrew is **uphneihem al-yechparu** — and their faces are not ashamed.

**Chapher** — to be confounded, to blush.

## What the verse describes

Looking, and then shining.

Not effort. A face turned toward something reflects it.

## Where Moses does it

*And it was so, when Moses came down from Mount Sinai… **the skin of his face shone while he talked
with Him**.*

He did not know: *Moses did not know that the skin of his face shone*.

## What Paul does with that

*But we all, with unveiled face, **beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being
transformed into the same image from glory to glory**.* See the note on
2 Corinthians 3:16–18.

The Greek is **katoptrizomenoi** — beholding, or reflecting. Both senses are argued.

The transformation is passive and the only active verb is the looking.

## What comes next in the psalm

*This poor man cried out, and the LORD heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles.*

*The angel of the LORD **encamps all around those who fear Him**, and delivers them.*

*Oh, **taste and see that the LORD is good**.* See the note on Psalms 34:8–10.

## The teaching section

*Come close, children, and listen… Who among you **loves being alive**?*

*Then **keep your tongue from evil**… go after peace, chase it down.* See the note on
Psalms 34:11–14.

## The line about the bones

*He guards all his bones; **not one of them is broken**.*

Quoted at the crucifixion.

> **The two verbs.** Look, and shine. The only active thing in the sentence is the looking, and the
> radiance is what happens to a face that is turned toward something.
