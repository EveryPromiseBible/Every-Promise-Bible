Two "once"s set side by side, and one of them answers the other.

## The first

*People **die once**; that is set. And after that comes **the judgement**.*

The Greek is *it is appointed* — laid down, assigned.

Two facts about everybody, stated without qualification, and the writer does not
soften either.

## The second

*So **Christ was offered one time**, to carry away the sins of many.*

The Greek is **hapax** — once, once for all. The same word as in the first clause.

The parallel is the argument: one death for people, one offering for Him.

That is the point the whole letter has been building. The priests *stand ministering
daily, offering repeatedly the same sacrifices*, and He *sat down*. See the note on
Hebrews 10:11–13.

## "To carry away"

The Greek is **anaphero** — to bear up, to carry. It is the verb the Greek Old
Testament uses of the scapegoat carrying the sins away into the wilderness.

## The second appearance

*And **He will appear a second time** — **sin will have nothing to do with it** — to
bring salvation to those who are watching for Him.*

The clause in the middle is the striking one.

The first appearance dealt with sin. The second is described as having no relation to
it at all.

## Who it is for

*Those who are **watching for Him**.*

The Greek is **apekdechomai** — to await eagerly, to expect. Paul uses it of creation
groaning and waiting.

## The pattern underneath it

On the Day of Atonement the high priest went into the holy place with blood, and the
people waited outside to see him come out again.

If he came out, the sacrifice had been accepted.

The letter has just spent a chapter on that ceremony. See the note on
Hebrews 9:6–7.

## What is not in the sentence

Any second offering.

The letter says it elsewhere in so many words: *He then would have had to suffer often
since the foundation of the world; but now, **once at the end of the ages**, He has
appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself.*

## And the verse a chapter later

*By one offering He has **perfected forever** those who are being sanctified.*

> **What the two "once"s do together.** A man dies once and is judged. Christ was
> offered once and carries it. The writer puts them in one sentence so that the second
> is read as the answer to the first.
