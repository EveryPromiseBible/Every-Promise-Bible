Paul dates God's love by when it acted, and the timing is the whole argument.

## The condition

*While we were **still without strength**.*

The Greek is **asthenes** — weak, powerless, sick. It is the word used of invalids
in the Gospels.

Not *while we were doing our best*. Without strength.

## The timing

*At **just the right time**, Christ died for the ungodly.*

The Greek is *according to the season* — at the appointed moment, on schedule.

Nothing about it was a rescue improvised when things got bad enough. It happened at
the hour it was set for.

## Who for

*For **the ungodly**.*

Paul chooses the strongest word he has. Not the struggling, not the sincere. The
ungodly — those without reverence for God at all.

He uses the same word in chapter 4 about the God *who justifies the ungodly*.

## The human comparison

*It is a rare thing for anyone to die even for **a righteous person**, though for **a
good person** someone might possibly dare to die.*

Paul is careful here, and slightly wry. For a merely upright man — the sort you
respect and do not warm to — almost nobody would. For a genuinely good man, someone
might just about work up the nerve.

That is the top of what human love manages, and Paul says even that is uncommon.

## The contrast

*But God **puts His own love for us on display** in this: **while we were still
sinners**, Christ died for us.*

The Greek verb is present tense and continuous — He goes on demonstrating it. The
cross is described as an exhibit that stays up.

*While we were still sinners.* Not after we repented, not once we improved. Still.

## What the demonstration is for

Paul is making a case about certainty. The verses either side are about hope:

*Hope does not disappoint, because the love of God has been poured out in our hearts
by the Holy Spirit.*

And after: *much more then, having now been justified by His blood, **we shall be
saved from wrath through Him**.*

The argument runs from the harder thing to the easier. If He did that for enemies,
the rest follows.

## The phrase Paul keeps using

*Much more.* Five times in this chapter.

He never argues that grace merely matches the damage. At every step he says it
exceeds it.

> **What the cross settles about God's disposition.** Not that He loves us now that
> something has changed — that He loved us when nothing had. The demonstration is
> dated to the worst possible moment on purpose, so that no later moment can be worse.
