Paul is being forced into boasting by people in Corinth who boast well, and he
tells the greatest thing that ever happened to him in the third person.

## "I know a man in Christ"

He is talking about himself. He says so a few verses later — *lest I should be
exalted above measure by the abundance of the revelations* — but he will not put
it in the first person while he is describing it.

The distance in the grammar is the point of the paragraph.

## Fourteen years

*Fourteen years ago.*

He had not mentioned it. Not in Galatians, not in Romans, not in either
Thessalonian letter. Fourteen years of preaching and letter-writing and never
once bringing it up, and it comes out now only because the Corinthians have
pushed him into a boasting contest he did not want.

## "God knows"

*Whether he was in the body I do not know, or out of the body I do not know; **God
knows**.*

He says it twice. The most remarkable experience in his life, and the thing he
insists on is what he is not able to tell you about it.

## The third heaven

**Paradise** in the second sentence appears to be the same place named twice —
Paul restates the vision rather than describing a second stage.

The word is Persian in origin: a walled garden, a royal park. The Greek Old
Testament uses it for Eden, and Jesus uses it to the thief on the cross.

## What he heard

*Words beyond all speaking, words that no human being is permitted to say aloud.*

The Greek is **arrheta** — unspoken, unspeakable — and then a second clause: it
is *not permitted*. Both that they cannot be put into words and that he is not
allowed to try.

So the content is withheld. Whatever Paul was shown, the church did not get it
here.

## What he did pass on

He kept that to himself for fourteen years. What he did hand over, he introduces
in the other letter with the same claim to have got it directly: *I received from
the Lord what I also delivered to you* — the bread and the cup.

Of everything he was given, that is the piece he made sure everybody had. See the
note on 1 Corinthians 11:23–26.

> **What comes immediately after.** The thorn. *Lest I should be exalted above
> measure.* Paul places the highest experience and the standing affliction in the
> same paragraph, and the sentence that ties them together is *My grace is
> sufficient for you.*
