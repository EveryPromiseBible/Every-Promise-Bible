The rebuke is public, and it lands on a set of feet. Paul does not leave it there: what he says
next is the most concentrated paragraph on justification in the letter.

## What had happened

Peter came to Antioch and ate with Gentile believers.

*But when they came, **he withdrew and separated himself**, fearing those who were of the
circumcision.*

And the rest followed, *so that even Barnabas was carried away with their hypocrisy*.

## The verb

*When I saw that **their feet were not walking straight** toward the truth of the good news.*

The Greek is **orthopodousin** — from **orthos**, straight, and **pous**, foot.

The word appears once in the New Testament. It means to walk a straight line.

## Where the fault was, and where it was not

Peter had not changed his mind. He is the one who stood up at the council in Jerusalem and argued
the opposite case, and this passage gives the cause of the withdrawal outright: he pulled back
*fearing those who were of the circumcision*. Fear, not conviction.

But it does not follow that the matter was merely behavioural, and Paul plainly does not treat it
that way. What a man does at a table is a statement. This one said, without a word, that Gentile
believers were not quite acceptable as they stood — and that is a gospel, a false one, preached by
a chair.

Which is why Paul answers a withdrawal with doctrine: *a man is not justified by the works of the
law*, and *if righteousness comes through the law, then Christ died in vain*. Nobody reaches for
that to correct an inconsistency. He reached for it because the thing the feet were veering away
from is named in the charge itself — **the truth of the gospel**.

## The phrase

**Pros tēn alētheian tou euangeliou** — toward the truth of the gospel.

The gospel is a direction here. Something you can be seen veering away from.

## The setting

*I said to Peter **in front of them all**.*

The Greek is **emprosthen pantōn**.

Public conduct, public correction. The withdrawal had been visible to the whole church.

## The charge

*"If you, a Jew, **live like the nations and not like the Jews**, how is it that **you press the nations
to live like the Jews**?"*

The Greek is **ethnikōs zēs kai ouchi Ioudaïkōs** — you live Gentile-wise and not Jewish-wise.

And **anankazeis Ioudaïzein** — you compel them to Judaize.

## The inconsistency

Peter had been eating with them. That was his practice, and God had given him a vision about it:
*what God has cleansed you must not call common*. See the note on Acts 10:9–16.

Withdrawing from the table now says, without a word being spoken, that the Gentiles must change to
be acceptable.

## Why it mattered so much

A shared table was the visible statement that both groups were on the same footing.

Pulling back from it preaches a different gospel more effectively than an argument would.

## What Paul says next

*Knowing that a man is **not justified by the works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ**.* See
the note on Galatians 2:15–16.

And *I do not set aside the grace of God; for if righteousness comes through the law, **then Christ
died in vain**.* See the note on Galatians 2:19–20.

The most concentrated statement of justification in the letter is the continuation of a rebuke
about dinner.

## What the letter is fighting

*I marvel that you are turning away so soon… **to a different gospel**.* See the note on
Galatians 1:6–7.

## What is not recorded

Peter's reply.

## What Peter wrote later

*Our beloved brother Paul, according to the wisdom given to him, has written to you… **in which are
some things hard to understand**.* See the note on 2 Peter 3:15.

He calls him beloved.

## And at the council

*"God, who knows the heart, acknowledged them by giving them the Holy Spirit, just as He did to us,
and **made no distinction** between us and them."*

Peter's own speech in Jerusalem, arguing the position Paul had to remind him of at the table.

> **The word.** **Orthopodousin** — they were not walking a straight line, and the line is named:
> *toward the truth of the gospel*. Paul confronts a set of feet and then preaches justification by
> faith at them, because at Antioch the feet were doing the preaching. Peter believed the right
> thing and was still, for an afternoon, announcing the wrong one — which is how much rests on a
> gospel being lived where people can see it, and how little of it rests on us that Christ still
> died for a man who got up from that table.
