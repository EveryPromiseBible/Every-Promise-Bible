One verse about two things that cannot occupy the same space.

## The claim

*Love has **no fear** in it.*

The Greek is *there is no fear in love*. Not that love overpowers fear — that fear is
not in it.

## The mechanism

*Love **grown full drives fear out the door**.*

**Teleia agape** — love brought to completion, matured, finished.

The verb is **ballo exo** — to throw outside. It is a physical, unceremonious word;
the same one used of casting out demons.

Fear is not persuaded to leave.

## What fear brings with it

*Fear brings **torment** with it.*

The Greek is **kolasis** — punishment, correction, penalty. It is the word used in
Matthew 25 for *everlasting punishment*.

So the fear John has in view is not nervousness. It is the fear of being punished,
and he says it carries its own punishment inside it.

## The diagnosis

*Anyone who is afraid **has not come to full growth in love**.*

Not *does not love enough*, and not *is a bad Christian*.

The Greek is that he has not been perfected — completed — in love.

And the whole letter has been clear about whose love that is: *we love Him **because He
first loved us***, in the very next verse.

The growing up is in the receiving.

## What the verse before it says

*Love has been perfected among us in this: that **we may have boldness in the day of
judgement**; because **as He is, so are we in this world**.* See the note on
1 John 4:17.

That is the context. Boldness on the day everybody is afraid of, and the reason given
is a present-tense comparison: *as He is* — not as He was, and not as we will be.

## What John does not say

He does not say fear is a sin, or tell anybody to stop being afraid.

He describes it as an indicator — the way a thermometer reports rather than accuses —
and points at the remedy, which is not an effort.

## The other fear in Scripture

*The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom.* Proverbs uses a different Hebrew
idea — awe, reverence, the response to size.

John is talking about the fear that expects a blow.

> **What the letter keeps saying about the order.** *We love Him because He first loved
> us.* Every command in 1 John about loving comes after a statement about being loved,
> and this verse names the growing-up as the thing that removes the fear.
