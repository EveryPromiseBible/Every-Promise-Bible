Paul thanks God for something that happened inside another man's chest.

## What the chapter is about

The collection for the poor believers in Jerusalem. Paul spends two whole chapters on it,
which is longer than he spends on almost anything else practical.

It opens with the Macedonians, who *in a great trial of affliction* and *deep poverty*
gave *beyond their ability, freely willing*.

## The thanks

*Thanks be to God, **who put into the heart of Titus** the same deep care for you that we
carry.*

The Greek is *who puts the same earnest care into the heart of Titus* — **didonti**,
giving, in the present.

Paul does not thank Titus. He thanks the one who put it there.

## The word

**Spouden** — diligence, earnestness, haste. The same word Paul uses three verses earlier
for the Corinthians' own zeal, and again at the end of the chapter.

It runs through the passage like a thread: the care God put in Titus, the care Paul carries,
the care he is asking the Corinthians for.

## The same care

*The **same** deep care for you **that we carry**.*

Not a lesser version delegated downward. The identical thing.

Titus is being sent because he already wants to go.

## The volunteering

*He **took our appeal to heart**, and **being more eager still**, he **set out for you on
his own initiative**.*

Three stages in one sentence: he accepted the request, he exceeded it, and then he went
before he was sent.

The Greek for *of his own accord* is **authairetos** — self-chosen. Paul used the same word
of the Macedonians a few verses back: they gave *freely willing*, self-chosen.

## Where the accepting ends

Paul makes the request, and Titus outruns it. The letter is careful to say both — *he
accepted the exhortation* and *being more diligent, he went of his own accord*.

Obedience and enthusiasm, in that order, in one man.

## What Paul does with the money

He will not carry it alone. *Avoiding this: that anyone should blame us in this lavish gift
which is administered by us — **providing honourable things, not only in the sight of the
Lord, but also in the sight of men**.*

Two brothers travel with Titus, one *chosen by the churches*. Paul builds the audit in.

## What he has just written

*For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that **though He was rich, yet for your
sakes He became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich.*

That is the argument of the whole chapter, and everything about Titus and the brothers is
the logistics under it.

## Who Titus was

A Greek, uncircumcised, whom Paul refused to have circumcised as a test case in Galatians.

He is the one who brought Paul the news from Corinth that lifted him — *God, who comforts
the downcast, comforted us by the coming of Titus*. See the note on 2 Corinthians 7:5–7.

Paul calls him *my partner and fellow worker concerning you*.

## Where the desire comes from

*It is **God who works in you both to will and to do** for His good pleasure.* See the note
on Philippians 2:12–13.

The willing is listed first, and it is God's work too.

> **What Paul noticed.** A man wanted to go, and Paul's response was to thank God — because
> he read the wanting itself as something that had been placed there.
