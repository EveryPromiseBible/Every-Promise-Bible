---
key: Ephesians 4:7–8
title: "Grace measured out, and a psalm He altered"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The doctrine half of the letter is over and the walking half has begun — and before Paul says one
word about behaviour, he says what every believer was handed.

## To each one

*And **to each one of us, individually**, grace was given.*

**Heni hekasto** — to one each. Paul has spent three chapters on *us*, the body, the household, the
temple. Now he singles everybody out.

And what is given to each is **charis** — grace. Not a task, not a standard, not a rank. The gifts
in the next verses grow out of this word, and the letter's most famous sentence has already told
you what kind of thing grace is: *by grace you have been saved, through faith, and that not of
yourselves; it is the gift of God*.

## Whose measure

*Measured out to us as **Christ's gift** decided.*

**Kata to metron tes doreas tou Christou** — according to the measure of the gift of Christ.

There is a measure, and it is not our capacity, our maturity or our record. The measuring is done
by His giving. That removes both anxieties in one clause: nobody was shortchanged, and nobody was
promoted.

## The word He changed

*"When He went up on high, He led a train of captives, and **He gave gifts to men**."*

Paul is quoting Psalm 68, and the psalm says *You have **received** gifts among men*. He changes the
verb — and it is not a slip. See the note on Psalms 68:15–18.

A conquering king came home up the processional road with the spoil of the battle behind him,
*received* the tribute, and then distributed it through the city to his own people. Receiving and
giving are two ends of one triumph.

So Paul reads the psalm through the ascension: the spoil was taken at a cross and an empty tomb,
carried up on high, and handed out. Every gift in the church is plunder from a battle none of us
fought.

## Captivity taken captive

**Echmaloteusen aichmalosian** — He took captive the captivity.

The thing that used to hold people is now the thing being held. *That through death He might
destroy him who had the power of death, and release those who through fear of death were all their
lifetime subject to bondage.*

> **Everything you have was carried up that hill and handed back down.** Grace to each one,
> measured by His gift and not by your fitness for it. Before Ephesians asks you to walk worthy of
> the calling, it tells you that the supply for the walk was distributed on the day He ascended,
> and your name was on the list.
