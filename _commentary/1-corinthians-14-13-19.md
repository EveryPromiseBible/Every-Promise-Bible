Paul describes two things that can be going on at once, and says he does both.

## The description

*If I pray in a tongue, **my spirit prays, but my understanding is unfruitful**.*

The Greek for *understanding* is **nous** — the mind, the reasoning faculty.

**Unfruitful** is **akarpos** — bearing no fruit. Paul is not saying the praying is
unfruitful. He says the mind is not the part producing anything.

## What he does not conclude

He does not conclude that it should stop. The next line is the resolution:

*"**What is it then?** I will pray with the spirit, **and I will pray with the
understanding also**; I will sing with the spirit, **and I will sing with the
understanding also**."*

Four verbs, two pairs, and the connector is *also* rather than *instead*.

## The prayer he asks for

*Therefore let the one who speaks in a tongue **pray that he may interpret**.*

Interpretation is itself something to be prayed for. Paul treats it as available on
request.

## Why it matters in a room

*"Otherwise, if you bless with the spirit, **how will the person sitting there** who does
not follow any of it **say the 'Amen'** to your thanksgiving?"*

The Greek is *the one who fills the place of the uninformed*.

The Amen is the congregation's part. It was the response in the synagogue and it is what
Paul is protecting — a person cannot agree with what they cannot follow.

## The concession he makes in the same breath

*"For **you indeed give thanks well enough**, but the other person is not built up."*

He does not withdraw the first half. The thanksgiving is fine. The problem named is one
of usefulness to somebody else in the room.

## The claim

*"**I thank my God that I speak with tongues more than you all**."*

Said to the church that was doing it most, and he outdoes them.

Nobody would guess it from Acts or from any of his letters, which is the point of him
having to say it.

## The comparison

*"Yet **in the church** I would rather speak **five words with my understanding**, so that
I might **teach others also**, than **ten thousand words in a tongue**."*

Five against ten thousand — a ratio of one to two thousand.

And the whole comparison is qualified at the front: **in the church**. Paul is ranking
the two for a gathering, not in general.

## The governing rule

The chapter states it repeatedly: *let all things be done for **edification**.*

**Oikodome** — house-building. Paul's whole test in this chapter is whether something
builds anybody up, and he applies it to a meeting.

## What he says four verses earlier

*He who speaks in a tongue **edifies himself**, but he who prophesies edifies the
church.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 14:1–5.

Paul states both as facts and then spends the chapter on which one belongs in a public
gathering.

## How the chapter ends

*Therefore, brethren, desire earnestly to prophesy, **and do not forbid to speak with
tongues**. Let all things be done decently and in order.*

Both clauses, in the same sentence, as his closing instruction.

> **What Paul never does in the chapter.** Argue that the thing is not real, not from
> God, or not for them. His entire concern is the person sitting in the seat who cannot
> say Amen to what they did not follow.
