The writer has been building a case out of Genesis 14 and Psalm 110, and here he adds
one more piece: an oath.

## What is being compared

*"Those other priests **were made priests with no oath sworn over them**."*

Aaron's line was appointed by command. Exodus says *take Aaron your brother, and his
sons with him* — an instruction, not a vow.

The writer has combed the Old Testament for a divine oath attached to the Levitical
priesthood and found none.

## The oath that does exist

*"**The Lord has sworn it and will not change His mind: You are a priest forever —
Melchizedek's kind of priest**."*

Psalm 110:4, the verse after the one about the footstool. See the note on
Mark 12:35–37.

David wrote it about a descendant of his. The order named is older than Aaron by four
hundred years.

## "Will not change His mind"

The Hebrew is *will not repent*. The same construction appears in Numbers — *God is not
a man, that He should lie, nor a son of man, that He should repent.*

An oath is a promise with a second layer under it. God swearing means the thing is
placed beyond revision.

The writer makes the point outright a chapter earlier: *God, determining to show more
abundantly to the heirs of promise the immutability of His counsel, **confirmed it by an
oath**.*

## Forever

The Greek is **eis ton aiona** — into the age.

A Levitical priest served from thirty to fifty and died. The writer says as much:
*there have been many priests, because they were prevented by death from continuing.*

The oath's subject does not vacate the post.

## The conclusion

*"**That is the measure of it: Jesus has become the guarantee of a better
covenant**."*

The Greek for *guarantee* is **engyos** — a surety, the person who stands good for
somebody else's debt. It appears once in the New Testament, here.

A surety is not a witness or a middleman. He is liable.

## The direction it runs

Elsewhere the writer calls Him **mesites**, mediator, standing between the two parties.
**Engyos** is stronger: He is not holding the covenant open, He is standing behind it.

The covenant's security rests on the person sworn about rather than on the people
covered by it.

## Where Melchizedek came from

He walks out of Genesis 14 with bread and wine, blesses Abram, receives a tenth, and
walks off the page. Four verses in the whole of the Old Testament, plus one line in a
psalm.

The writer of Hebrews builds two chapters on him. See the note on Genesis 14:17–20 and
on Hebrews 7:8.

> **What is unusual about the argument.** The writer's evidence is partly what is *not*
> in the text. No oath over Aaron, no genealogy for Melchizedek, no record of his birth
> or death — and he reads the silences as deliberate.
