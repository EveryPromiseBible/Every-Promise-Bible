Two comparisons, and both are chosen because they are the best of their kind.

## Where the psalm has been

*The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament shows His handiwork.*

*There is no speech nor language where their voice is not heard.* See the note on
Psalms 19:1–4.

Six verses on the sun running its circuit like a strong man to run a race, and then an abrupt
turn to the law.

## The turn

*The law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul; the testimony of the LORD is sure, making
wise the simple.*

Six statements, each with a name for the word, an adjective, and an effect. See the note on
Psalms 19:7–9.

Then this.

## The first comparison

*They are **more to be desired than gold**, than **heaps of the purest gold**.*

The Hebrew is **mipaz rav** — than much fine gold.

**Paz** is refined gold, the highest grade. And then *much* of it.

David does not compare the word to gold generally. He raises the comparison twice — the best
gold, and a lot of it.

## The second

*They are **sweeter than honey**, than **honey straight from the comb**.*

The Hebrew is **nophet tzuphim** — the honey that drips from the comb by itself, before pressing.

Again the superlative form. Not honey, but the first drip.

## Why these two

Gold is the standard of value. Honey is the standard of pleasure.

In a world with no sugar, honey was the sweetest thing anybody had ever tasted, and this is the
sweetest kind of honey.

The psalm picks the top of each scale and says the word is above it.

## Where the honey turns up again

*How sweet are Your words to my taste, **sweeter than honey to my mouth!*** See the note on
Psalms 119:97–104.

And in Ezekiel: *So I ate it, and **it was in my mouth like honey in sweetness***, of a scroll.

And John: *it was as sweet as honey in my mouth. But when I had eaten it, my stomach became
bitter*.

The scroll is sweet going in.

## The first use

*Moreover **by them Your servant is warned**.*

The Hebrew is **nizhar** — warned, admonished, enlightened. The root carries the idea of shining.

The same word the psalm might have used of the sun in the first half.

## The second

*And **in keeping them there is great reward**.*

The Hebrew is **eqev rav** — literally *much heel*, an idiom meaning consequence, the thing that
follows on behind.

Not payment for keeping them. What comes along after.

## What David asks immediately

*Who can understand his errors? **Cleanse me from secret faults**.*

*Keep back Your servant also **from presumptuous sins**; let them not have dominion over me.*

He praises the law and then asks to be cleared of what it has just shown him.

## The last verse

*Let **the words of my mouth** and **the meditation of my heart** be acceptable in Your sight, O
LORD, my strength and my Redeemer.*

The psalm has been about God's speech — in the heavens, and in the law.

It ends by asking that the psalmist's own speech be acceptable.

## What the whole psalm does

Sky, then scroll, then self.

Three witnesses, narrowing.

> **The comparisons.** Not gold and honey — *refined* gold in heaps, and the honey that drips
> from the comb before pressing. The top of both scales.
