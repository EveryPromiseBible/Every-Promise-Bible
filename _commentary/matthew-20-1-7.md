The setup of the parable, and the detail everybody remembers is in the last line.

## The five trips

Dawn, nine, noon, three, and five in the afternoon. The landowner goes out to the marketplace
five times in one day.

Nobody in the ancient world with a vineyard did this. You hire at dawn and you get on with it.

*"**The Son of Man has come to seek and to save that which was lost.**"*

## The first contract

*"**He settled with them on a denarius for the day** and sent them out."*

The Greek is **symphonesas** — having agreed, from which English gets *symphony*.

It is the only group in the parable that negotiates. Everybody hired later is told *I will pay you
whatever is fair*, and goes.

*Now to him who works, **the wages are not counted as grace but as debt**. But to him who does not
work but believes on Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is accounted for righteousness.*

**Romans** 4, and the two words are **misthos** and **charis**. The dawn workers are on a wage. See
the note on Matthew 20:13–16.

## Five o'clock

*"And about five o'clock he went out and found **still others standing there**."*

One hour of daylight left. Nobody hires at five.

## The question

*"**Why have you been standing here all day doing nothing?**"*

It sounds like an accusation, and the reply removes the charge.

## The answer

*"**Because no one hired us.**"*

They had been in the marketplace since dawn. They were available all day, in the right place, and
nobody wanted them.

That is the parable's account of the eleventh-hour men. Not lazy. Not late. Unchosen.

*But **God has chosen the foolish things of the world** to put to shame the wise, and God has chosen
the weak things of the world… and **the things which are despised God has chosen**, and the things
which are not, to bring to nothing the things that are.*

*"**You did not choose Me, but I chose you.**"*

## The same four words

*"'**You go into the vineyard too**,' he told them."*

Said to every group after the first, and to the five o'clock men without any figure attached at
all.

*Isaiah is very bold and says: "**I was found by those who did not seek Me; I was made manifest to
those who did not ask for Me**."*

**Romans** 10, and **Paul** applies it to people outside the covenant entirely.

## What prompted the parable

*Then Peter answered and said to Him, "See, we have left all and followed You. **Therefore what
shall we have?**"*

The chapter before. The parable is the answer to a disciple asking about his rate.

## Where it lands

*"So the last will be first, and the first last."*

The sentence closes chapter 19 and reappears at the end of the parable, framing it on both sides.

> **The line that changes the parable.** *Because no one hired us.* They had been standing there
> since dawn. The last men in are not the ones who came late — they are the ones nobody wanted, and
> the landowner had gone out five times.
