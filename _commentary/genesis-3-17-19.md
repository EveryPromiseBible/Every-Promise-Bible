The sentence on the man, and what is cursed in it is not the man.

## What is cursed

*"**The ground is cursed** because of you."*

Not Adam. The serpent is cursed in verse 14 and the ground is cursed here, and the
man is not.

The Hebrew is *cursed is the adamah for your sake* — and **adamah** is the ground he
was made out of and named after. See the note on Genesis 2:4–7.

## The three things

- *"In **toil** you will eat from it all the days of your life"*
- *"It will bring you **thorns and thistles**"*
- *"By **the sweat of your brow** you will eat your bread"*

Toil, thorns, and sweat. Work itself was not the curse — he had been given the garden
*to tend and keep it* before any of this. What changed is what work costs.

The Hebrew for *toil* is **itstsabon**, painful labour, and it is the same word used
of the woman's pain a few verses earlier. They get the same noun.

## Thorns

The Hebrew **qots** appears here for the first time in Scripture.

They are the visible mark of the curse on the ground — what grows without being
planted, in soil that used to produce a garden.

A cohort of Roman soldiers twisted a circle of them and pressed it onto His head. See
the note on Matthew 27:27–31.

## Sweat

*"By the sweat of your brow you will eat your bread."*

And in a garden on the Mount of Olives: *His sweat became like great drops of blood
**falling to the ground**.* See the note on Luke 22:39–46.

The sweat of a man's face, and the cursed ground receiving it.

## "Until you return to the ground"

*"For out of it you were taken. **Dust you are, and to dust you will return.**"*

The account closes the loop it opened. He was formed from dust and breathed into;
now the dust part is named as the destination.

## What is not in the sentence

Any statement that God has finished with him.

Three verses later God makes them tunics of skin and clothes them — the first death
in Scripture, and it is an animal killed to cover two people.

And before the sentence on the man, in verse 15, the promise to the serpent: *He
shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise His heel.* See the note on
Genesis 3:14–15.

The remedy is announced before the sentence is passed.

## Where the ground is dealt with

*Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us* —
Galatians 3, and the proof text Paul uses is *cursed is everyone who hangs on a
tree.* See the note on Galatians 3:10–14.

Thorns on His head, sweat on the ground, and a tree.

> **What Lamech named his son for.** *This one will **comfort us** concerning our work
> and the toil of our hands, **because of the ground which the LORD has cursed**.* He
> called him Noah, which means rest — and Genesis says he was the man who found grace.
> See the note on Genesis 6:5–8.
