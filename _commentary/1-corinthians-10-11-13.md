One of the most quoted promises in the New Testament, sitting immediately after a
list of Israel's worst failures in the desert.

## The setting

Paul has just run through the golden calf, the immorality at Peor, the grumbling
and the snakes. Then: *all these things happened to them as examples, and they
were written down as instruction for us.*

The word for examples is **typoi** — patterns, impressions, the mark a die leaves.
Paul reads Israel's history as a shape that repeats.

## The warning that comes first

*Let the one who thinks he is standing take care that he does not fall.*

This is the sentence the famous promise is attached to, and it is a warning
against confidence, not an encouragement to it. The order matters: watch
yourself, **then** the assurance.

## What is promised, precisely

- the testing is *common to everyone* — not unique, not a sign of being singled
  out
- God is faithful, and will not allow testing beyond what you are able to bear
- with the testing He *will also make the way of escape*

The last phrase is worth reading closely: the escape is **so that you are able to
bear it**. Not a door out of the situation — a way through it that does not
break you. Paul's own thorn, which he asked three times to have removed and did
not, is the same shape.

> **On "more than you can bear."** The verse is often quoted about suffering in
> general — God will not give you more than you can handle. Paul is talking about
> *temptation and testing*, and elsewhere he says flatly that he was burdened
> beyond his strength, so that he would rely on God who raises the dead. The
> comfort here is real but specific: no one is ever cornered into sin with no way
> out.
