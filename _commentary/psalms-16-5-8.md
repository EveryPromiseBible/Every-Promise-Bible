David describes what he was allotted, using the vocabulary of a land survey.

## The portion

*The LORD is **all I have, my cup, my share**.*

The Hebrew is the language of inheritance — *the portion of my inheritance and my
cup.*

It is what the Levites were told: *I am your portion and your inheritance among the
children of Israel.* They got no territory and were given Him instead. See the note
on Ezekiel 44:28–31.

David was a king with a kingdom, and he uses the landless tribe's language about
himself.

## The measuring line

*The lines have fallen for me in **lovely places**; yes, **the inheritance I have is
beautiful**.*

The *lines* are surveyor's cords — how land was measured and allotted.

David wrote this having spent years in caves, hunted by Saul, and later driven out
of his own capital by his son. He calls the plot he was given lovely.

## The night

*I will bless the LORD who counsels me; **even in the night my heart instructs me**.*

The Hebrew is *my reins instruct me in the night seasons* — the kidneys, which in
Hebrew idiom are the seat of the deepest inner life, below thought.

Something in him is being taught while he is awake in the dark.

The counsel comes first in the verse and the night instruction second. What he has
been given in the day is what surfaces at three in the morning.

## Set at the right hand

*I have **set the LORD always before me**; **He is at my right hand**, I will not be
shaken.*

The Hebrew for *set* is deliberate — placed, positioned. It is something David does.

And the right hand is the position of the advocate, or of the strong man who defends
you. In Zechariah 3 it is where the accuser was standing. See the note on
Zechariah 3:1–5.

Here it is occupied by somebody else.

## Where Peter takes this psalm

At Pentecost, in his first sermon, he quotes the verses that follow:

*Therefore my heart is glad and my glory rejoices; my flesh also will rest in hope.
For You will not leave my soul in Sheol, nor will You allow Your Holy One to see
corruption.*

Peter says David *spoke concerning the resurrection of Christ, that His soul was not
left in Hades, nor did His flesh see corruption.*

Paul quotes it too, at Antioch.

## How the psalm ends

*You will show me the path of life; in Your presence is fullness of joy; at Your
right hand are pleasures forevermore.*

> **What the psalm is titled.** *A Michtam of David* — a word nobody has definitively
> translated, possibly from a root meaning to engrave, or gold. Six psalms carry it,
> and every one of them was written in trouble.
