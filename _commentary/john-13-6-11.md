Peter refuses twice, in opposite directions, within three sentences.

## What is happening

He has laid aside His outer clothing, tied a towel round His waist, poured water into a basin, and
started washing feet. See the note on John 13:4–5.

*Knowing that the Father had given all things into His hands, and that He had come from God and
was going to God.*

John states the authority immediately before the basin.

## The refusal

*He came to Simon Peter, and Peter said to Him, "**Lord — are You going to wash my feet?**"*

The Greek word order puts the two pronouns side by side: **su mou** — *You, my* feet.

The objection is not to foot-washing. It is to who is holding the towel.

## The answer

*"**You do not understand now what I am doing, but afterward you will**."*

The Greek is **meta tauta** — after these things.

He does not explain. He says the understanding will come later.

## The second refusal

*"**You will never wash my feet**," Peter said. "**Never**."*

The Greek is **ou me nipses mou tous podas eis ton aiona** — a double negative with *unto the
age*. The strongest refusal Greek can construct.

## What Peter thinks he is doing

Defending Him. It is the same instinct as *far be it from You, Lord* when the cross was
predicted. See the note on Mark 8:31–33.

Both times he is protecting Jesus from something Jesus intends.

## The reply

*"**If I do not wash you, you have no part with Me**."*

The Greek is **ouk echeis meros met' emou** — you have no share with Me.

**Meros** — a portion, a share, an allotment. The word used of an inheritance.

## The reversal

*"**Then, Lord — not my feet only. Wash my hands and my head as well**."*

From *never* to *all of me* in one sentence.

Peter is consistent in the only way that matters to him: whatever gets him the share.

## The correction

*"**Anyone who has bathed does not need washing except for his feet; he is clean all over**."*

The Greek uses two verbs.

**Louo** — to bathe the whole body.

**Nipto** — to wash a part. The word used throughout the passage for the feet.

## The picture

A man bathed at home, walking to dinner in sandals on a dusty road, arriving with clean body and
dirty feet.

The bath is not repeated. The feet are washed at the door.

## The verdict

*"And **you are clean — though not all of you**."*

## The note

*He knew who was going to hand Him over. That is why He said, "You are not all clean."*

John supplies it, as he does throughout the chapter.

## Whose feet were in the basin

Judas was at that table.

The text does not say his were skipped, and nothing suggests they were.

## What He says afterwards

*"Do you know what I have done to you? You call Me Teacher and Lord, and you say well, for so I am.
**If I then, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's
feet**."*

*"**For I have given you an example, that you should do as I have done to you**."* See the note on
John 13:12–17.

## What He said at the start of the meal

*Having loved His own who were in the world, **He loved them to the end**.*

The Greek is **eis telos** — to the end, or completely.

> **The two verbs.** **Louo** — the whole-body bath, not repeated. **Nipto** — washing a part, done
> at the door. Peter asked for the first and was told he had already had it.
