key: Revelation 21:5–7
title: ""It is done""
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The One on the throne speaks directly, which He does almost nowhere else in the book, and what He
says is short.

## New in kind

*Look — **I am making everything new**.*

**Kaina** — new in kind, fresh, of a quality not seen before. Greek has another word, **nea**, for
new in time — the latest of a series. John's word is the other one.

And notice what is not said. Not *I am making all new things* — the objects are not being replaced.
*I am making all things new*: the same creation, remade.

The verb is present tense. He is doing it as He speaks.

## The finished word

*And He said to me, **It is done**.*

**Gegonan** — a perfect: it has come to be and it stands done.

There is one other place in John's writing where a work is declared finished in a single word from
the mouth of God, and it was said from a cross: ***Tetelestai*** — it is finished.

Two words at two ends of the story. One closes the work that made the new creation possible; the
other closes the story that work opened.

## Free

*Anyone who is thirsty — I will give him a drink from the spring of the water of life, and **it will
cost him nothing**.*

**Dorean** — as a gift, freely, without charge. It is the word Paul uses of the central transaction
of the gospel: *being justified **freely** by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ
Jesus.*

The last offer in the Bible is made on exactly the same terms as the first one, and the only
qualification named is thirst.

Jesus had said it Himself, standing up in the temple on the last day of the feast: *if anyone
thirsts, let him come to Me and drink.*

## What the drinker becomes

*I will be his God, and **he will be My son**.*

That is the covenant formula — *I will be their God and they shall be My people* — running through
the whole Bible from Abraham onward. Here at the end it is said in the singular, and the word is not
*people*. It is **huios**: son.

The promise that began as a nation's charter finishes as an adoption.

> **The first letter and the last.** Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end — and between those
> two claims He puts a free drink and a son. That is the whole scale of it: the One who holds the
> alphabet of everything bends down at the last page to offer water to anybody who is thirsty, and
> He has already paid for it.
