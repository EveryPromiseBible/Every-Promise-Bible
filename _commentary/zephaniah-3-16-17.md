title: "He will rejoice over you with singing"

The sentence about God that has no parallel anywhere else in Scripture.

## The address

*"On that day they will say to Jerusalem, '**Do not fear, Zion; do not let your hands hang limp.**'"*

The Hebrew is **al-yirpu yadayikh** — let not your hands drop. It is the idiom for giving up, and
it is what the prophets say to people who have stopped.

*Therefore **strengthen the hands which hang down, and the feeble knees**.*

**Hebrews** 12 uses the same phrase, and the instruction before it is *looking unto Jesus.*

## Who is with her

*"**The LORD your God is with you, the Mighty One who saves.**"*

The Hebrew is **gibbor yoshia** — a warrior who saves. **Gibbor** is the word in Isaiah's list of
names: *Wonderful, Counsellor, **Mighty God**, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.*

The one standing in the middle of the city is described with a soldier's noun.

## The three things He does

*"He will **take great delight in you**. In His love **He will quiet you** and rebuke you no more. He
will **rejoice over you with singing**."*

Three verbs, and God is the subject of all three. Nothing in the sentence is asked of Zion.

## The first

**Yasis alayikh besimchah** — He will exult over you with joy.

**Sus** is not quiet approval. It is the verb for a bridegroom's face at a wedding: *as the
bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so shall your God rejoice over you.* See the note on
Isaiah 62:1–5.

## The second

*"**In His love He will quiet you.**"*

The Hebrew is **yacharish be'ahavato** — He will be silent in His love.

**Charash** — to be still, to say nothing. It is a strange verb here and the ancient translators
found it strange too; the Greek Old Testament read it as *He will renew you in His love*.

Taken as it stands, it is the silence of somebody who has stopped bringing anything up.

*"For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and **their sins and their lawless deeds I will
remember no more**."*

*Love… **thinks no evil**… bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all
things.*

**Paul**'s phrase is **ou logizetai to kakon** — keeps no account of the wrong. It is a bookkeeping
term, and it is the same silence.

## The third

*"He will **rejoice over you with singing**."*

**Yagil alayikh berinnah** — He will spin with joy over you with a ringing cry.

**Gil** is the verb for whirling. **Rinnah** is a shout, the loud kind — the word used of the noise
at a coronation.

It is the only place in the Bible where God sings, and He is singing about people who three
chapters earlier were being searched for with lamps.

*"Likewise, I say to you, **there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who
repents**."*

*"It was right that we should **make merry and be glad**, for your brother was dead and is alive
again."*

**Luke** 15 has music and dancing in the house, and the father is the one who ordered it.

*Who for **the joy that was set before Him** endured the cross, despising the shame.*

## And in the upper room

*And when they had **sung a hymn**, they went out to the Mount of Olives.*

**Matthew** 26. The last thing recorded before Gethsemane is **Jesus** singing, and what they sang
at Passover was the Hallel.

> **The only place God sings.** Over a city He had spent two chapters condemning, in a sentence
> where every verb is His and nothing is asked of her — and the middle verb means He will say
> nothing more about it.
