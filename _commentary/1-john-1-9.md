The surprising word in the verse is not *forgives*. It is *just*.

## The condition

*If we **own up to our sins**.*

The Greek is **homologōmen** — from **homou**, same, and **legō**, to say. To say the same thing.

Agreement, not disclosure. God has already named it; the word is for saying the same about it.

## What it is not

Not a tariff. Nothing in the verse counts items or measures sorrow.

The tense is present — **homologōmen**, an ongoing habit of agreeing with God about what is true.

## The two grounds

*He **keeps His word** and He **does the just thing**.*

The Greek is **pistos estin kai dikaios** — He is faithful and righteous.

## The first

**Pistos** — faithful. He said He would; He does.

## The second

**Dikaios** — righteous, just.

This is the word that does the work. Forgiveness here is not framed as leniency. It is framed as
the correct verdict.

## Why it can be

*And **He Himself is the propitiation for our sins**, and not for ours only but also for the whole
world.* See the note on 1 John 2:2.

The Greek is **hilasmos**.

A debt that has been paid cannot justly be collected twice. That is the logic the word **dikaios**
is carrying.

## The two verbs

*He **forgives us our sins**, and He **washes us clean of every wrong**.*

The Greek is **aphē** — to send away, release. And **katharisē** — to cleanse.

Remission and cleansing. The first deals with the charge, the second with the stain.

## The scope

*Of **every wrong**.*

The Greek is **apo pasēs adikias** — from all unrighteousness.

**Adikia** is the negative of **dikaios**. He is **dikaios**, and He cleanses from **adikia**.

The two words are the same root, one negated. John has built the sentence so the cleansing matches
the character of the one doing it.

## What it stands against

*If we say **we have no sin in us**, we are lying to ourselves.* See the note on 1 John 1:8.

And *if we say we have not sinned, we make Him a liar*.

Verse 9 sits between two denials. It is the alternative to both.

## What the word is not doing

The verse is not a maintenance procedure for keeping a forgiveness that lapses.

Two chapters on, John's word to the church is in the perfect tense: *your sins **are forgiven** you*
— **apheōntai**, standing forgiven. See the note on 1 John 2:12.

And *if anyone does sin — **we have someone speaking for us** in front of the Father*. See the note
on 1 John 2:1.

The Advocate is stated as a present possession, before any confession is mentioned.

## The one who agreed

*"God, be merciful to me a sinner."*

The Greek is **hilasthēti** — the verb of the same root as **hilasmos**. See the note on
Luke 18:9–14.

*I tell you, this man went down to his house justified.*

> **The adjective.** *Faithful and just.* Not *merciful and lenient* — the verse says the forgiving
> is the righteous thing to do, because the account was settled elsewhere.
