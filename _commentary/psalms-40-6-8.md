David says God did not want the sacrifices, in the middle of a system built on
them.

## The four

*Sacrifice and offering You did not desire… **Burnt offering and sin offering You did
not require**.*

Four of the five Levitical offerings named and set aside.

Not that they were wrong — they were commanded in the same law David lived under. The
psalm is saying they were never the thing wanted.

Samuel had said it to Saul: *to obey is better than sacrifice.* Hosea: *I desire
mercy and not sacrifice.* Micah, Isaiah and Jeremiah all say versions of it.

## The line in between

*My ears You have opened.*

The Hebrew is **karah** — to dig, to bore through. *Ears You have dug for me.*

It is not the word for opening a door. It is the word for making a hole.

And the law it points back to is Exodus 21: a servant who could go free, saying *I
love my master… I do not want to go free*, brought to the doorpost, and his ear
pierced with an awl. See the note on Exodus 21:2–6.

Sacrifice was not wanted. A bored ear was.

## The answer

*Then I said, **see, I have come**.*

The Hebrew is *behold, I come* — the response of somebody who has heard an order and
is presenting himself.

An opened ear, and then an arrival.

## The scroll

*In the scroll of the book **it is written of me**.*

Whatever David meant by it, Hebrews takes it as spoken by Christ, and the writer of
Hebrews is not being subtle about it:

*Therefore, when He came into the world, He said: "Sacrifice and offering You did not
desire, but **a body You have prepared for Me**… Behold, I have come — in the volume
of the book it is written of Me — to do Your will, O God."*

Hebrews quotes the Greek Old Testament, which renders *ears You have dug* as *a body
You have prepared* — the part standing for the whole.

## The conclusion Hebrews draws

*He takes away the first that He may establish the second. By that will **we have been
sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all**.*

The four offerings God did not require, replaced by a body and a will.

## The delight

*I **delight** to do Your will, O my God. **Your law is here within my heart**.*

Not carried, not consulted. Inside.

Jeremiah's new covenant promise is the same: *I will put My law in their minds, and
write it on their hearts.*

> **Where the psalm starts.** *I waited patiently for the LORD… He brought me up out
> of a horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock.* The man
> writing about the pierced ear had been in a pit first.
