The hardest paragraph in the Abraham story, and it is written from Hagar's side.

## What has happened

Isaac has been born and weaned, and at the feast Sarah sees Ishmael *scoffing* and demands
that the boy and his mother be sent away.

*And the matter was very displeasing in Abraham's sight because of his son.*

God tells him to listen to Sarah, and adds: *of the son of the bondwoman I will also make a
nation, because he is your seed*.

## The morning

*So Abraham **rose early in the morning**.*

The same phrase used when he sets out for Moriah with Isaac a chapter later.

Twice in two chapters Abraham gets up early to do something to a son.

## The provisions

*He took **bread and a skin of water**, set them **on Hagar's shoulders**, gave her the boy,
and **sent her away**.*

That is the whole outfit. Bread and one waterskin, for a woman and a teenager going into a
desert.

Abraham is by this point an extremely wealthy man with hundreds of servants and herds.

## Where she goes

*She wandered off into the **wilderness of Beersheba**.*

The Hebrew is *she departed and wandered*.

This is her second time out here. Years earlier she had run from Sarah's harshness while
pregnant, and an angel found her at a spring — and she named God there: *You-Are-the-God-Who-
Sees*.

She is the first person in Scripture to give God a name.

## When the water runs out

*When the water was gone, she **laid the child under one of the bushes**.*

The Hebrew is *she cast the child*. Not gently.

He is old enough to be about sixteen — Ishmael was thirteen at circumcision and Isaac has been
born and weaned since. The word for *child* here is used across a wide range of ages.

## The distance

*Then **walked a little distance away — about a bowshot** — and sat down.*

A hundred yards or so. Far enough not to hear, close enough to see.

The measurement is given because she paced it. Of all the things in the account, the narrator
records how far away she chose to sit.

Ishmael grows up to be *an archer*. The unit of distance in the sentence is the one his life
gets measured in.

## The sentence

*"**I cannot watch him die**," she whispered.*

The Hebrew is *let me not see the death of the child*.

## The weeping

*And she sat there and **wept aloud**.*

The Hebrew is *she lifted her voice and wept*.

## What happens next

*And **God heard the voice of the lad**.*

Not hers. His.

The verse she is not told about: *what ails you, Hagar? Fear not, for **God has heard the
voice of the lad where he is**.*

**Ishmael** means *God hears*. The name Hagar was told to give him thirteen years earlier,
before he was born.

She is sitting a bowshot away crying, and the boy under the bush is the one being heard, and
his name has said so all along.

## What she is shown

*Then **God opened her eyes**, and she saw a well of water.*

Not created. Opened her eyes.

It was there.

## What became of him

*So God was with the lad; and he grew and dwelt in the wilderness, and became an archer.*

Twelve sons, twelve princes, and he is at Abraham's funeral: *and his sons Isaac and Ishmael
buried him in the cave of Machpelah*.

The two brothers stand at the same grave.

> **What the narrator records.** How far she sat. A bowshot — close enough to see and too far
> to hear, which was the point of choosing it.
