He arrives leaping over hills and says one thing twice.

## What comes before

*The voice of my beloved! Behold, **he comes leaping upon the mountains, skipping upon the
hills**.*

*He stands behind our wall; he is looking through the windows, gazing through the lattice.*

He is outside the house, at the window, before he speaks.

## The call

*My beloved spoke and said to me, **Rise up, my love, my beautiful one, and come away**.*

The Hebrew is **qumi lak ra'yati yaphati ulechi-lak**.

Two imperatives — arise, and go — with the reflexive **lak** attached to both.

## The repetition

He says it again at the end of the paragraph, in exactly the same words.

The four verses between are the reason.

## The first reason

*For look, **the winter is over, the rain is gone**.*

The Hebrew is **hasetav avar hagggeshem chalaph** — the winter has passed, the rain has gone.

In that climate the rains run from October to March and stop. There is no gradual spring. The
change is sudden and everybody notices it.

## The flowers

*The flowers appear on the earth.*

The Hebrew is **hannitzanim** — buds, blossoms. The word occurs once in the Bible, here.

## The singing

*The **time of singing** has come.*

The Hebrew is **et hazzamir**.

**Zamir** can mean singing, or pruning. Both fit the season, and the ambiguity is probably
deliberate.

**Et** — the appointed time. The word Ecclesiastes uses twenty-nine times in chapter 3. See the
note on Ecclesiastes 3:9–15.

## The dove

*And **the voice of the dove is heard in our land**.*

The turtledove is migratory. Jeremiah uses it as the standard of reliable timing: *the turtledove
and the swift and the swallow observe the time of their coming*.

Its call is the audible sign that the season has turned.

## The figs

*The fig tree **ripens its early fruit**.*

The Hebrew is **chanetah pagggeha** — puts forth her green figs.

**Pag** — the unripe early fig, which appears before the leaves.

The fruit that Jesus looked for on a tree that had leaves and nothing else. See the note on
Mark 11:12–14.

## The vines

*The **vines in blossom give their fragrance**.*

The Hebrew is **semadar** — the vine in flower.

Grape blossom is small and unremarkable to look at and carries a scent across a whole hillside.

## The repeat

*Rise up, my love, my beautiful one, and come away.*

The same line, unchanged.

The invitation is the whole speech. Everything between the two statements is the evidence that
the season permits it.

## What she does not do

*O my dove, in the clefts of the rock, in the secret places of the cliff, **let me see your face,
let me hear your voice**.*

He asks again, which suggests she has not come out.

And then: *catch us the foxes, the little foxes that spoil the vines*.

## What she says

*My beloved is mine, and I am his.*

*Until the day breaks and the shadows flee away, **turn, my beloved**.*

## The other winters

*He has made everything beautiful **in its time**.* See the note on Ecclesiastes 3:9–15.

*To everything there is a season.*

> **The structure.** One sentence, said twice, with four verses of evidence in between — and every
> item of evidence is something you could walk outside and check.
