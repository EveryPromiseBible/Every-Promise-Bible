The end of the passage Peter quotes to explain what has just happened.

## What is being explained

A hundred and twenty people speaking languages they had not learned, and a crowd from fifteen
regions hearing their own dialects. See the note on Acts 2:9–11.

And the accusation: *they are full of new wine*.

Peter's answer: *these are not drunk, as you suppose, since it is only the third hour of the
day*. Nine in the morning.

*But this is what was spoken by the prophet Joel.*

## What he has already quoted

*"I will pour out of My Spirit on all flesh; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy…
and on My menservants and on My maidservants I will pour out My Spirit in those days."* See
the note on Acts 2:17–18.

## The signs

*"I will give **wonders in the sky above and signs on the earth below** — **blood and fire and
rising smoke**."*

Joel's language. Peter quotes the whole paragraph rather than stopping at the part that has
obviously happened.

## The lights

*"**The sun will be turned to darkness and the moon to blood**."*

None of this happened on the day of Pentecost.

Peter quotes it anyway. He treats the whole passage as one movement that has now begun,
without claiming every clause has already occurred.

Joel's own phrase for it is *the day of the LORD*, and Peter keeps that too: *before that
great and shining day of the Lord comes*.

## Where the sun and moon go

Isaiah has it: *the moon will be ashamed and the sun will be put to shame, for the LORD of
hosts will reign on Mount Zion*. See the note on Isaiah 24:21–23.

And Jesus in the Olivet discourse: *immediately after the tribulation of those days the sun
will be darkened, and the moon will not give its light*.

## The last line

*"And it will be that **everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved**."*

The Greek is **pas hos an epikalesetai** — everyone, whoever.

The quotation stops there. That is where Peter ends it and starts preaching.

## What is remarkable about the stopping point

Joel's next clause is *for in Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there shall be deliverance, as the
LORD has said, among the remnant whom the LORD calls*.

Peter ends on *everyone*.

## Who quotes it next

Paul, in Romans, at the end of the argument about Jew and Greek:

*For there is no distinction between Jew and Greek, for the same Lord over all is rich to all
who call upon Him. For **whoever calls on the name of the LORD shall be saved**.* See the note
on Romans 10:9–13.

The same line, used to make the same point about the word *whoever*.

## The name in Joel

In the Hebrew the name is **YHWH**. *Everyone who calls on the name of the LORD.*

Peter applies it, in the same sermon, to Jesus: *God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified,
both Lord and Christ*.

## What the crowd asks

*Now when they heard this, they were **cut to the heart**, and said to Peter and the rest of
the apostles, "**Men and brethren, what shall we do?**"*

And the answer, and three thousand added that day. See the note on Acts 2:40–41.

> **Where Peter stops the quotation.** One clause before Joel narrows it to a remnant in
> Jerusalem. He ends on *everyone who calls*.
