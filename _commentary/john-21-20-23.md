Peter has just been restored — three questions, three answers, and a commission —
and then he immediately looks over his shoulder.

## The question he asks

*Lord, and what about this man?*

Jesus has just told Peter how he will die. Peter's response is to point at
somebody else and ask what their arrangement is.

It is such an ordinary reaction that John, who was the man being pointed at, has
no interest in softening it.

## The answer

*If I want him to remain until I come, what is that to you? You follow Me.*

The last two words are emphatic in Greek — *you*, as opposed to him. Jesus does
not tell Peter what will happen to John, and He does not rebuke the curiosity at
length. He redirects it in four words.

The instruction Peter is given is the same one he was given at the beginning, on
the same shore, three years earlier.

## The correction John adds

*So the word went around that this disciple would not die. But Jesus had not said
that.*

John is writing decades later, presumably as the last of them alive, and he stops
to correct a rumour that had grown up around a conditional sentence. *If I want*
had become *he will*.

It is a small, careful act of honesty, and it tells you something about the
writer: given the chance to leave a flattering story standing, he prints the exact
words instead.

> **Where it leaves the reader.** Each of them gets a different road — one
> martyred young, one apparently growing old — and neither is told the other's.
> The only instruction that fits both is the one Jesus gives: *you follow Me.*
