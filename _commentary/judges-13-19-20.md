They try to feed a visitor and end up watching him leave in the fire.

## What has happened

The angel appeared to Manoah's wife with the announcement about Samson. Manoah prayed for a second
visit, and *God listened to the voice of Manoah*.

The angel came back — *and the angel of God came to the woman again as she was sitting in the
field*. See the note on Judges 13:2–5.

She has to run and fetch her husband.

## What Manoah offers

*"Please let us detain you, and **we will prepare a young goat for you**."*

Hospitality. The same offer Abraham made at Mamre and Gideon made at the oak.

## The refusal

*"Though you detain me, I will not eat your food. But **if you offer a burnt offering, you must offer
it to the LORD**."*

The Hebrew is **laYHWH ta'alennah** — to the LORD you shall offer it.

The angel redirects the meal into a sacrifice, and names the recipient.

## The question

*"What is Your name, that when Your words come to pass we may honour You?"*

*"**Why do you ask My name, seeing it is wonderful?**"*

The Hebrew is **peli** — wonderful, incomprehensible.

The same root as **pele** in Isaiah 9: *His name shall be called Wonderful*. See the note on
Isaiah 9:6–7.

## The offering

*So Manoah took the young goat and a grain offering and **laid them on a rock**.*

The Hebrew is **al-hatzur** — on the rock.

No altar had been built. A stone in a field.

## The title

*An offering to the LORD — **the One who does wondrous things**.*

The Hebrew is **umaphli la'asot** — and doing wondrously.

The same root again — **pele**. The narrator picks up the angel's own word about His name.

## What they do

*And Manoah and his wife **stood watching**.*

The Hebrew is **umanoach ve'ishto ro'im**.

## What happens

*As the flame rose up from the altar toward heaven, **the angel of the LORD rose up in the flame
itself**.*

The Hebrew is **vayya'al malakh-YHWH belahav hammizbeach** — the angel went up in the flame of the
altar.

He does not depart and then the fire rises. He goes up in it.

## What that suggests

The one who received the offering went up with it.

The narrative does not explain the image. It records what the couple saw.

## Their response

*When they saw it, **they fell facedown to the ground**.*

## Who this is

*The angel of the LORD* — **malakh YHWH**.

The figure appears through the Old Testament and speaks as God in the first person: at the bush,
to Hagar, to Abraham on the mountain, to Gideon.

Manoah's conclusion two verses later is *we have seen God*.

## What follows

*The angel did not appear to them again. Only then did Manoah understand.*

*"**We are going to die**," he said to his wife, terrified. "**We have seen God!**"*

And his wife's reply, which is the most level-headed sentence in the chapter. See the note on
Judges 13:21–23.

## What the son turns out to be

*So the woman bore a son and called his name Samson; and the child grew, and the LORD blessed
him. **And the Spirit of the LORD began to move upon him**.*

The last verse of the chapter.

> **The image.** The angel goes up *in* the flame of the offering — not away first and then the
> fire. The narrator records it and does not explain it.
