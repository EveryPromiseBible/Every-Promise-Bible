The first resurrection in Scripture, and the mother's first words are an accusation.

## What has already happened

Famine. Elijah at the brook Cherith, fed by ravens.

Then Zarephath, and a widow gathering two sticks to bake a last meal for herself and her son
before they die.

*"The bin of flour shall not be used up, nor shall the jar of oil run dry."* See the note on
1 Kings 17:8–16.

She had fed him for many days out of a jar that would not empty.

## The illness

*Some time later the woman's son fell sick. He **grew worse and worse, until at last he stopped
breathing**.*

The Hebrew is **ad asher lo-notrah-bo neshamah** — until there was no breath left in him.

**Neshamah** — the breath God put in the man at the beginning. See the note on
Psalms 150:6.

## Her accusation

*She turned on Elijah: "**What have I done to you, man of God? Did you come here to bring my sin to
light and take my son?**"*

The Hebrew is **lehazkir et-avoni** — to bring my iniquity to remembrance.

She assumes the miracle brought scrutiny with it, and the scrutiny found something.

## What she is not

Israelite. A Sidonian widow in Baal country, whose theology is what she had.

Elijah does not correct it.

## The three words

*"**Give him to me**," Elijah said.*

The Hebrew is **teni-li et-benech**.

No argument, no defence, no explanation.

## What he does

*He **took the boy from her arms, carried him up to the room where he was staying**, and laid him on
**his own bed**.*

The Hebrew is **el-ha'aliyyah** — to the upper room.

The room she had given the prophet. He takes the child to the one place in the house that is his.

## The prayer

*"O LORD my God, **have You brought tragedy even to this widow I'm staying with**, by letting her son
die?"*

He does not defend God to her and he does not defend Him to Himself. The complaint goes up.

## The stretching

*He **stretched himself out over the boy three times**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyitmoded** — he measured himself out upon him.

A rare verb. The prophet lays his own body along the child's, matching himself to it.

## The second prayer

*"O LORD my God, **let this boy's life return to him**."*

The Hebrew is **tashov-na nephesh-hayyeled al-qirbo** — let the soul return into him.

## The answer

*And **the LORD heard Elijah**. The boy's life came back into him, and he lived.*

The Hebrew is **vayyishma YHWH beqol Eliyyahu** — the LORD listened to the voice of Elijah.

## The return

*Elijah picked him up, **carried him down** from the room, and gave him to his mother. "**Look**," he
said, "**your son is alive**."*

Up the stairs dead, down the stairs alive.

## What she says

*"**Now I know for certain that you are a man of God, and that the word of the LORD in your mouth is
truth**."*

She had called him *man of God* while accusing him. Now she says it as a conclusion.

## What Jesus does with her

*"But to none of them was Elijah sent except to **Zarephath, in the region of Sidon, to a woman who
was a widow**."*

Said in the Nazareth synagogue, and the congregation tried to throw Him off a cliff. See the note
on Luke 4:25–27.

## The other upper rooms

Elisha and the Shunammite's son, on the prophet's own bed in the room she built. See the note on
2 Kings 4:32–37.

Peter and Dorcas, in the upper room at Joppa. See the note on Acts 9:36–43.

Paul and Eutychus, at Troas, from a third-floor window. See the note on
Acts 20:7–12.

Four resurrections, all in upper rooms.

> **The three words.** *Give him to me.* No defence of himself, no correction of her theology —
> and he carries the argument upstairs and makes it to God instead.
