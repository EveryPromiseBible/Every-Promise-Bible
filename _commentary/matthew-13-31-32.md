title: "The smallest of seeds"

Two verses, and the disproportion is the whole parable.

## The seed

*"The kingdom of heaven is like **a mustard seed** that a man took and planted in his field. **It is
the smallest of all the seeds**."*

The Greek is **mikroteron** — smaller. It was proverbial: rabbinic writings use *as small as a
mustard seed* the way English uses *a drop in the ocean*.

## What it becomes

*"And yet when it has grown it is **the largest of the garden plants** — it becomes **a tree**, and
**the birds of the air come and nest in its branches**."*

Black mustard in that region reaches nine or ten feet, which for an annual herb is absurd.

## Where the tree and the birds come from

*"On the mountain height of Israel I will plant it; and it will bring forth boughs, and bear fruit,
and be a majestic cedar. **Under it will dwell birds of every sort**; in the shadow of its branches
they will dwell."*

Ezekiel 17, of a tender sprig taken from the top of a cedar and planted by God.

*"Its leaves were lovely, its fruit abundant… **the birds of the heavens dwelt in its branches**."*

Daniel 4, of Nebuchadnezzar's tree — the one that was cut down with the stump left in the ground.
See the note on Daniel 4:24–25.

Both images are kingdoms, and in both the birds are the nations sheltering in them.

## The disproportion

*"For **who has despised the day of small things**?"*

Zechariah, to men laying a foundation that the old men wept over because it was so much smaller
than Solomon's.

*But **God has chosen the foolish things of the world** to put to shame the wise, and God has chosen
the weak things of the world to put to shame the things which are mighty… **that no flesh should
glory in His presence**.*

*"But you, **Bethlehem Ephrathah, small among the clans of Judah**, too little to be counted."* See
the note on Micah 5:2.

## The other mustard seed

*"For assuredly, I say to you, **if you have faith as a mustard seed**, you will say to this
mountain, 'Move from here to there,' and it will move."*

**Matthew** 17, and the point is the same one twice: the size of the thing is not the measure of
what it does.

*"**Unless a grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it
produces much grain.**"*

**John** 12, said days before the crucifixion, and it is the smallest-to-largest argument applied
to Himself.

## What is planted, and by whom

*"A man took and planted it **in his field**."*

The parable before this one has an enemy sowing weeds in a field, and **Jesus** explains that one:
*He who sows the good seed is the Son of Man. The field is the world.*

> **The two ends of the sentence.** The smallest of all the seeds, and a tree with the birds of the
> air in it — which is Ezekiel's and Daniel's picture of an empire. The parable is one sentence
> long and it covers that whole distance.
