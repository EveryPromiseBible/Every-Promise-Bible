Matthew stops the narrative to say that this is the thing that was promised.

## The formula

*All of this happened **so that what the Lord had spoken through the prophet would come true**.*

The Greek is **hina plerothe** — that it might be fulfilled.

Matthew uses this construction more than a dozen times. It is his running argument: the story he
is telling is the one already written down.

## Through the prophet

The Greek is **dia tou prophetou** — *through* the prophet, and **hupo Kuriou** — *by* the Lord.

The distinction is deliberate. The Lord spoke; the prophet was the channel.

## The quotation

*"**The virgin will be with child and will give birth to a son, and they will call His name
Immanuel**."*

Isaiah 7:14, given to King Ahaz when Jerusalem was under threat from a northern alliance.

## The word

The Hebrew is **almah** — a young woman of marriageable age.

The Septuagint, translated by Jewish scholars two centuries before Christ, rendered it
**parthenos** — virgin. Matthew quotes their reading.

## What the sign was for

Ahaz was offered a sign *in the depth or in the height above*, and refused it: *I will not ask,
nor will I test the LORD*.

*Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign.* The sign is given to a king who declined one.

## The name

**Immanu-El** — with us, God.

Two Hebrew words. **Immanu** — with us. **El** — God.

The word order in Hebrew puts *with us* first.

## Matthew's translation

*That name means "**God with us**."*

He translates it, which tells you his readers included people who did not read Hebrew.

## The two names

Verse 21: *you shall call His name Jesus, for He will save His people from their sins*. See the
note on Matthew 1:20–21.

Verse 23: *they shall call His name Immanuel*.

One is what He does. The other is who He is. Matthew puts them three verses apart and does not
reconcile them.

## Who calls Him what

*You* shall call Him Jesus — Joseph, individually.

*They* shall call Him Immanuel — a plural, unspecified.

Nobody in the Gospels ever addresses Him as Immanuel. The name is a description, not a form of
address.

## Where Matthew ends

*"Lo, **I am with you always**, even to the end of the age."* See the note on
Matthew 28:16–20.

The Greek is **ego meth' humon eimi** — I am with you.

The Gospel opens with *God with us* and closes with *I am with you*, and the whole book is
between them.

## What Isaiah says three verses after the sign

*Take counsel together, but it will come to nothing; speak the word, but it will not stand, **for
God is with us**.*

The Hebrew there is **immanu El** — the same two words, used as a sentence rather than a name.

## Where the tent ends up

*And I heard a loud voice from heaven saying, "Behold, **the tabernacle of God is with men, and He
will dwell with them**."* See the note on Revelation 21:3–4.

> **The two names.** *Jesus* — what He will do. *Immanuel* — who He is. Matthew states both in
> four verses and lets them stand side by side.
