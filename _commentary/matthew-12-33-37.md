Jesus explains where words come from, and it is not the mouth.

## The tree

*"**Make the tree good and its fruit will be good**… because **a tree is known by
its fruit**."*

You do not fix fruit. You cannot improve an apple by working on the apple.

The fruit is diagnostic — it tells you what the tree is — and the remedy in the
sentence is applied to the tree.

## The mechanism

*"**The mouth speaks out of whatever fills the heart.**"*

The Greek is *out of the abundance of the heart* — out of the overflow, what is in
excess.

Speech is described as spillage. What comes out under pressure, without editing, is
simply the level of what was already in there.

## The storehouse

*"A good person brings good things out of **the good stored up inside**, and an evil
person brings evil things out of **the evil stored up inside**."*

The Greek is **thesauros** — a treasury, a storehouse, the word behind *treasure*.

Both people are described as bringing out. Nobody manufactures on the spot; they
withdraw from a store, and the store was filled earlier.

## Which is why guarding it matters

*Above all else, guard your heart, for from it flow the springs of life.* Proverbs
puts the heart first and everything else downstream — mouth, eyes, feet, in that
order. See the note on Proverbs 4:20–27.

Solomon's word for *guard* is a sentry's word. It assumes something is trying to
get in.

## "Every careless word"

*"On the day of judgment people will give an account for **every careless word**
they have spoken."*

The Greek is **argos** — idle, inactive, unemployed. Not wicked words. Words that
were doing no work.

He does not say they carry no weight. He says they will be accounted for.

## The last line

*"**By your words you will be declared right, and by your words you will be
condemned.**"*

The verb in the first half is the justification word Paul uses all through Romans.

Words are treated here not as a report on the heart but as evidence in a hearing.

## Who He is saying it to

The Pharisees, who had just said He cast out demons by Beelzebub. He calls them a
*brood of snakes* in the middle of this passage.

They had watched a blind and mute man healed and produced that sentence about it —
which is exactly his point. Their words came out of a store, and the store is what
he is naming.

> not able*, and two looked at the same country and said *we are well able*. Same
> forty days, same giants. And God's answer to the first group was *I will do to you
> the very things you spoke in My hearing.* See the notes on Numbers 13:26–33 and
