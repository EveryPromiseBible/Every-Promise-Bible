Paul is organising a collection for the poor in Jerusalem, and he refuses to
apply any pressure to it.

## The farming image

*The one who **sows sparingly** will reap sparingly too, and the one who **sows
generously** will reap generously too.*

The Greek behind *generously* is *with blessings* — literally, he who sows upon
blessings.

Paul reaches for agriculture rather than commerce. A farmer's seed is not spent; it
is placed. And nobody who has ever planted expects the harvest to match what went
into the ground.

## The freedom clause

*Let each one give **as he has settled it in his own heart**.*

The Greek is to purpose, to decide beforehand. It is his own decision, made
privately, in advance.

No amount is named anywhere in these two chapters. Paul never sets a figure.

## The two things ruled out

*Not with **a grudge** and not **because he is made to**.*

**Grudgingly** — the Greek is *out of grief*, with regret.

**Of necessity** — under compulsion, because someone applied pressure.

Both are excluded, and between them they cover most of what motivates religious
giving.

## What is wanted instead

*For it is a **cheerful giver** that God loves.*

The Greek is **hilaros** — the root of the English *hilarious*. Cheerful, merry.

The verse is quoting Proverbs 22:8 in the Greek Old Testament, and it is the
disposition rather than the sum that is named as loved.

## How Paul ran the collection

Read the two chapters and watch what he does not do.

He does not command. *I speak not by commandment* — chapter 8, in so many words.

He tells them the Macedonians gave *beyond their ability* out of *deep poverty*,
and that they *begged us urgently* for the privilege — and then says he is only
telling them this *to test the sincerity of your love*.

And: *if there is first a willing mind, it is accepted according to what one has,
and not according to what he does not have.*

He puts the whole thing on willingness.

## The reason he gives

*"For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that **though He was rich, yet
for your sakes He became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich."*

That is chapter 8, verse 9, and it is the only leverage Paul uses in the entire
appeal. See the note on 2 Corinthians 8:9.

## What he promises

Further down the same chapter: *God is able to make all grace abound toward you,
that you, always having all sufficiency in all things, may have an abundance for
every good work.*

Sufficiency for yourself and abundance for the work — and the Greek for
*sufficiency* is **autarkeia**, contentment, having enough.

> **What the money was for.** Not a building or a programme. A famine relief fund,
> carried by hand from Greek churches to Jewish believers in Jerusalem — people most
> of the donors would never meet.
