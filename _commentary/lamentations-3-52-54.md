Three lines of autobiography, and every detail in them turns up again.

## Hated for nothing

*Those who **hate me for no reason** hunted me down like a bird.*

The Hebrew is **oyevai chinnam** — my enemies without cause.

*"But this happened that the word might be fulfilled which is written in their law, '**They hated
Me without a cause**.'"*

**John** 15, and **Jesus** is quoting Psalm 69 — the same phrase, **chinnam**, and the same psalm
**John** goes back to at the crucifixion for the vinegar and the sponge.

## The pit

*They **threw me alive into the pit**.*

Jeremiah was let down by ropes into a cistern in the court of the guard, *and in the dungeon there
was no water, but mire. So Jeremiah sank in the mire.*

An Ethiopian palace official named Ebed-Melech went to the king, got permission, and pulled him
out with old rags under his armpits so the ropes would not cut him.

## The stone

*And **rolled a stone over me**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyaddu-even bi**.

*And he laid it in his new tomb which he had hewn out of the rock; and he **rolled a large stone
against the door of the tomb** and departed.*

*And they said among themselves, "**Who will roll away the stone** from the door of the tomb for
us?" But when they looked up, they saw that **the stone had been rolled away**.*

**Matthew** and **Mark**. A pit, a stone rolled over the top, and a man inside — and in the second
telling somebody else moves the stone from the outside.

## Cut off

*The waters closed over my head; I said, **I am cut off**.*

The Hebrew is **nigzarti** — I am cut off, severed. It is a passive: something was done to him.

*For **He was cut off from the land of the living**; for the transgressions of My people He was
stricken.*

Isaiah 53:8, and the Hebrew there is **nigzar** — the same verb, in the same voice.

Lamentations has a man say it about himself and turn out to be wrong. Isaiah has it said about
somebody else and it is exact.

## The waters

*The waters closed over my head.*

*"Save me, O God! For the waters have come up to my neck… I have come into deep waters, where the
floods overflow me."*

Psalm 69 again. **John** quotes verse 9 of it about the temple and verse 21 about the vinegar, and
between them sits this.

## What comes next in the poem

*I called on Your name, O LORD, from the deepest pit. You heard my voice.* See the note on
Lamentations 3:55–57.

> **The three details.** Thrown alive into a pit, a stone rolled over the top, and *I am cut off*
> — **nigzar**, which is the verb Isaiah 53 uses. All three are said by a man who was pulled out
> of a cistern with rags under his arms.
