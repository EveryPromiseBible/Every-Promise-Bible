A garden planted for one man, and the text gives the map.

## The planting

*The LORD God **planted a garden in the east, in a place called Eden**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyitta** — He planted. A gardener's verb.

**Eden** — delight, pleasure. The garden is *in* Eden, not identical with it.

## The placing

*And there **He placed the man He had formed**.*

The man was made outside it and put in.

The Hebrew is **vayyasem** — He set, He put. And the verb in verse 15 is **vayyannichehu** — He
caused him to rest there.

## The trees

*He made every kind of tree grow up from the ground — trees **lovely to look at and good to eat
from**.*

Beauty first, then food. The Hebrew is **nechmad lemareh vetov lema'achal**.

The order is the order of the temptation: *the woman saw that the tree was **good for food**, that
it was **pleasant to the eyes***.

## The two in the middle

*And in the very middle of the garden stood **two trees: the tree of life, and the tree of the
knowledge of good and evil**.*

Both in the middle. The forbidden one is not fenced off in a corner.

The permission comes first: *of every tree of the garden you may freely eat*. The prohibition is
one tree. See the note on Genesis 2:15–17.

## The river

*A river flowed out of Eden to water the garden, and from there **it split into four streams**.*

The Hebrew is **ve'nahar yotze me'eden** — a river going out from Eden.

It rises in Eden and waters the garden, and divides after leaving.

## The first

*The **Pishon**, winding through the land of **Havilah**, where there is gold — and **the gold of
that land is good**, along with **bdellium and onyx stone**.*

The narrator stops the geography to grade the gold.

**Bdolach** — bdellium, an aromatic gum resin, pale and translucent. It is the substance manna is
compared to: *its colour was like the colour of bdellium*.

**Shoham** — onyx, or possibly carnelian. The stone set in the high priest's shoulder-pieces with
the names of the tribes engraved on it, and in the breastplate.

## Why the minerals are listed

Genesis 2 spends three verses on the produce of the ground outside the garden — gold, resin,
stone.

They are the materials of the tabernacle and the priest's dress, named on the first pages of the
Bible, before there is anything to build.

## The rest

*The **Gihon**, flowing through the land of **Cush**. The third was the **Tigris**, running east of
Assyria. And the fourth was the **Euphrates**.*

Two of the four are still on the map. Two are not identifiable.

**Gihon** is also the name of the spring at Jerusalem where Solomon was anointed king.

## What the man is given to do

*Then the LORD God took the man and put him in the garden of Eden **to tend and keep it**.*

The Hebrew is **le'ovdah uleshomrah** — to serve it and to guard it.

Both verbs are used later of the Levites' work in the tabernacle.

Work exists before the fall. What the curse adds is thorns and sweat.

## What is lost

*So He drove out the man; and He placed cherubim at the east of the garden of Eden, and a flaming
sword which turned every way, **to guard the way to the tree of life**.* See the note on
Genesis 3:22–24.

The same verb — **lishmor**, to guard. The man was put there to guard it; afterwards something
else guards it against him.

## Where the river turns up again

*And he showed me **a pure river of water of life**, clear as crystal, proceeding from the throne of
God and of the Lamb.*

*In the middle of its street, and on either side of the river, was **the tree of life**.* See the
note on Revelation 22:1–2.

A river, and the tree, and no sword.

> **The detour.** Three verses of the map stop to say that the gold of that land is good — the
> materials of the tabernacle and the priest's stones, listed before there is a priesthood.
