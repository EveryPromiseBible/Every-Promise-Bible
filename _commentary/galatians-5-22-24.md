Nine words, and the noun in front of them is singular.

## Fruit, not fruits

*The **fruit** the Spirit grows is…*

One fruit with nine characteristics, not nine items to be collected separately.

And set it against the phrase Paul used eight verses earlier: *the **works** of the
flesh are evident.* Works, plural, and manufactured. Fruit, singular, and grown.

Nobody grows fruit. A tree does, and what the farmer does is stay attached to the
soil and the season.

## Whose fruit it is

*The fruit **the Spirit** grows.*

The genitive is His. It is not the believer's fruit produced with the Spirit's
assistance.

Jesus said the same in the vine passage: *apart from Me you can do nothing*, and the
branch's whole job is to remain. See the note on John 15:5–8.

## The nine

**Love, joy, peace** — Godward, and the first three.

**Patience, kindness, goodness** — toward other people.

**Faithfulness, gentleness, self-control** — the inward three, and it is worth
noticing where self-control lands.

It is last, and it is fruit. Not the root of the list but the ninth thing grown out
of it. The one quality most people try to start with is the one Paul puts at the
end.

## The line that follows

*There is **no law standing against things like these**.*

Paul has spent five chapters arguing against putting believers under law, and here
is his answer to the obvious objection.

Nothing on that list needs legislating. No statute was ever passed against joy.
Produce this and the law has no business with you — not because it was lowered, but
because it has been outgrown.

He says it another way in Romans: *love is the fulfilment of the law.*

## What Paul says about the flesh

*Those who belong to Christ **have crucified** the flesh with its passions and its
cravings.*

Perfect tense — done, and standing done. Not *should crucify*, and not *are
crucifying daily*.

Paul reports it as an accomplished fact about everyone who belongs to Christ, the
same way he reports it in Romans 6: *our old man was crucified with Him.*

## The instruction in the passage

There is only one, and it is four verses earlier: *walk in the Spirit, and you
shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh.*

A promise attached to a direction of travel. Nothing in the paragraph tells anybody
to work on the list.

> **What Paul feared for them.** *You have become estranged from Christ, you who
> attempt to be justified by law; you have fallen from grace.* That is the letter's
> charge — not immorality. The Galatians' problem was that they had started trying,
> and Paul's remedy is a fruit that grows.
