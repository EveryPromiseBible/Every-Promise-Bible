Paul tells them what they are, and the sentence is in the indicative rather than the
imperative.

## The statement

*But **you are not living out of the old nature; you are living in the Spirit**.*

Not *you should*. He is describing.

The Greek is *you are not in the flesh but in the Spirit* — a statement of location, the
same construction as *in Christ Jesus* at the top of the chapter. See the note on
Romans 8:1–4.

## The qualifier

*If indeed **the Spirit of God lives in you**.*

The Greek **eiper** can be read *if indeed* or *since indeed*. Paul is not raising a
doubt; he is naming the ground of what he has just said.

## The definition

*And if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, **that person is not His**.*

The plainest test in the letter, and it is not about behaviour, background, or
observance.

Paul has spent seven chapters dismantling the alternatives.

## The three names

In two verses the same Spirit is called **the Spirit of God**, **the Spirit of Christ**,
and then simply **the Spirit**.

Paul moves between them without explanation.

## The body and the Spirit

*But if Christ is in you, then **the body may be dead because of sin, but the Spirit is
life because of righteousness**.*

Two things held together. The body is not exempted from the situation, and the Spirit is
called life.

The Greek is that the Spirit **is** life — not gives it, in this clause.

## The long title

*And if **the Spirit of Him who raised Jesus from the dead** lives in you.*

Paul could have written *the Spirit*. He spells out the resurrection, because the whole
next clause depends on it.

He does the same thing in chapter 4 about Abraham: *God, who **gives life to the dead**
and calls those things which do not exist as though they did.*

## The promise

*Then **the One who raised Christ from the dead will bring life to your mortal bodies as
well** by His Spirit who lives in you.*

**Mortal bodies** — the Greek is **thneta**, subject to death.

The same power, applied to the same kind of object. Paul's argument is that the Spirit
already living in a person is the link between the two events.

## What Paul says elsewhere in the same terms

*Who will **transform** our lowly body that it may be conformed to His glorious body,
according to the working by which He is able even to subdue all things to Himself.* See
the note on Philippians 3:20–21.

## Where the chapter goes next

*Therefore, brethren, **we are debtors — not to the flesh**, to live according to the
flesh.*

And then the sonship: *you received the Spirit of adoption by whom we cry out, "Abba,
Father." The Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God.*

> **What the passage settles.** Where a person is, rather than how they are doing. Paul
> states the location first and the consequences after — including one about a body he
> has just called mortal.
