The warning is not that the confidence will be taken. It is that it can be thrown out.

## The verb

*So **do not fling away your confidence**.*

The Greek is **mē apobalēte** — do not cast off, do not throw away.

An active verb. Nobody removes it; the owner discards it.

## The noun

**Parrēsia** — freedom of speech, the right of a citizen to speak openly in the assembly.

The letter's word for the access we now have: *having **boldness** to enter the Holiest by the blood
of Jesus*. See the note on Hebrews 10:19–21.

## What is at stake

Not salvation, in the noun's usual sense here. The freedom to come in and speak.

A man who throws that away still belongs and stops approaching.

## The attached clause

*There is **a great reward tied to it**.*

The Greek is **megalēn misthapodosian** — great recompense.

## What they had already borne

*You endured a great struggle with sufferings… you had compassion on me in my chains, and **joyfully
accepted the plundering of your goods**.* See the note on Hebrews 10:32–34.

The Greek is **meta charas prosedexasthe** — with joy you received it.

## Why they could

*Knowing that you have **a better and an enduring possession for yourselves in heaven**.*

They had already done the hard part once. The exhortation is to a group with a record, not to
beginners.

## The requirement

*What you need is **staying power**.*

The Greek is **hupomonēs gar echete chreian** — you have need of endurance.

**Hupomonē** — remaining under. Bearing weight without shifting.

## The order

*So that **when you have done what God wants, you will receive what He promised**.*

The Greek is **to thelēma tou Theou poiēsantes komisēsthe tēn epangelian**.

## What sits between the two

Time.

The doing and the receiving are both stated. What the sentence inserts between them is the need for
endurance, which is only needed if there is a gap.

## What the gap is not

Evidence that the promise failed.

## The quotation that follows

*"For yet a little while, and **He who is coming will come and will not tarry**. Now the just shall
live by faith."* See the note on Hebrews 10:37–38.

From Habakkuk, who was told to write the vision and wait: *though it tarries, wait for it*.

## The line after that

*But we are **not of those who draw back to perdition**, but of those who believe to the saving of the
soul.*

The Greek is **hupostolēs** — a shrinking back, a furling of sails.

## What comes immediately next

*Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.* See the note on
Hebrews 11:1–3.

Chapter 11 is the answer to this instruction: a list of people who did exactly this.

*These all died in faith, **not having received the promises**, but having seen them afar off.*

## The parallel earlier

*Copy the people who **trusted God, held on, and came into what He promised**.* See the note on
Hebrews 6:11–12.

The same three-part sequence, in the same letter.

> **The one who does it.** Not *do not let it be taken* — *do not throw it away*. The verb makes the
> loss an act of the person who had it.
