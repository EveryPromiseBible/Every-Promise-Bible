Haman has everything, and the writer lets him list it out loud so the last
sentence can land.

## The inventory

*He boasted to them of his **glorious riches**, his **many sons**, and **every way
the king had promoted him**… "And besides all that, **Queen Esther invited no one
but me**."*

Wealth, sons, rank, and a private banquet with the king and queen. In that world
there was nothing else to want.

He counts it in front of an audience he has assembled for the purpose — his wife
and his friends, called in to hear it.

## The sentence that undoes it

*"**Yet all of this means nothing to me** as long as I see Mordecai the Jew
sitting at the king's gate."*

The Hebrew is stronger than the English can quite carry: all of it is worth
nothing to him.

One man, sitting down, not moving. That is the entire offence.

## What Mordecai did

*Mordecai **did not rise or so much as tremble** before him.*

He did not insult Haman. He did not speak. He sat still.

The writer adds *or so much as tremble* — Haman is not even given the compliment
of fear.

## "Still, he held himself in"

Haman restrains himself in public and goes home to say it. The rage is not
managed; it is stored.

By the end of the same conversation his wife and friends have proposed the
gallows, and *the thing pleased Haman, and he had the gallows made.* Fifty cubits
— seventy-five feet, higher than anything needed for the job, built to be seen
across the city.

## Where he is standing when he says it

He has just come from Esther's first banquet, and he has been invited back
tomorrow. He thinks the invitation is an honour.

It is a trap that has already closed. The reader can see it; Haman is at home
counting his sons.

> **What the boast cost him.** He builds a gallows that night for a man he will be
> leading through the streets on a horse by tomorrow afternoon, and he will hang
> on it himself before the second banquet is over. See the notes on Esther 6:10–11
> and Esther 7:7–10.
