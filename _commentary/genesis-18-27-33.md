The longest negotiation in the Bible, and it moves in one direction.

## What started it

Three visitors at Mamre, a meal, and the promise about Sarah. Then two of them go on toward
Sodom and one stays.

*And the LORD said, "**Shall I hide from Abraham what I am doing?**"*

The disclosure is deliberate. God says why: *for I have known him, in order that he may command
his children and his household after him*.

## The opening

Abraham has already asked once: *would You also destroy the righteous with the wicked? Suppose
there were fifty righteous within the city… far be it from You!*

And the line that carries the whole argument: *"**Shall not the Judge of all the earth do
right?**"*

The answer was yes — fifty, and He would spare the whole place.

## The self-description

*"Now that I have been **so bold** to speak to the LORD, though **I am only dust and ashes**."*

The Hebrew is **aphar va'epher** — dust and ashes, an assonant pair.

He says it twice in the passage. Every step down is prefaced with an apology.

## The five

*"What if the fifty are **five short**? Will You destroy the whole city **for lack of five**?"*

He does not ask for forty-five. He asks about the gap.

## The descent

Forty-five. Forty. Thirty. Twenty. Ten.

Six requests in all, counting the first. The intervals shrink as he goes: five, five, ten,
ten, ten.

## The politeness

*"**May the LORD not be angry**, but let me speak."*

Twice. And the last one: *"May the LORD not be angry, but **let me speak just once more**."*

He is the one who calls a halt.

## The answer every time

*"I will not do it."*

Not once does God resist, negotiate upward, or express impatience. Every request is granted
immediately and in full.

Abraham stops before God does.

## Why ten

The text does not say. Ten is the traditional minimum for a Jewish congregation — a **minyan**
— though that is much later.

Abraham may have been counting Lot's household: Lot, his wife, two daughters, two sons-in-law,
and whoever else. He may have been asking whether one family would be enough.

## What happens

*And the LORD went His way as soon as He had finished speaking with Abraham; and Abraham
returned to his place.*

There were not ten.

## What happened anyway

*And it came to pass, when God destroyed the cities of the plain, that **God remembered
Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow**.*

The city was not spared. Lot was pulled out — and the angels had to take him by the hand:
*while he lingered, the men took hold of his hand… the LORD being merciful to him*.

Abraham asked for the city and got his nephew.

## What Peter says about Lot

*And delivered righteous Lot, who was oppressed by the filthy conduct of the wicked… **the
LORD knows how to deliver the godly out of temptations**.*

Peter calls him righteous three times in two verses, which nothing in Genesis prepares you
for.

## Where Abraham was standing the next morning

*And Abraham went early in the morning to the place where he had stood before the LORD. Then
he looked toward Sodom and Gomorrah… and behold, the smoke of the land which went up like the
smoke of a furnace.*

He went back to the spot.

> **Who stopped.** Abraham. Six requests, six grants, no resistance at any point — and the
> negotiation ended because the man ran out of nerve, not because God ran out of patience.
