God answers a sentence His people are saying to each other, and the answer comes
with an oath attached.

## What they were saying

*Our sins and failures weigh us down. We are wasting away because of them. How
then can we live?*

This is not defiance. It is despair — people who have concluded the account is
too far gone to be worth addressing.

Ezekiel is prophesying to exiles. The city has fallen; the worst has already
happened. Their question is whether there is any point.

## The oath

*As surely as I live, declares the Lord GOD.*

God swears by Himself, which He does rarely. Whatever follows is being underlined
as hard as language allows.

## The answer

*I take no pleasure in the death of the wicked. My joy is that the wicked turn
from their ways and live.*

Two halves, negative and positive. The Hebrew for *pleasure* is about delight or
desire — He is not describing reluctance to punish. He is denying any appetite for
it at all.

And the positive is stronger than mere permission: His *joy* is in the turning
and the living.

## The last line

*Turn back, turn from your evil ways. Why will you die, house of Israel?*

It ends on a question, and the question is almost bewildered. Given that the door
is open and He has just sworn He wants them through it — why?

> **Where it turns up again.** Peter says God *does not want anyone destroyed; He
> wants every last one to turn*, and Paul says God desires all people to be
> saved. The New Testament keeps making the same claim, and this is the oath
> underneath it. See the note on 2 Peter 3:9.
