**Jude** quotes a book that is not in the Bible, and names the man he is quoting.

## The quotation

*"**Enoch, the seventh man in line from Adam**, spoke about these very men long before they came."*

The words that follow are not in Genesis. They are in **1 Enoch**, a Jewish apocalyptic work written
in the couple of centuries before **Christ**, and **Jude**'s quotation is close to its opening
paragraph.

## What that does and does not mean

It does not make **1 Enoch** Scripture, any more than **Paul** quoting Aratus on Mars Hill — *for we
are also His offspring* — makes an astronomical poem Scripture, or quoting Epimenides in **Titus** 1
canonises a Cretan prophet.

A writer under inspiration can quote a true sentence from anywhere. **Jude** says Enoch *spoke* it;
he does not say the book is holy.

## The seventh from Adam

*"**The seventh man in line from Adam.**"*

Genesis 5 gives the line: Adam, Seth, Enosh, Cainan, Mahalalel, Jared, Enoch. Seven.

*And **Enoch walked with God; and he was not, for God took him**.*

The one man in the genealogy whose entry does not end *and he died*.

*By faith **Enoch was taken away so that he did not see death**, "and was not found, because God had
taken him"; for before he was taken he had this testimony, **that he pleased God**.*

**Hebrews** 11, and the next verse is *without faith it is impossible to please Him* — which makes
the pleasing a matter of trust rather than performance.

## What he said

*"**Look — the Lord is on His way, and thousands upon thousands of His holy ones are with Him.**"*

**Elthen kyrios en hagiais myriasin autou** — the Lord came with His holy myriads.

The verb is an aorist: *the Lord came*. Prophetic certainty stated in the past tense, the way Isaiah
53 does it.

*The LORD came from Sinai… **He came with ten thousands of saints**; from His right hand came a fiery
law for them.*

Deuteronomy 33, which is probably where **1 Enoch** got it.

*"When the Son of Man comes in His glory, **and all the holy angels with Him**, then He will sit on
the throne of His glory."*

*Behold, **He is coming with clouds**, and every eye will see Him.*

## Ungodly, four times

*"To bring **everyone** to account… for all **the ungodly** things **the ungodly** have done in their
ungodliness, and for all the hard words **ungodly** sinners have spoken against Him."*

**Asebeis, esebesan, asebeias, asebeis** — the root four times in one sentence. **Jude** is quoting a
verse that hammers one word.

## Who is not in the sentence

The people **Jude** is writing to. He has told them in verse 1 that they are *kept for Jesus Christ*,
and he tells them in verse 24 who is able to present them *faultless*. See the note on
Jude 1:24–25.

*There is therefore now **no condemnation** to those who are in **Christ Jesus**.*

> **What the quotation is doing here.** The first prophecy in Scripture's chronology is about the
> Lord's return, spoken by a man who did not die — and Jude, writing about people who slipped in
> unnoticed, reaches past all of it to the seventh man from Adam.
