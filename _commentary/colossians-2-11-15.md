Paul runs five verbs together and every one of them is God's.

## The circumcision

*In Him you were also circumcised, with **a circumcision no human hand performed**.*

The Greek is **acheiropoietos** — not made with hands.

Colossae was being pressed toward Jewish observance. Paul does not argue about the
knife; he says the thing it stood for has already happened.

## Buried and raised

*You were **buried with Him** in baptism, and in that same baptism **you were raised with
Him too**.*

Both in one sentence, and both past tense.

## How

*By **trusting what God was at work doing** — the God who raised Him from the dead.*

The Greek is *through faith in the working of God*. The faith is in what God did, not in
what the believer manages.

## The condition before

*And you, who were **dead in your trespasses** and in the uncircumcision of your flesh.*

Dead. Not weakened, not struggling. Paul's diagnosis leaves nothing for the patient to
contribute.

## The verb

*He **made alive together with Him**.*

One Greek word — **suzoopoieo**, to make alive together with.

## The forgiveness

*Forgiving you **every trespass**.*

The Greek is **charizomai** — from **charis**, grace. To give freely, to grant as a
favour. It is not the courtroom word for acquittal; it is the word for a gift.

**Every trespass** — all of them, in one clause, with no accounting.

## The note

*He **erased the written note that stood against us**, with all its demands set down in
our disfavor.*

The Greek is **cheirographon** — a handwritten document, specifically an IOU. A debtor
wrote it out in his own hand as an acknowledgement of what he owed.

**Erased** is **exaleipho** — to wipe out, to sponge off. Ancient ink sat on the surface
of papyrus and could be washed away, leaving the sheet blank.

## Removed and nailed

*He has **removed it from between us**, **nailing it to the cross**.*

Two more verbs. Taken out of the way, and then fixed to the beam.

The charge sheet is put where the body went.

## The last verse

*He **stripped the rulers and authorities of their weapons**, **put them on open
display**, and **led them in triumph** in it.*

The triumph image again — the Roman victory procession, the general in front and the
defeated walking behind. Paul uses it in 2 Corinthians of the believers. See the note on
2 Corinthians 2:14–17.

Here the ones in the column are the powers.

## Where the display happened

*In it* — in the cross.

The place that looked like the defeat is named as the parade.

## What Paul is arguing against

The verses on either side: *let no one judge you in food or in drink, or regarding a
festival or a new moon or sabbaths*, and *why do you subject yourselves to regulations —
do not touch, do not taste, do not handle?*

The list of what God did is his answer to a list of what the Colossians were being told
to do.

> **Whose hands are in the passage.** Not the reader's. Circumcised without hands,
> buried, raised, made alive, forgiven, the note erased and nailed up — and the only
> thing asked of anybody is trusting what God was at work doing.
