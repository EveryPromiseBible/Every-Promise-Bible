One verse of geography, and it explains everything that happens next.

## The departure

*Then they **set out from Mount Hor** along the way to the Red Sea.*

Aaron has just died on Mount Hor, in sight of the whole congregation, and they have mourned him
thirty days.

## The direction

*Along the way to **the Red Sea**.*

Which is backwards.

They are heading for Canaan, and this road runs south, toward the water they crossed forty years
ago.

## The reason

*Going **the long way around Edom**.*

The Hebrew is **lisbov et-eretz Edom** — to go around the land of Edom.

## What had happened with Edom

Moses had sent messengers: *please let us pass through your country. We will not pass through
fields or vineyards, nor will we drink water from the wells; **we will go along the King's
Highway**.*

*Then Edom said to him, "**You shall not pass through, lest I come out against you with the
sword**."*

They offered to pay for water. The answer was no, twice, and then Edom came out with a large
force.

## Who Edom was

Esau's descendants. Israel's cousins.

And the instruction was explicit: *do not meddle with them, for I will not give you any of their
land… **because I have given Ar to the descendants of Lot as a possession***.

They were forbidden to fight, so they walked around.

## The cost

A detour of many days, south and then east, over the worst terrain in the region, in order to
avoid a war they were not permitted to fight.

## The result

*And **the soul of the people grew weary on the journey**.*

The Hebrew is **vattiqtzar nephesh-ha'am badderek** — the soul of the people was shortened by the
way.

**Qatzar** — to be short, to be cut short. The idiom for patience running out.

The same construction as *the LORD's soul was shortened* over Israel's misery, and Samson's soul
*was vexed to death* by Delilah's daily questions.

## What the verse is doing

It gives the reason before the sin.

The next verse is the complaint that brings the serpents. The narrator supplies the context first
and does not use it as an excuse.

## What they say

*"**Why did you bring us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? There is no bread. There is no
water. And we loathe this miserable food**."* See the note on Numbers 21:5.

Two of the three complaints contradict each other in the same breath.

## What comes next

*So the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people, and many of the people
of Israel died.*

*Then the LORD said to Moses, "**Make a fiery serpent, and set it on a pole; and it shall be that
everyone who is bitten, when he looks at it, shall live**."* See the note on
Numbers 21:8–9.

## What Jesus does with it

*"And as **Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness**, even so must the Son of Man be lifted
up."* See the note on John 3:14–18.

## The victory just before

The same chapter opens with the king of Arad attacking and taking prisoners, and Israel vowing a
vow and destroying the cities. The place was called **Hormah**.

A victory, then a funeral, then a detour, then a complaint. Numbers puts them in that order.

> **The idiom.** *The soul of the people was shortened by the way.* Numbers gives the reason for
> the impatience before recording what came out of it, and does not treat the reason as an excuse.
