The chapter opens by telling a barren woman to sing, and then to build for
children she does not have.

## Who is being addressed

*Sing, **you who never held a child**. Break into song and shout for joy, **you who
never knew the pains of birth**.*

In that world this was the worst thing that could be said about a woman, and the
chapter opens by naming it twice and then commanding singing.

The singing comes first. Before anything has changed.

## Where the chapter sits

Isaiah 54 follows Isaiah 53 — *wounded for our transgressions… by His stripes we
are healed… He shall see His seed.*

The barren woman sings because of what happened in the chapter before. The
Servant's suffering is what produces her children.

## The instruction

*So **make the tent wider. Stretch out the curtains. Do not hold back. Lengthen your
ropes and drive your stakes down deep.**"*

Five commands, and they are all construction work on an empty tent.

She is told to prepare accommodation before there is anyone to accommodate — to
buy the cot, in effect, while still childless.

## The two halves of it

*Lengthen your ropes* — the guy lines go further out, because the tent is getting
bigger.

*Drive your stakes down deep* — and the Hebrew is *strengthen your stakes*. A wider
tent takes more wind; the anchoring has to go deeper as the span increases.

Both at once. Wider and better anchored, not one or the other.

## What follows

*"**You will spread out to the right and to the left.** Your children will take
over the nations and settle the emptied cities."*

The Hebrew for *spread out* is to break forth, to burst through.

## What is not asked

Nothing in the passage tells her to produce a child, or to try. The enlargement is
what she does; the children are what God does.

*For the woman left alone will have more children than the woman with a husband,
says the LORD.*

## Where the chapter goes

Further down: *you will be established in righteousness… their righteousness is
from Me, says the LORD.* See the note on Isaiah 54:13–14.

And the guarantee He gives for the whole thing is a rainbow: *as I have sworn that
the waters of Noah would no longer cover the earth, so have I sworn that I would
not be angry with you.*

> **Who Paul says this is about.** He quotes verse 1 in Galatians 4, of *the
> Jerusalem above, which is the mother of us all* — the covenant of promise rather
> than the covenant of law. The barren woman with more children than the married
> one is, in his reading, grace outproducing the law.
