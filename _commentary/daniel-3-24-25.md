He counts the men going in and counts again, and the arithmetic has changed.

## The king's reaction

*Then King Nebuchadnezzar was **astounded and rose up in haste**.*

The Aramaic is **tevah** — alarmed, startled.

He had ordered the furnace heated seven times hotter, and the men who threw them in were killed by
the heat.

## The check

*"**Did we not throw three men, bound, into the fire**?"*

He asks his counsellors to confirm a number he watched himself.

## The confirmation

*They answered, "**True, O king**."*

## What he sees

*"But I see **four men, loose, walking about in the middle of the fire, and they are not harmed**."*

Three details, in order: the count, the ropes, the walking.

## The first change

Four.

## The second

**Sheryan** — loosed, freed.

They were bound in their coats, trousers, turbans and other garments. The fire has burned the ropes
and left the clothes.

## The third

*Walking about.* Not standing, not crouching.

The furnace has become somewhere to walk.

## What is not burned

*The fire had no power over their bodies; **the hair of their head was not singed**, nor were their
garments affected, and **the smell of fire was not on them**.*

Nothing about them even smells of it.

## The fourth

*"And the fourth looks like **a son of the gods**."*

The Aramaic is **bar-elahin**.

A pagan king describing what he sees in the only vocabulary he has.

## What he says afterwards

*"Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-Nego, **who sent His Angel and delivered His
servants who trusted in Him**."*

He revises the phrase himself: **mal'akeh** — His messenger.

## What they had said before going in

*"Our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and He will deliver us
from your hand, O king. **But if not**, let it be known to you that we do not serve your gods."* See
the note on Daniel 3:16–18.

## The clause

*But if not.*

They state the outcome they hope for and then remove it from the decision.

## What was not promised

Nobody told them there would be a fourth man.

The confidence in verse 17 is not a prediction of rescue. It is a refusal to bow either way.

## Where He meets them

Not outside. He is in the fire with them, and the deliverance happens inside it.

## What Isaiah says with the same picture

*"When you pass **through the waters**, I will be with you… **when you walk through the fire**, you
shall not be burned, nor shall the flame scorch you."* See the note on Isaiah 43:1–4.

Through, both times.

## What the king does next

Promotes them, and issues a decree.

The furnace was built to make people bow to an image. It produces an imperial edict about the God
of the men who would not.

## The name for a fourth man in the fire

*"Behold, **the virgin shall be with child, and bear a Son, and they shall call His name
Immanuel**," which is translated, "**God with us**."*

That is the whole content of the fourth figure. Nebuchadnezzar has no vocabulary for it and says
*a son of the gods*; the New Testament gives the arrangement a name and makes it the point of the
incarnation.

*"For He Himself has said, '**I will never leave you nor forsake you**.'"*

**Hebrews** 13 quotes it in a chapter about people who were tortured, mocked, chained and sawn in
two — and the sentence stands over both outcomes.

## What the fire consumed

Three went in bound. Four are walking loose. The only thing burned was the rope.

*"**Therefore if the Son makes you free, you shall be free indeed.**"*

*Stand fast therefore in the liberty by which **Christ has made us free**, and do not be entangled
again with a yoke of bondage.*

## But if not

*"Our God whom we serve is able to deliver us… **But if not**, let it be known to you that we do
not serve your gods."*

*"O My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from Me; **nevertheless, not as I will, but as
You will**."*

**Matthew** 26, and it is the same shape: the outcome asked for, and then removed from the
decision. In Gethsemane the answer was *if not*, and nobody came into the fire with Him.

*Then all the disciples forsook Him and fled.*

Three men in a furnace had a fourth. The one place in Scripture where the fourth figure is absent
is the one place He was the one in it.

## And the smell

*The smell of fire was not on them.*

*Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or
famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword?… Yet **in all these things we are more than
conquerors** through Him who loved us.*

> **The count.** Three went in bound; four are walking loose. The only thing the fire consumed was
> the rope.
