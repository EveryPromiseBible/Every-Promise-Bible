Paul has spent chapter 1 describing a society in freefall, and every reader
nodding along is the person chapter 2 is written to.

## The trap

*So you have no defence, whoever you are, when you sit in judgment on someone
else. In the very act of judging another person you condemn yourself, because you
who judge are doing the same things.*

The first word is *therefore*. Everything in chapter 1 was setting this up. Paul
built a list his audience would agree with, and then turned and said: the
agreeing is the problem.

## The question in the middle

*Do you think so lightly of the riches of His kindness and patience and
restraint?*

Three words for God's forbearance, and each is a treasury word — *riches* of
them. Kindness, patience, and holding back.

The charge is not that the judgmental person has broken more rules. It is that
they have **misread what the kindness was for**, treating a delay in judgement as
either approval or absence.

## What the kindness is doing

*Not realising that it is God's kindness that is leading you to a change of
mind.*

The verb means to lead by the hand, to conduct somewhere. Kindness is described
as the thing doing the leading, and the destination is repentance.

That is worth stating plainly, because it is the reverse of how the process is
usually imagined. Not: repent, and then God will be kind. His kindness is what
brings a person round.

## The alternative

*But with a hard and unchanged heart you are storing up wrath for yourself.*

The same treasury image, turned over. Both the kindness and the wrath are being
accumulated; which one a person ends up drawing on depends on what they did with
the first.

> **Where the argument goes.** Paul is on his way to *all have sinned*, and he
> gets there by closing every exit — the pagan in chapter 1, the moralist here,
> the religious in the rest of chapter 2. Nobody is left standing, which is
> exactly the ground he needs for chapter 3.
