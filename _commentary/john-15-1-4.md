Two people are named in the first sentence, and neither of them is the branch.

## The true vine

*"**I am the true vine**, and **My Father is the gardener**."*

Israel had been called a vine repeatedly, and every time the account ends in
disappointment — Isaiah's vineyard that produced wild grapes, Jeremiah's noble vine
gone degenerate, Psalm 80's vine brought out of Egypt and then broken down.

**True** is **alethinos** — genuine, real, the authentic one. He is not correcting the
image; He is claiming to be the thing the others were pictures of.

## The gardener

The Greek is **georgos** — a farmer, a tiller of soil. The one who owns the vineyard
and works it.

Both roles in the sentence belong to God. Nobody else is doing anything yet.

## The two operations

*"Every branch in Me that **bears no fruit He takes away**, and every branch that
**does bear fruit He prunes clean**, so that it will bear more."*

The Greek does a thing here that translation struggles with. **Airo** means to lift
up, and also to take away — and vinedressers do lift trailing branches off the ground
so they can get light.

**Kathairo** is to clean, to prune. The two verbs are almost the same word, and John
put them side by side deliberately.

Both operations happen to branches, and both are the gardener's. The branch does not
prune itself.

## What pruning is for

*"So that **it will bear more**."*

The stated purpose is not correction. A vine is cut back hardest where it is
producing, because unpruned wood makes leaves instead of grapes.

## Already clean

*"**You are already clean** because of the word I have spoken to you."*

The same root as *prunes clean*, one verse later. He uses the pruning word about them
and then says the work is done.

And the agent named is His word — not their effort, not the pruning yet to come.

Judas has just left the room. John notes it at the end of chapter 13: *he then, having
received the piece of bread, went out immediately. And it was night.* See the note on
John 13:27–30.

## The one instruction

*"**Remain in Me**, and I will remain in you."*

The Greek is **meno** — abide, stay, remain. It appears eleven times in this chapter.

Not work, not produce, not strive. Stay attached.

## The reason

*"**A branch cannot bear fruit by itself**; it has to remain in the vine. **Neither
can you**, unless you remain in Me."*

The argument is from horticulture. A branch on the ground does not make grapes more
slowly; it makes none.

And then the sentence that follows in the next verse: *apart from Me you can do
nothing.* See the note on John 15:5–8.

> **Where He said it.** On the way from the upper room to Gethsemane, at night —
> possibly passing the temple, whose gate carried a great golden vine, or through
> vineyards on the way to the Kidron. He is hours from the arrest and talking about
> fruit.
