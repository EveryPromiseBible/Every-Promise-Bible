Two verses, six verbs, and no speech at all.

## The arrival

*When they came to **the place God had shown him**.*

The Hebrew is **el-hammaqom asher amar-lo ha'Elohim**.

The command had been deliberately vague: *offer him on one of the mountains **of which I shall tell
you***.

Three days of walking without knowing which one.

## The six verbs

**Built** an altar.

**Arranged** the wood.

**Bound** his son.

**Laid** him on the altar.

**Reached out** his hand.

**Took** the knife.

Nothing else. The narrator gives the sequence and no interior access at all.

## What is missing

Any word from either of them.

Isaac spoke in verse 7 and does not speak again in the chapter. Abraham answered him and says
nothing more until the angel calls.

The silence over these two verses is the loudest thing in Genesis.

## The altar

*Abraham **built an altar there and arranged the wood on it**.*

He builds it himself, stone by stone, with his son watching.

However long that took, Isaac stood there.

## The binding

*He **bound his son Isaac**.*

The Hebrew is **vayya'aqod** — and he bound.

**Aqad** — to bind, and it occurs once in the Hebrew Bible. Here.

Jewish tradition names the whole episode from it: **the Akedah**, the Binding.

## Isaac's age

Not stated. He carries a load of wood up a mountain, which means he is not a small child.

Jewish tradition variously makes him a young man of twenty-five or thirty-seven.

Whatever the number, he could have run.

## What that means

The binding required his consent, or something close to it.

The narrative does not say so. It says he was bound, and does not record a struggle.

## The position

*And **laid him on the altar, on top of the wood**.*

The wood he had carried up.

## The hand

*Then Abraham **reached out his hand and took the knife**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyishlach Avraham et-yado vayyiqqach et-hamma'achelet**.

The verse ends there.

## What stops it

*But the Angel of the LORD called to him from heaven and said, "**Abraham, Abraham!**"*

*"Do not lay your hand on the lad, or do anything to him."* See the note on
Genesis 22:11–12.

The name twice, which is how urgency is spoken in Hebrew.

## The interval

Between the knife going up and the voice is one verse division.

The narrator does not draw it out and does not need to.

## What was intended

*"For **now I know that you fear God**, since you have not withheld your son, your only son, from
Me."*

The Hebrew is **velo chasachta** — and you have not withheld.

## The word that comes back

*He who **did not spare His own Son**, but delivered Him up for us all, how shall He not with Him also
freely give us all things?* See the note on Romans 8:31–34.

The Greek is **ouk epheisato** — did not spare. The Septuagint's word for **chasach**.

Paul uses Genesis 22's own verb, of a Father who did not stop.

> **The six verbs.** Built, arranged, bound, laid, reached out, took. Two verses, no speech, and
> the narrator gives no interior access to either of them.
