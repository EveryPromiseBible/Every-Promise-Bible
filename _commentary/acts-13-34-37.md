Paul is preaching in a synagogue in Antioch, and he uses David to make an
argument that turns on a single observable fact.

## The argument

The psalm says *You will not let Your Holy One see decay*. Paul's point is simple
and almost forensic:

- David said it
- David died, was buried, and **did see decay** — his tomb was a known location
- therefore he was not talking about himself

*But the One God raised up saw no decay at all.* The psalm had to be about
somebody else, and Paul says who.

## The line about David

*After he had served God's purpose in his own generation, [he] fell asleep.*

It is a remarkable summary of a life that filled several books. Not "after he had
won his wars" or "after he had built the kingdom." He served God's purpose **in
his own generation** — the phrase locates the whole thing inside a lifetime, with
a start and a finish, and treats the finish as unremarkable.

David is not diminished by it. He is placed: a man who did what was his to do, in
the years he had, and then slept.

## What the argument is for

David saw corruption; the one the psalm is about did not. And then **Paul** states the conclusion:

*"**Therefore let it be known to you, brethren, that through this Man is preached to you the
forgiveness of sins; and by Him everyone who believes is justified from all things from which you
could not be justified by the law of Moses.**"*

**Acts** 13:38–39, the next two verses, and it is the clearest statement of justification by faith
anywhere in **Acts**.

**Dikaioutai** — is justified. The verb **Paul** builds Romans and Galatians on, used here in a
synagogue sermon years before he wrote either.

## The two clauses

*Everyone who believes* — **pas ho pisteuon**. No category excluded.

*From all things* — **apo panton**. Nothing held back.

And the comparison: *from which you could not be justified by the law of Moses.* The law is not
attacked. It is described as unable to do this particular thing.

*For **by the deeds of the law no flesh will be justified in His sight**, for by the law is the
knowledge of sin.*

*Being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in **Christ Jesus**.*

## The sure mercies of David

*"**I will give you the sure mercies of David.**"*

**Ta hosia Dauid ta pista** — the holy, faithful things promised to David. Isaiah 55, quoted here as
the guarantee of the resurrection: the promises to David cannot hold unless somebody in the line does
not stay dead.

> **What the two halves do together.** Paul's whole case rests on the difference
> — David's work ended and his body decayed; the other did not. But the way he
> says it about David is not dismissive. Serving God's purpose in your own
> generation is offered as a good and sufficient description of a life, by a man
> arguing that only one person ever escaped the ending.
