The bleakest search in the book, and the New Testament agrees with its finding.

## The search

*See, says the Teacher, this is what I found, **adding one thing to another to reach the sum of
it**. **What my soul kept seeking I have not found.***

The Hebrew is **cheshbon** — a reckoning, an accounting, a sum. He is describing an audit.

And the result is a negative. He looked, and the thing he was looking for was not there.

## The count

*One man among a thousand I found, but among all of them not one woman.*

The line is often read as a verdict on women, and it is not what the sentence does. The
Preacher's tally is one in a thousand and then zero — his point is the size of the shortfall,
not the sex of the people in it. He has just said in the same breath that *what my soul kept
seeking I have not found*.

Read as an audit, the result is that he could not fill the quota anywhere.

## Where the same audit was run again

*As it is written: "**There is none righteous, no, not one**; there is none who understands;
there is none who seeks after God. They have all turned aside."*

**Paul** runs the identical count in **Romans** 3, quoting the Psalms, and gets a rounder
number. Not one in a thousand. None.

And he does it for a reason: *that every mouth may be stopped, and all the world may become
guilty before God… for **all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, being justified
freely by His grace** through the redemption that is in **Christ Jesus**.*

The negative finding is the setup. Ecclesiastes stops at the shortfall; **Romans** takes three
more verses and arrives at *freely*.

## The upright one

*God made humankind **upright**, but we have gone looking for **endless clever schemes**.*

The Hebrew is **yashar** — straight. And **chishvonot rabbim** — many devices, from the same
root as the *sum* he was trying to reach. The word for his audit and the word for their schemes
are the same word.

*For as by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so also by **one Man's obedience many
will be made righteous**.*

**Paul** answers the verse directly. Made upright, and gone crooked — and then one Man in whom
the original description held, and a count that runs the other way.

## Who was found

*One man among a thousand I found.*

*"Which of you convicts Me of sin?"*

*Who committed no sin, nor was deceit found in His mouth.*

**Peter** uses the search vocabulary: **oude heurethe dolos** — neither was guile *found*. The
Preacher audited a thousand and came up short by one, and the New Testament reports the one, in
the language of an audit.

## And the women in the count

The Preacher found none. The gospels are careful to record who was standing at the cross when
the men had gone, and who reached the tomb first.

*Now **Mary Magdalene** and Mary the mother of Joses observed where He was laid.*

*"**Woman, why are you weeping? Whom are you seeking?**"*

The Preacher says *what my soul kept seeking I have not found*. **John** puts the same verb in
the mouth of a woman in a garden, and she is the first person in the world to be told where He
is.

> **What the audit produced.** A shortfall — one in a thousand, then none. Paul runs the same
> count in Romans 3, gets none at all, and calls it the setup: *being justified freely by His
> grace*.
