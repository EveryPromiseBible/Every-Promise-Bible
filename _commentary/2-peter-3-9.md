Peter is answering scoffers who have noticed that the promised return has not
happened, and he reframes the delay completely.

## The accusation he is answering

The verses before it have people asking where the promise of His coming is, since
everything carries on as it always has.

Peter does not argue about the timetable. He changes the category the delay
belongs to.

## The sentence

*The Lord is not dragging His feet over what He promised, whatever some people
call it. He is being patient with us. He does not want anyone destroyed. He wants
every last one to turn.*

The word behind *patient* is **makrothumeo** — long-tempered, slow to anger, the
quality of someone who could act and does not yet.

What is being read as inability or indifference is a decision being held open.

## What He wants

Two statements, one negative and one positive, and both are unrestricted:

- **not wanting** anyone to be destroyed
- **wanting** every last one to come to repentance

Peter puts God's desire on record in the plainest possible terms, in a chapter
otherwise full of fire and dissolving elements.

## What the delay is for

That is the answer to the scoffers. The time that looks like absence is the time
being given — and Peter says so again five verses on: *consider the patience of
our Lord as salvation.*

The waiting is not neutral. It is rescue in progress.

> **The verse before it.** *With the Lord a day is like a thousand years, and a
> thousand years like a day.* Peter answers the timing question twice — once by
> saying God's reckoning of time is not ours, and once by saying the delay is
> deliberate mercy. He does not pick between them.
