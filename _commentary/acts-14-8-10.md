Paul is preaching, and something in the audience catches his eye.

## Where this is

**Lystra**, in the interior of what is now Turkey. A Roman colony with no synagogue mentioned
— Paul and Barnabas had just been run out of Iconium and had come here on foot.

Timothy is from Lystra. He may well be in this crowd.

## The man

*At Lystra there was a man **sitting who had no strength in his feet**.*

The Greek is **adunatos tois posin** — powerless in the feet.

*He had **never walked in his life**; he had been **unable to since birth**.*

Luke states it three ways in one sentence: no strength, never walked, from birth.

Luke is a physician and he takes the case history first.

## The parallel

The same three-part description appears at the temple gate in chapter 3 — *a certain man
lame from his mother's womb was carried, whom they laid daily at the gate*.

Peter's miracle and Paul's are told in matching language, which is one of the things Acts
does deliberately.

## What he was doing

*He **listened while Paul was speaking**.*

The Greek is an imperfect — he kept listening.

Nothing else is recorded of him. He is in the crowd, sitting, hearing.

## What Paul saw

*Paul **looked closely at him**.*

The Greek is **atenisas** — gazed intently, fixed his eyes on. Luke's word, used fourteen
times in Acts and twice elsewhere in the whole New Testament.

Stephen uses it looking into heaven; the crowd uses it staring at Peter and John.

## What he saw

*And **seeing that he had faith to be made well**.*

The Greek is **echei pistin tou sothenai** — has faith to be saved, or healed. **Sozo** does
both jobs.

Nothing was said. The man had not asked, and Luke does not record him calling out.

Faith arrived while a man sat listening — which is exactly what Paul writes elsewhere: *faith
comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God*. See the note on Romans 10:16–17.

## The command

*He said **in a loud voice**, "**Stand up straight on your feet**."*

The Greek is **anastethi orthos** — stand upright.

Said loudly, in the middle of a sermon, to a man everybody in Lystra knew could not.

## The result

*And the man **jumped up and began to walk**.*

The Greek is **hellato** — leaped. And then an imperfect: he kept walking.

Isaiah's line: *then the lame shall leap like a deer*.

## What happens next

The crowd decides they are gods. *The gods have come down to us in the likeness of men!* —
Barnabas is Zeus and Paul is Hermes *because he was the chief speaker*.

The priest of Zeus brings oxen and garlands to the gates.

Ovid records a local legend that Zeus and Hermes had once visited this region disguised and
been turned away by everyone but an old couple. Lystra was not going to make that mistake
twice.

## What Paul does

Tears his clothes and runs in among them: *why are you doing these things? **We also are men
with the same nature as you***.

And then the shortest summary of natural revelation in Acts: *He did not leave Himself
without witness, in that He did good, gave us rain from heaven and fruitful seasons, filling
our hearts with food and gladness.*

## How the day ends

*Then Jews from Antioch and Iconium came there; and having persuaded the multitudes, **they
stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, supposing him to be dead**.*

The same crowd, the same afternoon.

*However, when the disciples gathered around him, he rose up and went into the city.* And
the next day he walked to Derbe.

He comes back through Lystra on the way home, *strengthening the souls of the disciples*.

## What Paul says to the crowd afterwards

They try to sacrifice to him, and he tears his clothes and shouts the shortest gospel summary in
**Acts**:

*"**Men, why are you doing these things? We also are men with the same nature as you, and preach to
you that you should turn from these useless things to the living God**, who made the heaven, the
earth, the sea, and all things that are in them."*

*"**Nevertheless He did not leave Himself without witness, in that He did good, gave us rain from
heaven and fruitful seasons, filling our hearts with food and gladness.**"*

To a crowd with no Scripture, he starts with weather and harvests. It is the same argument
**Matthew** 5 makes about the sun and the rain falling on everybody.

## Faith to be healed

*"**Paul, observing him intently and seeing that he had faith to be healed.**"*

**Luke** notes that **Paul** looked at him — **atenisas** — and saw it. The man has not spoken.

*So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.*

He had been listening to **Paul** preach. **Luke** puts the hearing before the faith and the faith
before the command.

## How that day ended

*Then Jews from Antioch and Iconium came there; and having persuaded the multitudes, **they stoned
Paul and dragged him out of the city, supposing him to be dead**.*

The crowd that tried to worship him stones him within the paragraph.

*From now on let no one trouble me, for **I bear in my body the marks of the Lord Jesus**.*

> **What Paul was looking for while he preached.** Not attention. Luke says he fixed his eyes
> on a man and saw that he had faith to be healed — before a word had been said by anybody
> but Paul.
