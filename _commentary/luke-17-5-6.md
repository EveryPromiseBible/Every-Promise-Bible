The apostles ask for an increase, and the answer they get is not more of what
they had in mind.

## Why they asked

Read the four verses before it. Jesus has just said that if your brother sins
against you seven times a day and turns back seven times saying he repents, you
must forgive him.

*Give us more faith* is what they say next. It is not a request about mountains
or miracles. It is a response to being told to forgive somebody seven times
before dinner.

## The answer

*If your faith were the size of a mustard seed, you could say to this mulberry
tree, "Pull yourself up by the roots and plant yourself in the sea," and it would
obey you.*

He does not give them more. He tells them the quantity was never the issue.

A mustard seed was proverbially the smallest thing anyone handled — and the
mulberry, with its deep root system, was proverbially among the hardest trees to
shift. Smallest possible faith, hardest possible object, and the tree obeys.

## The instruction inside it

*You could **say** to this tree.* The faith in the illustration is exercised by
speaking to the thing.

That is worth noticing in a passage about forgiveness. The uprooting Jesus
describes is not something felt into being; it is addressed.

## What comes immediately after

A parable about a servant who comes in from the field and serves dinner before
eating — *we are unworthy servants; we have only done our duty.*

Jesus keeps the two together: faith the size of a seed can uproot a tree, and the
people exercising it are not to think of themselves as impressive for it.

> **The word "size."** The Greek is literally *if you have faith as a grain of
> mustard* — a comparison of kind as much as quantity. Something alive and
> small, which is what a seed is. Not a measured amount of a substance.
