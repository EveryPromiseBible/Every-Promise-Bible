Luke tells the birth in two sentences and spends most of them on where the baby
was put.

## "Wrapped Him around and around in strips of cloth"

Ordinary practice — newborns were bound in bands of cloth, believed to help the
limbs grow straight. Every mother in that world did it.

What is not ordinary is that Luke mentions it three times in this chapter. The
angel gives it to the shepherds as the identifying sign: *you will find a baby
wrapped in strips of cloth and lying in a feeding trough.*

The sign by which the Messiah could be recognised was that He looked exactly like
any other newborn, in a place no other newborn would be.

## The trough

The Greek is **phatne** — a manger, the box animals eat from.

Luke does not describe a stable, and there is a good case that the setting was
the lower room of a village house where animals were kept at night, since the
word he uses for *inn* is the one he later uses for the upper room at the Last
Supper.

Either way, the detail is the same: the first bed was a feeding trough.

## "No room left for them"

Luke states it without accusation. Nobody is blamed and no innkeeper appears in
the text — that character comes from later tradition.

Bethlehem was crowded because of the census. There was simply no space, and a
young couple took what was available.

> **What He is laid in.** A container that food is put in, in a town whose name
> means *house of bread*, by a mother who would later hear Him say *I am the bread
> of life.* Luke does not point at any of that. He just records where they put
> Him.
