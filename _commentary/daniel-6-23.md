One verse, and it gives the reason at the end.

## The king's night

*Then **the king rejoiced greatly**.*

Darius had spent the night fasting, refusing entertainment, unable to sleep, and had
gone to the den *very early in the morning, with a lamenting voice.*

The man who signed the decree is the one who could not rest. Daniel had a better
night than the king did.

## The lifting

*He gave the order to **lift Daniel out of the den**. So Daniel was lifted up.*

Said twice. He goes down by being thrown and comes up by being lifted.

## The verdict

*And **no wound was found on him**.*

The Aramaic is that no injury was discovered. It is an inspection — they looked him
over.

Not that he survived, or was mauled but recovered. Nothing on him.

## The reason

*Because **he had trusted in his God**.*

That is the whole explanation the text offers.

Not because he was brave, or innocent, or valuable to the empire — all of which were
true. Because he trusted.

Hebrews puts him in the roll of faith by his act rather than his name: *who through
faith… **stopped the mouths of lions**.*

## What he had done to get there

The decree forbade petitions to anyone but the king for thirty days, on pain of the
den.

*When Daniel knew that the writing was signed, he went home. And in his upper room,
with his windows open toward Jerusalem, **he knelt down on his knees three times that
day, and prayed and gave thanks before his God, as was his custom since early
days**.*

He did not increase his praying or make a demonstration of it. He did not reduce it
either. *As was his custom.*

## How old he is

Daniel was taken to Babylon as a youth in the first deportation. By the reign of
Darius the Mede he is an old man — most reckonings put him in his eighties.

Sixty years in that court, under a succession of empires, and *they could find no
charge or fault, because he was faithful; nor was there any error or fault found in
him.*

His enemies say it themselves: *we shall not find any charge against this Daniel
unless we find it against him concerning the law of his God.*

## The morning question

Darius, at dawn, at the mouth of the den: *"Daniel, servant of the living God, has
your God, whom you serve continually, been able to deliver you from the lions?"*

And the answer from the dark: *"O king, live forever! **My God sent His angel and
shut the lions' mouths**."*

> **What the king wrote afterwards.** A decree to every people and nation and
> language: *that in every dominion of my kingdom men must tremble and fear before
> the God of Daniel. For He is the living God… He delivers and rescues.* A night in a
> pit turned into an empire-wide proclamation.
