The turning point of the whole book, spoken by a young man nobody had asked, and
it is the answer to everything the three friends could not solve.

## Where it comes in

Elihu has just described a man on a sickbed — flesh wasting, bones jutting out, his
soul drifting toward the grave, his life inching toward the messengers of death.
See the note on Job 33:19–22.

Then: *Yet even then, **if there is a messenger at his side**…*

## The one in a thousand

*One who **speaks for him**, one in a thousand.*

The Hebrew is a **melitz** — an interpreter, a spokesman, a go-between. It is the
word used of the interpreter standing between Joseph and his brothers.

And *one in a thousand* — there is one such person, and He is not common.

This is exactly what Job had been crying out for. In chapter 9: *He is not a man,
as I am, that I should answer Him… **there is no umpire between us**, who might
lay his hand on us both.* In chapter 16: *my witness is in heaven, my advocate is
on high.*

Job knew what was missing. Elihu names it.

## What the messenger does

*To **remind this person of what is right and true**.*

Not to examine him. Not to search out his sin, prescribe steps, or diagnose the
cause — which is what all three friends spent twenty-five chapters doing.

The Hebrew is to declare to the man His **uprightness** — God's righteousness,
shown to him.

The messenger's job is to show a suffering man what God is like. That is the whole
commission.

## Then God speaks

*Then **God is gracious to him** and says, "Spare him. Do not let him go down into
the pit. **I have found the price for his release**."*

The Hebrew for *gracious* is **chanan** — the verb behind *chen*, grace.

## The ransom

*I have found the price for his release.* The Hebrew is **kopher** — a ransom, a
covering, the price paid to redeem a life that was forfeit.

It is the same root as **kippur**, the covering of the Day of Atonement.

Read who does the finding. Not the sick man. Not the friends. **I have found it.**
God locates the ransom Himself, and announces it as the reason the man is spared.

There is no condition attached to it in Elihu's sentence. A messenger shows him
God's righteousness; God says a price has been found; the man does not go down.

## What is on the other side of it

The next verse: *then his flesh grows fresh again, softer than a child's. He
returns to the strength of his young days.*

The healing follows the ransom. It does not produce it. See the note on
Job 33:25–28.

> **Who the messenger is.** *There is one God and one Mediator between God and men,
> the Man Christ Jesus, who gave Himself a ransom for all.* One God, one Mediator,
> one ransom — and Elihu says it in a book that has no priest, no temple and no
> covenant in it anywhere.
