Two men walking away from Jerusalem on the day of the resurrection, and the man
they are talking about falls into step beside them.

## Where they were going

*A village called **Emmaus**, sixty stadia out from Jerusalem — a walk of about
seven miles.*

The name means warm springs, warm baths. There were hot springs in that country
and a village took its name from them.

They are walking away from the city, and they are on the road on the same day the
tomb was found empty.

## What they were doing

*They were talking the whole way about everything that had happened… **talking it
over and turning it around** between them.*

The Greek for the second is to debate, to dispute — they are going over it and
getting nowhere.

They have all the facts. Luke's next paragraph shows they know about the
crucifixion, the third day, the women at the tomb, the angels, and the men who
went and found it empty.

Facts, and no sight.

## "Their eyes were held"

*Jesus Himself came up and walked along with them. But **their eyes were held**,
and they did not recognise Him.*

The Greek is passive — something was holding them. Luke does not say what.

He is physically present, close enough to walk seven miles with them, and it does
nothing.

## What He does about it

Not what you would expect. He does not identify Himself, and He does not show them
the marks — that comes later, in the room, with the others.

*And beginning at Moses and all the Prophets, **He expounded to them in all the
Scriptures the things concerning Himself**.*

He gives them a Bible study. Seven miles of it, from Genesis forward.

## Which He thought was better

He had the option of simply appearing. Instead He walked the whole way concealed
and opened the Scriptures — and their own verdict afterwards is about the road,
not the moment of recognition:

*Were our hearts not burning inside us **while He talked with us on the road,
while He was opening the Scriptures to us**?*

The burning happened before they knew who He was. It happened while the Book was
being opened.

## What was in that sermon

Moses and all the Prophets. Whatever else was in it, Genesis was, and Jacob's
blessings over the twelve sons are in Genesis.

> **What He calls them.** *Foolish ones, and slow of heart to believe in all that
> the prophets have spoken.* Not slow to believe the women, or the empty tomb.
> Slow to believe what was already written down.
