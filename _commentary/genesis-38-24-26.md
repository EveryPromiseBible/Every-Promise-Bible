key: Genesis 38:24–26
title: She is more in the right than I am
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Judah passes a death sentence and is handed back his own belongings.

## Where this sits

Genesis interrupts the Joseph story for a whole chapter to tell it. Joseph has just been sold
to Egypt, and the brother who proposed the sale is the subject of the next twenty-six verses.
See the note on Genesis 37:25–28.

## The report

*"**Your daughter-in-law Tamar is guilty of immorality**, and she is now pregnant because of
it."*

Tamar had been married to Judah's eldest son, then to the second, and both had died. She had
been sent back to her father's house to wait for the third son, Shelah, and Shelah had grown up
and nothing had happened.

## The sentence

*"**Bring her out**," Judah ordered, "**and let her be burned**."*

Three words in the Hebrew for the verdict. No trial, no hearing, and no interval.

He is not the injured party and he is not a judge. He passes a capital sentence on a woman in
his family the moment he hears about it.

## What she sends back

*"**I am pregnant by the man who owns these.**" And she said, "**Please look closely — whose
seal and cord and staff are these?**"*

She does not name him. She sends the evidence and asks him to identify it himself.

The **seal** was a cylinder worn on a cord at the neck, used to sign contracts. The **staff**
was personal and carved. Together they are the ancient equivalent of a man's signature and
identity papers, and he had handed them over as a pledge.

## The recognition

*Judah **recognized them at once**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyakker** — and he recognised.

It is the same verb, in the same form, that his own brothers used on his father one chapter
earlier: *do you know whether it is your son's tunic or not?* — and Jacob *recognised it*.

Judah helped hold up a bloodied coat and ask a father to identify it. Now somebody holds up his
own things and asks him to do the same.

## What he says

*"**She is more in the right than I am**," he said, "**since I would not give her to my son
Shelah**."*

The Hebrew is **tsadeqah mimmenni** — she is more righteous than I.

He does not argue, mitigate, or have her quietly removed. He says it out loud, in front of
whoever brought the report, and he names the reason: he had withheld what he owed her.

## What is not recorded

Any punishment.

The sentence is dropped, the woman lives, and *he never was intimate with her again*.

## What comes of it

Twins. Perez and Zerah.

*Perez begot Hezron, and Hezron begot Ram…* — the line runs straight through this chapter to
Boaz, to Jesse, to David.

## Where her name appears

*Judah begot **Perez and Zerah by Tamar**.*

The third verse of the New Testament. Matthew is listing the ancestry of Jesus Christ and puts
her in it by name. See the note on Matthew 1:1–3.

Four women are named in that genealogy: Tamar here, Rahab of Jericho, Ruth the Moabitess, and
*her who had been the wife of Uriah*.

Matthew could have left all four out. Genealogies routinely did. He puts them in.

## What that says

The line the Messiah comes through does not run around this chapter. It runs through it.

Nobody in Genesis 38 behaves well. Judah is the one who suggested selling his brother for
silver, and the tribe that produces the Lion of Judah, the sceptre, and the King is descended
from the twins born at the end of it. See the note on Genesis 49:8–12.

> **What was handed back to him.** His own seal, his own cord, his own staff — and a question
> asking him to say whose they were. He recognised them, and the first thing he said was that
> she was in the right.
