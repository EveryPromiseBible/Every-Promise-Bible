The Sadducees bring a riddle designed to make resurrection look absurd, and He
answers it from a verb.

## Who they are

*The Sadducees — **the ones who say there is no resurrection**.*

Matthew defines them in the same breath as introducing them. They were the priestly
aristocracy, they ran the temple, and they accepted only the five books of Moses as
binding.

That last detail matters for what He quotes.

## The riddle

Seven brothers, one widow, the levirate law from Deuteronomy 25. *So at the
resurrection, whose wife will she be?*

It is not a genuine question. It is a stock debating trap, designed to make the
doctrine collapse under its own arithmetic.

## The diagnosis

*"You are wrong, and it is because **you do not know the Scriptures or the power of
God**."*

Two failures named, and both are shortages rather than errors. They knew a great deal
of Scripture professionally.

## The first answer

*"At the resurrection people **do not marry** and are not given in marriage. They are
**like the angels** in heaven."*

The premise of the riddle is wrong, so the riddle does not arise.

## The second answer

*"Have you never read what God said to you: '**I am** the God of Abraham, the God of
Isaac, and the God of Jacob'? He is **not the God of the dead, but of the living**."*

Exodus 3, at the burning bush — from the books the Sadducees accepted.

The whole argument rests on a tense. God did not say *I was* their God. He said **I
am**, and He said it centuries after all three men were buried.

## What that requires

If God is presently the God of Abraham, then Abraham presently is.

The argument would fail entirely on a past tense, and Jesus builds the resurrection
of the dead on the present one.

## "What God said to you"

Not *what God said to Moses*. He tells them the sentence was addressed to them.

## The reaction

*When the crowds heard this, they were **astounded** at His teaching.*

And Luke adds that some of the scribes said *Teacher, You have spoken well* — the
Pharisees, who did believe in resurrection, enjoying the Sadducees being beaten with
their own five books.

## What this week is

Passion week. Four delegations come at Him in two chapters — the chief priests and
elders, the Pharisees and Herodians, these Sadducees, and a lawyer — and each goes
away with nothing.

*And from that day on no one dared ask Him another question.* See the note on
Matthew 22:41–46.
