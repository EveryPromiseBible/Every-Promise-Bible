Two sentences at the end of the fruit passage, and the second one is oddly small
after everything that came before it.

## The premise

*If the Spirit is our life.*

Not a condition being tested. Paul is stating what is already true of the people he
is writing to, and building the instruction on top of it.

## The verb

*Then let us **keep in step** with the Spirit.*

The Greek is **stoicheo** — a military word, to march in line, to keep rank. It is
not the word Paul used earlier in the chapter for *walk in the Spirit* (**peripateo**,
the ordinary word for going about).

This one is about following a lead you did not set.

## The order in the sentence

Life first, then the walk. The stepping does not produce the life; it follows it.

The whole letter is built against the reverse order. See the note on Galatians 3:1–5.

## What is warned against

*Let us **not become full of ourselves**, **provoking** one another, **envying** one
another.*

Three items, and they run in sequence: conceit, then provocation, then envy.

The Greek for *full of ourselves* is **kenodoxos** — empty glory. *Provoking* is
**prokaleo**, to challenge, to call somebody out to fight.

## Why these three

Paul has just listed the works of the flesh and the fruit of the Spirit — nine of
each, in the verses immediately before.

Having named adultery, idolatry and murder on one side, he ends the passage on
conceit and rivalry between believers.

The small things are the ones the churches in Galatia were actually doing to each
other: *if you bite and devour one another, beware lest you be consumed by one
another.*

## What the fruit is

*Love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness,
self-control.*

**Fruit** is singular in the Greek. One fruit with nine facets — not nine separate
achievements to be worked up one at a time.

Against such there is no law.

## What comes next

*Brethren, if a man is overtaken in any trespass, you who are spiritual **restore such
a one in a spirit of gentleness**.*

The Greek for *restore* is **katartizo** — the word for setting a broken bone.

That is the first thing Paul asks of people who are keeping in step. See the note on
Galatians 6:1–2.

> **What the marching image gives you.** In a line, nobody sets their own pace and
> nobody is out in front. Conceit and rivalry are simply what breaking rank looks
> like.
