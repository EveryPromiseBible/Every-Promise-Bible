Paul's last section, and the verb he keeps returning to is not what people
expect from a passage about warfare.

## The word he repeats

**Stand.** Four times in four verses: stand against the schemes, stand your
ground, and — having done everything — *stand*.

Not advance, not conquer, not take. The posture being described is holding a
position that has already been won, which is consistent with everything in the
first three chapters: seated with Christ, above every ruler and authority.

You defend ground you already hold.

## "Be strong in the Lord"

The verb is passive — *be strengthened*. It is not an exhortation to summon
resolve. And the strength named is *the strength of His might*, the same phrase
Paul used in chapter 1 of the power that raised Christ.

## Who the fight is with

*Not against flesh and blood.*

Paul rules out people first. Whatever the Ephesians were facing — and the city
was famous for its magic and its temple of Artemis — he tells them the opposition
is not the human beings in front of them.

Four terms follow: ruling powers, authorities, world-rulers of this present
darkness, spiritual armies of wickedness in the heavenly places. He does not
define them, and no one since has been able to sort them into a tidy hierarchy.

## "The whole armour of God"

Said twice. It is **God's own** armour — Isaiah describes the LORD putting on
righteousness as a breastplate and a helmet of salvation, and Paul is handing the
same equipment to the church.

Nothing on the list is manufactured by the wearer.

> **What is in the list.** Truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith,
> salvation, the word of God — and then prayer. Every item is something already
> given in the first half of the letter. Chapter 6 is chapters 1 to 3, put on.
