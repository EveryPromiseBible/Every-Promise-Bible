The circle widens, and John gives up trying to count.

## What has just happened

The Lamb took the scroll, and the four creatures and twenty-four elders went down in front
of Him with harps and golden bowls of incense — *and the incense is the prayers of God's
people*. See the note on Revelation 5:7–8.

They sang the new song: *You are worthy… for You were slain, and have redeemed us to God by
Your blood.*

Then John looks again.

## The position

*They were standing **all the way around the throne and the creatures and the elders**.*

Concentric. The elders and creatures are already round the throne; the angels are round
all of it.

The redeemed are nearer than the angels in this scene.

## The number

*There were **tens of thousands of tens of thousands** of them, and **thousands upon
thousands** more.*

The Greek is **muriades muriadon kai chiliades chiliadon** — myriads of myriads and
thousands of thousands.

Daniel has the same construction at the Ancient of Days: *a thousand thousands ministered
to Him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before Him.*

It is not arithmetic. It is the largest phrase the language has.

## The volume

*And what they were saying, **at full volume**, was this.*

The Greek is *with a loud voice*.

## The seven

*The Lamb **deserves it all** — the Lamb **who was killed** — the **power**, the **wealth**,
the **wisdom**, the **strength**, the **honor**, the **glory**, the **blessing**.*

Seven items, and one article governing all of them in the Greek — they come as a single
package rather than a list.

Seven is the number Revelation uses for completeness throughout: seals, trumpets, bowls,
churches, spirits, lampstands.

## The reason

*The Lamb **who was killed**.*

The Greek is the perfect participle — slain, and standing slain.

It is what John saw when he turned to look at the Lion: *a Lamb as though it had been
slain*.

The worthiness is attached to that and to nothing else, twice in the chapter.

## What the elders sang and what the angels sing

The elders' song is *You have redeemed **us**… and have made **us** kings and priests*.

The angels' song has no *us* in it. They are not the redeemed, and they do not claim to
be.

Peter says they want to look into it: *things which angels desire to look into*.

## What comes next

*And **every creature which is in heaven and on the earth and under the earth and such as
are in the sea, and all that are in them**, I heard saying:*

*"Blessing and honour and glory and power be to Him who sits on the throne, and to the
Lamb, forever and ever!"*

The circle widens a third time, to everything that exists.

And the four living creatures said *Amen*, and the elders fell down and worshipped.

## The structure of the chapter

A sealed scroll and nobody worthy. John weeping. The Lion who is a Lamb. The scroll taken.
The elders. The angels. Then all creation.

Four expanding rings, and the object of every one is the same.

> **Where the count breaks down.** John reaches for myriads of myriads and thousands of
> thousands and stops. The seven things they name are given as one package — and the
> reason attached is a single perfect participle: slain.
