Two verses in the middle of a chapter about a marriage, and they are about weapons.

## What the chapter is

Isaiah 54 follows immediately after the suffering servant of chapter 53. It opens *sing, O
barren, you who have not borne*, and it is addressed to a woman who has been left.

*For your Maker is your husband… for the LORD has called you like a woman forsaken and
grieved in spirit.*

## The concession

*If **anyone attacks you, it will not be My doing**.*

The Hebrew is *behold, they shall surely assemble, but not because of Me*.

Attacks are not denied. Their origin is.

The same clause the chapter's most quoted verse depends on: *no weapon formed against you
shall prosper* — formed, which grants that they get made.

## The outcome

*Whoever comes against you **will fall before you**.*

The Hebrew is *shall fall for your sake*.

## The blacksmith

*Look, **I created the blacksmith** who **fans the coals into flame** and **forges the
weapon for its work**.*

Three clauses about the manufacture: the man, the fire, and the product.

God claims the craftsman.

## The destroyer

*And **I created the destroyer to lay waste**.*

The Hebrew is *the waster to destroy*.

## The last line

*Both are Mine.*

The Hebrew is emphatic. Isaiah has already said it of the whole business: *I form the
light and create darkness; I make peace and create calamity; I, the LORD, do all these
things.*

## What the argument is

The verse is not about the weapons failing to exist. It is about who made the man who
makes them.

The next verse draws the conclusion: *no weapon formed against you shall prosper, and every
tongue which rises against you in judgment you shall condemn.* See the note on
Isaiah 54:17.

The reasoning runs: the smith is Mine, so the product is not outside My reach.

## The heritage

*"This is **the heritage of the servants of the LORD**, and **their righteousness is from
Me**," says the LORD.*

The chapter's final sentence, and it ends on where the righteousness comes from rather
than on the weapons.

## What is promised earlier in the chapter

*"For the mountains shall depart and the hills be removed, but **My kindness shall not
depart from you**, nor shall My covenant of peace be removed."*

And: *"**In righteousness you shall be established**; you shall be far from oppression, for
you shall not fear."*

The Hebrew for *established* is **kun** — fixed, set firm. The same word used of the
priests standing in the Jordan. See the note on Joshua 3:14–17.

## What the chapter promises about children

*"**All your children shall be taught by the LORD**, and great shall be the peace of your
children."* See the note on Isaiah 54:13–14.

Jesus quotes that line in John 6: *it is written in the prophets, "And they shall all be
taught by God."*

> **What is not claimed.** That nothing is made. Isaiah grants the smith, the fire and the
> finished weapon, and then says who made the smith.
