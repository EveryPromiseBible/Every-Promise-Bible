The opposition changes tactics. Chapter 2 was displeasure; this is ridicule.

## The escalation

*It made him **furious**, and his anger **spilled over into scorn**.*

The Hebrew has him burning with anger and then mocking. The mockery is what the anger
turns into when there is nothing else available.

Sanballat has no legal move — Nehemiah holds the king's letters.

## The audience

*In front of **his companions and the army of Samaria** he sneered.*

He assembles a crowd first. This is a performance rather than a confrontation; he never
speaks to Nehemiah, only about him.

## The four questions

- *"**What are these feeble Jews even doing?**"*
- *"**Do they think they can rebuild this?**"*
- *"**Will they offer sacrifices and finish in a single day?**"*
- *"**Can they breathe life back into stones that are nothing now but burned
  rubble?**"*

Every one is a question, and none of them is asked for an answer. It is the standard
shape of contempt.

**Feeble** in the Hebrew is *withered, languishing* — the word for a plant going limp.

## The stones

*Burned rubble.*

Not an exaggeration. Nebuchadnezzar's men had burned the city a hundred and forty years
earlier and the debris was still lying where it fell. Nehemiah's night ride found places
where the animal could not get through.

Sanballat's sneer is factually accurate about the material. The builders are using the
burnt stones.

## Tobiah's line

*"**That little wall of theirs**," he said, "**if a fox climbed up on it, he'd knock the
stones right down**."*

The lightest weight he can name. Not an army, not a battering ram. A fox.

Tobiah's contribution is the joke, and he delivers it standing beside Sanballat while
the army laughs.

## What Nehemiah does with it

He does not answer them. He turns to God mid-chapter — *hear, O our God, for we are
despised* — and then:

*So we built the wall, and **the entire wall was joined together up to half its
height**, for **the people had a mind to work**.*

The Hebrew is *the people had a heart to work*.

The reply to the mockery is the next verse of the construction report.

## What follows

When ridicule fails, the opposition moves to a plot to attack, and the builders work
with a weapon in one hand and a tool in the other: *every one of the builders had his
sword girded at his side as he built.*

## How long it took

Fifty-two days, and the last verse of the chapter about it says the surrounding nations
*were very disheartened in their own eyes; for they perceived that this work was done by
our God.*

The fox joke was made about a wall that went up in under two months.

> **What the mocking was aimed at.** Not the wall — the builders. *Feeble Jews.* The
> book answers it by recording the height reached in the same paragraph.
