The image is chosen carefully: a corpse is not a fake body.

## The sentence

*As **a body without breath is dead**, so **this faith without works is dead**.*

The Greek is **hōsper gar to sōma chōris pneumatos nekron estin**.

## The word

**Pneuma** — breath, spirit, wind.

Genesis: *the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the
breath of life*.

## The comparison

Body is to breath as faith is to works.

## Which is which

The body is faith. The works are the breath.

Read that way, the works are not the body's substitute; they are the sign that it is alive.

## What a corpse is

Not a counterfeit. Not a different substance.

The right shape, complete, and not breathing.

## What James is not saying

He does not say the faith was never real, or that works must be added to make it real.

He says a faith with no works is in the condition of a body with no breath — which is a diagnosis,
not an accusation of fraud.

## The demonstration

*Show me your faith without your works, and I will show you my faith by my works.* See the note on
James 2:18.

The audience is another person. Nobody in the argument is showing anything to God.

## The two examples

Abraham, and Rahab. See the notes on James 2:21 and James 2:25.

A patriarch and a prostitute. One offered a son; the other hid two spies and lied to the police.

## What the works were

Neither is a religious act.

Both are things done at personal risk on the strength of something already believed.

## The verse quoted

*And the Scripture was fulfilled which says, "**Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him
for righteousness**."*

The Greek is **eplērōthē** — was filled full.

The believing was in Genesis 15. The offering was in Genesis 22. Decades apart.

## The order

Faith first, by about thirty years. The works fill it out later.

## What Paul quotes

The identical verse, for the opposite-looking conclusion: *if Abraham was justified by works, he has
something to boast about, **but not before God**.* See the note on Romans 4:1–3.

## The two courtrooms

Paul specifies **pros Theon** — before God.

James specifies *show me*.

They are not answering the same question, and each says which one he is answering.

## The case James is against

*If a brother or sister is naked and destitute of daily food, and one of you says to them, "**Depart
in peace, be warmed and filled**," but you do not give them the things which are needed, what does
it profit?* See the note on James 2:14–17.

That is the faith the letter calls dead: a man with a warm coat, saying a blessing.

## What the letter says at the start

*Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and comes down from the Father of lights.*
See the note on James 1:16–18.

*Of His own will He brought us forth by the word of truth.*

The letter's account of how anyone comes to be alive at all is a birth nobody arranged.

> **The corpse.** He does not call the faith fake. He calls it a body in the right shape with
> nothing moving in it.
