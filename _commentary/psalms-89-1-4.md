title: Love is built to stand forever

The psalm opens with the largest claim in the Psalter and ends in the wreckage of it.

## What he undertakes to do

*I will **sing of the LORD's steadfast love forever**. With my mouth I will make Your
faithfulness known **to all the ages to come**.*

**Chesed** and **emunah** — covenant love and faithfulness. The two words run through all
fifty-two verses as a pair, and they are the psalm's subject.

## The sentence

*"**Love is built to stand forever.** In the heavens You have set Your faithfulness firm."*

The Hebrew is *mercy shall be **built** up forever* — **yibbaneh**, a construction word. He is
describing **chesed** as a structure going up, with the foundations in the sky rather than on
the ground.

## The covenant quoted

*"I have made a covenant with My chosen one. **I have sworn to David My servant, I will make
your line stand forever, and build your throne for all generations.**"*

The promise from 2 Samuel 7, quoted back. See the note on 2 Samuel 7:1–3.

## How the psalm ends

Badly. *But You have cast off and abhorred… You have profaned his crown by casting it to the
ground… **Lord, where are Your former lovingkindnesses, which You swore to David in Your
truth?***

The throne is gone, the walls are broken, and the psalm that opened with *forever* ends by
asking where the promise went. It is the last psalm of Book III and it does not resolve.

## What happened to the promise

*"He will be great, and will be called the Son of the Highest; and the Lord God will **give Him
the throne of His father David**. And He will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and **of
His kingdom there will be no end**."*

Gabriel, in **Luke**, to a girl in Nazareth — answering Psalm 89's closing question about a
thousand years late and in full. The throne, the house of Jacob, and *forever*: every term the
psalm lost is handed back, and it is handed to **Jesus**.

*For **all the promises of God in Him are Yes**, and in Him Amen, to the glory of God through
us.* — **Paul**, on what happens to a promise that looked broken.

## What the psalmist did with a promise he could not see

Sang about it anyway, in the first line, and called it *forever*.

*Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, **the evidence of things not seen**.*

> **How the psalm is built.** It opens on *forever* and closes on *how long?* Between them is
> the covenant with David, quoted in full — and the answer to the closing question was
> delivered by an angel to a village girl.
