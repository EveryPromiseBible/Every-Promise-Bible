Three verses of arithmetic, and every calculation comes out the same way.

## The comparison

*Better is **one day in Your courts than a thousand anywhere else**.*

The Hebrew is **ki tov-yom bachatzerecha me'alef**.

A thousand to one.

## The second

*I would rather **stand at the threshold of the house of my God** than **live at ease in the tents of
the wicked**.*

The Hebrew is **histophef** — a rare verb, occurring once. Probably *to be a doorkeeper* or *to
stand at the threshold*.

## What he is asking for

Not a seat inside. The doorway.

Whatever the least position in the house is, he prefers it to the best position outside.

## The contrast

*The tents of the wicked.*

**Ohalei-resha** — tents of wickedness.

Tents, against a house. Temporary against permanent, and he names the difference.

## The two titles

*For the LORD God is **a sun and a shield**.*

The Hebrew is **shemesh umagen**.

The only place in the Old Testament where God is called a sun. The surrounding nations worshipped
one, and Israel's poetry mostly avoids the word.

**Magen** — shield. The word used to Abram: *I am your shield, your exceedingly great reward*.

## What the pair does

Light and protection. What you see by, and what you get behind.

## The two gifts

*The LORD gives **grace and glory**.*

The Hebrew is **chen vekavod** — favour and weight.

## The promise

*No good thing does He withhold from those who **walk uprightly**.*

The Hebrew is **lo yimna-tov laholechim betamim** — He will not withhold good from those walking in
integrity.

## The construction

A double negative. Not *He gives good things* — *He withholds no good thing*.

The difference matters: it closes the question of what might have been kept back.

## What Paul does with the same shape

*He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all, **how shall He not with Him also
freely give us all things?***

The same argument, from a completed act to what it rules out. See the note on
Romans 8:31–34.

## The last line

*O LORD of hosts, **blessed is the one who trusts in You**.*

The Hebrew is **ashrei adam boteach bach**.

**Ashrei** — the word that opens the Psalter, and the third time this psalm uses it. Verse 4:
*blessed are those who dwell in Your house*. Verse 5: *blessed is the one whose strength is in
You*. And here.

## The three blessings

Those who dwell there. Those on the road. Those who trust.

The psalm blesses the arrived, the travelling, and the ones who have neither.

## Who is speaking

Somebody who is not there.

The whole psalm is written from outside — envying birds in the rafters, describing pilgrims on
the road, and asking for a place in the doorway. See the note on
Psalms 84:1–4.
