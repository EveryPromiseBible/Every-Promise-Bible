Paul has just said no eye has seen it. This is the next sentence.

## What comes before

*What no eye has seen and no ear has heard, what has never risen into the heart of anyone, is
what God has prepared for those who love Him.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 2:6–9.

Three channels closed: observation, report, and imagination.

That verse is usually quoted on its own, as if the point were how unknowable it all is.

## The next word

*But.*

*God has **unveiled these things to us by His Spirit**.*

The Greek is **apekalupsen** — revealed, uncovered. The root of **apokalupsis**.

The passage is not about mystery. It is about how the knowing arrives, and Paul says so
immediately.

## The searching

*For the Spirit **searches everything, even the deep places of God**.*

The Greek is **eraunai** — searches, examines. And **ta bathe tou theou** — the depths of God.

Not searching in order to find out. The word is used of exploring, of going through
thoroughly.

## The analogy

*For **who among men knows what is in a man except the spirit of that man within him**?*

A person's interior is available to one party.

Nobody outside a man can get at his thoughts. He has private access to himself.

## The application

*In the same way, **no one has known what is in God except the Spirit of God**.*

The same closed circle Jesus describes: *no one knows the Son except the Father, and no one
knows the Father except the Son*. See the note on Matthew 11:25–27.

And the same door in it: *and anyone the Son chooses to show Him to*.

## What was received

*And what we have received is **not the spirit of the world but the Spirit who is from God**.*

Two spirits contrasted, and the Corinthians had a great deal of the first — the letter is
mostly about it.

## The purpose

*So that we may **know the things God has freely given us**.*

The Greek is **charisthenta** — the things graciously given. From **charis**, grace.

The stated reason for the Spirit's presence is that the reader should know what has already
been handed over.

Not to acquire anything. To find out what is already in the account.

## The speaking

*These are the very things **we speak, not in words taught by man's wisdom but in words taught
by the Holy Spirit**.*

Paul has already said what his preaching was not: *my speech and my preaching were not with
persuasive words of human wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power*. See the
note on 1 Corinthians 2:1–5.

Corinth paid to hear men speak well. He is telling them why he did not.

## The last clause

*Matching **spiritual things with what is spiritual**.*

The Greek is **pneumatikois pneumatika sunkrinontes** — and it is genuinely ambiguous.

**Sunkrino** can mean *compare*, *combine*, or *interpret*. And the dative can be things or
people.

Three readings survive: comparing spiritual things with spiritual things, explaining spiritual
truths to spiritual people, or expressing spiritual truths in spiritual words.

## What follows

*But the natural man does not receive the things of the Spirit of God, for they are foolishness
to him; nor can he know them, **because they are spiritually discerned**.*

The Greek for *natural* is **psuchikos** — soul-driven. The same word Paul uses of the body
that is sown. See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:42–44.

## The last line of the chapter

*"For who has known the mind of the LORD that he may instruct Him?" **But we have the mind of
Christ**.*

Paul quotes Isaiah's rhetorical question and then answers it.

> **Where the famous verse actually goes.** *No eye has seen* is not the end of the sentence.
> The next word is *but*, and the next clause says the Spirit has already uncovered it.
