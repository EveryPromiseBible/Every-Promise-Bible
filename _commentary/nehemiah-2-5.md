One verse, and it is the request the whole book comes out of.

## What is happening

*And then I answered the king.*

Nehemiah is cupbearer to Artaxerxes — the man who tastes the wine first. It put him in
the king's presence daily and made him, of necessity, someone the king trusted with his
life.

The king has just noticed his face is sad, and Nehemiah says he *was very much afraid*.
Sadness in front of a Persian king was a serious matter.

## The gap between the chapters

The news from Jerusalem came in the month of Chislev. This conversation happens in
Nisan.

Four months. He wept, fasted and prayed through the whole of chapter 1 and said nothing
to the king until asked.

## The half-second

Between the question and this verse there is one clause: *so I prayed to the God of
heaven.*

Standing in front of the king, mid-conversation, with no time to withdraw. It is the
shortest recorded prayer in the Old Testament and nothing of its content is given.

## The wording

*"**If it pleases the king, and if your servant has found favor with you**."*

Careful, formal court language. He does not demand and he does not explain his
theology to a Persian.

## What he asks for

*"**Send me to Judah**."*

Not permission to resign. Not funds. He asks to be sent — to go on the king's business,
with the king's authority behind him.

## How he names the place

*"To **the city where my ancestors are buried**."*

He does not say Jerusalem. He says the city of his fathers' tombs.

Persian religion held the graves of ancestors in high regard, and a king could
understand a man wanting to repair a family burial ground even if he had no interest in
the God of Israel.

Nehemiah says something entirely true in terms his hearer would feel.

## The purpose

*"**So that I may rebuild it**."*

Stated flat, at the end, with nothing after it.

## What he was actually asking

The rebuilding of Jerusalem's walls had been stopped by royal decree, in this same
king's reign, recorded in Ezra 4 — *give the command to make these men cease, that this
city may not be built.*

He is asking Artaxerxes to reverse Artaxerxes.

## What he gets

The king asks how long he will be gone, and Nehemiah *set him a time*. He had the
answer ready.

Then letters to the governors, timber from the king's forest, and an escort of army
officers and horsemen.

He had thought through the whole logistics of it during four months of praying.

> **What he says about the outcome.** *And the king granted them to me, **according to
> the good hand of my God upon me**.* The letters are in his bag and the credit goes
> elsewhere. See the note on Nehemiah 2:9–10.
