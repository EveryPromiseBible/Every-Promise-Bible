He says something hard, they are shocked, and He says it again.

## What has just happened

A man ran up and knelt: *good Teacher, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life?*

*Then Jesus, looking at him, **loved him***, and said *one thing you lack: go your way, sell
whatever you have and give to the poor*.

*But he was sad at this word, and went away sorrowful, **for he had great possessions**.* See the
note on Mark 10:17–22.

Mark alone records the look and the love.

## The look

*Jesus **looked around at His disciples** and said.*

The Greek is **periblepsamenos** — looked all round.

Mark uses this verb of Jesus five times. He watches the room before He speaks.

## The first statement

*"**How hard it is for people with riches to enter the kingdom of God**."*

The Greek is **duskolos** — with difficulty.

## The reaction

*They were **stunned** to hear it.*

The Greek is **ethambounto** — amazed, astounded.

In their world, wealth was evidence of God's favour. Deuteronomy could be read that way. A rich
man was assumed to be the likeliest candidate, not the least.

Jesus has just inverted the odds.

## The repetition

*So **He said it again**.*

He does not soften it or explain it away. Mark records that He restated it.

## The address

*"**Children**."*

The Greek is **tekna**.

He uses it once in Mark, here, immediately after saying something that has frightened them.

## The second statement

*"**How hard it is for people who lean on their money** to get into the kingdom of God."*

The Greek here in most manuscripts is **tous pepoithotas epi chremasin** — those who have put
their trust in riches.

The second statement is the first one with the mechanism named. Not the having. The leaning.

## The image

*"**It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle** than for a rich man to enter the
kingdom of God."*

The largest animal in Palestine, and the smallest hole anybody handled daily.

There is a medieval suggestion that a gate in Jerusalem was called the Needle's Eye. There is no
evidence such a gate existed, and the disciples' reaction shows they heard it as impossible, not
as difficult.

## The reaction again

*That **astonished them even more**.*

The Greek is **perissos exeplessonto** — exceedingly struck out of themselves.

## The question

*"**Then who can possibly be saved?**"*

Which is the right conclusion. If the man with every advantage cannot, nobody can.

## The answer

*Jesus **looked right at them** and said, "**For people it is impossible. But not for God. With God,
everything is possible**."*

The Greek is **emblepsas autois** — looking straight at them. The same verb Mark used of the look
He gave the rich man.

## What the answer does

It does not lower the bar. It grants the impossibility and moves the agent.

*Para anthropois adunaton, all' ou para Theo* — with men impossible, but not with God.

## What Peter says next

*"See, **we have left all and followed You**."*

And the answer: *there is no one who has left house or brothers or sisters or father or mother or
wife or children or lands, for My sake and the gospel's, **who shall not receive a hundredfold now
in this time** — houses and brothers and sisters and mothers and children and lands, **with
persecutions** — and in the age to come, eternal life.* See the note on
Mark 10:28–31.

The list of what is received leaves out one item that was in the list of what was left: a father.

And *with persecutions* is inside the promise, not after it.

## The last line

*"But many who are first will be last, and the last first."*

> **The second saying.** The first names the category, the second names the mechanism: *those who
> lean on their money*. And the disciples' answer to both is *then who can be saved?*
