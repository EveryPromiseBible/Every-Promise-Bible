The first of the seven woes, and the charge is about a door.

## What they did with it

*"You **shut the door of the kingdom of heaven in people's faces**. You will not
go in yourselves, and you will not let anyone else go in either."*

The image is of men standing in a doorway. Not outside it and not inside it —
blocking it.

Luke's version says they *took away the key of knowledge*.

## Who this is said to

Teachers of the law and Pharisees. The most careful, most scrupulous, most
observant men in the nation.

The people who shut the kingdom are not the ones you would expect to. They are the
religious experts, and what shuts it is what they preach.

## "Pretenders"

The Greek is **hupokrites** — an actor, one who speaks from under a mask. It was
the ordinary word for a stage performer before it became an insult.

Jesus uses it seven times in this chapter.

## The widows

*"You **eat up widows' houses**, and to cover it you make long prayers."*

Two things stacked: what they take, and what they use to hide it.

The Greek for *cover* is a pretext, a screen. The praying is the cover story.

## The convert

*"You cross land and sea to win a single convert, and when you have won him, **you
make him twice as fit for hell as you are**."*

The effort is real — land and sea, for one person. Nobody accuses them of laziness.

What they hand him at the end of it is the problem. A man taught to stand on his
own performance ends up further from the door than the man who never went looking.

## Where the same thing happens in Job

Three men travelled to sit with a friend in ashes, and what they preached to him —
experience, tradition, a formula — produced bitterness and a defence rather than
rest. And God's verdict on all three was *you have not spoken of Me what is
right.* See the notes on Job 4:7–11, Job 8:8–19 and Job 11:13–15.

Preaching that puts a man back on his own resources shuts the door on him,
whatever it costs the preacher to deliver it.

> **How the chapter ends.** After seven woes: *Jerusalem, Jerusalem… how often I
> have longed to gather your children together.* The harshest chapter in the
> Gospels finishes in tears over the city, and with the word *until*. See the note
> on Matthew 23:37–39.
