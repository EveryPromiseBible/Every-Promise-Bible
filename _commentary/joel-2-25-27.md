A promise to restore something nobody can restore.

## What is restored

*"I will **restore to you the years** the locusts have eaten."*

Not the crops. The **years**.

Property can be replaced and a harvest can be regrown. Time is the one commodity in
the passage that cannot be handed back, and it is the one named.

## The four

*"The **swarming** locust and the **hopping** locust, the **destroying** locust and
the **gnawing** locust."*

Four Hebrew words, probably stages of the same insect's life cycle, and Joel opens
his book with them in the reverse order: *what the chewing locust left, the swarming
locust has eaten; what the swarming locust left, the crawling locust has eaten.*

Wave after wave, each finishing what the last one started, until nothing is left.

## Whose army it was

*"**My great army that I sent among you.**"*

Joel does not soften it. The locusts are called God's army, and He says He sent them.

The same passage that promises restoration takes responsibility for the damage.

## The reversal

*"You will have **plenty to eat and be satisfied**."*

The Hebrew is *eat in plenty and be satisfied* — and the book opened with a land
stripped bare, the grain offering cut off from the house of the LORD, and the cattle
groaning because there was no pasture.

## Said twice

*"**Never again will My people be put to shame.**"*

Verse 26 and verse 27, in the same words. Joel repeats it as a bracket.

## The knowing

*"Then you will know that **I am in the midst of Israel**, that I am the LORD your
God, and there is no other."*

The restoration has a purpose beyond the food, and it is stated: so that they will
know where He is.

## What comes immediately after

*"And it shall come to pass afterward that **I will pour out My Spirit on all
flesh**; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams,
your young men shall see visions."*

Peter stands up at Pentecost and quotes exactly that: *this is what was spoken by the
prophet Joel.*

The locust chapter runs straight into the outpouring, and the New Testament dates the
fulfilment to a morning in Jerusalem.

## What the book asked for

Between the locusts and the promise, Joel's call: *"Now, therefore," says the LORD,
"turn to Me with all your heart… **so rend your heart, and not your garments**."*

Tearing your clothes was the visible sign of grief. He asks for the thing the sign
stood for.

> **The reason given for the whole promise.** *For He is gracious and merciful, slow
> to anger, and of great kindness.* Joel quotes God's own self-description from Exodus
> 34 as the grounds for turning back. See the note on Exodus 34:5–7.
