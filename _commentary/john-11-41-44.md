He prays out loud and explains, in the middle of the prayer, why He is doing it out loud.

## The stone

*So they **took the stone away** from the place where the dead man had been laid.*

They did it. Martha's objection about the smell is in the previous paragraph, and the stone
still comes off. See the note on John 11:38–40.

## The prayer

*And Jesus **lifted His eyes** and said, "**Father, I thank You that You have heard Me**."*

Past tense. He thanks Him for something already done, standing in front of a sealed-then-open
tomb with nothing yet visible.

No request is recorded. The prayer that is answered is not in the text.

## The aside

*"I knew that **You always hear Me**, but **I said it for the sake of the people standing
here**, so that they would believe that You sent Me."*

He explains the prayer while praying it.

The one thing He wants them to conclude is not that He is powerful. It is *that You sent Me*
— which is the argument of John's whole Gospel.

## The shout

*He called out **in a loud voice**, "**Lazarus, come out!**"*

The Greek is **kraugasen phone megale** — cried with a great voice.

The old preachers' remark is worth keeping: had He not said the name, the whole graveyard
would have come.

And He says as much elsewhere: *the hour is coming in which all who are in the graves will
hear His voice and come forth*.

## What came out

*And **the man who had been dead came out**.*

John's phrasing is deliberately awkward. Not *Lazarus came out* — the dead man came out.

## How he came out

*His **hands and feet still bound in strips of linen**, and a **cloth still wrapped around
his face**.*

Wound in grave bands, ankles and wrists tied, a napkin over the head.

He walked, or hopped, or was somehow moved. John does not explain.

## The second instruction

*"**Untie him**," Jesus said, "**and let him go**."*

The Greek is **lusate auton kai aphete hupagein**.

Two instructions in the whole episode, and both are given to bystanders. Move the stone.
Take the wrappings off.

The raising is His. The stone and the cloths are theirs.

## The name

**Lazarus** — the Greek form of **Eleazar**, which means *God has helped*.

## The other tomb

At His own, the cloths are left behind: *the handkerchief that had been around His head, not
lying with the linen cloths, but **folded together in a place by itself***.

Lazarus came out wearing his. Jesus left His in the tomb.

One was raised to die again. The other was not.

## What it cost

*Then many of the Jews who had come to Mary, and had seen the things Jesus did, believed in
Him. But some of them went away to the Pharisees.*

And the council: *if we let Him alone like this, everyone will believe in Him.*

Then Caiaphas: *it is expedient for us that one man should die for the people* — and John
notes he *did not say this on his own authority*, but prophesied.

The seventh sign in John's Gospel is the one that gets Him killed.

## Six days later

*Then, six days before the Passover, Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was who had been
dead… **there they made Him a supper**; and Martha served, but Lazarus was one of those who
sat at the table with Him.*

Martha serving, and Lazarus at dinner. And Mary with the alabaster jar.

> **The two instructions.** *Take the stone away* and *untie him*. Everything in between was
> His, and both of the things He asked for were things hands could do.
