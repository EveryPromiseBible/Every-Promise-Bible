Paul in front of the Athenian council, and he opens with something he found on a walk.

## Where he is

*Paul stood up **in the middle of the Areopagus**.*

Mars Hill — the council that oversaw religion and morals in Athens. It had brought
Socrates to trial four centuries earlier on a charge of introducing new gods.

Paul is there on the same charge in substance: *he seems to be a proclaimer of foreign
gods*.

## Why he came

The verses before: *while Paul waited for them at Athens, **his spirit was provoked within
him** when he saw that the city was given over to idols.*

The Greek is **paroxuneto** — the sharp-stirring word, the root of *paroxysm*.

## The opening

*"Men of Athens, I see that **in every way you are an unusually god-fearing people**."*

The Greek is **deisidaimonesterous** — a word that can be read as *very religious* or as
*superstitious*, and Paul may have chosen it because it can carry both.

He begins with an observation about them rather than an accusation.

## What he had been doing

*"As I was walking through your city and **looking carefully at the things you worship**."*

He had studied the shrines before speaking.

## The find

*"I even found an altar with this inscription: **TO THE UNKNOWN GOD**."*

Greek writers record altars in Athens *to unknown gods*, raised to cover any deity who had
been overlooked.

## The move

*"**The One you worship without knowing Him** — that is **the One I am telling you
about**."*

Not *your gods are wrong*. He takes their own admission of ignorance and fills it.

The Greek is *what you worship in ignorance, this I proclaim to you*.

## What he says about God

*"The God who **made the world and everything in it**, being **Lord of heaven and earth**,
**does not live in temples built by hands**."*

He is standing below the Parthenon.

Stephen had said the same at his trial — *the Most High does not dwell in temples made
with hands* — and Paul had been holding the coats.

## The second claim

*"Nor is He **served by human hands as though He needed anything**."*

The Greek is **prosdeomenos** — as though needing something in addition.

The whole ancient sacrificial economy assumed the gods needed feeding. Paul removes the
premise.

## The direction

*"Since **it is He who gives to everyone life and breath and all things**."*

Nothing goes up. Everything comes down.

## How the speech goes on

He quotes two Greek poets — *in Him we live and move and have our being*, and *we are also
His offspring* — and argues from them that God cannot be an image of gold or silver.

No Old Testament quotation in the whole address, which is unlike every other sermon in
Acts.

## Where it lands

*"Truly, these times of ignorance God overlooked, but **now commands all men everywhere to
repent**, because He has appointed a day on which He will judge the world in righteousness
by the Man whom He has ordained. He has given assurance of this to all **by raising Him
from the dead**."*

## The reaction

*And when they heard of the resurrection of the dead, **some mocked**, while others said,
"We will hear you again on this matter."*

*However, **some men joined him and believed**, among them Dionysius the Areopagite, a
woman named Damaris, and others with them.*

A member of the council, and a named woman.

## Where he went next

*After these things Paul departed from Athens and went to Corinth* — where he says he
arrived *in weakness, and in fear, and in much trembling*, determined to know nothing but
Christ crucified. See the note on 1 Corinthians 2:1–5.

> **What he starts from.** An inscription admitting ignorance. Paul does not argue them
> out of their religion — he points at the one altar in the city that was left blank and
> says he has come to fill it in.
