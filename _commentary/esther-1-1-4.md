The book opens on the most powerful man on earth, and God is not mentioned. He
will not be mentioned anywhere in the ten chapters that follow.

## What is missing

There is no name of God in Esther. No prayer is recorded, no temple, no
sacrifice, no prophet, no miracle, no angel, no law quoted.

That absence is the book. It is the story of a people God had told, through
Hosea, *you are not My people* — and it is set in exactly that season, when His
face was hidden and His hand was not.

He is not absent from the story. He is not named in it. There is a difference,
and the whole of Esther is written to show it.

## The empire

*From India all the way to Cush, a hundred and twenty-seven provinces.*

Ahasuerus is Xerxes. The reach described is the largest empire the world had seen
to that point, and the writer measures it in provinces rather than miles.

The Jews are somewhere inside it, scattered, with no land, no king and no temple
to go to.

## A hundred and eighty days

*For a long while — a full **hundred and eighty days** — he laid out before them
the wealth of his glorious kingdom.*

Six months of display. The verb is to show, to make visible; the whole of it is
about being seen.

Then a further seven-day feast for everybody in Susa, which the next verses
describe down to the marble pillars and the silver couches.

## What the world looks like at the start

Every visible thing belongs to Ahasuerus. The wealth is his, the law is his, the
army is his, and by the end of chapter 3 the life of every Jew in the empire is
his to sign away.

That is where the book starts, and it starts there deliberately. The reader is
meant to look at all of it and ask the question the whole book answers.

## The God who is not mentioned

Esther never names Him. Not once in ten chapters — no prayer recorded, no miracle, no prophet,
no temple, no law quoted.

And nothing in the book happens by accident.

*And we know that **all things work together for good** to those who love God, to those who
are the called according to His purpose.*

**Paul**'s sentence is the thesis of a book that never mentions its subject.

> **The name hidden in the text.** Four times in the Hebrew of Esther the letters
> of the name **YHWH** stand at the start of four consecutive words, spelled out
> forwards twice and backwards twice. And once, in Esther 7:5, the letters of
> **I AM** — *ehyeh* — are formed the same way, in the sentence where the king
> demands to know who has dared to do this thing. The name is in the book. It is
> written the way He was working: present, and not announced.
