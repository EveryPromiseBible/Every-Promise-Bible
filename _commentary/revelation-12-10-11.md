A loud voice in heaven announces a defeat, and it names both what the enemy did
for a living and what put him out of business.

## His job description

*The one who accused our brothers… the one who stood in front of our God accusing
them, day and night.*

That is what he was doing. Not tempting, in this passage — **accusing**, in
front of God, without stopping. The word behind it is a courtroom word, and it is
where the title Satan comes from: the accuser, the adversary in a legal sense.

Job 1 and Zechariah 3 both show him at that work. Zechariah's scene is the
clearest: the high priest standing in filthy clothes with Satan at his right hand
to accuse him — and God rebuking the accuser and re-clothing the man.

## What beat him

Three things, and the order is worth keeping:

- **the Lamb's blood** — the ground of it. Accusation has nothing to work with
  once the charge has been answered.
- **the message they told** — their testimony, spoken. What was true was said
  out loud.
- **they would not cling to their own lives even with death in front of them** —
  people who had stopped being controlled by the last threat available.

The first is what was done for them. The second and third are what they did with
it.

## "Now it has arrived"

*Salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of His
Christ.* The announcement of the accuser's fall is made in the same breath as
the announcement that the kingdom has come.

The two are treated as one event.

> **Where the accusing stopped.** Romans asks *who is going to lay a charge
> against the ones God Himself has chosen?* and answers by pointing at the one at
> God's right hand — not accusing, but interceding. Revelation shows the same
> thing from the other side: the seat the accuser used to occupy is empty. See
> the note on Romans 8:31–34.
