A birth, and then in the same breath a throne — and everything in between is missing.

## What is not in the verse

*She gave birth to a son… and her child was **snatched up to God, and to His throne**.*

Between those two clauses lie the manger, Nazareth, the Jordan, Galilee, Gethsemane, the cross and
the empty tomb. John says none of it.

That is because chapter twelve is telling the story from the dragon's side of it. He is standing in
front of the woman *to devour her Child as soon as it was born*, and from where he stands the whole
account is: the child came, and the child was taken to the throne, and he could not stop either.

The cross is not omitted because it does not matter. It is omitted because from the enemy's point of
view it did not work.

## The verb

**Herpasthe** — snatched, seized, carried off by force. It is the word Paul uses of the church:
*then we who are alive and remain shall be **caught up** together with them in the clouds*, and the
word he uses of his own experience of being *caught up to the third heaven*.

A word for something sudden and irresistible, and it is used here of an ascension.

## The rod

*A boy who is going to **herd all the nations with an iron rod**.*

Psalm 2, and the verb is **poimanei** — from **poimen**, shepherd. He will *shepherd* the nations
with a rod of iron.

The two halves of that image belong together and are usually pulled apart. A shepherd's rod is for
the flock's protection, and the reason it is iron is that nothing gets past it.

The same Person says *I am the good shepherd; the good shepherd gives His life for the sheep.*

## A place already made ready

*The woman ran off into the desert, to **a place God had already made ready for her**.*

**Hetoimasmenon** — a perfect passive participle. Prepared, and standing prepared, before she needed
to run.

And she is fed there for the whole time — which is what a desert is for in this book's memory. A
nation was fed in one for forty years with bread that came down every morning, and the Man who was
taken up to the throne said *I am the bread of life* about it.

> **He had somewhere ready before the running started.** The child was not rescued from the throne's
> reach — He was taken to it. And the woman is not told to survive; she is carried to a prepared
> place and fed there. Nothing in this chapter is improvised, including the part that looks like
> flight.
