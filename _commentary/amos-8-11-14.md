The prophet names a shortage of an unusual commodity.

## Who is speaking

Amos was a herdsman from Tekoa and a tender of sycamore figs, not a professional
prophet — he says so himself: *I was no prophet, nor was I a son of a prophet.*

He is preaching in the northern kingdom during a boom. Amos 6 describes ivory beds,
lambs from the flock, wine by the bowlful.

## The announcement

*"The days are coming," declares the Lord GOD, "when **I will send a famine over the
land**."*

Famine is one of the standard judgments. He then withdraws the usual meaning.

## What kind

*"**Not a hunger for bread, nor a thirst for water**, but **a hunger to hear the words of
the LORD**."*

The Hebrew is *not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of
the LORD*.

## The searching

*"People will **stagger from sea to sea** and **wander from north to east**, **searching
everywhere for the word of the LORD**, and **they will not find it**."*

The Hebrew for *stagger* is the verb for reeling, wandering like a drunk.

Three directions and no result. The last clause is the whole point: not that nobody
looks, but that looking does not work.

## The direction they do not go

North and east, and sea to sea — and not south. Jerusalem, the temple, and the place
Amos himself had come from are south.

## What made it a famine

Not that God stopped speaking. Amos 2 has already recorded the complaint: *I raised up
some of your sons as prophets… but you gave the Nazirites wine to drink, and commanded
the prophets saying, "Do not prophesy!"*

And in chapter 7 the priest of Bethel tells Amos to leave: *go, flee to the land of
Judah… but never again prophesy at Bethel.*

The famine is the granting of a request.

## Who faints

*"The **lovely young women and the strong young men** will faint from thirst."*

The strongest and the healthiest. A famine takes them last, which is how you know how bad
it is.

## What they had been swearing by

*"Those who **swear by the shame of Samaria**, who say, '**As surely as your god lives,
Dan**,' and, '**As surely as the way of Beersheba lives**.'"*

Three shrines. Dan had one of Jeroboam's golden calves; Beersheba was a pilgrimage site
in the far south.

They are not irreligious. They have three functioning centres of worship and a famine of
the word at the same time.

## What Amos had already said about their services

*"I hate, I despise your feast days… Take away from Me the noise of your songs… **But let
justice run down like water**, and righteousness like a mighty stream."*

## What the New Testament says stands

*The grass dries out. The flower drops off. **But what the Lord says stands, and keeps
standing**.* See the note on 1 Peter 1:24–25.

> **What is being taken away.** Not the buildings, the songs, or the pilgrimages. Those
> stay. What goes is the word — and Amos says people will cross the country looking for
> it.
