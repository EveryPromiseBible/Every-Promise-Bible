Three questions and an answer, and the writer makes the reader do the work.

## The method

The passage is a cross-examination. Each question has an obvious answer and the writer
supplies it, and the effect is that the conclusion is drawn rather than announced.

## The first

*"**Some of them heard and pushed Him to it anyway** — though **not all of the ones Moses
brought out of Egypt did**."*

The exception matters. Caleb and Joshua came out of Egypt and went in.

The writer is careful not to say the whole generation.

## The second

*"And **who was it He was sick of for forty years?**"*

*"Was it not **the ones who sinned, the ones whose bodies dropped in the desert?**"*

The Greek is *whose carcasses fell in the wilderness* — the phrase from Numbers 14.

Forty years of walking, and a funeral every few days.

## The third

*"And **who did He swear about, that they would never enter His rest?**"*

*"**The ones who refused to believe**."*

The Greek is **apeithesasin** — those who disobeyed, who refused to be persuaded.

It is a word about the will rather than the intellect. The same root John uses: *whoever
refuses to believe the Son will not see life*. See the note on John 3:31–36.

## The conclusion

*"So we can see it plainly: **they could not get in. Unbelief was what kept them out**."*

The Greek is *because of unbelief* — **di' apistian**.

One cause is named, and it is not the giants, the walls, or the Anakim.

They had accurate intelligence. All twelve spies agreed on the facts. See the note on
Numbers 13:26–33.

## What the writer does with it

He turns it on the reader immediately. The next verse: *therefore, since a promise remains
of entering His rest, **let us fear lest any of you seem to have come short of it**.* See the
note on Hebrews 4:1–3.

## The diagnosis he gives there

*The good news came to us **just as it came to them**. But **hearing it did them no good,
because it never mixed with any faith** in the ones who heard it.*

The Greek is **sunkerannumi** — blended, compounded into one substance.

Same message, same preaching. What was missing was the thing that combines with it.

## What the wilderness generation actually did

Stood at Kadesh, heard twelve reports, listened to ten, and refused.

Numbers records what they said: *let us select a leader and return to Egypt.*

And then, told the sentence, they changed their minds and went up anyway — and were beaten,
because *the ark of the covenant of the LORD and Moses did not depart from the camp*.

## The other kind of unbelief

The writer's warning is not aimed at people who never heard. It is aimed at people who
heard, were on the road, and stopped at the border.

## Where the argument lands

*There remains therefore a **Sabbath rest** for the people of God.* See the note on
Hebrews 4:8–11.

The offer outlives the generation that missed it.

> **The one cause the writer names.** Not the opposition and not the difficulty of the
> land. He asks three questions with obvious answers and then says it in four words:
> unbelief was what kept them out.
