Paul gives a young man three verbs and one title.

## The title

*But you, **man of God**.*

The Greek is **anthrope theou**.

It is the standing Old Testament title for a prophet — Moses, Samuel, Elijah, Elisha. Paul
uses it of Timothy, twice in the two letters.

## The first verb

*Run away from all of it.*

The Greek is **pheuge** — flee. The root of *fugitive*.

**All of it** — what he has just described: men who *suppose that godliness is a means of
gain*, and the love of money as a root everything grows out of. See the note on
1 Timothy 6:9–10.

Paul's counsel there is not to resist. It is to leave.

He says the same about immorality — *flee sexual immorality* — and about idolatry.

## The second verb

*Chase after what is right.*

The Greek is **dioke** — pursue, hunt down. The word used of persecutors chasing their
target.

He uses the same verb of love: *pursue love*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 14:1–5.

Run from one thing, run after another. Both are running.

## The five

*After **what is right**, after **a life turned toward God**, after **faith**, **love**,
**endurance**, **gentleness**.*

Six in the Greek: righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, meekness.

The last is **praupathian** — gentleness of spirit. It occurs once in the New Testament.

## The third

*Fight **the good fight that faith fights**.*

The Greek is **agonizou ton kalon agona tes pisteos** — a triple play on the same root.
**Agon** is a contest, a struggle in the arena; the root of *agony*.

Not a battle. A contest, with a prize at the end.

## The fourth

*Take hold of **the life that lasts**.*

The Greek is **epilabou** — seize, grasp firmly. The same verb used of Jesus taking the
sinking Peter by the hand, and of Him taking the man with dropsy by the arm. See the note
on Luke 14:1–6.

## What it is

*The life **you were called into**, the life **you spoke for so well in front of many
witnesses**.*

Two clauses about the past. He was called, and he made a public confession.

The taking hold is of something already given and already professed.

## What follows

*I urge you in the sight of God **who gives life to all things**, and before Christ Jesus
**who witnessed the good confession before Pontius Pilate**.*

Timothy's confession before witnesses, and Christ's before Pilate, in one sentence.

## Who Timothy was

A young man Paul had picked up at Lystra, son of a Jewish mother and a Greek father, left
in charge at Ephesus — the city where Paul had warned that *savage wolves will come in
among you*. See the note on Acts 20:28–31.

The second letter tells him *let no one despise your youth*, and *stir up the gift of God
which is in you*.

## Where the chapter ends

*Command those who are rich in this present age **not to be haughty, nor to trust in
uncertain riches but in the living God, who gives us richly all things to enjoy**.* See the
note on 1 Timothy 6:17–19.

The chapter warns about wanting money and ends by telling the wealthy to enjoy what they
have and give it away.

> **The four verbs.** Flee, pursue, contend, take hold. Three of them are about motion, and
> the last one is about something that was already handed over.
