Four things said about Abraham in two verses, and each one is worth its own line.

## He did not waver

*He **did not waver at God's promise in unbelief**.*

The Greek is **diakrino** — the same verb behind *discerning the body* in
1 Corinthians 11 and *does not doubt* in Mark 11. It means to be divided, to be in
two minds.

He was not split. He was not holding the promise in one hand and the arithmetic of
his own age in the other.

## He grew strong

*He **grew strong in faith**.*

The Greek is a passive — he was strengthened. Something was done to him.

And note the direction. Twenty-four years passed between the first promise and this
chapter, and his faith did not wear down over them; it was built up.

## He gave glory to God

*And **gave glory to God**.*

Before the son. Before any evidence at all. Glory is given for something not yet
received.

## He was fully convinced

*Fully convinced that **what God had promised, God was able to do**.*

The Greek is **plerophoreo** — carried full, filled to capacity.

And look at what he was convinced of. Not that it would be easy, or when it would
happen, or how. That God was **able**.

## The verdict

*That is why **it was credited to him as rightness**.*

Paul comes back to Genesis 15:6, which is the sentence this whole chapter has been
unpacking. See the note on Genesis 15:6.

Righteousness was entered on his account — and the thing entered against it was
believing that God could do what God said.

## What was not on the ledger

Not his obedience, which was real but patchy. Not his record — he lied about his
wife to two kings, and took Hagar because the promise seemed slow.

The chapter's whole argument is that this is the point: *to him who does not work
but believes on Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is accounted for
righteousness.*

**Justifies the ungodly.** Paul's phrase, three verses into the chapter.

> **Who else it was written for.** *Now it was not written for his sake alone that
> it was imputed to him, but for us also.* Paul's next sentence. The ledger entry
> made in Genesis 15 was recorded as a precedent, and the account it applies to is
> the reader's. See the note on Romans 4:23–25.
