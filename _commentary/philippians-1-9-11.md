---
key: Philippians 1:9–11
title: "A prayer with no orders in it"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul opens the letter by telling the Philippians what he wants for them — and every single item is
addressed to God rather than to them.

## Love with judgement in it

*That your love may overflow more and more, into **real knowledge and every kind of insight**.*

**Epignosis** — knowledge that has come to rest on its object. And **aisthesis** — sense-perception,
the trained faculty that can tell one thing from another. It occurs nowhere else in the New
Testament.

Paul will not let love be a mood. He asks for love that knows things and can tell the difference —
which is what love has to become if it is going to be any use in a real church with real
disagreements in it.

## Between good and best

*So that you may **weigh what truly matters and settle on the best**.*

**Dokimazein ta diapheronta** — to test the things that differ. The hard choices in a Christian life
are rarely between good and evil; they are between two goods, and only one of them is yours.

*And so be **genuine and clear of stumbling** all the way to the day of Christ.* **Eilikrineis** —
sincere, and the old sense of it is *judged in sunlight*, held up to the window with nothing
concealed. **Aproskopoi** — not tripping, and not tripping anybody else.

## Where the fruit comes from

*Filled full with that harvest of righteousness **which comes through Jesus Christ**.*

Paul plants the source inside his own prayer, in case anybody thought the Philippians were expected
to grow this themselves.

The fruit is *of* righteousness — it grows out of a righteousness already possessed, and that
righteousness came by gift: *those who receive abundance of grace and of the **gift** of
righteousness will reign in life through the One, Jesus Christ.*

Nobody produces a harvest by wanting one. You get a harvest by having the right thing in the ground,
and Paul says what is in the ground was put there by Christ.

## Not one command

Read it again and look for an imperative. There is none. Every verb is something Paul asks God to
do: that your love may abound, that you may approve, that you may be filled.

He is writing to a church he loves, about the qualities he most wants to see in them, and he does
not ask them for any of it. He asks Him.

> **To the glory and praise of God.** That is where the prayer ends, and it is the reason the whole
> thing is addressed upward. If the Philippians had produced this themselves the credit would have
> had to go somewhere else. Paul asks God for it so that when it appears, everybody knows who grew
> it — *He who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ.*
