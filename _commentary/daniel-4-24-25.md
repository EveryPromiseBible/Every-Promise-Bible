---
key: Daniel 4:24–25
title: "Until you know"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
A prophet reads a king his sentence, and the last clause is the reason for the whole thing.

## Who is delivering it

*"This is the meaning, O king, and this is the decree of the Most High **that has come upon my lord
the king**."*

Daniel calls him *my lord the king* while telling him he is going to eat grass.

The verse before this: *Daniel… was astonished for a time, and his thoughts troubled him. So the
king spoke, and said, "Belteshazzar, do not let the dream or its interpretation trouble you."*
Daniel answers, *my lord, **may the dream concern those who hate you**.*

The prophet does not want to say it. The pagan king has to reassure the prophet.

## The sentence

*"You will be **driven away from among men**… you will **eat grass like an ox**, and you will be
**wet with the dew of heaven**."*

Every clause is a removal of something human: company, food, shelter.

## The clause it is all for

*"**Until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdoms of men and gives them to whomever He
will**."*

The Aramaic is **ad di-tinda** — until you know.

The whole sentence has a terminus and the terminus is knowledge. It is not punishment with a fixed
term; it is described as running until something is understood.

*"For **I desire mercy and not sacrifice, and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings**."*

*"And this is eternal life, **that they may know You**, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom You
have sent."*

## What Daniel says next

*"Therefore, O king, let my advice be acceptable to you: **break off your sins by being righteous,
and your iniquities by showing mercy to the poor**. Perhaps there may be a lengthening of your
prosperity."*

The prophet, unasked, tells him how to try to avert it — and the specific remedy he names is
kindness to the poor.

*"Pure and undefiled religion before God and the Father is this: **to visit orphans and widows in
their trouble**."*

## The stump

*"**Leave the stump and roots in the earth**, bound with a band of iron and bronze."*

The tree is cut down and the root is deliberately left. The sentence has recovery built into its
own imagery before it is carried out.

*"For **there is hope for a tree**, if it is cut down, that it will sprout again, and that its
tender shoots will not cease… **at the scent of water it will bud** and bring forth branches like a
plant."*

Job 14, and it is the same image used as an argument for hope.

*There shall come forth **a Rod from the stem of Jesse**, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots.*

Isaiah's stump is the house of David after it has been felled, and what grows out of it is
**Christ**.

## The one who came down voluntarily

Nebuchadnezzar is brought down until he knows who rules.

*Who, being in the form of God… **made Himself of no reputation**, taking the form of a
bondservant… He **humbled Himself** and became obedient to the point of death.*

*"He has **put down the mighty from their thrones**, and exalted the lowly."*

**Luke** 1, in the Magnificat, sung by a girl who had been told her son would have a throne — and
the sentence she chooses is Daniel's.

> **The terminus.** *Until you know.* The sentence is not measured in years in the first instance
> but in understanding, and the prophet delivering it begs the king to avoid it by being kind to
> the poor.
