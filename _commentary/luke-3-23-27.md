A genealogy, and three things about **Luke**'s are unlike **Matthew**'s.

## The hedge

*"**Everyone took Him for the son of Joseph.**"*

The Greek is **hos enomizeto** — as was supposed, as was reckoned.

**Luke** has just spent two chapters on the virgin birth. He puts the qualifier in the first line of
the list so the legal descent and the actual conception are not confused.

## The direction

**Matthew** runs forward — Abraham begot Isaac, Isaac begot Jacob — and stops at Joseph.

**Luke** runs backwards, and does not stop at Abraham.

*"…the son of Enosh, the son of Seth, **the son of Adam, the son of God**."*

Seventy-seven names, ending at the first man and then at God. **Matthew** is writing to Jews and
starts at Abraham; **Luke** is writing to a Gentile and goes back to the point where everybody is
included.

*And He has made from one blood **every nation of men** to dwell on all the face of the earth.*

*For as in Adam all die, even so **in Christ all shall be made alive**.*

*The first man Adam became a living being. **The last Adam** became a life-giving spirit.*

**Paul** puts the two ends of **Luke**'s list together, and the genealogy is the reason the comparison
works: the line runs from Adam to Him and stops.

## The thirty years

*"**Jesus was about thirty years old when He began.**"*

The age a Levite entered the service of the tabernacle. The age Joseph stood before Pharaoh, and
David became king.

Nothing is recorded of eighteen of those years except one afternoon in the temple at twelve, and
*Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and men.*

## Why there are two lists

**Matthew**'s runs through Solomon; **Luke**'s through Nathan, another son of David. The oldest
explanation, and the most economical, is that **Matthew** gives Joseph's line and **Luke** gives
Mary's, with Joseph named as Heli's son by marriage.

Either way both arrive at David, which is what the promise required.

*"**And the Lord God will give Him the throne of His father David.**"*

> **Where Luke stops.** Not at Abraham. *The son of Adam, the son of God* — and it is the same
> evangelist who quotes Isaiah as far as *all flesh shall see the salvation of God*.
