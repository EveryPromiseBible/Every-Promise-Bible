A carpenter tells a professional fisherman where the fish are, at the wrong time
of day.

## Everything about it is wrong

Fishing on that lake was night work, with nets, in the shallows. Jesus says
daytime, deep water.

And Simon has just spent the entire night proving there was nothing there.

## What Simon says

*"Master, **we worked hard all night long and came up with nothing at all**."*

He states his case first, and it is a good one. Experience, effort, and the
evidence of the last twelve hours all agree.

*"**But because You say so**, I will let the nets down."*

The Greek is *at Your word*. That is the entire reason given, and it is set
against everything he knows about his own trade.

## Net, singular

Simon says he will let down *the net* — one. Jesus had said *let your **nets**
down*, plural.

Luke keeps the difference. And the next sentence is *the **nets** began to tear
apart in their hands.*

He went out with less than he was told to take.

## What came up

*Such an **enormous shoal** of fish that the nets began to tear.*

The same water. The same lake he had swept all night. The fish were not brought
from somewhere else.

## Both boats

*They waved to their partners in the other boat to come and help them, and they
came, and **the two boats were loaded so full** that they began to settle low in
the water.*

The other boat — the one He did not get into — is filled as well.

The catch outruns the vessel that was lent. James and John, who had nothing to do
with the loan, go home with a full boat too.

## What it cost him

The loan of an empty boat and one more cast of a net he had already washed and
put away.

Simon gave the small thing he had at the end of a wasted night, and the return
nearly sank two boats.

## What he says next

*"Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, O Lord."*

Not thank you. The size of the catch tells him who is in the boat with him, and
his first instinct is to ask Him to leave.

*"Do not be afraid. From now on you will catch men."*

> **What they did with the haul.** *They brought their boats to land, left
> everything, and followed Him.* The biggest catch of Simon's life is sitting on
> the shore, and he walks away from it. It was never the point; it was the proof.
