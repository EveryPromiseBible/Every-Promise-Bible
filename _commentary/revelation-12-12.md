A verse with a celebration in the first half and a warning in the second.

## What has just happened

*So the great dragon was cast out… **he was cast to the earth, and his angels were cast out
with him**.*

And the loud voice: *"Now salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power
of His Christ have come, for **the accuser of our brethren, who accused them before our God
day and night, has been cast down**."* See the note on Revelation 12:10–11.

## The celebration

*So **celebrate, you heavens**, and everyone whose home is up there.*

The Greek is **euphrainesthe** — be glad, make merry.

Heaven is told to rejoice because something has been removed from it.

## The dwellers

*Everyone whose home is up there.*

The Greek is **hoi ouranois skenountes** — those who tabernacle in the heavens.

## The other half

*But **it will be terrible for everyone living on the earth and on the sea**.*

The Greek is **ouai** — woe.

The same event is good news in one place and bad news in another. What is expelled from
heaven arrives somewhere.

## The reason

*Because **the devil has come down to you in a towering rage**.*

The Greek is **thumon megan** — great wrath. **Thumos** is the hot kind, the boiling-over
kind, as distinct from settled anger.

## Why he is angry

*He knows he has **only a little time left**.*

The Greek is **oligon kairon** — a short season.

**Kairos**, not **chronos**. Not a quantity of time but an allotted opportunity.

The rage is described as a symptom of the knowledge. He is not furious because he is losing;
he is furious because he can see the clock.

## What he does with it

The rest of the chapter: he pursues the woman, the earth helps her by swallowing the flood
from his mouth, and then —

*And the dragon was enraged with the woman, and he went to make war with the rest of her
offspring, who keep the commandments of God and have the testimony of Jesus Christ.*

## The word Peter uses

*Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil **walks about like a roaring lion**,
seeking whom he may devour.* See the note on 1 Peter 5:8.

Peter's picture is a hungry animal. John's is a defeated official with a deadline.

## How the brethren overcame

The verse before this one gives it in three parts:

*And they overcame him by **the blood of the Lamb** and by **the word of their testimony**,
and they **did not love their lives to the death**.*

## What is not said

Nothing here suggests the outcome is in question. The dragon has already been thrown down
before the woe is pronounced, and the celebration is in the same verse as the warning.

Revelation keeps that order everywhere: the throne room in chapter 4 comes before the seals in
chapter 6.

## Where it ends

*The devil, who deceived them, was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone… and they will be
tormented day and night forever and ever.*

The accuser who worked *day and night* is dealt with in the same phrase.

> **What the rage is a symptom of.** Not strength. He is described as knowing exactly how much
> time is left, and the fury is the reaction to the number.
