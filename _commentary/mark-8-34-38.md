Jesus calls the crowd over — not just the disciples — and says the hardest thing
in Mark.

## The setting

Peter has just confessed Him as the Messiah, and then rebuked Him for saying He
would be killed. Jesus has answered that sharply.

Then He calls the crowd, so that what follows is said to everybody rather than to
the twelve.

## "Pick up his cross"

The image was not yet a metaphor. Crucifixion was a Roman execution his hearers
had seen, and a condemned man carried the crossbeam to the place through public
streets.

Everyone in that crowd knew what a man carrying a cross was: someone already
sentenced, walking to it.

## The exchange

*Whoever tries to hold on to his life will lose it, and whoever gives up his life
for My sake and for the sake of the good news will save it.*

The word for *life* here is **psyche** — life, self, soul. The same word runs
through the next two sentences, which is why they belong together:

*What good does it do a man to gain the whole world and lose his own soul? What
could a man possibly offer in exchange for his soul?*

## The commercial language

*Gain*, *lose*, *exchange* — the vocabulary is a merchant's, and the question is
about price.

The second question is the sharper one. Suppose the trade has already been made
and the world is in hand: what is left to buy the soul back with? The answer the
question expects is nothing.

> **Whose loss is being described.** *For My sake and for the sake of the good
> news.* Jesus does not commend self-denial in general — Mark's Gospel is not
> asking for austerity. The letting go is for a person, and He names Himself.
