---
key: 1 Peter 1:18–19
title: "What it cost to get you out"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Peter describes the purchase in the vocabulary of a slave market, and then names a price that has no
exchange rate.

## Bought out

*You know **what it took to get you out**.*

**Elytrothete** — you were ransomed. The **lytron** was the sum paid to release a slave or a
prisoner of war, and Jesus used the noun about Himself: *the Son of Man came… to give His life a
**ransom** for many.*

The picture is not a fine being paid on your behalf. It is a purchase that changes whose you are.

## What you were bought out of

*The life you inherited from your ancestors was **empty, going nowhere**.*

**Mataias anastrophes patroparadotou** — futile conduct handed down from the fathers.

That is a bold thing to write to people whose religion and culture came from their parents. Peter
does not say their tradition was wicked; he says it was **mataia** — hollow, achieving nothing. And
the tragedy of it is in the compound: it was handed down. Somebody passed it on in good faith.

You were bought out of one inheritance and given another — *an inheritance incorruptible, undefiled,
and that does not fade away, reserved in heaven for you.*

## Money that rots

*Not with **silver or gold — that kind of money corrodes**.*

**Phthartois** — perishable things. Peter calls the two most enduring stores of value on earth
*perishable*, which is either economic illiteracy or a very long view.

Silver tarnishes, and empires that were paid in gold are gone. Nothing that decays can purchase
something that does not.

## The two words for the Lamb

*A lamb with **no defect on Him anywhere, not one mark**.*

**Amomou kai aspilou** — without blemish and without spot.

The first is the technical term for the inspection a priest carried out: the animal had to be
examined and found free of any deformity. The second means unstained — nothing picked up from
outside, nothing that had rubbed off on the way.

He passed both examinations. Pilate said *I find no fault in Him*, and the thief beside Him said
*this Man has done nothing wrong*, and the centurion running the execution said *certainly this was
a righteous Man*. Peter, who watched, adds that He was inspected and clean.

## Before there was anything to buy

*He indeed was **foreordained before the foundation of the world**, but was manifest in these last
times for you.*

The Lamb was chosen before the world existed — before there was a slave market, a debt, or a person
in it. The rescue was not a response to an emergency. It was the plan the world was built around.

> **Nothing you own could have paid it.** Peter names the two currencies everybody trusts and rules
> them both out as too perishable to buy an imperishable life. What was handed over instead was the
> blood of Christ, and He was examined by everyone who touched the case and found without a mark.
> That is what you cost, and it is why the purchase cannot be reversed.
