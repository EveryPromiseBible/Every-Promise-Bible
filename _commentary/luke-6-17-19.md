He comes down from a night of prayer and stands on flat ground with a crowd around Him.

## What preceded it

*Now it came to pass in those days that **He went out to the mountain to pray, and continued all
night in prayer to God**.*

*And when it was day, He called His disciples to Himself; and **from them He chose twelve**, whom He
also named apostles.*

The night of prayer, then the choosing of the twelve, then this.

## The descent

*Then He came down the mountain with them and **stood on a wide stretch of level ground**.*

The Greek is **epi topou pedinou** — on a level place.

Luke's version of the sermon is preached here, which is why it is called the Sermon on the Plain.

Matthew has *He went up on a mountain, and when He was seated*. Luke has Him standing on flat
ground.

## The two groups

*A **great gathering of His disciples** was there, and **an enormous crowd of people**.*

The Greek distinguishes them: **ochlos matheton** — a crowd of disciples, and **plethos polu tou
laou** — a great multitude of the people.

## Where they came from

*From all over **Judea** and from **Jerusalem** and from **the coastal region around Tyre and Sidon**.*

Tyre and Sidon are Gentile cities in Phoenicia.

Luke names them, which means the crowd is mixed before any commission has been given about
Gentiles.

## Why they came

*They had come **to listen to Him and to be healed of their diseases**.*

The Greek is **akousai autou kai iathenai** — to hear Him and to be healed.

Two purposes, and the hearing is named first.

## The third group

*And those who were **tormented by unclean spirits were set free**.*

## The reaching

*Everyone in that crowd was **trying to touch Him**.*

The Greek is **ezetoun haptesthai autou** — imperfect. They kept seeking to touch Him.

## The reason

*Because **power was flowing out from Him**, and it was healing them all.*

The Greek is **dunamis par' autou exercheto** — power was going out from Him. Imperfect again.

**Iato pantas** — He was healing them all.

## What the crowd had noticed

Not that He was healing individuals. That contact worked.

The same word appears in the woman with the issue of blood: *I perceived power going out from
Me*. See the note on Luke 8:43–48.

There the crowd pressed and only one drew. Here the whole crowd is reaching.

## The scale

**Pantas** — all.

Luke says it flatly. Nobody in that crowd was turned away or left as they came.

## What He says next

*Then He lifted up His eyes toward His disciples, and said:*

*"**Blessed are you poor, for yours is the kingdom of God. Blessed are you who hunger now, for you
shall be filled. Blessed are you who weep now, for you shall laugh**."* See the note on
Luke 6:20–23.

And then four woes, which Matthew does not have: *woe to you who are rich… woe to you who are full…
woe to you who laugh now*.

## Who He is looking at

*He lifted up His eyes **toward His disciples**.*

The beatitudes in Luke are in the second person — *blessed are **you** poor* — and addressed to the
men He picked that morning.

## What He asks them to do

*"**But I say to you who hear**: Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you."*

The Greek is **alla humin lego tois akouousin** — but to you who are hearing, I say.

## The power that kept going out

*Power **was going out** from Him and He **was healing** them all.* The imperfects.

*How **God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power**, who went about doing good
and **healing all who were oppressed by the devil**, for God was with Him.*

**Peter**, summarising the entire ministry to a Roman officer in **Acts** 10, uses the same two
words: anointed with power, and healed **all**.

*Jesus Christ is **the same yesterday, today, and forever**.*

## Who was in the crowd

Judea, Jerusalem, and Tyre and Sidon — two Gentile ports.

*"And **I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all peoples to Myself**."*

**Luke** puts the Gentile cities in the crowd list before **Jesus** has said a word of the sermon,
and the same evangelist writes the book where the gospel reaches Rome.

## What He does with His hands

*"And **the whole multitude sought to touch Him**."*

He is the one being touched, by a crowd, and nothing in the passage records Him withdrawing. The law
worked the other way: contact with the unclean transferred uncleanness, and here the traffic runs
the opposite direction.

*Then He put out His hand and touched him, saying, "I am willing; be cleansed."*

*For **He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us**, that we might become the righteousness of God
in Him.*

**2 Corinthians** 5 describes the same exchange at full scale.

> **The imperfects.** Power *was going out*, and He *was healing* them all — continuous tense, of a
> crowd from Judea, Jerusalem and two Gentile cities, who came to hear and be healed in that order.
