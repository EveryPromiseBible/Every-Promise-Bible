Three verses to close a reign, and the Chronicler's version leaves out most of it.

## The name

*So **David, the son of Jesse**, reigned over all Israel.*

The Hebrew is **David ben-Yishai**.

Called the son of Jesse at the end, as he was at the beginning — the eighth son, out with the
sheep when Samuel came.

## The arithmetic

*He reigned over Israel **forty years** — **seven years in Hebron** and **thirty-three years in
Jerusalem**.*

Seven and a half in Hebron over Judah alone, while Ish-bosheth held the north.

Then the elders came and *anointed David king over Israel*, and he took the stronghold of Zion.

## The formula

*And he died **at a good old age, full of days, full of riches and honor**.*

The Hebrew is **seivah tovah sava yamim osher vekavod**.

**Sava yamim** — satisfied of days. The phrase used of Abraham and Isaac. See the note on
Genesis 25:7–10.

## What the Chronicler leaves out

Bathsheba. Uriah. Nathan and *you are the man*. The child that died.

Amnon and Tamar. Absalom at the gate, and the flight from Jerusalem barefoot, and *O my son
Absalom*.

Adonijah declaring himself king while David lay dying.

All of it is in Samuel and Kings. None of it is in Chronicles.

## Why

Chronicles is written for the returned exiles, and its subject is the temple and the line that
built it.

It is not concealment — the other books were in circulation. It is a different book with a
different purpose.

## What Chronicles keeps instead

The ark brought up with singers and instruments. The census and the plague and the threshing floor
of Ornan. The plans for the temple, the materials gathered, the Levites organised into courses.

And this chapter: David giving three thousand talents of gold from his own treasure, and the
leaders giving after him.

## The prayer just before

*"**Yours, O LORD, is the greatness, the power and the glory, the victory and the majesty**… **But who
am I, and who are my people**, that we should be able to offer so willingly as this? For **all things
come from You, and of Your own we have given You**."*

The last recorded prayer of David, offered over a building fund.

## What he says about the span

*"For **we are aliens and pilgrims before You**, as were all our fathers; **our days on earth are as a
shadow**, and without hope."*

Said in the same prayer, by a man about to be described as satisfied of days.

## The succession

*And **Solomon his son reigned in his place**.*

Chronicles does not record the contest with Adonijah, the summons to Bathsheba, or the executions
that followed.

## The last verses of the book

*Now the acts of King David, first and last, indeed they are written in the book of Samuel the
seer, in the book of Nathan the prophet, and in the book of Gad the seer.*

Three sources named, all lost.

## The word that outlives him

*"**And your house and your kingdom shall be established forever** before you."*

Nathan's promise, in 2 Samuel 7. See the note on 2 Samuel 7:12–16.

The lamp that keeps burning through Kings, named at every low point. See the note on
1 Kings 11:41–43.

> **What Chronicles omits.** Bathsheba, Absalom, and Adonijah. It is not concealment — Samuel and
> Kings were in circulation — it is a book written for people rebuilding a temple.
