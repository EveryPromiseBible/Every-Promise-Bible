Three things are said about Noah, and then three about the world he lived in.

## What is said about him

- **righteous** — right, in the sense of standing straight with God
- **blameless among the people of his time** — the word is used of sacrificial
  animals without defect
- **he walked with God**

The third phrase is used of only one other man in Genesis: Enoch, four chapters
earlier. Both are described the same way, and both stand out from a genealogy of
people who simply lived and died.

## The word "blameless"

It does not mean sinless. Genesis will show Noah drunk and shamed within a
chapter of leaving the ark, and it does not edit that out.

*Blameless among the people of his time* is a comparison. In a world the next
verses call corrupt and violent, he was the one who was not.

## What is said about the world

*The earth was corrupt in God's sight and full of violence. God looked at the
earth and saw how ruined it was.*

The Hebrew word for *corrupt* and the word for *ruined* are the same root — and
it is the same root used a few verses later when God says He will destroy them.

The judgement matches the diagnosis exactly. They had ruined it; it would be
ruined. God is described as confirming a state of affairs rather than inventing a
penalty for it.

> **What kept Noah afloat.** Hebrews says he built the ark *by faith*, moved with
> godly fear, having been warned about things not yet seen. And Genesis, before
> any of the building starts, says the thing the whole account rests on: *Noah
> found favour in the eyes of the LORD.* The favour comes first, in the verse
> before this paragraph.
