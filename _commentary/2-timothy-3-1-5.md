Eighteen descriptions in a row, and the first and last are the same thing.

## The warning

*Understand this, and **do not let it take you by surprise**: in the last days there will come
**stretches of time that are hard to live through**.*

The Greek is **kairoi chalepoi** — difficult seasons.

**Chalepos** appears twice in the New Testament. The other use is Matthew's description of the
Gadarene demoniacs: *exceedingly fierce*.

Not merely unpleasant. Dangerous.

## The word for times

**Kairoi** — not **chronoi**. Seasons, periods with a character, rather than a stretch of
calendar.

The prediction is of recurring seasons, not one continuous decline.

## The first item

*People will be **in love with themselves**.*

The Greek is **philautoi** — self-lovers. It heads the list.

## The second

*And **in love with money**.*

**Philarguroi** — silver-lovers.

The list begins with two compounds of **phileo**, love.

## The last item

*Their pleasures will have **far more of their love than God ever does**.*

**Philedonoi mallon e philotheoi** — lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God.

Two more **phileo** compounds, closing the list.

## The shape

The catalogue opens with two wrong loves and closes with two more.

Everything in between is what those loves produce. Paul has framed the whole list as a disorder
of affection rather than a failure of rules.

## What is in the middle

*They will **brag about what they are**, and they will **look down on everyone who is not**.*

*They will **throw their words against God**, and they will **not listen to their own parents**.*

*They will **take everything and thank no one**, and **nothing at all will be sacred to them**.*

## The family word

*The ordinary affection that holds a family together **will simply be missing in them**.*

The Greek is **astorgoi** — without **storge**, the natural love between parent and child.

The alpha-privative: the thing is absent, not corrupted.

## The rest

*They will **refuse to be reconciled**, they will **tear other people's names to pieces**, and they
will have **no hold whatever on their own appetites**.*

**Aspondoi** — literally *without libation*, without a truce. In Greek usage, a war in which no
terms can be agreed.

**Diaboloi** — slanderers. The same word used of the devil.

## The last three before the pleasures

*They will **betray the ones who trust them**, they will **rush ahead without a thought**, and they
will be **swollen up with their own importance**.*

**Tetuphomenoi** — from **tuphos**, smoke. Puffed up, clouded, conceited.

## The sting

*And they will **keep the outward shape of a devout life** while **denying the very power that makes
such a life real**.*

The Greek is **morphosin eusebeias** — a form of godliness. And **ten dunamin autes ernemenoi** —
having denied its power.

**Morphosis** — the outline, the shape, the appearance.

## Who is being described

Not the pagan world outside. People with a form of godliness — inside the church, and the last
line makes it explicit.

## The instruction

*Have nothing to do with people like that.*

The Greek is **apotrepou** — turn yourself away from.

Timothy is not told to argue with them.

## What follows

*But you must continue in the things which you have learned… **from childhood you have known the
Holy Scriptures**.* See the note on 2 Timothy 3:14–15.

And *all Scripture is given by inspiration of God*. See the note on
2 Timothy 3:16–17.

The remedy offered against eighteen items is a book and the two women who taught it to him.

## The power the form is denying

The list ends on the sentence that explains it: *having **a form of godliness** but **denying its
power***.

**Morphosis** — an outline, the shape of a thing without the substance of it. And what the shape is
missing is not sincerity or effort. Paul never calls the power an experience. He names it:

*Christ **the power of God** and the wisdom of God.* *I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ, for
it is **the power of God to salvation** to everyone who believes.*

So the last-days religion Paul describes is not irreligion. It is godliness with the Person taken
out — a form that keeps the vocabulary and denies the only thing in it that ever changed anybody.

Which is also why the antidote he gives Timothy two paragraphs later is not vigilance but
Scripture: *the Holy Scriptures, which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith which
is in Christ Jesus*.

> **The frame.** Four compounds of *love* — self, money, pleasure, God — at the two ends of the
> list. Everything between them is what those affections produce.
