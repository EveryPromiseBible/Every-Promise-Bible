The psalm is a retelling of Israel's history, and this is its summary of the
exodus in two lines.

## What it records

*He brought His people out with silver and gold, and not one of their tribes
stumbled.*

Two claims. They left with wealth — the payment described in Exodus 11 and 12 —
and the whole company got out.

The Hebrew behind *stumbled* means to totter or be feeble. The psalmist is
describing a mass departure of perhaps a very large number of people, including
the old and the very young, in which nobody fell behind.

## What the psalm is doing

Read the whole of Psalm 105 and the pattern is unmistakable: it is a catalogue of
things God did, verb after verb, with almost nothing about what Israel did. He
sent, He turned, He spoke, He brought out, He spread a cloud, He opened the rock.

The psalm's purpose is stated at the start — remember, give thanks, tell what He
has done. This verse is one line in a very long list of that.

## What they had done the night before

The verse is not describing a crowd that happened to be in good health. It is
describing the morning after Passover.

Every household in that nation had, the previous evening, eaten a lamb —
*roasted with fire… his head with his legs and with the appurtenance thereof*,
the whole animal, and the instruction was to eat it, not merely to kill it. The
blood went on the doorframe and the lamb went inside them.

They ate the lamb, and they came out the next morning with nobody feeble among
them. The psalm puts the two facts side by side because they belong side by side.

## The scale of it

However many they were, the number included the elderly, the newborn, and
everyone in between — a whole population that had been in hard labour under
Egyptian taskmasters for generations.

Not one of them stumbled. Not one lagged behind. That is what is being recorded,
and the psalm records it as a thing God did.

> **The lamb and the table.** Paul calls Him *Christ our Passover*, sacrificed for
> us, and the table He left His disciples is the table of that lamb. The bread is
> His body, broken. What the night of the exodus produced in Israel is what the
> body was given for. See the notes on 1 Corinthians 11:23–26 and Matthew 8:14–17.
