key: Job 4:12–16
title: A voice in the night
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Eliphaz grounds his whole argument in something that happened to him in the dark.

## What he claims

*"A word came stealing to me, **secret**, and my ear caught only a whisper of it."*

The Hebrew is **ganav** — stolen. A word smuggled to him, of which he heard a fragment.

## What it was like

*"**Fear gripped me**, and trembling with it, and every bone in me shook. A spirit brushed
past my face, and **the hair on my body stood up**. It stood still, but **I could not make out
its form**."*

Terror, a presence he cannot identify, and a shape he cannot see. He reports all of it as
credentials.

## What it told him

*"Can a mortal be more righteous than God? Can a man be more pure than his Maker?"*

True, as a sentence. And useless as an answer to a blameless man on an ash heap, which is what
he uses it for.

## How this compares to the rest of the book

Every other revelation in Job is direct and unmistakable. God speaks *out of the whirlwind*,
and the book says who is speaking. See the note on Job 38:1–3.

Eliphaz has a whisper he could not make out, from a form he could not see, and builds three
speeches on it.

*Beloved, **do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits**, whether they are of God.*

**John** gives the test in the next verse, and it is Christological: *every spirit that
confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God.*

## Where certainty is located instead

*And so we have **the prophetic word confirmed**, which you do well to heed as a light that
shines in a dark place.*

**Peter** had been on the mountain and heard the voice himself — and still says the written
word is the surer thing.

*"**Sanctify them by Your truth. Your word is truth.**"*

## What the verdict is

*"You have not spoken of Me what is right."*

Of the four men who argue with Job, the one who opens with a night vision is the one God names
first when the rebuke comes. See the note on Job 42:7–8.

> **What he actually had.** A whisper he only caught part of, from a shape he could not make
> out, in the dark — and three speeches built on it, to a man God had already called blameless
> twice.
