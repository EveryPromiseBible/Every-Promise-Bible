Paul is in the middle of organising a collection for the poor in Jerusalem, and
he stops to give the reason. It turns out to be the whole gospel in a line.

## The structure

*Though He was rich, for your sakes He became poor, so that you, through His
poverty, might become rich.*

Four movements, and they cross over:

| Him | you |
|---|---|
| was rich | were poor |
| **became poor** | **become rich** |

Nothing is left in the middle. What was His went one way; what was ours went the
other.

## "You know the grace"

Paul opens with *for you know* — he is not teaching them something new. He is
reminding a church that had gone cold on a promised collection what grace had
already cost.

The word is **charis**, and here it is applied to an act rather than an attitude:
the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ *is* the exchange he then describes.

## Where the argument lands

He never orders them to give. The next verse says he is not commanding, and a few
lines on comes the sentence about God loving a cheerful giver.

Instead he sets one act beside another: Christ emptied Himself for you, so
finish what you started for them. Generosity is argued from generosity received,
not from obligation.

> **What the riches are.** Paul does not spell it out here, which is why the verse
> travels so well. Read alongside Ephesians 1 — every blessing, already given, in
> the heavenly places — and the wealth being described is not narrow. What He
> gave up was real; what came back the other way is described in the same
> unqualified terms.
