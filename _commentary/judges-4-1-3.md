Three verses of setup, and the last four words are what the chapter turns on.

## The cycle

*After Ehud died, **Israel again did what was evil** in the LORD's eyes.*

The book runs on it: the people turn away, an oppressor comes, they cry out, God raises up a
deliverer, the land has rest, the deliverer dies, and it starts again. Seven times.

The word *again* is doing the work.

## What they were sold into

*So **the LORD sold them into the hand of Jabin**, a Canaanite king who reigned in Hazor.*

Hazor is the city Joshua burned — *Joshua turned back at that time and took Hazor… he burned
Hazor with fire.* It is the one town in the conquest narrative singled out as *the head of all
those kingdoms*.

It has been rebuilt, and a king of the same name is back in it. What is not finished comes
back with a throne.

## The iron

*He had **nine hundred iron chariots**.*

The same technology that stopped Judah in chapter 1. *The LORD was with Judah, and he drove out
the mountain dwellers, but could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley, because they had
chariots of iron.* See the note on Judges 1:16–21.

Twenty years later the chariots are still the fact everybody is reckoning with.

## How long

*For **twenty years** he crushed the Israelites without mercy.*

The Hebrew is *mightily oppressed*. Two decades — long enough that most people fighting under
Barak had never known anything else.

## The four words

*So **they cried out to the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyitz'aqu** — the same verb as Israel groaning in Egypt, and as Abel's blood
from the ground.

Nothing else is recorded. No repentance, no reform, no altar rebuilt, no vow. Judges reports a
cry and then reports God answering it, and it does that seven times without once making the
answer conditional on the quality of the crying.

Paul quotes it in the middle of his argument that there is no difference between Jew and
Greek: *for **"whoever calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved."***

## Who is already in place

The next verse: *now **Deborah, a prophetess**, the wife of Lapidoth, was judging Israel at
that time.*

She is not raised up in response. She is already sitting under a palm tree between Ramah and
Bethel when the cry goes up.

## What the chapter ends with

A tent peg, a woman who was not an Israelite, and the general with nine hundred chariots dead
on the floor of somebody's tent.

*Has God not chosen **the poor of this world to be rich in faith**?*

> **The whole of what they did.** Cried out. Judges records the cycle seven times and never
> once attaches a condition to the answer.
