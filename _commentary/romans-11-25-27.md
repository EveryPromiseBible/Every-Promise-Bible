The conclusion of three chapters Paul has been building since chapter 9.

## Why he says it

*In case you should **think too highly of yourselves**.*

The Greek is *lest you should be wise in your own opinion*.

He is writing to Gentile believers in Rome who had started to look down on the Jews who had
not believed.

He has already told them off for it: *do not boast against the branches. But if you do boast,
remember that you do not support the root, but the root supports you.*

## The image behind it

An olive tree. Some branches broken off, and wild branches grafted in *contrary to nature*.

*Do not be haughty, but fear. For if God did not spare the natural branches, He may not spare
you either.*

## The mystery

*I do not want you to be uninformed about **this mystery**.*

The Greek is **musterion** — something previously hidden, now disclosed.

## The first half

*A **hardening has come over part of Israel**.*

The Greek is **porosis apo merous** — hardening in part.

Two limits in one clause: it is partial, and it is temporary.

Paul has already made the first point at length: *God has not cast away His people whom He
foreknew… even so then, at this present time there is a remnant according to the election of
grace.*

And he offers himself as the evidence: *for I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of
the tribe of Benjamin*.

## The clock

*And it **lasts until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in**.*

The Greek is **achris hou** — until.

**Pleroma** — fullness, the full number.

## The result

*And in this way **all Israel will be saved**.*

The Greek is **kai houtos** — and thus, and in this manner.

Not *and then*. The manner, not just the sequence.

## The quotation

*"**The Deliverer will come out of Zion**, and He will **turn ungodliness away from Jacob**."*

Isaiah 59, the chapter immediately before *arise, shine, for your light has come*. See the
note on Isaiah 60:1–3.

*"And **this is My covenant with them, when I take away their sins**."*

Isaiah 59:21 and Jeremiah 31, combined. See the note on Isaiah 59:21.

## What Paul says next

*Concerning the gospel they are enemies for your sake, but concerning the election they are
beloved for the sake of the fathers. **For the gifts and the calling of God are
irrevocable**.*

The Greek is **ametameleta** — not to be repented of, not taken back.

## The summary

*For as you were once disobedient to God, yet have now obtained mercy through their
disobedience, even so these also have now been disobedient, that through the mercy shown you
they also may obtain mercy. **For God has committed them all to disobedience, that He might
have mercy on all**.*

## How the argument ends

Not with a conclusion. With a doxology:

*Oh, the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! **How unsearchable are
His judgments and His ways past finding out!** For who has known the mind of the LORD? Or who
has become His counsellor? Or who has first given to Him and it shall be repaid to him? For of
Him and through Him and to Him are all things, to whom be glory forever. Amen.*

Three chapters of the hardest material in Paul, and he finishes by saying he cannot see the
bottom of it.

## What he opened with

*I tell the truth in Christ, I am not lying… **I have great sorrow and continual grief in my
heart**. For I could wish that I myself were accursed from Christ for my brethren, my
countrymen according to the flesh.*

The doctrine and the grief are in the same three chapters.

> **The two limits in one clause.** Partial, and *until*. Paul states both before he says
> anything about the outcome — and the warning attached is against Gentile self-congratulation.
