The first sentence of the book, and it starts with a voice.

## Where it comes from

*The LORD **called to Moses** and spoke to him **from the Tent of Meeting**.*

The Hebrew for *called* is **vayyiqra** — and it is the Hebrew name of the whole book.

Exodus ends with the tabernacle finished and the glory filling it so densely that *Moses was
not able to enter*. Leviticus opens with a voice coming out of it.

The book exists because a man could not walk in, and had to be told how.

## Where God is

*The place where **God had come to dwell among His people**.*

The Hebrew is **ohel mo'ed** — the tent of appointed meeting. **Mo'ed** is the word for a
fixed time and place, the same root as the appointed feasts.

Not a shrine to visit. A rendezvous.

## The address

*"Speak to **the Israelites**."*

To everybody, not to the priests. Chapters 1 to 7 tell the people what to bring; chapters 6
onward turn and tell the priests what to do with it. See the note on Leviticus 6:24–30.

## The condition

*"**When any of you wants to bring an offering** to the LORD."*

The Hebrew is *when a man of you brings near*, and the burnt offering in particular is
voluntary. Nothing here is a summons.

The compulsory offerings come later.

## The word

**Qorban** — the noun for the offering.

From the root **qarab**, to come near, to approach. The same root as the noun for a relative,
and the verb for a battle joining.

The word for the gift is a word about proximity. What is being described is not a payment but
an approach.

Mark keeps it in Greek letters and translates it: *Corban — that is, a gift*.

## What is acceptable

*"Bring one **from the herd or the flock** — an animal from among **your own cattle, sheep,
or goats**."*

Domestic animals only. Nothing wild, nothing hunted.

Which means it cost something. A man could not go out and find one; he had to take it from
what he was living on.

David makes the same point at Araunah's threshing floor: *I will not offer burnt offerings to
the LORD my God with that which costs me nothing*.

## The three grades

The chapter allows a bull, a sheep or goat, or two birds — descending by what the worshipper
could afford, and each one is called *a sweet aroma to the LORD*.

The same phrase for the bull and for the pigeons.

Joseph and Mary brought the birds. See the note on Luke 2:33–35.

## The five offerings

Burnt, grain, peace, sin, and guilt. The first three are voluntary and the last two are
required.

Leviticus puts the voluntary ones first.

## What the book is for

*"You shall be holy, for I am holy."*

And the sentence that recurs like a refrain: *I am the LORD your God*.

Peter quotes the first to Gentile believers. See the note on 1 Peter 1:14–16.

> **What the noun means.** *Qorban*, from the root for coming near. The word for the sacrifice
> is a word about distance being closed.
