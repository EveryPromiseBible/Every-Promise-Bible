key: Philemon 1:8–9
title: "I would rather ask"
updated: 2026-08-09

An apostle with the authority to give an order declines to use it, and says so in writing.

## What he could do

*"So — **I could be perfectly blunt with you. In Christ I have every right to give you an order and
tell you flatly what you ought to be doing**."*

The Greek is **pollen en Christo parresian echon epitassein soi to anekon** — having much boldness
in Christ to command you what is fitting.

**Epitasso** is the military word: to issue an order to a subordinate. **Paul** has the standing and
he names it.

## What he does instead

*"**I would rather ask.**"*

**Dia ten agapen mallon parakalo** — for love's sake I rather appeal.

**Parakaleo** — to call alongside, to urge, to plead. It is the verb behind **parakletos**, the name
for the Holy Spirit.

He states the right and sets it down, in the same sentence, and gives the reason: **love**.

## Why it matters what he asks for

He is asking a slave-owner to take back a runaway. Under Roman law Philemon could have had Onesimus
branded, crucified, or thrown to the beasts, and no court would have looked twice.

**Paul** has the authority to make it an order and refuses, because an obedience produced by
pressure would not be the thing he wants.

*"But I did not want to do anything without your consent, **so that your good deed might not be by
compulsion, as it were, but voluntary**."*

Four verses on, and the Greek is **kata anagken** against **kata hekousion** — by necessity, or
willingly.

*So let each one give as he purposes in his heart, **not grudgingly or of necessity; for God loves a
cheerful giver**.*

## Who else had every right and did not use it

*Who, being in the form of God, **did not consider it robbery to be equal with God, but made Himself
of no reputation**, taking the form of a bondservant.*

*"**Or do you think that I cannot now pray to My Father, and He will provide Me with more than
twelve legions of angels?**"*

**Matthew** 26, in the garden, to a man with a sword. The right is stated and not exercised, and
the reason given in the next verse is that the Scriptures had to be fulfilled.

*Now the Lord is the Spirit; and where the Spirit of the Lord is, **there is liberty**.*

## The self-description

*"**Paul, an old man and now also a prisoner of Christ Jesus.**"*

He signs the letter, three times, not as an apostle but as a prisoner — and in verse 1 as *a
prisoner of Christ Jesus*, not of Rome.

> **The sentence he writes and then declines to act on.** *In Christ I have every right to give you
> an order.* He puts the authority on the page so that Philemon knows it is real, and then asks
> instead — and later says explicitly that a compelled answer is not what he is after.
