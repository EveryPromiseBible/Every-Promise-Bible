Paul argues against a sin by telling them what they are.

## The question

*Do you not know that **your bodies are parts of Christ Himself**?*

The Greek is **mele Christou** — members, limbs.

Paul asks *do you not know* six times in this chapter alone. He is not informing them; he is
reminding them of something they had been told and were not using.

## What Corinth was

A port city with a reputation bad enough that the Greeks had coined a verb from its name.
Temple prostitution on the acropolis above it.

The men he is writing to had been going, and defending it with a slogan: *all things are
lawful for me*.

## The horror

*Shall I then **take what belongs to the body of Christ and join it to a prostitute? It must
never be**.*

The Greek is **me genoito** — Paul's strongest negative, the one he uses fourteen times in
Romans. *God forbid*, in the old versions. *Absolutely not.*

The argument is not that it is dirty. It is that it involves taking a limb of Christ
somewhere.

## The second question

*Or do you not know that the man who is **joined to a prostitute is one body with her**?*

The Greek is **kollomenos** — glued, cemented.

Paul refuses the idea that a transaction is only a transaction.

## The quotation

*For **the two, He says, shall become one flesh**.*

Genesis 2, the marriage sentence, quoted about the least marital arrangement available in
the city.

The verse Jesus uses about what God has joined together. Paul applies it here to show that
the mechanism does not care about the intention.

## The turn

*But **the one who is joined to the Lord is one spirit with Him**.*

The same verb — **kollomenos**, glued.

One flesh in the first case, one spirit in the second. Paul sets the two unions in the same
grammar and lets the comparison do the work.

## Why it settles the question

Not a rule. A collision: a man already joined to the Lord in spirit cannot join his body
elsewhere without dragging the first union into it.

## What he says next

*Flee sexual immorality. Every sin that a man does is outside the body, but he who commits
sexual immorality **sins against his own body**.*

The Greek for *flee* is **pheugete** — run. Present imperative, keep running.

Joseph is the model, and he left his coat behind.

## The last argument

*Or do you not know that **your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit** who is in you, whom
you have from God, and you are not your own? For **you were bought at a price**; therefore
glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God's.*

The Greek is **naos** — the inner sanctuary, not the temple complex.

Paul uses the same image of the whole church in chapter 3. Here it is one body.

## The slogans and the answers

*All things are lawful for me* — *but not all things are helpful*.

*All things are lawful for me* — *but I will not be brought under the power of any*.

*Foods for the stomach and the stomach for foods* — *but the body is for the Lord, and the
Lord for the body*.

He quotes their line every time and answers it every time, and never once by denying the
first half.

## The verses before it

*And such were some of you. **But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were
justified** in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of our God.*

Three verbs, all past. The list of what they used to be sits immediately before *do you not
know*.

> **The word used twice.** Glued. Paul puts the two unions in identical grammar — one body
> with her, one spirit with Him — and the argument is that the second one is already true.
