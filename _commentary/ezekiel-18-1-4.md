A saying is quoted, and then forbidden.

## The saying

*"**The parents eat sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge**."*

The Hebrew is **avot yokelu voser veshinnei habbanim tiqheinah**.

**Boser** — unripe, sour fruit.

## What it means

We are paying for what they did.

## Who is saying it

Exiles in Babylon. Their grandparents' generation is blamed for the catastrophe, and the proverb is
a way of saying the sentence is not theirs.

## What is true in it

Consequences do run downhill. The exile did follow generations of policy.

The proverb is not factually absurd, which is why it had currency.

## What is wrong with it

Used as they were using it, it makes repentance pointless.

If the verdict was fixed by a previous generation, there is nothing for this one to do.

## The ban

*"**You will no longer use this proverb in Israel**."*

The Hebrew is **im-yihyeh lakem od meshol hammashal hazzeh** — an oath formula, and the strongest
form of prohibition.

## The oath

*"**As surely as I live**, declares the Lord GOD."*

He swears on His own life to stop a saying.

## The ground

*"For **every living soul belongs to Me**, the parent as well as the child. **Both alike are Mine**."*

The Hebrew is **kol-hannefashot li hennah**.

The answer to the proverb is not a theory of justice. It is a claim of ownership.

## What that removes

If both generations belong equally to Him, neither is standing in for the other.

## The principle

*"**The soul who sins is the one who will die**."*

The Hebrew is **hannefesh hachotet hi tamut**.

## What the chapter does with it

Three test cases: a righteous man, his violent son, and that son's righteous son.

*The son shall not bear the guilt of the father, nor the father bear the guilt of the son.* See the
note on Ezekiel 18:19–20.

## What it also says

A wicked man who turns is not held to his record. *None of the transgressions which he has
committed shall be remembered against him.* See the note on Ezekiel 18:21–23.

The principle cuts toward mercy in the same breath as toward responsibility.

## The question He asks

*"**Do I have any pleasure at all that the wicked should die?**" says the Lord GOD, "and not that he
should turn from his ways and live?"*

## The closing line

*"**Cast away from you all the transgressions which you have committed**, and get yourselves a new
heart and a new spirit. For why should you die, O house of Israel?"* See the note on
Ezekiel 18:30–32.

## The answer to that command

*"I will give you **a new heart** and put **a new spirit** within you."* See the note on
Ezekiel 36:26–27.

The thing they are told to get is later promised as a gift.

## The other prophet with the same proverb

*"In those days they shall say no more: 'The fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the children's
teeth are set on edge.'"*

Jeremiah, in the paragraph immediately before the new covenant. See the note on
Jeremiah 31:31–34.

Both prophets kill the same saying, and both put the new covenant next to it.

> **The ground of the ban.** Not a theory about fairness — *both alike are Mine*. Neither generation
> is standing in for the other because both are held directly.
