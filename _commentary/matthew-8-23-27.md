Matthew tells the storm in five verses and puts the emphasis in a different place
from Mark.

## Who got in first

*Then **He stepped into the boat, and His disciples went with Him**.*

Matthew's order. He gets in; they follow.

Whatever happens on that lake happens to men who are where they are because they
were following.

## The word for the storm

*Without warning **a violent squall** came down on the lake.*

The Greek is **seismos** — the word for an earthquake. Matthew uses it of the ground
shaking at the crucifixion and at the tomb.

He calls this weather an earthquake at sea.

## "And Jesus was asleep"

Three words, set against the earthquake, in the same sentence.

The boat is *buried* by the waves — the Greek is being covered over — and He is
asleep.

Mark adds the detail about the cushion in the stern. Matthew leaves it out and lets
the contrast stand bare. See the note on Mark 4:35–41.

## What they say

*"**Lord, save us! We are going down!**"*

Matthew's version is a prayer. In Mark it is an accusation — *do You not care that we
are going down?*

Matthew has them asking Him to do something. Mark has them asking whether He cares.

## What He says first

*"**Why are you so frightened? Where is your trust?**"*

Before the sea. Matthew is precise about the order: He addresses them, and then He
addresses the storm.

The Greek is *you of little faith* — **oligopistos**, the word Matthew uses five
times, always to disciples, and always about worry.

## Then the sea

*He **stood up and spoke sharply** to the winds and to the sea, and everything went
**completely still**.*

The Greek for *completely still* is a great calm — the same adjective as the *great*
tempest a few verses earlier.

The size of the calm matches the size of the storm.

## What they conclude

*"**What kind of man is this**, that even the winds and the sea do what He tells
Him?"*

They do not answer their own question. Matthew leaves it hanging.

Anyone who knew the Psalms had the answer: *He calms the storm, so that its waves are
still* — Psalm 107, about the LORD.

## What they had already seen

This is Matthew 8, and by this point in the chapter He has cleansed a leper, healed
the centurion's servant at a distance, healed Peter's mother-in-law, and cast out
spirits with a word.

They had watched all of it, and still asked what kind of man He was.

> **What is on the other side of the lake.** Two men out of the tombs, so violent
> that nobody could pass that way. The storm was on the route to them. See the note
> on Luke 8:30–33.
