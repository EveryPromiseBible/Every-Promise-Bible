He looks up expecting nothing and finds an animal that has been stuck there for some time.

## Where the knife stopped

*"Abraham, Abraham!"… "**Do not lay your hand on the lad**… for now I know that you fear God."*
See the note on Genesis 22:11–12.

The name twice, which is how urgency is spoken in Hebrew.

## The lift of the eyes

*Abraham **looked up**, and there in a thicket was **a ram, caught by its horns**.*

The Hebrew is **vayissa Avraham et-einav** — and Abraham lifted his eyes.

The third time in this chapter. He lifts his eyes and sees the place, and now he lifts them and
sees the ram.

## The animal

**Ayil** — a ram. Not a lamb.

He had told Isaac *God will provide for Himself **the lamb***. What is in the thicket is a full
grown male sheep, which is the animal a lamb becomes.

## The horns

*Caught by its horns.*

The Hebrew is **ne'echaz basvach bekarnav** — held fast in a thicket by its horns.

An animal caught this way cannot free itself and cannot run. It has been there long enough to
get thoroughly tangled.

Whatever hour it wandered in, it was before Abraham lifted his eyes.

## The substitution

*He went and took the ram and offered it up **in place of his son**.*

The Hebrew is **tachat beno** — in the place of, instead of, under.

The clearest statement of substitution in the Torah's narrative, and it is a story, not a
statute.

## The naming

*So Abraham named that place **The LORD Will Provide**.*

The Hebrew is **YHWH Yireh** — the LORD will see.

**Ra'ah** is the verb: to see. The Hebrew for providing is God seeing to it.

It is the same word Abraham used to Isaac on the way up: *God will see for Himself the lamb*.

## The proverb

*And **to this day people say, "On the mountain of the LORD it will be provided."**"*

The Hebrew is **behar YHWH yera'eh** — on the mountain of the LORD it will be seen.

A saying that outlived the event and was still current when Genesis was written down.

## The place

*Go to the land of **Moriah**.*

*Then Solomon began to build the house of the LORD at Jerusalem **on Mount Moriah**, where the LORD
had appeared to his father David.*

The temple went up on the same ridge, and every sacrifice offered there for a thousand years was
offered on the mountain named for a ram in a bush.

## What was seen there

*Behold! **The Lamb of God** who takes away the sin of the world!* See the note on
John 1:29–31.

Abraham said *lamb* and got a ram. The word he used stayed open.

## What comes after

*"**By Myself I have sworn**, says the LORD, because you have done this thing… **in your seed all
the nations of the earth shall be blessed**."* See the note on Genesis 22:15–18.

God swears by Himself, because *He could swear by no one greater*, and Hebrews builds an argument
on that oath.

## Where the horns end up

*And I looked, and behold, in the midst of the throne… **stood a Lamb as though it had been
slain***. See the note on Revelation 5:6–8.

> **The timing.** The ram was in the thicket before Abraham looked up — caught by its horns,
> which is a way of being stuck that takes a while to happen.
