Written after the worst thing David ever did, and the astonishing thing is the
direction he faces.

## When

The heading on the psalm says it: *when Nathan the prophet came to him, after he
had gone in to Bathsheba.*

Adultery, and then a man murdered by arrangement to cover it. A year of silence
before the prophet arrived.

## "Never leaves my sight"

*For I know my rebellion, **my sin never leaves my sight**.*

The Hebrew is that it is *before me continually*. He is not confessing something
he had forgotten.

## "Against You, You only"

*Against You, **You only**, have I sinned, and done what is evil in Your sight.*

Uriah is dead. Bathsheba's life is wrecked. A child will die. And David says
*against You only*.

He is not dismissing them. He is naming who the offence is finally against — the
psalm is addressed to God and he refuses to spread the account across other
defendants.

## The sentence that follows

*So **You are right when You speak, You are clear when You judge**.*

That is the move. He does not argue for a mitigating circumstance. He rules in
God's favour before God has said anything.

The Hebrew is that God is *justified* in His speaking and *clear* in His judging.

## Set against Job

God's one charge against Job, out of the whirlwind: *would you condemn Me so that
you could be in the right?*

Two positions and only one can be occupied. Job took his own for thirty chapters
and God had to come out of a storm about it.

David takes the other one at the top of the psalm, before anything is asked of
him. He agrees God is right and puts himself in the wrong, and everything he
receives in the rest of the psalm comes down that channel. See the note on
Job 40:6–8.

## What he asks for next

*Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean. Wash me, and I shall be whiter than
snow.*

Hyssop is the little bunch of herb used to put blood on the doorframes at
Passover, and to sprinkle a cleansed leper.

He asks to be handled the way a leper is handled — by an application of blood he
does not administer himself.

## And what he does not offer

*You do not delight in sacrifice, or I would bring it… **the sacrifices of God are
a broken spirit**.*

A king with a treasury and a tabernacle says the one offering that would work is
not something he can fund.

> **What he asks not to lose.** *Do not cast me from Your presence, and do not take
> Your Holy Spirit from me.* Under the old covenant the Spirit came and went — he
> had watched it happen to Saul. It is a prayer no believer since Pentecost has had
> to pray.
