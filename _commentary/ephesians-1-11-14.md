The end of a single sentence that has run since verse 3, and it closes on a deposit.

## The inheritance

*In Him **we were also given a share in what is His**.*

The Greek is **eklerothemen** — we were allotted, we obtained an inheritance.

**Kleros** — a lot, the piece of ground assigned by casting lots. The word behind *clergy* and
*inheritance*.

## The design

*Marked out beforehand by the design of **the One who works everything according to what He decides
and wants**.*

The Greek is **kata ten boulen tou thelematos autou** — according to the counsel of His will.

Two words for intention: **boule**, deliberate purpose, and **thelema**, will.

## The purpose

*So that **we, who were the first to put our hope in Christ**, would live to the praise of His glory.*

The Greek is **tous proelpikotas** — those who first hoped.

Probably Jewish believers. The next verse turns to *you also*, the Gentile readers.

## The turn

*And **you too**.*

The Greek is **en ho kai humeis** — in whom you also.

## The sequence

*When you **heard the word of truth**, the good news that rescued you, and when you **believed in
Him**, you were **stamped with the seal**.*

Three steps, in order: heard, believed, sealed.

## The seal

*You were **stamped with the seal of the promised Holy Spirit**.*

The Greek is **esphragisthete** — you were sealed.

**Sphragizo** — to press a signet into wax or clay.

## What a seal did

Two things: marked ownership, and guaranteed that what was sealed had not been tampered with.

A merchant's seal on a cargo said whose it was and that it was intact.

## Who the seal is

The Spirit Himself. Not something He gives.

## The deposit

*Who is **the first payment of everything that is coming to us**.*

The Greek is **arrabon** — a down payment, an earnest.

A Semitic loanword into Greek, and a commercial one. In modern Greek it still means an engagement
ring.

## What an arrabon was

Not a token. The first instalment of the actual sum, which legally obliges the rest.

Paul uses it twice more: *who also has given us the Spirit as a guarantee*.

## The term

*The pledge held for us **until the day the people He purchased are set free entirely**.*

The Greek is **eis apolutrosin tes peripoieseos** — unto the redemption of the possession.

**Apolutrosis** — the buying back. The word for ransoming a slave.

## What is still owed

The body.

*Even we ourselves groan within ourselves, eagerly waiting for the adoption, **the redemption of our
body**.* See the note on Romans 8:18–22.

The deposit is the Spirit; the balance is a resurrection.

## The refrain

*To the praise of His glory.*

Three times in the paragraph — verses 6, 12, and 14.

The first is attached to the Father's choosing, the second to the Son's work, the third to the
Spirit's sealing.

## The sentence

Verses 3 to 14 are one sentence in Greek. Over two hundred words without a full stop.

Paul starts *blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ* and does not come up for
air.

> **The word.** **Arrabon** — a first instalment of the actual sum, which legally commits the
> payer to the rest. Not a token, and not a promise. Part of the money.
