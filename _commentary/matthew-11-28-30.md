The most quoted invitation in the Gospels, and the picture in the middle of it
is the part most likely to be missed by a reader who has never worked an animal.

## A yoke is for two

The **zygos** is the wooden beam across the necks of a pair of oxen. A single ox
does not wear one. The whole point of the device is that two animals pull
together and the load is shared between them.

So *take My yoke on your shoulders* is not an offer of a lighter burden to carry
alone. It is an offer to be paired with Him. That is why the sentence can hold
two things that sound contradictory — come to Me and rest, and take up a yoke —
without any strain. The rest is not the absence of work. It is the end of
pulling alone.

## "My yoke"

The emphasis in the Greek falls on the possessive, and it matters, because
everybody He was speaking to already had a yoke on.

The rabbis of the period spoke of the *yoke of the law* — the teaching a
disciple took on from his teacher. Jesus is not asking people to pick up a
burden they had been free of. He is telling them to swap.

That is also why the next line is *learn from Me*. It is the language of
apprenticeship, and the reason He gives for coming is not that He is powerful:
*because I am gentle, and I am humble in heart*. He recommends Himself on His
manner, which is not how anyone recruits disciples.

## The word behind "fits easily"

**Chrestos.** It means kind, good, serviceable — and it was used of things that
were well made, and of wine that had mellowed. In the Greek Old Testament it is
the word in *taste and see that the LORD is good*.

Translating it as *easy* has done some damage, because it sounds like a promise
that nothing will be demanding. The word is nearer to *this one fits*. A badly
made yoke rubs an animal raw over a day's work. A well-made one is shaped to the
neck wearing it.

The claim is not that there is no work. It is that this yoke will not take the
skin off you.

## Who is invited

*All of you who are worn out and carrying too much.* The two words describe
being exhausted by labour and being loaded up by someone else.

There is no other entry condition in the sentence. Not repentance first, not
understanding first. Tiredness is the qualification, which is a strange basis for
an invitation and is exactly what makes this one memorable.

> **Where else this word turns up.** *Rest* here is the same idea running through
> Hebrews 3 and 4 — a rest already prepared, entered by coming rather than by
> achieving. Matthew's version puts a person and a yoke at the centre of it: the
> rest is not a place so much as a partnership.
