The last page of the harshest of the prophets, and it turns.

## What has come before

Eight and a half chapters of judgement. Amos was a shepherd and a dresser of
sycamore figs, not a professional prophet, and the book is unrelenting: *I hate, I
despise your feast days… take away from Me the noise of your songs.*

Five visions of destruction, and then this.

## The word

*"In that day I will **raise up David's fallen shelter**."*

The Hebrew is **sukkah** — a booth, a hut, the temporary shelter Israel lived in
during the Feast of Tabernacles.

Not the house of David, and not the throne. A **shelter**, and a fallen one.

The dynasty is described at its lowest — not a palace but a lean-to, and that
collapsed.

## The four verbs

*I will **raise up**… I will **mend** its broken walls… **set its ruins right**…
**build it as it was in the days of old**.*

Every one of them is His. Nothing in the sentence is asked of anybody.

## Who it is for

*"**So they may possess what is left of Edom and all the nations that bear My
name**."*

Edom — Esau's people, Israel's oldest enemy. And *all the nations that bear My
name*.

Gentiles, named in the promise, in the last paragraph of Amos.

## What James did with it

Acts 15, the Jerusalem council. The question is whether Gentiles have to be
circumcised and keep the law to belong.

Peter reports what happened at Cornelius's house. Paul and Barnabas describe the
miracles among the nations. And then James stands up and quotes this passage:

*"**After this I will return and will rebuild the tabernacle of David, which has
fallen down**… so that the rest of mankind may seek the LORD, even all the Gentiles
who are called by My name."*

His conclusion: *therefore I judge that we should not trouble those from among the
Gentiles who are turning to God.*

The verse that settled the first great church controversy is the second-to-last
paragraph of Amos.

## What the tabernacle of David was

Not the tabernacle of Moses. David pitched a tent for the ark in Jerusalem and left
it there, with singers and musicians before it continually — no veil, no sacrifice
system, and access that the Mosaic arrangement did not allow.

It stood for about forty years, until Solomon's temple.

## What follows

*"**The days are coming** when the plowman will overtake the reaper."* See the note
on Amos 9:13–15.

> **How the book ends.** *"I will plant them in their own land, and **never again will
> they be uprooted**," says the LORD your God.* Nine chapters of demolition, and the
> last word is planting.
