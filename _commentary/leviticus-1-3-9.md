The first offering in the book, and the distinguishing feature is that none of it comes back.

## The specification

*Let it be **a male without flaw**.*

The Hebrew is **tamim** — whole, complete, without blemish. **Mum** is the noun for what
disqualifies. See the note on Song of Songs 4:6–7.

## Where it is brought

*To **the entrance of the Tent of Meeting**.*

The door. Not inside, and not at a distance.

## The manner

*Offering it **freely**, so that **you may be welcomed and accepted** before the LORD.*

The Hebrew is *of his own free will* — **lirtsono**, according to his pleasure. And
**leratson**, for acceptance.

The same root does both jobs: he brings it willingly, and it is received favourably. The
worshipper's willingness and God's acceptance are the same word.

## The hand

*Lay your hand on the animal's head, and **it will be received on your behalf, standing in
your place to make things right for you**.*

The Hebrew is **samakh** — to lean, to press down with weight. Not a touch.

A man puts his weight on the head of an animal that is about to die, and the transaction runs
in both directions: what he is goes onto it, and what it is comes back to him.

*And the LORD has laid on Him the iniquity of us all.* See the note on Isaiah 53:4–6.

## The killing

*There **the bull is put to death before the LORD**.*

The worshipper kills it, not the priest. Leviticus is precise about this — the man who
brought it does the slaughtering.

## The blood

*Aaron's sons, the priests, bring its blood and **sprinkle it against the sides of the
altar**.*

The priest's work starts with the blood. Everything before that is the man's.

## The dismembering

*The animal is then **skinned and cut into pieces**.*

The hide comes off, and it is the one part not burnt. The priest keeps it. See the note on
Leviticus 7:1–10.

Everything else goes on the fire.

## The washing

*The **inner parts and the legs are washed clean with water**.*

The two least presentable parts. The entrails and the lower legs, which is where the dirt is.

They are washed and then burnt with the rest, which means the fire receives nothing unclean.

## The totality

*Then the priest **burns it all on the altar — the whole animal, given entirely to God**.*

The Hebrew name of this offering is **olah** — from **alah**, to go up. What ascends.

The old English name is *holocaust*, from the Greek: wholly burnt.

Of the five offerings, this is the only one where nobody eats anything.

## The result

*A gift by fire, **a fragrance that pleases the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **reach nichoach** — a soothing aroma, a restful smell. From the root for rest,
the same as Noah's name.

Its first use is after the flood, when Noah builds an altar: *and the LORD smelled a soothing
aroma*.

Paul uses it of Christ: *an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweet-smelling aroma*. And
of a gift sent to him in prison: *a sweet-smelling aroma, an acceptable sacrifice, well
pleasing to God*.

## Where the fire came from

Leviticus 9: *fire came out from before the LORD and consumed the burnt offering… when all
the people saw it, they shouted and fell on their faces*.

The altar fire was lit once, and the standing order was that it never went out.

> **The one offering nobody eats.** Skin removed, entrails washed, and the rest burnt entire —
> and the phrase for the smoke means *a restful smell*.
