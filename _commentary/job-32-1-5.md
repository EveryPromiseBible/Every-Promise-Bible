Twenty-nine chapters of argument end because everyone has run out of words, and a
young man who has been sitting there the whole time stands up.

## Why the three stopped

*There was nothing left to say, because **in Job's own eyes he was in the right**,
and no argument of theirs could move him.*

That is the writer's own summary, not a friend's accusation. After everything they
threw at him, Job's settled position is that he is right.

He had said it himself in chapter 29 — *I wrapped myself in righteousness, and it
clothed me* — and in that speech he refers to himself well over forty times and to
God about five. See the note on Job 29:14–17.

The three men's preaching did not humble him. It hardened him.

## Elihu

*But there was a young man sitting by, **listening to it all**.*

He has been present since chapter 3 and this is the first the reader hears of him.
Nine chapters of speeches, and he was in the room for every one.

*Elihu, son of Barachel the Buzite, from the family of Ram.* The fullest genealogy
given to anyone in the book.

His name means *He is my God*.

## What he is angry at

Two things, and they are stated separately:

- *angry at **Job** for making himself out to be more in the right than God*
- *angry at **the three friends** too, because they had found no real answer, yet
  they had gone right on condemning Job anyway*

He is the only person in the book who is against both sides of the argument. And
his charge against the three is precise: they had no answer, and they kept
condemning anyway.

## Why he waited

*All this time Elihu had **held his tongue, waiting**, because the others were
older than he was and it seemed only fitting.*

Nine chapters of silence out of respect.

## What follows him

Elihu speaks for six chapters. Nobody interrupts him. Job — who answered every one
of the other three, at length, ten times over — does not say a word back.

And when Elihu finishes, the next verse is *then the LORD answered Job out of the
whirlwind.*

## How the argument ends

*So these three men **ceased answering Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes**.*

They gave up. Thirty chapters of argument end not in agreement but in exhaustion.

## What Elihu is angry about

Two things, and the book states both: *against Job, because he justified himself rather than
God*, and *against his three friends, because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned
Job*.

He is the only person in the book angry at both sides, and on both counts he is closer to
right than they were.

## The reason for the silence

*Because they were years older than he.*

Four men had been talking for nine chapters and the youngest in the room said nothing until
they were finished.

**James**: *let every man be **swift to hear, slow to speak**, slow to wrath.*

## Who speaks after him

God, out of the whirlwind, without a pause. Elihu finishes, and *then the LORD answered Job out
of the storm* — and unlike the three, Elihu is never rebuked at the end.

**Jesus**: *"everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and **he who humbles himself will be
exalted**."*

> **What he says he is not doing.** *I will not show partiality to anyone, nor
> flatter any man, for I do not know how to flatter — my Maker would soon take me
> away.* The three came to defend a system. He says at the outset that he has no
> side.
