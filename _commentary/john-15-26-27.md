The fourth of the Helper passages, and this one gives Him a job with a courtroom name.

## Where it sits

At the end of the chapter about hatred: *if the world hates you, you know that it hated Me
before it hated you*.

And the quotation from the psalm — *they hated Me without a cause*. See the note on
John 15:22–25.

Then this. The paragraph about being hated ends with an arrangement for testimony.

## The title

*"**When the Helper comes**."*

The Greek is **parakletos** — one called alongside. An advocate, a counsel for the defence, a
supporter in court.

John uses it four times in this discourse, and once in his letter, of Christ: *we have an
Advocate with the Father*.

The word is legal.

## Who sends Him

*"Whom **I will send to you from the Father** — the Spirit of truth, **who comes from the
Father**."*

Both, in one sentence. Sent by the Son, proceeding from the Father.

This clause is the one the Eastern and Western churches split over in the eleventh century —
the **filioque**, whether the Spirit proceeds from the Father *and the Son*. The Great Schism
runs through this verse.

## The name

*"**The Spirit of truth**."*

John's usual title for Him. He uses it three times in the discourse.

Not the Spirit of power, in this Gospel. Truth.

## The work

*"**He will be the witness to the truth about Me**."*

The Greek is **marturesei peri emou** — He will testify concerning Me.

The Spirit's stated function here is to give evidence about somebody else.

Jesus says the same in the next chapter: *He will not speak on His own authority… **He will
glorify Me**, for He will take of what is Mine and declare it to you*. See the note on
John 16:12–15.

## The second witness

*"**And you also are to tell it**."*

The same verb. **Martureite** — you bear witness.

Two witnesses, which is what the law required: *by the mouth of two or three witnesses the
matter shall be established*.

The Spirit and the disciples, testifying about the same person.

## Their qualification

*"Because **you have been with Me from the beginning**."*

Not training. Presence.

It is the criterion Peter applies when they replace Judas: *of these men who have accompanied
us all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us… one of these must become a
witness with us of His resurrection*.

## Where the pairing turns up

*And we are His witnesses to these things, and so also is the Holy Spirit whom God has given to
those who obey Him.*

Peter, before the Sanhedrin, in Acts 5. The same two witnesses, standing in an actual court.

## The word that came from it

**Martus** — witness. It drifted into English as *martyr*, because of what happened to the
people in this room.

## What follows immediately

*"These things I have spoken to you, **that you should not be made to stumble**. They will put
you out of the synagogues; yes, the time is coming that whoever kills you will think that he
offers God a service."*

And the reason He is telling them in advance: *that when the time comes, you may remember that
I told you of them*.

Which is what Saul of Tarsus thought he was doing.

## What the Helper is for

Not comfort in the modern sense. The old English *comforter* is from **confortare** — to
strengthen.

He is described in this Gospel as teaching, reminding, convicting, guiding, and — here —
testifying.

> **The Spirit's stated job in this verse.** Evidence about somebody else. And the disciples
> are the second witness, on the strength of having been in the room.
