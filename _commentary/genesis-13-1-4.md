He goes back the way he came, and stops at the exact spot he left.

## Up from Egypt

*So Abram **went up from Egypt** to the Negev, with his wife and everything he had.*

The Hebrew is always *up* from Egypt and *down* into it, regardless of the map. The
direction of travel in Scripture is theological before it is geographical.

What he is coming up from is the episode where he called Sarai his sister and Pharaoh
took her into the palace. See the note on Genesis 12:10–20.

## What he came out with

*Abram had become **very wealthy in livestock, silver, and gold**.*

The Hebrew is **kabed** — heavy. The word for glory shares the root.

He arrived in Egypt driven by famine, told a half-truth that cost him his wife, was
found out by a pagan king and escorted to the border — and he leaves heavy.

Nothing is taken back off him. Verse 2 sits there without a word of comment.

## Everything he had

*With his wife and **everything he had**, and Lot went with him.*

Sarai is back. Lot is still with him — and the wealth in this verse is what makes them
part company nine verses later, because *the land was not able to support them dwelling
together.*

## The return route

*From the Negev he journeyed **from place to place** until he came back to Bethel.*

The Hebrew is *by his journeys* — the same stages, in reverse. He retraces the trip.

## The place

*To **the spot between Bethel and Ai** where he **had first pitched his tent and built
an altar**.*

Bethel means *house of God*. Ai means *ruin*. He pitches between them, as he had
before.

Moses records the location twice — in chapter 12 and again here — with the same
wording, so the reader knows it is the same ground.

## What he does there

*There Abram **called on the name of the LORD once more**.*

He goes back to the altar and picks up where he stopped.

There is no sacrifice recorded, no confession, no vow. He calls on the name, which is
what he had been doing before he went down.

## What is not in the passage

No rebuke. God does not appear in chapter 13 until Lot has gone, and when He does the
first words are *lift up your eyes now and look.*

Moses tells the Egypt story plainly and then tells this one plainly, and leaves the
two side by side.

> **What the altar was for.** He built one at Shechem when the land was promised, and
> one here. He builds another at Hebron at the end of the chapter. The altars mark the
> route, and this one was still standing when he came back to it.
