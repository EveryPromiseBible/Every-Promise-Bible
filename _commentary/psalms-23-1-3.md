The best-known psalm in the world, and the verbs in its first three lines are all
His.

## Who does what

*He lays me down… He leads me… He restores… He guides me.*

Four verbs, one subject. The sheep in this psalm is not described as following
well, finding pasture, or keeping up. Everything active belongs to the shepherd.

The one thing said about the sheep is *I lack nothing* — a state, not an
achievement.

## Lying down

*He lays me down in green pastures.*

Sheep do not lie down when they are hungry, frightened, or bothered by other
animals. A resting flock is the sign that every one of those has been dealt with.

The Hebrew has the shepherd causing it. He does not merely permit the rest; he
brings the conditions about.

## Still waters

The phrase is *waters of rest* — quiet water, water that has stopped moving.
Sheep will not drink from a fast stream; the noise and movement frighten them,
and a wet fleece will drown them.

A shepherd in that country dammed a pool or found a still place. The provision is
shaped to what the animal can actually use.

## "He restores my soul"

The Hebrew is *he brings my soul back* — the verb for returning, turning around.
It is used elsewhere of bringing back a wanderer.

Not merely refreshment. Retrieval.

## The reason at the end

*He guides me along right paths **for the sake of His name**.*

The motive is stated, and it is not the sheep's merit. The shepherd's reputation
is bound up with where the flock ends up.

> **What the psalm does not skip.** Verse 4 goes straight into the valley of the
> shadow of death, and the shepherd is still there — *You are with me.* The green
> pastures and the valley are on the same path, led by the same person.
