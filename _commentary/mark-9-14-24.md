The most honest sentence anyone says in the Gospels, and Jesus answers it.

## The father's careful wording

*If there is anything You can do, have pity on us and help us.*

He has been living with this for years — *since he was small* — and he has just
watched the disciples fail. The *if* is not rudeness. It is a man who has stopped
expecting.

Notice also *help **us***. He is not only asking for the boy.

## What Jesus does with the "if"

*If you can believe — everything is possible for the one who believes.*

He picks up the father's own word and hands it back. The man had put the *if* on
Jesus' ability; Jesus moves it.

This is worth reading carefully, because it can be made cruel. Jesus is not
saying the healing depends on the father generating enough certainty — the next
thirty seconds rule that out.

## The answer

*Lord, I do believe. Help me with the part of me that does not.*

Both halves at once, and the man does not pretend. He has faith and he has
unbelief, and he says so, and he asks for help with the second.

Jesus heals the boy immediately. No further requirement, no waiting for the doubt
to clear.

> **What that settles.** Whatever "believing" means in this passage, it evidently
> includes a man who admits mid-sentence that he does not fully believe. The
> faith that works here is not a quantity achieved. It is a request made to the
> right person, doubt and all.
