Seven proverbs, and the second one describes something no household would have allowed.

## The servant in the will

*A **wise servant will rule over a shameful son** and **share the inheritance among the
brothers**.*

The Hebrew is **eved maskil** — a servant who acts with insight. And **yachaloq nachalah** — he
will divide the inheritance in the midst of brothers.

A slave had no standing to inherit anything. The proverb has him not merely receiving a portion
but dividing it out among the sons.

## Who took that position

*But **made Himself of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant**, and coming in the
likeness of men.*

**Paul**'s word is **doulos** — a slave. And the same paragraph ends with *God has highly
exalted Him*, which is the proverb's first clause.

*For both He who sanctifies and those who are being sanctified are all of one, for which reason
**He is not ashamed to call them brethren**.*

*And if children, then heirs — heirs of God and **joint heirs with Christ**.*

**Hebrews** supplies the brothers and **Romans** supplies the division. The Servant who acted
with insight now shares the inheritance among sons who had disgraced themselves, which is the
proverb read forward.

## The crucible

*The crucible is for silver, the furnace for gold, but **the LORD is the one who tests hearts**.*

**Matzreph** — the refiner's pot. **Kur** — the smelting furnace. **Bochen** — assays, tests for
purity, the word used of a metallurgist proving a sample.

*That the genuineness of your faith, being much more precious than gold that perishes, **though
it is tested by fire**, may be found to praise, honour and glory **at the revelation of Jesus
Christ**.*

**Peter** keeps the metal and the fire and moves the verdict to a day. The testing in the proverb
has no stated outcome; in **1 Peter** the outcome is praise, and it is handed to the person
tested.

*"He will sit as **a refiner and a purifier of silver**."*

## Mocking the poor

*Whoever **mocks the poor insults their Maker**.*

The proverb from chapter 14, repeated with a harder verb — **lo'eg**, to deride, to jeer.

*"Inasmuch as you did it to one of the least of these My brethren, **you did it to Me**."*

And at a cross: *"He saved others; let Him save Himself"* — the only place in Scripture where
the deriding and the Maker are the same scene. The Greek in **Luke** is **exemykterizon**, to
turn up the nose.

## The dry crust

*Better **a dry crust with quiet** than a house full of feasting and strife.*

The same measure as chapter 15's plate of greens, and **Luke** records the third option: a house
where the fatted calf is killed *because* the son came home. See the note on
Proverbs 15:16–23.

> **What the second proverb allows.** A slave dividing the inheritance among the sons. Nothing
> in that household's law permitted it — and Philippians has a Servant exalted, Hebrews has Him
> calling them brothers, and Romans has them made joint heirs.
