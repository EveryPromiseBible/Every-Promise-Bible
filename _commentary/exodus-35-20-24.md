The materials for the tabernacle arrive out of the crowd's own possessions.

## The going out

*Then **the whole community of Israel went out from Moses' presence**.*

He has just read them the whole specification — the tent, the coverings, the ark, the table, the
lampstand, the altar, the court, the priestly garments.

And the invitation: *whoever is of a willing heart, let him bring it as an offering to the LORD*.

## The two clauses

*And **everyone whose heart stirred him, everyone whose spirit made him willing**, came back
carrying an offering.*

The Hebrew is **kol-ish asher-nesa'o libbo** — whose heart lifted him.

And **kol asher nadevah rucho** — whose spirit made him willing.

**Nadav** — to volunteer, to offer freely. The root behind **nedavah**, freewill offering.

## What is not there

A levy, a quota, or a census-based assessment.

Both phrases in the verse are about interior motion.

## What it was for

*For **the work on the tent of meeting**, for all its service, and **for the holy garments**.*

## Who brought

*They came, **men and women alike**, all with willing hearts.*

The Hebrew is **ha'anashim al-hannashim** — the men with the women.

Named explicitly, in a context where the priesthood is male and the whole cultic apparatus is
male.

## The gold

*Bringing **brooches and earrings, rings and pendants** — every kind of gold jewelry.*

Personal ornament. Not bullion.

Everything on the list is something somebody was wearing.

## Where the gold came from

*And the LORD had given the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians, so that **they granted
them what they requested**. Thus they plundered the Egyptians.*

Four hundred years of unpaid labour, and the wages came out at the end in jewellery.

## The other thing that was made of it

*And he received the gold from their hand, and he fashioned it with an engraving tool, and **made a
moulded calf**.* See the note on Exodus 32:1–4.

The same category of material, out of the same ears — *break off the golden earrings which are in
the ears of your wives*.

Chapter 32 and chapter 35 are the same gold going two directions.

## The gesture

*Each one **lifting up his gold as a wave offering to the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **tenuphah** — a wave offering, the gesture of lifting and moving something before
the LORD.

The technical term for a sacrificial rite, applied to jewellery.

## The other materials

*Everyone who had **blue, purple, or scarlet yarn, fine linen, goat hair, ram skins dyed red, or
fine leather** brought it.*

*Everyone who could give an offering of **silver or bronze** brought it… and everyone who had
**acacia wood** the work could use brought that too.*

Three tiers, and the phrasing accommodates each: those who had gold, those who could give silver
or bronze, those who had wood.

Nobody is excluded by what they happened to own.

## Who spins

*Every woman with skill **spun with her own hands** and brought what she had spun.* See the note on
Exodus 35:25–29.

Labour as well as material. The yarn is made, not donated.

## What happens

*The people are bringing **far more than we need**.* See the note on Exodus 36:4–7.

Moses has to issue an order to stop.

> **The two phrases.** *Whose heart lifted him*, and *whose spirit made him willing*. No levy, no
> quota — the whole tabernacle is funded out of two clauses about interior motion.
