The bread is described, and then a sample of it is put away where nobody will eat it.

## The name

**16:31** *Israel named it **manna**.*

The Hebrew is **man hu** — *what is it?* Exodus says so earlier in the chapter, **16:15** again: *when the
children of Israel saw it, they said to one another, "What is it?" For they did not know
what it was.*

The name is the question they asked, and it stuck.

## The description

Still **16:31**: *It was **white, like coriander seed**, and it **tasted like wafers made with honey**.*

Numbers adds, **11:8** again: *its taste was like the taste of pastry prepared with oil*, and that they
ground it in mills, beat it in mortars, cooked it in pans and made cakes of it.

Two descriptions of the same thing, and both are about how good it was.

## What it was for

**16:32** *"**Keep an omer of it for the generations to come**, so they can **see the bread I fed you
in the wilderness when I brought you out of Egypt**."*

An **omer** — about two litres, and the daily ration per person named earlier in the
chapter.

One day's food, kept.

## Why it is strange

The chapter has just established that manna could not be kept. Anyone who left it overnight
found it, **16:20** again, *full of worms and stank* — except on the sabbath eve.

The one jar is an exception to the rule the chapter spends twenty verses establishing.

## Where it went

**16:33** *"Take a jar, put an omer of manna in it, and **set it before the LORD**."*

**16:34**: *Aaron set it **before the Testimony**, to be preserved.*

The Testimony is the ark with the tablets in it. The jar goes into the holiest place in
Israel.

Hebrews lists the contents, **9:4**: *the ark of the covenant… in which were **the golden pot that
had the manna**, Aaron's rod that budded, and the tablets of the covenant.*

Three objects: bread, a dead stick that flowered, and stone.

## Why those three

Each is a record of something the nation got wrong. The manna is the complaining in the
wilderness, the rod is Korah's rebellion, and the tablets are what was broken over the
calf.

The ark holds the evidence, and the lid over it is the mercy seat, with blood on it once a
year.

## What Jesus says about it

*"Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and are dead. **This is the bread which
comes down from heaven, that one may eat of it and not die**."*

And: *"**I am the living bread** which came down from heaven."*

That is the discourse that emptied the room. See the note on John 6:60–65.

## What Revelation says

**Revelation 2:17** again: *"To him who overcomes I will give some of **the hidden manna** to eat."*

The jar in the ark, described as hidden — and offered.

## What they complained about

Numbers 11: *"**we remember the fish which we ate freely in Egypt**, the cucumbers, the
melons, the leeks, the onions, and the garlic. But now our whole being is dried up; **there
is nothing at all except this manna**."* See the note on Numbers 11:4–9.

The same food, twenty chapters apart, described as honey wafers and as nothing at all.

> **What the jar is.** A day's ration of bread that could not normally last a night, kept
> in a box in the holiest room in the nation — so that people who never ate it could see
> what their fathers were fed.
