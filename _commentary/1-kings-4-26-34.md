The height of the reign, described at length, with two facts in it the reader is meant to notice.

## The horses

*Solomon had **forty thousand stalls of horses** for his chariots and **twelve thousand horsemen**.*

Chronicles reads four thousand. The Hebrew numerals are easily confused in transmission.

## The problem with horses

*But **he shall not multiply horses for himself**, nor cause the people to return to Egypt to
multiply horses.*

Deuteronomy 17, the law of the king. See the note on Deuteronomy 17:14–20.

The narrator records the stalls without comment and lets the law do the work.

## The supply

*The district governors, each in his month, supplied everything King Solomon and all who came to
his table needed, and **never let a thing run short**.*

Twelve governors, one month each. The system worked.

*They also brought **barley and straw for the chariot horses**.*

## The gift

*And **God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure**, a heart as wide as **the sand on the
seashore**.*

The Hebrew is **rochav lev** — breadth of heart.

**Sand on the seashore** is the covenant image, used of Abraham's descendants. Applied here to the
capacity of one man's mind.

## The comparison

*His wisdom surpassed the wisdom of **all the people of the East** and all the wisdom of **Egypt**.*

The two recognised centres of learning. Mesopotamian omen literature and Egyptian instruction
texts were the ancient world's curriculum.

## The named rivals

*Wiser than **Ethan the Ezrahite**, and **Heman, Calcol, and Darda**, the sons of Mahol.*

Ethan and Heman have psalms attributed to them — Psalm 89 and Psalm 88.

The narrator names the competition, which means readers knew who they were.

## The output

*He spoke **three thousand proverbs**, and his songs numbered **a thousand and five**.*

Proverbs preserves a fraction. Two psalms are attributed to him, and the Song of Songs.

## The subjects

*He **described the trees**, from the great cedar of Lebanon to the small hyssop that grows out of
the wall; he taught of **animals and birds, of creeping things and fish**.*

Botany from the largest to the smallest, and then the four categories of Genesis 1.

His wisdom is described as natural history, not only aphorism.

## The traffic

*People came from **every nation** to listen to Solomon's wisdom, sent by **all the kings of the
earth** who had heard how wise he was.*

The queen of Sheba is the one the book gives a chapter to: *the half was not told me*.

See the note on 1 Kings 10:23–25.

## What the Hebrew says about the horses again

Chapter 10 closes the account: *also Solomon had horses imported from Egypt*.

The chapter that begins with wisdom beyond measure ends with the trade route the law named.

## What comes next

*But **King Solomon loved many foreign women**.* See the note on 1 Kings 11:1–3.

Seven hundred wives, three hundred concubines, and *his wives turned his heart*.

## The verdict

*So the LORD became angry with Solomon, because **his heart had turned from the LORD God of Israel,
who had appeared to him twice**.*

The heart that was as wide as the sand.

## What he asked for

*"Give to Your servant an **understanding heart** to judge Your people."* See the note on
1 Kings 3:5–14.

The Hebrew was **lev shomea** — a hearing heart.

The book uses the word *heart* at every stage: the request, the gift, and the turning.

> **The word.** *Heart* runs through the whole account — a hearing heart asked for, a heart wide as
> the sand given, and a heart turned away in chapter 11.
