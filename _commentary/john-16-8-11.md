Three things the Spirit will prove the world wrong about, and each reason is unexpected.

## The verb

*"And when He comes, **He will show the world how wrong it has been**."*

The Greek is **elenxei ton kosmon** — will convict the world.

**Elencho** — to expose, to cross-examine, to prove wrong. A courtroom word for demonstrating a
case.

## The three subjects

*About **sin**, and about **righteousness**, and about **judgment**.*

## The first reason

*"About sin, **because they do not believe in Me**."*

Not because they break commandments.

The sin the Spirit presses is the refusal of the person, and the reason is stated in the singular:
one thing, not a catalogue.

## Why that framing

The law already convicts of conduct. *By the law is the knowledge of sin.*

What is new is that the sin now at issue is a verdict on a man.

## The second reason

*"About righteousness, **because I am going to My Father and you will not see Me any more**."*

Which is the strangest clause in the three.

## What it means

The world had judged Him a blasphemer and executed Him.

The ascension reverses the verdict. A man the court condemned is received by the Father, and His
disappearance is the evidence.

The disciples cannot produce Him. His absence is the proof.

## The third reason

*"About judgment, **because the ruler of this world stands condemned already**."*

The Greek is **kekritai** — perfect passive. Has been judged, and the judgment stands.

## When

Two chapters earlier: *the ruler of this world is coming, and he has nothing in Me*.

And in chapter 12: *now is the judgment of this world; **now the ruler of this world will be cast
out***.

The trial is over. What remains is the sentence.

## What the three have in common

Every one of them is decided by something that happened to Jesus.

Sin, by what people do with Him. Righteousness, by where He went. Judgment, by what His death did
to the accuser.

None of the three is argued from behaviour.

## Who is convicted

*The world* — **ton kosmon**.

Not the disciples. The Spirit's work described here is external, and it is prosecutorial.

## What He does for the disciples instead

*"When He, **the Spirit of truth**, has come, He will guide you into all truth."* See the note on
John 16:12–15.

*"He will glorify Me, for He will take of what is Mine and declare it to you."*

Two different offices: convicting the world, and guiding the disciples.

## Why He had to leave first

*"**It is better for you that I go away**. If I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you."*
See the note on John 16:5–7.

## What happens seven weeks later

Peter, standing up with the eleven: *this Jesus, whom you crucified, God has made both Lord and
Christ*.

*Now when they heard this, they were **cut to the heart**, and said, "Men and brethren, what shall we
do?"*

Three thousand that day. The first sermon after the Spirit came convicts a crowd of exactly the
first item.

> **The second reason.** *Because I am going to My Father and you will not see Me any more.* The
> absence is the evidence — a man the court condemned, received.
