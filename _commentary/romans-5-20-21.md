Paul says what the law was for, and it is not what most people assume.

## Why it came

*The law **came in alongside**, so that **the trespass would increase**.*

The Greek for *came in alongside* is **pareiserchomai** — to slip in beside, to
enter as a secondary thing. It is not the main line of the argument; it comes in
next to it.

And the purpose clause is startling: *so that the trespass would increase.*

That is Paul's own account of what the law was given to do. Not to reduce sin — to
make it abound, to bring it out into the open where it could be seen for what it
is. He says the same thing in chapter 7: *sin, taking opportunity by the
commandment, produced in me all manner of evil desire.*

## The comparison

*But where sin increased, **grace overflowed all the more**.*

The Greek is **huperperisseuo** — Paul stacks *huper* (over, beyond) on top of a
word that already means to abound. Superabounded. He appears to have built the word
for this sentence.

Whatever height sin reached, grace is described as clearing it.

## The two reigns

*Just as **sin reigned in death**, **grace might reign through righteousness**.*

Both verbs are the same — to reign as a king. Paul is describing two thrones, and
only one of them can be occupied.

Sin's reign operated *in death*. Grace's reign operates *through righteousness*,
and its outcome is *eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.*

Note what grace reigns **through**. Not through leniency, and not by overlooking
anything. Through righteousness — which had to be supplied, and was.

## The question this provokes

Paul knows exactly what the next objection is, and he puts it himself at the top of
the very next paragraph:

*What shall we say, then? Shall we keep on sinning, so that there will be all the
more grace?*

Nobody asks that question of a message about trying harder. It is the question
grace always draws, and the fact that Paul has to answer it is a sign he had
preached grace the way he says he did.

His answer is not a rule. It is a funeral: *we died to sin; how can we go on living
in it any longer?* See the note on Romans 6:1–2.

## Where the chapter has been

The whole of Romans 5 is two men — *by one man's disobedience many were made
sinners, so also by one Man's obedience many will be made righteous.*

Adam's act reached everyone born after him without their doing anything. Paul's
argument is that Christ's act works the same way, and *much more*.

> **The phrase he keeps using.** *Much more.* Five times in this chapter. Paul is
> not arguing that grace matches what Adam did. He argues at every step that it
> exceeds it.
