Two images from a smallholding, applied to a household.

## What the psalm is

One of the Songs of Ascents — Psalms 120 to 134, sung by pilgrims going up to Jerusalem
for the feasts.

Short psalms, meant to be walked to.

## How it opens

*Blessed is every one who fears the LORD, who walks in His ways. **When you eat the labour
of your hands, you shall be happy, and it shall be well with you**.*

Eating what your own hands produced was not guaranteed in that world. Deuteronomy's curses
include *you shall plant a vineyard and not gather its grapes* and *another shall eat it*.

## The vine

*Your wife **within your house** will be like **a fruitful vine**.*

The Hebrew is *in the innermost parts of your house* — the private quarters.

A vine is not a decorative plant. It takes four years before it bears, it needs pruning
and staking, and it produces for generations.

Israel is called a vine repeatedly, and Jesus calls Himself the true one.

## The olive shoots

*Your children **around your table** will be like **young olive shoots**.*

The Hebrew is *olive plants* — the suckers that come up around the base of a mature olive
tree.

An olive takes fifteen years to bear properly and can live for centuries. The shoots at
the base are the next tree, growing from the same root, already there while the old one is
still producing.

## The two locations

*Within your house* and *around your table*.

Both are interior. The psalm's picture of blessing is a room.

## The verdict

*Look, **this is how the man is blessed who fears the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is *behold, thus shall the man be blessed*.

The psalm opened with the same clause and closes the first half by repeating it.

## What follows

*The LORD bless you **out of Zion**, and may you **see the good of Jerusalem all the days of
your life**. Yes, may you **see your children's children**.*

*Peace be upon Israel.*

The psalm widens from a table to a city to a nation in three lines, and the last word is
**shalom**.

## Where the psalm sits

Psalm 127 is directly before it — *unless the LORD builds the house, they labour in vain
who build it*, and *children are a heritage from the LORD*. See the note on
Psalms 127:1–2.

The two are usually read together: one says the building is God's, and this one describes
what is in the house.

## What is not in it

Anything spectacular. A wife, children, a table, food you grew yourself, and grandchildren
before you die.

The Songs of Ascents were sung by people walking to a feast, and this is the one about
what they had left at home.

> **What the two plants have in common.** Both take years, both come from a root, and both
> outlast the man they belong to. The psalm chooses the two slowest crops in the country.
