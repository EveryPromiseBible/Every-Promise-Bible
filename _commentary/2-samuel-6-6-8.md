The hardest paragraph in the David narrative, and the trouble started with a cart.

## What they were doing

Bringing the ark to Jerusalem. Thirty thousand chosen men, music, and *David and all the house
of Israel played music before the LORD on all kinds of instruments*.

It is a celebration, and the intention is right.

## What was wrong with it

*They set the ark of God on **a new cart**.*

New, expensive, and not the instruction.

The ark was to be carried on poles by Levites, on their shoulders. Exodus specifies it and
Numbers repeats it: *they shall not touch any holy thing, lest they die*.

The cart was the Philistines' method. When they sent it back they had no other option — two
milk cows and a new cart, and it worked, because they had not been told. See the note on
1 Samuel 6:10–12.

Israel copies the Philistines.

## The stumble

*When they came to **Nacon's threshing floor**, the oxen stumbled, and **the ark tipped**.*

A threshing floor is a flat exposed rock, usually swept. The wheels catch or the ground
changes.

## The reach

*Uzzah **reached out his hand and took hold of it to steady it**.*

The Hebrew is *he put out his hand to the ark of God and grasped it*.

The instinct is decent. Something holy is about to hit the dirt and he catches it.

## What happened

*And the anger of the LORD burned against Uzzah for what he had done, and **God struck him down
there beside the ark**, and he died **there in the presence of God**.*

The Chronicler adds the reason: *because he put his hand to the ark*.

## Why it is hard

Because the man was trying to help, and because the cart was somebody else's decision. Uzzah
was one of the household where the ark had been kept for twenty years; he had grown up with it
in the house.

Familiarity is the thing the text puts its finger on. A man who has walked past it every day
for two decades reaches out without thinking.

## David's reaction

*David's heart broke open. **He was angry that the LORD had burst out against Uzzah**.*

Both. Grief and anger, and the Hebrew records the anger without softening it.

## The name

*And to this day that place is called **Perez-uzzah** — "the breaking-out against Uzzah."*

The Hebrew is **perets** — a breach, a bursting through.

The same word David had used three chapters earlier, and joyfully: *the LORD has broken through
my enemies before me, like a breakthrough of water*. He named that place **Baal-perazim**.

Same noun. One is a victory and one is a death, and David names both.

## What he does about it

Stops. See the note on 2 Samuel 6:9–11.

Three months later he comes back and does it correctly, and the Chronicler records what he had
worked out in the meantime:

*"Because you did not do it the first time, the LORD our God broke out against us, **because we
did not consult Him about the proper order**."*

*And the children of the Levites **bore the ark of God on their shoulders, by its poles**, as
Moses had commanded.*

## What the cart was

A shortcut. Faster, easier, and borrowed from people who did not have the instructions.

Nothing about the celebration was insincere. The problem was the method, and the passage does
not pretend otherwise.

> **What the two names have in common.** *Perets* — a bursting through. David used it for a
> victory in chapter 5 and for a death in chapter 6, and both places kept the name.
