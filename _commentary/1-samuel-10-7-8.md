Two verses of instruction, and everything that goes wrong later goes wrong here.

## What has just happened

Samuel has poured oil on Saul's head privately and kissed him: *is it not because the LORD has
anointed you commander over His inheritance?*

Then three signs, given in advance: two men at Rachel's tomb with news about the donkeys, three
men at the terebinth tree of Tabor with bread, and a procession of prophets coming down from the
high place.

*Then the Spirit of the LORD will come upon you, and you will prophesy with them and **be turned
into another man**.*

## The first instruction

*"Once these signs come to pass, **do whatever your hand finds to do, for God is with you**."*

Wide latitude. No brief, no list.

The Hebrew is *do for yourself what your hand finds*.

## The second

*"Then **go down to Gilgal ahead of me**. I'll come to you there to offer burnt offerings and
fellowship offerings."*

A specific place, and a specific division of labour. Samuel does the offering.

## The third

*"**Wait seven days until I arrive** and tell you what to do."*

The Hebrew is **shivat yamim tochel** — seven days you shall wait.

Two commands in one sentence: wait the seven days, and then receive instructions.

The waiting is not the whole of it. The waiting is so that the instructions can arrive.

## What is odd about the placement

This instruction is given at the anointing, years before the crisis it applies to.

Some readers take it as a standing arrangement for any future emergency; others as a specific
appointment. Either way, Saul remembers it — the narrative in chapter 13 shows he was counting
days.

## What happens in chapter 13

Thirty thousand Philistine chariots at Michmash. The army hiding in caves. Men slipping across
the Jordan.

*He waited seven days, according to the time set by Samuel. But Samuel did not come to Gilgal,
and the people were scattered from him.*

He waits the full seven. Then he offers the burnt offering himself, and *as soon as he had
finished presenting the burnt offering, Samuel came*. See the note on
1 Samuel 13:11–14.

## What he missed

Not the seventh day. He missed the second half of the instruction.

*Wait until I come and show you what you shall do.*

He kept the count and did not wait for the content. Whatever Samuel arrived to say, Saul never
heard it.

## The verdict

*"You have not kept the command the LORD your God gave you. **If you had, He would have
established your kingdom over Israel for all time**."*

A dynasty, against a few minutes.

## What Saul says for himself

*"I saw the men scattering, and you didn't come when you said you would, and the Philistines
were massing… so **I forced myself** to do it."*

Every clause is factually correct, and he says out loud that he made himself do it.

## The instruction that is easy to miss

*Do whatever your hand finds to do, for God is with you.*

He was given enormous freedom in verse 7 and one fixed point in verse 8, and the one fixed point
is the one he broke.

## What the waiting was for

*"**Wait seven days**, till I come to you and show you what you should do."*

Seven days with an army melting away and the Philistines massing. Saul waits the whole seven,
and then offers the sacrifice himself in the last hour before Samuel arrives.

He kept the number and missed the point. The instruction was not *wait seven days*. It was
*wait until I come*.

*"Whoever believes will **not act hastily**."*

Isaiah's word, about a foundation stone laid in Zion — and Peter and Paul both apply that
stone to Christ.

## What he says for himself

*"I forced myself, therefore, and offered a burnt offering."*

The Hebrew is *I compelled myself*. He knows it is wrong while he is doing it, and does it
because the situation looks like it will not hold.

*Are you so foolish? **Having begun in the Spirit, are you now being made perfect by the
flesh?***

> **The half he did not keep.** He waited the seven days. The instruction was *wait until I come
> and tell you what to do* — and the telling never happened.
