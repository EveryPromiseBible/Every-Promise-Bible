Paul settles the Galatian question by going back four hundred and thirty years before the
law.

## The precedent

*It is just as it was with Abraham: **he believed God, and it was counted to him as
righteousness**.*

Genesis 15:6, quoted here and in Romans 4.

Paul's argument is chronological. The verse comes before circumcision in Genesis 17, and
centuries before Sinai.

## The word

**Counted** — the Greek is **logizomai**, the accounting term. It is entered on a ledger
by somebody else. See the note on Romans 4:6–8.

## The conclusion

*Understand, then, that **it is the people of faith who are the sons of Abraham**.*

The Greek is *those of faith* — **hoi ek pisteos**, those out of faith.

Not those of descent, and not those of observance.

This is the sentence the whole letter turns on. The Galatians were being told they needed
to become descendants of Abraham by circumcision; Paul says the descent is already
established by the same thing Abraham had.

## The Scripture that sees ahead

*And **the Scripture, seeing ahead of time** that God would set the nations right by
faith, **announced the good news to Abraham long before it came**.*

The Greek is **proeuengelisato** — pre-evangelised. Paul says the gospel was preached to
Abraham.

He personifies Scripture as the one doing the foreseeing.

## What was announced

*"**In you all the nations shall be blessed**."*

From Genesis 12, and repeated in the oath after Moriah. See the note on
Genesis 22:15–18.

Paul reads *all the nations* as the point. The Gentiles were in the promise from the
first sentence of it.

## The result

*So then, **those who are of faith are blessed together with Abraham, who believed**.*

The Greek is *blessed **with** believing Abraham* — **sun**, alongside.

Not blessed like him. Blessed with him, in the same act.

## What comes immediately after

*For as many as are **of the works of the law are under the curse**; for it is written,
"Cursed is everyone who does not continue in all things which are written in the book of
the law, to do them."*

Paul quotes Deuteronomy, and the sting is in **all things**. The law's own terms are
total.

And then: *Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, **having become a curse for
us** — for it is written, "Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree."*

## Why the letter exists

Men had come to Galatia teaching that Gentile converts must be circumcised. Paul opens
the letter without his usual thanksgiving — *I marvel that you are turning away so soon*
— and this chapter begins *O foolish Galatians! Who has bewitched you?*

## The verse the argument lands on

*That **the blessing of Abraham might come upon the Gentiles in Christ Jesus**, that we
might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.*

## What Paul says about the seed

*He does not say, "And to seeds," as of many, but as of one, "And to your Seed," **who is
Christ**.*

He rests an argument on a singular noun.

> **The order Paul insists on.** Faith counted as righteousness first, then circumcision,
> then four hundred and thirty years, then the law. He is not arguing about which is
> better — he is arguing about which came first, and what that makes the other one.
