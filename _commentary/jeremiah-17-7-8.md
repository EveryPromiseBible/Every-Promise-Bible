The second half of the pair, and the weather in it is the same.

## The two clauses

*Blessed is the one who **trusts in the LORD**, whose **confidence rests in Him**.*

Two verbs where the cursed man had three. The Hebrew for *confidence* is **mibtach** — a
refuge, the thing you put your weight on.

## The tree

*He is like **a tree planted by the water**.*

The Hebrew is *transplanted* — **shathal**, set out deliberately, not self-seeded.

Somebody put it there.

## The roots

*Sending its roots down **along the stream**.*

The Hebrew is *spreading out its roots by the river*.

The water is not falling on it from above. The tree is drawing from a source at its feet
that does not depend on the weather.

That is the whole mechanism of the passage.

## The heat

*It has **no fear when the heat comes**.*

The heat is not prevented. It arrives, and the tree does not react.

Jeremiah does not describe a sheltered life. He describes the same climate as verse 6.

## The leaves

*Its **leaves stay green**.*

The Hebrew is *its leaf shall be green*. Not merely alive. The colour is the same as it
was.

## The drought

*In **a year of drought it does not worry**.*

The Hebrew is *shall not be anxious*.

A year, not a week. The word for *anxious* is the same root Jeremiah uses elsewhere for
care and dread.

## The last line

*It **never stops bearing fruit**.*

The Hebrew is *shall not cease from yielding fruit*.

Not more fruit than usual and not fruit in season only. It does not stop.

## The comparison held

Both men in the pair face the same conditions — heat, drought, a year of it. See the note
on Jeremiah 17:5–6.

One is in a salt land and cannot see good when it comes. One is by a stream and does not
notice the drought.

## Where the image starts

Psalm 1: *he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that brings forth its
fruit in its season, whose leaf also shall not wither.*

The psalm's man delights in the law. Jeremiah's trusts in the LORD.

## What the same chapter says two verses later

*The heart is **deceitful above all things**, and desperately wicked; **who can know
it?**"*

And the answer, immediately: *I, the LORD, search the heart.*

Jeremiah sets the two trees on either side of a sentence about how little a man can tell
about his own inside.

## Where the tree turns up at the end of the Bible

*In the middle of its street, and on either side of the river, was **the tree of life**,
which bore twelve fruits, each tree yielding its fruit every month.*

A tree by a river, bearing every month.

> **Where the water is.** Not falling on the leaves. Underground, at the roots, reached
> by a system the drought never touches — and Jeremiah says the tree does not even
> register the year.
