The psalm reports God's anger and what He did about it, and the two are in the
wrong order.

## The anger

*So the LORD heard, and **He was furious**… **because they did not believe in God**,
they did not trust Him to save them.*

The charge is unbelief, and it is named twice.

The psalm is a long retelling of Israel's history, and it does not soften this.

## The word that follows

*Yet.*

Everything after that conjunction is provision.

## What He did

*He **commanded the skies above** and **opened the doors of heaven**. He **rained
down manna** for them to eat.*

Three verbs, all of them about the sky being opened.

The Hebrew for *doors* is the word for the doors of a house. The heavens are
described as a building with entrances.

## The two names for it

*He gave them **the grain of heaven**. Mortals ate **the bread of angels**.*

The Hebrew of the second is **lechem abbirim** — bread of the mighty ones. Young's
Literal keeps it that way: *food of the mighty.*

Some read it as angels' bread, some as the bread of the strong. Either way the point
is the same: it is not food that belongs to the eaters.

Men ate what came from somewhere above them.

## "To the full"

*He sent them **food to the full**.*

The Hebrew is *to satisfaction*. Not rations, not a subsistence measure.

## The sequence in the passage

Unbelief. Anger. And then the doors of heaven opening and bread falling on the camp.

The psalm puts the provision immediately after the complaint about the unbelief, and
does not explain the order.

## What the manna was

*Something fine and flaky, delicate as frost.* They asked *what is it?* — **man
hu** — and the question became the name. See the note on Exodus 16:13–15.

It fell six mornings a week for forty years and stopped the day they ate the produce
of the land.

## What Jesus does with this psalm

*"Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and are dead… **I am the living bread
which came down from heaven**. If anyone eats of this bread, he will live forever."*

He grants the comparison and then says what is wrong with it: they ate, and they
died.

The crowd had quoted this very psalm at Him — *He gave them bread from heaven to
eat.* See the note on John 6:47–51.

> **What the psalm says a few verses later.** *In spite of all this they still
> sinned, and did not believe in His wondrous works.* The psalm's whole method is to
> lay unbelief and provision side by side, over and over, for seventy-two verses.
