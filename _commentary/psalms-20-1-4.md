---
key: Psalms 20:1–4
title: May He remember all your offerings
updated: 2026-08-09
---
A blessing prayed over a king before a battle, and it asks God to remember something.

## Who is speaking

The congregation, over the king. Every line is second person — *may the LORD answer **you***
— and the psalm's second half switches to *now I know that the LORD saves His anointed*.

**Mashiach** — His anointed. The psalm is a prayer for the king and it uses the title the whole
Old Testament is reaching toward.

## What is asked first

*May **the name of the God of Jacob** keep you safe.*

Not the God of Abraham, who left everything at a word, or of Isaac, who never left the land.
**Jacob** — the supplanter, the one who cheated his brother and wrestled at the ford.

The name chosen for a battle prayer is the one attached to the least impressive of the three.

## Where the help comes from

*May He **send you help from the sanctuary** and support you from Zion.*

Not from the army, the armoury or the alliance. From the place the offerings were made.

## The line to stop on

*May He **remember all your offerings** and delight in your burnt sacrifice.*

The whole request rests on sacrifices already made. The prayer does not ask God to look at the
king's record, his cause or his readiness — it asks Him to remember what was burned on an
altar.

*For by one offering **He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified**.* And in the
same paragraph: *their sins and their lawless deeds **I will remember no more**.*

**Hebrews** puts the two together — one sacrifice remembered, and the sins forgotten. Psalm 20
asks for exactly that arrangement and did not yet have the offering that could carry it.

## What is asked last

*May He **give you the desire of your heart** and bring all your plans to pass.*

> **What the prayer is built on.** Not the king's fitness for the battle. *May He remember all
> your offerings* — a request that God would look at an altar rather than at a man.
