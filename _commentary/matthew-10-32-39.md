The end of the mission charge, and it is the hardest paragraph in it.

## Where it sits

He has sent the twelve out with instructions: no gold, no bag, no extra tunic, stay in worthy
houses, shake the dust off unresponsive towns.

And warnings: *behold, I send you out as sheep in the midst of wolves*. See the note on
Matthew 10:16–20.

*Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul.*

*Are not two sparrows sold for a copper coin? And not one of them falls to the ground apart from
your Father's will… **you are of more value than many sparrows**.*

Then this.

## The confession

*"**Whoever owns Me in front of others, I will own that person in front of My Father** in
heaven."*

The Greek is **homologesei en emoi** — confesses in Me. An unusual construction.

*"But whoever **disowns Me in front of others, I will disown** in front of My Father."*

The same verb both ways, and the second is the one Peter does in a courtyard. See the note on
Matthew 26:69–75.

Peter is standing in this room hearing it.

## The correction

*"**Do not imagine that I came to bring peace to the earth**."*

The Greek is **me nomisete** — do not suppose.

He heads off an assumption, which means it was being made.

The angels had announced *on earth peace, goodwill toward men*, and Isaiah had called Him
*Prince of Peace*.

## The sword

*"**I did not come to bring peace, but a sword**."*

Luke's version has *division* where Matthew has *sword*, which is the interpretation.

The sword is not something He wields. It is what happens when He is introduced into a
household.

## The households

*"I came to turn **a man against his father, a daughter against her mother, a daughter-in-law
against her mother-in-law**."*

He is quoting Micah, almost exactly: *for son dishonours father, daughter rises against her
mother, daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law; **a man's enemies are the men of his own
house**.*

Micah is describing a society in collapse. Jesus applies it to what following Him will cost.

## Why it happens

Because the confession in verse 32 has to be made somewhere, and the first place it is heard is
usually at home.

In a first-century Jewish or Roman household, that decision was not private. It affected the
family's standing, its trade and its worship.

## The comparison

*"**Anyone who loves father or mother more than Me is not worthy of Me**."*

The Greek is **huper eme** — above Me, more than Me.

Not *instead of*. The claim is comparative and it is placed above the fifth commandment's
object.

Nobody in that culture could hear it as anything but enormous.

## The cross

*"And **whoever does not take up his cross and walk behind Me** is not worthy of Me."*

The first mention of a cross in Matthew, and it comes long before His own.

The disciples hearing it knew what a man carrying a crossbeam was walking toward. They did not
yet know He would.

## The paradox

*"**Whoever clings to his life will lose it, and whoever lets his life go for My sake will find
it**."*

Matthew records this twice — here, and after Peter is called Satan. See the note on
Matthew 16:24–28.

## What follows immediately

*"He who receives you receives Me… and **whoever gives one of these little ones only a cup of
cold water** in the name of a disciple, assuredly, I say to you, he shall by no means lose his
reward."*

After the sword and the households and the cross: a cup of water.

The chapter ends on the smallest possible act.

> **What the sword is.** Not something He carries. It is what the confession in verse 32 does
> when it is first made — and the first room it is made in is usually the family's.
