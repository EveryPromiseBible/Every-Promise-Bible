He is riding into Jerusalem to shouts of *Hosanna*, the crowds are spreading their
cloaks on the road, and He is crying.

## The weeping

*When He came near enough to see the city, **He wept over it**.*

The Greek is **klaio** — to wail, to sob out loud. It is not the quiet word John
uses at Lazarus's tomb.

He is at the top of the Mount of Olives, with the whole city and the temple spread
out below in the sun, at the height of the only public acclamation He ever
received.

Nobody could tell Him anything about Himself from the size of a crowd.

## "On this day of all days"

*"If you had only known, **even now, on this day of all days**, what would bring
you peace."*

It is a broken sentence. He does not finish it.

**This day.** Nisan 10, the day the Passover lamb was taken into the house and set
aside — four days before it was killed. Israel was choosing its lamb that morning,
and the Lamb was coming down the hill.

## "Hidden from your eyes"

*"But it is hidden from your eyes."*

The peace was there. The seeing was not.

## What He describes next

*"Your enemies will **throw up a wall of earth around you** and close you in and
press you from every side… **they will not leave one stone standing on another**."*

Roman siege practice, described forty years before it happened. Titus built a wall
of earth right round Jerusalem in AD 70 to stop anyone escaping, and Josephus, who
was there, describes it — timber stripped from the countryside for miles.

The temple came down and the stones were prised apart.

## The reason He gives

*"— **because you did not recognise the time when God came to you**."*

The Greek is *the time of your visitation* — **episkope**, the moment of being
looked in on.

Not because of their sins in general. Because of a specific missed morning.

## What Daniel had already said

*After the sixty-two sevens, the Anointed One will be cut off… the people of the
prince who is to come will destroy the city and the sanctuary.*

Written in Babylon five hundred years earlier, in that order: the Messiah cut off
first, the city destroyed after. See the note on Daniel 9:26.

He is weeping over a date that was already on paper.

> **What He does immediately after.** Rides down the hill, walks into the temple,
> and turns over the tables. The weeping and the whip are the same afternoon. See
> the note on Matthew 21:12–17.
