Paul's word for what the law was, and it is a job title from a Greek household.

## The custody

*Before this faith came, we were **kept under guard by the law, shut in and waiting**.*

Two verbs. The Greek is **ephrouroumetha** — held in a garrison, guarded — and
**sunkleiomenoi**, shut up together, enclosed.

The same verb he used four verses earlier: *the Scripture shut everything up together
under sin*. See the note on Galatians 3:19–22.

## What it was waiting for

*For **the faith that was going to be revealed**.*

The custody has a terminus built into it.

## The word

*So the law was **our guardian**.*

The Greek is **paidagogos**, and it does not mean teacher.

A **paidagogos** was a household slave who walked a boy to school, carried his things, kept
him from trouble on the road, and handed him over at the door. He taught nothing. His job
was to get the child there and keep him safe on the way.

The post ended when the boy came of age.

## What he did

*Keeping us and **leading us to Christ**.*

The Greek is *unto Christ* — the destination.

## The purpose

*That we might be **made right by faith**.*

Not made right by the guardian. The guardian delivers the boy to the place where it
happens.

## The end

*But **now that faith has come, we are no longer under a guardian**.*

The Greek is *we are no longer under a paidagogos*.

Not that the guardian was bad, or failed, or was dismissed for cause. The child arrived.

## What Paul is not saying

That the law was cruel. A **paidagogos** was strict by design and the household paid for
one because the road was dangerous.

Paul's argument is entirely about the term of the appointment.

## What comes next

*For **you are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus**.*

The Greek is **huioi** — sons, adults, not **nepioi**, infants.

And then: *there is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is
neither male nor female; for **you are all one in Christ Jesus**.*

## The picture continued

The next chapter takes the same household further: an heir who owns everything and is
indistinguishable from a slave until the day his father set. See the note on
Galatians 4:1–3.

## What the Galatians were being told

To take on circumcision and the calendar. Paul's answer is that they are proposing to
hire the escort back after arriving.

He says it plainly at the end of chapter 4: *how is it that you turn again to the weak and
beggarly elements, to which you desire again to be in bondage?*

> **What the job was.** Not to teach and not to make anybody righteous. To keep a child
> safe and get him to the door — and Paul's whole point is that the appointment had an
> end date written into it.
