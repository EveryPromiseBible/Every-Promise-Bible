The last chapter of Zechariah, and the survivors of the nations that attacked Jerusalem come back
as pilgrims.

## Who goes up

*"Then **all the survivors from the nations that came against Jerusalem** will go up, year after
year, to worship the King."*

Not a remnant of Israel. The remnant of the armies.

The chapter has just described those nations gathered for war against the city. The people
climbing the hill to the festival are the ones who lost the siege.

*For **He Himself is our peace**, who has made both one, and has broken down the middle wall of
separation.*

## Which festival

*"To celebrate **the Festival of Booths**."*

**Sukkot** — Tabernacles. Of the three pilgrim feasts, it is the one where Israel moved out of
their houses for a week and lived in shelters made of branches, to remember the wilderness.

It is chosen here because it is the feast about God living with people who have nowhere
permanent.

*And the Word became flesh and **dwelt among us**.*

**John**'s verb is **eskenosen** — pitched His tent, from **skene**, the noun the Greek Old
Testament uses for the booths of this feast.

*And I heard a loud voice from heaven saying, "Behold, **the tabernacle of God is with men**, and He
will dwell with them, and they shall be His people."*

**Revelation** 21, and the Greek is **skene** again.

## The water

Zechariah 14 is preoccupied with water — *living waters shall flow from Jerusalem*, and then the
rain in verse 17.

By the first century the Feast of Booths had a daily ritual built on exactly this chapter: a
priest carried water in a golden pitcher from the pool of Siloam and poured it out at the altar,
while the people prayed for the winter rains.

*On **the last day, that great day of the feast**, **Jesus** stood and cried out, saying, "**If anyone
thirsts, let him come to Me and drink. He who believes in Me, as the Scripture has said, out of
his heart will flow rivers of living water**."*

**John** 7:37. He stands up during the water ceremony of Tabernacles, on the last and largest day
of it, and offers Himself in place of the pitcher.

*But this He spoke concerning the Spirit, whom those believing in Him would receive.*

## And the light

The same feast had four great lampstands lit in the Court of the Women, so bright the rabbis said
every courtyard in Jerusalem reflected them.

*Then **Jesus** spoke to them again, saying, "**I am the light of the world.**"*

**John** 8:12 — the next paragraph, the same feast.

## The rain clause

*"And if any of the peoples of the earth will not go up… **then no rain will fall on them**."*

The sanction matches the ritual. The feast at which rain was prayed for is the feast whose refusal
costs it.

*"**Ask the LORD for rain in the springtime.**"* See the note on Zechariah 10:1.

## What the last verses of the book say

*"In that day **HOLINESS TO THE LORD shall be engraved on the bells of the horses**… and every pot
in Jerusalem and Judah shall be holiness to the LORD of hosts."*

The inscription from the high priest's turban, on horse harness and kitchen pots. The book ends by
abolishing the distinction between the sacred and the ordinary.

*Therefore, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, **do all to the glory of God**.*

> **Where He stood up.** In the middle of this feast, on the day the water was poured out for the
> rain Zechariah 14 makes the sanction — and He said *if anyone thirsts, come to Me*.
