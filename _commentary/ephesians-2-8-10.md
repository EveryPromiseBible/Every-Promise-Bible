Three verses, and the third one is the reason the first two are not the end of the
subject.

## The sentence

*For it is **by grace you have been saved, through faith**.*

The Greek for *have been saved* is a perfect passive participle — **sesosmenoi**. Done, and
standing done.

**By grace** is the means; **through faith** is the channel.

## What is the gift

*And **this did not come from you** — it is **the gift of God**.*

The Greek *touto* is neuter, and neither *grace* nor *faith* is neuter. Grammatically it
points at the whole arrangement rather than at one word in it.

Readers have argued about this for centuries, and the neuter is what makes it arguable.

## The exclusion

*It is **not the result of anything you have done**, so **no one has a thing to boast
about**.*

The Greek is *not of works, lest anyone should boast*.

Paul removes the boast by name. He does the same in Romans: *where is boasting then? It is
excluded.* See the note on Romans 3:21–22.

## What comes before it

The chapter opens: *and you He made alive, who were **dead in trespasses and sins**.*

Dead. That is the diagnosis the three verses answer, and it is why the passage has no
human verb in it until the last line.

And in between: *but God, who is rich in mercy, because of His great love with which He
loved us… **made us alive together with Christ**.* See the note on Ephesians 2:4–7.

## The turn

*For **we are what He has made**.*

The Greek is **poiema** — a thing made, a work of craft. It is the root of *poem*, and it
occurs twice in the New Testament.

Paul has just spent two verses saying salvation is not a work. Then he calls the reader a
work — God's.

## What we were made for

*Created in Christ Jesus **for good works**.*

The very thing excluded in verse 9 reappears in verse 10 on the other side of the
equation.

Not works that produce the making. Works the making produces.

## When they were arranged

*Which God **prepared long beforehand**.*

The Greek is **proetoimasen** — prepared in advance.

The works were ready before the worker.

## What is left to do

*So that **we would walk in them**.*

The Greek is **peripatesomen** — walk about, go about our lives.

Not achieve them, build them, or generate them. Walk in ones already laid out.

## Where the chapter goes next

*Therefore remember that **you, once Gentiles in the flesh**… were **without Christ, aliens
from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise, having no
hope and without God in the world**.*

*But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been **brought near by the blood of
Christ**.*

## The three tenses of the chapter

Dead. Made alive and seated. Walking in prepared works.

Paul keeps the order and never lets the third one become the cause of the first two.

> **The word that resolves it.** *Poiema.* Not the result of anything you have done — and
> then, in the next line, a thing made, for works that were laid out in advance to walk
> in.
