The day's work is weighed, and the number is the point.

## The day

*So she gleaned in the field **until evening**.*

The Hebrew is **ad-ha'arev**.

She started in the morning — *she came and has continued from morning until now* — and worked
through, with one break for the meal.

## The threshing

*And **beat out what she had gleaned**.*

The Hebrew is **vattachbot** — she beat it out.

Beating with a stick, rather than driving oxen over it on a floor. The method for small
quantities.

She does the processing herself, at the end of a full day in the field.

## The measure

*And it was **about an ephah of barley**.*

An ephah is roughly two-thirds of a bushel — somewhere near thirty pounds of grain.

## What that means

A gleaner following reapers might expect one or two pounds in a day.

The daily manna ration was an omer per person, and an ephah is ten omers. She has brought home
enough to feed two women for something over two weeks.

## Why the number is so high

*"Also **let grain from the bundles fall purposely for her**… and do not rebuke her."* See the note
on Ruth 2:15–16.

Boaz had told the harvesters to pull grain out of the bound sheaves and drop it in her path.

She does not know. She thinks it was a good field.

## The carrying

*Then **she took it up and went into the city**.*

Thirty pounds, on foot, into Bethlehem.

## What Naomi sees

*And **her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned**.*

The Hebrew is **vattere** — and she saw.

The quantity is visible before anything is said. Naomi's first question is *where have you gleaned
today?*

## The leftovers

*So she brought out and **gave to her what she had kept back after she had been satisfied**.*

The roasted grain from the noon meal, which she had not finished. See the note on
Ruth 2:14.

She carried the surplus of her lunch home for her mother-in-law, along with the ephah.

## Naomi's reaction

*"Where have you gleaned today? And where did you work? **Blessed be the one who took notice of
you**."*

The Hebrew is **yehi makkirek baruch** — blessed be the one who acknowledged you.

## The name

*"The man's name with whom I worked today is **Boaz**."*

*"**Blessed be he of the LORD, who has not forsaken His kindness to the living and the dead**!"*

The Hebrew is **chasdo** — His **chesed**.

## What has changed in Naomi

She came home saying *do not call me Naomi; call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly
with me*.

*I went out full, and the LORD has brought me home again empty.*

This is the first blessing she pronounces in the book, and it is over the God she had accused.

## The relationship

*"**This man is a relation of ours, one of our close relatives**."*

The Hebrew is **miggo'alenu** — of our redeemers. See the note on
Leviticus 25:23–28.

Naomi has known this all along and says it now.

## What she says next

*"**It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his young women**."*

*So she stayed close by the young women of Boaz, to glean until the end of barley harvest and
wheat harvest.*

Barley harvest runs into wheat harvest — about seven weeks of work.

> **The number.** Thirty pounds, from one day's gleaning, when a good day would be two or three.
> Naomi could read what had happened off the weight of the sack.
