title: What if she will not come
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The servant raises the one thing that could stop the errand, and the answer is about where the
son must stay.

## The question

*"But **what if the woman won't come back with me** to this land? Should I then take your son
back to the country you came from?"*

It is a fair question and he asks it before he swears the oath. He is being sent five hundred
miles to invite a stranger to marry a man she has never met.

## What the plan does not include

Compulsion.

Nothing in the chapter allows the servant to bring her back against her will, and Abraham's
answer never suggests it. *And if the woman is not willing to follow you, **then you will be
released from this oath**.*

There is a stated failure clause, and it is her refusal.

When the moment comes, they ask her outright: *"Will you go with this man?" And she said, "**I
will go**."* Two words in the Hebrew, and nobody has argued her into them. See the note on
Genesis 24:66–67.

## The prohibition

*"**Be careful not to take my son back there**," Abraham said.*

Said twice in the passage. It is the one absolute in the whole arrangement.

## Why the son does not go

Abraham had been called out of that country himself — *get out of your country, from your
family and from your father's house*. Going back would undo the call. See the note on
Genesis 12:1–3.

And Isaac had already been on the mountain in chapter 22, laid on wood his father had arranged.
From that point Genesis never has him leave the land. Even in a famine, in chapter 26, he is
told plainly: *do not go down to Egypt*. See the note on Genesis 26:1–2.

## What the arrangement is a picture of

The Father sends. The servant goes. **The son stays.**

That is the fixed point of the chapter, and it is the fixed point of the thing the chapter
draws: the Son is not sent again, and the Spirit is the one who travels. See the note on
Genesis 24:1–4.

Jesus gave the reason on the last night: *it is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do
not go away, **the Helper will not come to you**; but if I depart, I will send Him to you.*

## What the servant is given

*"**The LORD, before whom I walk, will send His angel before you** and prosper your way."*

He is sent with an assurance and no guarantee about the outcome, and he asks his one question
and then goes.

> **The one absolute.** Everything in the chapter has a contingency except this. If she says no
> the servant walks away free — but the son does not leave the land, and Abraham says it twice.
