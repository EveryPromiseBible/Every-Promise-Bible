Eight couplets, and one of them is about how money behaves.

## The two ways it comes

*Money **grabbed fast slips fast away**. Money **gathered slowly grows**.*

The Hebrew of the first is wealth from vanity, from nothing, from vapour — the same
root as Ecclesiastes' *vanity of vanities*. The older rendering is *wealth gotten
by vanity shall be diminished.*

And the second is literally *gathered by the hand* — a handful at a time, by
labour.

The proverb is not against money. It is about the manner of its arrival, and it
says the manner determines whether it stays.

## What comes fast, goes fast

The verb in the first line is to become small, to dwindle. It does not say the
money is taken away — it says it shrinks.

## Hope stretched thin

*Hope **stretched thin** makes the heart sick. A longing filled is a **tree of
life**.*

The Hebrew is *hope deferred*. Something delayed past the point the heart can hold
it.

And the answer is not a lowered expectation. It is a longing **filled** — and the
image chosen for it is the tree in the garden.

## The lamp

*The light of the upright **burns clear and glad**. The lamp of the wicked
**flickers out**.*

The first verb carries rejoicing. A light that is glad.

## Where the wisdom comes from

*The teaching of the wise is a **spring of life**, turning your feet from the
snares of death.*

A spring, not a cistern. Something with its own source.

## The one about advice

*Pride only breeds more quarrels. **Wisdom listens, and takes advice**.*

The Hebrew is *with the well-advised is wisdom*. It is not the clever who are wise
in Proverbs; it is the ones who let themselves be counselled.

## And the flag

*The clever act on what they know. **The fool spreads his folly like a flag**.*

The Hebrew is that he spreads it out, unfurls it, makes a display.
