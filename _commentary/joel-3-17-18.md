The end of a short and violent book, and it finishes with wine running down a hillside.

## What the book has been about

Locusts. Four kinds of them, in waves, stripping the country bare.

*What the chewing locust left, the swarming locust has eaten; what the swarming locust left, the
crawling locust has eaten.*

Joel reads the invasion as a summons: *blow the trumpet in Zion… **rend your heart, and not your
garments***.

## The promise in the middle

*"**So I will restore to you the years that the swarming locust has eaten**."*

And then the passage Peter quotes at Pentecost: *I will pour out My Spirit on all flesh*. See
the note on Joel 2:28–29.

## The knowing

*Then **you will know that I am the LORD your God, dwelling in Zion, My holy mountain**.*

The Hebrew is **shokhen** — dwelling, tabernacling.

## The city

*Jerusalem will be **holy**, and **strangers will never march through her again**.*

The Hebrew is **zarim** — foreigners, and in this context invaders.

Joel has spent three chapters on armies coming through.

## The wine

*On that day the **mountains will drip with new wine**.*

The Hebrew is **asis** — sweet wine, fresh from the press.

Mountains are not where vineyards are. Terraced hillsides are, and the picture is of so much
that it runs off the slopes.

## The milk

*And the **hills will flow with milk**.*

Milk means pasture. Enough grass on the hills for herds.

The two together are the standing description of the land: *a land flowing with milk and honey*.
See the note on Deuteronomy 6:1–3.

## The water

*All the **streambeds of Judah will run with water**.*

The Hebrew is **aphiqim** — wadis. Channels that are dry for most of the year and flood after
rain.

In a country where water is the constraint, a wadi running is the whole promise.

## The fountain

*A **fountain will flow out of the house of the LORD** and water the **Valley of Acacias**.*

The Hebrew is **nachal ha-shittim** — the wadi of the acacias.

Acacia is desert wood. It grows where nothing else does, and it is the timber the tabernacle
was made of — the ark, the table, the altar, the boards.

A stream from the temple, watering the valley where the desert wood grows.

## Where else the water comes out of the building

*Water flowing out from under the threshold of the temple… **wherever the river flows, everything
will live**.* See the note on Ezekiel 47:7–10.

And *a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding from the throne of God and of
the Lamb*.

Three prophets and the same picture: one source, inside the sanctuary, and the water goes out.

## What Amos has

*"Behold, the days are coming," says the LORD, "when the plowman shall overtake the reaper…
**the mountains shall drip with sweet wine, and all the hills shall flow with it**."* See the
note on Amos 9:13–15.

Nearly the same sentence, in another prophet, and both books end on it.

## The last line of Joel

*"For I will acquit them of the guilt of bloodshed, whom I had not acquitted. **For the LORD
dwells in Zion**."*

The book closes on the same clause it opened this passage with. He is living there.

## What was happening when he wrote

The fields stripped, the vine dried up, the pomegranate and the palm withered, *because joy has
withered away from the sons of men*.

The wine on the mountains is written by a man looking at a dead orchard.

> **Where the fountain starts.** Out of the house — and the valley it waters is named for
> acacia, the desert wood the tabernacle was built out of.
