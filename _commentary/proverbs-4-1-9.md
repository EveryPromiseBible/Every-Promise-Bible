A father quoting his own father, which is the only place in Proverbs this happens.

## The address

*Listen, **my children**, to a father's teaching.*

The Hebrew is plural — sons.

## The credential

*For **I too was a son once, to my father**, tender, **the only child of my mother**.*

The Hebrew is **rak veyachid** — tender and only.

He is not speaking from authority. He is speaking as somebody who was on the other side of this
conversation.

## The quotation

*And **he taught me and said**.*

Everything from here to verse 9 is the grandfather's words, quoted.

If the traditional attribution holds, this is Solomon quoting David.

## The first instruction

*"**Let your heart hold fast to my words**, keep my commands and you will live."*

## The imperatives

*"**Get wisdom, get understanding**."*

The Hebrew is **qeneh** — acquire, buy, get possession of. The verb for a purchase.

Said twice in two verses.

## The line

*"**Wisdom is where everything begins**, so get wisdom, and **with all you have, get
understanding**."*

The Hebrew is **reshit chokhmah** — the beginning, or the chief part, of wisdom.

And *with all your getting* — **bekhol-qinyanekha**, with everything you have acquired.

Spend the lot on it, if that is what it takes.

## The two verbs

*"Do not forsake her, and **she will protect you**; love her, and **she will keep you safe**."*

The Hebrew is **shamar** — guard, keep watch over.

The relationship is reciprocal in the passage. Every instruction to the son has a corresponding
action from wisdom.

## The exchange

*"**Prize her, and she will lift you up**; embrace her, and **she will bring you honor**."*

The Hebrew for *prize* is **salsel** — exalt, esteem highly. A rare word.

And **chabbeq** — embrace, hug.

Wisdom is personified as a woman throughout the first nine chapters of Proverbs, and this is
the most affectionate the language gets.

## The crown

*"She will place **a graceful wreath on your head**, she will **crown you with beauty**."*

The Hebrew is **livyat chen** — a garland of grace. And **ateret tipheret** — a crown of glory.

The same phrase used earlier: *my son, hear the instruction of your father… for they will be a
graceful ornament on your head, and chains about your neck*.

## What the father does not offer

Money, position, or an inheritance.

He hands over the thing he was given, and it is a way of thinking.

## The rest of the chapter

*"Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life."* See the note on
Proverbs 4:20–27.

And *the path of the just is like the shining sun, that shines ever brighter unto the perfect
day*.

## What Solomon asked for

*"Give to Your servant an understanding heart to judge Your people, that I may discern between
good and evil."*

And the answer: *because you have asked this thing, and have not asked long life for yourself,
nor riches… behold, I have also given you what you have not asked*.

Riches and honour arrived because they were not the request.

## Where the New Testament puts wisdom

*Christ Jesus, who became for us wisdom from God — and righteousness and sanctification and
redemption.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 1:26–31.

And *in whom every treasure of wisdom and knowledge lies hidden*. See the note on
Colossians 2:1–3.

> **Whose words these are.** A father telling his sons what his own father told him — the one
> place in the book where the teaching is traced back a generation.
