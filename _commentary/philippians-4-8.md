A list of eight, and the verb at the end is the whole instruction.

## Where it sits

Immediately after *be anxious for nothing*, and the peace that guards the heart and mind. See
the note on Philippians 4:6–7.

The guarding verb there is **phrouresei** — a garrison word.

This verse is what the garrison is protecting.

## The eight

*Whatever is **true**.*

**Alethe** — true, as opposed to false or unreal.

*Whatever is **worthy of honor**.*

**Semna** — grave, dignified, weighty. Used of how deacons and older men should be.

*Whatever is **right**.*

**Dikaia** — righteous, just.

*Whatever is **pure**.*

**Hagna** — chaste, unmixed. The same root as the purifying in 1 John 3:3.

*Whatever is **lovely**.*

**Prosphile** — and it occurs once in the New Testament. From **pros** and **phileo** —
toward-loving. Things that call out affection.

*Whatever is **well spoken of**.*

**Euphema** — fair-sounding, of good report. The root of *euphemism*.

## The two summaries

*If anything is **excellent**.*

**Arete** — virtue, moral excellence. The great word of Greek ethics, and Paul uses it once.

*If anything **deserves praise**.*

## Where the list comes from

It is not a Christian vocabulary.

**Arete** is Aristotle's word. **Semnos**, **prosphiles** and **euphemos** are the currency of
Greek moral philosophy.

Paul, writing from a Roman prison to a Roman colony, uses the vocabulary his readers already
had for a good life — and does not baptise it or apologise for it.

Whatever is true, wherever it comes from.

## The verb

*Let your thinking **dwell on these**.*

The Greek is **logizesthe** — reckon, take account of, calculate.

An accounting word. It is the verb Paul uses in Romans of counting yourself dead to sin, and of
righteousness being credited to Abraham. See the note on Romans 6:8–11.

Not *daydream about*. Enter these in the ledger.

## The next verse

*The things which you learned and received and heard and saw in me, **these do**.*

Think on those, and do these.

And the promise: *and the God of peace will be with you*.

Verse 7 has the peace of God guarding; verse 9 has the God of peace present. Paul brackets the
list with both.

## What the letter is

The most cheerful thing Paul wrote, from custody, with a verdict pending. See the note on
Philippians 4:4–5.

*Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice.*

## What comes after

*I have learned, in whatever state I am, to be content.* See the note on
Philippians 4:11–12.

And *my God shall supply all your need according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus*.

## What the instruction is not

A ban on facing hard things.

The same letter names his chains, the risk of death, two women falling out, and a friend who
*was sick almost unto death*.

The list is about where the attention settles, not about what is admitted to exist.

> **The verb at the end.** *Logizomai* — an accounting word. Not *dwell on* in the sense of
> drifting. Reckon these; enter them in the book.
