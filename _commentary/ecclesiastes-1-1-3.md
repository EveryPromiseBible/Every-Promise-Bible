The book opens with the thesis, and then spends twelve chapters testing it.

## Who is speaking

*These are the words of **the Teacher, a son of David, who once wore the crown in Jerusalem**.*

The Hebrew is **Qoheleth** — one who assembles, or one who speaks to an assembly. It is a
participle, not a name.

A son of David who reigned in Jerusalem, and who says later *I have attained greatness, and
excelled more than all who were before me in Jerusalem*.

## The word

*Vapor, he says. **Only vapor**.*

The Hebrew is **hevel** — breath, vapour, mist.

The Illumination reads it literally rather than as *vanity*, which came into English through
the Latin *vanitas*. The Hebrew word is physical: the breath you can see on a cold morning.

## The image

*Breath on a cold morning, **gone before you can hold it**.*

Not worthless. Not meaningless. Untenable — it will not stay in the hand.

Everything the book examines fails the same test: it is real, and it will not keep.

## The name Abel

**Hevel** is also the name of Adam's second son.

A man whose whole life in the text is two verses long and ends in a field.

## The superlative

*Vapor of vapors*, in the Hebrew — **havel havalim**, the same construction as *holy of holies*
and *song of songs*.

The most vaporous of vapours. Hebrew makes superlatives by repeating the noun.

Five occurrences of the word in one verse.

## The question

*So **what does a person truly gain** from all the hard work he does **under the sun**, from the
sweat of every long day?*

The Hebrew is **yitron** — profit, surplus, what is left over. A commercial word.

After the ledger is settled, what remains?

## Under the sun

**Tachat hashemesh** — the phrase appears twenty-nine times in the book.

It sets the boundary of the investigation. Everything Qoheleth examines, he examines within
this world, on its own terms, without appeal to anything outside it.

The question is honest, and the frame is the reason the answer keeps coming back empty.

## What he tried

Wisdom, pleasure, building projects, gardens, pools, silver, gold, singers, and *whatever my
eyes desired I did not keep from them*.

*Then I looked on all the works that my hands had done… and indeed all was vanity and grasping
for the wind.* See the note on Ecclesiastes 2:9–11.

He did not conclude this from a distance. He ran the experiment.

## What he keeps returning to

*There is nothing better for a man than that he should eat and drink, and that his soul should
enjoy good in his labour. **This also, I saw, was from the hand of God**.*

Bread, work, and company — held loosely, received as a gift.

## The end of the book

*Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: **fear God and keep His commandments**, for this
is man's all.* See the note on Ecclesiastes 12:13–14.

## What is on the other side of the sun

*If then you were raised with Christ, **seek those things which are above**, where Christ is,
sitting at the right hand of God.* See the note on Colossians 3:1–4.

The one category Qoheleth's method could not reach.

## What the New Testament does with the word

*You do not know what will happen tomorrow. For **what is your life? It is even a vapour** that
appears for a little time and then vanishes away.*

James uses the exact image, and draws it into a sentence about planning.

> **The word.** **Hevel** — vapour, breath on a cold morning. Not worthless and not meaningless.
> Real, and impossible to keep hold of.
