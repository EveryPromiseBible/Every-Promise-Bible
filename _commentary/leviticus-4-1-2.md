A new offering is introduced, and the case it covers is the one nobody planned.

## Where this comes

Leviticus 1 to 3 are the burnt offering, the grain offering and the peace offering — all
voluntary, all bringing something to God.

Chapter 4 begins the required ones. The mood changes.

## The formula

*The LORD said to Moses, "**Speak to the Israelites and tell them**."*

The standard opening. Leviticus is almost entirely direct speech.

## The case

*"**When someone sins unintentionally**, doing what the LORD has commanded not to be done."*

The Hebrew is **bishgagah** — in error, by straying, inadvertently.

**Shagah** — to wander, to go astray. The verb used of a drunk staggering and of sheep wandering
off.

## What it covers

Not defiance. Sin committed without knowing it was sin at the time.

Later in the chapter: *when the sin which they have committed **becomes known to them***.

The offering is for the moment somebody finds out.

## What is not covered

*But the person who does anything presumptuously… **that one brings reproach on the LORD, and he
shall be cut off from among his people**.*

The Hebrew is **beyad ramah** — with a high hand.

Numbers 15 states it. Defiant sin has no offering prescribed in the system.

## Why this needed legislating

The other offerings assume the worshipper is bringing something. This one assumes he has just
discovered something.

The category is unsettling in a way the voluntary offerings are not: it means a person could be
guilty without knowing.

## The last clause

*"Here is **the way home**."*

The Hebrew introduces the procedure that follows — the bull, the hand on the head, the blood, the
fat, the burning outside the camp.

The translation renders the function of the instruction rather than the syntax: what follows is
the route back.

## The four cases

The chapter runs through them in order of seriousness by office.

**The anointed priest** — a young bull, blood sprinkled seven times before the veil. See the note
on Leviticus 4:3–12.

**The whole congregation** — a young bull, the elders laying hands on it.

**A ruler** — a male goat.

**A common person** — a female goat or a lamb.

## The direction of the scale

The higher the office, the heavier the offering, and the further in the blood goes.

The priest's blood goes inside the tent, to the veil and the incense altar. The common person's
goes on the horns of the outer altar.

## The hand

*He **lays his hand on its head**.*

The Hebrew is **vesamak yado** — and he shall lean his hand.

**Samak** — to press, to lean weight on. The same verb used of Moses laying hands on Joshua.

## Outside the camp

The priest's bull is burned entirely outside the camp, *in a clean place, where the ashes are
poured out*.

*Therefore Jesus also, that He might sanctify the people with His own blood, **suffered outside the
gate**.* See the note on Hebrews 13:11–13.

Hebrews builds an argument on where the carcass was taken.

## The word from the cross

*"Father, forgive them, **for they do not know what they do**."*

Which is the definition in this verse, spoken over the people carrying it out.

## What Paul says about his own case

*Although I was formerly a blasphemer, a persecutor, and an insolent man; but **I obtained mercy
because I did it ignorantly in unbelief**.* See the note on 1 Timothy 1:12–14.

He puts himself in this chapter's category.

> **The word.** **Shagah** — to wander, to stagger, to stray. The offering is not for defiance; it
> is for what a person did before they knew, and it starts the moment they find out.
