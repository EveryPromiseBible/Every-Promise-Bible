---
key: 2 Samuel 7:18–21
title: Who am I, and what is my family?
updated: 2026-08-09
---
David goes in, sits down, and asks a question he does not expect an answer to.

## The posture

*Then King David **went in and sat down before the LORD**.*

Not knelt, not prostrated. **Sat.**

It is the only place in the Old Testament anybody is described praying that way, and it is
worth noticing what he has just been told: that God will build him a house and establish it
forever, with nothing asked of him.

*But this Man, after He had offered one sacrifice for sins forever, **sat down** at the right
hand of God.*

The priests under the old covenant stood, every day, because the work was never finished.
There were no chairs in the tabernacle.

## The question

*"**Who am I, O Lord GOD, and what is my family, that you have brought me this far?**"*

Both halves are about qualification, and both answers are *nothing much*. The youngest of
eight, out with the sheep, from a village, in a family nobody thought to call in from the
field. See the note on 1 Samuel 16:6–7.

## What he says next

*"And **as if that were not enough in your eyes**, O Lord GOD, you have spoken about the
far-off future of your servant's house."*

He is not being modest. He is doing arithmetic and cannot make it come out.

*Now to Him who is able to do **exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think**.*

## The line in the middle

*"**Is this how you deal with a mere man**, O Lord GOD?"*

The Hebrew is difficult and the sense is something like *and this is the law of man?* — is
this how people are normally treated?

The answer is that it is not, and he knows it.

## What he stops trying to do

*"**What more can David say to you? You know your servant.**"*

He gives up on the speech. There is nothing he can add that God does not already know, and he
says so and stops.

## The two reasons given

*"**For the sake of your word**, and because **it was your heart to do it**, you have done this
whole great thing."*

Not one reason about David. God's word, and God's own inclination.

*Having predestined us to adoption as sons by Jesus Christ to Himself, **according to the good
pleasure of His will**.*

The Greek is **eudokia** — what pleased Him to do. Paul is reaching for the same category, and
he uses it three times in one paragraph.

> **Where he sat.** In front of God, in a tent, having just been told he would be given a
> house that never ends. The priests had nowhere to sit down because the work was not
> finished; David is offered something already settled and takes a seat in it.
