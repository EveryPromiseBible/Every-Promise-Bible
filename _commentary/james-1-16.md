Six words on their own, and they hinge the paragraph. Everything before them is about where trouble
does *not* come from, and everything after is about what does come down.

## What he is guarding

*Do not get this wrong, **dear friends**.*

**Me planasthe** — do not be led astray, do not wander. And then **adelphoi mou agapetoi**, my
beloved brothers, which is how James speaks when he is at his most serious.

The error he is heading off is named three verses earlier: *let no one say when he is tempted, "I am
tempted by God."* Somebody in those churches was tracing the pressure back to the Father.

## Every good thing, and where it comes from

*Every good gift and every perfect gift **is from above**, coming down from the **Father of lights**.*

The direction is one-way. James allows no mixed supply: what comes down from there is good, and if
it is not good it did not come from there.

**Pater ton photon** — the Father of the lights, meaning the ones in the sky. He made them.

## The astronomy in the verse

*With whom there is **no variation or shadow of turning**.*

Both words are technical. **Parallage** is the observed change in a heavenly body's position —
our word *parallax* comes straight off it. **Trope** is the turning of a body on its course; the
solstice was called the **tropai** of the sun.

So James picks the two things every light in the sky does. The sun climbs and sinks and stands
differently in December than in June. The moon has phases, and half of it is always dark. Everything
up there casts a shadow at some point, and everything up there eventually turns away.

The Father who made them does none of it. No phase, no season, no eclipse, no angle from which He
looks different.

## How we know it is true

Not because the trouble makes sense. It does not always. We know it because of what He did on the
one occasion when the whole question was settled in public:

*He who **did not spare His own Son**, but delivered Him up for us all, how shall He not with Him
also freely give us all things?*

That is the fixed point. Whatever else is unexplained, God's disposition toward you was settled when He
gave Jesus Christ at the cross, and there is no later evidence that overturns it.

> **Do not get this wrong.** It is the one thing in the paragraph James says outright, and it is
> about God's character rather than about our behaviour. There is no version of the Father that is
> against you waiting behind the version that is for you. *God is light, and in Him is no darkness
> at all* — no darkness, and no time of day when there is.
