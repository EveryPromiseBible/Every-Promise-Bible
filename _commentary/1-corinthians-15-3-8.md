The oldest summary of the Christian message in existence, and Paul says he did not write
it.

## The transmission

*For I **handed on** to you, first of all, **the very thing I myself received**.*

The Greek is **paredoka** and **parelabon** — the technical pair for passing on a fixed
tradition. Rabbinic teaching used the same vocabulary.

Paul is quoting. He says so.

He was converted within a few years of the crucifixion, so what he is repeating predates
his own conversion — which puts this formula within a handful of years of the event.

## "First of all"

The Greek is *among the first things*, in order of importance.

## The four clauses

Each begins with *that*, which is how a creed is set out:

- *That **Christ died for our sins** according to the Scriptures*
- *That **He was buried***
- *That **He rose again the third day** according to the Scriptures*
- *That **He was seen***

Two of the four carry *according to the Scriptures*. The death and the resurrection are
both attached to the Old Testament.

## The burial

*That He was buried.*

A short clause doing a specific job. A buried man is a dead man, and the tomb is a
location somebody could have gone to.

## The list of witnesses

*Cephas, and then **the twelve**.*

Peter first. The Gospels give the women first, which is why the omission is noticeable —
Paul is listing official witnesses in a legal sense, and a woman's testimony carried no
weight in that world.

*After that He was seen by **more than five hundred brothers all at one time**, and **most
of them are still here with us to this present day**, though some have fallen asleep.*

That clause is an invitation. Most of them can be asked.

*After that He was seen by **James**.*

The Lord's brother, who did not believe during His life. See the note on
Acts 1:12–14.

*And then by **all the apostles**.*

## The last one

*And **last of all He was seen by me too**, as by **a child born at the wrong time**.*

The Greek is **ektroma** — a miscarriage, an untimely birth. It is a harsh word, and Paul
chose it about himself.

The next verse explains: *for I am the least of the apostles, who am not worthy to be
called an apostle, **because I persecuted the church of God**.*

He is on the list, and he says how he got there. See the note on Acts 9:3–6.

## The verse after that

*But **by the grace of God I am what I am**, and His grace toward me was not in vain; but I
laboured more abundantly than they all, **yet not I, but the grace of God which was with
me**.*

He says it twice in one sentence.

## What the whole chapter is for

Some in Corinth were saying there is no resurrection of the dead. Paul sets out the
tradition first, and then follows their premise all the way down. See the note on
1 Corinthians 15:12–19.

> **What Paul does not claim.** Authorship. He opens the most important paragraph he ever
> wrote by saying he is passing on what he was handed — and then puts himself at the end
> of the witness list with the ugliest word available.
