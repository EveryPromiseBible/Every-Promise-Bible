Paul has spent the chapter on Abraham, and here he says why he bothered.

## "Not written down for his sake alone"

*The words "**it was credited to him**" were not written down for his sake alone.
They were written **for ours as well**.*

The sentence Paul is quoting is Genesis 15:6 — *Abraham believed God, and it was
accounted to him for righteousness.*

The Greek for *credited* is **logizomai**, a bookkeeping word: to reckon into an
account, to enter on a ledger.

Abraham had nothing to put in. It was entered anyway, and Paul says the entry was
recorded in Scripture as a precedent.

## What we believe

*It will be credited **to us who believe in the One who raised Jesus our Lord from
the dead**.*

Abraham believed God could bring life out of a dead womb — *he did not consider his
own body, already dead… or the deadness of Sarah's womb.*

The object of faith in both cases is a God who raises the dead. Paul makes them the
same faith.

## The two clauses

*He was **handed over because of our wrongdoing**, and He was **raised so that we
could be put right with God**.*

Two events, two prepositions, and both are *because of* in the Greek.

Handed over **because of** our sins — they were the reason He went to the cross.

Raised **because of** our justification — and that is where the sentence gets
interesting. Young's Literal keeps it: *raised up because of our being declared
righteous.*

## Which way round

Not *raised so that we might one day be justified*. **Because** we were.

The debt was settled at the cross, and the resurrection is what happened after the
settlement — not the means of it but the proof of it. He went in carrying the sins
and came out without them.

If anything had been left owing, the tomb would still be shut. The empty tomb is
God's receipt.

## Why it is in this chapter

Romans 4 is Paul's case that righteousness has always been credited rather than
earned — from Abraham, who was declared righteous *while still uncircumcised*, four
hundred and thirty years before the law existed.

The chapter ends by extending the same entry to the reader, and then chapter 5
opens: *therefore, having been justified by faith, we have peace with God.*

> **What Abraham did not have.** A law, a temple, a priesthood, a Bible, or a
> synagogue. He had a promise and a night sky, and the ledger entry was made on the
> spot.
