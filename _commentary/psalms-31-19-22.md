Two verbs for the same goodness — one hidden, one public.

## The exclamation

*How great is Your goodness, **stored up** for those who fear You.*

The Hebrew is **mah rav tuvcha asher-tzafanta** — how abundant Your goodness, which You have
treasured up.

**Tzaphan** — to hide, to store, to lay by. The word used of Moses' mother hiding him three
months, and of the psalmist hiding the word in his heart.

## The second verb

*Poured out for those who **shelter in You**.*

The Hebrew is **pa'alta** — You have worked, done, accomplished.

The goodness is both stored and already worked.

## The audience

*Right **before the eyes of everyone**.*

The Hebrew is **neged benei adam** — in front of the sons of men.

Stored in secret; spent in public.

## What is hidden and what is not

The stock is out of sight. The spending is not.

## The hiding place

*You **hide them in the cover of Your presence**.*

The Hebrew is **besetter paneicha** — in the secret of Your face.

**Setter** — a covert, a hiding place. The same word as *he who dwells in the secret place of the
Most High*. See the note on Psalms 91:1–2.

## What they are hidden from

*Away from **the schemes of men**.*

The Hebrew is **merukhsei ish** — from the conspiracies, the bindings-together, of man.

## The second shelter

*You keep them safe in Your shelter, away from **the strife of tongues**.*

The Hebrew is **meriv leshonot** — from the quarrel of tongues.

Two threats named: what men plot, and what men say. The psalm treats speech as the second danger,
worth its own line.

## What David was in

*Blessed be the LORD, for He showed me His wondrous love when I was **a city under siege**.*

The Hebrew is **be'ir matzor** — in a fortified city, or a city of siege. Read either way.

## What he had said

*In my alarm I had said, **I am cut off from Your sight**.*

The Hebrew is **nigrazti minneged eineicha** — I am cut off from before Your eyes.

## The correction

*Yet **You heard the voice of my pleading** when I cried to You for help.*

He records the panicked sentence and then records that it was wrong.

The psalm keeps both: what he said in the alarm, and what was actually happening while he said it.

## The verse from the middle of the psalm

*Into Your hand I commit my spirit.* See the note on Psalms 31:5.

The last words from the cross, with one word added: *Father*.

## What the psalm ends with

*Be of good courage, and **He shall strengthen your heart**, all you who hope in the LORD.* See the
note on Psalms 31:23–24.

## Where the storing goes

*In whom are **hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge**.* See the note on Colossians 2:1–3.

The Greek is **apokruphoi** — hidden away, stored.

Hidden in Christ, and hidden for those who are in Him.

> **The two verbs.** *Stored up* and *worked out* — the same goodness, kept out of sight and then
> spent where everyone can see it.
