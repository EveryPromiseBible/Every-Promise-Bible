The last sentence Peter wrote.

## The word

*Keep growing.*

The Greek is **auxanete** — grow, increase. It is the ordinary word for a plant or a
child.

Present imperative: keep at it, continuously.

## The two directions

*Deeper into **grace**, and deeper into **knowing Jesus Christ**.*

The Greek is *in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ*, with one
article covering both.

Peter does not say grow in holiness, in discipline or in service. Two things, and both are
about what is received.

## The knowing

**Gnosis** — and Peter has used the fuller form **epignosis** four times in the letter's
first chapter.

It is the theme of the whole letter: *grace and peace be multiplied to you **in the
knowledge of God***. See the note on 2 Peter 1:1–2.

## What "instead" answers

The verse before: *beware lest you also **fall from your own steadfastness**, being led away
with the error of the wicked.*

Peter names the danger and then, instead of a defensive instruction, gives a growth one.

The counter to being carried off is not to brace. It is to grow.

## The doxology

*The glory is His, **now and on into the day that has no end**. Amen.*

The Greek is *both now and to the day of eternity* — a phrase that appears once in the New
Testament.

## What is unusual about it

Doxologies in the New Testament are usually addressed to God the Father. This one is to
Christ, and Peter closes his last letter with it.

## What the chapter is about

Scoffers asking *where is the promise of His coming?*, the answer that *one day is with the
Lord as a thousand years*, and: *the Lord is not slack concerning His promise… but is
longsuffering toward us, **not willing that any should perish**.*

Then the day of the Lord coming as a thief, and the elements melting.

And the question: *since all these things will be dissolved, **what manner of persons ought
you to be**?*

## What he says about Paul two verses earlier

*As also **our beloved brother Paul**, according to the wisdom given to him, has written to
you… in which are **some things hard to understand**, which untaught and unstable people
twist to their own destruction, **as they do also the rest of the Scriptures**.*

Peter calls Paul's letters Scripture, admits parts are difficult, and says people distort
them. All in one sentence, and it is the only place in the New Testament where one apostle
comments on another's writing.

## Why he was writing

*Knowing that shortly **I must put off my tent**, just as our Lord Jesus Christ showed me.
Moreover I will be careful to ensure that **you always have a reminder of these things after
my decease**.*

This is a man setting down what he wants left behind.

> **His last instruction.** Not to hold the line. To keep growing — and the two things
> named are grace and knowing a person.
