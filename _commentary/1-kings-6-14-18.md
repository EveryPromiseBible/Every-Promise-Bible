The most expensive stonework in Israel's history, and none of it could be seen.

## What was underneath

*Great, costly stones, dressed square at the quarry* — chapter 5, and the finest
masonry money could buy, cut and finished in the dark.

## What went over it

*He **lined the inside walls with boards of cedar** — from the floor of the house up
to the rafters of the ceiling.*

Every internal surface panelled. Then the floors in cypress, then the whole interior
overlaid with gold.

## The line

*All cedar, **not a single stone in sight**.*

That is the writer's own summary, and it is the point of the paragraph.

The stones were quarried, hauled, squared and set with enormous care, and then
covered so completely that nobody entering the building would ever see one.

The work on them was not for display.

## Cedar

Cedar of Lebanon is close-grained, aromatic, and resistant to rot and insects. It was
the prestige timber of the ancient world, shipped down the coast in rafts by Hiram's
men.

It does not decay, and it does not admit what usually gets into wood.

## The carving

*Carved with **gourds and open flowers**.*

Under the gold — which is applied in the verses following — there is carving nobody
would ever see either.

Gourds and open flowers, hidden under metal, in a room only priests entered.

## The inner room

*At the far end of the house he built off twenty cubits with boards of cedar, from
floor to rafters, and set that space apart as **the inner sanctuary, the Most Holy
Place**.*

A perfect cube, twenty by twenty by twenty, entered once a year. See the note on
Hebrews 9:6–7.

## What was in the walls

Stone, from a quarry, cut to fit somewhere it had never been.

Cedar over the stone. Gold over the cedar. And carving in between that the gold
covered.

Layer on layer, and the visible surface is the last one applied.

## Where the letters take it

*You also, as living stones, are being built up a spiritual house* — Peter.

And Paul: *in Him the whole building, being fitted together, grows into a holy
temple in the Lord, in whom you also are being built together for a dwelling place of
God in the Spirit.* See the notes on 1 Peter 2:4–5 and Ephesians 2:17–19.

Stones, quarried out of the dark, covered in something that does not rot and
something that does not tarnish.
