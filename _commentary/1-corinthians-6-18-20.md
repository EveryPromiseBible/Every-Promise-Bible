Paul gives one instruction about running away and then three reasons about
ownership.

## The verb

*"**Flee** sexual immorality."*

The Greek is **pheugo** — run. Not resist, not manage, not stand firm.

Paul uses the same word for idolatry, and Joseph is the model — *he left the garment
in her hand and fled outside.* See the note on Genesis 39:7–12.

There are things the New Testament says to stand against and things it says to run
from, and Paul is clear which this is.

## The distinction

*"Every other sin a person commits is **outside the body**, but the one who sins
sexually **sins against his own body**."*

Paul's own argument, and readers have long noticed it will not survive a pedantic
reading — gluttony and drunkenness reach the body too.

He is not writing a taxonomy. He is saying this one goes somewhere the others do not,
and his reason is in the verses either side: *the two shall become one flesh.*
See the note on Genesis 2:21–25.

## The temple

*"Do you not know that **your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit** who is in you,
**whom you have from God**?"*

The Greek is **naos** — the inner sanctuary, not the courts.

And this one is singular. Three chapters earlier Paul said the same of the church
collectively; here he says it of a person. See the note on 1 Corinthians 3:16–17.

*Whom you have from God* — the Spirit is described as a possession received rather
than an achievement.

## The ownership

*"**And that you are not your own?**"*

Four words, and they are the argument of the whole passage.

Corinth's slogan, quoted by Paul twice in this chapter, was *all things are lawful
for me.* This is his answer to it: the question is not what is permitted but whose
you are.

## The price

*"**You were bought at a price.**"*

The Greek is a market word — purchased, the way you buy something at a stall.

Paul does not name the sum here. He does elsewhere: *not with corruptible things,
like silver or gold… but with the precious blood of Christ.*

## The conclusion

*"So **glorify God in your body** and in your spirit, which are God's."*

Note where it lands. Not *avoid* — glorify. The instruction is positive and it is
about the body specifically.

## What Corinth was

The city had a reputation across the empire; *to Corinthianise* was a Greek verb for
living immorally.

Paul had just listed what some of them had been — and then: *and such were some of
you. But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified.*

Three verbs, all past tense, before any of this instruction.

> **Where the argument starts.** *Do you not know?* — six times in this one chapter.
> Paul's remedy for the Corinthians' behaviour is repeatedly to tell them what is
> already true of them.
