The second speech from the whirlwind, and it contains the only direct charge God
lays against Job in the entire book.

## The one question

*"**Would you really cancel My justice? Would you condemn Me so that you could be
in the right?**"*

That is it. Forty-two chapters, and this is the whole indictment.

Not the boils. Not any sin the friends dug for. Not one item off Eliphaz's,
Bildad's or Zophar's lists — because none of them was there.

The charge is that Job put God in the wrong in order to be in the right himself.

## What "cancel My justice" means

The Hebrew is to break, to annul, to make void — the word used of breaking a
covenant.

Job's defence had required a verdict on God. If Job is innocent and God sent the
suffering, then God is unjust. That is the only place the argument could go, and
Job followed it there: *it is God who has bent me down and drawn His net tight
around me.*

## The choice in the sentence

There are two positions and only one of them can be occupied. Either God is right,
or I am.

Job had spent thirty chapters taking the second one — and every man does, by
instinct. Being wrong is the harder of the two to say out loud.

David took the other one: *against You, You only, have I sinned… **so You are
right when You speak, You are clear when You judge**.* He wrote it after the worst
thing he ever did. See the note on Psalms 51:3–4.

That is the whole difference. One man justifies himself and God ends up in the
dock; the other agrees God is right and finds himself in the clear.

## Where the self-justifying came from

Job did not start here. Chapter 1: *the LORD gave and the LORD has taken away;
blessed be the name of the LORD.*

It came out of him after twenty-five chapters of men telling him to account for
himself. A man who is made to defend himself long enough ends up with a case, and
a case needs a defendant on the other side. See the note on Job 29:14–17.

## "Brace yourself again"

The same words as chapter 38 — *stand up now, brace yourself like a strong man.*

He is being stood up a second time, not knocked down.

## What Job says back

*"I know that You can do all things… I spoke of things I did not understand. **It
was me.**"*

He takes the other position, and the book turns over in that sentence. See the
note on Job 42:1–3.

> **Where Paul lands in the same place.** *For I know that nothing good lives in me
> — that is, in the old nature.* It is not despair; it is the end of an argument.
> A man who has settled that stops trying to build a defence, and can take a
> righteousness that was given to him. See the note on Romans 7:18–20.
