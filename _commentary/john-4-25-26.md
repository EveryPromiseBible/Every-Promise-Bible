The first person He tells is a Samaritan woman at a well at noon.

## Where the conversation has got to

She has asked for the water that means she will not have to come back and draw. He has asked
about her husband. She has said *I have no husband*, and been told *you have had five, and
the one you have now is not your husband*.

Then she changed the subject to worship — *our fathers worshipped on this mountain, and you
Jews say that in Jerusalem is the place*.

And He answered: *the hour is coming, and now is, when the true worshippers will worship the
Father in spirit and truth*.

## Her sentence

*"I know that **Messiah is coming** — the One who is called the Christ."*

The Hebrew word and the Greek word, side by side, both meaning *anointed*.

The Samaritans expected a figure they called the **Taheb**, the restorer — based on
Deuteronomy's *a Prophet like me from your midst*, since they held only the five books of
Moses.

## What she expects Him to do

*"When He comes, **He will make everything clear to us**."*

The Greek is **anangelei hemin panta** — He will announce all things to us.

Her expectation is a teacher who settles the arguments. She has just been in one about which
mountain.

## The answer

*"**I am He, the very one speaking to you now**."*

The Greek is **ego eimi, ho lalon soi** — I am, the one speaking to you.

Two words, and the pronoun is not grammatically necessary. John has been building **ego eimi**
through the whole Gospel, and here it is at a well in Samaria.

## Who gets told

Not the crowd, not the Sanhedrin, not Nicodemus in the previous chapter.

A woman, in Samaria, with five husbands behind her and a man she is not married to at home,
at the hottest hour of the day — which is when a woman went to the well if she was avoiding
the other women.

He tells her outright, and He does not tell anyone else this plainly until the trial.

## What happens the moment after

*And at this point His disciples came, and they marvelled that He talked with a woman.*

They say nothing. John notes both: *yet no one said, "What do You seek?" or "Why are You
talking with her?"*

## What she does

*The woman then **left her waterpot**, went her way into the city, and said to the men, "Come,
see a Man who told me all things that I ever did. Could this be the Christ?"*

She leaves the thing she came for.

Her sentence to the town is a question, not a claim.

## The result

*And many of the Samaritans of that city believed in Him **because of the word of the
woman**.* See the note on John 4:39–42.

And then two days of Him staying, and: *now we believe, not because of what you said, for we
ourselves have heard Him*.

## What He said in between

To the disciples, with the townspeople walking out across the field toward Him: *My food is
to do the will of Him who sent Me*. See the note on John 4:31–34.

The meal He is talking about is this conversation.

## Where He says it again

In the garden, to the men with torches: *"Who are you looking for?" "Jesus of Nazareth."
"**I am He**."* See the note on John 18:4–9.

And they fall over.

At the well it is said to one woman and she goes and tells a town.

> **Who He said it to.** Not a council and not a crowd. A woman at a well in the middle of
> the day, in the wrong country, who had come at that hour to avoid meeting anybody.
