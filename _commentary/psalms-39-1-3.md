A man decides to say nothing, and the decision does not hold.

## The resolution

*I said, **I will watch my ways so my tongue does not sin**.*

The Hebrew is *I will take heed to my ways*.

It is a deliberate policy, adopted in advance.

## The guard

*I will **keep a guard on my mouth**.*

The Hebrew is **machsom** — a muzzle. The word occurs once in the Bible.

Not a watchman at a door. A muzzle.

David asks for something gentler elsewhere: *set a guard, O LORD, over my mouth; keep watch
at the door of my lips*. See the note on Psalms 141:3–4.

There he asks God to post a sentry. Here he straps something on himself.

## Why

*While **the wicked stand before me**.*

The Hebrew is *while the wicked is before me*.

The restraint is for their benefit, or at least because of them. He does not want to give
them anything.

## What he did

*So I stayed **silent, still, holding back even from good**.*

The Hebrew is *I was mute with silence; I held my peace even from good*.

Three words for the same thing, and then the admission: he suppressed the good along with
the rest.

Total silence is indiscriminate. Shutting the mouth shuts everything.

## What it cost

*And **my sorrow only stirred**.*

The Hebrew is *my sorrow was stirred up*.

The silence did not settle anything. It agitated it.

## The heat

*My **heart grew hot inside me**.*

## The verb

*The more **I mused**, the more the fire burned.*

The Hebrew is **hagig** — from the root **hagah**, the meditation word. To mutter, to
murmur, to go over something.

It is the same root as Joshua 1:8 and Psalm 1: *in His law he meditates day and night*. See
the note on Joshua 1:6–9.

Here the muttering is of grievance rather than of Scripture, and the effect is the same:
what is turned over grows.

## The break

*Then **my tongue could hold no longer**.*

The Hebrew is *then I spoke with my tongue*.

The muzzle fails.

## What comes out

*"LORD, **make me know my end**, and what is the measure of my days, that I may know how
frail I am."*

*"Indeed, You have made my days as handbreadths, and my age is as nothing before You.
**Certainly every man at his best state is but vapour**."*

The Hebrew is **hevel** — the Ecclesiastes word.

## Where the psalm ends

*"Remove Your gaze from me, that I may regain strength, before I go away and am no more."*

It is one of the bleakest closings in the psalter, and nothing resolves it.

## What Jeremiah says about the same fire

*Then I said, "I will not make mention of Him, nor speak anymore in His name." **But His word
was in my heart like a burning fire shut up in my bones**; I was weary of holding it back,
and I could not.*

The same experiment, the same result.

## What silence did

*I was mute with silence, I held my peace even from good; and my sorrow was stirred up. **My
heart was hot within me; while I was musing, the fire burned.***

He decided not to speak and it made things worse. The Hebrew has the fire kindling *in* the
musing, not despite it.

## Where the same fire is a good sign

*And they said to one another, "**Did not our heart burn within us** while He talked with us on
the road, and while He opened the Scriptures to us?"*

Two men on the Emmaus road, describing the identical sensation — a heat inside while thinking —
and in their case the cause was **Jesus** walking beside them explaining the Bible.

The psalm has a man burning alone with nobody to say it to. Luke has the same burning with
Somebody in the conversation.

## What he finally asks

*"**LORD, make me to know my end**, and what is the measure of my days, that I may know how
frail I am."*

And then: *"**And now, Lord, what do I wait for? My hope is in You.**"*

The psalm that begins with a man muzzling himself ends with him talking to the only one worth
breaking the silence for.

*Casting all your care upon Him, for He cares for you.*

> **What the muzzle produced.** Not calm. He suppressed the good along with the bad, the
> sorrow stirred, and the turning-over set it alight — and the psalm's first words come out
> under pressure.
