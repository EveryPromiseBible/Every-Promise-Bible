The Hebrew line can be read two ways, and both readings are true of the same God.

## The blessing

*Blessed be the Lord, who **day after day carries our burdens**.*

The Hebrew is **yom yom ya'amos-lanu**.

## The doubled word

**Yom yom** — day day. The repetition is the Hebrew way of saying *daily, without a gap*.

## The verb

**Amas** — to load up, to lade. A pack-animal word.

## The ambiguity

The Hebrew has no object attached to the verb.

It can be read *He loads us with benefits* — the older English reading — or *He bears our burden*.

The verb is the same either way: loading. The question is which direction the load is going.

## Why both stand

He puts good things on us, and He takes weight off us.

The Hebrew line will carry either, and the psalm gives no ruling.

## The pause

*Selah.*

The word appears seventy-one times in the Psalms and its meaning is not settled — a musical rest,
or a direction to lift the voice.

Here it falls between the carrying and the salvation.

## The title

*The God who is **our salvation**.*

The Hebrew is **ha'el yeshu'atenu** — the God, our salvation.

**Yeshuah** — the noun behind the name Jesus.

## The two lines about death

*Our God is **a God who saves**, and to the Lord GOD belongs **escape from death**.*

The Hebrew is **le-YHWH Adonai lammavet totza'ot** — to the LORD belong the goings-out from death.

## The word

**Totza'ot** — exits, outlets. The word used of the boundary lines of territory, where a border runs
out.

Death has exits, and the psalm says who holds the keys to them.

## Where that is said again

*"I am He who lives, and was dead… and **I have the keys of Hades and of Death**."* See the note on
Revelation 1:17–18.

## What the psalm has been doing

*Let God arise, let His enemies be scattered.* The opening line, and the words Israel said when the
ark set out.

The whole psalm is a procession — God moving from Sinai to the sanctuary.

## The verse Paul quotes

*You have ascended on high, **You have led captivity captive**; **You have received gifts among men**.*
See the note on Psalms 68:17–18.

*Therefore He says: "When He ascended on high, He led captivity captive, and **gave gifts to men**."*
See the note on Ephesians 4:7–10.

Paul turns *received* into *gave*.

## The line before that

*A father of the fatherless, a defender of widows, is God in His holy habitation. **God sets the
solitary in families**.* See the note on Psalms 68:4–6.

The psalm of the marching army stops to name the three people with nobody.

> **The two readings.** *He loads us with benefits*, or *He bears our burden* — the Hebrew verb is
> the same word for loading, and the psalm does not say which way the weight is moving.
