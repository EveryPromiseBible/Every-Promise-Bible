Paul explains why God set the whole thing up on believing rather than on performance,
and then describes the God being believed.

## Why it rests on believing

*That is why it rests on believing — **so that it can be a matter of grace**, and so
that **the promise stands guaranteed**.*

Two reasons, and the second is the practical one.

A promise conditional on my keeping is only as certain as my keeping. Move it onto
believing and it moves onto grace, and a promise resting on grace has nothing shaky
underneath it.

The Greek for *guaranteed* is **bebaios** — firm, valid, the word used of a legally
binding document.

## Who it covers

*Not only for those who have the law, but **for those who share the believing of
Abraham**. He is **the father of us all**.*

The line is drawn by believing, not by descent. Paul has already pointed out that
Abraham was declared righteous while still uncircumcised — before the mark, before
the law, before anything that would exclude anybody.

## The two things God does

*The God who **gives life to the dead** and **calls things that do not exist as
though they already do**.*

Paul picks these two out of everything he could have said about God, because both
are what Abraham's situation required.

**Gives life to the dead** — Abraham's body *as good as dead*, Sarah's womb *dead as
well*, in Paul's own words four verses later.

**Calls things that do not exist as though they already do** — the Greek is to call,
to name, to summon. God speaks of a thing in the present tense while it is still
nothing.

## Where God did that

*"**I have made you a father of many nations**."*

Paul quotes Genesis 17, and the tense is the point: *I have made* — past, completed,
said to a childless man of ninety-nine.

And in the same breath God changed his name. **Abram** means exalted father.
**Abraham** means father of a multitude.

He had one son by a servant girl and no son by his wife. From that day everybody in
the camp had to call him *father of a multitude*, every time they wanted his
attention, for a year before Isaac was born. See the note on Genesis 17:3–8.

## The order

God said it first. The name came before the child, the promise before the evidence,
and Abraham's part was to answer to the name he had been given.

*He is our father **in the sight of the God he believed**.*

> **What Genesis 1 sounds like beside this.** *Then God spoke: "Let there be light."
> And light was.* The same pattern, and Paul reaches for it as a description of who
> Abraham was dealing with. See the note on Genesis 1:3–5.
