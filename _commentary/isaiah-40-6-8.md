The second voice in the chapter, and it is told to cry out and asks what.

## The question

*A voice says, **Cry out**. And I asked, **What shall I cry?***

The prophet is commissioned and does not know the content. The answer he is given is not
encouraging.

## The answer

*All flesh is grass, and all its beauty like the flower of the field. The grass withers, the
flower fades, **when the breath of the LORD blows over it**.*

The Hebrew for *breath* is **ruach** — wind, breath, spirit. The same word for what withers the
grass in verse 7 and what hovers over the water in Genesis 1.

*Surely **the people are grass**.*

Said flatly, in the chapter that opens *comfort, comfort My people*.

## The turn

*The grass withers, the flower fades, **but the word of our God stands forever**.*

**Devar eloheinu yaqum le'olam** — the word of our God rises up and stands.

Two things are being compared and only one of them is temporary.

## Who quotes it, and what he adds

*Because "**all flesh is as grass, and all the glory of man as the flower of the grass**. The
grass withers, and its flower falls away, **but the word of the LORD endures forever**." **Now
this is the word which by the gospel was preached to you.***

**Peter** quotes all three verses and then, in one sentence, identifies the permanent thing. The
word that outlasts the grass is not an abstraction about Scripture's durability. It is the
message they had already heard.

## And what the Word did

*And **the Word became flesh** and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory.*

**John** 1. The thing Isaiah says stands forever, over against flesh that withers, became the
flesh.

*"Heaven and earth will pass away, but **My words will by no means pass away**."*

**Jesus** says of His own speech what Isaiah said of God's.

## What the chapter does with the withering

It does not use it to frighten anybody. Five verses later:

*Behold, the Lord GOD shall come with a strong hand… **He will feed His flock like a shepherd; He
will gather the lambs with His arm, and carry them in His bosom**.*

Grass in verse 7, and a shepherd carrying lambs in verse 11.

> **What Peter does with the verse.** He quotes it whole and then names the permanent thing in a
> single clause: *this is the word which by the gospel was preached to you.* Not durable
> Scripture in the abstract — the message the readers had already been told.
