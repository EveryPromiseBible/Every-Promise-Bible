Early in the Sermon on the Mount, He answers a charge nobody has made yet.

## The denial

*"**Do not imagine that I have come to tear up the Law or the Prophets**."*

The Greek is **katalusai** — to demolish, to pull down. It is used of a building being
taken apart, and of the temple: *destroy this temple*.

He raises the accusation before anyone brings it, which means He knew what the sermon
would sound like.

## The correction

*"I have come to **fill it full**."*

The Greek is **plerosai** — to fill, to complete, to bring to its intended fullness.

Not to keep it, in the first instance. To fill it.

## The guarantee

*"Until heaven and earth pass away, **not the smallest letter, not the least stroke of a
pen, will drop out of the Law**."*

The Greek is **iota** — the smallest Hebrew letter, **yod** — and **keraia**, a horn: the
tiny serif that distinguishes one Hebrew letter from another.

He picks the two smallest marks on a page.

## The term

*"Until **every last bit of it has been accomplished**."*

The Greek is **genetai** — until all comes to pass.

Two *untils* in one sentence: until heaven and earth pass, and until it is all
accomplished.

Luke's version of the same saying sits directly beside *the law and the prophets ran up to
the time of John*. See the note on Luke 16:14–18.

Both halves are His, in the same breath, in both Gospels.

## The ranking

*"Whoever **sets aside one of the smallest of these commands**, and teaches others to do
the same, will be **called least in the kingdom of heaven**."*

Least in the kingdom, not outside it.

## The standard

*"Unless **your righteousness goes further and deeper than the righteousness of the
teachers of the law and the Pharisees**, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven at
all."*

The Greek is **perisseuse** — exceed, overflow, go beyond.

## What that meant to the hearers

The Pharisees were the most scrupulous people in the country. They tithed garden herbs and
counted paces on the sabbath.

Telling a crowd on a hillside that they needed to exceed that is not a call to try
harder. Nobody in the audience could out-Pharisee a Pharisee.

## What the sermon does next

Six paragraphs, each beginning *you have heard that it was said… but I say to you*:

Not murder, but anger. Not adultery, but the look. Not divorce by certificate. Not oaths,
but plain speech. Not an eye for an eye, but the other cheek. Not love your neighbour and
hate your enemy, but love your enemies.

Every one goes inward, past what a court can reach.

That is the filling full.

## Where it lands

*"Therefore **you shall be perfect, just as your Father in heaven is perfect**."*

The Greek is **teleioi** — complete, brought to the end.

## Who is speaking

Paul's summary of the same thing: *Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to
everyone who believes.* See the note on Romans 10:1–4.

**Telos** — the end, and also the goal.

> **The two words to hold together.** Not one letter drops out, and not one of them is
> what He came to keep — He came to fill. And the standard He sets in the same paragraph
> is higher than the most careful men alive.
