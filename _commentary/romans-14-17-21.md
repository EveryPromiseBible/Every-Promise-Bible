key: Romans 14:17–21
title: "Not food and drink"
updated: 2026-08-09

The definition of the kingdom, given in the middle of an argument about a menu.

## The sentence

*"**For the kingdom of God is not food and drink, but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy
Spirit.**"*

Three nouns, and they are the three great terms of the letter's first eight chapters.

**Dikaiosyne** — righteousness. *The righteousness of God is revealed from believing to believing.*

**Eirene** — peace. *Therefore, having been justified by believing, we have peace with God through our
Lord Jesus Christ.*

**Chara** — joy. *We also rejoice in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now
received the reconciliation.*

**Paul** compresses eleven chapters into six words and uses them to settle a dispute about vegetables.

## In the Holy Spirit

The qualifier governs all three. None of them is a mood the reader is asked to produce.

*Now hope does not disappoint, because **the love of God has been poured out in our hearts by the
Holy Spirit** who was given to us.*

*But the fruit of the Spirit is love, **joy, peace**, longsuffering.*

## The measure of service

*"**Whoever serves Christ in this way is pleasing to God and approved by others.**"*

**Euarestos to theo** — well-pleasing to God. The same adjective **Paul** used in 12:1 of the living
sacrifice.

## What is worth pulling down

*"**Do not destroy the work of God for the sake of food.**"*

**Katalye** — demolish, unbuild. It is the verb **Jesus** uses of the temple: *destroy this temple*.

Set against it, two verses earlier: *let us pursue the things which make for peace and the things by
which one may **edify** another* — **oikodomes**, building up.

Two construction words, and **Paul** puts the argument entirely in those terms: the question is not
who is right about the meat, but which direction the building is going.

*Therefore comfort each other and **edify one another**, just as you also are doing.*

## The last clause

*"**It is good neither to eat meat nor drink wine nor do anything by which your brother stumbles.**"*

**Paul** ends the paragraph by voluntarily accepting the weak brother's restriction — not because he
thinks it is required, and he has said twice that it is not.

*For though I am free from all men, **I have made myself a servant to all**, that I might win the
more.*

> **What he does with a dispute about food.** Answers it by defining the kingdom in three words —
> righteousness, peace and joy — which are the three things the first eight chapters of the letter
> spent their length establishing.
