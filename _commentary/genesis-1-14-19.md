Day four, and the sun arrives three days after the light did.

## What they are for

*"Let lights appear in the sky to separate day from night, and **to mark the seasons, the
days, and the years**."*

The Hebrew for *seasons* is **mo'adim** — and it is not a word about weather. It is the word
Leviticus 23 uses of the **appointed feasts**: Passover, Unleavened Bread, Firstfruits,
Pentecost, Trumpets, Atonement, Tabernacles.

The calendar of the feasts is hung in the sky on the fourth day, before there is a nation to
keep them.

## What the feasts turn out to be

*Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us.* *He has become the **firstfruits** of those
who have fallen asleep.* *When the day of **Pentecost** had fully come…*

Paul's summary of the whole calendar: *a shadow of things to come, but **the substance is of
Christ***. See the note on Colossians 2:16–17.

Genesis 1 sets the clock; the feasts are the appointments on it; and every appointment is
about one Person.

## The light that came first

There was light on day one and no lamp in the sky until day four. See the note on
Genesis 1:3–5.

The light did not come from the sun. The sun was made to carry light that already existed.

## The greater and the lesser

*"**The brighter one to rule the day, the softer one to rule the night**."*

The moon makes nothing. It has no light in it at all — every beam it throws down at night is
light it received and is passing on.

Which is the whole job description. *You are the light of the world* is said by the One who
had just said *I am the light of the world*, and the order matters.

## The four words

*"**And He made the stars as well**."*

Every star in the universe, in a subordinate clause at the end of a sentence about a calendar.

The heavens *declare the glory of God*, and Genesis reports their manufacture as an aside.

## Where day four ends up

*But to you who fear My name **the Sun of Righteousness shall arise with healing in His
wings**.* See the note on Malachi 4:1–3.

And at the far end: *the city had no need of the sun or of the moon to shine in it, for the
glory of God illuminated it, **and the Lamb is its light***.

Day four is temporary. The lamps come down when the Light they were pointing at is in the
room.

> **What the lights were for.** Not mainly illumination — **mo'adim**, appointed times. The
> feast calendar was fixed in the sky on the fourth day, and every feast on it turned out to
> be a description of Christ.
