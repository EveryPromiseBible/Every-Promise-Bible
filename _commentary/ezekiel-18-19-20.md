Ezekiel is answering a proverb the exiles kept repeating, and the answer is one
of the clearest statements of individual responsibility in the Old Testament.

## The proverb he is dismantling

Earlier in the chapter: *the parents eat sour grapes, and the children's teeth
are set on edge.*

It was a complaint. The exiles were saying they were suffering for their
ancestors' sins — which had the convenient effect of making their own conduct
beside the point.

God tells them to stop using it.

## The ruling

*The child will not share the guilt of the parent, nor the parent the guilt of the
child. The righteousness of the righteous will be credited to him, and the
wickedness of the wicked will be charged against him.*

Two accounting words — **credited** and **charged**. Each account stands alone;
nothing transfers between them.

That is the principle, and Paul later uses the same vocabulary when he says
righteousness is credited to Abraham through faith.

## "The soul who sins is the one who will die"

Stated twice in the chapter. It is a sentence about death being earned rather
than inherited from a parent's record.

Read alongside Romans, where death entered through one man and life comes through
one Man, the two are addressing different questions: Ezekiel is answering people
blaming their fathers, Paul is describing what Adam and Christ each transferred
to those in them.

## Where the chapter is going

The whole point of this argument is the invitation at the end of it: *turn and
live… why will you die, house of Israel?*

The individual accounting is not stated to frighten. It is stated so that nobody
can say their outcome was fixed by somebody else's file — see the note on Ezekiel
33:10–11, where the same chapter's conclusion comes with an oath.
