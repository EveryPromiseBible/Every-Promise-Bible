Paul reports on a collection, and the people who gave are the ones who had to persuade him.

## Who he is talking about

The Macedonian churches — Philippi, Thessalonica, Berea.

*Moreover, brethren, we make known to you the grace of God bestowed on the churches of Macedonia:
that in **a great trial of affliction**, the abundance of their joy and **their deep poverty**
abounded in the riches of their liberality.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 8:1–2.

The Greek is **kata bathous ptocheia** — down-to-the-depth poverty. **Ptocheia** is beggary, not
modest means.

## The testimony

*I can **vouch for them**.*

The Greek is **marturo** — I bear witness. Paul puts his own name to the figure.

## The amount

*They gave **as much as they were able, and past what they were able**.*

The Greek is **kata dunamin… para dunamin** — according to ability, beyond ability.

Two prepositions, one noun. Up to the limit and then over it.

## Who asked

*And **nobody had to ask them**.*

The Greek is **authairetoi** — self-chosen, of their own accord.

The word appears twice in the New Testament, both in this chapter.

## The reversal

*They came **asking us, pleading with us and refusing to be put off**, for **the favor of a share in
this**.*

The Greek is **meta polles parakleseos deomenoi hemon ten charin** — with much entreaty begging
us for the grace.

Paul was not fundraising among them. They had to press him to be allowed in.

## The word for the collection

**Charis** — grace, favour.

Paul uses it of the collection itself throughout these two chapters. Money, called grace.

## What they wanted

*That we would take their gift and **take up with them the shared work of caring for the Lord's
people**.*

The Greek is **koinonian tes diakonias** — fellowship of the ministry.

**Koinonia** — partnership, having in common. They were not asking to donate. They were asking to
be partners in it.

## What he did not expect

*And it went **far beyond anything we had looked for**.*

The Greek is **ou kathos elpisamen** — not as we had hoped.

## The order

*They **gave themselves first to the Lord**, and then to us.*

The Greek is **heautous edokan proton to Kurio**.

Paul names the sequence, and it is the sentence the whole paragraph exists for. The money came
second.

## The last clause

*Because **that was what God wanted**.*

**Dia thelematos Theou** — through the will of God.

## What the collection was for

The poor believers in Jerusalem. Paul spent years organising it across the Gentile churches, and
it is the practical expression of everything he argued about Jew and Gentile in one body.

*For it pleased those from Macedonia and Achaia to make a certain contribution for the poor among
the saints who are in Jerusalem.*

## What he asks Corinth

*Therefore, as you abound in everything — in faith, in speech, in knowledge, in all diligence, and
in your love for us — **see that you abound in this grace also**.*

*A year ago you were the first not only to act but to want to act. **Now finish the doing of it**.*
See the note on 2 Corinthians 8:10–12.

Corinth was rich, started first, and had not finished. Macedonia was destitute and had.

## The example he ends on

*For you know **the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He
became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich.* See the note on
2 Corinthians 8:9.

## The rule

*For **I do not mean that others should be eased and you burdened**, but by an equality.* See the
note on 2 Corinthians 8:13–15.

> **The sequence Paul names.** *They gave themselves first to the Lord, and then to us.* The
> paragraph is a fundraising report and that is the line it is written for.
