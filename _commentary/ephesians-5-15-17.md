Three short clauses, and the middle one is a market transaction.

## The instruction

*So **watch carefully how you walk**.*

The Greek is **blepete akribos** — look precisely, examine closely.

**How you walk** — Paul's standing word for how a life is conducted. He uses it six times
in this letter.

## The comparison

*Not as fools walk **but as the wise do**.*

## The purchase

*Buying back **the time you are given**.*

The Greek is **exagorazomenoi ton kairon** — redeeming the time.

**Exagorazo** is a market word: to buy out, to purchase entirely. It is the same verb Paul
uses of Christ redeeming us from the curse of the law. See the note on
Galatians 3:10–14.

**Kairos**, not **chronos**. Not clock time — the opportune moment, the window while it is
open.

The image is of buying something off the shelf before somebody else does.

## The reason

*Because **the days are evil**.*

Paul gives one reason and it is not encouraging.

He is writing from Roman custody, and the letter mentions his chains three times.

## The conclusion

*For this reason **do not be foolish, but understand what the will of the Lord is**.*

The Greek is **suniete** — comprehend, put together.

Paul does not say *find out*. The knowing is described as understanding rather than
discovering.

## What comes immediately after

*And do not be drunk with wine, in which is dissipation; but **be filled with the Spirit**.*

The Greek is a present passive imperative — *be being filled*, continuously.

And then: *speaking to one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and
making melody in your heart to the Lord, **giving thanks always for all things**.*

The buying back of time is followed immediately by singing and thanks.

## Where Paul says it again

*Live wisely toward those who are outside, and **buy up every hour you are given**.* See the
note on Colossians 4:5–6.

Almost identical, and there the context is conversations with outsiders.

Both letters were written from the same imprisonment.

## What Paul means by the days being evil

The letter says it in chapter 6: *we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against
principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this age*.

And *take up the whole armour of God, that you may be able to withstand in the evil day*.
See the note on Ephesians 6:10–13.

## What is not said

Nothing about efficiency. The Greek is about seizing an opportunity, not about managing a
schedule.

## Where the chapter has come from

*Therefore be imitators of God, as dear children. And walk in love.* See the note on
Ephesians 5:1–2.

And the whole practical half of the letter runs from chapter 4: *walk worthy of the calling
with which you were called*.

> **What is being bought.** Not more hours. **Kairos** — the moment while it is available —
> and Paul's stated reason for the urgency is that the days are bad.
