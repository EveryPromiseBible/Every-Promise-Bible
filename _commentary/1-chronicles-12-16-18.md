David is not king yet. He is a fugitive in a stronghold, and armed men keep
arriving — some from Saul's own tribe, which is exactly the problem.

## The question he asks them

*If you come to me in peace, to help me, then my heart will be knit to yours. But
if you come to betray me…*

He puts it to them straight, and leaves the judging to God rather than
interrogating them or turning them away. A hunted man cannot afford either
paranoia or naivety, and David does something in between: he names both
possibilities out loud and waits.

## The answer

The Spirit comes on Amasai, and what comes out is not a speech but a chant, three
beats of *peace*:

> Peace, peace to you, and peace to all who help you, for your God is your help.

He does not offer terms or negotiate a position. He declares whose side they are
on and blesses the man they are joining — and the blessing is grounded not in
David's prospects but in God being his help.

## Who is arriving

Men of Benjamin and Judah, coming to David at the stronghold while he is still an outlaw with
a price on him.

Benjamin is Saul's tribe. Some of these men are relatives of the king hunting him.

## What David asks

*"**If you have come peaceably to help me, my heart will be united with you**; but if to
betray me to my enemies, since there is no wrong in my hands, may the God of our fathers look
and bring judgment."*

He cannot tell. He asks them straight out and leaves the answer to them.

## What comes over Amasai

*Then **the Spirit came upon Amasai**, chief of the captains, and he said:*

*"**We are yours, O David; we are on your side, O son of Jesse! Peace, peace to you, and peace
to your helpers, for your God helps you.**"*

The Hebrew is **shalom, shalom** — said three times in one sentence, over a man living in a
cave.

## What the confession is

Not an offer of service. A statement about whose they are — *we are yours* — spoken before
David has a throne, an army or anything to give them.

*If you **confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus** and believe in your heart that God has
raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.*

Paul's two verbs are the two things Amasai does: says it out loud, and attaches himself to a
king nobody has crowned yet.

## What David does about it

*Then David received them, and **made them captains of the troop**.*

Men who arrived under suspicion are given command.

> **What David does with it.** He welcomed them and *made them leaders of his
> raiding bands* — command, straight away, to men who had been potential
> assassins ten minutes earlier. The chapter is a long list of people who came
> over to David before he had anything to give them, and the writer is keeping
> count on purpose.
