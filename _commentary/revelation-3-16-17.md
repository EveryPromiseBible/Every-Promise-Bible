The only church on the list that has nothing to say against itself, and the longest inventory said
against it.

## The temperature

*"As it is you are **tepid — not one thing, not the other**."*

The Greek is **chliaros** — lukewarm. It appears once in the New Testament.

## The city's water

Laodicea had no spring. Water came by aqueduct from Hierapolis, six miles away.

Hierapolis had hot mineral springs. Colossae, ten miles the other way, had cold mountain water.

By the time either reached Laodicea it was tepid and full of mineral deposit — famously undrinkable.

## What the image is

Hot water heals. Cold water refreshes. Laodicea's arrived doing neither.

The complaint is not about intensity of feeling. It is about being useless at the point of arrival.

## The verb

*"And **My mouth is going to throw you back up**."*

The Greek is **emesai** — to vomit. The root of *emetic*.

The natural reaction to their own water supply.

## The self-assessment

*"You keep saying: **I am rich, I have done well for myself, there is nothing I need**."*

Three claims in a row.

## Whether it was true

Financially, yes. Laodicea was a banking centre. When an earthquake levelled the city in AD 60,
Tacitus records that it rebuilt itself without imperial assistance.

It was also known for a black wool textile trade and for a medical school that produced an eye
ointment.

## What He lists

*"And **you have no idea what you actually are** — a wretch, pitiful, **dirt poor, blind, with nothing
on**."*

Poor — in a banking town.

Blind — in a town that made eye-salve.

Naked — in a town that made clothing.

Every item on the list is the city's own export.

## The clause

*"You have no idea."*

The Greek is **ouk oidas** — you do not know.

The problem is not the poverty. It is that the inventory is news to them.

## What He offers

*"I counsel you to **buy from Me gold refined in the fire**, that you may be rich; **and white garments**,
that you may be clothed; **and anoint your eyes with eye salve**, that you may see."* See the note on
Revelation 3:18–19.

The three items, sold back to them by the one who is outside.

## Where He is standing

*"Behold, **I stand at the door and knock**."* See the note on Revelation 3:20.

The most quoted evangelistic verse in Revelation is addressed to a church, from outside it.

## The other end of the list

Philadelphia, a few miles up the road: *you have a little strength*, and an open door nobody can
shut. See the note on Revelation 3:9–10.

Little strength, door open. Great wealth, door shut and Christ on the other side of it.

## What the rebuke ends with

*"**As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten**. Therefore be zealous and repent."*

The harshest letter of the seven contains the word *love*, and gives it as the reason for the rest.

> **The exports.** Poor in a banking town, blind in a town that made eye-salve, naked in a town that
> made cloth. Every item on the list is the thing they were famous for.
