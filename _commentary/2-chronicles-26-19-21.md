The censer is still in his hand when it happens.

## The rage

*Then Uzziah **flew into a rage**. The censer was in his hand… and **while he
raged at the priests**, leprosy broke out on his forehead.*

He is corrected and his answer is fury. The chronicler puts the anger and the
leprosy in the same sentence, in that order.

## Where it appeared

*Leprosy **on his forehead**.*

The forehead is where the high priest wore the gold plate engraved *HOLINESS TO
THE LORD*.

A man standing where only the priest could stand, and the mark comes out on the
one place the priest's holiness was displayed.

## Where he was standing

*There before the priests, in the house of the LORD, **beside the altar of
incense**.*

The location is spelled out. Not later, not at home, not on the way out. At the
altar, in the middle of the act.

## Two directions at once

*They **rushed him out**, and **he himself hurried to leave**.*

The priests were pushing and he was already going. A leper could not remain in
that house and he knew it before anyone had to explain.

*Because the LORD had struck him.* The chronicler names who did it and does not
soften it.

## What it cost

*So King Uzziah was a leper **until the day he died**. He lived in a separate
house, a leper, **cut off from the house of the LORD**.*

Fifty-two years on the throne and the last of them spent in isolation, with his
son running the kingdom.

*Cut off from the house of the LORD* — the one place he had forced his way into is
the one place he can never enter again.

## The shape of the reign

- *as long as he sought the LORD, God gave him success*
- *he was marvellously helped, till he was strong*
- *when he had grown strong, his heart became proud*
- *the LORD had struck him*

Sought, helped, strong, proud, struck. The chronicler lays it out in order so
nobody can miss which step led to which.

> **What Isaiah saw the year this man died.** *In the year that King Uzziah died,
> I saw the Lord, high and lifted up, and His train filled the temple.* A king who
> forced his way into the sanctuary is buried, and a prophet is shown the One
> whose throne it actually is — with a live coal from the altar carried to his
> mouth by a seraph, because no one takes it for himself.
