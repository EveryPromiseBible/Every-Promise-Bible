One verse, spoken in the first person, and it interrupts John's greeting.

## Who is speaking

*This is **the Lord speaking**.*

The Greek is **legei Kurios ho Theos**.

The book has been John writing to seven churches. Here a voice takes over the sentence for a
verse and then hands it back.

## The letters

*I am **the first letter and the last**.*

The Greek is **ego eimi to Alpha kai to O** — I am the Alpha and the Omega.

The first and last characters of the Greek alphabet.

## What that claims

Not just *first and last*. The whole alphabet, and therefore everything that can be spelled with
it.

The Hebrew equivalent would be **aleph** and **tav**, and the rabbis used that pair the same way
— for completeness, from A to Z.

## The gloss

*The place everything starts and the place everything ends.*

The full form appears later in the book: *I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the
End, the First and the Last*. See the note on Revelation 22:12–15.

## The three tenses

*The One who is **here now**, who **was here before**, who is **on His way**.*

The Greek is **ho on kai ho en kai ho erchomenos** — the being one, and the was one, and the
coming one.

## The grammar

**Ho on** — the one being. It is exactly what the Septuagint puts in God's mouth at the burning
bush: **ego eimi ho on** — I AM THE BEING. See the note on Exodus 3:13–15.

**Ho en** — the one who was. Greek has no participle for the past tense of *to be*, so John
breaks the grammar rather than lose the parallel. It is ungrammatical on purpose.

## The third

Not *the one who will be*, which is what the pattern requires.

**Ho erchomenos** — the coming one.

The future tense of God's existence is stated as an arrival.

## The title

*The One who **holds all power**.*

The Greek is **ho pantokrator** — the Almighty, the all-ruling.

It appears nine times in Revelation and once elsewhere in the New Testament. The Septuagint uses
it for **YHWH Tzeva'ot** — the LORD of hosts.

## Where the verse sits

*Grace to you and peace **from Him who is and who was and who is to come**.* — verse 4, John's
greeting, using the same three-part formula.

*Behold, He is coming with clouds, and every eye will see Him.*

*"Even so, Amen."*

And then this verse, in the first person.

## Who is writing

*I, John, both your brother and companion in the tribulation… was on the island that is called
Patmos.* See the note on Revelation 1:9–11.

A prisoner on a rock in the Aegean, writing to seven congregations under pressure.

## What follows

The turning to see the voice, the seven lampstands, and the figure among them.

*And when I saw Him, **I fell at His feet as dead**. But He laid His right hand on me, saying to
me, "Do not be afraid; **I am the First and the Last**."* See the note on
Revelation 1:17–18.

The same title, applied by the figure to Himself, with a hand on John's shoulder.

## Where it closes

*"I am the Alpha and the Omega, the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End."*

*The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all.* See the note on
Revelation 22:20–21.

> **The grammar.** *Ho on, ho en, ho erchomenos.* The middle term is ungrammatical Greek — there
> is no past participle of *to be* — and John keeps the parallel rather than the grammar.
