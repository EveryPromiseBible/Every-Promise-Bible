Paul closes the prosecution and states what the law was actually for.

## What comes before

Three chapters of indictment. The Gentile world in chapter 1, the moralist in chapter 2,
the Jew in chapter 3 — and then a chain of six Old Testament quotations:

*There is none righteous, no, not one… their throat is an open tomb… their mouth is full
of cursing and bitterness… there is no fear of God before their eyes.*

Most of the quotations are about the mouth. Then Paul draws his conclusion about mouths.

## Who the law addresses

*Whatever the law says, **it says to those who live under the law**.*

The Greek is *to those in the law*.

Paul is heading off the obvious objection. The quotations he has just stacked up are from
Israel's own Scriptures, addressed to Israel — so the people most confident of their
standing are the ones being described.

## The purpose

*The point is that **every mouth would be closed** and **the whole world would stand
accountable to God**.*

The Greek for *closed* is **phrasso** — to stop up, to fence, to muzzle. It is used of
damming a stream and of stopping the mouths of lions.

**Accountable** is **hupodikos** — a legal term for a defendant liable to judgment, with
no defence left to enter.

The image is a courtroom where the case has been made and nobody has anything to say.

## The verdict

*So **no human being will be put right with Him by doing what the law requires**.*

The Greek is *by works of the law no flesh shall be justified in His sight*.

Paul repeats it in Galatians in almost identical words.

## What the law does instead

*What the law does is **show us what sin is**.*

The Greek is *through the law is the knowledge of sin* — **epignosis**, full
recognition.

Not the cure. The diagnosis.

Paul says it again in chapter 7: *I would not have known sin except through the law. For
I would not have known covetousness unless the law had said, "You shall not covet."*

## What the law is not accused of

Anything. Paul defends it repeatedly — *the law is holy, and the commandment holy and
just and good*, and *do we then make void the law through faith? Certainly not! On the
contrary, we establish the law.*

The argument is about function, not quality.

## The very next word

*But now.*

*But now **the righteousness of God apart from the law is revealed**… even the
righteousness of God, through faith in Jesus Christ, **to all and on all who believe**.
For **there is no difference**.*

Three chapters of prosecution, one sentence of *every mouth stopped*, and then *but
now*.

## What follows

*Being **justified freely by His grace** through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.*

The Greek for *freely* is **dorean** — as a gift, without cause, for nothing. It is the
word used of hating *without a cause*.

## Where boasting goes

*Where is boasting then? **It is excluded**.*

Same idea as the closed mouth, four verses on.

> **What the silence is for.** Paul does not stop the mouths to leave the room quiet. The
> next thing in the chapter is *but now* — and the sentence after the courtroom is about
> a gift.
