The tribe that gets no land is told what it gets instead.

## Where the chapter sits

Directly after Korah's rebellion and Aaron's rod that budded. Korah's complaint was about
who gets to come near; this chapter sets out what the nearness costs and what it pays.
See the note on Numbers 16:1–15.

## The gift

*"**And I give you a portion**," the LORD said.*

The Hebrew is *I have given you the charge of My heave offerings*.

## What is on the list

*"The offerings the people bring Me — **the grain gifts, the sin offerings, the guilt
offerings, the holy things** — these are yours."*

*"**The first ripe fruits, the first oil and wine and grain, the firstborn of the flocks,
the things devoted to Me** — I give them to you."*

The firsts. Not what is left over after the altar; the first of each crop and each flock.

## Who eats it

*"**Yours and your sons' and daughters'** to eat."*

The whole household, sons and daughters both.

## The redemption price

*"Every **firstborn son and unclean animal is redeemed at a set price**."*

Five shekels, according to the verses that follow. A firstborn son belonged to God from
the night of the Passover, and was bought back.

## The name for it

*"This is your bread, **a covenant of salt, unbreakable, forever**."*

Salt because salt does not change, ferment or spoil. It is the term for an agreement that
cannot be dissolved. See the note on Leviticus 2:11–13.

The same phrase is used of David's throne.

## What they do not get

*"You will have **no land of your own among Israel, no inheritance of fields**."*

Every other tribe received territory. Levi received forty-eight towns scattered through
everyone else's allotments — including the six cities of refuge. See the note on
Numbers 35:9–15.

A man fleeing a death ran into a Levite town and lived off the priests' provision.

## The exchange

*"For **I am your portion and your inheritance**."*

The Hebrew for *portion* is **cheleq** — a share, an allotted piece of ground. It is a
surveying word.

God uses the land word about Himself and gives it to the tribe with no land.

## The last line

*"When others count their acres, **you will count Me**."*

## Where it turns up in the psalms

*The LORD is **the portion of my inheritance** and my cup; You maintain my lot. The lines
have fallen to me in pleasant places.*

David, who owned a kingdom, uses the Levite's line about himself.

And Asaph: *my flesh and my heart fail, but God is the strength of my heart and **my
portion forever**.*

Jeremiah in the ruins: *"**The LORD is my portion**," says my soul, "therefore I hope in
Him."*

## What the Levites lived on

The tithe, and they tithed on that in turn — a tenth of the tenth, the best of it, up to
the priests. See the note on Numbers 18:21–32.

> **The trade.** No fields, no boundary stones, no inheritance to pass on. In place of
> all of it, one sentence: *I am your portion.*
