A man goes from a prison cell to second in Egypt inside one morning.

## Where he had been

*Then Pharaoh sent and called Joseph, and **they brought him hastily out of the dungeon**;
and he shaved, changed his clothing, and came to Pharaoh.*

Thirteen years — sold at seventeen, in Potiphar's house, then in prison on a false charge,
then forgotten for two more years by a butler who had promised to remember him.

## What he had just said

Pharaoh had told him *I have heard it said of you that you can understand a dream to
interpret it*.

Joseph's answer: *"**It is not in me; God will give Pharaoh an answer of peace**."*

The same disclaimer Daniel gives Nebuchadnezzar centuries later. See the note on
Daniel 2:29–30.

## The assessment

*The plan pleased Pharaoh and all his officials.*

Joseph had not only interpreted. He had added an administrative proposal — a fifth of the
harvest, stored through seven years — that nobody asked him for.

## Pharaoh's question

*"Can we find anyone like this man, **one in whom the Spirit of God lives?**"*

The Hebrew is *a man in whom is the Spirit of God*.

A pagan king says it, about a Hebrew slave, in front of his whole court.

## The reasoning

*"**Since God has shown you all this**, there is **no one as discerning and wise as you**."*

Pharaoh attributes the ability to God and promotes the man anyway.

## The appointment

*"**You will be over my house**, and all my people will do as you say. **Only in the throne
will I be greater than you**."*

## The insignia

*He took **the signet ring from his own hand** and put it on Joseph's.*

A signet ring was the seal of authority. Whatever it stamped was law.

*He **dressed him in fine linen** and hung **a gold chain around his neck**.*

Linen and gold — the dress of an Egyptian official, and both appear in the tomb paintings.

*He had him **ride in the chariot of his second-in-command**, and men went ahead of him
calling out, "**Bow down!**"*

## The sentence

*"I am Pharaoh, but **without your word no one will lift a hand or foot in all of Egypt**."*

## The new name

*Pharaoh gave Joseph a new name, **Zaphenath-Paneah**.*

The meaning is disputed. Older readings take it as *revealer of secrets*; Egyptologists
have proposed *God speaks and he lives*.

Daniel is renamed Belteshazzar in the same way, by the same kind of court.

## The wife

*And gave him **Asenath, daughter of Potiphera the priest of On**, to be his wife.*

On is Heliopolis, the centre of Egyptian sun worship. Joseph marries into its priesthood.

Genesis records it without comment, and their two sons — Manasseh and Ephraim — become two
of Israel's tribes.

## His age

*Joseph was **thirty years old** when he stood before Pharaoh.*

Seventeen when he was sold. Thirteen years between the pit and the palace.

## What he names his sons

**Manasseh** — *for God has made me forget all my toil and all my father's house*.

**Ephraim** — *for God has caused me to be fruitful in the land of my affliction*.

He names the second one after the place, not in spite of it.

## What the whole thing was for

*"**God sent me before you to preserve life**."* And: *"**You meant it for evil against me,
but God meant it for good**."* See the note on Genesis 50:15–21.

> **What Pharaoh notices.** Not the administration, first. He asks his court whether they
> can find another man with the Spirit of God in him — and then hands over the ring.
