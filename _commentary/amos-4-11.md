The last of five identical paragraphs, and the refrain at the end of each is the point.

## The refrain

Five times in seven verses: *yet you have not returned to Me. So says the LORD.*

Empty stomachs. Withheld rain. Blight and mildew. Plague and the sword. And now Sodom.

Each paragraph names a disaster and ends with the same nine words. The chapter is a list of
things that did not work.

## The image

*"You were like **a burning stick snatched from the fire**."*

The Hebrew is **ke'ud mutzal missrefah** — a brand delivered out of the burning.

Not a survivor. A piece of firewood, already alight, pulled out by somebody's hand.

## Where the phrase goes

*And the LORD said to Satan, "The LORD rebuke you, Satan!… **Is this not a brand plucked from the
fire?**" Now Joshua was clothed with filthy garments, and was standing before the Angel.*

Zechariah 3, and it is the same Hebrew. A high priest in soiled clothes, with the accuser standing
at his right hand, and the defence offered on his behalf is not that he is clean. It is that
somebody already pulled him out.

*Then He answered and spoke to those who stood before Him, saying, "**Take away the filthy garments
from him.**" And to him He said, "See, **I have removed your iniquity from you, and I will clothe
you with rich robes**."*

Joshua says nothing in the whole scene.

*And on some have compassion, making a distinction; but others **save with fear, pulling them out
of the fire**.*

**Jude** keeps the image and makes it an instruction.

## What the brand did not do

Anything.

The verb is passive both times — **mutzal**, delivered. The stick is not commended for
extinguishing itself.

*He also brought me up out of a horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock.*

*For **when we were still without strength**, in due time Christ died for the ungodly.*

## And the refrain that follows it

*Yet you have not returned to Me.*

The brand is out of the fire and has still not turned round. Amos records the rescue and the
non-response in the same sentence, and the chapter's five disasters end with an instruction that
is not one:

*"**Prepare to meet your God, O Israel.**"*

## What the next chapter says instead

*"**Seek Me and live.**"* See the note on Amos 5:4–6.

Five paragraphs of things that failed to turn them, and then two words.

> **The picture.** Not a man who escaped a fire — a piece of firewood already burning, pulled out
> by a hand that is not his. Zechariah uses the same phrase of a high priest in filthy clothes,
> and the answer to the accuser is that the rescue has already happened.
