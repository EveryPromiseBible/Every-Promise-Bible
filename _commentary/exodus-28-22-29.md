Eight verses of fastening instructions, and the last sentence says why.

## The chains

*For the breastpiece, make **braided chains of pure gold, like cords**.*

The Hebrew is **sharsherot gavlut** — chains of twisted work.

## The upper rings

*Make **two gold rings** and fix them to its **two upper corners**, and tie the two gold cords to those
rings; then fasten the far ends of the cords **to the two filigree settings up on the shoulder
pieces**.*

The top corners are hung from the shoulders.

## The lower rings

*Make **two more gold rings** for the **two lower corners** of the breastpiece, on the inside edge next
to the ephod.*

*And **two more gold rings** low on the front of the shoulder pieces, near the seam **just above the
woven waistband**.*

Six rings in all.

## The cord

*Then **lace the breastpiece by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a cord of blue**.*

The Hebrew is **biphtil tekelet** — with a thread of blue.

**Tekelet** — the blue dye from a sea creature, the costliest colour in the tabernacle's palette.

## The purpose of all of it

*So it **sits snug above the waistband and never swings loose**.*

The Hebrew is **velo-yizzach hachoshen me'al ha'ephod** — and the breastpiece shall not come loose
from the ephod.

## What the eight verses are for

One sentence.

Top secured, bottom secured, gold chains above and a blue cord below, so the thing cannot shift.

## Why it matters

*So whenever Aaron walks into the Holy Place, he **carries the names of the sons of Israel over his
heart**, on the breastpiece of judgment — **a constant remembrance before the LORD**.*

Twelve stones, each engraved with a tribal name like a signet. See the note on
Exodus 28:15–21.

## The word

**Al-libbo** — over his heart.

Repeated in the next verse, of the Urim and Thummim: *so Aaron shall bear the judgment of the
children of Israel over his heart before the LORD continually*.

## The other word

**Tamid** — continually, perpetually.

The whole point of the fastenings is that the names cannot come off. The engineering exists to
make a relationship permanent.

## What the shoulders carry

*And you shall put the two stones on the shoulders of the ephod as memorial stones for the sons of
Israel. **So Aaron shall bear their names before the LORD on his two shoulders as a memorial**.*

Two onyx stones, six names on each, *according to their birth*.

Strength on the shoulders, affection at the heart, and the same twelve names on both.

## Who is not there

The tribes.

Only the high priest enters. The names go in without the people, fastened to a man's chest by
gold cord.

## What Hebrews says

*For **Christ has not entered the holy places made with hands**, which are copies of the true, but
into heaven itself, **now to appear in the presence of God for us**.* See the note on
Hebrews 9:23–24.

## Where the names end up

*I will write on him **the name of My God** and the name of the city of My God… **and My new name**.*
See the note on Revelation 3:12–13.

## The Song's version

*Set me as a seal upon your heart, as a seal upon your arm; for **love is as strong as death**.*

The two places the high priest wore the names, named by a bride.

> **Eight verses of fastenings.** Gold chains at the top, a blue cord at the bottom, six rings —
> all of it so that a set of names cannot come off a man's chest.
