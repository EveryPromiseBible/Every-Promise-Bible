One of Paul's severest paragraphs, and the last clause tells you what the whole
thing is for.

## Present in spirit

*Though I am absent in body I am **present with you in spirit**, and I have
already reached my judgment, as though I were standing there.*

He says the same thing to the Colossians — *absent in the flesh, with you in
spirit* — and there it is warm approval of a church holding its line. Here it is
a verdict.

The same phrase does both, which suggests it means something more than a fond
thought at a distance.

## When you are gathered

*In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, **when you are gathered together** — my
spirit with you, and the power of our Lord Jesus Christ with you.*

Three things are named as present at the meeting: the church, Paul's spirit, and
*the power of our Lord Jesus Christ*.

It is a gathering in the name, with the power, and Paul is careful to say so
before he says what is to be done.

## Handed over

*Such a one is to be handed over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh.*

The Greek for *handed over* is the same verb used of Jesus being delivered up. It
means to place into someone's hands.

**The flesh** here is set against **the spirit** in the next clause, and Paul's
usual use of *sarx* is not the body but the whole self as it operates apart from
God.

## The purpose clause

*…**so that the spirit may be saved** in the day of the Lord Jesus.*

Everything in the sentence bends toward this. The severity is instrumental. Paul
is not describing a punishment with an end in itself.

## What actually happened

If 2 Corinthians 2 refers to the same man — and most readers have taken it that
way — the sequel is Paul urging the church to *forgive and comfort him*, in case
he is *swallowed up with too much sorrow*, and to *reaffirm your love for him*.

The discipline worked, and Paul's next concern is that it not go too far.

> **What the chapter is really about.** Not the offender. Paul's anger in verse 2
> is at the congregation: *and you are proud!* A church that boasted of its
> tolerance is the target, and the sentence in verse 5 is the shock administered
> to it.
