The most extraordinary sentence in the prophets, and it is put in five words.

## Where it falls in the count

*After the sixty-two sevens…*

Seven sevens and sixty-two sevens make sixty-nine — four hundred and eighty-three
years from the command to restore and rebuild Jerusalem.

Then this.

## The Anointed One

*The **Anointed One** will be cut off.*

The Hebrew is **Mashiach** — Messiah. Daniel names Him by title, and the previous
verse has already used it: *until Messiah the Prince.*

## "Cut off"

The Hebrew **karath** is the ordinary word for executing a covenant-breaker, and it
is also the verb used for making a covenant — *to cut* a covenant, from the animals
divided in Genesis 15.

## "And will have nothing"

The clause everything turns on. The older rendering is *but not for Himself*.

He is cut off, and gets nothing out of it. There is no throne at the end of the
sixty-nine sevens, no kingdom, no crown, no gain.

Isaiah had said the same thing in his own words: *He was cut off from the land of
the living; **for the transgressions of My people** He was stricken.*

## What follows it

*The people of **the prince who is to come** will destroy the city and the
sanctuary.*

Written in Babylon, in the sixth century BC, about a city that at that moment lay
in ruins and had not yet been rebuilt.

Titus and the Roman legions did it in AD 70 — the city and the temple, and not one
stone left on another.

Jesus said the same, weeping, on the same hill: *they will not leave one stone
standing on another in you, because you did not recognise the time when God came to
you.* See the note on Luke 19:41–44.

## The order in the verse

The Messiah is cut off **first**. The city and the sanctuary are destroyed
**after**.

Daniel's sequence requires the Messiah to come and be killed before AD 70. The
temple He had to come to was gone within forty years of the crucifixion, and the
genealogies that would prove anyone's descent from David burned with it.

## "Its end will come like a flood"

The Hebrew is an overwhelming torrent. And then: *war will continue to the end, and
desolations are decreed.*

The sentence does not stop at AD 70. It runs on.

> **What is not in this verse.** The seventieth seven. Verse 27 describes it —
> a covenant made and broken in the middle of the week — and verse 26 stands between
> the sixty-ninth and it, describing a cutting off, a destroyed city, and a long
> stretch of war and desolation. The gap is where the sentence puts it. See the
> note on Daniel 9:24.
