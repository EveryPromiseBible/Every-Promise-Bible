One verse, and it explains why nothing under the sun has ever been enough.

## Where it sits

Directly after the famous list — *a time to be born and a time to die, a time to
plant and a time to pluck up, a time to weep and a time to laugh.*

Fourteen pairs, and then this verse is the Preacher's comment on all of them.

## "Beautiful in its time"

*He has made **everything beautiful in its time**.*

The Hebrew **yapheh** is beautiful, fitting, appropriate. Each thing suits the moment
it belongs to.

The weeping and the laughing are both on the list, and both are called beautiful in
their season.

## The clause the verse is known for

*He has also **set eternity in the human heart**.*

The Hebrew is **olam** — the age, the far distance, perpetuity. The same word used
of God's everlasting covenant.

Something with no horizon has been placed inside a creature who lives seventy years.

That is the Preacher's diagnosis of the human problem, and it is not a complaint
about sin. It is a description of design.

## What follows from it

*Yet **no one can fully grasp what God has done from beginning to end**.*

The capacity to want the whole thing, and no capacity to see it.

That is the tension the entire book runs on. Twelve chapters of a man with unlimited
means testing every avenue — wisdom, pleasure, building, wealth, work — and finding
each one *vapour*, because it is finite and he is not built for finite.

## Why the experiments failed

Not because the things were bad. He says elsewhere that eating your bread with joy
and enjoying your work are *from the hand of God.* See the note on
Ecclesiastes 2:24–26.

They failed because he was measuring them against something with no edge.

## The other half of the same observation

*God made humankind **upright**, but we have gone looking for **endless clever
schemes**.*

Ecclesiastes 7:29, and it is the Preacher's other conclusion about people: made
straight, and inventive about not staying that way.

## Where the Preacher lands

*Whatever God does, it shall be forever. Nothing can be added to it, and nothing
taken from it. God does it, that men should fear before Him.*

That is the very next verse. The thing that is permanent is what God does, and the
eternity in the heart is what recognises it.

> **What Augustine made of this verse.** *You have made us for Yourself, and our heart
> is restless until it rests in You.* Sixteen centuries of readers have taken the
> restlessness in this line as the point of it rather than the problem with it.
