The first thing declared holy in the Bible is not a place or a person. It is a
day, and it is a day off.

## "It was finished"

*And so it was finished — the heavens, the earth, and everything in them, **down to
the smallest detail**.*

The Hebrew is *all the host of them*, and the verb is completion.

The same idea is spoken from the cross: **tetelestai**, it is finished. See the note
on John 19:28–30.

## Why He rested

*On that seventh day He rested. **Not because He was weary, but because the work was
whole and good.**"*

Isaiah says it outright: *the everlasting God, the LORD, the Creator of the ends of
the earth, **neither faints nor is weary**.*

The rest is not recovery. It is what you do when a thing is done.

## What was blessed

*So God **blessed the seventh day and set it apart as holy**.*

The first use of the word **holy** in Scripture, and it is applied to a day.

He had blessed the creatures on day five and the man on day six. Now He blesses time
itself.

## What man's first day was

Adam was made on the sixth day, near the end of it.

So the first full day of his existence was the seventh — the one God had already
finished everything on and rested through.

He began in a completed world, with nothing to do, on a day that was already blessed.
Everything he ate had been grown before he arrived.

## What is missing from this day

Read the six days before and each closes the same way: *and there was evening, and
there was morning — the first day.*

The seventh has no evening and no morning recorded. The formula stops.

Hebrews builds on that: *there remains therefore a rest for the people of God. For he
who has entered His rest has himself also ceased from his works, as God did from
His.*

## The word

The Hebrew for rested is **shabath** — to cease, to stop. It is the root of
**Sabbath**.

It does not mean to relax. It means to stop working because the work is over.

## What Jesus said about the day

*"The Sabbath was made for man, and not man for the Sabbath."*

And when He healed on it and was challenged: *"My Father has been working until now,
and I have been working."*

> **Where the pattern lands in the letters.** *For we who have believed do enter that
> rest… let us therefore be diligent to enter that rest.* Hebrews 4, and the effort
> named in it is the effort of stopping.
