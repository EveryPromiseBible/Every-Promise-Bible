The prophet gets it wrong on the first candidate, and is corrected in his own head.

## Who is looking

*Samuel took one look at Eliab, the oldest, and **thought**.*

The Hebrew is *said* — but it is interior. Nothing is spoken aloud.

## What he concluded

*"**Surely this is the one** — the LORD's anointed is standing right here."*

Samuel is the last judge of Israel, the man who heard a voice in the night as a boy, and
who had anointed Saul.

He is a genuine prophet, and his first read is wrong.

## The correction

*"**Don't look at how handsome he is, or how tall he stands**."*

The height matters because of who the last king was. Saul was chosen partly for it:
*from his shoulders upward he was taller than any of the people.*

Samuel is applying the criteria that produced Saul.

## The verdict

*"**I have not chosen him**."*

## The principle

*"**The LORD does not see the way people see**. People look at **the outside — the face, the
height, the polish**. But **the LORD looks at the heart**."*

The Hebrew is *man looks at the eyes* — what is visible — *but the LORD looks at the
heart*.

## What the chapter has already established

The verses before: God tells Samuel *how long will you mourn for Saul, seeing I have
rejected him from reigning over Israel? **Fill your horn with oil, and go**.*

Samuel is afraid — *if Saul hears it, he will kill me* — and is told to bring a heifer and
say he has come to sacrifice.

## What follows

Seven sons pass by. Abinadab, Shammah, and four more.

*And Samuel said to Jesse, "**The LORD has not chosen these**."*

Then the question that breaks it open: *are all the young men here?* See the note on
1 Samuel 16:11–13.

## Who was not invited

The eighth son was not brought to the sacrifice. His own father left him with the sheep.

The men in the room applied the same criteria Samuel had, and Samuel had to ask.

## Where the sentence is quoted

*Man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart.*

It becomes one of the most quoted lines in the Old Testament, and it is spoken as a
correction to a prophet.

## What Eliab does later

At the valley of Elah, when David asks about Goliath, Eliab is the brother who turns on
him: *why did you come down here? And with whom have you left those few sheep in the
wilderness? **I know your pride and the insolence of your heart**.*

The chapter's own theme, in the mouth of the man it was first applied to.

## What God does not look at

*"**The LORD does not see as man sees; for man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD
looks at the heart.**"*

Samuel had taken one look at Eliab — tall, eldest, the obvious one — and concluded *surely the
LORD's anointed is before Him*.

## Who else was passed over on sight

*He has **no form or comeliness**; and when we see Him, there is no beauty that we should
desire Him. He is **despised and rejected** by men.* See the note on Isaiah 53:1–3.

The One the whole line runs to was looked at and dismissed by the same measure Samuel nearly
used on the family.

*"Is this not the carpenter's son?"… And they were offended at Him.* Jesus was measured by the
same rule Samuel nearly used, and dismissed on it.

## Who was not in the room

David. Nobody thought to call him in from the field, and the sacrifice could not proceed until
somebody went to get him.

*But **God has chosen the foolish things** of the world to put to shame the wise… and the
things which are despised God has chosen, and the things which are not, to bring to nothing
the things that are.*

> **Who is corrected.** Not the family. The prophet — silently, in his own thought, before
> he had said a word out loud.
