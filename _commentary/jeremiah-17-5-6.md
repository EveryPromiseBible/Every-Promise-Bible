The first half of a pair, and everything in it is about where the roots are.

## Who is speaking

*This is what **the LORD** says.*

Jeremiah attributes it directly. The comparison that follows is not the prophet's
opinion.

## The three clauses

*Cursed is the one who **trusts in man**, who **leans on mere flesh for his strength**,
whose **heart turns away from the LORD**.*

Three, and they run in sequence: trusting, leaning, and then the turning.

The heart's departure is listed third. It is the consequence rather than the cause.

## "Mere flesh"

The Hebrew is **basar** — flesh, meat. The word for what a body is made of.

Isaiah uses it the same way: *the Egyptians are men, and not God; and their horses are
flesh, and not spirit.*

## The picture

*He is like **a bush in the desert wastes**.*

The Hebrew is **arar** — a juniper or dwarf shrub of the wilderness. It survives; it does
not thrive.

## What he cannot do

*He **does not see when good comes**.*

The Hebrew is *he shall not see when good comes*.

The good arrives and he misses it. Nothing in the sentence says the good is withheld.

## Where he lives

*In **the parched places of the wilderness**, in **a salt land where no one can live**.*

Salt land is the ground around the Dead Sea — white, crusted, and nothing grows on it.

Jeremiah picks the most sterile landscape in the country.

## What Jeremiah was watching

Judah in its last decades, negotiating with Egypt against Babylon and then with Babylon
against Egypt. The whole political programme of the period was leaning on flesh.

He had been told at his call: *they will fight against you, but they shall not prevail
against you.*

## The verse in between the two halves

Two verses on, after the blessed man: *the heart is deceitful above all things, and
desperately wicked; who can know it?*

Jeremiah puts the two trees on either side of that sentence.

## The other half

*But **blessed is the one who trusts in the LORD**… He is like **a tree planted by the
water**.* See the note on Jeremiah 17:7–8.

Two men, two root systems, one climate. The drought comes to both.

## Where the image comes from

Psalm 1, which opens the psalter with the same figure: *he shall be like a tree planted
by the rivers of water… the ungodly are not so, but are like the chaff which the wind
drives away.*

Jeremiah keeps the tree and changes the opposite from chaff to a shrub.

## What the chapter goes on to say

*I, the LORD, **search the heart**, I test the mind, even to give every man according to
his ways.*

And then, in Jeremiah's own voice: *Heal me, O LORD, and I shall be healed; save me, and
I shall be saved, **for You are my praise**.*

> **What the two halves share.** The heat and the drought. Jeremiah does not describe one
> man in good weather and one in bad — the difference in the passage is entirely where
> the roots went.
