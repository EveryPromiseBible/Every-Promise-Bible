Two verses, and the whole point is the absence of anybody higher.

## The problem

*When God made Abraham a promise, **there was no one greater for Him to swear by**.*

An oath works by staking something larger than yourself. A man swears by God because God
outranks him and will hold him to it.

For God there is no such party.

## What He did about it

*So **He swore by His own name**.*

The Hebrew of Genesis 22 is *by Myself I have sworn*.

The guarantee and the guarantor are the same, which sounds like a weakness and is the
opposite. If God is the highest thing there is, an oath sworn on Himself is backed by
everything that exists.

## The quotation

*"**Bless you I will; multiply you I will**."*

The Greek doubles both verbs — *blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will
multiply you*. It is the Hebrew intensive construction carried straight over.

The same doubling appears in Jabez's prayer: *bless me, really bless me*. See the note on
1 Chronicles 4:9–10.

## When it was sworn

Genesis 22, on the mountain, immediately after the knife was stopped. See the notes on
Genesis 22:11–12 and Genesis 22:15–18.

Not at the call in Genesis 12, and not at the covenant in Genesis 15. After the son was
laid on the wood.

## What the writer says about Abraham next

*And so, **after he had patiently endured, he obtained the promise**.*

The Greek is **makrothumesas** — long-tempered, slow to burn.

Twenty-five years between the promise of a son and Isaac's birth. And Abraham never saw
the descendants as the stars of the sky.

Hebrews 11 says so plainly: *these all died in faith, not having received the promises,
but having seen them afar off.*

## Why the writer brings it up here

Two verses earlier he told them to *imitate those who through faith and patience inherit
the promises*. See the note on Hebrews 6:9–10.

Abraham is the worked example. The connective is *for* — this is why he said it.

## Who the oath is for

The writer's argument does not stop at Abraham. Four verses on: *God, determining to show
more abundantly **to the heirs of promise** the immutability of His counsel, confirmed it
by an oath.* See the note on Hebrews 6:16–17.

## The other oath in the letter

*The LORD has sworn and will not relent: You are a priest forever according to the order
of Melchizedek.*

The writer makes the same move there — an oath over the priesthood that the Levitical
line never had. See the note on Hebrews 7:20–22.

Two oaths in the letter, one about a family and one about a priest.

> **What is unusual about it.** God does not need to swear. The writer's point is that
> He did it anyway, and the reason given is not doubt on His side.
