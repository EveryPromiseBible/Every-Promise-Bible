The first instruction in the letter, and it is about arithmetic.

## The verb

***Count it** nothing but joy.*

The Greek is **hegeomai** — to reckon, to consider, to lead the mind to a conclusion. It
is an accounting word.

James does not say *feel* joy. He says work it out and put it in that column.

## "Nothing but"

*Count it **nothing but joy**.*

The Greek is *all joy* — **pasan charan**. Not partly, not on balance.

## The landing

*When you **land in the middle of trouble**.*

The Greek is **peripipto** — to fall around, to fall among. It is the word Luke uses of
the man on the Jericho road who *fell among thieves*.

You do not walk into it deliberately. It happens on the way somewhere.

## "When"

Not *if*. James assumes it.

## Every kind

*Every kind of it there is.*

The Greek is **poikilos** — many-coloured, variegated, of every shade.

Peter uses the identical word two verses apart in his letter: *though now for a little
while you have been grieved by **various** trials*, and then *stewards of the
**manifold** grace of God.*

The same adjective for the trouble and for the grace.

## The word for trouble

**Peirasmos** — a trial, a test, an examination. It is also the word in *lead us not
into temptation*.

James uses it here about circumstances that come at a person from outside, and eleven
verses later he uses the related verb about being drawn toward evil — and says
flatly that God does not do that. See the notes on James 1:13 and James 1:14.

Same word family, two different things, deliberately separated inside one chapter.

## Why he can say it

Because of the next verse, which starts *you know why*. See the note on James 1:3.

The joy is not asked for in the abstract. It is the conclusion of an argument James is
about to make.

## Who is writing

*James, a bondservant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ* — the Lord's brother, who
did not believe in Him during His life, and who led the Jerusalem church through the
persecutions in Acts.

He is writing to *the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad* — refugees.

> **What is not being asked for.** Not that the trouble be enjoyed, and not that it be
> called good. James asks for a calculation, and gives the working in the next sentence.
