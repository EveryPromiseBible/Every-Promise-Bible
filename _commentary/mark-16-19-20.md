The last two verses of Mark, and they run in two directions at once.

## The textual question

The earliest manuscripts of Mark end at verse 8, with the women fleeing the tomb *for they were
afraid*.

Verses 9 to 20 are absent from Sinaiticus and Vaticanus, and the church has read them anyway for
most of its history. The question is old and is not settled here.

## The ascension

*He was **taken up into heaven**.*

The Greek is **anelemphthe** — was taken up. A passive.

Luke gives the fuller account in Acts: *while they watched, He was taken up, and a cloud
received Him out of their sight*. See the note on Acts 1:6–8.

## The seat

*And **He sat down at the right hand of God**.*

Psalm 110:1, quoted more than any other Old Testament verse in the New Testament. See the note
on Psalms 110:1.

The sitting is the point. Hebrews builds on it: *every priest **stands** ministering daily…
but this Man, after He had offered one sacrifice for sins forever, **sat down***. See the note
on Hebrews 10:11–13.

There were no chairs in the tabernacle.

## The going out

*And **they went out and told the news everywhere**.*

The Greek is **pantachou** — everywhere.

He sits and they go. The two clauses are next to each other, and the contrast is deliberate.

## The clause

*And **the Lord worked right alongside them**.*

The Greek is **sunergountos** — working together with. The root of *synergy*.

A genitive absolute: while the Lord was co-working.

He has just been described as seated at the right hand of God, and in the same sentence He is
working with them on the ground.

## The confirmation

*Confirming the message by **the signs that came with it**.*

The Greek is **bebaiountos** — a legal word. To ratify, to guarantee, to make binding.

The signs are described as corroboration of the message rather than as the message.

Hebrews says it the same way: *God also bearing witness both with signs and wonders, with
various miracles, and gifts of the Holy Spirit*. See the note on Hebrews 2:1–4.

## What the signs were

The verses before list them: casting out demons, new tongues, taking up serpents, drinking
anything deadly without harm, laying hands on the sick.

Two of them happen in Acts. Paul shakes a viper off his hand on Malta *and suffered no harm*,
and hands are laid on the sick throughout.

## The shape of the ending

Mark's Gospel is the shortest, the fastest, and the one that uses *immediately* forty times.

It ends with a man sitting down and a group of men going everywhere, and the last word in the
Greek is about the signs following.

## What the other three end with

Matthew: *I am with you always*. See the note on Matthew 28:16–20.

Luke: *and they were continually in the temple praising and blessing God*.

John: *and there are also many other things that Jesus did, which if they were written one by
one, I suppose that even the world itself could not contain the books*.

## The two positions

Seated, and working alongside.

Mark puts them in consecutive sentences and does not explain the arrangement.

> **The two clauses side by side.** He sat down, and He worked alongside them. Mark states both
> in one sentence, and the second is a present participle.
