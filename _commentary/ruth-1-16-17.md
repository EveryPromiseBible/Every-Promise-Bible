The most quoted words in the book, said by a foreigner to a bitter widow on a road.

## Where they are

On the road out of Moab, heading for Judah.

Naomi has lost her husband and both sons. She has told her two daughters-in-law to go home —
*return each to her mother's house*.

Orpah kissed her and went. Ruth clung to her.

## What Naomi had argued

*"Turn back, my daughters, go — for I am too old to have a husband… **would you wait for them till
they were grown?**"*

*"**It is more bitter for me than for you**, for the hand of the LORD has gone out against me."*

She is not being coy. Her case is sound: there is nothing in Judah for a Moabite widow.

## The refusal

*But Ruth answered, and **her words have never stopped echoing**.*

## The two negatives

*"**Don't ask me to leave you. Don't beg me to turn back**."*

The Hebrew is **al-tiphge'i vi** — do not press me, do not urge me.

She closes the argument before making her own.

## The five clauses

*"Wherever you go, I will go. Wherever you stay, I will stay. **Your people will be my people, and
your God will be my God**. Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried."*

Movement, dwelling, nation, God, and grave.

Each one is more permanent than the last. The list ends with the one that cannot be undone.

## What she is renouncing

Moab, her family, her gods — Chemosh, to whom children were burned — and any prospect of
remarriage in a country that had a law against her people entering the assembly. See the note on
Deuteronomy 23:3–6.

## The oath

*"**May the LORD deal with me, and worse still, if anything but death ever comes between us**."*

The Hebrew is **koh ya'aseh YHWH li vekoh yosiph** — thus may the LORD do to me, and thus may He
add.

The standard self-curse formula, and she swears it by **YHWH** — the covenant name of the God she
has just claimed.

A Moabitess taking an oath in the name of Israel's God, on a road, to a woman who has nothing.

## What Naomi says back

Nothing.

*When she saw that she was determined to go with her, **she stopped speaking to her**.*

## What Naomi says in Bethlehem

*"**Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara**, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me. I went
out full, and the LORD has brought me home again empty."*

**Naomi** — pleasant. **Mara** — bitter. The same root as the water at Marah. See the note on
Exodus 15:22–27.

## What the narrator adds

*So Naomi returned, and **Ruth the Moabitess her daughter-in-law with her**.*

She has just said she came home empty, and the sentence naming her emptiness names Ruth standing
next to her.

## The timing

*Now they came to Bethlehem **at the beginning of barley harvest**.*

The last clause of the chapter. Everything that follows happens because they arrived in the
right week.

## What Boaz says about her

*"**The LORD repay your work, and a full reward be given you by the LORD God of Israel, under whose
wings you have come for refuge**."* See the note on Ruth 2:11–12.

The Hebrew is **kenaphav** — His wings. And **lachasot** — to take shelter.

He uses the language of the psalms about a woman who has been in the country a few weeks.

## Where it ends

*And they called his name Obed. He is the father of Jesse, the father of David.*

> **The order of the five clauses.** Go, stay, people, God, grave. Each is harder to reverse than
> the one before, and the list ends with a burial plot.
