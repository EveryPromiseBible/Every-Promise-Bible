Moses recounts the years nobody talks about, and the summary at the end of it is
generous.

## The circling

**2:1** *"For **many days we wandered around the hill country of Seir**."*

The Hebrew is *we compassed mount Seir many days*.

This is the bulk of the forty years, and Deuteronomy gives it one sentence.

Numbers 33 lists the camps — about forty of them — and says almost nothing about any.

## The word

**2:3** *"**You have gone around this mountain long enough. Now turn north**."*

The Hebrew is *rav lakem* — enough for you, sufficient.

The circling was not indefinite. It ended with a sentence.

## Who they are about to pass

**2:4** *"You are about to pass through the territory of **your relatives, the descendants of
Esau**, who live in Seir."*

Jacob's brother's family, four centuries on. See the note on Genesis 27:38–40, where
Isaac tells Esau his dwelling will be away from the dew of heaven — and Seir is exactly
that country.

## The prohibition

**2:5** *"**Do not provoke them**, because **I will not give you any of their land — not even
enough to set your foot on**."*

The Hebrew is *not so much as a foot breadth*.

Israel is marching to take a country, and is told there is a border they may not cross.

## The reason

**2:5** again: *"**I have given Mount Seir to Esau as his possession**."*

Esau, who sold the birthright and was blessed second, has a land grant from God, and
Israel is not permitted to touch it.

Deuteronomy says the same about Moab and Ammon, Lot's descendants, in the same chapter.

## What they must do instead

**2:6** *"You may **buy food from them with money**… and **buy water from them**."*

They pay. An army of that size could have taken what it wanted.

## The summary

**2:7** *"For **the LORD your God has blessed you in everything your hands have done**."*

Said about the wandering years, and about a people who had been walking in circles as a
consequence of refusing to go in.

## The two clauses

**2:7** again: *"He has **watched over your journey through this great wilderness**."*

**2:7** again: *"**These forty years the LORD your God has been with you, and you have lacked
nothing**."*

The Hebrew is *you have not lacked a thing*.

Deuteronomy 8 adds the detail, **Deuteronomy 8:4**: *your garments did not wear out on you, nor did your foot
swell, these forty years.*

## What the people said about the same period

*"Why did you drag us up out of Egypt into this terrible place?"* See the note on
Numbers 20:2–5.

The same forty years, described by the people inside them and by Moses looking back, and
the two accounts do not resemble each other.

## Where Moses is standing

On the plains of Moab, in the eleventh month of the fortieth year, weeks from his own
death, addressing a generation who were children when it started.

## The verdict on years that were forfeited

**2:7** again: *"The LORD your God has **blessed you in everything your hands have done**… These forty years
the LORD your God has been with you, and **you have lacked nothing**."*

Read what that is being said about. Forty years of walking in circles, which happened because
the nation refused to go in. It is the consequence of unbelief, and Moses' summary of it is
*blessed in everything* and *you lacked nothing*.

Paul, **Romans 5:20**: *where sin abounded, **grace abounded much more**.*

## What ended it

**2:3** again: *"You have gone around this mountain long enough. **Now turn north**."*

Not a rebuke, and no conditions attached. A sentence, and the direction changes.

**Matthew 11:28**: *Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest*, Jesus said —
and the offer has no conditions in it either.

> **The verdict on the wasted years.** Blessed in everything, watched over, and lacking
> nothing. Moses says it about the period the nation spent going in circles.
