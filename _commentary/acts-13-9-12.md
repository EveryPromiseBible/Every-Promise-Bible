The first miracle of Paul's ministry is a judgment, and it happens in front of a Roman
governor.

## The change of name

*Then **Saul, who is also called Paul**.*

Luke has used *Saul* since chapter 7. From this verse on it is Paul, for the rest of the
book.

Both were his from birth — a Hebrew name and a Roman one, which was ordinary for a Jew
with citizenship. Luke switches at the point the mission turns toward the Gentile world.

And from here the pair is *Paul and Barnabas* rather than *Barnabas and Saul*.

## Who is opposing him

**Elymas** the sorcerer, also called **Bar-Jesus** — son of Joshua. Luke calls him *a
Jewish false prophet*.

He is attached to the household of Sergius Paulus, the proconsul, who had sent for
Barnabas and Saul because he *sought to hear the word of God*.

Elymas *withstood them, seeking to turn the proconsul away from the faith*.

## The look

*Filled with the Holy Spirit, **looked straight at him**.*

The Greek is **atenisas** — to gaze intently, to fix the eyes. Luke uses it repeatedly:
Stephen looking into heaven, the disciples watching the ascension, the crowd staring at
Peter and John.

## The charge

*"You are **full of every kind of deceit and every kind of trickery**. You are **a son of
the devil**, an **enemy of everything that is right**."*

The Greek for *son of the devil* is **huie diabolou** — set against his own name,
Bar-Jesus, son of Joshua.

## The question

*"Will you never stop **twisting the straight paths of the Lord**?"*

The Greek is *making crooked the straight ways*.

The image is from Isaiah, quoted of John the Baptist: *make His paths straight*.

## The sentence

*"Look now — **the hand of the Lord is on you. You will be blind, unable even to see the
sun, for a time**."*

**For a time** — the Greek is **achri kairou**, until a season. The blindness is given an
end.

Paul had been struck blind himself on the Damascus road, and had spent three days without
sight before Ananias came. See the note on Acts 9:3–6.

He pronounces on another man the thing that had happened to him, and includes the time
limit.

## What followed

*Immediately **a mist and a darkness came over him**, and he **went groping about, looking
for someone to lead him by the hand**.*

The Greek for *mist* is **achlus**, a medical term for a clouding of the eye. Luke the
physician uses it once.

*Looking for someone to lead him by the hand* — the Greek is **cheiragogous**, hand-leaders.
It is the same word used of Paul being led into Damascus.

## The result

*When the proconsul saw what had happened, **he believed, astonished at the teaching of the
Lord**.*

Luke says he was astonished at the **teaching**, not at the blindness.

Sergius Paulus is described as *an intelligent man*, and an inscription naming a Sergius
Paulus has been found on Cyprus.

## Where they go next

Straight to the mainland, where John Mark leaves them and Paul preaches in a synagogue in
Antioch of Pisidia. See the note on Acts 13:13–15.

> **What is on the page.** A false prophet with a Jewish name blocking a Roman's hearing,
> and the first thing recorded of Paul under that name is a temporary blindness — the same
> one he was given himself.
