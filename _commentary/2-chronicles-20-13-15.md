The answer arrives while everybody is still standing there.

## Who was present

*All Judah stood there before the LORD, with **their little ones, their wives, and their
children**.*

The chronicler names them.

An invading army is coming and the nation has brought its infants to a prayer meeting.

## Where the word comes from

*Then, **in the middle of the assembly**, the Spirit of the LORD came on **Jahaziel son of
Zechariah — a Levite of the sons of Asaph**.*

Not the king, not a priest, and not a prophet by office.

**The sons of Asaph** were the temple musicians — David's appointed singers. Twelve psalms
carry Asaph's name.

The man who speaks is from the choir.

His genealogy runs four generations deep in the verse, which is the chronicler's way of
saying he was nobody in particular.

## The address

*"Listen, **all Judah, and you people of Jerusalem, and you, King Jehoshaphat**."*

The king is named last.

## The instruction

*"**Do not be afraid or dismayed** because of this great multitude."*

The Hebrew pair — *fear not, neither be dismayed* — is the same one Moses gave Joshua, and
Joshua gave his commanders with their feet on five kings' necks. See the note on
Joshua 10:22–27.

## The reason

*"For **the battle is not yours, but God's**."*

The Hebrew is *the battle is not yours but God's*.

It answers the prayer's exact words. Jehoshaphat had said *we have no power against this
great multitude*; the reply concedes the point and reassigns the fight.

## What they are told to do

The verses after: *"Tomorrow go down against them… **You will not need to fight in this
battle. Position yourselves, stand still and see the salvation of the LORD** with you."*

Four instructions and three of them are about not moving.

## What Jehoshaphat did

*And Jehoshaphat **bowed his head with his face to the ground**, and all Judah and the
inhabitants of Jerusalem **bowed before the LORD, worshipping**.*

Then the Levites stood up *to praise the LORD God of Israel with voices loud and high*.

## The morning

*"**Believe in the LORD your God, and you shall be established; believe His prophets, and
you shall prosper**."* See the note on 2 Chronicles 20:16–19.

And then the singers were appointed *to go out before the army*, singing *praise the LORD,
for His mercy endures forever*.

## What happened

*Now when they began to sing and to praise, **the LORD set ambushes** against the people of
Ammon, Moab, and Mount Seir… and they were defeated.*

The three invading nations turned on each other. Judah arrived at the lookout and found
*dead bodies fallen on the earth. No one had escaped.*

## What they collected

*They found among them **an abundance of valuables on the dead bodies, and precious
jewellery**, which they stripped off for themselves, **more than they could carry away**.
And they were **three days gathering the spoil**.*

Three days to pick up what fell in a battle they did not fight.

## The name of the valley

*On the fourth day they assembled in **the Valley of Berachah**, for there they blessed the
LORD.*

**Berachah** — blessing.

## The sentence

*"**Do not be afraid nor dismayed because of this great multitude, for the battle is not yours,
but God's.**"*

Not *God will help you fight*. The ownership of the battle is transferred.

*"**The LORD will fight for you, and you shall hold your peace.**"* Said at the Red Sea, to
people with water in front and an army behind. See the note on Exodus 14:10–14.

## Where the word lands

*Then **the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jahaziel**… a Levite of the sons of Asaph, **in the
midst of the assembly**.*

Not on the king, and not on a prophet with a title. On a temple musician standing in the
crowd, named with four generations of ancestry so that nobody could say he was invented.

## The instruction given

*"**You will not need to fight in this battle. Position yourselves, stand still, and see the
salvation of the LORD**, who is with you."*

The Hebrew for *salvation* is **yeshuah** — the noun behind the name given to Mary's son. See
the note on Exodus 14:10–14.

*"You shall call His name **JESUS**, for He will save His people from their sins."*

Stand still, and watch the salvation of the LORD. It is said twice in Scripture at the moment
an army is in view, and both times the word is His name.

## What is finished before it starts

*"**Tomorrow go down against them.**"*

They are sent out to a battle that has already been decided, to occupy ground rather than to
take it.

*"**It is finished!**" And bowing His head, He gave up His spirit.* See the note on
John 19:28–30.

> **Who the word came through.** A man from the temple choir, in the middle of the crowd,
> with his genealogy given so nobody mistakes him for somebody important — and what he
> says is that the fight belongs to somebody else.
