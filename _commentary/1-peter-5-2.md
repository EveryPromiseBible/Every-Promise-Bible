Peter's instruction to elders, and every clause has a *not this, but that* in it.

## Who is speaking

*The elders who are among you I exhort, **I who am a fellow elder** and a witness of the
sufferings of Christ.*

The Greek is **sumpresbuteros** — co-elder. He puts himself alongside them, not above.

The man traditionally regarded as first among the apostles introduces himself sideways.

## The command

*Look after **God's sheep**.*

The Greek is **poimanate to poimnion tou theou** — shepherd the flock of God.

Whose flock is stated first. The verb is the one Jesus used on the beach: *tend My sheep*.
See the note on John 21:15–17.

Peter is passing on an instruction he received personally, after failing.

## The qualifier

*The ones **right there with you**.*

The Greek is **to en humin** — the flock among you.

Not sheep in general. The ones physically present.

## The first pair

*Watch over them **because you want to, not because somebody is forcing your hand**.*

The Greek is **me anagkastos alla hekousios** — not by compulsion but willingly.

## The second pair

*Not because there is **money in it you'd be ashamed of**, but because **you are eager to**.*

The Greek is **aischrokerdos** — for shameful gain. And **prothumos**, eagerly, with
forwardness of mind.

The same disqualification Jethro named for judges: *men who hate dishonest gain*. See the note
on Exodus 18:19–23.

And Paul's list for elders: *not greedy for money*.

## The third pair

The next verse: *nor as being lords over those entrusted to you, but **being examples to the
flock**.*

The Greek is **katakurieuontes** — lording it over. The word Jesus used of the Gentile rulers:
*you know that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them… yet it shall not be so among
you*.

## What sheep need

Not driving. Eastern shepherding worked by leading — the shepherd walked in front and the
flock followed his voice.

*He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out… and the sheep follow him, for they know
his voice.* See the note on John 10:1–6.

## The negative Peter keeps in view

Ezekiel's indictment: *woe to the shepherds of Israel who only feed themselves. Should not
shepherds feed the flock?… You have not strengthened the weak or healed the sick.* See the
note on Ezekiel 34:1–6.

Peter's three pairs are the positive form of Ezekiel's list.

## The reward

*And when the Chief Shepherd appears, you will receive **the crown of glory that does not fade
away**.* See the note on 1 Peter 5:4.

## What follows

*Likewise you younger people, submit yourselves to your elders. Yes, all of you be submissive
to one another, and **be clothed with humility**.*

The Greek for *clothed* is **egkombosasthai** — to tie on a garment with knots, specifically
the apron a slave tied over his clothes.

Peter had watched somebody do that with a towel. See the note on John 13:4–5.

## The line after it

*"**God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble**."*

*Therefore **humble yourselves under the mighty hand of God**, that He may exalt you in due
time, **casting all your care upon Him, for He cares for you**.*

The Greek is **epiripsantes** — having thrown it on Him. A participle attached to the humbling:
the way you humble yourself is by throwing the worry over.

> **How the instruction is framed.** Three times, *not this, but that*. Not forced, not for
> money, not lording it — and Peter signs himself as a fellow elder.
