Paul thanks God for how a message was taken, and then the paragraph turns very dark.

## The thanksgiving

*And this is why **we never stop thanking God**.*

The Greek is **adialeiptos** — unceasingly. The same adverb he uses of prayer three chapters later:
*pray without ceasing*. See the note on 1 Thessalonians 5:16–18.

## What they did with it

*When you received the message you heard from us, **you did not take it in as something men had
thought up**, but for what it truly is, **the word of God**.*

The Greek uses two different verbs.

**Paralabontes** — received, in the sense of taking delivery of a tradition.

**Edexasthe** — welcomed, accepted. The word for receiving a guest.

They took delivery, and they welcomed it.

## The distinction

**Logon anthropon** — a word of men. **Logon Theou** — a word of God.

Paul is not claiming the message was well-delivered. He is claiming what it was, and thanking God
that they saw it.

## The result

*And **it is at work in you who believe**.*

The Greek is **energeitai** — is working, is operative. Present tense.

**Energeo** — the root of *energy*. Paul uses it of God's working.

The word is described as something doing something, not something believed about.

## The comparison

*For you, dear friends, **became like the churches of God in Judea**.*

The Greek is **mimetai egenethete** — you became imitators.

## What the resemblance is

*You **suffered the same things at the hands of your own countrymen** that they suffered.*

Not doctrine or practice. Persecution, and from their own neighbours.

Acts records it: a mob, a riot, and Paul and Silas sent away by night.

## The hard verses

*From the Jews, **who killed the Lord Jesus and their own prophets**, and drove us out, and do not
please God, and set themselves against everyone.*

## How to read it

Paul is a Jew, writing about the specific authorities who opposed him, in a letter to a
congregation being attacked by its own townspeople.

*I say then, has God cast away His people? **Certainly not! For I also am an Israelite**.*

*My heart's desire and prayer to God for Israel is that they may be saved.* See the note on
Romans 10:1–4.

The same author, and the same subject, at length.

## The charge

*Hindering us from speaking to the Gentiles **so that they might be saved**.*

The Greek is **koluonton** — preventing.

The specific accusation is obstruction of the Gentile mission. That is what the paragraph is
about.

## The last line

*So they are always **filling up the measure of their sins**; and the wrath has come upon them **to the
very limit**.*

The Greek is **eis telos** — to the end, or completely.

The phrase is ambiguous in Greek and has been argued over since the second century.

## Where Paul's own argument goes

*For **I do not desire, brethren, that you should be ignorant of this mystery**… that blindness in
part has happened to Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in. **And so all Israel will
be saved**.* See the note on Romans 11:25–27.

*For **the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable**.*

## The verse the sermon rests on

*You did not take it in as something men had thought up, but **for what it truly is, the word of
God**.*

Paul's own letters are named that way, in one of his own letters.

Peter says the same about them: *as also in all his epistles… **which untaught and unstable people
twist to their own destruction, as they do also the rest of the Scriptures***.

> **The two verbs.** **Paralabontes** — took delivery. **Edexasthe** — welcomed, as a guest. And
> the result is stated in the present tense: it *is working*.
