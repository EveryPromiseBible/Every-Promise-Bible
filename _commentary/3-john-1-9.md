title: "Who loves being first"

The one sentence in the letter about the problem, and the diagnosis is a single phrase.

## The charge

*"I wrote to the church. But **Diotrephes, who loves being first among them, will have nothing to do
with us**."*

The Greek is **ho philoproteuon auton** — the one loving to be first among them. The word occurs
once in the New Testament and **John** appears to have coined it.

It is not *the false teacher*, *the heretic*, or *the immoral man*. The whole account of what is
wrong with him is a compound adjective about seating.

## What he does

*"Spreading vicious nonsense about us… he will not welcome the brothers himself, and he stops the
people who want to, and **throws them out of the church**."*

Four actions, and three of them are about hospitality — the exact thing Gaius is commended for two
verses earlier. See the note on 3 John 1:5–6.

The letter is a study in contrast, and the axis is who gets let in.

## Where the disciples had the same argument

*Now there was also a dispute among them, as to **which of them should be considered the greatest**.*

**Luke** 22, at the Last Supper, minutes after the bread and the cup.

*"**But he who is greatest among you, let him be your servant.** And whoever exalts himself will be
humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted."*

*"You know that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them… **Yet it shall not be so among you**."*

## Who is first

*And **He is the head of the body, the church**, who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead,
that **in all things He may have the preeminence**.*

**Colossians** 1, and the Greek is **proteuon** — the participle inside **John**'s coinage. It is
used of **Christ** once in the New Testament, and **Diotrephes** is described with the same root and
a *love of* attached to the front.

The office is taken. That is the whole answer to the man.

## And the one who took the lowest place

*Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into His hands, and that He had come from God
and was going to God, **rose from supper and laid aside His garments, took a towel and girded
Himself**.*

**John** 13, and the sentence puts the credentials and the towel in the same breath: knowing that He
held everything, He washed their feet.

*Who, being in the form of God… **made Himself of no reputation**, taking the form of a bondservant.*

## What John proposes to do

*"**So if I come, I am going to bring up what he has been doing.**"*

**Hypomneso** — I will call attention to. No excommunication, no counter-faction, and no letter to
the congregation over his head. He says he will raise it in person.

*There is a great deal more I could say to you, and **I am not going to do it with a pen**. I expect
to see you before long, and then we can talk it all out.*

Both of **John**'s short letters end with the same refusal to handle a difficulty in writing.

> **The diagnosis.** One compound word, apparently invented for him: **philoproteuon**, loving to be
> first. And the participle inside it is used once elsewhere in the New Testament — of Christ, that
> *in all things He may have the preeminence*.
