He prays once in the whole book, and it is at the end.

## Where he is

Blind, in Gaza, grinding grain in a prison — the work of an animal or a woman.

The Philistines had gouged his eyes out, bound him with bronze, and brought him into the temple
of Dagon to entertain three thousand people on the roof.

*Call for Samson, that he may perform for us.* See the note on Judges 16:21–25.

## The detail the narrator drops in

*However, **the hair of his head began to grow again after it had been shaved**.*

Said quietly, before the feast, and then left alone.

## The boy

*Then Samson said to **the lad who held him by the hand**, "Let me feel the pillars."*

A child is leading the strongest man in Israel around by the wrist.

## The prayer

*Then Samson **called out to the LORD**: "**O Lord GOD, remember me**."*

The Hebrew is **zochreni na** — remember me, please.

He has spoken to God once before, after the jawbone, complaining of thirst. This is the only
petition in his story.

He uses three divine names in one sentence: **Adonai YHWH… Elohim**.

## What he asks for

*"**Please, God, give me strength just once more**."*

The Hebrew is **chazzeqeni na ak happa'am hazzeh** — strengthen me only this once.

He does not ask for his eyes, his freedom, or his life.

## The reason he gives

*"**That I may pay back the Philistines for my two eyes**."*

The Hebrew is **naqam achat mishshtei einai** — one vengeance for my two eyes.

Personal. He does not say *for Israel*, or *for Your name*.

The narrative records the motive as he stated it.

## The pillars

*And he reached for **the two central pillars on which the temple stood**, bracing himself against
them — **one hand on each**.*

Philistine temples excavated at Tell Qasile have exactly this construction: a roof supported by
two central wooden pillars on stone bases, close enough for a man to reach both.

## The sentence

*"**Let me die with the Philistines**," he said.*

The Hebrew is **tamot naphshi im-pelishtim**.

## The push

*Then **he pushed with all his might**, and the temple came down upon the rulers and all the people
inside.*

The five lords of the Philistines were there — the whole leadership in one building.

## The count

*And so **those he brought down at his death were more than all he had killed in his life**.*

The narrator does the arithmetic and states it flatly, as a fact about the ledger.

## What his life had been

A Nazirite from the womb, whose vow forbade wine, corpses and razors, and who broke every part of
it.

He killed a lion and later ate honey from its carcass. He married a Philistine. He told Delilah
the secret on the fourth asking, after she had used the first three answers to try to hand him
over.

*But he did not know that the LORD had departed from him.*

## Where he ends up

*Then his brothers and all his father's household came down and took him, and brought him up and
buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of his father Manoah.*

The same ground he was born on. *And he had judged Israel twenty years.*

## What Hebrews does with him

*And what more shall I say? For the time would fail me to tell of Gideon and Barak and **Samson**
and Jephthah.* See the note on Hebrews 11:32–34.

Named in the list, with no commentary.

> **The count.** More at his death than in his life — stated by the narrator as arithmetic, over
> a man who asked for strength once and gave a personal reason for it.
