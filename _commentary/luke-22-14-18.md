The first thing He says at the table, and it is about desire.

## The hour

*"**When the hour came, He took His place at the table.**"*

**Luke** has used *the hour* throughout the gospel for the appointed moment. It arrives here.

*"**Father, the hour has come. Glorify Your Son.**"*

## The sentence

*"**I have longed to eat this Passover with you before I suffer.**"*

The Greek doubles the noun and the verb — **epithymia epethymesa** — a Hebrew intensive: *with desire
I have desired*.

It is the strongest way the language has of saying He wanted this.

The Passover He has been waiting for is the one at which He is the lamb, and the desire is stated
before a word about the bread.

*Who for **the joy that was set before Him** endured the cross, despising the shame.*

*"**Therefore My Father loves Me, because I lay down My life** that I may take it again."*

## Before I suffer

**Pro tou me pathein.**

**Luke** puts the suffering in the same clause as the longing. He is not looking past the cross to the
resurrection; the meal He wanted is the one immediately before it.

## The vow

*"**I tell you, I will not eat it again until all of it comes true in the kingdom of God.**"*

And over the cup: *I will not drink of the fruit of the vine until the kingdom of God comes.*

Twice, He removes Himself from the meal He has just said He longed for. He hands it round and does
not take the next one.

*"**Blessed are those who are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb!**"*

*"Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, **I will come
in to him and dine with him, and he with Me**."*

## What the Passover was

A meal eaten standing, in haste, on the night a lamb died in each house so that the family inside
would live.

*For indeed **Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us**. Therefore let us keep the feast.*

He is sitting at the fourteenth-century commemoration of a substitution, on the night He becomes
one.

## Who is at the table

*"**And the twelve apostles with Him.**"*

All twelve. **Luke** puts the betrayer's presence a few verses later — *the hand of My betrayer is
with Me on the table* — after the bread and the cup, not before.

> **The doubled verb.** *With desire I have desired.* It is the strongest thing the Greek can say
> about wanting something, and what He says He wanted is the last meal before He suffered.
