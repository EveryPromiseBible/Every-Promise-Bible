The last picture in the book, and the writer takes his time over the details.

## Checked against chapter 1

- 7,000 sheep → **14,000**
- 3,000 camels → **6,000**
- 500 yoke of oxen → **1,000**
- 500 donkeys → **1,000**

Every figure exactly doubled. The numbers in chapter 1 were given so this could be
counted.

*And the LORD blessed **the last part of Job's life even more than the first**.*

## The daughters

*He named the first daughter **Jemimah**, the second **Keziah**, and the third
**Keren-happuch**.*

The sons are counted; the daughters are named. In the whole of Job the only people
given personal names besides Job, his friends and Elihu are these three girls.

The names are all about beauty and scent — **Jemimah** is dove, **Keziah** is
cassia, the fragrant bark, and **Keren-happuch** is *horn of eye-paint*, the flask
of dark cosmetic a woman kept.

The man came out of the ashes and named his daughters after perfume and a dove.

## "No women as lovely as Job's daughters"

*In all the land there were **no women as lovely** as Job's daughters.*

The writer states it as fact, in the same breath as the livestock count. It is
part of the blessing being listed.

Beauty is something God gives, and it is written into the ledger here alongside
the sheep.

## The inheritance

*And their father gave them **an inheritance among their brothers, right alongside
the sons**.*

That is not how it was done. Daughters inherited only where there were no sons —
the case of Zelophehad's daughters in Numbers 27 had to be brought to Moses
specially.

Job had seven sons. He gave the girls a share anyway, as equals.

A man who has lost everything and been given it back twice over is not careful
about the rules for dividing it.

## The last verse

*So Job died, old and full of days.*

The Hebrew is *satisfied with days*. He had enough.

He saw four generations — *his children and their children to the fourth
generation* — which is one hundred and forty more years after the ash heap.

## What is still missing

He is never told why. Not in these verses, not anywhere.

He gets his life back doubled, four generations of grandchildren, three beautiful
daughters with an inheritance, and no explanation at all.

> **What James says the end of it proves.** *You have heard how Job held out, and
> you have seen what the Lord finally made of it — that He feels for people deeply,
> and He is kind all the way through.* The New Testament's verdict on this
> chapter is not about endurance. It is about what God turned out to be. See the
> note on James 5:10–11.
