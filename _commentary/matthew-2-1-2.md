Foreigners arrive in Jerusalem asking a question that nobody in Jerusalem had
thought to ask.

## Who they were

**Magi** — the word covers astrologers, court advisers and priests of the eastern
kingdoms. Not kings, and Matthew never says how many; the three comes from the
number of gifts.

They were Gentiles, from outside the covenant, practising a discipline Israel's
own law forbade. And they are the first people in Matthew's Gospel to worship
Jesus.

## Their question

*Where is the child who has been born King of the Jews?*

They ask it in the capital, in the reign of a king who was famously paranoid
about his throne — a man who had already killed his wife and three of his own
sons.

They walk into Herod's city and ask where the real king is.

## Where they went wrong

They went to a palace. It is the obvious place to look for a royal birth, and it
is the wrong one — a detour that puts Herod on the scent and leads to the
soldiers being sent to Bethlehem.

The star had brought them a long way. It did not take them to Jerusalem; their
own reasonable assumption did.

## What was in Jerusalem

The chief priests and scribes answer Herod's question immediately: Bethlehem, and
they quote Micah.

They knew the address. Five miles down the road, and there is no record of any of
them going to look.

> **What the two groups had.** The scholars had the Scripture and stayed home.
> The foreigners had a light in the sky, an incomplete map, and enough interest to
> travel for months. Matthew reports both without comment, in the second chapter
> of a Gospel written to Jewish readers.
