The scene shifts to a place Job never sees and never learns about, and the whole
book turns on it.

## He got in

*The sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and **the adversary
came among them too, the accuser**.*

The Hebrew is **ha-satan**, with the article — *the* adversary, *the* accuser. It
is a job description before it is a name.

And the thing to see is that he is standing there. He has access. He walks into
the throne room and is asked a question and answers it.

## What he was doing

*"From **wandering the earth, roaming back and forth** across it."*

Peter uses the same picture: *your adversary the devil walks about like a roaring
lion, seeking whom he may devour.*

## Who brings up Job

*Then the LORD said to him, "**Have you noticed My servant Job?**"*

The accuser did not name him. God did.

And what God says about him is the same four things the writer said in verse 1 —
*blameless and upright, a man who walks with Me and turns away from evil* — with
one addition: *there is no one on earth like him.*

That is God's own verdict on Job, spoken out loud, before any of the trouble
starts.

## What Job never knew

He is not told about this conversation. Not in chapter 1, not in chapter 42, not
anywhere in the book.

Forty-two chapters of arguing about why it happened, and the reader has the answer
from page one while Job never gets it.

## What has changed since

The one thing Job did not have is the one thing this scene depends on: nobody was
standing between him and that accuser.

*If anyone sins, we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous.*
*He always lives to make intercession for them.* And in Revelation the accuser of
the brethren is *cast down* — the one who *accused them before our God day and
night.*

Job had no mediator in that room. He says so himself in chapter 9: *there is no
umpire between us, who might lay his hand on us both.*

That is the whole difference between his position and ours, and it is not a small
one.

> **What the accuser wanted.** *Does Job fear God for nothing?* His entire case is
> that the man is bought. Take the hedge down, he says, and he will curse You to
> Your face. The wager was never really about Job's suffering. It was about whether
> anyone loves God for who He is.
