Six building materials, one fire, and the builder walks out either way.

## The credit

*By **the grace of God given to me**, like a wise master builder I laid a foundation.*

The Greek is **sophos architektōn** — the word behind *architect*. The chief craftsman on a site.

He names his own competence and attributes it in the same clause.

## The handover

*And **someone else is building on it**.*

Apollos came after him. The chapter opens with the Corinthians dividing into parties over which
name they belonged to.

*I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the increase.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 3:5–9.

## The warning

*But let each one **be careful how he builds**.*

The Greek is **blepetō pōs** — let him watch how.

Not what, or how much. How.

## The fixed point

*For **no one can lay any foundation other than the one already laid, which is Jesus Christ**.*

The Greek is **themelion gar allon oudeis dunatai theinai**.

Not *should not*. **Ou dunatai** — cannot. The foundation is not on offer as a choice.

## The materials

*Gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay or dry straw.*

Six, in two sets of three. Descending in value, and — more to the point — descending in what they
will survive.

## What separates them

Not cost. Combustibility.

Gold, silver and stone go through fire and come out. Wood, hay and straw do not.

## The test

*The work of each will be **made plain**, for **the day will bring it out into the open**, because it is
**revealed by fire**.*

The Greek is **en puri apokaluptetai** — in fire it is revealed.

## What the fire does

*And **the fire itself will test what sort of work each has done**.*

The Greek is **hopoion estin** — of what sort.

Not how much. The quality, not the quantity.

## The first outcome

*If **what a man has built endures**, he will receive a reward.*

The Greek is **misthon lēmpsetai** — he will receive wages.

## The second

*If a man's work is burned up, **he will suffer the loss of it**.*

The Greek is **zēmiōthēsetai** — he will be fined, he will forfeit.

## What is not at stake

*He himself **will be saved**, yet only as one who **has come through the flames**.*

The Greek is **hōs dia puros** — as through fire.

The man is saved. The work is not. Paul separates the two and keeps them separate.

## The picture

A builder standing outside a burning house, alive, watching a year's work go.

Nothing in the passage suggests his standing was ever in question.

## What the foundation is not

*For we are God's fellow workers; **you are God's field, you are God's building**.*

The people are the building. The materials being described are what is put into them.

## What follows immediately

*Do you not know that **you are the temple of God** and that the Spirit of God dwells in you?* See the
note on 1 Corinthians 3:16–17.

The Greek is **naos** — the inner sanctuary, not the temple precincts.

## The letter's own use of gold

Solomon's temple was overlaid with gold and built of stone.

The materials that survive are the ones a sanctuary is made of.

## The conclusion of the chapter

*Therefore **let no one boast in men**. For all things are yours… **and you are Christ's, and Christ is
God's**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 3:21–23.

The argument that started with party slogans ends by giving them everything they were fighting over.

> **The two outcomes.** The work can burn to nothing and the builder still walks out. Paul puts the
> man and his work on separate accounts, and does not let them touch.
