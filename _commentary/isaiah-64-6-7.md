Two verses of the bleakest self-assessment in the Old Testament, and they are a prayer.

## Who is speaking

Not God. This is Israel, praying — the chapter is one long petition that begins *oh, that You
would rend the heavens! That You would come down!*

The sentence everyone quotes is something the people say about themselves.

## The uncleanness

*All of us have become like one who is **unclean**.*

The Hebrew is **tame** — the ritual word. The condition that barred a person from the
sanctuary and made whatever they touched unclean too. See the note on
Leviticus 11:29–38.

## The famous clause

*And **all our righteous acts are like filthy rags**.*

The Hebrew is **tsidqotenu** — our righteousnesses, plural. Not our sins.

And **beged iddim** — a garment of a menstruous woman. The Hebrew is more specific and more
shocking than *filthy rags*, and it belongs to the same purity system: something that made a
person unclean for a period.

The good works are what is being described, not the bad ones.

## The withering

*We all **wither like a leaf**, and **like the wind our sins sweep us away**.*

Two images in one line: the leaf that dries and the wind that takes it.

The same picture as Psalm 1, in reverse. There the righteous man's leaf does not wither. See
the note on Psalms 1:1–3.

## The silence

*No one calls on Your name or **rouses himself to take hold of You**.*

The Hebrew is **mitorer** — stirs himself up, wakes himself. And **lehachaziq**, to grasp,
seize, hold fast.

Nobody is even trying.

## The reason given

*For **You have hidden Your face from us** and left us to waste away in the grip of our
wrongs.*

The people say the failure to pray is a consequence of the hiding, not the cause of it.

Isaiah does not adjudicate. It is a prayer, and it says what people in that condition say.

## What the chapter asks for

*Oh, that You would rend the heavens! That You would come down!*

The Hebrew for *rend* is **qara** — tear.

Mark answers it with one verb: *the heavens **split wide open***, at the Jordan. See the note
on Mark 1:9–11.

The same word Mark uses again for the temple veil.

## What the chapter says next

*But now, O LORD, **You are our Father; we are the clay, and You our potter**, and all we are
the work of Your hand.*

The prayer moves from filthy rags to a potter's wheel in three verses.

*Do not be furious, O LORD, nor remember iniquity forever. Indeed, please look — **we all are
Your people**.*

## What God answers

The next chapter: *"I was sought by those who did not ask for Me; I was found by those who did
not seek Me. I said, 'Here I am, here I am,' to a nation that was not called by My name."*

Paul quotes it in Romans, applying it to the Gentiles.

The prayer says *no one calls on Your name*. The answer says *I was found by those who did not
seek Me*.

## The other righteousness

*And **be found in Him, not having my own righteousness**, which is from the law, but that
which is through faith in Christ.* See the note on Philippians 3:9.

Paul had more religious credentials than anyone he knew, and used the word **skubala** —
refuse — of the lot.

## Where the book ends

*"For as the new heavens and the new earth which I will make shall remain before Me," says the
LORD, "so shall your descendants and your name remain."* See the note on Isaiah 66:7–9.

> **What is being called filthy.** Not the sins. *Our righteousnesses* — and the Hebrew names
> a specific kind of ritually unclean cloth.
