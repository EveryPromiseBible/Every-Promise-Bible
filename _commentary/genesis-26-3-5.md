The only chapter in Genesis where God speaks to Isaac at length, and what He says
is almost entirely a repetition.

## What is promised

*Stay in this land, and I will be with you and bless you… I will make your
descendants as many as the stars in the sky, and I will give them all these
lands, and through your offspring all nations on earth will be blessed.*

Every item was said to Abraham first. Isaac receives no new terms — he receives
his father's covenant restated in his own hearing, with his own name on it.

## The reason given

*Because Abraham obeyed me and kept my charge, my commands, my decrees, and my
instructions.*

This is the striking part. The reason offered to Isaac is his father's obedience,
not his own.

Isaac is at this point a middle-aged man whose chief recorded acts are being
nearly sacrificed, receiving a wife arranged for him, and — in the very next
paragraph — lying about that wife exactly as his father had twice done.

He inherits a promise anchored in somebody else's faithfulness. That is the
shape of it.

## The instruction

*Stay in this land.*

One command, and it runs against the obvious response to famine. Abraham had gone
down to Egypt in a famine and it went badly. Isaac is told to stay in the place
of scarcity, with a promise attached.

The hundredfold harvest a few verses later happens in the land he was told not to
leave.

> **Where the New Testament puts the reader.** *If you belong to Christ, then you
> are Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise.* Paul makes the same
> move Genesis does here — an inheritance received on the strength of somebody
> else's faithfulness. See the note on Galatians 3:26–29.
