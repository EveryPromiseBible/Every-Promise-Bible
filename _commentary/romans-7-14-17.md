Paul describes a man losing, and switches to the present tense to do it.

## The concession

*We know that **the law is spiritual**.*

The Greek is **pneumatikos** — belonging to the Spirit.

He has just said *the law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good*. Nothing in the
chapter is an attack on the law.

## The contrast

*I am the one who is not — **sold off, and under sin as its slave**.*

The Greek is **sarkinos, pepramenos hupo ten hamartian** — fleshly, having been sold under sin.

**Piprasko** — to sell, as a slave at auction.

## The tense

Present, from verse 14 to the end of the chapter.

The first half of the chapter is past tense — *when we were in the flesh*. Then it changes, and
does not change back.

Readers have argued for centuries about who is speaking: Paul before conversion, Paul now, or any
person trying to keep the law by effort.

## The puzzle

*My own actions are **a puzzle to me**.*

The Greek is **ou ginosko** — I do not understand, I do not acknowledge.

## The description

*The thing I mean to do, **I do not do**; the thing I hate **is the very thing I keep finding in my
hands**.*

The Greek is **ou gar ho thelo touto prasso** — for what I will, this I do not practise.

Wanting is not the problem. He wants the right thing throughout the passage.

## What that establishes

The will is intact and the outcome is wrong.

Whatever the failure is, it is not a failure to want.

## The odd conclusion

*And if I do the thing I do not want to do, **then I am agreeing with the law that the law is
good**.*

His resistance to his own conduct is evidence that he shares the law's verdict on it.

The wretchedness is itself a testimony.

## The location

*So **it is no longer I myself who do it, but sin living in me**.*

The Greek is **he oikousa en emoi hamartia** — the sin dwelling in me.

**Oikeo** — to inhabit, to keep house.

## What that is not

An excuse.

Paul says the same thing twice in the chapter, and both times immediately after taking
responsibility. It is a diagnosis of location, not a transfer of blame.

## The famous cry

*O wretched man that I am! **Who will deliver me from this body of death?***

The Greek is **talaiporos** — miserable, distressed.

## The answer

*I thank God — **through Jesus Christ our Lord**!*

Not a technique. A person.

## The next sentence

*There is therefore now **no condemnation** to those who are in Christ Jesus.*

*For **the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has made me free** from the law of sin and
death.* See the note on Romans 8:1–4.

The chapter break hides the fact that the answer to chapter 7 is the first line of chapter 8.

## What has already been said

*Knowing this, that **our old man was crucified with Him**, that the body of sin might be done away
with.* See the note on Romans 6:6–7.

*Reckon yourselves to be **dead indeed to sin, but alive to God** in Christ Jesus.* See the note on
Romans 6:8–11.

Chapter 6 states the fact. Chapter 7 describes what happens when a person tries to achieve it by
effort. Chapter 8 names who does it.

> **What the passage establishes.** The will is intact and the outcome is still wrong. Whatever is
> failing, it is not the wanting — and the answer, when it comes, is a person rather than a method.
