Paul is praying that they would know the power at work in them, and to describe
its size he points at one event.

## The measure he uses

*That was the strength He put to work in Christ when He raised Him from the dead
and seated Him at His own right hand.*

The resurrection is offered as the unit of measurement. Not a general statement
that God is powerful — the specific force applied on one morning, named as the
same force now at work toward believers.

## The height

*High above every ruler and every authority and power and lordship, and above
every name that can be named, not only in this present age but in the age still
to come.*

Paul empties the vocabulary. Four categories of authority, then *every name that
can be named*, then both ages — this one and the next.

He is deliberately leaving nothing outside the sentence. Whatever it is, whatever
it is called, whenever it exists, it is under.

## The turn at the end

Everything so far is about Christ's exaltation. Then:

> *And He put everything under His feet, and gave Him, over all things, **as head
> to the church, which is His body**.*

The elevation is not held privately. He is given as head *to the church* — and a
head and a body are not in two locations. Paul will say it outright two chapters
on: seated together with Him in the heavenly places.

Where the head is seated is where the body is.

> **"The fullness of the One who fills everything."** Paul calls the church that,
> which is an extraordinary thing to call a scattered collection of first-century
> congregations with the problems his letters keep addressing. He does it anyway.
