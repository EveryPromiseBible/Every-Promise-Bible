The dream comes true in an Egyptian granary and only one man in the room knows it.

## His position

*Joseph was **the governor of the land**, the one who sold grain to all its people.*

The Hebrew is **shallit** — the one with authority. He is thirty-nine or so, shaved and
dressed as an Egyptian, speaking through an interpreter.

## The bow

*So his brothers came and **bowed low before him, their faces to the ground**.*

The dream, from chapter 37: *behold, my sheaf arose and also stood upright; and indeed your
sheaves stood all around and bowed down to my sheaf*. See the note on Genesis 37:5–11.

Their answer at the time was *shall you indeed reign over us?* and they hated him for it.

## The recognition

*Joseph **saw his brothers and knew them at once**, but he **acted like a stranger and spoke
to them harshly**.*

The Hebrew is *he made himself strange to them*.

He was seventeen when they last saw him. They are grown men who have aged twenty-two years,
and he picks them out of a queue.

## The asymmetry

*Joseph knew them, **though they did not know him**.*

The sentence is repeated in the passage because it is the engine of the whole story.

Everything that happens in the next three chapters depends on one party knowing and the other
not.

## What he remembered

*And **he remembered the dreams he'd once had about them**.*

The Hebrew is *Joseph remembered the dreams which he dreamed of them*.

Not the pit. Not the silver. The dreams.

## The accusation

*"**You are spies. You've come to see where the land is unprotected**."*

The Hebrew is *you have come to see the nakedness of the land*.

It is a plausible charge. Ten Semitic men arriving together from Canaan, at the border, in a
famine.

## Their defence

*"We are **all sons of one man. We are honest men**, not spies."*

The Hebrew for *honest* is **kenim** — true, upright.

Said by ten men who sold their brother and lied to their father about it for two decades.

## What the interrogation extracts

*"Your servants are **twelve brothers**, sons of one man in Canaan. **The youngest is with our
father right now, and one is no longer living**."*

That is what he was after. He now knows Benjamin is alive and at home, and he knows what they
say about him.

They volunteer the family structure under pressure without any idea who is listening.

## The test

*"**You will not leave this place unless your youngest brother comes here**."*

The whole remaining plot is set here. Everything after this — the silver in the sacks, Jacob's
refusal, Judah's pledge, the cup — comes out of one condition.

## The three days

*So he put them all **in custody for three days**.*

Three days in an Egyptian cell for men who had left a brother in a pit and sat down to eat.

## What they say on the third day

*"**We are truly guilty concerning our brother**, for we saw the anguish of his soul when he
pleaded with us, and we would not hear. Therefore this distress has come upon us."*

The first time it is spoken aloud in twenty-two years. Genesis has not recorded Joseph's
pleading until now — the account of the sale never mentioned it.

And: *they did not know that Joseph understood them, for he spoke to them through an
interpreter.*

## What he does

*And **he turned himself away from them and wept**.*

The first of the times he cries in this story, and there are several. He weeps at the sight
of Benjamin, he weeps when he tells them who he is, and he weeps on his father's neck.

Then he comes back and takes Simeon and binds him in front of them.

> **The sentence the story runs on.** *Joseph knew them, though they did not know him.* It is
> stated twice, and everything for the next three chapters depends on it holding.
