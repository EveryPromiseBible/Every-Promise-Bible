Paul lets the two righteousnesses speak in their own voices, and they sound
nothing alike.

## The first one

*Moses describes the righteousness that comes from the law like this: "**The person
who does these things will live by them**."*

Leviticus 18. One verb, and everything hangs on it: **does**.

It is not a harsh statement. It is simply the arrangement, stated plainly.

## The second one speaks differently

*But the righteousness that comes from believing **speaks a different way**.*

Paul personifies it. Believing-righteousness has a voice, and the first thing out of
its mouth is a prohibition on two questions.

## The two forbidden questions

*"**Do not say in your heart, 'Who will go up into heaven?'**" — that is, to bring
Christ down — "**or, 'Who will go down into the deep?'**" — that is, to bring Christ
up from among the dead.*

Both are attempts to fetch. One reaches up, one reaches down, and both assume the
thing needed is somewhere else and has to be retrieved.

Paul rules out both, and gives the reason in each case as a completed event. Christ
has already come down. He has already come up.

There is nothing to go and get.

## What it says instead

*"**The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart.**" That is the word of
believing that we preach.*

Paul is quoting Deuteronomy 30, where Moses says the commandment *is not too
mysterious for you, nor is it far off. It is not in heaven… nor is it beyond the
sea… but the word is very near you, in your mouth and in your heart.*

Two locations. The mouth first, then the heart — and Paul keeps Moses' order.

## What he does with it next

*If you **confess with your mouth** the Lord Jesus and **believe in your heart**
that God has raised Him from the dead, you will be saved. For with the heart one
believes unto righteousness, and **with the mouth confession is made unto
salvation**.*

He states it twice, mouth and heart both times.

The Greek for *confess* is **homologeo** — to say the same thing, to agree out
loud. It is the verb behind *the High Priest of our confession* in Hebrews 3. See
the note on Hebrews 3:1–2.

## What "saved" covers

**Sozo** and **soteria** are wider than the English. The same words are used in the
Gospels of a woman made well, a blind man healed, a sinking man pulled out of
water, and a girl raised.

Rescue, healing, wholeness, preservation. It is one word doing all of that work.

> **Where the chapter goes.** *For whoever calls on the name of the LORD shall be
> saved* — Joel — and then Paul's chain of questions: how shall they call on Him in
> whom they have not believed, and how shall they believe in Him of whom they have
> not heard, and how shall they hear without a preacher? The whole passage is about
> speech, from beginning to end.
