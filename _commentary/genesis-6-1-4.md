Four verses that look like a detour, and they are the reason for the flood.

## What had been promised

*I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her Seed; **He shall
bruise your head**, and you shall bruise His heel.* See the note on Genesis 3:14–15.

One promise has been standing since the garden, and everything hangs on it: a Seed is coming
through the woman, and He will crush the serpent.

The enemy heard it too. He heard it before Adam was out of the gate.

## The sons of God

*The **sons of God** saw that the daughters of men were beautiful, and they took wives from
among them, whomever they chose.*

The Hebrew is **bene ha'elohim**. The phrase occurs in the Hebrew Bible outside this chapter
in Job, and every time it means heavenly beings — *the sons of God came to present themselves
before the LORD*, and *when the morning stars sang together, and **all the sons of God shouted
for joy***.

Jude describes them: *the angels who did not keep their proper domain, but **left their own
abode**, He has reserved in everlasting chains under darkness* — and the next sentence puts
Sodom beside them for *going after strange flesh*. See the note on Jude 1:5–7.

Peter says the same and sets it in this chapter: *God did not spare the angels who sinned…
and did not spare the ancient world, but saved Noah, one of eight people*. See the note on
2 Peter 2:4–6.

## The Nephilim

*There were **giants** on the earth in those days.*

The Hebrew is **nephilim**, from **naphal** — to fall. The fallen ones.

They are described as *the mighty men who were of old, men of renown* — great in size, great
in strength, and great in wickedness.

## What was actually being attacked

Not morality in general. The bloodline.

Corrupt the human line and there is no woman's Seed, no crushed head, and no Christ. This is
the first attempt on Him, and it comes four chapters after the promise.

It is the same move every time it is tried again: Pharaoh ordering the Hebrew boys into the
Nile, Athaliah destroying the royal seed, Herod at Bethlehem, and the dragon in Revelation 12
*standing before the woman who was ready to give birth, to devour her Child as soon as it was
born*. See the note on Revelation 12:5–6.

## The hundred and twenty years

*"**My Spirit shall not strive with man forever**, for he is indeed flesh; yet his days shall
be one hundred and twenty years."*

A limit set on the span of a human life. The chapter before this records men living eight and
nine hundred years; from here the numbers come down.

## Noah

*Noah was a just man, **perfect in his generations**.*

The Hebrew is **tamim** — whole, sound, without blemish. It is the technical word Leviticus
uses of an animal fit for the altar.

And **in his generations** is **betoledotav** — in his line, his descent. What is being said
about Noah is said about his stock: uncorrupted, unmixed, human all the way down.

An unblemished line, kept back out of a world that had been got at — which is how the Seed
still had a road to travel.

## Why he was kept

Not because he was the best man available. The verse before the description is the one that
explains it: *but **Noah found grace** in the eyes of the LORD*. See the note on
Genesis 6:5–8.

Grace first, in verse 8. The character is described afterwards, in verse 9.

And Hebrews gives the ground of it: *by faith Noah… became **heir of the righteousness which
is according to faith***. Not righteousness he produced. Righteousness he inherited, on the
same terms anybody has ever had it. See the note on Hebrews 11:4–7.

## What he was told to build

A vessel with one door in its side, covered inside and out with **kaphar** — the word for
atonement — carrying everyone in it through the water that fell on everything else. See the
note on Genesis 6:13–22.

The line was preserved so the Seed could come. The eight aboard were preserved by getting
inside the covering.

> **What the chapter is really about.** Genesis 3 promised a Seed who would crush the
> serpent's head. Genesis 6 is the serpent going after the line that Seed had to come
> through — and God answering it by preserving one unblemished household, on grace, inside a
> boat sealed with the word for atonement.
