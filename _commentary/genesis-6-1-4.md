Four of the strangest verses in the Bible, and the honest thing to say about them
first is that nobody is certain what they describe.

## "Their days will be a hundred and twenty years"

Two readings have been argued for a very long time, and both are serious:

- **a new limit on human lifespan** — from now on, people live at most 120 years
- **a countdown to the flood** — 120 years of reprieve before the waters come

The second fits the immediate context better. The sentence begins *My Spirit will
not contend with humanity forever*, which sounds like a clock running out on a
situation rather than a change to biology. And the flood is the next thing that
happens in the narrative.

The first reading has a real problem: several people after this live far longer —
Noah, Shem, Abraham at 175. If it were a hard limit, it did not take.

## The sons of God

*The sons of God saw that these daughters were beautiful, and they took wives
from among them, whomever they chose.*

Three explanations have long competed: angelic beings, the line of Seth
intermarrying with the line of Cain, or dynastic rulers taking women by right.
The phrase *sons of God* elsewhere in the Old Testament usually means heavenly
beings, which is why the first reading is the oldest — but nothing here settles
it.

> **What the passage is doing.** Whatever the details, the direction is clear:
> the phrase *took… whomever they chose* is the language of power used without
> restraint, and it sits immediately before God looking at the earth and seeing
> that every inclination of the human heart was bent. Genesis is describing a
> world coming apart, and it does not stop to explain the mechanics.
