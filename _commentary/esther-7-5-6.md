The king's question, and the answer that ends Haman.

## The question

*"**Who is he? Where is the man** who has dared in his heart to do such a thing?"*

The king does not know. He signed the decree himself — sealed it with his own
ring — and never asked which people it was for.

He is furious at a crime he authorised.

## The name written into the sentence

In the Hebrew of this verse, the first letters of four consecutive words spell
out **EHYEH** — *I AM*. The name God gave Moses at the bush: *I AM WHO I AM.*

It stands in the king's demand to know who has dared to do such a thing.

That is the fourth and last of the hidden names in Esther. The letters of **YHWH**
are formed the same way four times elsewhere in the book — twice reading forwards
and twice reading backwards.

The name is never printed in this book. It is built into the text, in the places
where the story turns, and it is written in the one sentence where the question
being asked out loud is *who is behind this?*

## The answer

*"The man, the enemy, this wicked Haman."*

Three phrases, and no verb between them. In Hebrew it lands like three blows.

She names him in front of the king, at the king's table, with Haman sitting at
it.

## What she had waited for

Two banquets, two days. She had the sceptre and the offer of half the kingdom on
the first day and asked only for another dinner.

The delay put Haman in the room on the night after his humiliation, at the exact
hour the king's mind had already turned against him.

## "Haman stood frozen with terror"

The Hebrew is that he was terrified before the king *and the queen*.

He had not known she was a Jew. Nobody in the palace had — Mordecai had told her
not to say. The decree he wrote and sealed included the woman sitting opposite
him.

> **What the king still does not know.** He never learns that God is in this. He
> goes to his grave believing he lost his temper twice and settled a matter of
> court intrigue. The book lets him. The name is in the text and not in his mouth.
