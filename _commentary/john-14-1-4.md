Said to men who had just been told one of them would betray Him and one would deny Him.

## The context

Judas has gone out into the night. Peter has just been told about the rooster.

*"Do not let your hearts be troubled."*

Said in that room, to those men.

## The word

The Greek is **tarassestho** — stirred up, agitated.

John has used it of Jesus twice: at the tomb of Lazarus, and a few verses earlier when He
announced the betrayal — *He was troubled in His spirit*. See the note on
John 13:21–26.

He tells them not to be what He had just been.

## The instruction

*"**You trust in God; trust in Me as well**."*

The Greek can be read as two statements, two commands, or one of each. The construction is
deliberately ambiguous and every reading puts Himself alongside God as the object.

## The house

*"In My Father's house there are **many rooms**."*

The Greek is **monai** — abodes, dwelling places. From **meno**, to remain — the abiding word
that runs through the vine chapter. See the note on John 15:9–11.

The old versions have *mansions*, from the Latin **mansiones**, which meant stopping places on
a road.

## The aside

*"**If it were not so, I would have told you**."*

A remarkable sentence. He appeals to His own track record with them: I have not misled you
about anything else.

## The purpose

*"**I am going there to prepare a place for you**."*

The going is toward a cross, and He describes it as preparation for accommodation.

## The promise

*"And if I go and prepare a place for you, **I will come back and take you to Myself**."*

The Greek is **paralempsomai** — I will take you alongside, receive you to Myself.

## The reason

*"**So that where I am, you may be there too**."*

Not the place. The person.

The stated purpose of the whole arrangement is location relative to Him.

He prays the same thing an hour later: *Father, I desire that they also whom You gave Me may
be with Me where I am, that they may behold My glory*.

## The last clause

*"And you know where I am going, and **you know the way**."*

Which Thomas immediately contradicts: *Lord, we do not know where You are going. How can we
know the way?*

And the answer: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life*. See the note on
John 14:5–7.

## What the Jewish wedding looked like

A betrothal, and then the bridegroom went back to his father's house to build on an extra
room. When the father judged it ready, the son came for the bride, often at night, with a
shout.

The bride did not know the day.

*Here is the bridegroom! Come out to meet him!* See the note on Matthew 25:1–13.

## The other passage on the same subject

*For the Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a shout… and the dead in Christ will rise
first. Then we who are alive and remain shall be **caught up together with them** in the
clouds to meet the Lord in the air. And thus **we shall always be with the Lord**.* See the
note on 1 Thessalonians 4:16–18.

Paul's conclusion is the same as this one: not the place, the company.

## What the discourse ends with

*"Peace I leave with you, My peace I give to you; not as the world gives do I give to you.
**Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid**."* See the note on
John 14:25–27.

The same sentence, closing the chapter it opened.

> **The stated purpose.** Not the rooms. *That where I am, you may be there too* — and Paul
> ends his own version of the passage the same way.
