The word comes after three years, and the chapter pauses to tell you who else was in
Israel.

## The wait

*A long time passed — **into the third year of the drought**.*

Three years by the brook and at Zarephath. James gives the figure as three years and six
months, and Jesus uses the same number in Nazareth.

## The instruction

*"**Go, show yourself to Ahab**. I'm about to send rain on the land."*

Show yourself — the opposite of the command that opened chapter 17, which was *hide
yourself*.

And the rain is announced before the contest on Carmel has happened. God tells Elijah
the outcome first.

## The severity

*By now **the famine in Samaria was severe**.*

Samaria is the capital. The famine has reached the palace.

## Ahab's man

*Ahab had called for **Obadiah, the man in charge of his palace**.*

The Hebrew title is *over the house* — the highest office under the king, the royal
steward.

The name means **servant of the LORD**.

## The parenthesis

*(Obadiah was a man who **feared the LORD deeply**. When Queen Jezebel had been **killing
off the prophets of the LORD**, Obadiah had **taken a hundred of them and hidden them in
two caves, fifty in each**, and **kept them supplied with bread and water**.)*

The historian stops the narrative to put this on record.

A hundred men, in two caves, fed through a famine, by the chief of staff of the
household that was hunting them.

## The risk

Jezebel was in the same palace. Obadiah was requisitioning bread and water during a
drought, for men who were officially dead.

## What Elijah did not know

Three chapters later, Elijah says twice that he is the only one left. See the note on
1 Kings 19:9–18.

A hundred prophets were alive in two caves, kept there by a man in Ahab's own house,
and the historian has already told the reader.

## The errand

*"Let's **go through the land, to every spring and every valley**. Maybe we'll find
enough grass to **keep the horses and mules alive** so we don't lose all the animals."*

That is the king's priority in the third year of a national famine. The horses.

Ahab is out looking for grass. Elijah is on the road to find him.

## The division

*They **split the land between them** to search it — Ahab going one way, Obadiah
another.*

Which is how Obadiah, not Ahab, is the one who meets Elijah — and the meeting terrifies
him. He is convinced the Spirit will carry Elijah off and Ahab will kill him for a false
report.

*"I your servant have feared the LORD from my youth. Was it not reported to my lord what
I did… how I hid a hundred men of the LORD's prophets?"*

He is the only person in the chapter who has to say his own résumé out loud.

## Elijah's answer

*"As the LORD of hosts lives, before whom I stand, **I will surely present myself to him
today**."*

> **What the chapter puts side by side.** A king searching for grass to keep his horses,
> and his steward keeping a hundred prophets alive in caves. The famine reached both of
> them and they spent it differently.
