key: Psalms 103:13–14
title: He remembers that we are dust
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Two verses about what God knows, and neither is a warning.

## The comparison

*As **a father shows tenderness to his children**, so the LORD is tender to those who fear Him.*

The Hebrew verb is **racham** — from **rechem**, the womb. It is compassion with a physical
root, the feeling a parent has in the body.

*"If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, **how much more will
your Father who is in heaven give good things to those who ask Him!**"*

**Jesus** argues from the same comparison, and in the same direction — from the human father up.

## What He knows

*For **He knows how we are made**. He remembers that we are dust.*

The Hebrew is **yatsar** — the potter's verb, the one used in Genesis 2 when the man was formed
out of the ground. See the note on Genesis 2:4–7.

He knows the frame because He shaped it.

## What He does with the knowledge

Not lower the standard, and not excuse anything. He remembers.

*For we do not have a High Priest who cannot sympathise with our weaknesses, but One who **was
in all points tempted as we are**, yet without sin.*

**Hebrews** adds the thing the psalm could not yet say: that He does not only know what dust is
like from the outside.

## The verses on either side

Before: *as far as the east is from the west, so far has He removed our transgressions from
us.*

After: *as for man, his days are like grass… **but the mercy of the LORD is from everlasting to
everlasting**.*

The frailty is set between the removal of the sins and the permanence of the mercy. It is
never left standing on its own.

## What is not in the passage

Any suggestion that being dust is a problem to be solved.

*But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that **the excellence of the power may be of God
and not of us**.*

> **The verb.** *He remembers.* Not overlooks, not tolerates, not makes allowance for — the
> psalm says He keeps in mind what we are made of, and calls that tenderness.
