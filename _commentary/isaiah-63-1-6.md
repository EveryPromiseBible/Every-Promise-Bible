---
key: Isaiah 63:1–6
title: "I have trodden the winepress alone"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
A figure comes up the road with red on His clothes and is asked twice who He is.

## The first question

*Who is this coming from Edom, from the city of Bozrah, **His robes stained deep red**? Who is
this so splendid in what He wears, **striding forward in the fullness of His strength**?*

The Hebrew for *striding* is **tso'eh** — bending forward, marching with purpose.

## The answer

*"**It is I**, He answers. I who speak what is right. **I who am mighty to save**."*

The Hebrew is **ani medabber bitsdaqah, rav lehoshia** — I who speak in righteousness, abundant to
save.

The first thing said by a figure covered in blood is that He is able to rescue.

## The second question

*Why then is Your clothing red, like the clothing of one who has trodden the winepress?*

## The word said twice

*"I have **trodden the winepress alone**. **Of all the peoples, no one was there with Me.**"*

And again: *"I looked, and **there was no one to help. I was appalled that no one gave support.**
**So My own arm brought Me victory.**"*

Four statements of the same fact. The aloneness is the passage's insistence, not its background.

*Then all the disciples **forsook Him and fled**.*

*"Indeed the hour is coming, yes, has now come, that you will be scattered, each to his own, and
**will leave Me alone**. And yet I am not alone, because the Father is with Me."*

## And then even that

*"**My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?**"*

The one place in Scripture where the sentence *there was no one with Me* is true without
remainder.

## Who wears the robe at the end

*He was clothed with **a robe dipped in blood**, and His name is called **The Word of God**… And He
Himself **treads the winepress of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God**.*

**Revelation** 19 puts the rider on a white horse in Isaiah 63's clothing and gives Him a name.
The figure Isaiah's watchman could not identify is identified.

## The year and the day

*"For **a day of reckoning was in My heart, and the year of My redeeming had come**."*

The Hebrew is **shenat ge'ulai** — the year of my redeemed ones. A day of vengeance and a year of
redemption, and the year is the longer of the two.

*"The Spirit of the LORD is upon Me… to proclaim **the acceptable year of the LORD**" —* and
**Luke** records that **Jesus** stopped reading there, in the middle of Isaiah 61's sentence,
before the words *and the day of vengeance of our God*.

He read the year and left the day, rolled up the scroll, and sat down.

> **What is repeated four times.** That He was by Himself. *No one was there with Me. There was
> no one to help. My own arm brought Me victory.* Whatever else the winepress is, Isaiah will not
> let the reader think anybody stood in it with Him.
