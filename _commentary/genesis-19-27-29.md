Three verses of aftermath, and the last one explains the whole chapter.

## The morning

*Early the next morning Abraham returned to **the place where he had stood before the LORD**.*

He goes back to the exact spot.

The negotiation had ended there — fifty, forty-five, forty, thirty, twenty, ten — and then *the
LORD went His way, and Abraham returned to his place*. See the note on
Genesis 18:27–33.

He is there at first light, which means he had not stopped thinking about it.

## What he sees

*He looked down toward Sodom and Gomorrah and all the land of the plain, and he saw **dense
smoke rising from the land, like smoke from a furnace**.*

The Hebrew is **kevshan** — a smelting kiln.

The same phrase as Sinai on the third day: *the smoke ascended like the smoke of a furnace*.
See the note on Exodus 19:16–19.

Two columns of smoke in Genesis and Exodus, described identically, and they mean opposite
things.

## What he does not know

Whether Lot is alive.

Genesis has just told the reader. Abraham is looking at the smoke without that information, and
the text does not say he ever found out.

## The verse

*So when God destroyed the cities of the plain, **He remembered Abraham**, and He brought Lot
out of the catastrophe.*

The Hebrew is **vayyizkor Elohim et-Avraham**.

**Zakar** — remembered. It never means He had forgotten; it is the verb for God acting on a
commitment.

*God remembered Noah* while the water was up. *God remembered His covenant with Abraham* in
Egypt. *God remembered Rachel*. See the note on Genesis 30:22–24.

Every time, something happens immediately after.

## Whose name is in the sentence

Abraham's.

The rescue is attributed, in the text, to the man on the hill rather than to the man in the
city.

Lot's own record in the chapter: he lingered, he had to be taken by the wrist, and he argued
about the destination.

## What Peter attributes it to

*Righteous Lot, who was oppressed by the filthy conduct of the wicked… the Lord knows how to
deliver the godly out of temptations.* See the note on 2 Peter 2:7–9.

Genesis credits the intercessor. Peter credits the man's own condition.

Both are in Scripture and neither cancels the other.

## The clause at the end

*Out of the catastrophe that overthrew **the cities where Lot had lived**.*

The Hebrew is *in which Lot dwelt*.

Genesis names it one last time. Chapter 13: pitched his tent as far as Sodom. Chapter 14:
dwelling in Sodom. Chapter 19: sitting in the gate.

The last mention of his address is in the sentence about it being destroyed.

## Where Lot ends up

Zoar, and then afraid of Zoar, and then a cave in the mountains with his two daughters — which
is where the chapter finishes, and the last episode in it is the worst thing in Genesis after
the flood.

The nations that come out of it, Moab and Ammon, are Israel's neighbours and enemies for the
rest of the Old Testament.

And Ruth the Moabitess is David's great-grandmother.

## Where Abraham is

On a hill, at dawn, at the place he prayed.

> **Whose name is in the rescue sentence.** Not the man who was rescued. *God remembered
> Abraham* — and Genesis puts it in the same verse as Lot being brought out.
