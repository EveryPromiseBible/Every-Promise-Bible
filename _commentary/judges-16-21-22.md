Two verses at the bottom of the story, and the second one turns it.

## The capture

*The Philistines **seized him, gouged out his eyes**, and brought him down to **Gaza**.*

Gaza is where the story of his decline started — he had gone there after a prostitute, and
carried the city gates away on his shoulders to a hill.

He comes back through the same gate as a prisoner.

## What he had lost first

*And she said, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" So he awoke from his sleep, and said,
"I will go out as before, at other times, and shake myself free!" **But he did not know that
the LORD had departed from him**.*

The eyes go afterwards. The line about not knowing comes before the blinding.

## The eyes

Samson's story is full of them. *I saw a woman in Timnah… **get her for me, for she pleases
me well**.* The Hebrew is *she is right in my eyes*.

The book's refrain is *everyone did what was right in his own eyes*.

The first thing they take is what he had been following.

## The shackles

*They bound him in **bronze shackles**, and there in the prison **he ground grain at the
mill**.*

The Hebrew is *he became a grinder in the prison house*.

Grinding was women's work, or a donkey's. The strongest man in Israel turning a millstone in
the dark.

## The state

*Blind and shamed.*

## The turn

*But **little by little, the hair of his head began to grow back again** — quietly,
unnoticed.*

The Hebrew is *the hair of his head began to grow again after it had been shaven*.

Nobody notices, because a prisoner turning a mill is not looked at.

## What the hair was

Not the source of the strength. The Nazirite vow was — no razor, no wine, no contact with the
dead — and the uncut hair was its visible sign.

He had broken the other two clauses repeatedly. The hair was the last part of the vow still
standing, and Delilah got that too.

## What he prays

*"O Lord GOD, **remember me, I pray! Strengthen me, I pray, just this once**, O God, that I
may with one blow take vengeance on the Philistines for my two eyes!"*

The only recorded prayer of his life that is not a complaint. Earlier he had prayed for water
and been given it out of a hollow place.

## What happens

Three thousand on the roof, watching a blind man brought out to entertain them at a festival
for Dagon.

*And Samson took hold of the two middle pillars… **so the dead that he killed at his death
were more than he had killed in his life**.*

## Where he ends up

*Then his brothers and all his father's household came down and took him, and brought him up
and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of his father Manoah.*

The same stretch of country where, at the start, *the Spirit of the LORD began to move upon
him*.

## Where he is listed

Hebrews 11, in the roll call: *Gideon and Barak and **Samson** and Jephthah, also David and
Samuel and the prophets — who through faith subdued kingdoms, worked righteousness, obtained
promises*.

He is on the list.

## The verse that carries it

*But the hair of his head began to grow again.*

The narrator drops it in and moves on. Everything in the last scene rests on a sentence about
something growing slowly in the dark, that nobody was watching.

> **What the narrator notices.** Not the mill and not the chains. That something was growing
> back, in a prison, where no one was looking at him.
