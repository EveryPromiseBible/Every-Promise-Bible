One verse, seven hundred years early, and it names the village.

## Which Bethlehem

*But you, **Bethlehem Ephrathah**.*

There were two towns of that name. Micah specifies the one in Judah, using its
older name, so there is no confusion with the Bethlehem in Zebulun.

The prophecy is precise enough that it could only be fulfilled in one place.

## "Too little to be counted"

*Small among the clans of Judah, **too little to be counted**.*

Bethlehem did not make the list. When Joshua divided the land and the towns of
Judah were enumerated, Bethlehem is not in the Hebrew text of that list at all.

It is the village David came from, and even that had not made it a place.

## What comes out of it

*Out of you will come **one for Me**, a ruler over Israel.*

*For Me.* The ruler is produced for God's own purposes before He is produced for
Israel's.

## The last clause

*Whose beginnings reach back **into the far distance, into ancient days, into
times older than memory**.*

The Hebrew is *from of old, from everlasting* — **mi-qedem, mi-mei olam**. The
same phrase is used in Scripture of God's own eternity.

A ruler with a birthplace and no beginning. Micah says both in one sentence and
does not explain how.

## Who read it out loud

Herod asked the chief priests and scribes where the Christ was to be born, and
they answered immediately: *in Bethlehem of Judea, for thus it is written by the
prophet.*

They knew the verse, quoted it accurately from memory, and did not go the five
miles to look.

## What it took to fulfil it

Joseph and Mary lived in Nazareth, ninety miles north. A Roman emperor ordered a
census of the whole empire, and it required every man to return to his ancestral
town.

Caesar Augustus moved the population of the Roman world to get one pregnant woman
to a village too little to be counted.

> **What is beside it in the chapter.** The verse before speaks of a judge of
> Israel struck on the cheek with a rod; the verses after describe Him standing and
> shepherding *in the strength of the LORD*, and *He shall be their peace.* Micah
> puts the striking and the shepherding on the same page.
