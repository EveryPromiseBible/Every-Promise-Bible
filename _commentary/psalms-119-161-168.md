The **Shin** stanza, near the end of the psalm, and the man is still being hunted.

## The situation

*Rulers have **hunted me for no good reason**.*

The Hebrew is *princes have persecuted me without cause*.

The psalm never explains who they are or what the accusation was. It refers to them
repeatedly — *the proud have forged a lie against me*, *the wicked have laid a snare for
me*.

## The response

*Yet **my heart stands in awe before Your words**.*

The Hebrew is *my heart stands in awe of Your word*.

Princes on one side and a sentence on the other, and the awe goes to the sentence.

## The image

*I take a joy in all the promise You made **like one who finds a heap of hidden spoil**.*

The Hebrew is *as one who finds great spoil*.

Plunder after a battle — the thing a soldier hopes for and does not expect. Judah spent
three days collecting it after Jehoshaphat's battle. See the note on
2 Chronicles 20:13–15.

The comparison is to a windfall rather than to a wage.

## The pair

*I **hate the lie, I cannot bear the falsehood**, but oh, **the teaching You have given, I
love**.*

The psalm does this constantly — a hatred and a love in one verse, and the hatred is
always of falsehood.

## The count

*Seven times a day **I stop to praise You** for all the fair decisions of Your justice.*

Seven — completeness rather than a schedule, though later Jewish and Christian practice
took it as one and built the daily offices on it.

## The famous line

*Great peace belongs to those who **love Your teaching**; and **there is nothing left to make
them stumble**.*

The Hebrew is *nothing shall offend them* — **mikshol**, a stumbling block.

The Greek Old Testament renders it **skandalon**, the trigger of a trap.

The man saying it is being hunted by rulers. The peace is not an absence of pressure.

## The waiting

*LORD, **I have waited, hoping for Your rescue**, and I have lived out all Your good
commands.*

## The love

*My soul has kept the steady words You gave, and oh, **I love them, love them past all
telling**.*

The Hebrew is *and I love them exceedingly*.

## The last verse

*I've kept Your precepts and the words You keep, for **all my ways lie open, LORD, before
You**.*

The Hebrew is *for all my ways are before You*.

It is the reason given for the keeping, and it is not fear. He is describing a life lived
in sight of somebody.

## Where the psalm ends

Two stanzas later, at the very end:

*I have gone astray like a lost sheep; **seek Your servant**, for I do not forget Your
commandments.*

The longest chapter in the Bible, one hundred and seventy-six verses about loving God's
word, ends with a man saying he is lost and asking to be looked for.

> **The comparison the stanza turns on.** Not a wage and not a duty. A heap of spoil — what
> a man finds after a battle he expected to lose.
