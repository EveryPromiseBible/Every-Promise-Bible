Three verses, and every one of them records something being handed over.

## The darkness

*"**It was about the sixth hour, midday, and darkness came over the whole land until the ninth
hour.**"*

Noon to three. Three hours, in the middle of the day, at Passover — which falls at full moon, when
a solar eclipse is astronomically impossible.

*"**And it shall come to pass in that day**," says the Lord GOD, "**that I will make the sun go down
at noon, and I will darken the earth in broad daylight.**"*

Amos 8, and the next clause is *I will turn your feasts into mourning.*

## The curtain

*"**And the curtain of the temple was torn down its middle.**"*

**Eschisthe meson** — split down the middle. **Matthew** and **Mark** add the direction: from the top
down.

The veil shut off the Holy of Holies, which one man entered once a year with blood. **Luke** puts
its tearing in the same sentence as the darkness, before the death.

*Therefore, brethren, having **boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus**, by a new and
living way which He consecrated for us, **through the veil, that is, His flesh**.*

**Hebrews** 10, and the veil and the flesh are the same object in the sentence.

## The last word

*"**Father, into Your hands I commit My spirit.**"*

A quotation of Psalm 31:5, with one word added at the front. See the note on
Psalms 31:5–8.

**Paratithemai** — I place alongside, I deposit for safekeeping. It is a banking word, and the psalm
uses it of a man expecting to live.

Jewish children were taught to say that verse at bedtime. He dies saying His prayers, with **Father**
put in front.

## The three sayings Luke records

*"**Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do.**"*

*"**Assuredly, I say to you, today you will be with Me in Paradise.**"*

*"**Father, into Your hands I commit My spirit.**"*

**Luke** keeps three of the seven and all three are addressed to somebody: twice to the Father, once
to a criminal. The first is a request for the people driving the nails, and the second is granted to
a man with nothing to offer, on the day.

## What the centurion says

*So when the centurion saw what had happened, he glorified God, saying, "**Certainly this was a
righteous Man!**"*

**Luke**'s wording differs from **Matthew**'s and **Mark**'s. The officer in charge of the execution
gives the verdict Pilate had given three times and acted against.

> **What Luke puts in three verses.** The sun failing at noon at full moon, the veil torn down its
> middle, and a bedtime prayer with one word added to the front of it.
