The first church council, and the first speech is Peter's.

## What is being decided

Men had come down from Judea to Antioch teaching *unless you are circumcised according
to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved.*

Paul and Barnabas had *no small dissension and dispute* with them, and the matter was
sent up to Jerusalem.

## The procedure

*The apostles and elders **came together to look into the matter**.*

Both groups. It is a deliberation, not a ruling handed down.

## The debate

*After **a long discussion**, Peter stood up.*

The Greek is *much disputing*. Luke does not summarise it, and he does not pretend it was
brief or comfortable.

## What Peter appeals to

*"Brothers, you know that **in the early days God made a choice among us**: that **by my
mouth the Gentiles should hear the message** of the good news and believe."*

The Cornelius episode, roughly ten years earlier. He had already had to explain it once
in Jerusalem. See the note on Acts 11:15–18.

The choosing is God's, and Peter describes himself as the mouth.

## The witness

*"And **God, who knows the heart**, testified for them **by giving them the Holy Spirit
just as He gave Him to us**."*

The Greek for *who knows the heart* is **kardiognostes** — heart-knower. It appears twice
in the New Testament, both times in Acts, both times about God choosing.

The evidence Peter brings is not an argument. It is something God did, and he says God is
the one who testified.

## "Just as He gave Him to us"

The comparison is exact. Peter does not allow a two-tier arrangement.

## The distinction

*"**He made no distinction at all between us and them**."*

The Greek is *he discriminated nothing between*. It is the same verb Peter used of
himself in chapter 11 — *the Spirit told me to go with them, **doubting nothing**.*

## The cleansing

*"**Cleansing their hearts by faith**."*

The Greek is **katharisas** — purified. It is the ritual word, the one for making
something fit to approach God with.

Two things in the clause: what was cleansed, and how.

**Their hearts** — not their diet, their calendar, or their bodies. The dispute was about
an external mark, and Peter answers about an internal cleaning.

**By faith** — the mechanism, stated in two words, with nothing else in the sentence.

## What he says next

*"Now therefore, **why do you test God** by putting a yoke on the neck of the disciples
**which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear?**"*

And then the sentence the council turns on:

*"But we believe that **through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved in
the same manner as they**."*

The comparison is reversed. Not *they are saved like us*. **We** are saved like **them**.

## What happened after

*Then all the multitude kept silent and listened to Barnabas and Paul.*

James then speaks, quotes Amos, and the letter goes out to Antioch with four requests and
no circumcision.

> **What settles it.** Not a text and not a precedent. Peter reports that God gave the
> same Spirit and cleansed the same organ by the same means — and the room goes quiet.
