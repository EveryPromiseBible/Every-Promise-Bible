Luke's version of this discourse says something the other Gospels do not: armies,
a siege, and a clock that starts running.

## The sign He gives

*"When you see **armies camped in a ring around Jerusalem**, then you will know
that her ruin is close."*

Matthew and Mark have *the abomination of desolation standing where it ought
not.* Luke, writing for a Gentile readership, gives the plain military fact.

## The instruction

*"Let everyone in Judea **run for the hills**, and let those inside the city **get
out of it**, and let those out in the countryside **not go in**."*

Three directions, all outward. Nobody is told to defend the city.

The historian Eusebius records that the Christians of Jerusalem left before the
siege closed, and crossed the Jordan to Pella. They had been told.

## What happened

In AD 70 Titus surrounded Jerusalem, and the city and the temple were destroyed.
Josephus, who was there, describes the famine inside the walls, and *it will be
bitter in those days for a woman carrying a child* reads as understatement beside
his account.

*They will fall by the sword, and they will be carried off as captives among all
the nations.*

The scattering that followed lasted nineteen centuries.

## The clock

*"And Jerusalem will be **trampled under Gentile feet** until the times of the
Gentiles **have run their course**."*

**Until.** The trampling is given an end.

*The times of the Gentiles* is a defined period — Luke's phrase, and the only
place it appears. The Greek for *run their course* is to be fulfilled, to be
filled up, as a measure is filled.

## Daniel's statue

The same period is what Nebuchadnezzar saw as a statue: gold, silver, bronze,
iron, and feet of iron mixed with clay — a succession of Gentile empires running
from the Babylon that burned the first temple onward.

And in Daniel's interpretation it ends the same way, with a stone *cut from the
mountain but not by human hands*, which breaks all of it and stands forever. See
the note on Daniel 2:44–45.

## What the two passages share

Both put Jerusalem under Gentile rule for a stated stretch. Both put a term on
it. Neither describes the arrangement as permanent.

Matthew's version of the same night ends with *you will not see Me again until
you say, "Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord."* The same word again.
See the note on Matthew 23:37–39.

## The clock that is running

*"Jerusalem will be trampled by the Gentiles **until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled**."*

**Achri hou plerothosin kairoi ethnon** — until the seasons of the nations are filled full.

*For I do not desire, brethren, that you should be ignorant of this mystery… that blindness in part
has happened to Israel **until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in. And so all Israel will be
saved**.*

**Romans** 11, and **Paul** uses the same construction — a limit with an **until** on it — and then
says what is on the far side.

He gives the reason two verses later: *for the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable.*

## The mercy inside the sentence

*"**But woe to those who are pregnant and to those who are nursing babies in those days!**"*

In the middle of a judgement oracle, a sentence of pity for the people who will find it hardest to
run.

*"**O Jerusalem, Jerusalem**… how often I wanted to gather your children together, as a hen gathers
her chicks under her wings, but you were not willing!"*

*Now as He drew near, He saw the city and **wept over it**, saying, "If you had known, even you,
especially in this your day, the things that make for your peace!"*

**Luke** 19, and the same evangelist. The man predicting the siege is recorded crying about it from
the ridge above the city — and **Jesus** gives the reason in the next breath: *because you did not
know the time of your visitation.*

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that **while we were still sinners, Christ died for
us**.*

## And what He says four verses on

*"Now when these things begin to happen, **look up and lift up your heads, because your redemption
draws near**."* See the note on Luke 21:25–28.

> **What He says immediately after.** Signs in the sun and moon and stars, and
> then *when these things begin to happen, look up and lift up your heads, for
> your redemption draws near.* The instruction in the siege was to run for the
> hills. The instruction at the end is to look up.
