One line, and it is about food.

## The blessing

*"Asher's food will be **rich**; he will provide **delicacies fit for a king**."*

The Hebrew for *rich* is **shamen** — fat, the choicest part. And the second
phrase is *dainties of a king*, the food set on a royal table.

**Asher** means happy, blessed. Leah named him: *happy am I, for the daughters
will call me blessed.*

## The ground

Asher's portion was the coastal strip in the far north-west, running up toward
Tyre and Sidon — the richest agricultural land in Israel, olive country.

Moses says it in Deuteronomy 33: *let Asher dip his foot in oil.*

## Where Jesus went into it

*Jesus went out from there and departed to the region of **Tyre and Sidon**.*

That is Asher's border, and it is the only recorded time He left Israel's
territory altogether.

There a Canaanite woman came after Him about her daughter, and what she got from
Him was a sentence about bread:

*"It is not right to take **the children's bread** and toss it to the little
dogs."*

## The children's bread

He calls it bread, and He calls it the children's.

She does not argue with the arrangement. She takes the picture and finishes it:
*"Yes, Lord — and even the little dogs eat **the crumbs** that fall from their
master's table."*

A crumb off that table broke a demonic hold on a child in another house.

Asher's line said *rich food, delicacies fit for a king*, and the meeting that
happens on Asher's ground is a conversation about whose table the bread comes
from. See the note on Matthew 15:21–28.

## What she got

*"Woman, your faith is great. Let it be done for you just as you desire."* And her
daughter was healed from that hour.

She asked for a crumb and it was enough.

> **What Moses added over Asher.** *As your days, so shall your strength be.* Set
> beside Jacob's line about rich food, the two blessings on this tribe are about
> being fed and being strong for the length of the days you get. See the note on
> Deuteronomy 33:24–25.
