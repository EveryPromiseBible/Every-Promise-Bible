---
key: Jude 1:3–4
title: "The letter he did not want to write"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
**Jude** says out loud that this is not the letter he sat down to write.

## What he intended

*"Dear friends, **I had my heart set on writing to you about the rescue we all share**."*

**Pasan spouden poioumenos graphein hymin peri tes koines hemon soterias** — making all haste to
write to you about our common salvation.

He wanted to write about the gospel. Twenty-five verses of warning follow instead, and he tells the
reader that.

## What he wrote

*"**Instead I find I have to write this, and press you hard to put up a fight for the faith.**"*

**Ananken eschon** — I had necessity.

**Epagonizesthai** — to contend, to struggle over. From **agon**, the contest — the root of *agony*.
It occurs once in the New Testament.

## What is being contended for

*"**The faith that was handed over to God's people once and for all.**"*

**Hapax paradotheise** — once for all delivered.

**Hapax** is the word **Hebrews** uses of the sacrifice: *He offered Himself once for all*. It means
there is not going to be a second delivery.

**Paradidomi** — handed over, passed on. It is the word for transmitting a tradition, and **Paul**
uses it of the resurrection report: *I delivered to you first of all that which I also received.*

The faith is described as a deposit received rather than a position argued out, which is why the
verb attached to it is *guard* rather than *develop*.

## The charge

*"**Certain men have slipped in among you** unnoticed."*

**Pareisedysan** — they wormed their way in. The word suggests a side door.

## What they do with grace

*"**They twist the grace of our God into a licence for filth.**"*

**Ten tou Theou hemon charita metatithentes eis aselgeian** — transposing the grace of our God into
licentiousness.

**Metatithemi** — to shift, to transfer, to change one thing into another.

*What shall we say then? **Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? Certainly not!** How
shall we who died to sin live any longer in it?*

**Paul** asks the question in **Romans** 6 because his gospel had already provoked it — *why not say,
"Let us do evil that good may come"? — as we are slanderously reported and as some affirm that we
say.*

A gospel nobody could distort in this direction would not be the one **Paul** preached. **Jude** and
**Paul** name the abuse and neither of them answers it by making grace smaller.

*For **you, brethren, have been called to liberty; only do not use liberty as an opportunity for the
flesh**, but through love serve one another.*

## And what they deny

*"**They will not have our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ.**"*

**Ton monon despoten kai kyrion hemon Iesoun Christon arnoumenoi.**

**Despotes** — absolute owner. The men who turn grace into permission are described, in the same
clause, as refusing the one who owns them.

> **The sentence he begins with.** *I had my heart set on writing to you about the rescue we all
> share.* He does not get to write that letter, and he leaves the disappointment on the page.
