He is fed twice a day by birds for the better part of a year, and then the water stops.

## The instruction

*"**Leave here. Turn east** and go **hide yourself** by the Kerith Ravine."*

Three commands, and the third is the odd one. Elijah has just walked into Ahab's throne
room and shut the sky. The next thing he is told is to disappear.

The Hebrew for *Kerith* comes from a root meaning *to cut off*.

## The two provisions

*"You'll **drink from the brook**, and **I've told the ravens** to bring you food
there."*

Water from the ground and food from the air.

## The ravens

The Hebrew is **orev**. Ravens are on the unclean list in Leviticus, and they are birds
that scavenge and do not share — Job asks *who provides food for the raven?*

They are also the birds that feed their own young badly. The psalm says God *gives to
the beast his food, and to the young ravens which cry.*

These ones carry food past their own nests to a man in a ravine.

## Where the meat came from

Bread and meat, twice a day, in a famine. Nobody in Israel was eating meat twice a day
that year.

The text does not say where it came from and Elijah does not ask.

## The obedience

*So Elijah **did exactly what the LORD said**. He went and settled by the Kerith
Ravine.*

The Hebrew is *he went and did according to the word of the LORD*. It is the same
formula used of him at Zarephath and on Carmel.

## The rhythm

*Bread and meat **in the morning**, bread and meat **again in the evening**.*

Twice daily, at the hours of the morning and evening sacrifice.

Enough for the day and no more. The same arrangement as manna, which could not be
stored.

## Then

*But **after a while the brook dried up**, because no rain had fallen on the land.*

He watched it go down. A brook does not stop in a morning — it narrows, slows, becomes
pools, then mud, then stones.

## Why it dried

*Because no rain had fallen on the land.*

The drought was the one Elijah had called for. He prayed it and then drank from a stream
that it emptied.

## What did not happen

No new instruction while the water was falling. The word comes in the next verse, after
the brook is gone: *arise, go to Zarephath.* See the note on 1 Kings 17:8–16.

The next place is a Gentile town in Jezebel's home territory, and the next provider is a
widow with a handful of flour.

## The pattern

Ravens, then a widow, then the whole nation on Carmel. Each provision is stranger than
the last and each one ends before the next is named.

## What dried up

*And it happened after a while that **the brook dried up**, because there had been no rain in
the land.*

The drought Elijah had announced. His own word is what empties his water supply.

He is not in the wrong place, and he has not been disobedient. The brook dries because he
prayed and God answered.

## What did not dry up

The word.

*Then **the word of the LORD came to him**, saying, "Arise, go to Zarephath… **I have commanded
a widow there to provide for you**."*

The ravens stop and the widow starts, and the interval between the two is one sentence. He is
never without provision — he is only ever without the *previous* provision.

*And my God shall **supply all your need** according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus.*

## Where he is sent next

To a Gentile town in Sidon — Jezebel's home country — to be fed by a widow with a handful of
meal who is preparing a last meal for herself and her son.

Jesus picked that out, in Nazareth, in the sermon that got Him thrown out of town: *there were
many widows in Israel in the days of Elijah… **but to none of them was Elijah sent** except to
Zarephath, in the region of Sidon, to a woman who was a widow.*

He read Isaiah 61, said *today this Scripture is fulfilled*, and then named two occasions when
God went outside Israel. That is the line that made them try to throw Him off a cliff. See the
note on Luke 4:16–21.

> **What he was told to do at the brook.** Hide. Not preach, not organise, not
> intercede. The most public prophet in the Old Testament spends the first stretch of
> his ministry sitting by a stream watching birds arrive.
