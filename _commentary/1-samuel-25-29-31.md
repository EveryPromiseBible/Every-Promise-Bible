A woman standing in a ravine in front of four hundred armed men talks a future
king out of a massacre.

## The picture she gives him

*"The life of my lord will be **bound safe in the bundle of the living**, kept by
the LORD your God."*

The Hebrew is *bound in the bundle of life*. The image is of valuables tied up in a
cloth and carried — the way a traveller secured what he could not afford to lose,
by keeping it on his person.

The phrase entered Jewish liturgy and is still cut into gravestones.

## And the picture for his enemies

*"But the lives of your enemies He will **sling away like a stone from a sling**."*

She is talking to the man who killed Goliath with a sling. She takes the weapon
from the best day of his life and hands it to God.

Two images in one sentence — one for what God keeps and one for what God discards
— and she picked both for this listener.

## What she argues from

*"When the LORD has done for my lord **all the good He has promised**, and made you
**ruler over Israel**…"*

David is a fugitive living in caves. Saul is on the throne. She speaks of the
kingdom as settled.

Nobody had crowned him. She had heard the reports, worked out what he was, and
addresses him accordingly.

## The thing she is protecting

*"…then you will have **no cause for grief, no burden on your conscience** for
shedding blood without cause or for taking vengeance yourself."*

That is her whole case. Not *spare us* — she barely mentions her household.

She argues that a man on his way to a throne cannot afford to arrive at it with
this on him. The Hebrew is *no stumbling of heart*, no obstacle in the conscience.

She is defending his future from his present temper.

## What she asks for

*"And when the LORD deals well with my lord, **remember your servant**."*

One line, at the very end, and it is the same request the thief makes on the cross.

## What David says back

*"Blessed is the LORD God of Israel, who sent you this day to meet me. **Blessed is
your advice**, and blessed are you, because you have kept me this day from coming
to bloodshed and from **avenging myself with my own hand**."*

He blesses God, then the counsel, then her. And he names precisely what he was
about to do: avenge himself.

He had spared Saul twice by then, refusing to touch the LORD's anointed. He was
minutes from slaughtering a household over an insult about food.

## The wisdom in it

The chapter introduced her as *a woman of good sense* — **sekel**, insight that
produces good outcomes. This is what it looks like in operation: right words, right
man, right moment, at considerable personal risk.

See the note on 1 Samuel 25:2–3.

> **What she did not do.** Tell her husband. *But she did not tell Nabal her
> husband.* The chapter records it without comment, and then records that when she
> did tell him, in the morning, when he was sober, his heart died within him.
