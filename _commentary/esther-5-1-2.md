Two verses, and they carry the weight of the whole book.

## The third day

*On the **third day** Esther put on her royal robes.*

Three days of fasting are behind this sentence. She had asked for them, and she
comes out of them on the third day and walks in.

## What she wore

*Her **royal robes**.*

Not sackcloth. She had been in mourning clothes at the end of chapter 4 — the
Hebrew here is literally *she put on royalty*.

She approaches him as a queen, in what belongs to her position, not as a
petitioner in rags.

## The law she is breaking

She had spelled it out herself two chapters earlier: anyone who goes into the
inner court unsummoned is put to death, *unless the king holds out the golden
sceptre.* She had not been called for thirty days.

She has no right to be standing there. That is the fact the scene begins with.

## What happened

*When the king saw Queen Esther standing in the court, **she won his favour**, and
he **held out to her** the golden sceptre.*

The Hebrew is *she obtained grace in his eyes* — **chen**, the same word the book
has used over her from the beginning, unearned favour.

She did not earn the sceptre by three days of fasting and she did not argue her
way to it. It was extended.

## Who moves first

The sceptre goes out before she says anything. She has not stated her business,
made her case, or explained why she came.

And then: *so Esther came near and **touched the top of the sceptre**.*

She approaches because it was held out, and what she touches is the thing that
spared her. The order in those two verses is the order the whole book runs on.

## What was on the other side of it

*"What is your request? It shall be given you, **up to half the kingdom**."*

That is the next sentence. The law said death; the sceptre said half an empire.

> **Where the throne came from.** She is standing in a room she only occupies
> because Vashti refused to come when she was summoned. One woman would not come
> when she was called; another came when she was not called, and lived. See the
> note on Esther 1:9–12.
