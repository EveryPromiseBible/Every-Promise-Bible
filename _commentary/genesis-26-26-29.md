---
key: Genesis 26:26–29
title: The men who sent him away come back
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The people who ran him out of town arrive at his tent asking for a treaty.

## Who turns up

*Then **Abimelech came to him from Gerar**, with **Ahuzzath his adviser** and **Phicol the
commander of his army**.*

A king, a counsellor and a general. This is a state delegation, and it has travelled to him.

## What they had done

*"**Go away from us, for you are much mightier than we**."* They had stopped up his father's
wells with earth and told him to leave.

Then his herdsmen dug at Esek and Gerar's herdsmen claimed it. Then Sitnah, and they claimed
that too. See the note on Genesis 26:22.

## What Isaac says

*"**Why have you come to me, since you were hostile to me and sent me away?**"*

He says it plainly. He does not pretend the history is not there, and he does not refuse them
either.

## Their answer

*"**We have seen clearly that the LORD is with you.**"*

The Hebrew is *seeing we have seen* — the doubled form, for certainty.

They are not making a theological statement about their own beliefs. They are reporting an
observation. Something about this man's life had become impossible to explain any other way,
and Genesis records that the people who evicted him were the ones who worked it out.

Potiphar did the same in Egypt: *his master saw that the LORD was with him*. See the note on
Genesis 39:1–6.

## The revision of history

*"**Just as we did not harm you but always treated you well and sent you away in peace.**"*

That is not what happened. They stopped his wells and drove him out.

Isaac lets it stand. He does not correct the record, and the next verse is *so he made them a
feast, and they ate and drank*.

## The last clause

*"**And now you are blessed by the LORD.**"*

The men who sent him away pronounce the blessing over him themselves.

## The table

*You prepare **a table before me in the presence of my enemies**.* See the note on
Psalms 23:5.

Not away from them. In front of them — and in Genesis 26 the enemies are the guests.

## What made them come

Nothing Isaac argued. He had dug, been robbed, moved on, dug again, been robbed, moved on, and
dug again — and *the LORD has made room for us, and we shall be fruitful in the land*.

He never took one of those wells back by force. The chapter that ends with a king asking him
for a covenant is the chapter in which he yields three times.

## What is dug that afternoon

*It came to pass the same day that Isaac's servants came and told him about the well which they
had dug, and said, "**We have found water**."*

He names it Shibah, and the town is Beersheba to this day.

The treaty is signed and the water comes up the same afternoon.

## What he never did

Argue a claim, raise a man, or take a well back.

Esek, and he moved. Sitnah, and he moved. Rehoboth, and there was room at last. The chapter
that ends with a king asking him for a treaty contains no moment where Isaac was vindicated in
a dispute.

*Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God*, Jesus said — and Paul put
the working version of it plainly: *if it is possible, as much as depends on you, **live
peaceably with all men**… Do not be overcome by evil, but **overcome evil with good**.*

## What he serves them

*So he **made them a feast**, and they ate and drank.*

The men who stopped his wells and drove him out get dinner, and the well his servants were
digging that same afternoon comes in with water.

> **Who says it.** *We have seen clearly that the LORD is with you… and now you are blessed by
> the LORD.* Spoken over Isaac by the king who had thrown him out, in Isaac's own tent, with
> the army commander standing there.
