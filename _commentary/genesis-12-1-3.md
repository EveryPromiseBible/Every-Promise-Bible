Everything after Babel starts here, with one man and a sentence.

## The three things to leave

*"**Leave your country, your people, and your father's household**."*

Three, and they narrow as they go — nation, clan, then the household he was raised
in.

The last is the hardest and it is named last.

## Where to

*"**Go to the land I will show you.**"*

Not a land I have shown you. **Will** show.

Hebrews notes it: *he went out, **not knowing where he was going**.* See the note on
Hebrews 11:8–12.

He is given a direction and no destination.

## The seven

Count the promises:

- **I will make you into a great nation**
- **I will bless you**
- **I will make your name great**
- **you will be a blessing**
- **I will bless those who bless you**
- **whoever curses you I will curse**
- **through you all the families of the earth will be blessed**

Seven, and six of them begin with *I will*.

## The name

*"I will make **your name great**."*

Babel is the chapter immediately before this. *Come, let us build ourselves a city,
and a tower whose top is in the heavens; and **let us make a name for ourselves**.*

Two chapters, two projects, and the same phrase. One group tries to build a name and
is scattered; one man is given one and told to walk.

## The last clause

*"**Through you all the families of the earth will be blessed.**"*

The nations were just divided in chapter 10 and scattered in chapter 11.

The first thing said about the man God calls out of them is that they will all be
blessed through him.

Paul calls this line *the gospel preached beforehand to Abraham* — *in you all the
nations shall be blessed.*

## What Abram had

No law, no covenant sign, no Scripture, no priest and no tabernacle. A sentence, and
a road.

*So Abram departed as the LORD had spoken to him* — the next verse — *and Abram was
seventy-five years old.*

## The Illumination's note on it

*A call built entirely on promise: God asking Abram to walk, and **God Himself
carrying the outcome**.*

Nothing in the seven is contingent on his performance. The only thing asked is the
leaving.

## What he did with it

The rest of Genesis 12 is Abram going down to Egypt in a famine and telling a king
his wife was his sister. See the note on Genesis 12:10–20.

The promises stand.

> **Where the blessing and cursing clause turns up.** *I will bless those who bless
> you, and whoever curses you I will curse.* Balaam is hired to curse Israel and can
> only bless them, and quotes this line back at Balak: *blessed is he who blesses you,
> and cursed is he who curses you.*
