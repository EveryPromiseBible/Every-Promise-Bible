The line that walked away from God builds the first city and invents most of civilisation.

## What Cain does with exile

*Cain built a city and **named it after his son**.*

The man told he would be *a fugitive and a wanderer* builds a walled town and puts a family
name on it. The first city in Scripture is founded by a man running from a sentence.

## What the line produces

- **Jabal** — the father of those who live in tents and keep livestock
- **Jubal** — the father of all who play the harp and flute
- **Tubal-Cain** — who forged tools of bronze and iron

Herding, music, metalwork. Genesis credits the arts and the industries of the world to Cain's
descendants and does not sneer at any of it.

What is missing from the list is not culture. It is God. Nobody in these eight verses calls on
His name.

## The boast

*"Adah and Zillah, hear my voice. **I have killed a man for wounding me**, a young man for
injuring me."*

The first recorded poem in the Bible, and it is a song about a killing. He performs it to his
two wives.

*Wounding* and *injuring* — the injuries are less than the answer.

## The arithmetic

*"If Cain is avenged seven times, then **Lamech seventy-seven**."*

God had set a mark on Cain to stop the killing. Lamech takes the number that protected his
ancestor and multiplies it into a threat.

Five generations from Eden, and the family's grasp of grace has become a scale of revenge.

## Where the number turns up again

*Then Peter came to Him and said, "Lord, how often shall my brother sin against me, and I
forgive him? Up to seven times?" Jesus said to him, "I do not say to you, up to seven times,
but up to **seventy times seven**."*

The same figure, in the same shape, running the opposite way.

Lamech counted to seventy-seven and meant vengeance. Jesus counted to seventy-seven and meant
forgiveness, and then told a parable about a man forgiven a debt he could never have paid. See
the note on Matthew 18:23–35.

## The other Lamech

There are two men of that name in Genesis, one in each line. This one boasts about a killing.
The other names his son Noah, saying *this one will comfort us*. See the note on
Genesis 5:25–32.

Two Lamechs, two songs, and the next chapter follows the one who wanted comfort.

## What the chapter does next

*And Seth also had a son, and he named him Enosh. **Then men began to call on the name of the
LORD**.*

The very next verse after the boast.

> **The same number, twice.** Lamech turned the mark of protection on Cain into a scale for
> revenge and set it to seventy-seven. Jesus was asked how far forgiveness goes and gave the
> same figure.
