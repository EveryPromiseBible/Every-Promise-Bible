Five words in Hebrew, quoted three times in the New Testament.

## What the book is

An argument. Habakkuk complains and God answers, twice.

*O LORD, how long shall I cry, and You will not hear?*

The answer is that the Babylonians are coming — *that bitter and hasty nation* — which makes
the second complaint worse than the first: *why do You look on those who deal treacherously,
and hold Your tongue when the wicked devours a person more righteous than he?*

## Where he goes to wait

*I will stand my watch and set myself on the rampart, and **watch to see what He will say to
me**.*

He climbs the wall like a sentry and waits for an answer.

## The instruction

*"**Write the vision and make it plain on tablets**, that he may run who reads it. For the
vision is yet for an appointed time… **though it tarries, wait for it**; because it will surely
come, it will not tarry."*

Large letters, so a man running past can read it.

## The contrast

*Look at the proud one: **he is swollen with himself, and nothing in him is straight**.*

The Hebrew is **uppelah** — puffed up, swollen. And *his soul is not upright in him*.

## The line

*But **the righteous will live by his faith**.*

The Hebrew is **vetsaddiq be'emunato yichyeh**.

**Emunah** — faithfulness, steadiness, reliability. The word used of Moses' hands held up until
sunset: *and his hands were steady until the going down of the sun*.

It carries both senses at once: trusting, and staying trusting.

## Where it goes

Paul, in Romans: *for in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith; as it is
written, "**the just shall live by faith**."* See the note on Romans 1:16–17.

Paul, in Galatians: *that no one is justified by the law in the sight of God is evident, for
"the just shall live by faith."*

And Hebrews, at the end of the tenth chapter, leading straight into the faith chapter: *now the
just shall live by faith; but if anyone draws back, My soul has no pleasure in him*.

Three quotations, three different arguments, one sentence from a minor prophet.

## The Reformation

Luther's account of his own turning point is about Romans 1:17 and this line behind it.

The three great slogans — by faith alone, by grace alone, by Scripture alone — all have this
clause somewhere underneath them.

Five words, written large on a tablet so a runner could read them.

## The rest of the passage

*Wine betrays him. The arrogant man is **never still**. He **opens his throat as wide as the
grave**. Like death, **he is never satisfied**.*

The Hebrew for *the grave* is **She'ol**.

*He gathers every nation to himself and sweeps up all the peoples as his own.*

An empire described as an appetite. Proverbs uses the same image of the grave: *Sheol and
Abaddon are never full*.

## The five woes

What follows is a series of them, aimed at the conqueror: woe to him who increases what is not
his, who covets evil gain, who builds a town with bloodshed, who gives drink to his neighbour,
and who says to wood *awake!*

And in the middle: *the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, as
the waters cover the sea*.

## How the book ends

*Though the fig tree may not blossom, nor fruit be on the vines; though the labour of the olive
may fail, and the fields yield no food… **yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God
of my salvation**.*

A man who started by shouting at God finishes by listing everything that could fail and saying
he will be glad anyway.

> **What the word carries.** *Emunah* — faithfulness and trust in one word. The verse says the
> righteous man lives by it, and three New Testament writers build on the clause.
