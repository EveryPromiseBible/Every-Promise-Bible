---
key: 2 Corinthians 5:1–5
title: "The tent, the building, and the deposit"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul made tents for a living, and when he wants a word for the body he reaches for the thing his
hands knew.

## A tent is not a failure

*The earthly **tent** we are living in.*

**Skenos** — a tent, and the same root as the word John uses when he says the Word *tabernacled*
among us. A tent is not a botched house. It is exactly the right structure for people who are
travelling, and it is meant to come down.

Against it Paul sets **oikodome** — a building — *a house **not made with human hands**, standing
forever in the heavens.*

**Acheiropoietos**, not hand-made, is one of the New Testament's quiet categories: the stone cut out
of the mountain without hands in Daniel, the temple Jesus was accused of promising, the
circumcision made without hands in Colossians. Everything in that category was made by God without
our assistance, and everything in it lasts.

## The groaning is allowed

*And in this tent we **groan**.*

**Stenazomen** — the same verb Paul uses twice in Romans 8, where the creation groans and we groan
along with it and the Spirit groans in us with sounds that will not go into words.

So the ache is not unbelief. It is the correct response of someone built for a house and currently
living under canvas.

## Not stripped — covered

*Not because we want to be left with nothing on, but because we want to be **covered over**.*

**Ependysasthai** — to put a garment on *over* what is already worn. Paul is not longing to escape
the body. He wants the tent absorbed into the building, the way a coat goes over a shirt without
the shirt being taken off first.

*So that everything in us that dies may be **swallowed up by life**.* **Katapothe** — gulped down.
The same verb stands in the resurrection chapter of his other letter, where *death is swallowed up
in victory*, and behind both is Isaiah's promise that God *will swallow up death forever*.

Notice the direction. Life does the swallowing.

## The deposit

*And God Himself is the One who **shaped us for this very thing**, and He has given us the Spirit as
the **down payment** on it.*

**Arrabon** — a first instalment that legally binds the rest of the sale. Not a token of goodwill; a
part-payment, of the same substance as the whole, handed over so that the buyer is committed.

In modern Greek the word survives as the engagement ring.

> **He made you for it, and He has already paid something down.** The longing is not a design fault
> — *He has prepared us for this very thing*. And the Spirit in you is not a consolation while you
> wait for the real thing; He is the first instalment of it, paid in the same currency, by a God
> who does not forfeit deposits. Jesus rose in a body, and the guarantee that you will too is
> already living in you.
