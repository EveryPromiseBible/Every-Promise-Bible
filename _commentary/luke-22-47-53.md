The last miracle before the cross is performed on a man who came to arrest Him.

## The interruption

*He was **still speaking** when a crowd arrived.*

He had just said *rise and pray, lest you enter into temptation*, having found them asleep.

## The description

*And the man called **Judas, one of the Twelve**, was walking in front of them.*

The Greek is **heis ton dodeka** — one of the twelve.

Every Gospel adds that phrase. None of them lets it pass.

## The sign

*He came up close to Jesus **to kiss Him**.*

A kiss on the cheek was the ordinary greeting between a disciple and his rabbi.

Matthew records the arrangement: *whomever I kiss, He is the One; seize Him*.

## The question

*"**Judas — with a kiss you hand over the Son of Man?**"*

The Greek is **philemati ton huion tou anthropou paradidos** — with a kiss you betray the Son of
Man?

He uses his name.

Matthew records the other address: *friend, why have you come?* — **hetaire**, the same word used
of the complaining labourer in the vineyard. See the note on Matthew 20:13–16.

## The disciples

*The men around Him saw what was coming and said, "**Lord, should we strike with the sword?**"*

They ask.

And then somebody does not wait for the answer.

## The strike

*And one of them **did strike the high priest's servant and cut off his right ear**.*

John names them both: *Simon Peter, having a sword, drew it and struck the high priest's servant,
and cut off his right ear. **The servant's name was Malchus**.*

Luke, a physician, notes which ear.

## What he was aiming at

Not an ear.

## The command

*Jesus said, "**Stop. No more of this**."*

The Greek is **eate heos toutou** — permit as far as this, let it be to this point.

## The healing

*And **He touched the man's ear and healed him**.*

Luke alone records it.

The last recorded miracle before the crucifixion is performed on a man in the arresting party, to
undo damage done by a disciple.

## What Matthew records Him saying

*"Put your sword in its place, for **all who take the sword will perish by the sword**."*

*"Or do you think that I cannot now pray to My Father, and **He will provide Me with more than
twelve legions of angels**?"*

A legion was six thousand. He names the number available and does not ask.

## The rebuke

*"**Am I a bandit, that you come for Me with swords and clubs?**"*

The Greek is **lesten** — a robber, a brigand. The same word used of the two crucified with Him.

*"Day after day I was there with you in the temple, and **you never laid a hand on Me**."*

They had every opportunity, in daylight, in public. They came at night with a mob.

## The last line

*"But **this is your hour, and the reign of darkness**."*

The Greek is **he exousia tou skotous** — the authority of darkness.

## What He said the night before this

*"The hour has come that the Son of Man should be glorified."*

Two hours are named in this Gospel. He calls one His and one theirs.

## Where the darkness goes

*Now it was about the sixth hour, and **there was darkness over all the earth until the ninth
hour**.*

Noon to three. See the note on Luke 23:44–46.

> **The last miracle before the cross.** An ear, restored on a man who had come to make the arrest,
> after a disciple asked whether to use the sword and then used it.
