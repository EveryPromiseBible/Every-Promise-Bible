---
key: 1 Corinthians 12:15–20
title: "The foot that cannot resign"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul gives two body parts a speech, and both of them say the same thing: *I do not belong.* His
answer is not encouragement. It is a fact about where they are.

## The argument that does not work

*If the foot should say, "Because I am not a hand, I do not belong to the body," **it does not for
that reason stop belonging to the body**.*

Then the ear says it about the eye, and gets the same answer.

Notice what Paul does not do. He does not tell the foot it is valuable, or ask it to think better
of itself, or produce a list of things feet are good at. He tells it that its opinion has no
jurisdiction.

Membership is not maintained by the member's estimate of itself. You cannot resign from a body by
comparing yourself unfavourably with somebody in it.

## Which two he chose

Foot and ear — and each measures itself against the part that is nearest and most obviously
superior. The foot looks at the hand: same joints, same fingers-and-toes design, and one of them
gets to touch faces and hold pens while the other is in a shoe. The ear looks at the eye: same side
of the head, and one of them gets sunsets.

Paul understands that comparison always happens close to home. Nobody envies the far country; they
envy the person one seat along.

## Who did the placing

*But now **God has placed** the parts, each one of them, in the body **just as it pleased Him**.*

**Etheto** — He set them, appointed them, put them in position. And **kathos ethelesen** — as He
willed, as it pleased Him.

That is the same construction as the great sentence at the front of Ephesians: *having predestined
us to adoption as sons by Jesus Christ to Himself, **according to the good pleasure of His will***.

So placement is a matter of His pleasure, not of the member's usefulness. The foot is where it is
because He liked it there.

And the head of this body is named later in the same letter and again in Ephesians: *He put all
things under His feet, and gave Him to be head over all things to the church, which is His body*.
A body with that head does not need its feet to have a case for themselves.

> **You cannot argue your way out.** The foot's speech is theologically sound, emotionally honest,
> and completely without effect. It is not a hand. It never will be. And it is in the body — because
> Christ put it there, and because it was joined to Him by one Spirit and not by an application.
> *He does not for that reason cease to be part of the body.* Paul says it twice, in case the ear
> was not listening.
