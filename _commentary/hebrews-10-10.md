One sentence, and every phrase in it is load-bearing.

## What comes before

A quotation from Psalm 40, put in Christ's mouth:

*"Sacrifice and offering You did not desire, **but a body You have prepared for Me**… Behold, I
have come — in the volume of the book it is written of Me — **to do Your will, O God**."* See
the note on Psalms 40:6–8.

And the writer's comment: *He takes away the first that He may establish the second*.

## The will

*And it is **by that will of His** that we have been made holy.*

The Greek is **en ho thelemati** — in which will.

Not by an achievement. By a decision, and the decision quoted two verses earlier is *I have
come to do Your will*.

## The verb

*We **have been made holy**.*

The Greek is **hegiasmenoi esmen** — a perfect passive periphrastic. Having been sanctified, and
standing in that state.

Perfect tense: done, and the result continues.

Passive: done to us.

## The means

*Through **the body of Jesus Christ**.*

Not His teaching, not His example. The body — the one the psalm said was prepared.

## The frequency

*Offered **one time and never again**.*

The Greek is **ephapax** — once for all.

The letter's key word. It uses it three times: *He did this once for all when He offered up
Himself*, *He entered the Most Holy Place once for all*, and here.

## Why frequency is the argument

The whole chapter runs on it.

*For the law, having a shadow of the good things to come… can never with these same sacrifices,
which they offer continually year by year, make those who approach perfect.*

*For then would they not have ceased to be offered? **For the worshippers, once purified, would
have had no more consciousness of sins**.* See the note on Hebrews 10:1–2.

The annual repetition was the proof that it had not worked. A cure that has to be re-administered
every year is not a cure.

*But in those sacrifices there is a **reminder of sins every year**.*

The Day of Atonement was an annual memo.

## The contrast in posture

*And every priest **stands** ministering daily and offering repeatedly the same sacrifices,
which can never take away sins.*

*But this Man, after He had offered one sacrifice for sins forever, **sat down** at the right
hand of God.* See the note on Hebrews 10:11–13.

There were no chairs in the tabernacle. The work was never finished, so nobody sat.

## What follows

*For by one offering **He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified**.*

Two tenses in one clause. Perfected — perfect tense, finished. Being sanctified — present, in
progress.

Both are true of the same people at the same time, and the letter puts them in the same
sentence without embarrassment.

## The witness

*But the Holy Spirit also witnesses to us; for after He had said before, "**This is the covenant
that I will make with them… I will put My laws into their hearts**"… then He adds, "**Their sins
and their lawless deeds I will remember no more**."*

*Now where there is remission of these, **there is no longer an offering for sin**.*

The argument closes itself. If the record is not kept, the offering has no function.

## What the chapter does with it

*Therefore, brethren, having boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus… **let us draw
near**.* See the note on Hebrews 10:22.

> **Why the number matters.** A sacrifice repeated annually is an annual reminder that it did
> not work. The letter's whole case rests on one word: *ephapax*.
