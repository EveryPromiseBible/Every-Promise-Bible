The famous line is quoted as a general warning about secret sin. In its own
chapter it means something much more specific.

## The condition

Moses agrees to the request, with terms:

- arm yourselves and cross the Jordan with everyone else
- stay until the LORD has driven out His enemies and the land is subdued
- **then** come back, free of obligation, and this land is yours

The settlement on the east bank is allowed. What is not allowed is settling
there while the rest of the nation fights.

## What the warning is about

*But if you fail to do this, you will be sinning against the LORD, and know for
certain your sin will find you out.*

The sin named is **not going**. Leaving your brothers to take the land while you
graze cattle in safety. That is the thing that would find them out.

It is a promise-keeping verse, not a hidden-sin verse. Moses is telling men who
have just made a commitment that failing to keep it will catch up with them.

## What they promised

*Every one of us who can bear arms will cross over to fight before the LORD, as
you have said.* Wives, children, flocks and herds stay; the fighting men go.

And they kept it. Joshua 22 records Joshua sending them home years later with a
blessing, having done exactly what they said — *you have not deserted your
brothers over this long time.*

> **What it cost them anyway.** Their land, being east of the river, was the first
> territory Israel lost. Reuben and Gad are the tribes carried off earliest in
> the Assyrian invasions. They kept the promise and they still ended up living on
> the exposed side of the border, which is the quiet ending the chapter never
> spells out.
