A man who has just been set free asks to come along, and it is the one request
of that kind in the Gospels that gets turned down.

## The refusal

*As Jesus was getting back into the boat, the man who had been set free asked if
he could come along with Him. But Jesus would not let him.*

Everyone else is called to follow. This man asks to and is sent home.

He had been living among the tombs, cutting himself with stones, so strong that
chains would not hold him. Now he is dressed and in his right mind and wants to
get in the boat, and Jesus says no.

## What he is sent to do

*"Go home, to your own people, and tell them what the Lord has done for you, and
how much mercy He had on you."*

Two words carry the commission: **what the Lord has done**, and **mercy**. No
training, no doctrine, no instruction on what to say beyond his own story.

He had been an eyewitness for about an hour.

## Where he went

*So the man went off and began telling it all through the Decapolis, the ten
towns of that region.*

He was told to go home to his own people. He covered ten cities.

This is Gentile territory — the region where the pigs were, where the locals had
just asked Jesus to leave. The first missionary to the Decapolis is a man with no
qualifications except a before and an after.

> **What happened later.** When Jesus returns to that area in Mark 7 and 8, the
> reception is completely different — crowds come to Him and He feeds four
> thousand there. Nobody says why. But somebody had been telling that region what
> the Lord had done for him for the intervening months.
