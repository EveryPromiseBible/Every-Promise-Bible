A father tells his son to keep two things close, and describes where to keep
them.

## The two

*Do not let love and faithfulness leave you.*

The Hebrew pair is **hesed and emet** — covenant love and dependability. It is one
of the standard descriptions of God's own character, the words He speaks over
Himself when He passes by Moses on the mountain.

So the son is being told to wear what God is like.

## The two places

*Tie them around your neck, write them on the tablet of your heart.*

Outside and inside. Around the neck is visible — an ornament, or the cord a seal
hung on. On the tablet of the heart is where nobody else sees.

Both, and the order matters: the visible one is named first, but the inward one
is the permanent one. Jeremiah later promises a covenant written on hearts rather
than stone, using the same image.

## What is promised

*They will add to your days, long years and a peaceful life… you will find favour
and good name in the sight of God and everyone.*

Favour with both — God and people. Luke uses almost exactly this phrasing of
Jesus growing up in Nazareth: increasing in favour with God and with people.

## The condition

*Do not forget my teaching, let your heart hold on to my commands.*

Not *learn* — do not forget. The instruction assumes it has already been given.
Much of Proverbs is a father repeating what a son has heard before, on the
understanding that knowing something and keeping hold of it are different jobs.

> **What comes three verses later.** *Trust in the LORD with all your heart and do
> not lean on your own understanding.* This paragraph is the run-up to it, and it
> is worth reading them together: hold on to what you were taught, and do not
> rely on your own reading of the situation.
