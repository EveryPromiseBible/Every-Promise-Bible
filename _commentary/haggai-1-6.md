Five clauses, and every one of them is about effort that does not arrive anywhere.

## When it was said

*In the second year of King Darius, in the sixth month, on the first day of the month.*

Haggai dates everything to the day. 29 August 520 BC, by the usual reckoning.

The exiles have been back from Babylon for about sixteen years. They laid the temple foundation
and then stopped.

## What they were saying

*"**The time has not come**, the time that the LORD's house should be built."*

Not a refusal. A postponement.

## What God says about that

*"Is it time for you yourselves to dwell in your panelled houses, **and this temple to lie in
ruins**?"*

Panelled — the Hebrew is **sephunim**, the word used of Solomon's cedar work.

Not shelter. Finish work.

## The five

*You have **planted much and gathered little**.*

*You **eat, but you are never full**.*

*You **drink, but you are never satisfied**.*

*You **put on clothes, but you never feel warm**.*

*You **earn wages, only to pour them into a purse full of holes**.*

The Hebrew of the last is *he who earns wages, earns wages to put into a bag with holes*.

## The shape of the list

Every clause is a completed action followed by a missing result.

The planting happened. The eating happened. The wages were earned. Nothing is said about
laziness or failure of effort.

The problem is that the output does not stay.

## The pattern behind it

Deuteronomy 28 lists it among the curses: *you shall carry much seed out to the field but gather
little in… you shall have olive trees throughout all your territory, but you shall not anoint
yourself with the oil*.

Haggai is quoting the covenant document at people who knew it.

## The diagnosis

*"You expected much, and it came to little. **What you brought home, I blew away**. And why?…
**Because My house lies in ruins while each of you hurries home to his own**."* See the note on
Haggai 1:9–11.

The Hebrew is *you run, each man, to his own house*.

## What the prophet asks for

*"**Consider your ways**."*

He says it twice in the chapter, and again later. The Hebrew is *set your heart on your ways*.

Not *repent* in the abstract. Look at the pattern.

## What they did

*Then Zerubbabel… and Joshua… **with all the remnant of the people, obeyed the voice of the
LORD their God**… and they came and worked on the house of the LORD of hosts, their God.*

Twenty-three days later. Haggai dates that too.

## What he says next

*"**I am with you**," says the LORD.*

Five words, and they come before any of the work is done.

## And the promise about the building

*"The glory of this latter temple shall be greater than the former… and in this place I will
give peace."*

Said about a building the old men had wept over because it was so much smaller than Solomon's.

## Where the same warning goes

*"Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves
break in and steal."*

And the barn-builder: *"Fool! This night your soul will be required of you; then whose will
those things be which you have provided?"*

> **What each clause records.** The work was done. Planted, eaten, earned — and then the result
> is missing. Nothing on the list is about not trying.
