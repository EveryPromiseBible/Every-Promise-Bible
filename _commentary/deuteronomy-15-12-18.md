key: Deuteronomy 15:12–18
title: The awl at the doorpost
updated: 2026-08-09
---
A law about releasing servants, and the exception at the end of it is one of the clearest
pictures of Christ in the Torah.

## The release

*"If a fellow Hebrew, a man or a woman, sells himself to you and serves you six years, in the
seventh year **you must set him free**."*

Six years, and out. The debt does not matter, the sum does not matter, and the arithmetic is
not consulted.

## How he leaves

*"And when you set him free, **do not send him away empty-handed**. Load him up generously
from your flock, your threshing floor, your winepress."*

The Hebrew is **ha'aneiq ta'aniq** — you shall surely adorn his neck. The verb is doubled, and
it comes from the word for a necklace.

He arrives with nothing and leaves with a share of the flock, the grain and the wine.

## The reason

*"**Remember that you were once slaves in Egypt, and the LORD your God redeemed you.** That is
why I am giving you this command."*

They had walked out of Egypt loaded with silver and gold they had not earned. See the note on
Exodus 12:33–36.

The law is telling them to do to their servants what was done to them.

## The exception

*"But if your servant says to you, '**I don't want to leave you**,' **because he loves you and
your household and is happy with you**."*

The only ground given is love. Not debt, not fear, not lack of anywhere to go — the law names
three affections and no obligations.

He is legally free. The six years are up and the door is open.

## What is done

*"Then take **an awl and pierce his ear against the door**, and he will be your servant for
life."*

Against the doorpost of the house — the place the blood went on the night of the Passover.

An ear opened, at a door, by somebody who had every right to walk away.

## Where the picture is picked up

*Sacrifice and offering You did not desire; **My ears You have opened**… Then I said, "Behold,
I come; in the scroll of the book it is written of Me. I delight to do Your will, O my God."*

Psalm 40, and Hebrews quotes it of Christ — using the Greek version, which renders the opened
ear as *a body You have prepared for Me*.

*Behold, I have come to do Your will, O God.*

## The word used of Him

*Who, being in the form of God… **took the form of a bondservant**.*

The Greek is **doulos** — a slave. Not a hired man and not a servant by contract.

And what He says to the men He is leaving: *no longer do I call you servants… **I have called
you friends**.*

The Master became the bondservant, and the bondservants were called friends. It runs both ways
in the same night.

## The last line of the law

*"Do not think it a hard thing to set your servant free… **and the LORD your God will bless
you in everything you do**."*

> **What the awl marks.** Not a debt. Six years are up, the man is free, and he says he does
> not want to go — and the mark is put through his ear at the door of the house he chose.
