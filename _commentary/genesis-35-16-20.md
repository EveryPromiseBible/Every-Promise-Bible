A woman dies naming her son, and her husband changes the name before she is buried.

## Where they are

*They journeyed on **from Bethel**.*

Bethel is where Jacob had just built an altar and been renamed Israel a second time. The
family is on the road south.

## The labour

*Rachel went into labor — and it was **a hard, bitter labor**.*

The Hebrew is *she had hard labour*, and the word is repeated in the next verse for
emphasis.

## What she had asked for

Years earlier: *give me children, or else I die.* And when Joseph was born: *the LORD shall
add to me another son* — which is what **Joseph** means.

She got the second son and it cost her.

## The midwife

*"**Do not be afraid; you have another son**."*

The last words spoken to her are the answer to the prayer she had prayed.

## Her name for him

*With her last breath she named him **Ben-oni, "son of my sorrow."**"*

The Hebrew **oni** is sorrow, trouble, affliction. The same root as the name Jabez carried.
See the note on 1 Chronicles 4:9–10.

She names him after what it cost.

## His father's name for him

*But **his father called him Benjamin, "son of my right hand."**"*

**Ben-yamin.** The right hand is the place of strength and of favour.

Jacob does not argue with what she saw. He gives the boy a different name and it is the one
that stands.

The last name in Scripture is given by the father.

## The burial

*So Rachel died and was **buried on the road to Ephrath, that is, Bethlehem**.*

Not in the family tomb at Machpelah, where Sarah, Abraham, Isaac, Rebekah and Leah are. She
is buried at the roadside where she died.

**Ephrath is Bethlehem.** The narrator adds it so nobody misses where.

## The pillar

*Jacob set up a pillar over her grave — **the marker of Rachel's tomb, standing there to
this day**.*

## Where the tomb turns up again

Jeremiah: *a voice was heard in Ramah, lamentation and bitter weeping, **Rachel weeping for
her children**, refusing to be comforted, because they are no more.*

And Matthew quotes it — over Bethlehem, after Herod's soldiers.

## What Jacob says about it at the end of his life

Fifty years later, blessing Joseph's sons in Egypt: *but as for me, when I came from Padan,
Rachel died beside me… and **I buried her there on the way to Ephrath, that is,
Bethlehem***.

He is blind and dying and still mentions the roadside.

## Where Benjamin goes

He becomes the youngest of the twelve, the one Jacob will not let out of his sight, and the
one the whole of the Joseph reunion turns on. See the notes on Genesis 43:1–7 and
Genesis 44:1–13.

Paul was of the tribe of Benjamin, and says so twice.

> **The two names.** One given by a dying woman out of what it cost her, and one given by
> the father over the same child — and Genesis records both and then only ever uses the
> second.
