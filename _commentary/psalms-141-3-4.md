Having asked for his prayer to be received as incense, David asks for a sentry on the
place it comes out of.

## The request

*Set **a guard**, LORD, **over my mouth**.*

The Hebrew is **shomrah** — a watch, a guard post. The word appears once in the Bible,
here.

He is not asking to be silenced. He is asking for a sentry.

## The door

*Keep watch at **the door of my lips**.*

The Hebrew for *door* is **dal** — the leaf of a door, the part that swings.

A door is not a wall. It opens and shuts, and the point of a guard on it is that some
things go through and some do not.

## Who he asks

Not himself. *Set a guard, LORD.*

The chapter before, in Psalm 140, he is describing men *who sharpen their tongues like a
serpent*. He knows what a mouth does.

## What comes before it

*Let my prayer stand before You **like incense**, let my lifted hands be the evening
offering.* See the note on Psalms 141:1–2.

The order is deliberate. He asks for his words to be treated as temple offering, and the
next thing he asks is that a guard be posted where they come from.

## The second request

*Do not let **my heart turn toward evil**.*

The mouth in verse 3, the heart in verse 4. He moves inward.

The Hebrew is *do not incline my heart* — the same verb used of stretching out or
bending toward something.

## What he is afraid of

*Or **busy itself with wrongdoing**, **sharing the feast of those who do harm**.*

The Hebrew is *to practise wicked deeds with men who work iniquity*.

The danger named is not solitary. It is joining in.

## The table

*Let me **never taste their delicacies**.*

The Hebrew for *delicacies* is **manammim** — dainties, pleasant things.

He does not describe their company as repulsive. He calls the food good and asks not to
eat it.

## What he asks for instead, in the next verse

*Let the righteous strike me; it shall be a kindness. And let him rebuke me; it shall be
excellent oil; let my head not refuse it.*

He would rather be hit by an honest man than fed by a dishonest one, and he calls the
blow oil on the head.

## Where the psalm was written from

The title is *a psalm of David*, and the last verses are a man asking to be kept out of
a net set for him.

## What the New Testament asks for

*Let your words always be gracious, with salt in them.* See the note on
Colossians 4:5–6.

And James: *if anyone does not stumble in word, he is a perfect man, able also to
bridle the whole body.*

> **What is being asked.** Not for the door to be sealed. For somebody to be standing at
> it — and the request comes from a man who had just asked for what goes through it to
> be received as incense.
