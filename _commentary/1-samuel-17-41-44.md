The giant's only recorded speech to David, and it is four sentences of contempt.

## The approach

**17:41** *The Philistine **came steadily closer**, his **shield-bearer walking ahead**.*

The chapter has already described the equipment: a bronze helmet, a coat of mail weighing
five thousand shekels, bronze greaves, a bronze javelin, and, **17:7**: a spear whose *staff was like
a weaver's beam*.

And a man in front of him carrying the shield, because the armour is too heavy to carry a
shield as well.

## The look

**17:42** *When he **looked David over** and saw **only a boy, ruddy and handsome**, he **sneered at
him**.*

The Hebrew is *he disdained him*.

**Ruddy and handsome** is the same description Samuel was given at the anointing. See the
note on 1 Samuel 16:11–13.

The thing that marked him out to the prophet is the thing that makes the giant laugh.

## The question

**17:43** *"**Am I a dog**," he said, "**that you come at me with sticks?**"*

David had a staff, a sling, and five stones from the brook.

The plural — *sticks* — is contempt for the whole equipment.

**Dog** was the strongest insult in that culture, and it is the giant applying it to
himself as a measure of the offence.

## The cursing

**17:43** again: *And he **cursed David by his gods**.*

## The threat

**17:44** *"**Come here**, and **I'll give your flesh to the birds and the wild animals**."*

## What David says back

The next verses, and they are the reply the chapter is built for:

**17:45** *"**You come to me with a sword, with a spear, and with a javelin. But I come to you in the
name of the LORD of hosts**, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied."*

**17:46–47** *"**This day the LORD will deliver you into my hand**… and all this assembly shall know that
**the LORD does not save with sword and spear; for the battle is the LORD's**."*

And the threat is returned, expanded: *I will give the carcasses of the camp of the
Philistines this day to the birds of the air and the wild beasts.* See the note on
1 Samuel 17:45–47.

## What the armies had been doing

Forty days. Goliath came out morning and evening and shouted, and, **17:11**: *when Saul and all Israel
heard those words, they were dismayed and greatly afraid*.

Nobody had answered for nearly six weeks.

## Who was in the camp

Saul, who was, **1 Samuel 9:2**: *taller than any of the people from his shoulders upward* — the one man
physically closest to the challenge — offering rewards to anyone else who would go.

## What David had already told Saul

**17:34–37** *"Your servant has killed both lion and bear… **the LORD, who delivered me from the paw of
the lion and from the paw of the bear, He will deliver me** from the hand of this
Philistine."*

He argues from a record nobody witnessed, made while looking after sheep.

## The armour

Saul put his own armour on him and David took it off, **17:39**: *I cannot walk with these, for I have
not tested them.*

He goes out in what he came in.

> **What the giant assessed.** Correctly, as far as it went — a boy with a stick. The reply
> David gives does not dispute the inventory; it names a different party to the fight.
