Two disciples propose burning a village down, and they have a Bible verse for it.

## Why the village refused Him

*The village would not receive Him, because He was clearly heading for
Jerusalem.*

Samaritans worshipped on Mount Gerizim and regarded Jerusalem's temple as
illegitimate. A pilgrim on the road there was a walking insult.

The refusal was religious, and it was rude.

## The proposal

*Lord, do You want us to call fire down out of heaven and burn them up, **the way
Elijah did**?*

James and John are not inventing this. Elijah had called fire down on soldiers
sent to arrest him, in that same region, and they knew the passage.

They have precedent, provocation, and permission-seeking manners. Everything is
in order except the request.

## The rebuke

*You have no idea what kind of spirit you are speaking out of. The Son of Man did
not come to destroy people's lives. He came to save them.*

He does not argue about Elijah. He tells them they have misread which era they
are standing in and what He came to do.

And the diagnosis is about the **source** of the suggestion, not its logic.

## Who these two were

The same pair who a chapter later ask for the seats on His right and left.
Jesus nicknamed them Sons of Thunder, and Luke keeps giving us evidence for why.

John, who wanted to incinerate a village, is the one who ends up writing *God is
love* and *we love Him because He loved us first.*

## The last line

*And they simply went on to another village.*

No confrontation, no curse, no shaking of dust recorded. The response to being
turned away is to keep walking.

> **What He had just done.** *He set His face to go to Jerusalem, and nothing
> would turn Him from it.* Luke puts that sentence three verses before this — the
> road they were refused passage on was the road to the cross. He was on His way
> to die for Samaritans, and would not let anyone burn one.
