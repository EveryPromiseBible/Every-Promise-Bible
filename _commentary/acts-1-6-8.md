They ask about a date and are handed a map.

## The question

*"Lord, **is this the time? Are You going to restore the kingdom to Israel now?**"*

The last question the disciples ask Him, and it is about politics.

After three years, a crucifixion, a resurrection and forty days of teaching about the kingdom
of God, this is what they want to know.

## What is right about it

The restoration itself is not corrected. He does not say the kingdom will not be restored to
Israel.

Paul takes it up in Romans 11: *and so all Israel will be saved*. See the note on
Romans 11:25–27.

Only the timing is refused.

## The answer

*"It is **not for you to know the times or the seasons**; the Father has set those by **His own
authority**."*

The Greek is **chronous e kairous** — durations and appointed moments. Two different words for
time, and both are off the table.

Jesus had already said it: *of that day and hour no one knows, not even the angels of heaven,
nor the Son, but only the Father*. See the note on Matthew 24:36–41.

## The turn

*"**But you are going to receive power** when the Holy Spirit comes on you."*

The Greek is **dunamin** — power, capacity. Where *dynamite* comes from, though the English
word carries the wrong sense: this is enablement rather than explosion.

The clock is the Father's business. The power is theirs.

## The purpose

*"And **you will be My witnesses**."*

The Greek is **martures** — witnesses. Where *martyr* comes from, and the word drifted to
that meaning because of what happened to the people in this room.

A witness reports what he saw. It is not a role that requires expertise, only presence.

Peter's criterion for replacing Judas, a few verses later: *one of these men who have
accompanied us… must become a witness with us of His resurrection*.

## The four places

*"In **Jerusalem**, and in all **Judea**, and in **Samaria**, and out to the **farthest edge of
the earth**."*

The table of contents for the whole book.

Chapters 1 to 7: Jerusalem.

Chapter 8: Judea and Samaria — and it takes a persecution to get them out. *They were all
scattered throughout the regions of Judea and Samaria… therefore those who were scattered went
everywhere preaching the word.*

Chapters 13 onward: outward, and the book ends in Rome, unhindered. See the note on
Acts 28:28–29.

## The one nobody wanted

Samaria. The disciples had once offered to call down fire on a Samaritan village.

It is on the list, in the middle, and Philip goes there in chapter 8.

## What happens immediately

*Now when He had spoken these things, while they watched, **He was taken up, and a cloud
received Him out of their sight**.*

And two men in white: *"Men of Galilee, why do you stand gazing up into heaven? **This same
Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will so come in like manner** as you saw Him go
into heaven."*

They ask about the timing and are told to stop looking at the sky.

## What Luke says the book is about

*The former account I made, O Theophilus, of all that Jesus **began** both to do and teach.*

Volume one is what He began. Acts is the continuation.

> **What He substitutes for the date.** Power, a job description, and four place names — and
> the last of them has no edge to it.
