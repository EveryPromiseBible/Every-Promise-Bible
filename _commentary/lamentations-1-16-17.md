Five times in the first poem the city says nobody is comforting her, and the word matters.

## The refrain

*Because **the comforter who could revive me is far away**… Zion reaches out her hands, but
**there is no one to comfort her**.*

The Hebrew is **menachem** — a comforter, from **nacham**, to comfort, to console, to sigh with.

Chapter 1 says it in verses 2, 9, 16, 17 and 21. It is the poem's refrain: *she has none to
comfort her.*

## What the comforter would do

*The comforter who could **revive** me.*

The Hebrew is **meshiv naphshi** — one who restores my soul.

The phrase is the psalm's: *He restores my soul.* See the note on Psalms 23:1–3.

Lamentations 1 is Psalm 23 with the shepherd missing.

## The hands

*Zion **reaches out her hands**, but there is no one.*

**Pershah tsiyyon beyadeha** — Zion spreads out her hands. It is the posture of prayer and the
posture of a person asking to be held.

*"**All day long I have stretched out My hands** to a disobedient and contrary people."*

**Paul** quotes Isaiah at the end of **Romans** 10, and the hands are the other way round.

## Who was promised

*"And I will pray the Father, and He will give you **another Helper**, that He may abide with you
forever."*

**Parakletos** — one called alongside. **John** 14, and the Greek Old Testament's word for the
missing comforter in this passage is from the same family: **parakalon**.

*"**I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you.**"*

Said in the same paragraph, to men about to be as alone as Zion was.

## And the one who had none

*Then **all the disciples forsook Him and fled**.*

*"Could you not watch with Me one hour?"*

*I looked for someone to take pity, but there was none; **and for comforters, but I found none**.*

Psalm 69, quoted in the gospels four times about the crucifixion. The refrain of Lamentations 1 —
*no one to comfort* — is said in the first person by somebody in Psalm 69, and **John** 19 has the
vinegar from that psalm handed up on a sponge.

## What is promised at the end

*"Blessed are those who mourn, for **they shall be comforted**."*

**Paraklethesontai**. And *the God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our tribulation, that we
may be able to comfort those who are in any trouble.*

> **The refrain.** Five times in one poem: nobody is comforting her. The Greek word the New
> Testament uses for the Holy Spirit is built from the same root as the word she is missing.
