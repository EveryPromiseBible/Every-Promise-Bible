He takes a commandment everyone in the crowd had kept, and moves it somewhere
nobody can keep it.

## The formula

*"**You have heard that it was said**… **But I say to you**."*

He uses it six times in this chapter. Nobody had ever spoken that way about the
law — the scribes taught by citing older rabbis, and He cites nobody.

## What He does with it

The commandment was *do not murder*. Almost every man on that hillside had a clean
record on it.

*"But I say to you, anyone who **nurses anger** against his brother… anyone who says
'You are worthless'… anyone who says 'You fool'."*

The offence moves from the hand to the temper, and from the temper to a word said
under the breath.

## Which direction the standard is going

The Sermon on the Mount does not lower the bar to something manageable. It raises
it past the reach of anybody in the audience — and He is only getting started;
adultery goes the same way four verses later. See the note on Matthew 5:27–30.

That is what the passage is for. The law was never a ladder; it is a mirror. Paul
says so: *by the law is the knowledge of sin*, and *the law was our tutor to bring
us to Christ.*

A man who thinks he has kept it has not read it closely enough. Jesus reads it
closely.

## The gift at the altar

*"If you are bringing your gift to the altar, and right there you remember that
**your brother has something against you**, leave your gift where it is."*

Note the direction. Not that you have something against him — that he has something
against you.

You are the one who is owed nothing, and you are the one told to leave.

*Leave your gift where it is, in front of the altar. Go.* The offering waits on the
stone while a man walks out of the temple to find somebody.

## Settle quickly

*"Settle it with him quickly, **while the two of you are still on the road
together**."*

The picture is two men walking to court. There is a stretch of road before the
judge, and He says use it.

## Where this leaves the hearer

The last line is *you will not get out of there until you have paid the very last
coin.*

There is no relief anywhere in the passage. That is deliberate — the Sermon closes
with a house falling flat, and the whole of it drives to one place: a man who
cannot produce this righteousness, and needs one given.

> **What He said just before starting.** *Unless your righteousness exceeds the
> righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees, you will by no means enter the kingdom
> of heaven.* The most meticulous men alive, and He says you need better than
> theirs. Then six paragraphs showing what better looks like.
