Jesus opens His public ministry by reading a passage aloud and stopping in the
middle of a sentence.

## What He read

Isaiah 61, and the list is specific:

- good news to the poor
- healing for the brokenhearted
- release for the prisoners
- sight for the blind
- freedom for everyone being crushed
- **the year of the Lord's favour**

That is the job description He chose for Himself, out of the whole scroll, on the
first day.

## Where He stopped

Isaiah's sentence does not end at *the year of the Lord's favour*. It continues:
*and the day of vengeance of our God.*

Jesus reads to the comma, rolls up the scroll, hands it back and sits down. The
day of vengeance is not cancelled — it simply is not what He had come to
announce that morning.

*Today, in your hearing, this Scripture has come true.*

## The year of favour

The phrase points at the Jubilee — the fiftieth year in Leviticus 25 when debts
were released, slaves went free and land returned to its family. As far as the
record shows, Israel never once kept it.

Jesus announces it as arriving in Him, on that Sabbath, in that room.

> **What the anointing was for.** *The Spirit of the Lord is on Me, because He
> has anointed Me* — and every item in the list that follows is something done
> for somebody else: the poor, the brokenhearted, the prisoners, the blind, the
> crushed. He states the anointing and then spends the rest of the Gospel
> spending it.
