Five verses of economics, and they explain why the next chapter is remarkable.

## The policy

*Now there was **not a single blacksmith to be found anywhere in Israel**.*

The Hebrew is **charash** — a craftsman in metal.

*Because the Philistines had said, "**Otherwise the Hebrews will make swords or spears**."*

Disarmament by controlling the trade. Not confiscating weapons — removing the ability to make
them.

The Philistines had iron and Israel did not. The archaeology supports it: iron working spread
along the coast before it reached the hill country.

## The dependency

*So **all Israel had to go down to the Philistines** to get their plowshares, mattocks, axes, and
sickles sharpened.*

Farm tools. Not weapons — the tools a man needs to eat.

Every farmer in the country had to walk into enemy territory once a season and pay for the
privilege.

## The prices

*The charge was **two-thirds of a shekel** for plowshares and mattocks, and **a third of a
shekel** for sharpening forks and axes and for **fixing an ox-goad**.*

The verse is famously difficult in Hebrew and the figures are reconstructions, but the shape is
clear: a price list, set by the occupying power.

## What is on the list

An ox goad.

A long pole with a metal point, for driving cattle. Shamgar killed six hundred Philistines with
one. See the note on Judges 3:31.

The tool named in the price list is the one a judge had used as a weapon.

## The tally

*So on the day of battle, **not a soldier with Saul and Jonathan had a sword or spear in his
hand — only Saul and his son Jonathan carried them**.*

Two swords in the national army.

Six hundred men, and two blades between them. The rest have farm implements.

## Why the passage is here

It sits between Saul's disqualification and Jonathan's raid.

The chapter before had the army hiding in caves, thickets, rocks, pits and cisterns. See the
note on 1 Samuel 13:5–7.

The chapter after has two men climbing a cliff.

This paragraph is the arithmetic in between, and it is set out so that nothing in chapter 14
can be attributed to equipment.

## The last line

*And **a detachment of Philistines had gone out to the pass at Michmash**.*

A garrison, in a defile, above a gorge.

That is what Jonathan and his armour-bearer climb up to the next morning. See the note on
1 Samuel 14:1–5.

## What the passage does not say

That God had abandoned them, or that the situation was hopeless.

It reports the industrial position of a small nation under occupation, accurately, and moves
on.

## Where the imagery is reversed

*They shall **beat their swords into plowshares**, and their spears into pruning hooks.*

Isaiah and Micah, both. The metal moves the other way.

Joel reverses it again for a war oracle: *beat your plowshares into swords*.

Israel in this chapter has neither, and has to pay a foreign smith to sharpen the plough.

> **What is on the Philistine price list.** An ox goad — the tool a judge had once killed six
> hundred of them with. The occupying power is charging Israel to keep it sharp.
