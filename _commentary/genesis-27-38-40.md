Esau asks a second time, and what he gets back is the first blessing with the key word
reversed.

## The question

*"**Do you have only one blessing**, my father? Bless me too, my father!"*

## The weeping

*And **Esau wept aloud**.*

The Hebrew is *Esau lifted up his voice and wept*. It is the third description of his
crying in five verses.

## What Jacob had been given

*"May God give you **of the dew of heaven, of the fatness of the earth**, and plenty of
**grain and new wine**."* See the note on Genesis 27:28–29.

Three things: dew, fatness, grain and wine.

## What Esau is given

*"Your dwelling will be **away from the richness of the earth, away from the dew of
heaven above**."*

The same two nouns, in reverse order, with one preposition changed.

The Hebrew preposition **min** can mean *of* or *away from* — Jacob got *of* the dew and
*of* the fatness; Esau's line uses the same word the other way.

It is not a lesser blessing. It is the first one with the direction turned round.

## The dew

In that country dew was not decorative. Through the rainless summer, the night moisture
off the Mediterranean was what kept crops alive.

Edom is the country south-east of the Dead Sea — rock, cliffs, and very little of it.

## The sword

*"**You will live by the sword**."*

Esau's descendants become Edom, and the Old Testament records the relationship: refusing
Israel passage in Numbers, subdued by David, in revolt under Joram, condemned in Obadiah
for standing by while Jerusalem fell.

Herod the Great was an Idumean — an Edomite — and he is the king on the throne when
Jacob's line produces the Messiah.

## The service

*"And **you will serve your brother**."*

The oracle before they were born: *the older shall serve the younger.*

## The one thing given to him

*"**But when you grow restless, you will throw his yoke from off your neck**."*

The Hebrew for *grow restless* is to become restive, to roam, to break loose.

It happened. 2 Kings records it: *in his days Edom revolted from under the hand of
Judah, and made a king over themselves.*

## What is not in either blessing

Neither son is given the covenant in this chapter. That comes two chapters later, at
Bethel, when Jacob is a fugitive sleeping on a stone: *in you and in your seed all the
families of the earth shall be blessed.*

Isaac blesses him again before he leaves, and this time it is explicit — *the blessing of
Abraham*.

## What Esau does next

*"**The days of mourning for my father are at hand; then I will kill my brother Jacob**."*

Rebekah hears of it and sends Jacob to Haran, telling Isaac it is about finding a wife.
She says *stay with him a few days*.

She never sees him again.

## Where they meet

Twenty years later, Jacob crossing back with his family, terrified, having sent droves of
livestock ahead — and *Esau ran to meet him, and embraced him, and fell on his neck and
kissed him, and they wept.*

> **How the two blessings differ.** Not in the nouns. Dew and fatness are in both — and
> the whole difference in Esau's is which side of them he lives on.
