Mark's account of the table is the shortest, and the order of the sentences
carries something the other accounts do not put as plainly.

## The order in Mark

*Then He took a cup, gave thanks, and passed it to them, **and every one of them
drank from it**. "This is My blood," He said.*

They drank first. The explanation comes after the cup is empty.

Mark is the only one who reports it in that sequence. In Matthew the words come
with the cup; here the drinking is a completed act, and then Jesus tells them
what they have just done.

Until it went into them, it was wine at a Passover table like any other. What
made it His blood was not a formula said over the cup. It was His word, and the
disciples had already swallowed it.

## Torn

*He picked up the bread. He gave thanks over it, **tore** it, and put it into
their hands.*

The Greek verb is to break, and the Illumination renders it *tore* — the tearing
of a flat loaf by hand.

Isaiah 53 says *He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our
iniquities.* The bread is broken before it is given, and the giving is
hand-to-hand: *put it into their hands.*

## Two things named, and what each is for

- **the body** — *this is My body*
- **the blood** — *the blood of the new covenant, poured out for many*

The blood is given a stated purpose in the text: the covenant, and in Matthew's
account, *for the forgiveness of sins.* The body is given without a stated
purpose here — and Matthew 8:17 supplies it: *He took our infirmities and carried
our diseases.*

Two elements, two provisions. Paul's warning in 1 Corinthians 11 about *not
discerning the body* turns on holding them together. See the note on
1 Corinthians 11:27–30.

## "Every one of them drank"

Mark records the participation. Judas is at that table in Mark's account — the
betrayal has already been announced in the paragraph above.

## The last supper is not the last

*"I will not drink of the fruit of the vine again until the day I drink it new in
the kingdom of God."*

He says it while the cup is still in His hand. The meal is left unfinished on
purpose, with a date set for the rest of it.

> **What they did next.** *Then they sang together, and went out to the Mount of
> Olives.* At Passover the closing hymn was the Hallel, Psalms 115–118 — which
> includes *I shall not die but live, and declare the works of the LORD*, and *the
> stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.* He sang that on the way
> to Gethsemane.
