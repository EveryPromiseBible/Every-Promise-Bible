The famine reaches Canaan, and Jacob has to say the obvious thing out loud.

## The situation

*When Jacob learned there was **grain in Egypt**.*

Egypt has grain because a Hebrew slave interpreted two dreams about cows and ears of corn and
was put in charge of the storage. See the note on Genesis 41:33–36.

Seven years of collecting, and the whole world comes to buy. See the note on
Genesis 41:53–57.

## The rebuke

*He said to his sons, "**Why do you just stand there looking at one another?**"*

The Hebrew is *why do you look at one another?*

Ten grown men in a house with no food, waiting for somebody to say something.

Twenty-two years have passed since they sold their brother, and they have not told their
father.

## The instruction

*"I've heard there's grain in Egypt. **Go down and buy some for us, so we can live and not
die**."*

The Hebrew is *that we may live and not die*.

## The number

*So **ten of Joseph's brothers went down**.*

Ten. Exactly the ten who were at the pit — Reuben, who tried to save him, and the nine who
agreed to the sale.

## Who stays

*But Jacob **did not send Benjamin, Joseph's brother**, along with them, for he was **afraid
something might happen to him**.*

*Joseph's brother* — his full brother, the other son of Rachel.

Jacob has already lost one son of that mother, as he believes, to a wild animal. See the note
on Genesis 37:25–28.

The Hebrew is *lest harm befall him* — **ason**, the same rare word Jacob uses again in the
next chapter and nowhere else in Scripture except once more in Exodus.

## The others in the queue

*So the sons of Israel came to buy grain **among the others who came**, because the famine had
reached the land of Canaan too.*

They are in a line with everybody else. No special treatment, no introductions.

## What is about to happen

*So his brothers came and **bowed low before him, their faces to the ground**.* See the note
on Genesis 42:6–17.

The dream from chapter 37 — the sheaves bowing — arrives in an Egyptian granary two decades
later, and only one man in the room recognises it.

## What Jacob believes

That Joseph is dead. He has believed it for twenty-two years, since the coat came back
covered in goat's blood.

*And he mourned for his son many days… "For I shall go down into the grave to my son in
mourning."*

## What the famine did

Everything in the rest of Genesis follows from it — the journeys down, the silver in the
sacks, the cup in Benjamin's, the revelation, and finally the whole family living in Goshen.

And from Goshen comes Exodus.

## The words Jacob does not know he is saying

*Go down… that we may live and not die.*

Egypt is where the family survives, and it is where they end up as slaves for four hundred
years.

Both are true and Genesis says both.

> **What Jacob had to tell them.** To stop looking at each other. Ten men in a famine,
> waiting — and the one thing that gets them moving is their father naming the obvious.
