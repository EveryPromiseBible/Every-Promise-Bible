key: Zephaniah 1:12–13
title: "I will search Jerusalem with lamps"
updated: 2026-08-09

God is described going through a city with a light, and what He is looking for is a particular kind
of complacency.

## The image

*"In that hour **I will search Jerusalem with lamps**."*

The Hebrew is **achappes** — I will search thoroughly, ransack. It is the verb for turning a house
over, used of Laban going through Jacob's tents looking for his gods.

Lamps, plural, in daylight. The picture is of somebody determined not to miss a corner.

## Who is being looked for

*"Those who **sit thick and settled over their wine**."*

The Hebrew is **haqqofe'im al-shimreihem** — those congealed on their lees.

It is a winemaking term. Wine left standing on its sediment too long goes thick and flat: it keeps,
and it stops developing. Jeremiah uses the same image of Moab — *he has settled on his dregs, and
has not been emptied from vessel to vessel.*

The people being searched for are not violent. They have simply stopped moving.

## What they say

*"Who say in their hearts, '**The LORD will do nothing, neither good nor bad**.'"*

Practical atheism rather than the theoretical kind. They have not denied that God exists. They have
concluded He is not going to act.

*"Where is the promise of His coming? For since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as
they were from the beginning of creation."*

**Peter** quotes the same position six centuries later and answers it: *the Lord is not slack
concerning His promise… but is longsuffering.*

## The futility

*"They will build fine homes and **never live in them**; they will plant lovely vineyards and **never
taste the wine**."*

The covenant curses of Deuteronomy 28, quoted almost exactly.

*"**Fool! This night your soul will be required of you; then whose will those things be which you
have provided?**"*

**Luke** 12, and the parable is Zephaniah's paragraph as a story: barns built, wine laid down, and a
man talking to himself about the future.

## The other lamp

*"Or what woman, having ten silver coins, if she loses one coin, **does not light a lamp, sweep the
house, and search carefully until she finds it**? And when she has found it, she calls her friends
and neighbours together, saying, 'Rejoice with me, for I have found the piece which I lost!'"*

**Luke** 15. The same three elements — a lamp, a house, a thorough search — and the Greek for
*search carefully* is **zetei epimelos**.

Zephaniah has God going through a city with lamps looking for people to call to account.
**Jesus** tells a story with the same equipment and the searcher is looking for something she wants
back.

He tells it to Pharisees who were complaining that He ate with sinners, and the point He draws is
about a party.

## The lamp that came into the house

*"For nothing is secret that will not be revealed, nor anything hidden that will not be known and
come to light."*

*"**I am the light of the world.**"*

*But **if we walk in the light** as He is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the
blood of Jesus Christ His Son cleanses us from all sin.*

**John** puts the searching light and the cleansing in one sentence, and does not present the first
as a threat.

## And what the book does with these people later

*"I will leave in your midst **a meek and humble people**, and they shall trust in the name of the
LORD."*

Zephaniah 3, and the ones who are left are not the ones who defended themselves.

> **The winemaking word.** *Congealed on their lees* — wine left on its sediment until it stops
> developing. The charge is not wickedness. It is having settled, and having concluded God will not
> do anything either way.
