A property law, and the reason given for it removes the concept of freehold.

## The rule

*The land is **not to be sold for good**.*

The Hebrew is **litzmituth** — in perpetuity, permanently, beyond recall.

## The reason

*Because **the land is Mine**.*

The Hebrew is **ki-li ha'aretz**.

Two words. Everything in the chapter follows from them.

## The status

*You are **only foreigners and guests on it, living there as My tenants**.*

The Hebrew is **gerim vetoshavim immadi** — sojourners and settlers with Me.

**Ger** — a resident alien. **Toshav** — a temporary resident.

The same words used of Abraham buying a burial plot: *I am a foreigner and a visitor among you*.

## What that does to the market

There is no permanent sale of land in Israel.

What is transacted is the crops until the next Jubilee, and the price is calculated on the number
of harvests remaining.

## The provision

*So throughout the land you hold, you must **always allow for the land to be redeemed**.*

The Hebrew is **ge'ullah titnu la'aretz** — you shall grant redemption for the land.

## The first route

*If a fellow Israelite becomes poor and sells some of their property, **their nearest relative is to
come and buy back what was sold**.*

The Hebrew is **go'alo haqqarov elav** — his redeemer, the one near to him.

**Go'el** — the kinsman-redeemer. The one with both the family right and the means.

## What the go'el did

Bought back sold land. Bought back a relative sold into slavery. Avenged a killed relative. And
in one book, married a widow to raise up a name on the dead man's inheritance.

## The second route

*If someone has **no one to redeem it but later prospers** and can buy it back themselves, they are
to **figure the years since the sale**, repay the balance, and return to their own property.*

The arithmetic is prescribed. Not a negotiation.

## The third

*But if they cannot repay it, what was sold **stays in the buyer's hands until the Year of
Jubilee**.*

## The Jubilee

*In the Jubilee **it is released, and the seller returns to their own property**.*

The fiftieth year. *And you shall proclaim liberty throughout all the land to all its
inhabitants.*

The Hebrew is **deror** — release, liberty. The word inscribed on the Liberty Bell.

Every fiftieth year, land returned to its original family, whatever had happened in between.

## What the system prevents

Permanent dispossession. A family could fall, but not permanently — the clock was always running
toward the reset.

## Where the go'el turns up

*"**Boaz is our relative**, one of our near kinsmen."*

And at the gate, with ten elders sitting, the nearer kinsman who declines: *I cannot redeem it for
myself, lest I ruin my own inheritance*.

*So Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife.* See the note on Ruth 4:9–10.

The book of Ruth is this law, worked out in a story.

## What Job says

*For **I know that my Redeemer lives**, and He shall stand at last on the earth.* See the note on
Job 19:25–27.

The Hebrew is **go'ali** — my kinsman-redeemer.

## What Isaiah calls Him

*Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel.*

The title appears thirteen times in Isaiah.

## What Jesus reads in Nazareth

*"To proclaim **the acceptable year of the LORD**."*

Isaiah 61, which draws on Jubilee language, read aloud in a synagogue — and *today this Scripture
is fulfilled in your hearing*. See the note on Luke 4:16–21.

> **The two words.** *The land is Mine.* Everything in the chapter — the redemption right, the
> arithmetic, the fiftieth-year reset — follows from a statement about ownership.
