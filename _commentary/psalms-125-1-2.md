Two verses, two geographical facts, and both are being used as arguments.

## Where it sits

One of the Songs of Ascents — the fifteen psalms sung walking up to Jerusalem.

The pilgrims are looking at the thing the psalm describes as they sing it.

## The first comparison

*Those who **trust in the LORD** are like **Mount Zion, which cannot be shaken, which stands
forever**.*

The Hebrew is **lo yimmot** — shall not be moved, shall not totter.

Zion is a limestone ridge. It does not do anything, and it has been there for as long as
anybody can measure.

The psalm compares people to it, and the comparison is about immobility rather than height.

## The second

*As **the mountains gather around Jerusalem**, so **the LORD gathers around His people**.*

The geography is exact. Jerusalem sits in a bowl — the Mount of Olives to the east, Scopus to
the north-east, and higher ground on most sides.

Militarily it is a poor position. Almost every approach looks down into the city.

The psalm takes the same fact and reads it as a ring of protection.

## The two facts

Zion does not move. Jerusalem is surrounded.

Both are ordinary observations about the landscape. The psalm turns the first into a statement
about people who trust, and the second into a statement about God.

## The duration

*From this moment and forever.*

The Hebrew is **me'attah ve'ad olam** — from now and to eternity.

## What follows

*For the sceptre of wickedness shall not rest on the land allotted to the righteous, **lest the
righteous reach out their hands to iniquity**.*

A reason is given for the protection: prolonged oppression makes good people do bad things.

The psalm is realistic about what pressure does.

## The prayer

*Do good, O LORD, to those who are good, and to those who are upright in their hearts.*

## The last line

*As for such as turn aside to their crooked ways, the LORD shall lead them away with the workers
of iniquity. **Peace be upon Israel**.*

The same closing line as Psalm 128. See the note on Psalms 128:5–6.

## Where the shaking language goes

*Now this, "Yet once more," indicates the removal of those things that are being shaken… that
the things which cannot be shaken may remain. Therefore, since we are receiving **a kingdom
which cannot be shaken**, let us have grace.* See the note on Hebrews 12:25–29.

Hebrews uses the same idea, and its contrast is between the mountain that burned and could not
be touched, and Mount Zion.

## What the pilgrims were doing

Walking uphill, in a group, singing about a hill.

The Songs of Ascents are the shortest psalms in the book, and the images in them are all things
visible from the road: hills, watchmen, a weaned child, dew on Hermon, olive shoots round a
table.

Fifteen psalms of ordinary sights, sung by people going up to a feast.

> **Two facts about the landscape.** The ridge does not move, and the city is ringed by higher
> ground. The psalm uses the first of people and the second of God — and the second is what
> makes Jerusalem hard to defend.
