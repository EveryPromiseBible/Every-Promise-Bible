A heading verse, and it establishes the credentials of the speaker before He says anything.

## The word

*A **burden**.*

The Hebrew is **massa** — a load, something lifted and carried. Used of prophetic oracles,
usually heavy ones.

Zechariah's last two sections both open with it, and so do Isaiah's oracles against the
nations.

## The subject

*The word of the LORD **concerning Israel**.*

What follows is the most concentrated stretch of end-times material in the Minor Prophets:
Jerusalem as *a cup of drunkenness to all the surrounding peoples*, and *a very heavy stone
for all peoples*.

## The three participles

*Who **stretches out the heavens**, who **lays the foundation of the earth**, who **forms
the spirit within a person**.*

All three are Hebrew participles — continuous action, not finished acts.

Heavens, earth, and the inside of a man. The list runs from the largest thing to the
smallest, and the smallest gets equal billing.

## The stretching

The Hebrew is **noteh** — the verb for pitching a tent.

Isaiah uses it repeatedly: *who stretches out the heavens like a curtain, and spreads them
out like a tent to dwell in*.

## The founding

**Yosed** — laying a foundation. Building language.

Job has the same image and God asks the question directly: *where were you when I laid the
foundations of the earth?*

## The forming

**Yotser** — the potter's word. The verb of Genesis 2, where God *formed man of the dust of
the ground*, and of Jeremiah at the potter's house.

The spirit inside a person is described as something shaped on a wheel.

## Why the credentials are given

Because of what He is about to promise. Jerusalem besieged by all nations and not taken;
the house of David *like God*; and the verse in the middle of the chapter:

*"And I will pour on the house of David and on the inhabitants of Jerusalem **the Spirit of
grace and supplication**; then they will **look on Me whom they pierced**. Yes, they will
mourn for Him as one mourns for his only son."*

John quotes that line at the cross, when the soldier uses the spear.

## The pattern elsewhere

Isaiah does the same thing before a promise: *thus says God the LORD, who created the
heavens and stretched them out, who spread forth the earth… I, the LORD, have called You in
righteousness.*

The credentials come first, and then the undertaking.

## The third clause

Of the three, only the last is about an individual.

The God who pitches the sky is described, in the same breath and the same grammar, as the
one who forms the interior of one person.

## What Paul does with the same idea

*It is God who **works in you both to will and to do** for His good pleasure.* See the note
on Philippians 2:12–13.

And *we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works*.

> **Where the sentence ends.** Heavens, earth — and then the spirit inside a person, listed
> third and in the same continuous tense as the other two.
