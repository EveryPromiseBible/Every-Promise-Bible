The blessing has already gone, and this is the sound of finding out.

## The cry

*He let out **a loud and bitter cry**.*

The Hebrew is *a great and exceedingly bitter cry*.

Hebrews looks back at it: *he found no place for repentance, **though he sought it
diligently with tears**.*

## What he asks for

*"**Bless me — me too, my father!**"*

Not the birthright, which he had already sold for stew. The blessing.

## Isaac's answer

*"**Your brother came deceitfully and took your blessing**."*

Isaac names it accurately. He does not defend Jacob and does not soften it.

And the chapter has already recorded his reaction on discovering it: *Isaac **trembled
exceedingly**.* Then, in the same breath: *I have blessed him — **and indeed he shall be
blessed**.*

He does not take it back. Hebrews lists that as faith. See the note on
Hebrews 11:17–22.

## Esau's complaint

*"**Isn't he rightly named Jacob?** This is **the second time** he has taken advantage of
me. **He took my birthright, and now he has taken my blessing**."*

**Yaaqov** — heel-grabber, supplanter. Esau turns the name into a verb.

His accounting is half right. The birthright he sold himself, in chapter 25, for a bowl
of red stew, and Genesis says he *despised* it.

## The plea

*"**Haven't you saved even one blessing for me?**"*

## What was given away

*"I have made him **lord over you**, and I have given him **all his relatives as
servants**, and I have **sustained him with grain and new wine**."*

Three items, and Isaac lists them as things already handed over:

- **Lordship** over the brother
- **The household** as servants
- **Grain and new wine** — the sustaining

The Hebrew for *sustained* is **samak** — to lean on, to uphold, to support. It is the
word for the hand laid on the head of a sacrifice.

## The grain and the wine

The same two elements Melchizedek carried out to Abram at the end of a battle. See the
note on Genesis 14:17–20.

## The question at the end

*"So **what is there left that I can do for you, my son?**"*

It is a real question, and there is no answer to it. The blessing was one thing, and it
had been spoken.

## Why it could not be recalled

A spoken blessing in that culture was an act, not a wish. Once said, it stood — which is
why the whole chapter turns on getting the words out of the old man's mouth.

## What Esau gets

A different word entirely, and it is mostly a description of a hard country. See the note
on Genesis 27:38–40.

> **What Isaac says he has done.** Made him lord, given him the household, sustained him
> with grain and wine. Not *I intended to* — the verbs are finished, and the man in front
> of him is the one they were meant for.
