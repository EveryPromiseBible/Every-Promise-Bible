One verse, and the two objects in it are never described.

## Where they go

*"Into **the breastpiece of judgment**, place the Urim and the Thummim."*

The breastpiece is the folded square of gold, blue, purple and scarlet with twelve engraved
stones in four rows. See the note on Exodus 39:8–21.

It is folded double, which makes it a pouch. The Urim and Thummim go inside.

## The names

**Urim** — from **or**, light. Plural: lights.

**Thummim** — from **tamam**, to be complete. Plural: perfections, or completenesses.

The two words begin with the first and last letters of the Hebrew alphabet, aleph and tav.

## What they were

Nobody knows.

Scripture never describes them. No dimensions, no material, no picture.

The usual guesses are two stones or lots, drawn or cast, giving a yes or a no. The narrative
usage fits that: questions put to God through the priest come back with binary answers.

The text is silent, and the silence is worth respecting.

## Where they rest

*"So they rest **over Aaron's heart** every time he comes before the LORD."*

The verse before says the same of the stones: *so Aaron shall bear the names of the sons of
Israel on the breastplate of judgment **over his heart**, when he goes into the holy place*.

Twelve names and the means of decision, in the same pouch, on the same chest.

## What they are called

*"Aaron always carries **the means of decision for the Israelites** over his heart."*

The Hebrew is **mishpat** — judgment, decision, verdict.

The breastpiece itself is named for it: **choshen mishpat**, the breastplate of judgment.

## How they were used

*"He shall stand before Eleazar the priest, who shall **enquire before the LORD for him by the
judgment of the Urim**."*

Joshua's commissioning, and the arrangement is that even the successor of Moses asks through a
priest.

Saul uses it: *therefore Saul said to the LORD God of Israel, "Give a perfect lot"* — and again,
in his last days, when it stops working: *when Saul enquired of the LORD, the LORD did not
answer him, either by dreams or by Urim or by the prophets*.

## When they disappear

After the exile, the returning community hits a problem: some priests could not prove their
descent.

*And the governor said to them that they should not eat of the most holy things **till a priest
could consult with the Urim and Thummim**.*

They are waiting for something they no longer have.

That is the last mention in the Old Testament.

## Where the position is significant

Over the heart, and only when he goes in.

Not consulted at a distance. The priest carries the twelve names and the means of decision into
the presence together.

## The other pieces on him

Two onyx stones on the shoulders, six tribal names on each — *Aaron shall bear their names
before the LORD on his two shoulders as a memorial*.

Shoulders and heart. The strength and the affection, and the same twelve names on both.

## Where the New Testament puts it

*Seeing then that we have a great High Priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son
of God, let us hold fast our confession.* See the note on Hebrews 4:14–16.

And *He always lives to make intercession for them*. See the note on
Hebrews 7:15–17.

The names are still being carried in.

> **What Scripture never tells us.** What they were. No size, no material, no picture — only
> where they sat, and that a priest wore them over his heart whenever he went in.
