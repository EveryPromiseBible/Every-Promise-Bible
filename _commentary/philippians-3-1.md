A transition verse, and it explains why the letter repeats itself.

## The signal

*And now, my brothers, **as for the rest of it**.*

The Greek is **to loipon** — finally, for the rest.

Which usually means a letter is ending. This one runs two more chapters.

Paul does the same thing in other letters; the word marks a turn rather than a conclusion.

## The command

*Be glad in the Lord.*

The Greek is **chairete en Kurio** — rejoice in the Lord.

## How often he says it

*Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice!*

Sixteen occurrences of joy or rejoicing in four chapters, in a letter written from custody with an
unheard case.

The verb is also the ordinary Greek greeting, which means Paul is punning on *hello* every time.

## The admission

*Writing **the same things over again is no weariness to me**.*

The Greek is **ta auta graphein humin emoi men ouk okneron** — to write the same things to you is
not irksome to me.

**Okneros** — hesitant, shrinking, reluctant. The word used of the lazy servant.

He knows he is repeating himself and says so.

## The reason

*And for you it is **solid ground to stand on**.*

The Greek is **humin de asphales** — for you it is safe, secure.

**Asphales** — from **a-sphallo**, not slipping. Firm footing.

The same root Luke uses at the start of his Gospel: *that you may know the certainty of those
things in which you were instructed*. See the note on Luke 1:1–4.

## What repetition is for

Not information. Footing.

Paul's argument is that a thing said once is heard; a thing said again is stood on.

## What comes immediately after

*Beware of dogs, **beware of evil workers, beware of the mutilation!***

Three warnings in one verse, and the tone changes completely.

**Katatome** — the cutting, the mutilation. A savage pun on **peritome**, circumcision.

## What he says about himself

*If anyone else thinks he may have confidence in the flesh, **I more so**.*

Circumcised the eighth day, of the stock of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of the
Hebrews, a Pharisee, blameless concerning the law.

*But what things were gain to me, these **I have counted loss for Christ**.* See the note on
Philippians 3:7–8.

**Skubala** — refuse, dung.

## Where the chapter goes

*That I may know Him and the power of His resurrection… **forgetting those things which are behind
and reaching forward** to those things which are ahead.* See the note on
Philippians 3:12.

## And where it lands

*For **our citizenship is in heaven**, from which we also eagerly wait for the Saviour.* See the note
on Philippians 3:20–21.

## What the letter comes back to

*Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice!*

*Do not be anxious about anything… **and the peace of God will stand guard over your hearts**.* See
the note on Philippians 4:6–7.

The repetition Paul defended in 3:1 happens in 4:4, one chapter later, in the same words.

> **The reason for repeating.** Not that they have forgotten — *for you it is solid ground to stand
> on*. **Asphales**, the state of not slipping.
