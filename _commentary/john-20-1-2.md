The first person at the tomb reaches the wrong conclusion and runs for help.

## The day

*On **the first day of the week**.*

The Greek is **te mia ton sabbaton** — the first of the sabbaths, the idiom for Sunday.

## The hour

*While it was **still dark**.*

The Greek is **skotias eti ouses**.

Mark says *very early in the morning… when the sun had risen*. John says dark. She set out before
dawn and arrived as it was breaking.

John uses darkness as a marker throughout: Nicodemus comes at night, Judas goes out and *it was
night*, and the light shines in the darkness.

## Who

*Mary Magdalene.*

Luke names her as one *from whom seven demons had come out*, and among the women who supported
the work from their own means.

John mentions only her. The other Gospels name several women, and her *we* in verse 2 suggests
John knows she was not alone.

## What she sees

*And she saw that **the stone had been taken away** from the entrance.*

The Greek is **ermenon ek tou mnemeiou** — taken away, lifted out.

That is all she sees. She does not go in.

## What she concludes

*"**They have taken the Lord out of the tomb, and we do not know where they have put Him**."*

Grave robbery. Which is the reasonable inference from an open tomb before sunrise.

## The pronoun

*We do not know.*

The Greek is **ouk oidamen** — plural.

John names one woman and lets her plural stand, which is why the other accounts do not contradict
him.

## What she does

*So **she ran**.*

To Peter, and to *the other disciple, the one Jesus loved* — John's way of naming himself
throughout the book.

Peter had denied Him three days earlier. She goes to him first.

## What she does not say

That He is risen.

She had heard the predictions along with everyone else. Standing at an open tomb, she reports a
theft.

## The race

*So Peter set out, and the other disciple with him.* See the note on John 20:3–10.

Both men run. The younger one arrives first, looks in, and waits.

## What she does after they leave

*But **Mary stood outside by the tomb weeping**.*

The men go home. She stays.

*And as she wept she stooped down and looked into the tomb, and she saw two angels in white
sitting, one at the head and the other at the feet.*

## The question

*"Woman, why are you weeping?"*

*"Because they have taken away my Lord, and I do not know where they have laid Him."*

The same sentence, third time. She has said it to the disciples, and now to angels, and she is
about to say it again to a man she takes for the gardener.

## The name

*Jesus said to her, "**Mary!**"*

*She turned and said to Him, "**Rabboni!**"* See the note on John 20:11–16.

She does not recognise Him by sight. She recognises her own name.

## What she is sent to do

*"Go to My brethren and say to them, '**I am ascending to My Father and your Father, and to My God
and your God**.'"*

The first person to see Him is the first person sent, and *My brethren* is what He calls the men
who ran.

> **What she reports.** Not a resurrection — a theft. She had heard the predictions like everyone
> else, and at an open tomb before dawn she drew the ordinary conclusion.
