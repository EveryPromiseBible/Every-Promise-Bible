Six statements built the same way, and no two use the same noun.

## The turn

The psalm has spent six verses on the heavens — *the heavens declare the glory of God*, and the sun
running its circuit like a strong man to run a race. See the note on
Psalms 19:1–4.

Then, without a transition, it changes subject.

## The pattern

Each line has three parts: a name for the word, an adjective, and an effect.

Six times, without variation in the structure.

## The first

*The **law** of the LORD is **perfect**, **reviving the soul**.*

The Hebrew is **torat YHWH temimah meshivat naphesh**.

**Torah** — instruction, teaching. Not primarily legislation.

**Temimah** — complete, whole, without defect. The sacrificial word.

**Meshivat naphesh** — turning back the soul. The phrase Ruth's neighbours use of Obed: *he shall
be to you a restorer of life*.

## The second

*The **testimony** of the LORD is **sure**, **making the simple wise**.*

**Edut** — testimony, witness. The word for the tablets in the ark.

**Peti** — the simple, the naive. Not the fool; the person who has not learned yet.

## The third

*The **rules** of the LORD are **right**, **giving joy to the heart**.*

**Piqqudim** — precepts, things appointed. A word used only in the Psalms.

## The fourth

*The **command** of the LORD is **clear**, **giving light to the eyes**.*

**Mitzvah** — commandment.

**Barah** — pure, clear, bright. And the effect matches the adjective.

## The fifth

*The **fear** of the LORD is **pure**, **lasting forever**.*

**Yir'ah** — fear, reverence.

The odd one out. Fear is a response, not a text — and the psalm lists it with the others.

## The sixth

*The **judgments** of the LORD are **true**, and all of them are **just**.*

**Mishpatim** — judicial decisions.

## The six effects

Reviving. Making wise. Giving joy. Giving light. Lasting. Being just.

Not one of them is *convicting*. The psalm's account of the law is entirely restorative.

## What comes next

*They are **more to be desired than gold**… **sweeter than honey**, than honey straight from the comb.*

*By them Your servant is warned, and **in keeping them there is great reward**.* See the note on
Psalms 19:10–11.

## What David asks immediately after

*Who can understand his errors? **Cleanse me from secret faults**.*

*Keep back Your servant also **from presumptuous sins**.*

The praise of the law is followed by a request to be cleared of what it exposes.

## The last verse

*Let **the words of my mouth** and **the meditation of my heart** be acceptable in Your sight, O LORD,
my strength and my Redeemer.*

The psalm has been about God's speech — in the sky and in the law. It ends by asking about the
psalmist's own.

## The shape of the whole

Sky, scroll, self. Three witnesses, narrowing.

> **The six lines.** Same structure, six different nouns, six different effects — and not one of
> the effects is conviction.
