James has just used Abraham. Then he reaches for the least likely person in the
Old Testament to put beside him.

## The pairing is deliberate

- **Abraham** — the father of the nation, the man the whole argument about faith
  is usually conducted through
- **Rahab** — a Canaanite, a prostitute, an outsider with no covenant standing at
  all

If his point were that respectable people prove their faith by respectable
behaviour, the second example destroys it. He chooses the two most distant
points on the map and says the same thing happened in both.

## What her "work" was

*She took the messengers in and sent them out another road.*

That is the entire deed. She hid two men and pointed them at the hills. It is not
charitable giving or moral improvement — it is one decision, made at real risk,
that showed which side she had joined.

## "Shown to be right"

The verb is the same one under discussion throughout the chapter, and its range
matters: it can mean *made* right, and it can mean *shown* to be right. Read the
question James is asking — how can you tell a living faith from a dead one — and
the second sense is the one carrying the sentence.

Nobody made Rahab righteous by hiding spies. What she did made visible where she
already stood.
