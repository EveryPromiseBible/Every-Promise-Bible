---
key: Matthew 17:24–27
title: "For My tax and yours"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The only miracle in the gospels performed to pay a bill, and the sentence in the middle of it is
the point.

## The tax

*"Does your teacher not pay **the temple tax**?"*

The **didrachmon** — two drachmas, a half-shekel. Exodus 30 sets it: every man over twenty pays it
*as a ransom for himself*, and the money goes to the service of the tabernacle.

It is not a Roman tax. It is the upkeep of His Father's house.

## Peter's answer

*"**Yes, He does**," Peter said.*

Answered on the doorstep, without asking.

## What Jesus says first

*When Peter came into the house, **Jesus spoke first**.*

**Prophthasen auton** — He anticipated him. Peter has not mentioned the conversation.

## The question

*"From whom do the kings of the earth collect duties and taxes — **from their own children, or from
others**?" "From others," Peter answered. "**Then the children are free**," Jesus said."*

The Greek is **eleutheroi eisin hoi huioi** — the sons are free.

The argument is watertight and the claim inside it is enormous. It is His Father's house. A son of
the house does not pay for the upkeep of the house, and Peter has just agreed to the principle
before seeing where it lands.

*Therefore **you are no longer a slave but a son**, and if a son, then an heir of God through
**Christ**.*

*For **the law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came through Jesus Christ**.*

## What He does anyway

*"**But so that we give them no offence**, go down to the sea and throw out a hook."*

The Greek is **hina me skandalisomen** — that we may not trip them up.

He establishes the exemption and waives it. The freedom is real and it is not used as leverage.

*For though I am free from all men, **I have made myself a servant to all**, that I might win the
more.*

*"All things are lawful for me," but not all things are helpful.*

## The coin

*"Take the first fish you catch, open its mouth, and you will find **a four-drachma coin**."*

A **stater** — exactly two temple taxes. Not approximately.

## The four words

*"Take it and give it to them **for My tax and yours**."*

One coin, two men. Peter is not told to find his own.

**Matthew** does not record Peter catching the fish. The story ends with the instruction, and the
last thing in it is the arithmetic: the free Son pays for the man who is not.

*For **you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He
became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich.*

*Knowing that you were not redeemed with corruptible things, like silver or gold… but with **the
precious blood of Christ**.*

**Peter** writes that, decades later, in a letter about being ransomed — and Exodus calls this half-
shekel *a ransom for himself*.

> **The coin.** A stater — two temple taxes exactly, in the mouth of the first fish. The Son who
> has just established that He is exempt pays it, and pays for Peter with the same coin.
