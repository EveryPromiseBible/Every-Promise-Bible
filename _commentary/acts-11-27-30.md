A famine is announced before it arrives, and the church's response is to give money
away.

## The messenger

*One of them named **Agabus** stood up and **indicated by the Spirit** that a great
famine was coming over the whole world.*

**Agabus** comes from a root meaning *locust*. A man named for the insect that eats a
harvest stands up to announce a shortage.

He appears twice in Acts and both times with a warning — here, and later at Caesarea,
binding his own hands and feet with Paul's belt.

## The confirmation

*Which **happened in the days of Claudius Caesar**.*

Luke writes this after the fact and says so. Roman sources record serious grain
shortages under Claudius, and Judea was hit hard in the mid-forties.

The prophecy is dated and checked in the same sentence.

## Who was warned

Antioch. A Gentile church, a long way north, outside the famine's worst.

They are told about a hardship that is going to fall on somebody else.

## What they did with the warning

*So the disciples, **each according to what he had**, determined to **send help** to the
brothers living in Judea.*

They do not stockpile. Told that a famine is coming, they send their supplies south.

## "Each according to what he had"

The Greek is *as each was prospered*. Nobody is assessed a fixed amount and nobody is
told what anyone else gave.

## Who they sent it to

*Living in Judea* — the mother church in Jerusalem, the people who had been suspicious
of Antioch's existence in the first place. Peter had to explain himself in the previous
chapter for eating with Gentiles.

The Gentile church sends money to the Jewish one that questioned it.

## Who carried it

*Sending it to the elders by the hands of **Barnabas and Saul**.*

Barnabas is the man who sold a field and laid the money at the apostles' feet in
chapter 4, and who vouched for Saul when nobody in Jerusalem would meet him.

Saul is on his first recorded assignment for the church, and it is a delivery job.

## What Antioch was

Three verses earlier: *the disciples were **first called Christians** in Antioch.*

The name is given in this chapter, and the first thing the people carrying it do is
this.

## Where the money came from

Antioch is a Gentile church, weeks old, sending relief to the Jewish believers in Judea — the people
who had been arguing about whether they should be admitted at all.

*For you know **the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He
became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich.*

**Paul** uses that sentence as the argument for a collection, in **2 Corinthians** 8, and he spends
two chapters on the same kind of transfer.

## What the church was called

*And the disciples were first called **Christians** in Antioch.*

Four verses earlier. The congregation that gets the name is the one that immediately sends money
somewhere else.

*"**By this all will know that you are My disciples, if you have love for one another.**"*

## Each according to what he had

*"**Each according to what he had.**"* **Kathos euporeito tis** — as anyone prospered.

*So let each one give as he purposes in his heart, not grudgingly or of necessity; for **God loves a
cheerful giver**.*

No amount is set, no proportion is named, and **Luke** does not record anybody being told what to
send.

> **The order of events.** The warning came first, then the collection, then the
> famine. Luke sets it down in that sequence — the relief was already on the road
> before anyone in Judea was hungry.
