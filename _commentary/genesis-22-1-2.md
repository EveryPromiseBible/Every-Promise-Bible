The opening of the hardest chapter in Genesis, and the reader is told at the start what
Abraham is not told.

## What the reader knows

*Sometime later, **God tested Abraham**.*

The Hebrew is **nissah** — to test, to prove, to put to the trial.

Moses tells us in the first line. Abraham has no such sentence.

James says God tempts nobody toward evil; this is the other word. See the note on
James 1:13.

## The call

*He called to him, "**Abraham**." And Abraham answered, "**Here I am**."*

The Hebrew is **hinneni** — here I am, at your disposal.

He says it three times in this chapter: to God here, to Isaac on the way up, and to the
voice that stops him. See the note on Genesis 22:11–12.

## The four descriptions

*"**Take your son — your only son, Isaac, the one you love**."*

Four, and each narrows:

- **Your son**
- **Your only son** — the Hebrew is **yachid**, only, unique. Ishmael has been sent away
  one chapter earlier. See the note on Genesis 21:8–11.
- **Isaac** — the name means *he laughs*
- **The one you love** — and this is the first time the word **ahav**, love, appears in
  the Bible

## The first occurrence of love

Jewish tradition notes it: the word for love enters Scripture here, in the sentence asking
a father to give up the son.

## The destination

*"**Go to the land of Moriah**."*

2 Chronicles: *Solomon began to build the house of the LORD at Jerusalem **on Mount
Moriah***.

The same ridge, and about a three-day walk from Beersheba — which is what Genesis says it
took.

## The instruction

*"There, **on a mountain I will show you**, offer him up to me as a burnt offering."*

**A mountain I will show you** — the same construction as the original call: *get out of
your country… **to a land that I will show you***.

Forty years earlier, the same open ending.

## What is not explained

Anything. No reason is given, no assurance offered, and no alternative mentioned.

## The line Moses adds

*Every word landed heavier than the last: **your son, your only son, the one you love**.*

## What Abraham did

*So Abraham **rose early in the morning**, saddled his donkey, split the wood, and went.*

Genesis gives no conversation with Sarah and no night of wrestling. It gives an itinerary.

## What he said at the foot of the hill

*"Stay here with the donkey; the lad and I will go yonder and worship, **and we will come
back to you**."*

Plural. Hebrews explains it: *he had worked it out that God could raise the dead.* See the
note on Hebrews 11:17–22.

## What he said on the way up

Isaac: *"Behold, the fire and the wood, but **where is the lamb** for a burnt offering?"*

And the answer: *"My son, **God will provide for Himself the lamb**."*

## What was on the mountain

A ram caught in a thicket by its horns, behind him, already there.

He names the place **Jehovah Jireh** — *the LORD will provide* — and Moses adds the saying
still current when he wrote: *in the Mount of the LORD it shall be provided.*

## Where the chapter ends

An oath, sworn by God on Himself, because there was nobody greater. See the note on
Genesis 22:15–18.

> **The word that enters Scripture here.** Love. Genesis has covered creation, a flood,
> Babel, and three generations of a family without using it — and the first time it
> appears, it is in the sentence about the son on the mountain.
