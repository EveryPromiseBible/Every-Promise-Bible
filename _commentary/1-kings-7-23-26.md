A bronze basin so large the writer calls it a sea.

## The size

*Ten cubits across from rim to rim, five cubits deep, and thirty cubits around… **it
held two thousand baths of water**.*

Roughly fifteen feet across, seven and a half deep, and something in the order of ten
to twelve thousand gallons.

It was cast in bronze, a handbreadth thick, in one piece.

## What it replaced

The tabernacle had a bronze laver, made from the mirrors of the women who served at
the door, for the priests to wash their hands and feet before serving.

Solomon's version is the same item at enormous scale. Chronicles says plainly what it
was for: *the Sea was for the priests to wash in.*

## The twelve oxen

*It rested on **twelve bronze oxen — three facing north, three west, three south,
three east** — all their hindquarters turned inward, the Sea riding on their backs.*

Four groups of three, facing outward in every direction, and the whole weight of the
water on them.

Twelve is the number of the tribes, and the same arrangement as the camp in the
wilderness — three tribes on each side of the tabernacle, facing out.

## The order they are listed in

North, west, south, east.

That is not the order anybody would use to describe a circle if they were going
clockwise. It is the reverse.

## Why oxen

The ox is the working animal of the Old Testament, and the law protected it in the
middle of its labour: *do not muzzle an ox while it treads out the grain.*

Paul takes that law twice and applies it to those who preach and teach — *is it oxen
God is concerned about, or does He say it entirely for our sake?* See the notes on
1 Corinthians 9:7–10 and 1 Timothy 5:17–18.

Whatever else the twelve are, they are carriers.

## The washing

*Christ also loved the church and gave Himself up for her, so that He might make her
holy, **washing her clean with water through the word**.* See the note on
Ephesians 5:25–27.

The water in Ephesians is the word, and Paul says so in the same sentence.

## The lily

*Its rim curved out **like the rim of a cup, like the bloom of a lily**.*

A vessel holding twelve thousand gallons of bronze-cast water, and the writer records
that the edge was shaped like a flower.

## What happened to it

Ahaz cut the panels off the stands and *removed the Sea from the bronze oxen that
were under it, and put it on a pavement of stones.*

The same Ahaz whose stairway the shadow went back on. See the note on
2 Kings 20:8–11.

And when Babylon came: *the bronze Sea that was in the house of the LORD, the
Chaldeans broke in pieces, and carried all the bronze to Babylon.*

> **What Revelation puts in front of the throne.** *Before the throne there was **a sea
> of glass, like crystal**.* Solomon's held twelve thousand gallons for priests who
> needed washing. John's is glass — still, and not needed.
