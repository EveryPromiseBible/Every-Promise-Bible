The army arrives at the battlefield and finds it already over.

## The timing

*And **the moment they began to sing and to praise**.*

The Hebrew is **uve'et hechellu berinnah utehillah** — at the time they began with singing and
praise.

**Hechellu** — from **chalal**, to begin.

The verse marks the start, not the finish. Nothing had been accomplished yet by the singing.

## What had come before

Three nations marching on Judah. Jehoshaphat's prayer ends: *we have no power against this great
multitude… **nor do we know what to do, but our eyes are upon You***. See the note on
2 Chronicles 20:10–12.

And the answer through Jahaziel: *the battle is not yours, but God's… **you will not need to fight
in this battle***. See the note on 2 Chronicles 20:16–19.

## The formation

*He appointed **those who should sing to the LORD**, and who should **praise the beauty of holiness**,
**as they went out before the army**.*

Choir in front. Soldiers behind. See the note on 2 Chronicles 20:20–21.

The words they sang: *Praise the LORD, for His mercy endures forever*.

## What that means tactically

The most exposed position in the column is held by unarmed singers.

## What happened

*The LORD **set ambushes** against the men of Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir who had come against
Judah, **and they were struck down**.*

The Hebrew is **natan YHWH me'arevim** — the LORD set ambushers.

## Who the ambushers were

Unstated.

The chronicler does not say whether they were angelic, human, or the confusion itself. He names the
one who set them and leaves it there.

## What the enemy did

*The Ammonites and Moabites **turned on the men of Mount Seir** to destroy them utterly, and when
they had finished with them, **they helped destroy one another**.*

The Hebrew is **azeru ish-bere'ehu lemashchit** — each helped his neighbour to destroy.

## The word

**Azar** — to help.

The verb is a helping verb, used of mutual annihilation. The chronicler's irony is in the word
choice.

## The coalition

The three nations came as allies. The alliance is what kills them.

## The arrival

*When Judah came to **the lookout point over the wilderness**, they looked toward the multitude — and
**there were only corpses, fallen to the ground. Not one had escaped**.*

The Hebrew is **peleitah** — a survivor, an escapee. There was none.

## What Judah did in the battle

Nothing. They walked to a vantage point and looked.

## What came after

Three days collecting the spoil — *more than they could carry away*.

And they assembled in a valley to bless the LORD, *therefore the name of that place was called **The
Valley of Berachah** — blessing*. See the note on 2 Chronicles 20:25–26.

## The other army that turned on itself

*Three hundred blew the trumpets, and **the LORD set every man's sword against his companion**.* See
the note on Judges 7:19–22.

Gideon's men broke jars. Jehoshaphat's men sang. Neither drew a sword.

## The reason given

*And **the fear of God was on all the kingdoms of those countries** when they heard that the LORD had
fought against the enemies of Israel.*

*So the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet, for his God gave him rest all around.*

> **The tense.** *The moment they began.* Not when they finished, and not because the singing was
> impressive — the verse marks the start of it as the moment the ambush was set.
