The last image in the psalm is a shield that has been enlarged until it goes all the way round.

## The turn

*But **let everyone who takes shelter in You be glad**.*

The Hebrew is **chosei bak** — those taking refuge in You.

**Chasah** — to flee for protection. The psalm's word for the people it prays for.

## The joy

*Let them **sing for joy without end**.*

The Hebrew is **le'olam yerannenu** — forever let them shout for joy.

**Ranan** — a loud ringing cry, not a quiet contentment.

## The covering

*Spread Your covering over them.*

The Hebrew is **vetasek alemo** — and You shall cover over them.

The verb is related to **sukkah**, the booth. A roof of branches over people who would otherwise be
in the open.

## The condition

*So those who **love Your name** may rejoice in You.*

The Hebrew is **ohavei shemeka**.

## The blessing

*For **You bless the righteous, LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **ki-attah tevarek tzaddiq**.

## The last line

*Like a shield You **surround them with favor**.*

The Hebrew is **katzinnah ratzon ta'terennu** — with favour as a large shield You will encircle him.

## The word for shield

**Tzinnah** — not the small round buckler, **magen**, carried on the arm.

The **tzinnah** is the body shield, the full-length one. Goliath had a man walking in front of him
carrying his.

## The verb

**Atar** — to encircle, to crown.

A shield covers one side. This one is described going all the way round.

## What is inside it

**Ratzon** — favour, acceptance, good pleasure. The word used of a sacrifice being accepted.

The shield is not made of armour in this line. The surrounding substance is favour.

## Where the psalm began

*Give ear to my words, O LORD, **consider my meditation**.*

The Hebrew is **hagigi** — my murmuring, my groaning. See the note on Psalms 5:1–3.

And *my voice You shall hear in the morning; in the morning I will direct it to You, and I will look
up*.

The verb **arak** is a sacrificial word — to lay the pieces in order on the altar.

## What he asked for on the way

*Lead me, O LORD, in Your righteousness because of my enemies; **make Your way straight before my
face**.* See the note on Psalms 5:8–10.

## The two halves of the psalm

Enemies and open graves in the first part. A body shield and unending shouting in the last two
verses.

Nothing is said to have changed in the circumstances between them.

## Where the same shield turns up

*Above all, taking **the shield of faith** with which you will be able to quench all the fiery darts
of the wicked one.* See the note on Ephesians 6:14–17.

The Greek is **thureos** — the door-shaped shield, the Greek equivalent of the **tzinnah**. From
**thura**, a door.

> **The size.** Not the small buckler — the **tzinnah**, the full-body shield, and the verb says it
> goes all the way round. What it is made of, in this verse, is favour.
