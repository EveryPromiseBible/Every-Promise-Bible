An anchor goes down. This one goes up, and inward.

## The image

*That hope is **our anchor**. **It holds; it does not slip**.*

The Greek is **agkuran… asphalē te kai bebaian** — safe and firm.

**Asphalēs** — from **a-**, not, and **sphallō**, to trip or make fall. Something that cannot be
made to stumble.

**Bebaios** — steadfast, having a firm footing. A legal word for a guarantee that will stand up.

## Where it is

*And it **reaches all the way inside, past the curtain**.*

The Greek is **eiserchomenēn eis to esōteron tou katapetasmatos** — entering into the inner side of
the veil.

## The direction

An anchor drops into what it cannot see, and holds in ground the sailor never inspects.

This one is not on the seabed. It is fixed inside the sanctuary.

## What is behind the curtain

The Most Holy Place. One man, once a year, with blood.

*The Holy Spirit was saying something by all of this: as long as that front room was still standing,
the road into the holiest place had not yet been opened.* See the note on Hebrews 9:8–10.

## What changed

*Then, behold, the veil of the temple was torn in two **from top to bottom**.* See the note on
Matthew 27:50–51.

## Who is in there

*Where **Jesus has already gone in ahead of us, for our sake**.*

The Greek is **prodromos** — forerunner. The word appears once in the New Testament.

## What a forerunner is

A scout, or the man who swims ashore with the line.

In harbour practice, when a ship could not enter at low water, a small boat carried the anchor
inside the harbour and set it, and the ship was then drawn in to where the anchor already was.

A forerunner implies followers. A high priest going in alone did not.

## The title

*Made **High Priest forever — Melchizedek's kind of priest**.*

The Greek is **kata tēn taxin Melchisedek** — according to the order of Melchizedek.

## Why it matters

Aaron's priests died and were replaced. *Because He continues forever, He has an unchangeable
priesthood.* See the note on Hebrews 7:24–25.

## The subject he keeps returning to

He raised Melchizedek in chapter 5 and broke off — *of whom we have much to say, and hard to
explain*. See the note on Hebrews 5:9–11.

Then the warning, then the encouragement, then the oath, then the anchor — and the sentence ends by
naming Melchizedek again.

Chapter 7 finally takes it up.

## What the anchor is tied to

*Two immutable things, in which **it is impossible for God to lie**.* See the note on Hebrews 6:18.

A promise and an oath.

## The invitation the anchor makes possible

*Let us **draw near** with a true heart in full assurance of faith.* See the note on Hebrews 10:22.

And *having **boldness to enter the Holiest** by the blood of Jesus*. See the note on
Hebrews 10:19–21.

The place the anchor is set is the place we are told to walk into.

> **The direction.** An anchor drops. This one is fixed inside the sanctuary, where the forerunner
> already is — which means the ship is being drawn toward it, not held back by it.
