Paul's last words to the Ephesian elders, and he hands them over to something rather than
somebody.

## The handover

*"And now, brothers, **I commit you to God and to the word of His grace**."*

The Greek is **paratithemai** — to deposit, to entrust, as you would leave valuables with
someone.

He is leaving and knows he will not be back — he has just told them *you all… will see my
face no more*. See the note on Acts 20:17–21.

He does not appoint a successor or leave instructions. He deposits them with two things.

## What the word can do

*"**Which is able to build you up** and to **give you an inheritance among all those who
are set apart**."*

Two verbs attached to the word:

- **Build up** — **oikodomesai**, house-building. The same word Paul uses throughout
  1 Corinthians 14 about a meeting. See the note on 1 Corinthians 14:1–5.
- **Give an inheritance** — the word is credited with doing the giving.

## What he wanted

*"**I have wanted no one's silver or gold or clothing**."*

Clothing is on the list because in that world cloth was wealth. Achan's confession begins
with a Babylonian garment.

## The hands

*"You yourselves know that **these hands provided for my own needs** and for those who
were with me."*

He is speaking to men who had watched him do it. Paul was a tentmaker, and Acts 18 has
him working with Aquila and Priscilla in Corinth at the same trade.

Not only his own needs — *and for those who were with me*. He funded the team.

## The purpose

*"In everything I showed you that **by working like this we must help the weak**."*

The Greek is **antilambanesthai** — to take hold of in support. The same root Paul uses
in Romans 8 of the Spirit taking hold of the other end of the load. See the note on
Romans 8:26–27.

He worked so that he could give.

## The saying

*"And **remember the words of the Lord Jesus**, how He said, '**It is more blessed to give
than to receive**.'"*

It is not in any Gospel.

Acts preserves it and nothing else does — one sentence of Jesus that survived only
because Paul quoted it to a group of elders on a beach at Miletus, and Luke was
listening.

## The comparison

*More blessed **to give** than **to receive**.*

The Greek is a straight comparative. Both are blessed; one more so.

## What happens next

*And when he had said these things, **he knelt down and prayed with them all**. Then they
all wept freely, and **fell on Paul's neck and kissed him**, sorrowing most of all for the
words which he spoke, that they would see his face no more.*

*And they accompanied him to the ship.*

Luke was there — the passage is in the *we* sections — and this is one of the few scenes
in Acts where he records the emotion of a farewell.

> **What he leaves them.** Not a structure and not a name. God, and the word of His
> grace — and he says outright that the word is the thing capable of building them and
> giving them the inheritance.
