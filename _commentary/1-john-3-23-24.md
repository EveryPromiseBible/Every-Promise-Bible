John states the commandment in the singular, and then names two things.

## The singular

*And **this is what He commanded**.*

The Greek is **haute estin he entole autou** — this is His commandment.

One commandment. Not a list.

## The two halves

*That we **trust the name of His Son Jesus Christ**, and **love one another**.*

The Greek is **hina pisteusomen… kai agapomen**.

Believing and loving, joined under one noun.

## What that does

John has been talking about commandments in the plural — *whatever we ask we receive from Him,
because we keep His commandments*.

Here he collapses them into one, and the one has two parts that he does not separate.

## The first verb's tense

**Pisteusomen** — aorist. A decisive act.

**Agapomen** — present. A continuing one.

The Greek distinguishes them and English cannot.

## What has just been said

*My little children, **let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth**.*

*If our heart condemns us, **God is greater than our heart**, and knows all things.* See the note on
1 John 3:17–18.

The paragraph before is about a conscience that accuses, and this is the answer to it.

## The abiding

*The one who keeps what He commanded **stays in Him, and He stays in that one**.*

The Greek is **menei** — abides, remains, dwells.

**Meno** is John's word. It occurs more than sixty times in his Gospel and letters.

Mutual: he in Him, and He in him.

## The test

*And **this is how we know He stays in us**.*

The Greek is **en touto ginoskomen**.

John writes the letter to give people grounds for knowing: *these things I have written to you who
believe… **that you may know that you have eternal life***.

## The evidence

*From **the Spirit He gave us**.*

The Greek is **ek tou Pneumatos hou hemin edoken**.

Not from performance, and not from feeling. From a gift.

## What Paul says in the same construction

*The Spirit Himself **bears witness with our spirit** that we are children of God.* See the note on
Romans 8:15–17.

## What follows immediately

*Beloved, **do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits**, whether they are of God; because many
false prophets have gone out into the world.* See the note on 1 John 4:1.

Having named the Spirit as the evidence, John immediately says not every spirit is Him.

## The test he gives

*Every spirit that **confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh** is of God.*

The content of the confession, not the intensity of the experience.

## Where the two halves come from

*"A new commandment I give to you, **that you love one another; as I have loved you**."* See the note
on John 13:31–35.

*"This is the work of God, **that you believe in Him whom He sent**."* See the note on
John 6:28–31.

John heard both and joins them into one.

> **The singular.** *This is His commandment* — one noun, two verbs, and John does not separate
> believing from loving even grammatically.
