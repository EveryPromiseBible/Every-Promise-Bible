The only thing in the creation account God called *not good*, and what He did
about it.

## The deep sleep

*The LORD God caused **a deep sleep** to fall over the man.*

The Hebrew is **tardemah** — the same word used of Abram in Genesis 15, when the
covenant was cut and only the fire walked between the pieces.

Adam is unconscious for the making of his bride. He contributes nothing but the
material, and he was asleep while it was taken.

## What was taken

*God took **a part of his side** and closed the place up again.*

The Hebrew **tsela** is usually rendered rib, and it is the ordinary word for a
side — the side of the ark, the side of the tabernacle.

She is made from the side, not from the head or the feet, and God does the
fashioning. The verb is the one used of a builder.

## "And He brought her to the man"

God performs the introduction. The first marriage has a witness and a giver, and
they are the same Person.

## What Adam says

*"**At last** — this one is **bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh**."*

The Hebrew opens with an exclamation — *this time!* He had just named every animal
and found *no helper suitable for him*, which is what makes the *at last* land.

Paul takes this exact phrase and applies it to the church: *we are parts of His
body, **of His flesh and of His bones**.* See the note on Ephesians 5:28–30.

## The three verbs

*This is why a man **leaves** his father and mother and **holds fast** to his wife,
and the two **become one flesh**.*

Leave, hold fast, become one. In that order, and Jesus quotes the whole sentence in
Matthew 19 as the definition, then adds *what God has joined together, let not man
separate.*

**Hold fast** is **dabaq** — to cling, to be glued to. It is used of skin sticking
to bone, and of Ruth clinging to Naomi.

And *one flesh* is not a metaphor for cooperation. The Hebrew is one flesh.

## What Paul says about it

*He who is joined to a harlot is one body with her, for "the two," He says, "shall
become one flesh."*

Paul reaches for this verse to explain why sexual union outside marriage is not a
minor thing. On his reading it does the same joining regardless of whether the
covenant is there — which is his whole argument for why it is not casual.

## The last line

*They were both naked, and **they felt no shame** — nothing to hide, nothing to
fear, completely at home with each other.*

Two people fully known and entirely unafraid. That state lasts exactly one chapter,
and the first thing the fall produces is *I was afraid, because I was naked, and I
hid myself.*

> **What was not good.** *It is not good that man should be alone.* The only
> negative verdict in a chapter of *very good*, and it was pronounced over a man in
> a garden, in daily contact with God, with meaningful work and no sin. He still
> needed her.
