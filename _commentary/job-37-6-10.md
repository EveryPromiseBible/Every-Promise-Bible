Elihu's last speech, and he spends it on weather.

## The commands

*"He says to **the snow**, 'Fall on the earth,' and to **the rain**, 'Pour down.'"*

Weather addressed as something under orders.

Elihu is building toward the whirlwind — God answers Job out of one in the very next
chapter, and Elihu is describing storms when it arrives.

## What snow does to work

*"He **seals up every hand from its work**, so that all people He has made **may stop
and know it is His doing**."*

The Hebrew is that He puts a seal on the hand of every man.

A heavy snowfall stops the farming, the building, the travelling. Nobody works.

And Elihu gives a purpose for it: so that people stop, and know.

It is the only place in Scripture that offers a reason for winter, and the reason is
that it makes everyone put their tools down.

## The animals

*"The wild animals **slip into their dens** and stay in their shelters."*

The observation is exact. Animals go to ground before weather, and Elihu notes it as
part of the same obedience.

## The whirlwind

*"Out of its chamber comes **the whirlwind**, and the biting cold rides in on the
driving winds."*

The Hebrew for *chamber* is an inner room, a storehouse.

Elihu speaks of a whirlwind having a room it comes out of, and then God speaks to Job
*out of the whirlwind.*

## The ice

*"**By the breath of God the ice is formed**, and the wide waters freeze solid."*

The Hebrew is *by the breath of God ice is given*.

Freezing is a loss of heat, and Elihu attributes it to breath — which is
counter-intuitive, since breath is warm. But the Hebrew **ruach** is also wind, and
it is wind off cold ground that freezes standing water.

## What Elihu is doing with all this

The chapter ends: *the Almighty — **we cannot find Him out**; He is excellent in power,
in judgement and abundant justice; He does not oppress.*

He is arguing from snow, rain, ice and thunder to a God who is beyond working out,
and whose power is not oppressive.

Then the whirlwind arrives.

## Where God takes the same subject

*"Have you entered the treasury of the snow, or have you seen the treasury of the
hail?"* — Job 38, a chapter later.

God asks about the same storehouses Elihu mentioned, and asks Job whether he has been
inside them. See the note on Job 38:4–7.

> **The last thing Elihu says before God speaks.** *Therefore men fear Him; He shows
> no partiality to any who are wise of heart.* And then, mid-storm, the LORD answers
> — and never rebukes Elihu, though He rebukes the other three by name.
