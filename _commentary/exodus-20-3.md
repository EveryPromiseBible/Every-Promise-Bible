The first of the ten, and it is the one every other commandment sits on.

## What comes before it

*"**I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of
bondage**."*

That sentence is verse 2, and Jewish tradition counts it as the first of the ten words —
a statement rather than a command.

The rescue is announced before anything is asked. The commands follow an introduction of
who is speaking and what He has already done.

## The command

*You shall have **no other gods before me**.*

The Hebrew is **al panai** — before My face, in My presence.

Not *ahead of Me in rank*. In My sight.

## What the Illumination adds

*Let **no rival stand where I stand**.*

*I am **the God who came for you**; keep your heart turned toward me alone.*

## Why it is first

Everything else in the list depends on it. Murder, theft, adultery and false witness are
recognisable wrongs in any society; this one is the claim that makes the list a covenant
rather than a legal code.

## The name given

The **ten words** — Hebrew **aseret hadevarim**, which is where *Decalogue* comes from.
Exodus and Deuteronomy both call them words rather than laws.

## Who heard them

The whole nation, spoken aloud. It is the only time in Scripture God addresses an entire
people directly, and their response was to ask Him to stop. See the note on
Exodus 20:18–21.

## What was written

*Two tablets of stone, **written by the very finger of God**.* See the note on
Exodus 31:18.

And they were broken before they reached the camp, because of what the nation was doing
at the foot of the mountain while the writing was happening.

## What the nation had said

*"All that the LORD has spoken **we will do**."*

Said before they knew what would be spoken. See the note on Exodus 19:7–9.

## Where Jesus puts the first commandment

Asked which is the greatest, He does not quote Exodus 20. He quotes Deuteronomy 6:

*"**You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all
your mind**. This is the first and great commandment."*

Same content, stated as love rather than prohibition. See the note on
Matthew 22:34–40.

## What the rich young ruler was asked

Jesus listed five commandments to him — do not commit adultery, do not murder, do not
steal, do not bear false witness, honour your parents.

All five from the second table. The first table was not recited, and the one thing he was
told he lacked was about what he was holding on to. See the note on Luke 18:18–23.

## Where Paul locates the problem

*They **exchanged the truth of God for the lie**, and worshipped and served the creature
rather than the Creator.*

He puts it at the head of his indictment in Romans 1, in the same position it holds here.

> **What the sentence is about.** Not a ranking. The Hebrew is *before My face* — and the
> verse before it says who the face belongs to and what He had just done.
