Paul invents a word to describe himself, and it is a comparative built on a superlative.

## The self-description

*To me — **the last and littlest of all God's holy people, less than the least of them**.*

The Greek is **to elachistotero panton hagion**.

**Elachistos** — least. Already a superlative.

**Elachistotero** — a comparative formed on top of it. *Leaster than the least.*

The word does not exist in Greek before Paul. It is grammatically impossible and he wrote it
anyway.

## The escalation across his letters

*For **I am the least of the apostles**, who am not worthy to be called an apostle, because I
persecuted the church of God.* — 1 Corinthians, the earliest.

*Less than the least of all the saints.* — Ephesians, here.

*Sinners, **of whom I am chief**.* — 1 Timothy, the latest.

Least of the apostles, then least of all Christians, then chief of sinners. The estimate drops as
the years pass.

## What was given

*This grace was given: **to carry to the nations the news of a wealth in Christ that no one will
ever reach the end of**.*

The Greek is **to anexichniaston ploutos tou Christou** — the untraceable riches of Christ.

**Anexichniastos** — from **ichnos**, a footprint. Cannot be tracked to the end.

## Where else the word appears

*How far past our searching are His judgments, and **His paths — no one can trace where He has
walked**.* See the note on Romans 11:33–36.

Twice in the New Testament, both in Paul.

## The second commission

*And **to bring into the light for everyone to see** how all of us are brought in together on the
secret.*

The Greek is **photisai pantas** — to enlighten all.

## The secret

*That was **hidden away in God from the beginning of the ages**.*

The Greek is **apokekrummenou apo ton aionon en to Theo**.

Not hidden in a text somebody failed to read. Hidden in God.

## What he adds

*God, who **made every single thing by Jesus Christ**.*

The Greek is **to ta panta ktisanti**.

Paul attaches the creation clause to the sentence about the secret, and does not explain the
connection.

## The two tasks

Preach the riches, and make the arrangement visible.

The second is the odd one — not to preach it but to bring it into the light where it can be seen.

## Who the audience turns out to be

*To the intent that now **the manifold wisdom of God might be made known by the church to the
principalities and powers in the heavenly places**.*

The Greek is **polupoikilos sophia** — the many-coloured wisdom.

The demonstration is staged for an audience that is not human, and the exhibit is a mixed
congregation.

## What follows

*In whom we have **boldness and access with confidence** through faith in Him.*

*Therefore I ask that you do not lose heart at my tribulations for you, **which is your glory**.*

His imprisonment, described as their honour.

## The prayer that resumes the sentence

*For this reason **I bow my knees to the Father** of our Lord Jesus Christ… that He would grant you,
according to the riches of His glory, **to be strengthened with might through His Spirit in the
inner man**.* See the note on Ephesians 3:14–17.

The sentence broken off at verse 1 finally arrives.

> **The word.** **Elachistotero** — a comparative built on a superlative, which Greek does not
> permit. He wrote it anyway, and the self-estimate keeps dropping across the letters.
