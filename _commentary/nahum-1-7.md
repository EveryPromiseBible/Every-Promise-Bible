title: "But the LORD is good"

One verse of light in a book with almost none, and it is placed where the reader most needs it.

## Where it sits

Verse 6 is *who could stand in front of His anger? Who could endure the heat of His displeasure?*

Verse 8 is *against His enemies He comes like a flood that sweeps everything before it.*

Between them, verse 7.

## The three words

*"**But the LORD is good.**"*

**Tov YHWH.** Two words in Hebrew and the **but** is supplied by the position.

It is the shortest sentence in the book and it is the one everything else is measured against.

*"**Why do you call Me good? No one is good but One, that is, God.**"*

*"**I am the good shepherd.** The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep."*

**John** 10, and the Greek is **kalos** — good in the sense of beautiful, admirable. **Jesus** says
it twice in two verses and both times He attaches it to the dying.

## The stronghold

*"He is **a stronghold in the day of trouble**, a safe place when the storm hits."*

The Hebrew is **lema'oz beyom tzarah** — a fortress in the day of distress.

Nahum is writing about the fall of the most heavily fortified city in the world. Nineveh's walls
were reportedly wide enough to drive chariots along. The book spends two chapters watching them
fail.

And in the middle of it, one verse offering a different kind of fortification.

*The name of the LORD is a strong tower; the righteous run to it and are safe.* See the note on
Proverbs 18:9–16.

## The knowing

*"And **He knows them, He truly knows them**, all those who run to Him and hide themselves in His
care."*

The Hebrew is **veyodea chosei vo** — and He knows those who take refuge in Him.

**Yada** — to know by acquaintance, not by information.

*Nevertheless the solid foundation of God stands, having this seal: "**The Lord knows those who are
His**."*

**2 Timothy** 2, written by a man in a Roman prison to a young minister worried about people
falling away. **Paul**'s answer is that the register is not kept by us.

*"**I am the good shepherd; and I know My sheep, and am known by My own.**"*

*"And I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish; **neither shall anyone snatch them out
of My hand**."*

## The refuge word

**Chasah** — to seek shelter, to flee to for cover. It is the psalms' word: *blessed are all those
who put their trust in Him.*

The verse does not describe people who are strong. It describes people who ran somewhere.

*That we might have strong consolation, **who have fled for refuge to lay hold of the hope set
before us**.*

## What this verse is doing in this book

Nahum is three chapters of siege, chariots, corpses and locusts. The prophet's name means comfort,
and this is where the comfort is.

It is put in the middle of the description of the flood, and it is addressed to the people the
flood is not for.

> **Two words.** *Tov YHWH* — the LORD is good. The shortest sentence in the book, sitting between
> *who could endure the heat of His anger* and *He comes like a flood* — and the clause attached to
> it is that He knows, personally, everyone who ran to Him.
