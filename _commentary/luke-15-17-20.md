The son rehearses a speech in a pigsty, and his father ruins the plan by
running.

## What brings him home

*How many hired men on my father's place have more bread than they can eat, and
here I am starving to death.*

Luke is honest about the motive. It is hunger, and a comparison — the servants at
home eat better than this.

Nothing here is presented as a noble repentance. He *came to his senses*, which
is the language of a man waking up rather than a man reforming.

## The speech he prepares

Three parts: I have sinned against heaven, I have sinned against you, **take me
on as one of your hired men**.

The third part is a proposal. He has worked out terms — a job, a wage, a way to
be useful and stay fed without claiming anything.

## What the father sees

*While he was still a long way off, his father saw him.*

The distance is the detail. To see someone that far away you have to have been
looking, and to recognise him at that range you have to have been looking for a
while.

## The running

*His heart went out to the boy, and he ran — he ran down the road to meet him.*

In that culture, an elderly man did not run. It required hitching up the robe and
exposing the legs, and it was undignified in a way that would have been noticed
by the whole village.

He does it anyway, and he does it before a word of the speech is spoken. The
embrace and the kiss come first; the confession arrives afterwards, and the third
part of it never gets said at all.

> **What the speech never reached.** *Take me on as one of your hired men* is cut
> off by the father calling for the best robe. The bargain the son had worked out
> is not refused so much as never allowed into the conversation. See the note on
> Luke 15:21–24.
