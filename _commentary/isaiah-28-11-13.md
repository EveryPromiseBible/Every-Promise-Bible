A sentence about rest, sitting in the middle of one of the harshest chapters in Isaiah.

## Who is being addressed

The chapter opens on *the drunkards of Ephraim* and moves to the priests and prophets of
Jerusalem: *they have erred through wine… they err in vision, they stumble in
judgment.*

The people mishandling the word of God are drunk.

## What they had been mocking

Their own words are quoted in verse 10: *tsav latsav, tsav latsav, qav laqav, qav
laqav, zeer sham, zeer sham* — a sing-song jingle, mocking the prophet as though he were
teaching toddlers their letters.

## The answer

*So then, with **stammering lips and a foreign tongue** He will speak to this people.*

They complained that the prophet's Hebrew sounded like baby talk. They will hear a
language they genuinely cannot understand.

The Assyrian army spoke it. Isaiah is describing an invasion.

## The offer they refused

*The ones to whom He said, "**This is the resting place, let the weary rest here, this is
the place of repose**."*

The Hebrew is **menuchah** — rest, a settled place, a stopping.

It shares a root with the name **Noah**, and it is the word in Psalm 23: *He leads me
beside the still waters* — literally, *waters of rest*.

The offer had been made and it was for the weary.

## The refusal

*But **they would not listen**.*

Four words, and the whole chapter turns on them.

## What comes instead

*So the word of the LORD will become for them **command upon command, command upon
command, rule upon rule, rule upon rule, a little here, a little there**.*

Their own mocking jingle, handed back to them as the shape of what they will get.

Having refused the rest, what is left is the accumulation — line after line, rule after
rule, endlessly.

## The outcome

*So that they **go and fall backward, and are broken and snared and captured**.*

Four verbs, each worse than the last.

## Where Paul uses it

*"With men of other tongues and other lips I will speak to this people, and even so they
will not hear Me," says the Lord.*

He quotes this passage in 1 Corinthians 14 and draws his conclusion about tongues as a
sign from it. See the note on 1 Corinthians 14:20–25.

## The verse that follows in the chapter

*Therefore thus says the Lord GOD: "**Behold, I lay in Zion a stone for a foundation, a
tried stone, a precious cornerstone, a sure foundation. Whoever believes will not act
hastily**."*

Peter quotes it. Paul quotes it. In the middle of the chapter about the jingle and the
foreign tongue, Isaiah lays a cornerstone.

## The other rest

*"There remains therefore **a rest for the people of God**." Let us **be diligent to enter
that rest**, lest anyone fall according to the same example of disobedience.* See the
note on Hebrews 4:8–11.

Hebrews uses the same argument shape: an offer of rest, a refusal, and the offer still
standing.

> **What was on offer.** *This is the resting place, let the weary rest here.* The people
> it was said to were the ones who would not have it — and Isaiah records the sentence
> anyway.
