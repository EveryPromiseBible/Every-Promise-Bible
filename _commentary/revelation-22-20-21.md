Two verses, and the Bible is finished.

## The testimony

*The One who gives this testimony says, **Yes — I am coming, and soon**.*

The Greek is **nai, erchomai tachu**.

He has said it three times in this chapter: *behold, I am coming quickly* at verse 7, again at
verse 12, and now at verse 20 with **nai** — yes — in front of it.

## The word for soon

**Tachu** — quickly, suddenly. It describes the manner as much as the interval.

## The answer

*Amen. **Come, Lord Jesus**.*

The Greek is **erchou, Kyrie Iesou**.

Paul preserves the Aramaic of the same prayer at the end of 1 Corinthians: **Maranatha** — our
Lord, come.

The church's oldest recorded prayer, in the language they actually prayed it in, and John ends
the canon with the Greek of it.

## What has just been offered

*And the Spirit and the bride say, "Come!" And let him who hears say, "Come!" And **let him who
thirsts come. Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely**.* See the note on
Revelation 22:16–17.

Four *comes* in one verse, three of them inviting people in and one calling Christ back.

## The closing line

*The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.*

The Greek is **he charis tou Kyriou Iesou Christou meta panton**.

## Where the line comes from

It is a letter-ending.

Paul closes every letter he wrote with some form of it: *the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be
with you*. Revelation is addressed to seven churches and it signs off like the mail it is.

## The last word of the Bible

**Grace**, and then **amen**.

The Old Testament's last word is **cherem** — the ban, total destruction, the threat held off by
hearts turning between parents and children. See the note on Malachi 4:4–6.

The two canons end four words apart in Hebrew and Greek, and they are opposite words.

## Who it is for

*With you all* — **meta panton**.

Not with the overcomers, the sealed, the hundred and forty-four thousand, or the ones whose
churches got a good report in chapters 2 and 3. All of them.

The book that has spent twenty-two chapters distinguishing closes without distinguishing.

## What comes immediately before

The warning about adding to or taking from the words of the prophecy, and then this.

A severe sentence, and then grace, in that order.

## What the book opened with

*Grace to you and peace from Him who is and who was and who is to come.* See the note on
Revelation 1:4–6.

Grace at the beginning and grace at the end, with the seals, trumpets, bowls, and the throne in
between.

> **The last word.** Grace — spoken over *all*, in a book that spent twenty-two chapters
> distinguishing, and four words away from the curse the other canon ends on.
