They cross a lake in a storm to reach one man, and he is the first thing on the shore.

## The crossing

*They came across to the other side of the lake, into **the country of the Gadarenes**.*

Gentile territory. The Decapolis, east of the Galilee, with Greek cities and a pig-farming
economy.

## What the crossing cost

The night before: *a great windstorm arose, and the waves beat into the boat*, and *He was in the
stern, asleep on a pillow*.

*Peace, be still.* See the note on Mark 4:35–41.

The storm, and then this. Mark puts them side by side.

## The timing

*And **the moment Jesus stepped out of the boat**, a man came at Him out of the burial caves.*

The Greek is **euthus** — immediately. Mark's favourite word, used over forty times.

No interval. He gets out of the boat and the man is there.

## Where the man lived

*He lived out there **among the graves**.*

Limestone tombs cut into hillsides, large enough to shelter in. In Jewish law, the most defiling
place there is.

A man in a Gentile country, living in a cemetery, near a herd of pigs. Mark stacks the
uncleanness up.

## The restraints

*Nobody could keep him tied down anymore. They had tried it many times **with shackles and
chains**.*

The Greek distinguishes them: **pedais** — foot irons, and **halusesin** — chains.

The community had tried. Repeatedly. The verse records the attempts, not just the failure.

## What happened to them

*Every time **he tore the chains apart and broke the irons off his legs**.*

The Greek is **diespasthai** — pulled apart. And **suntetriphthai** — shattered, rubbed to
pieces.

## The verdict

*And **no one on earth was strong enough to bring him under control**.*

The Greek is **oudeis ischuen auton damasai** — nobody had the strength to tame him.

**Damazo** — the word for breaking a wild animal. James uses it of the tongue: *every kind of
beast has been tamed… but no man can tame the tongue*.

## The days and nights

*Night and day he was out **among the tombs and up in the hills, crying out**.*

The Greek is **krazon** — screaming.

No sleep is mentioned. The description covers the whole clock.

## The last detail

*And **cutting himself with stones**.*

The Greek is **katakopton heauton lithois**.

Mark alone records it. It is the detail that stops the passage being about strength.

The chains failed, and the community's only concern was containment. Nobody in the account had
been trying to help him.

## What he does when Jesus arrives

*When he saw Jesus from afar, **he ran and worshiped Him**.*

The man who could not be approached runs toward Him.

And then: *what have I to do with You, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I implore You by God that
You do not torment me.* See the note on Mark 5:6–13.

## The name

*"**What is your name?**" And he answered, saying, "**My name is Legion; for we are many**."*

A Roman legion was six thousand men.

## What he looks like afterwards

*They came to Jesus, and saw the one who had been demon-possessed and had the legion, **sitting
and clothed and in his right mind**.*

Sitting — the man who had never stopped moving. Clothed. Sane.

*And they were afraid.*

## What he asks for

To go with Him. And the answer is no.

*"**Go home to your friends, and tell them what great things the Lord has done for you**."*

The only person in Mark told to publicise it, and the only Gentile.

*And he departed and began to proclaim in the Decapolis all that Jesus had done for him, and all
marveled.*

> **What Mark adds.** *Cutting himself with stones.* Everyone in the story had been trying to hold
> him down; nobody had noticed that the danger to him was himself.
