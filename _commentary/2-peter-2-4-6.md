Peter builds an argument out of three historical cases, and every one of them is
treated as fact.

## The three

- **the angels who sinned** — thrown down, chained, kept for judgement
- **the ancient world** — the flood, with eight kept alive
- **Sodom and Gomorrah** — burned to ash

Three, in chronological order, and in each one the same two things happen: judgement
falls, and somebody is preserved through it.

## The pit

*He threw them down into **the pit**.*

The Greek is **tartaroo** — a verb built from *Tartarus*, and it appears nowhere
else in the New Testament.

In Greek thought Tartarus was the deepest place, below Hades, where the Titans were
confined. Peter borrows the word and uses it once.

## The chains

*He handed them over to **chains and to the dark**, and there they are being kept
until the judgement.*

Jude says the same in his own words: *angels who would not stay in the place that
was theirs; they walked out of their own home. He has them now in chains that will
never break.* See the note on Jude 1:5–7.

Two letters, independently, describing angels held in confinement for something
specific, and both put it next to Sodom and the flood.

## The eight

*He kept **eight alive, Noah among them**, a man who never stopped saying what was
right.*

The Greek is *a preacher of righteousness* — a herald.

That is the only place in Scripture we are told Noah preached. Genesis records the
building and not a word of the speaking, and Peter supplies it.

A hundred and twenty years of hammering, and a man telling anyone who came by what
it was for. Eight got in.

## Ash

*He burned Sodom and Gomorrah down **until there was nothing left but ash**.*

The Greek verb is to reduce to ashes — **tephroo**.

And the purpose: *He made of them **the specimen** of what is coming.* The word is
**hupodeigma**, a pattern, an exhibit, something set out to be looked at.

## What the argument is for

Peter is answering scoffers who say *where is the promise of His coming? Everything
goes on as it always has.*

His reply is a list of three times it did not. And the whole passage is one long
conditional, completed in verse 9: *then the Lord knows how to deliver the godly out
of temptations, and to reserve the unjust under punishment for the day of
judgement.*

The three cases are evidence for a rescue, not only for a sentence.

## Where the same comparison turns up

Jesus used it Himself: *as it was in the days of Noah… and likewise as it was in
the days of Lot.* Same two examples, same order.

> **The word Peter uses for Lot.** *Righteous Lot, who was oppressed by the filthy
> conduct of the wicked… **tormented his righteous soul** from day to day.* Genesis
> gives Lot a poor account of himself, and Peter calls him righteous three times in
> two verses.
