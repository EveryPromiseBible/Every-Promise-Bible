The last two verses of the book, and the accounting is done in faces.

## What has happened

The whirlwind, and four chapters of questions about wild goats, the ostrich, the horse, Behemoth
and Leviathan.

*I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, **but now my eye sees You**.* See the note on
Job 42:1–6.

And then the rebuke to the friends: *My wrath is aroused against you… **for you have not spoken of
Me what is right, as My servant Job has***.

Job prays for them, and *the LORD restored Job's losses when he prayed for his friends*.

## The doubling

*Indeed the LORD gave Job **twice as much as he had before**.*

Fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen, a thousand female donkeys.
Exactly double the opening inventory.

## The children

*He also had **seven sons and three daughters**.*

Not doubled. The same number as before.

The book does not say why, and the arithmetic is the kind readers notice.

## The daughters

*And he called the name of the first **Jemimah**, the name of the second **Keziah**, and the name of
the third **Keren-Happuch**.*

Dove, cassia, and horn of eye-paint.

The sons are not named. The daughters are, and the text adds *in all the land were found no women
so beautiful as the daughters of Job; and their father **gave them an inheritance among their
brothers***.

Which was not the ordinary practice.

## The years

*After all this Job lived **a hundred and forty years**.*

The Hebrew says nothing about his age when the trouble came. If a hundred and forty is what came
after, the whole disaster occupies a fraction of the life.

## What he saw

*And he saw **his children, and his children's children, four generations of them**.*

The Hebrew is **arba'ah dorot** — four generations.

Great-great-grandchildren.

## The phrase added

*Faces full of the future.*

The Illumination's rendering of what the Hebrew leaves implicit — a man looking at four rows of
descendants.

## The end

*And Job grew old, **full of days and full of gladness**.*

The Hebrew is **zaqen usava yamim** — old and satisfied of days.

The same phrase used of Abraham, of Isaac, and of David. See the note on
Genesis 25:7–10.

## The last clause

*And at last he died, **an old man whose end was richer than his beginning**.*

Which is the Illumination drawing out what verse 12 says: *the LORD blessed the latter days of
Job more than his beginning*.

## What is not restored

The ten children who died.

The new seven and three do not replace them. The book gives him more years, more livestock, and
more descendants, and does not pretend the first ten were returned.

## What he never learns

The reason.

The reader was told in chapter 1. Job is told about ostriches and rain on land where no man is,
and never about the wager.

## What James does with him

*Indeed we count them blessed who endure. **You have heard of the perseverance of Job and seen the
end intended by the Lord** — that the Lord is very compassionate and merciful.* See the note on
James 5:10–11.

**To telos Kuriou** — the end of the Lord.

James's word for what the book's last chapter is: an ending.

> **The arithmetic.** The livestock is doubled exactly. The children are not — seven sons and three
> daughters, the same count as the ten who died, and the book does not explain the difference.
