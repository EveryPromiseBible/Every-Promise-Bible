Joseph was asked to interpret a dream. He gives the interpretation and then, without
being asked, offers a national economic policy.

## Where he is standing

He was in a dungeon an hour ago. *They brought him quickly out of the dungeon; and he
shaved, changed his clothing, and came to Pharaoh.*

Thirteen years between the pit and this room — sold, enslaved, falsely accused,
imprisoned, and forgotten by the cupbearer for two more years.

## What he was asked for

An interpretation. Nothing else.

He gives it: seven years of plenty, seven of famine, *and the famine will deplete the
land.* And *the dream was repeated twice because the thing is established by God.*

## What he adds

*"So now, **let Pharaoh look for a discerning and wise man** and set him over the
land."*

Nobody requested advice. He supplies it anyway, in front of the most powerful man on
earth, straight out of a cell.

## The plan

- appoint **officers over the land**
- gather **a fifth** of the harvest during the seven good years
- **store up grain** under Pharaoh's authority
- **keep it safe in the cities**

A fifth — twenty per cent, set aside across seven years of surplus.

The Hebrew for *gather* is a taxation word. It is a levy, and it is planned before
there is any sign of trouble.

## What it is for

*"That food will be **a reserve for the land** through the seven years of famine…
**so the land will not perish**."*

The Hebrew for *reserve* is a deposit, something laid up.

The point is stated as survival rather than profit.

## What is not in the plan

Anything mystical. No prayer meeting, no further revelation, no waiting.

He is given seven years' notice and proposes storage.

## What happens

*So Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Since God has shown you all this, **there is no one as
discerning and wise as you**. You shall be over my house."*

He describes the qualifications for the job and is handed the job.

Egypt survives, and so does the family that threw him in the pit.

## Where the surplus goes

*Joseph gathered very much grain, as the sand of the sea, **until he stopped counting,
for it was immeasurable**.*

And when the famine came, *all countries came to Egypt to buy grain, because the
famine was severe in all lands.*

A nation with a plan feeds the world for seven years.

> **What he says to his brothers about all of it.** *You meant evil against me, but
> **God meant it for good**, in order to bring it about as it is this day, to save many
> people alive.* The dungeon and the granary are the same story, and he says so.
