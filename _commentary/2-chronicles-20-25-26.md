The gathering takes three times as long as the battle, and they were not in the battle.

## What they came for

*When Jehoshaphat and his people came to **collect the spoil**.*

They had marched out expecting a war. They arrive at a lookout point and find corpses. See the note
on 2 Chronicles 20:22–24.

## What was there

*They found **a great abundance of goods, garments, and precious things** among the bodies.*

The Hebrew is **rekush larov** — property in abundance.

## Where it came from

The three invading nations had brought their supply train.

An army on campaign carries everything: provisions, valuables, plunder taken on the way. All of it
is standing in the field with nobody left to guard it.

## The problem

*They stripped away **more than they could carry**.*

The Hebrew is **ve'ein masa** — and there was no carrying. Beyond transport.

## The duration

*And still it took them **three days to gather it all in**.*

Three days of collecting, after a battle that lasted as long as it took the singers to start.

## The ratio

The fighting: none. The singing: one song. The gathering: three days.

## What they had said before it

*"**We have no power against this great multitude that is coming against us; nor do we know what to
do, but our eyes are upon You**."* See the note on 2 Chronicles 20:10–12.

Three admissions in one sentence — no power, no plan, no idea — and then where they were looking.

## The fourth day

*On the fourth day **they assembled in the Valley of Beracah, for there they blessed the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **berakah** — blessing.

## The name

*That is why the place has been called **the Valley of Beracah — the Valley of Blessing — to this
day**.*

They name the valley after what they did in it, not after what happened to the enemy in it.

## What the chronicler notes

The naming comes from an assembly held on a day when nothing was collected.

Three days of loading, and then a day spent doing nothing but blessing.

## What follows

*Then they returned, **every man of Judah and Jerusalem, with Jehoshaphat in front of them, to go
back to Jerusalem with joy**, for the LORD had made them rejoice over their enemies.*

*So they came to Jerusalem, with stringed instruments and harps and trumpets, to the house of the
LORD.*

They left with singers in front and come home with an orchestra.

## The result abroad

*And **the fear of God was on all the kingdoms of those countries** when they heard that the LORD had
fought against the enemies of Israel.*

*Then the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet, for his God gave him rest all around.*

## The other valley

*Yea, though I walk through the **valley of the shadow of death**, I will fear no evil.* See the note
on Psalms 23:4.

And the next line: *You prepare a table before me **in the presence of my enemies**… my cup runs
over*. See the note on Psalms 23:5.

A table laid where the enemies are, and a cup that overflows. This chapter is that psalm as an
afternoon's work.

> **The arithmetic.** No fighting, one song, three days of carrying — and then a whole day set aside
> in a valley, which is the day the place got its name.
