He is in the middle of the shepherd discourse, and He says the most surprising thing
in it about His own death.

## The other sheep

*"There are **other sheep of Mine that do not belong to this fold**. **I must bring
them in** as well."*

Said to a Jewish audience, in Jerusalem, at the Feast of Dedication.

The Greek is *I have* — present tense. They are already His before they are brought.

## "They will listen to My voice"

Not *they might*. The whole discourse works this way: *My sheep hear My voice, and I
know them, and they follow Me.*

## One flock, one shepherd

*"And there will be **one flock, one shepherd**."*

Not one fold. The fold is the enclosure — the pen, the boundary. The flock is the
animals.

Ezekiel had promised it: *I will set up one shepherd over them, and he shall feed
them — My servant David.*

Paul spends a chapter of Ephesians on the same sentence: *He has made both one, and has
broken down the middle wall of separation.* See the note on Ephesians 2:14–18.

## Why the Father loves Him

*"**This is why the Father loves Me: I lay down My life** so that I may take it up
again."*

He states a reason for the Father's love, and the reason is the cross.

## The four times He says it

In three verses He says *I lay it down* or *take it up* four times, and each time the
verb is His own:

- *I lay down My life so that I may take it up again*
- *No one takes it from Me*
- *I lay it down of My own accord*
- *I have the right to lay it down, and the right to take it up*

## "No one takes it from Me"

Said to men who were picking up stones in the same chapter, in a city where a Roman
governor and a temple establishment would shortly do exactly that.

John's account of the death keeps the verse: *He bowed His head and **gave up** His
spirit.* Not *expired*. He handed it over.

Pilate is told the same thing at the trial: *you could have no power at all against Me
unless it had been given you from above.*

## The right

The Greek is **exousia** — authority, the right to act. He has it twice: to lay it down
and to take it up again.

The taking up again is claimed by Him here, and elsewhere in the New Testament the
resurrection is attributed to the Father and to the Spirit. All three, on the same
morning.

## The charge

*"**This is what My Father has charged Me to do**."*

Commandment, in the Greek. The laying down is obedience as well as choice, and the
chapter puts both in one sentence without deciding between them.

> **Where the discourse goes next.** *And there was a division again among the Jews
> because of these sayings.* John records the reaction to the shepherd chapter twice,
> and both times it splits the room.
