Martha has correct doctrine and a dead brother, and Jesus moves the thing she
believes from a date to a Person.

## What He says first

*"**Your brother will rise again.**"*

Deliberately ambiguous. He means in about twenty minutes. She hears something else.

## What she does with it

*"I know he will rise again, **in the resurrection on the last day**."*

She is not wrong. That was standard Jewish belief and Jesus taught it Himself.

But she has taken a promise about *now* and filed it under a date far enough away
to be no comfort at all. Her brother has been dead four days.

## The correction

*"**I am** the resurrection and the life."*

Not *there will be* a resurrection. **I am** it.

He takes an event she was waiting for and identifies it as Himself, standing in
front of her, in a village outside Jerusalem, on a Thursday.

It is the fifth of the seven *I am* sayings in John, and the only one spoken at a
graveside.

## The two halves

*"Whoever believes in Me **will live, even if he dies**. And everyone who is alive
and believes in Me **will never die at all**."*

Two different people are described.

The first has died — and lives anyway. Physical death happens and does not
terminate anything.

The second is alive and believing, and of him the Greek is emphatic: *ou me
apothane eis ton aiona* — a double negative, shall never ever die, unto the age.

Whatever death is for a believer in that sentence, it is not the thing the word
normally means. The New Testament's habit of calling it **sleep** — *our friend
Lazarus sleeps*, four verses of this chapter earlier — follows from it.

## The question

*"**Do you believe this?**"*

He puts it directly, to a grieving woman, before doing anything about the grave.

## Her answer

*"Yes, Lord. I believe that You are the Messiah, the Son of God, the One who was to
come into the world."*

She does not answer the question He asked. She answers a bigger one — not *do you
believe this claim* but *I believe who You are*.

Her confession is nearly word for word Peter's at Caesarea Philippi, and John puts
it in the mouth of a woman in a village, mid-funeral.

## What He does next

Weeps. Then *Lazarus, come forth.*

> **Why He waited.** John records it plainly and it is uncomfortable: *when He heard
> that he was sick, **He stayed two more days** in the place where He was.* And to
> the disciples: *Lazarus is dead. And I am glad for your sakes that I was not
> there, that you may believe.*
