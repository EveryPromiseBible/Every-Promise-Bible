Two short clauses in the middle of Paul's chapter on resurrection, and the second
one is startling.

## The chain

*The **sting of death is sin**, and the **strength of sin is the law**.*

Three things linked backwards: death gets its sting from sin, and sin gets its
power from the law.

Remove the last link and the whole chain goes slack.

## "Strength"

The Greek is **dunamis** — power, the word behind dynamite. Not *the measure of
sin* or *the definition of sin*. Its power source.

Paul says the same thing at length in Romans 7: *I would not have known covetousness
unless the law had said, "You shall not covet." But sin, taking opportunity by the
commandment, produced in me all manner of evil desire.*

And: *the commandment which was to bring life, I found to bring death.*

## Which does not make the law bad

Paul defends it in the same breath: *the law is holy, and the commandment holy and
just and good.*

The fault is not in the statute. It is in what a prohibition does when it meets the
old nature — a wall to push against, a line to notice.

Sin is not weakened by being told to stop. It is armed.

## Why this is in a chapter about resurrection

Paul has just quoted Hosea: *O Death, where is your sting? O Hades, where is your
victory?* It is a taunt, thrown at a defeated enemy.

Then he explains what made the sting sharp in the first place — and the two clauses
are there to show that the whole apparatus has been dismantled at the root, not
merely at the grave.

## The answer

*But **thanks be to God, who gives us the victory** through our Lord Jesus
Christ.*

Not *who helps us achieve* it. Gives — present tense, continuous in the Greek.

The victory is handed over, and the passage names where it came from and does not
name any contribution.

## What the chapter says next

*Therefore, my beloved brethren, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the
work of the Lord, knowing that **your labour is not in vain** in the Lord.*

The one command in the paragraph comes after the victory has been given, not
before it.

> **Where the chain is broken.** *You are not under law but under grace* — Romans
> 6:14, and the promise attached to it is *sin shall not have dominion over you.*
> Paul puts the two in one sentence, in that order. See the note on Romans 6:12–14.
