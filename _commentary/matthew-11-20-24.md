Jesus names three towns and says they are worse off than the cities everyone in
His audience would have named as the worst.

## The comparison

- **Chorazin and Bethsaida** measured against **Tyre and Sidon** — the Phoenician
  cities the prophets denounced for centuries
- **Capernaum** measured against **Sodom**

And in each case the verdict runs the same way: those places would have turned if
they had seen what these towns saw.

## What Capernaum had

It was His base of operations. Peter's house was there, and the synagogue. Matthew
was called at its tax booth. The centurion's servant, Peter's mother-in-law,
Jairus' daughter — the concentration of miracles in that one town is remarkable.

*You were lifted up to the sky* is a quotation aimed originally at the king of
Babylon in Isaiah 14, and the second half of the sentence comes with it.

## What they had not done

*Because they had not changed their minds.*

Not because they attacked Him, and not because they drove Him out — Nazareth did
that and is not on this list. The charge is that nothing changed.

They watched, and stayed the same.

## The principle underneath

More evidence means more responsibility. Jesus says it elsewhere in plainer terms
— to whom much is given, much will be required.

That cuts toward anyone with easy access to Scripture, sermons, and a Bible on
their phone.
