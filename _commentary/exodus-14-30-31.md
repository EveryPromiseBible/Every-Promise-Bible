The morning after, and what they see on the sand settles the matter.

## What the day was

*That day the **LORD saved Israel from the hand of Egypt**.*

The Hebrew is **yasha** — the verb behind **yeshua**, salvation. Its first major appearance
in the story of Israel is here.

Moses had used it the night before: *stand still, and **see the salvation of the LORD**,
which He will accomplish for you today.*

## What they saw

*And Israel **saw the Egyptians lying still along the shore**.*

The Hebrew is *dead upon the sea shore*.

They had watched the army come at them from behind — *the Egyptians marched after them, and
they were very afraid*. Now the same army is on the beach, and they are looking down at it.

## The change of position

The night before, the enemy was between them and the wilderness and they were pinned against
water.

In the morning they are across the water and the enemy is on the near side, face down.

Nothing about the geography changed except which side they were standing on.

## What it produced

*When Israel saw the great power the LORD had shown against Egypt, the people **stood in awe
of the LORD, and they trusted Him** — and they **trusted Moses His servant**.*

The Hebrew is *they feared the LORD and believed the LORD and His servant Moses*.

The fear and the belief are the same reaction, named twice.

## The last sentence

*They had **done nothing but walk and be still**, and **God had carried them through**.*

Moses' instruction had been exactly that: *the LORD will fight for you, and **you shall hold
your peace***.

The Hebrew for that is *you shall be silent* — **tacharishun**.

## What they had said the day before

*"**Is it because there are no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the
wilderness?** It would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than that we should
die in the wilderness."*

Complaint, on the record, hours before the crossing.

## The two redemptions

The blood on the doorposts got them out of Egypt. See the note on Exodus 12:1–13.

The sea got Egypt out of the way behind them. Two separate events, three days apart, and the
second one is the one they sing about.

Chapter 15 is the song: *the horse and its rider He has thrown into the sea… the LORD is a
man of war; the LORD is His name.*

## What Hebrews notices

*By faith they **passed through the Red Sea as by dry land**, whereas the Egyptians,
**attempting to do so, were drowned**.*

Two groups walked into the same corridor. The Greek for the second one is **peiran
labontes** — taking a trial of it, making an attempt. See the note on
Hebrews 11:23–29.

The water was identical. The difference is in the verbs.

## What Paul does with it

*All were **baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea**… now all these things happened
to them as examples, and they were written for our admonition.* See the note on
1 Corinthians 10:11–13.

He treats the crossing as a picture of something that happened to the reader.

## How long the belief lasted

Three days. *And when they came to Marah, they could not drink the waters of Marah, for they
were bitter… and the people complained against Moses.*

The chapter ends with them trusting, and the next one has them thirsty and saying so.

> **What they contributed.** The text says it in a sentence: they walked, and they kept
> quiet. The bodies on the shore were there when they looked up.
