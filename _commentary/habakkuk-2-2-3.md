The answer to a man standing on a wall, and it begins with an instruction about
handwriting.

## The response

*Then **the LORD answered me**.*

He had gone to his post expecting a correction. See the note on Habakkuk 2:1.

He gets an assignment instead.

## The instruction

*Write the vision, He said. **Carve it clear on tablets**.*

The Hebrew is *inscribe it upon tablets* — **luchot**, the same word as the tablets of
stone.

Not a scroll. Something durable and public.

## The purpose

*So that **anyone who reads it can run to tell it**.*

The Hebrew is *that he may run who reads it*.

Readers have divided over whether it means a man running past can read it, or a man who
reads it runs to spread it. The Hebrew allows both, and both need the same thing: it has to
be legible at speed.

Plainness is the point of the instruction.

## The waiting

*For **the vision still waits for its appointed time**.*

The Hebrew is **mo'ed** — an appointed time, a set season. The word used of the feasts.

The delay is scheduled rather than accidental.

## The verb

*It is **racing toward the end**, and **it will not lie**.*

The Hebrew is *it hastens to the end and will not lie*.

Two things said in one clause: it is hurrying, and it will not deceive.

## The instruction about the wait

*If it **seems slow, wait for it**.*

The Hebrew is *though it tarries, wait for it*.

**Seems** is the operative word. The vision has a set time and it is hurrying; the slowness
is in the watching.

## The guarantee

*It will surely come. **It will not be late**.*

The Hebrew doubles it — *coming it will come, it will not delay*.

## What comes immediately after

*Behold, the proud, his soul is not upright in him; **but the just shall live by his
faith**.*

The Hebrew is **emunah** — faithfulness, steadfastness, reliability.

Paul quotes it in Romans and Galatians. Hebrews quotes it in chapter 10, and adds the next
lines: *now the just shall live by faith; but if anyone draws back, My soul has no pleasure
in him.*

Three New Testament citations of one sentence, spoken to a man waiting for something
overdue.

## What the verse is answering

Habakkuk had asked *how long*. The reply does not give a date. It says there is one, that
it is fixed, and that the thing is moving faster than it looks.

## What Hebrews does with the passage

*For **yet a little while, and He who is coming will come and will not tarry**.*

Quoted directly after *you have need of endurance, so that after you have done the will of
God, you may receive the promise*.

The context there is a congregation that had lost property and was under pressure to go
back. See the note on Hebrews 10:23–25.

## The five woes that follow

The rest of Habakkuk 2 is five woes on the empire the prophet was complaining about — and
in the middle of them: *for the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the
LORD, as the waters cover the sea.* See the note on Habakkuk 2:12–14.

> **What he is told to do about the delay.** Write it down, large enough to read at a run
> — and then the reason: the appointment exists, the thing is hurrying, and what looks
> slow is not late.
