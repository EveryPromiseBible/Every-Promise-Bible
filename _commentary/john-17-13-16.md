In the middle of the prayer, He asks for one thing and specifically declines to ask for
another.

## Where He is

*"Now **I am coming to You**, and I am saying these things **while I am still in the
world**."*

He prays out loud, in front of them, on purpose. John is recording a private
conversation the disciples were meant to overhear.

## What it is for

*"So that they may have **My joy made full in them**."*

His joy, not a joy of their own. The Greek is *fulfilled*, brought to the top.

Said hours before the arrest.

## The word given

*"**I have given them Your word**, and the world has hated them."*

The two clauses are joined as cause and effect. What He handed over is what draws the
hostility.

## The reason for the hatred

*"Because **they are not of the world, just as I am not of the world**."*

He says it twice in four verses — once in verse 14 and again in verse 16, identically.

John's Gospel repeats things when it wants them settled.

## The request He does not make

*"**I am not asking You to take them out of the world**."*

He states the prayer He is declining to pray.

They stay. Everything that follows in Acts — the beatings, the shipwrecks, the prisons,
the markets and synagogues and courtrooms — happens because of this sentence.

## The request He does make

*"**But to keep them from the evil one**."*

The Greek can be *from evil* or *from the evil one*; the same phrase ends the Lord's
Prayer.

**Keep** is **tereo** — to guard, to watch over, to hold in custody. He has already used
it of Himself in verse 12: *while I was with them, I kept them.*

He is handing over a watch.

## The two things held together

Not out of it, and kept inside it. The prayer asks for protection in the place rather
than removal from it.

## What He asks next

*"**Sanctify them by Your truth**. Your word is truth. **As You sent Me into the world,
I also have sent them** into the world."*

Sent in, not called out. The commission in the next breath repeats the decision.

## Who does the keeping

*"**I do not pray that You should take them out of the world, but that You should keep them from the
evil one.**"*

**Tereses autous** — that You would guard them. The one thing asked for the disciples in the prayer
is protection, and the one thing not asked for is removal.

*Now to Him who is **able to keep you from stumbling**, and to present you faultless before the
presence of His glory with exceeding joy.*

*Who are **kept by the power of God through faith** for salvation ready to be revealed in the last
time.*

**Jude** and **Peter** both use the same verb, and both put the keeping on God's side.

## What He says He has already done

*"**While I was with them in the world, I kept them in Your name… and none of them is lost except
the son of perdition.**"*

The tally is given before the arrest, and He gives it as a report rather than a hope.

*"This is the will of the Father who sent Me, that **of all He has given Me I should lose nothing**,
but should raise it up at the last day."*

*"**Neither shall anyone snatch them out of My hand.**"*

## And the joy in the middle of it

*"**These things I speak in the world, that they may have My joy fulfilled in themselves.**"*

Hours before Gethsemane, in a prayer that goes on to name the hatred of the world, the subject is
His joy being transferred.

*Who for **the joy that was set before Him** endured the cross.*

> **Who else is in the prayer.** *I do not pray for these alone, but also for those who
> will believe in Me through their word.* Two verses later, everybody reading it is
> named in it.
