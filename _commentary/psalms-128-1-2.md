One of the Songs of Ascents — the fifteen psalms pilgrims sang walking up to
Jerusalem — and it is about a house rather than a temple.

## Who is happy

*Happy is **everyone** who fears the LORD, who walks in His ways.*

The Hebrew is **ashrei** — the word Psalm 1 opens with, and the word behind
*blessed are* in the Beatitudes.

**Everyone.** Not the priest, the Levite, or the notable. The psalm is deliberately
ordinary from the first line.

## Eating what you grew

*You will **eat the fruit of your labour**.*

It sounds unremarkable until you set it beside the curse in Deuteronomy 28: *you
shall build a house and not dwell in it; you shall plant a vineyard and not eat of
it… your ox shall be slaughtered before your eyes and you shall not eat of it.*

That is the shape of the curse — working and not getting to keep it. The blessing
here is the plain reversal: you eat what your own hands produced.

Isaiah says the same thing about the world put right: *they shall not build and
another inhabit; they shall not plant and another eat.*

## Two things, twice

*You will be **happy**, and **it will go well with you**.*

The Hebrew doubles it — good for you, and well with you. Inside and outside.

## What the rest of the psalm shows

*Your wife shall be like a fruitful vine in the very heart of your house, your
children like olive plants all around your table.*

A vine in the middle of the house and young olive trees round a table. Both are
slow-growing plants that take years before they yield, and both go on yielding for
generations.

The picture is a meal. That is the whole vision of blessing in this psalm — a man
eating what he grew, at a table, with his family around it.

## Where the blessing comes from

*The LORD **bless you out of Zion**.*

Not out of the field. The blessing on the house is sourced at the sanctuary, and
the psalm is sung by people walking toward it.

## And how far it reaches

*May you see your children's children. Peace be upon Israel.*

Grandchildren, and then the nation. The psalm starts with one man's fear of God
and ends with peace on a whole people.

> **Where it sits.** Psalm 127 is the one before it — *unless the LORD builds the
> house, they labour in vain who build it… He gives His beloved sleep.* The two are
> a pair: one says the building is His, and then this one describes the house.
