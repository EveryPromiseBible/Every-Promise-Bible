One verse, and it is the hinge of the whole book.

## Who the four are

Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah — renamed Belteshazzar, Shadrach, Meshach and
Abednego by the chief of the eunuchs.

Judean nobility, deported as teenagers, selected for looks and aptitude and put into a
three-year Babylonian training programme.

## What they were being taught

*The language and literature of the Chaldeans* — astronomy, astrology, divination,
mathematics, and the mythology of Babylon.

The syllabus of a pagan court, taught by the wise men Daniel later has to compete with.

## What comes before this verse

The refusal of the king's food, the ten-day test on vegetables and water, and the result:
*at the end of ten days their features appeared better and fatter in flesh than all the
young men who ate the portion of the king's delicacies.*

They had asked rather than demanded — *Daniel requested of the chief of the eunuchs* — and
God had given him *favour and goodwill* with the man.

## The gift

*To these four young men **God gave knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom**.*

The Hebrew names three: **madda** (knowledge), **haskel** (skill, insight) and **chokmah**
(wisdom).

It covers the whole curriculum, including the parts of it that were pagan.

The text does not say they excelled by working harder. It says God gave it.

## The addition

*And **to Daniel He gave more still: understanding in every kind of vision and dream**.*

The Hebrew is **chazon** — the vision word — and **chalomot**, dreams.

One of the four gets something the other three do not.

## What it is for

The whole book runs on it. Nebuchadnezzar's statue in chapter 2, the tree in chapter 4,
the writing on the wall in chapter 5, the ram and goat in chapter 8, the seventy weeks in
chapter 9, and the visions of chapters 10 to 12.

Daniel is the only man in the Old Testament who both receives visions and interprets other
people's.

## What he says about it later

Standing in front of Nebuchadnezzar, asked how he did it:

*"**This mystery was not opened to me because I have more wisdom than anyone living**."* See
the note on Daniel 2:29–30.

He declines the credit at the moment it is offered.

## The result of the training

*And in all matters of wisdom and understanding about which the king examined them, **he
found them ten times better** than all the magicians and astrologers who were in all his
realm.*

They were examined by the king and outperformed the professionals.

## Where Joseph stands beside him

Pharaoh's assessment of Joseph is nearly identical: *can we find anyone like this man, one
in whom the Spirit of God lives?* See the note on Genesis 41:37–45.

Two Hebrews in foreign courts, both raised by interpreting a king's dream, both given a
new name and a wife by the crown.

## How long Daniel lasted

*And Daniel continued **until the first year of King Cyrus**.*

Deported under Nebuchadnezzar, still in office when Babylon fell to Persia — roughly
seventy years, through four kings and two empires.

> **What the verse divides.** All four are given the whole curriculum. One is given
> something extra, and the extra is the reason there is a book.
