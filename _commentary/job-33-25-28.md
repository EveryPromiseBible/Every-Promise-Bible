Elihu is speaking — the young man who waited for the three friends to finish —
and this is the best passage in his long speech.

## The restoration

*Then his flesh grows fresh again, softer than a child's. He returns to the
strength of his young days.*

The same picture as Naaman coming up out of the Jordan with skin like a little
boy's. Not repaired. Made new.

And the second line adds the strength back — not merely healed, but restored to
what he could once do.

## What the word "then" is attached to

*Then* is the first word of the passage, and it is pointing back at something.

Elihu has described a man brought to the edge — laid low on his bed, his flesh
wasting until you can hardly see it, his life inching toward the messengers of
death. Then a messenger comes and shows him God's righteousness, and God says: *I
have found the price for his release.*

**Then** his flesh grows fresh again.

The order is fixed and it does not run the other way. Ransom found, and the
renewal follows it. Nothing in these four verses is produced by the sick man's
effort — he is on a bed, unable to eat, and a price is found for him by Somebody
else. See the note on Job 33:23–24.

The Hebrew for *ransom* is **kopher**, a covering, the price paid to buy back a
forfeited life — and it is what the New Testament is built on: *the Son of Man
came to give His life a ransom for many.*

## What the man then does

*He prays to God, and God welcomes him with delight. He looks on God's face and
shouts for joy.*

Three things: prayer, a welcome, and then his face lifted. The Hebrew for
*welcomes him with delight* is the word for accepting a sacrifice as pleasing.

## The confession he makes afterwards

*I sinned. I twisted what was straight, and it gained me nothing.*

It comes **after** the rescue, not before it — he is singing it to everyone as
testimony. The order in Elihu's account is: ransom found, life restored, and then
the honest account of what he had done.

> **Who Elihu was.** God's verdict at the end names Eliphaz, Bildad and Zophar and
> tells them they have not spoken what is right. Elihu is never rebuked, never
> corrected, and never answered by Job — who argued with all three of the others
> and has nothing to say to this one. He is the only voice in the book that speaks
> of a mediator and a ransom, and the storm that brings God's own voice comes
> immediately after he has finished.
