Luke's picture of what the first weeks actually looked like, and it is domestic.

## Two places

*Day after day they were together **in the temple courts**… and they broke bread
**from house to house**.*

Both, and they are different activities. The temple was public, Jewish, and
enormous — the courts held tens of thousands. The houses were small.

The teaching and the praise happened in the open; the meals happened where people
lived.

## "Day after day"

Not weekly. Luke says it twice in two verses — daily in the temple, and the Lord
adding to their number daily.

Whatever the shape of the early church was, it was not a Sunday event.

## Breaking bread

The phrase covers an ordinary meal and carries the memory of the Last Supper, and
Luke has just used it a few verses earlier as one of the four things they devoted
themselves to.

He does not separate the two. They *ate their meals with glad and generous
hearts* — the eating and the remembering are described in one sentence, at
somebody's table.

## The two words for how

- **glad** — the Greek is a strong word for rejoicing, exultation
- **generous** — literally *simplicity* of heart, the same root as the word
  behind God giving *with both hands* in James 1:5. Single-minded, without a
  hidden second motive.

That is how Luke describes the atmosphere: glad and unguarded.

## What it produced

*Praising God and finding favour with all the people. And the Lord added to the
church daily.*

The favour is with outsiders, and the adding is God's doing. Neither is presented
as the goal of the meals — both are reported as what followed.

## Who does the adding

*"**And the Lord added to the church daily those who were being saved.**"*

**Prosetithei** — imperfect, continuous. And the subject is named.

Nothing in the paragraph describes a strategy. **Luke** lists what they did — temple, homes, bread,
gladness, praise — and then credits the growth to somebody who is not in the list.

*"**I will build My church**, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it."*

*I planted, Apollos watered, but **God gave the increase**.*

## What the two words mean

*"**With gladness and simplicity of heart.**"*

**Agalliasei kai apheloteti** — exultation, and singleness. **Aphelotes** occurs once in the New
Testament and means without folds, uncomplicated.

*Who for **the joy that was set before Him** endured the cross.*

*"These things I have spoken to you, **that My joy may remain in you**, and that your joy may be
full."*

The gladness is not manufactured for the occasion. **John** 15 has it described as transferred.

## Having favour with all the people

*Having favour with all the people.* **Charin** — the same noun the New Testament uses for grace.

Four chapters later the same crowd is stoning Stephen. **Luke** reports the honeymoon without
pretending it lasted.

> **What is not in the picture.** No building, no programme, no name for the
> movement yet. A temple courtyard, other people's houses, and a shared table
> every day.
