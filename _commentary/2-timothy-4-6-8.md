The last words we have from Paul, written from a Roman cell with no expectation
of release.

## "Poured out like an offering"

The image is a drink offering — wine poured out beside a sacrifice, the last act
of a ritual and the smallest part of it. Paul does not compare himself to the
sacrifice. He is the libation poured alongside one.

*The hour of my going is here* — and the word for going is **analusis**, the noun
of the verb he used in Philippians for casting off. Same picture, years later,
with the ship now actually leaving.

## The three perfects

*I have fought the good fight. I have finished the race. I have kept the faith.*

All three are in the perfect tense — done, and the result stands. Note what he
does not claim: he does not say he won, or that he did it well. He says he
fought, he finished, he kept.

The middle one is not about coming first. The Greek is simply completing the
course.

## The crown

*Laid aside and kept for me* — a verb meaning reserved, put away for someone. It
is already in storage.

And then the line that stops it being about Paul:

> and not to me only, but to every last one who has loved His appearing.

The condition for the crown, stated by a man about to be executed, is not
achievement or office or martyrdom. It is having loved the fact that He is
coming.

> **Read on to verse 9.** Immediately after this — the crown, the finished race,
> the settled peace — Paul writes *do your best to come to me quickly*, and later
> asks for his cloak and his books. The grandeur and the cold cell are three
> sentences apart, and both are in the letter because both were true.
