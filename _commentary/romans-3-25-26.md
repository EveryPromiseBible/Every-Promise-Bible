The densest two verses Paul wrote, and the whole letter turns on them.

## What comes before

*But now the righteousness of God apart from the law is revealed… **for all have sinned and
fall short of the glory of God, being justified freely by His grace** through the redemption
that is in Christ Jesus.* See the note on Romans 3:23–24.

**Dorean** — freely, as a gift. The same word Revelation uses of the water at the end.

## The setting forth

*God **publicly set Him forth**.*

The Greek is **proetheto** — set out in front, displayed publicly.

Not hidden, not private. The word suggests something put where it can be seen.

## The word

*As **the place where His anger is satisfied and His mercy is met**.*

The Greek is **hilasterion**.

In the Greek Old Testament it is the standard word for the **kapporet** — the lid of the ark,
the mercy seat, where the blood was sprinkled once a year over the tablets of the law.

Hebrews uses the same word of it: *above it were the cherubim of glory overshadowing the mercy
seat*.

Paul says Christ is that.

## Through what

*This comes to us **through faith in His blood**.*

The blood is the Day of Atonement detail. The high priest went in with it, alone, once a year,
and sprinkled it on that lid.

## The first purpose

*God did it to **show His own rightness**, because **in His patience He had passed over the
sins committed before**.*

The Greek is **paresin** — a passing over, an overlooking. Not the same word as forgiveness.

This is the problem Paul is solving, and it is not the one most readers expect.

For centuries God had let sins go. David committed adultery and murder and was told *the LORD
has put away your sin*. On what basis?

An accountant who ignores debts has a problem. Paul says God's forbearance had left a question
hanging over His own justice.

## The second purpose

*And He did it to show His rightness **in this present hour**.*

Now. The cross settles the past accounts and the present ones.

## The sentence

*That **He Himself is just, and that He is the One who puts right the person who believes in
Jesus**.*

The Greek is **dikaion kai dikaiounta** — just and justifying.

That is the whole difficulty in four words. Anybody can be lenient. Anybody can be strict.

Doing both, on the same case, without either being a pretence, is the thing the passage claims
happened.

## The psalm about it

*Righteousness and peace have kissed.* See the note on Psalms 85:10–13.

One insists the account be settled and the other insists it be over, and the psalm puts them
face to face.

Paul is describing where they met.

## What follows immediately

*Where is boasting then? **It is excluded**. By what law? Of works? No, but by the law of
faith.*

The Greek is **exekleisthe** — shut out. A door closed on it.

## What he does next

Chapter 4: Abraham, and *he believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness* —
said of a man before he was circumcised and four hundred years before the law.

## Where it lands

*Therefore, having been justified by faith, **we have peace with God** through our Lord Jesus
Christ.* See the note on Romans 5:1–2.

> **The problem being solved.** Not how a sinner can be forgiven. How God could have been
> letting it go for two thousand years and still be called just — and Paul says the cross
> answers both.
