She was told to lie at his feet and wait to be told what to do. She speaks first, and what she
says is not what she was told to say.

## The waking

**3:8** *In the middle of the night **something startled him**. He turned — and there, at his feet, lay
a woman.*

The Hebrew is **vayyecherad** — he trembled. It is a strong word, used of Isaac when he
realised he had blessed the wrong son.

## The question

**3:9** *"**Who are you?**"*

## What she answers

**3:9** again: *"**I am Ruth, your servant.**"*

She gives her name, and the word she uses of herself is **amah** — a maidservant with standing
to be married, rather than the lower word she had used of herself in chapter 2.

## What she asks

**3:9** again: *"**Spread the corner of your garment over me**, for you are a kinsman-redeemer."*

The Hebrew for *corner* is **kanaph** — a wing, and also the hem of a robe.

## Where the same words came from

Boaz had blessed her in the field: *the LORD repay your work, and a full reward be given you by
the LORD God of Israel, **under whose wings you have come for refuge**.* See the note on
Ruth 2:11–12.

He said *under His wings*. She takes his own word back to him and asks him to be the wing.

## The other place it is used

**Ezekiel 16:8**: *When I passed by you and saw you… **I spread My wing over you and covered your nakedness**.
Yes, I swore an oath to you and entered into a covenant with you, and you became Mine.*

Ezekiel, describing what God did for Jerusalem. It is marriage language, and it is God's.

## The word she uses of him

**Goel** — kinsman-redeemer.

The office is set out in Leviticus 25: a relative near enough, able to pay, and willing to
act, who buys back what a family has lost. See the note on Leviticus 25:23–28.

Three conditions, and all three have to hold. A stranger cannot do it however rich. A relative
cannot do it if he has nothing. And no law can compel a man who does not want to.

## Who took the office

Hebrews says it of Jesus, **Hebrews 2:17**: *in all things He had to be **made like His brethren**, that He might
be a merciful and faithful High Priest.*

Near enough — He took flesh and blood, **Hebrews 2:14**: *that through death He might destroy him who had the
power of death*. Able to pay, **1 Peter 1:18**: *not with corruptible things, like silver or gold*. Willing,
**John 10:18**: *no one takes it from Me, but I lay it down of Myself.*

> **What she asks for.** Not food, and not a wage. She asks the man who told her she had come
> under God's wings to put his own over her.
