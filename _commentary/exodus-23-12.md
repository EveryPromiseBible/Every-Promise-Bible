The sabbath commandment restated, and this version gives the reason in terms of somebody
else.

## Where it sits

Immediately after the sabbath year — six years of sowing and a seventh of letting the
land go, so the poor may eat. See the note on Exodus 23:10–11.

The same shape at two scales: one year in seven for the ground, one day in seven for the
household.

## The command

*Six days do your work, but **on the seventh day rest**.*

The Hebrew is **shabath** — to cease, to stop. The word is about stopping rather than
about resting up.

## Who it is for

*So **your ox and your donkey may rest**, and **the son of your maidservant and the
foreigner may be refreshed**.*

Four parties named, and the householder is not one of them.

- **The ox and the donkey** — the working animals
- **The son of your maidservant** — a household slave born in the house
- **The foreigner** — the resident alien, with no legal standing

The list runs from livestock to the people with the least claim on the household. Every
one of them is somebody who could not have taken a day off on their own account.

## The word for refreshed

The Hebrew is **naphash** — from **nephesh**, the soul, the breath. To take breath, to be
re-souled.

It is used three times in the Old Testament. One of them is of God: *on the seventh day He
rested and **was refreshed**.*

## The reason in the other version

Exodus 20 grounds the sabbath in creation: *for in six days the LORD made the heavens and
the earth… therefore the LORD blessed the Sabbath day.*

Deuteronomy 5 grounds it in the exodus: *remember that you were a slave in the land of
Egypt… **therefore** the LORD your God commanded you to keep the Sabbath day.*

And this one grounds it in the ox and the slave.

Three statements of the same command with three different reasons attached.

## What the Illumination adds

*This rest is a gift, meant to **breathe life back into everyone under your care**.*

## What it is not

It is not framed as an obligation discharged. Nothing in this verse is about worship,
assembly or sacrifice.

It is a labour law, and the beneficiaries are the ones with no power to claim it.

## What Jesus said about the day

*"**The Sabbath was made for man, and not man for the Sabbath**."*

And He healed on it repeatedly, arguing each time from what people would do for an animal
on that day — an ox led to water, a donkey pulled out of a well. See the notes on
Luke 13:10–17 and Luke 14:1–6.

He is arguing from this verse. The animals are in the commandment.

## What Hebrews does with the rest

*There remains therefore **a Sabbath rest for the people of God**. Anyone who has entered
His rest has stopped working himself, the way God stopped when His own work was done.*
See the note on Hebrews 4:8–11.

## Who is missing from the verse

The owner. He is the one being commanded and the only one whose benefit is not mentioned.

> **What the command protects.** Not the householder's leisure. Two animals, a slave's
> son, and a foreigner — the four in the house who could not have stopped without being
> told to.
