Nine items in a list, and the sentence that closes it is about who decides.

## The nine

- **A word of wisdom**
- **A word of knowledge**
- **Faith**
- **Gifts of healings**
- **The working of miracles**
- **Prophecy**
- **The discerning of spirits**
- **Different kinds of tongues**
- **The interpretation of tongues**

## The two that are words

The first two are **a word** of wisdom and **a word** of knowledge — the Greek is
**logos**, a saying, an utterance.

Not wisdom as a quality, and not knowledge as a store of learning. A word of it, for an
occasion.

## Faith as a gift

Faith is in the list, which is worth noticing, because every believer is said to have
faith elsewhere.

This one is listed alongside healings and miracles rather than alongside salvation.

## Both plurals

*Gifts of **healings**.* Both nouns are plural in the Greek — **charismata iamaton**.

Not a healing gift held by a person, in Paul's phrasing. Gifts, plural, of healings,
plural.

## The refrain

Paul repeats the source clause four times in three verses:

*through **the Spirit**… according to **the same Spirit**… by **the same Spirit**… by **the
one Spirit**.*

He is writing to a church that was ranking itself by which gift a person had. The
repetition is the argument.

## The word for gifts

**Charisma** — from **charis**, grace. A gift of grace, something given without being
earned.

Paul uses the same root for salvation, for celibacy, and for these.

## The summary

*But **one and the same Spirit works all of these**, distributing to each one
**individually** just as **He wills**.*

Three claims in one sentence:

- **One and the same Spirit** — the ninth repetition of the point.
- **To each one individually** — the Greek is *to each his own*. Nobody has the set.
- **Just as He wills** — the Greek is **kathos bouletai**, according to His own
  deliberate choice.

The distribution is not by request, merit, seniority or hunger. Paul closes the list by
locating the decision entirely with the Spirit.

## What the chapter says three verses earlier

*There are **diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit**. There are **differences of
ministries, but the same Lord**. And there are **diversities of activities, but it is the
same God** who works all in all.*

Three sets, and each is attributed to a different person of the Trinity.

## What it is for

*But **the manifestation of the Spirit is given to each one for the profit of all**.*

The Greek is *for what is advantageous* — **sumphero**, to bring together, to benefit.

Stated before the list, and it governs it.

## Where the chapter goes

Into the body — one body, many members, the eye that cannot say to the hand *I have no
need of you*, and the parts that seem weaker being necessary.

And then, after *earnestly desire the best gifts*: *and yet I show you a more excellent
way* — and the whole of chapter 13.

> **What the nine have in common.** Not one of them is described as produced,
> cultivated, or achieved. Paul lists them, names one Spirit four times, and ends on the
> Spirit's own will.
