The sentence that ends a fifty-two-year reign is eleven words long.

## The hinge

*But **when he had grown strong**, his heart became proud, and it led to his
ruin.*

The strength was God's doing. The chapter has just said so twice — *God gave him
success*, *he was marvellously helped, till he was strong.*

And the strength is what he stumbled over. The Hebrew for *led to his ruin* is to
corrupt, to spoil — the same root used of the earth being corrupt before the
flood.

He did not stop being helped and then get proud. He got proud because of how far
the help had gone.

## What pride made him do

*He went into the temple of the LORD **to burn incense** on the altar of incense.*

Not idolatry. Not a foreign god. He walks into the house of the LORD to worship
the LORD.

The offence is not what he did. It is who he was when he did it.

## The line he crossed

*"It is **not for you**, Uzziah, to burn incense to the LORD, but **for the
priests, the sons of Aaron**, who are consecrated to do so."*

Two offices, and God had kept them apart the whole way through Israel's history.
Saul lost the kingdom for sacrificing; the sons of Korah died for claiming the
censer.

Uzziah had been given a throne, an army, towers, cisterns and international fame.
What he had not been given was a censer, and that is the one thing he reached for.

## Eighty men

*Azariah the priest went in after him, and with him **eighty priests of the LORD,
courageous men**.*

The chronicler calls them courageous, and they needed to be. They walked into the
sanctuary to stand in front of a king at the height of his power and tell him to
leave.

## What they told him

*"Leave the sanctuary, for you have been unfaithful, and **there will be no honour
for you from the LORD God**."*

He had come in to add to his honour. He is told, standing there with the censer
in his hand, that this will bring him none.

## Where the reign turns

*But **when he was strong his heart was lifted up, to his destruction**.*

Fifty-two years of a good reign — towers, cisterns, vineyards, an army, engines of war on the
walls — and the Chronicler puts the turn at the point of maximum strength.

*Therefore let him who thinks he stands **take heed lest he fall**.*

## What he tried to do

Burn incense on the altar of incense. Priest's work, in the holy place, done by a king.

Eighty priests go in after him — *valiant men* — and tell him to his face: *it is not for you,
Uzziah, to burn incense to the LORD, but for the priests, the sons of Aaron.*

## Why it mattered

Israel's two great offices were deliberately kept apart. Saul lost a kingdom for offering a
sacrifice. Korah's company died reaching for the censer. See the note on
Numbers 16:16–35.

Nobody is allowed to be both.

## The one exception

*"He shall build the temple of the LORD; He shall bear the glory, and **shall sit and rule on
His throne; so He shall be a priest on His throne**, and the counsel of peace shall be between
them both."*

Zechariah, of the Branch. A priest on a throne — the one combination the whole Old Testament
system forbids, promised as the thing the Messiah would be.

*We have such a High Priest, who **is seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in
the heavens**.*

**Hebrews** spends two chapters on why He can hold both, and the answer is that He is not of
Aaron's line at all but *according to the order of Melchizedek* — the priest-king of Salem who
has no genealogy. See the note on Genesis 14:17–20.

## What happened to Uzziah

Leprosy on his forehead, and he *was a leper until the day of his death, and dwelt in an
isolated house*, cut off from the temple he had walked into.

And the year he died is the year Isaiah looked up and saw *the Lord sitting on a throne, high
and lifted up* — one king out of the temple, and another one seen in it.

> **Only one man ever held both offices.** A king on a throne who is also a priest
> — Melchizedek in Genesis, and then the promise in Zechariah, *He shall be a
> priest upon His throne.* Uzziah reached for it and was struck. It belonged to
> Somebody. See the note on 2 Chronicles 26:19–21.
