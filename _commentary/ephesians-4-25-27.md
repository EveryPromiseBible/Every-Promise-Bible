Three instructions about anger, and the middle one permits it.

## Where this comes

Paul has just told them to *put off the old man* and *put on the new man*, and then begins a run of
specific replacements: stop lying and speak truth, stop stealing and work, stop corrupt speech and
build up.

Each item names what to stop and what to do instead.

## The first

*So **have done with lying**. Let each of you **speak the truth to the one next to you**.*

The Greek is **apothemenoi to pseudos** — having put off the falsehood.

Zechariah 8, quoted: *let each one of you speak the truth to his neighbour*.

## The reason

*Because **we belong to one another as parts of one body**.*

The Greek is **hoti esmen allelon mele** — because we are members of one another.

Not *because lying is wrong*. Because of what the congregation is.

Lying to another member is described the way a body deceiving itself would be described.

## The permission

*Be angry, and do not sin.*

The Greek is **orgizesthe kai me hamartanete**.

Psalm 4, quoted from the Septuagint.

## The grammar

**Orgizesthe** is an imperative.

Read strictly it commands anger. Most readers take it as a concession — *be angry if you must* —
but Paul does not soften it into a prohibition.

Anger is not the sin named. What follows it is.

## What He was angry about

*And when He had looked around at them **with anger**, being grieved by the hardness of their
hearts.*

Mark, of Jesus in the synagogue, before healing the withered hand. See the note on
Mark 3:1–6.

## The deadline

*Do not let **the sun go down while you are still burning**.*

The Greek is **ho helios me epiduetho epi to parorgismo humon** — let not the sun set upon your
provocation.

**Parorgismos** — exasperation, the state of being provoked. A different word from **orge** in
the previous clause.

## What the deadline does

It does not forbid the anger. It puts a clock on it.

The instruction is about how long, not whether.

## What sunset meant

The Jewish day begins at sunset. The anger is not to be carried into a new day.

## The consequence

*And **do not hand the devil a place to stand**.*

The Greek is **mede didote topon to diabolo** — do not give place to the devil.

**Topos** — a place, a foothold, an opportunity. The word for ground occupied.

## What is being described

Anger that stays overnight is described as territory conceded.

The mechanism is not stated. Paul names the outcome and moves on.

## What follows

*Let him who stole steal no longer, but rather **let him labour, working with his hands what is good,
that he may have something to give him who has need**.*

The thief is not merely told to stop. He is redirected past honest work to giving.

## And then

*Let **no corrupt word** proceed out of your mouth, but what is good for necessary edification.*

*And **do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God**, by whom you were sealed for the day of redemption.*

## The summary

*Let all bitterness, wrath, anger, clamour, and evil speaking be put away from you… **and be kind to
one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God in Christ forgave you**.* See the
note on Ephesians 4:31–32.

> **The middle instruction.** *Be angry* is an imperative, and the sin named is not the anger. What
> Paul regulates is how long it is allowed to stay.
