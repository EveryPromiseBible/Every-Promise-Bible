He tells the whole story again, and Genesis prints it twice.

## Where he is

In Bethuel's house, standing in front of the family, refusing to eat until he has explained his
errand.

*I will not eat until I have told about my business.*

## Why the repetition matters

Genesis is not a wordy book. Creation gets thirty-one verses.

This chapter is the longest in the book, and a large part of its length is the servant repeating
what the reader has just watched.

The retelling is the point: the family must hear the sequence in order to know what they are
deciding about.

## The opening

*"So **today I came to the spring and prayed**."*

The Hebrew is **va'avo hayyom el-ha'ayin** — and I came today to the spring.

**Today**. He is telling them the timeline is hours old.

## The address

*"**LORD, God of my master Abraham**."*

The same words he used at the well. He does not adjust the prayer for a foreign audience.

## The request

*"**If You are pleased, make my journey succeed**."*

The Hebrew here is **matzliach darki** — from **tsalach**, to prosper, to make successful.

## The word he swaps

At the well he prayed **haqreh-na lephanai** — cause it to happen before me, from **qarah**,
to meet by chance. See the note on Genesis 24:12–14.

In the retelling he uses **tsalach** — prosper.

He asked for a chance encounter. Recounting it, he calls it prospering.

Between the two words is the girl at the well.

## Where he was standing

*"**Here I stand by the spring**."*

The Hebrew is **hinneh anochi nitzav al-ein hammayim**.

The whole request is made from a fixed position. He does not go looking.

## The sign

*"When a young woman comes out to draw water and I say, '**Please give me a little water from your
jar**,' and she answers, '**Drink, and I'll draw water for your camels too**.'"*

Word for word what he prayed, and word for word what she said. See the note on
Genesis 24:17–20.

## The conclusion

*"**Let her be the one the LORD has chosen for my master's son**."*

The Hebrew is **hokiach YHWH** — the one the LORD has appointed, decided upon.

## What is left out

The retelling omits nothing important and adds one thing: he tells them Abraham made him swear
not to take a wife from the Canaanites, and that Abraham said *the LORD… will send His angel with
you and prosper your way*.

The family hears that the errand was under oath.

## What he says at the end of it

*"Now if you will **deal kindly and truly** with my master, tell me. And if not, tell me, that I may
turn to the right hand or to the left."*

**Chesed ve'emet** again. The pair. See the note on Genesis 24:26–27.

He asks for a plain answer and offers to leave.

## The family's reply

*"**The thing comes from the LORD; we cannot speak to you either bad or good**."*

The Hebrew is **meYHWH yatza haddavar** — from the LORD the matter has gone out.

They heard the same sequence the reader did and reached the same conclusion.

## Rebekah's

*"**Will you go with this man?**" And she said, "**I will go**."*

Two words in Hebrew: **elech**.

She is asked, and the answer is hers.

## What the chapter ends with

*Then Isaac brought her into his mother Sarah's tent, and he took Rebekah and she became his wife,
and he loved her. **So Isaac was comforted after his mother's death**.* See the note on
Genesis 24:66–67.

> **The word he swaps.** At the well he prayed for **qarah** — a chance meeting. Retelling it, he
> calls it **tsalach** — prospering. The girl at the well is what happened in between.
