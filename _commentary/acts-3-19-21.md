Peter's second sermon, preached on the temple steps to a crowd that has just watched a man walk.

## Where this is

Solomon's Porch. The lame man from the Beautiful Gate is holding on to Peter and John, *walking,
leaping, and praising God*.

*Why do you marvel at this? Or **why look so intently at us, as though by our own power or godliness
we had made this man walk?***

Peter deflects the attention in his first sentence.

## The charge

*"You denied the Holy One and the Just, and asked for a murderer to be granted to you, and **killed
the Prince of life**."*

And then: *"Yet now, brethren, I know that **you did it in ignorance**, as did also your rulers."*

He states it as bluntly as it can be stated, and then names it as the Leviticus 4 category — sin
committed unknowingly. See the note on Leviticus 4:1–2.

## The instruction

*"So **change your minds and turn back to God**."*

The Greek is **metanoesate** — from **meta** (change) and **nous** (mind).

**Epistrepsate** — turn round, turn back.

## The first result

*"So that **your sins may be wiped away**."*

The Greek is **exaleiphthenai** — to wipe out, to obliterate.

The verb for erasing writing from a wax tablet or scraping ink off papyrus.

Paul uses it of the record: *having wiped out the handwriting of requirements that was against
us*. See the note on Colossians 2:11–15.

## The second

*"So that **times of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord**."*

The Greek is **kairoi anapsuxeos** — seasons of refreshing.

**Kairos** — the opportune moment, the right time, as against **chronos**, duration.

**Anapsuxis** — a cooling down, a breathing space, recovery of breath. It appears once in the New
Testament, here.

## Where it comes from

*From the presence of the Lord* — **apo prosopou tou Kuriou**, from the face of the Lord.

## The third

*"And so that **He may send Jesus Christ**, who was proclaimed to you beforehand."*

## Where He is meanwhile

*"The One **heaven must receive until the time comes for all things to be restored**."*

The Greek is **dei ouranon dexasthai** — heaven must receive Him.

**Achri chronon apokatastaseos panton** — until times of restoration of all things.

**Apokatastasis** — restoration to a former state. A medical word for a bone set back, and an
astronomical one for a body returning to its position.

## The two words for time

**Kairos** in verse 20 — the seasons of refreshing that come now.

**Chronos** in verse 21 — the stretch of duration before the restoration.

Peter uses both, and distinguishes them.

## The witnesses

*"Which God spoke of **through the mouth of all His holy prophets from the beginning of the
world**."*

## What follows

Moses, quoted: *the LORD your God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from your brethren*.

And Samuel, and all the prophets. And Abraham: *in your seed all the families of the earth shall
be blessed*.

*To you first, God, having raised up His Servant Jesus, sent Him to bless you, **in turning away
every one of you from your iniquities**.* See the note on Acts 3:24–26.

## What happens to Peter

*Now as they spoke to the people, **the priests, the captain of the temple, and the Sadducees came
upon them**… and put them in custody until the next day.*

*However, **many of those who heard the word believed; and the number of the men came to about five
thousand**.*

Arrested mid-sermon, and the count is given anyway.

> **The two words.** **Kairos** — the refreshing that comes now, from the face of the Lord.
> **Chronos** — the stretch before the restoration. Peter uses both in consecutive verses.
