He asks two questions and gets three answers.

## The instruction

*David said to **Abiathar the priest, Ahimelech's son**, "**Bring me the ephod**."*

Abiathar is the only survivor of Nob — the priestly town Saul had massacred, eighty-five priests
in linen ephods killed by Doeg the Edomite.

*And Abiathar escaped… and fled after David.*

The priest with David is a refugee from the king David is running from.

## The ephod

The high priest's garment, with the breastpiece attached to it, and in the breastpiece the Urim
and Thummim. See the note on Exodus 28:30.

The means of enquiry.

## What he had just done

*David **strengthened himself in the LORD his God**.* See the note on 1 Samuel 30:6.

The strengthening first, then the ephod.

He does not ask for guidance from the floor of his despair. He gets up first.

## The first question

*"**Should I chase this raiding band?**"*

The Hebrew is **ha'erdoph acharei-haggedud** — shall I pursue after this troop?

## The second

*"**Will I catch them?**"*

Two questions, and they are the right two: whether to go, and whether it will work.

## Why the second matters

Four hundred men, three days behind, no idea which direction. The odds are bad and he asks about
them.

## The answer

*"**Chase them. You will surely overtake them, and you will surely rescue them all**."*

The Hebrew doubles both verbs: **hasseg tassig** and **hatztzel tatztzil** — overtaking you shall
overtake, delivering you shall deliver.

The infinitive absolute, twice. Certainty stated as strongly as Hebrew can state it.

## The third thing

He asked whether he would catch them. He was told he would recover everybody.

The extra clause was not requested.

## The pursuit

*So David set out, **he and the six hundred men with him**.*

The same six hundred who had been talking about stoning him a few verses earlier.

Nothing is said about a reconciliation. They go.

## The brook

*And they came to **the Besor Valley**.*

## The two hundred

*There **two hundred stayed behind, too worn out to cross the brook**.*

The Hebrew is **pigru** — they were exhausted, they gave out.

A third of his force cannot go on. They have been marching, and before that they had walked three
days back from Aphek and found their homes burnt.

## What he does about it

Leaves them, and presses on with four hundred.

No reproach is recorded.

## What happens to them afterwards

The men who fought want to give the two hundred nothing but their wives and children.

*But David said, "**You shall not do so, my brethren**… for **as his part is who goes down to the
battle, so shall his part be who stays by the supplies; they shall share alike**."*

*So it was, from that day forward; **he made it a statute and an ordinance for Israel**.*

A rule for the whole nation, made about two hundred men who could not cross a stream.

## Who is found on the way

An Egyptian slave, abandoned in a field three days earlier by his master because he fell ill. See
the note on 1 Samuel 30:11–15.

The intelligence that wins the battle comes from a man his own side threw away.

> **The third clause.** He asked whether to go and whether he would catch them. The answer added
> *you will surely rescue them all*, which he had not asked.
