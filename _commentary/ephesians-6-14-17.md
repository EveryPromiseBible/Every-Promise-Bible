Six pieces, and Paul names what each one is made of.

## The instruction

*So **stand**, then.*

The fourth time in five verses. *Stand against, withstand, having done all, stand,
stand therefore.*

Nothing in this passage is about advancing. The whole armour is issued for holding
ground.

## The belt

*Truth fastened **around your waist like a belt**.*

The soldier's belt held everything else — the scabbard hung from it, and the tunic
was tucked into it to free the legs. Nothing else works if it is loose.

## The breastplate

*Righteousness worn **over your chest**.*

Isaiah has it first: *He put on righteousness as a breastplate.* It is God's armour
before it is ours.

And whose righteousness it is has been settled earlier in the letter: *the gift of
righteousness*, *accepted in the Beloved*.

## The feet

*Your feet fitted with **the readiness that the good news of peace gives**.*

Roman military sandals were studded with hobnails, for grip.

The Greek is **hetoimasia** — preparedness, a firm footing.

The odd part is what it is made of: *the gospel of peace*. Peace, on the feet, in a
passage about a battle.

## The shield

*Above all of it, take up the shield, which is **faith**.*

**Thureos** — from *thura*, a door. The full-length Roman shield, four feet by two
and a half, covering the whole man.

Not the small round buckler. This one you get behind.

## The arrows

*With it you will be able to **put out all the burning arrows** of the evil one.*

Arrows wrapped in pitch-soaked tow and lit.

The Roman shield was wood covered in leather, and the leather was soaked in water
before a battle so that a flaming arrow sticking in it went out.

Paul says the shield extinguishes them. It does not deflect them; it takes them and
quenches them.

## The helmet

*The helmet, which is **salvation**.*

Isaiah again: *a helmet of salvation on His head.*

Paul's other version: *the hope of salvation as a helmet.* See the note on
1 Thessalonians 5:4–8.

## The sword

*And **the sword the Spirit gives, which is the word of God**.*

The Greek is **machaira** — the short stabbing sword, not the long broadsword. It was
close-quarters equipment.

And *word* here is **rhema** — the spoken word, the specific saying, rather than
**logos**.

That is what Jesus used in the wilderness. Three times: *it is written.*

## What is not covered

Nothing is issued for the back.

## What comes first

*Put on the whole armour of God, that you may be able to stand against the wiles of
the devil.* And the passage before all of it: *be strong in the Lord and **in the power
of His might**.* See the note on Ephesians 6:10–13.

> **What follows the armour.** *Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the
> Spirit… and for me, that utterance may be given to me.* Paul finishes the armour
> passage by asking a church to pray for his own courage — *that I may speak boldly, as
> I ought to speak.* He was in chains when he wrote it.
