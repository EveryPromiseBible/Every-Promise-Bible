---
key: 2 Corinthians 8:1–2
title: "He calls it grace before anybody has given anything"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul is about to ask for money, and he spends two chapters refusing to call it giving. The word he
uses ten times over is **charis** — grace.

## Whose grace it is

*We want you to know about **the grace God has poured out on** the churches of Macedonia.*

Not *the generosity of the Macedonians*. The grace **given to** them. Paul reports their offering as
something that happened to them rather than something they achieved.

That is the whole theology of Christian giving in the first sentence. What came out of Macedonia had
first come into Macedonia.

## The arithmetic that does not work

*In the middle of a long and grinding trial, their overflowing joy and their poverty — **poverty
right down to the bottom of them** — spilled over into riches of open-handed generosity.*

Set out the terms:

**Affliction**, and Paul calls it a *great testing*. **Joy**, overflowing. **Poverty** — and the word
is **ptocheia**, from **ptochos**, the crouching beggar who has nothing at all, not the poor man who
has little. Paul adds *down to the depth of it* in case anybody thinks he is being rhetorical.

Affliction plus beggary equals *riches of liberality*. No natural rule produces that sum. Something
was added to the equation that is not in the terms.

## The single eye

The word behind *open-handed generosity* is **haplotes** — singleness, simplicity, the opposite of
a divided mind. It is generosity described by the shape of the heart doing it rather than by the
size of the amount.

A single eye does not calculate. It does not run the figures to see what can be spared. That is why
Paul can call beggars generous: liberality here is not a quantity.

## Where they got it

Nine verses on, Paul tells you exactly what had been poured out on them:

*For you know **the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ**, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He
became poor, that you through His poverty might become rich.*

And the verb is **eptocheusen** — the same root as the Macedonians' poverty. He became the crouching
beggar. Grace ran downhill from His riches to their beggary, and out of their beggary to a church
in Jerusalem they had never met.

> **Grace given, then grace given away.** They did not give in order to be blessed; Paul says the
> grace of God was on them first, and the offering was the overflow. That is always the order. The
> Macedonians simply passed on the shape of what had been done to them by a Lord who had everything
> and ended with nothing, so that beggars could end up rich.
