The prayer at the centre of the psalm, and Solomon quotes it out loud.

## The summons

*Rise up, LORD, **to Your resting place**, You and **the ark of Your strength**.*

The Hebrew is **qumah YHWH limnuchatekha**.

**Qumah** — arise. It is Moses' word, said every time the ark set out in the wilderness: *rise
up, O LORD! Let Your enemies be scattered*. See the note on Numbers 10:33–36.

Moses said it at the start of a journey. The psalm says it at the end of one.

## The resting place

**Menuchah** — rest, a settled place.

The ark had gone before them *to search out a resting place for them*. Now it is asked to come
into one of its own.

## The title

*The ark of Your strength.*

The Hebrew is **aron uzzekha**.

## The two requests

*May Your **priests be clothed in righteousness**.*

The Hebrew is **yilbeshu tsedeq** — let them put on righteousness.

Priests were clothed. The garments were specified in detail — ephod, breastplate, robe, turban,
sash. The psalm asks for a different fabric.

*May Your **faithful ones sing for joy**.*

The Hebrew is **chasidekha** — Your covenant-loyal ones, from **chesed**.

## The plea

*For the sake of **David Your servant**, do not turn away the face of **Your anointed**.*

The Hebrew is **meshichekha** — Your messiah, Your anointed one.

The request is made on somebody else's account. Not *for my sake* — for David's.

## Who said this out loud

Solomon, at the dedication of the temple, with the ark being carried into the Holy of Holies:

*"Now therefore, **arise, O LORD God, to Your resting place, You and the ark of Your strength.
Let Your priests, O LORD God, be clothed with salvation, and let Your saints rejoice in
goodness. O LORD God, do not turn away the face of Your anointed; remember the mercies of Your
servant David**."*

The Chronicler records it as the end of the prayer, and the fire falls immediately after.

One change: Solomon says *clothed with salvation* where the psalm says *righteousness*.

## What God answers

*I will clothe her priests with **salvation**, and her faithful ones will sing aloud for joy.*
See the note on Psalms 132:15–16.

The answer takes both requests and gives more than was asked: righteousness requested, salvation
granted.

The psalm records the request and the reply, and the wording changes in between.

## The change of clothes elsewhere

*I will greatly rejoice in the LORD… for **He has clothed me with the garments of salvation, He
has covered me with the robe of righteousness**.*

Isaiah, and he has both words.

And the prodigal: *bring out the best robe and put it on him*. See the note on
Luke 15:21–24.

And Joshua the high priest in Zechariah, standing in filthy garments with Satan at his right
hand: *take away the filthy garments from him… see, I have removed your iniquity from you, and
I will clothe you with rich robes*. See the note on Zechariah 3:1–5.

## What the second half of the psalm is

God's answer, in the first person, and it is longer than the request.

*The LORD has sworn in truth to David; **He will not turn from it**.* See the note on
Psalms 132:13–14.

## The last line

*"There I will make the horn of David grow; **I have prepared a lamp for My Anointed**."*

The psalm ends where it began — on the anointed one, and on a promise that outlives him.

> **Who the plea is made for.** Not the person praying. *For the sake of David Your servant* —
> and Solomon quotes the whole verse standing in a finished temple.
