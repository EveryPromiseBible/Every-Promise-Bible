Three denials, and each one is louder than the last.

## Where he is

*Out in the courtyard Peter was **sitting**.*

Inside the high priest's compound, at a fire, while the trial goes on in the building.

He is the only disciple who got this close. The others had *forsaken Him and fled* in the
garden.

Matthew has already noted why he came: *Peter followed Him at a distance… to see the end*.

## The first

*One of **the servant girls** came over. "**You were with Jesus of Galilee**."*

Not an official. A girl on the staff.

*He **denied it in front of all of them**. "I do not know what you are talking about."*

A denial of comprehension, not of the fact. The first one is evasive.

## The move

*He got up and **moved toward the gateway**.*

Toward the exit, and not out of it. He gets closer to leaving three times and never leaves.

## The second

*Another girl saw him and said to the people standing there, "**This man was with Jesus of
Nazareth**."*

*Again he denied it, and this time **he swore an oath**: "**I do not know the man**."*

Now it is a flat denial of the person, under oath.

Matthew has recorded a teaching about this: *do not swear at all… let your yes be yes*.

## The third

*"**You are certainly one of them**," they said. "**Your accent gives you away**."*

The Greek is *your speech makes you evident*.

A Galilean could not say the gutturals the way a Judean did. Judges 12 has the same test
with *shibboleth*, and forty-two thousand men died on a pronunciation.

Peter's mouth convicts him before he opens it on the subject.

## What he did

*And Peter began to **curse and swear**. "**I do not know the man!**"*

The Greek is **katathematizein** — to call down curses, on himself if he is lying.

## The rooster

*And **right then a rooster crowed**.*

Luke adds the detail Matthew leaves out: *and the Lord turned and looked at Peter*.

## What he remembered

*And Peter remembered what Jesus had said to him: **before the rooster crows, you will deny
Me three times**.*

He had answered that prediction with *even if I have to die with You, I will not deny You*.

## The weeping

*He went outside and **wept bitterly**.*

The Greek is **eklausen pikros**.

Judas is in the next chapter, and his word is different — **metamelomai**, remorse. See the
note on 2 Corinthians 7:10–11.

Same night, same failure of nerve, two different words and two different ends.

## What had been said in advance

*"Simon, Simon! Indeed, Satan has asked for you, that he may sift you as wheat. But **I have
prayed for you, that your faith should not fail**; and **when you have returned to Me**,
strengthen your brethren."*

Not *if*. When. The restoration was arranged before the failure happened.

## Where the fire turns up again

John uses one unusual word for the coals in this courtyard — **anthrakian** — and uses it
exactly once more, on a beach, with breakfast on it. See the note on John 21:4–8.

Three denials at one fire, and three questions at the other.

## What he does fifty days later

Stands up in front of Jerusalem: *this Jesus, whom you crucified, God has made both Lord and
Christ*. Three thousand are added that day. See the note on Acts 2:40–41.

The man who could not hold his nerve in front of a servant girl preaches to the city that
killed Him.

> **What identified him.** Not evidence, and not a witness. The way he pronounced his words
> — the same mouth that was busy denying it.
