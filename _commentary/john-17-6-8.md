He tells the Father what He did, and it is one thing.

## Where this is

The night before the crucifixion, at the end of the upper room discourse. He has finished
speaking to the disciples and now speaks to the Father with them listening.

*Jesus spoke these words, lifted up His eyes to heaven, and said, "Father, the hour has come."*
See the note on John 17:1–5.

## The summary of His work

*"**I have made Your name known** to the people You gave Me out of the world."*

The Greek is **ephanerosa sou to onoma** — I manifested Your name.

He says it again at the end of the prayer: *I have declared to them Your name, and will declare
it*. See the note on John 17:24–26.

The prayer opens and closes with the same claim, and it is the only summary of His ministry He
gives here.

## Which name

*Father.*

He uses it six times in this prayer — *Father*, *Holy Father*, *righteous Father*.

The Old Testament calls God Father a handful of times, and always of the nation collectively.
Nobody addressed Him that way individually until Jesus did, and then taught others to: *when you
pray, say, Our Father*.

## The giving

*"**They were Yours; You gave them to Me**."*

Twice in one verse. They belonged to the Father first.

The same construction runs through the chapter: *those whom You gave Me I have kept*, and *I
pray for those whom You have given Me*. See the note on John 17:9–12.

## What they did

*"And **they have kept Your word**."*

Said the night they all abandoned Him.

Peter is hours from denying Him three times, and Jesus is telling the Father these men have kept
His word.

## What they know

*"Now they know that **everything You have given Me comes from You**."*

The Greek is **egnokan** — perfect tense. They have come to know it.

## How they came to know it

*"Because **I gave them the words You gave Me**, and **they took them in**."*

The Greek is **elabon** — they received.

Two handings: Father to Son, Son to them. And a receiving at the end of it.

## The certainty

*"They know for certain that **I came from You**, and they have believed that **You sent Me**."*

**Alethos** — truly, for certain.

Two clauses about origin and mission, and Jesus assigns both to men who are about to scatter.

## What the assessment is like

Generous past the evidence.

Peter said it in the earlier crisis — *we have come to believe, and we know* — after most of the
crowd left. See the note on John 6:66–71.

The disciples' own next line in this chapter's context is *now we are sure that You know all
things*, and Jesus answers *do you now believe? Indeed the hour is coming that you will be
scattered*.

He knows exactly what they are and still describes them to the Father this way.

## What the prayer asks for next

*"Holy Father, **keep through Your name those whom You have given Me**, that they may be one as We
are."*

The name He manifested becomes the thing they are kept by.

## Where the name ends up

*They shall see His face, and **His name shall be on their foreheads**.* See the note on
Revelation 22:3–5.

> **The one-line summary.** Asked what He had done, He says: *I have made Your name known.* And
> the name is Father.
