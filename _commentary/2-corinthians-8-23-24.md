The end of Paul's letter of introduction for the men carrying the collection.

## What is being organised

The relief fund for the poor believers in Jerusalem.

Paul spends two chapters on it — the Macedonians who gave *beyond their ability*, the argument
from Christ's poverty, and the practical arrangements.

And the audit: *avoiding this, that anyone should blame us in this lavish gift which is
administered by us — providing honourable things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in
the sight of men*.

He will not carry the money alone.

## Titus

*If anyone enquires about **Titus**, he is **my partner and fellow worker** concerning you.*

The Greek is **koinonos kai sunergos** — a sharer and a co-worker.

Titus had gone ahead willingly — *being more diligent, he went to you of his own accord*. See
the note on 2 Corinthians 8:16–17.

## The brothers

*Or if our **brethren are enquired of**, they are **messengers of the churches**.*

The Greek is **apostoloi ekklesion** — apostles of the churches.

The same word, in its ordinary sense: somebody sent with a commission. These men are not
apostles in the twelve's sense; they are delegates, appointed by congregations to travel with
the money.

One of them is named earlier only as *the brother whose praise is in the gospel throughout all
the churches, and not only that, but who was also **chosen by the churches** to travel with
us*.

Elected, by vote, to handle other people's donations.

## The title

*They are **the glory of Christ**.*

The Greek is **doxa Christou**.

Said of two unnamed men on a fundraising errand.

Paul has used **doxa** in this letter of the face of Moses, of the ministry of the Spirit, and
of the eternal weight of glory. Here it is applied to a delegation.

## The instruction

*Therefore **show to them, and before the churches, the proof of your love** and of our boasting
on your behalf.*

The Greek is **endeixin** — a demonstration, a showing forth.

He has been boasting about the Corinthians to the Macedonians — *I boast of you to them* — and
now the boast has to be substantiated in front of witnesses.

## What the whole chapter rests on

*For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that **though He was rich, yet for your sakes
He became poor, that you through His poverty might become rich**.* See the note on
2 Corinthians 8:9.

One sentence in the middle of a fundraising letter, and it is the theological centre of the
whole two chapters.

## What the next chapter says

*So let each one give as he purposes in his heart, **not grudgingly or of necessity; for God
loves a cheerful giver**.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 9:6–7.

The Greek is **hilaron** — hilarious, glad.

## Why the arrangements matter

Paul is handling a large sum of money belonging to churches he founded, destined for a church
that had been suspicious of him.

Every safeguard in these two chapters is there because he knew how it would look.

> **What he calls two unnamed couriers.** *The glory of Christ.* The phrase he has used of the
> face of Moses and of eternal weight, applied to men carrying a collection.
