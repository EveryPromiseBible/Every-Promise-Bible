Three verses of asking to be heard, and the word *morning* appears twice.

## The three requests

*Listen to my words.*

*Hear the **sighing I cannot even speak**.*

*Pay attention to the sound of my cry.*

Three verbs for the same thing, escalating. Words, then something below words, then a cry.

## The middle one

The Hebrew is **hagigi** — a rare noun. Musing, murmuring, groaning. Something between a
thought and a sound.

He asks God to attend to the part of the prayer that never made it into language.

Paul says the same thing about the Spirit: *the Spirit Himself makes intercession for us with
groanings which cannot be uttered*. See the note on Romans 8:26–27.

## The address

*My **King** and my **God**.*

Both possessives are personal. He is praying to a monarch he calls his own.

## The reason

*For it is **to You I pray**.*

The Hebrew is *for to You I will pray* — the ground given for being heard is simply the
direction of the prayer.

## The hour

*In the morning You hear my voice, LORD. **In the morning** I lay it all before You.*

Twice in one verse. The Hebrew word **boqer** is repeated for emphasis.

## The verb

*I **lay it all before You**.*

The Hebrew is **a'arok** — to arrange, to set in order.

It is a technical word. It is used of the priest laying the wood on the altar for the morning
sacrifice, and of setting out the showbread on the table.

The morning burnt offering was arranged at daybreak, every day, without exception. *One lamb
you shall offer in the morning, and the other lamb you shall offer at twilight.*

The psalmist uses the altar word for his prayer.

## What is not stated

What he lays out. The Hebrew has no object — *in the morning I will arrange*, and stops.

Translators supply *my prayer* or *my case*. The verse itself just says he sets it in order.

## The watching

*And **I wait and watch**.*

The Hebrew is **atsappeh** — to look out, to keep watch. The verb for a sentry on a wall.

Habakkuk uses it: *I will stand my watch and set myself on the rampart, and watch to see what
He will say to me*.

The prayer is not finished when the speaking stops.

## What the rest of the psalm is about

Enemies, and specifically their mouths: *there is no faithfulness in their mouth… their throat
is an open tomb; they flatter with their tongue*.

Paul quotes that line in his chain of quotations in Romans 3.

## How it ends

*But **let all those rejoice who put their trust in You**; let them ever shout for joy,
because You defend them; let those also who love Your name be joyful in You.*

*For **You, O LORD, will bless the righteous; with favour You will surround him as with a
shield**.*

The Hebrew for *shield* here is **tsinnah** — the large body shield, the full-length one, not
the small buckler.

## The heading

*To the Chief Musician. With flutes.*

> **The verb in the middle verse.** *Arrange* — the priest's word for laying out the wood and
> the bread at daybreak. And then he says he watches, which means the praying is not the end
> of it.
