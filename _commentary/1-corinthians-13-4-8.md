Fifteen short statements, and every one of them is a verb in the Greek.

## What it is not

Not a poem about romance. Paul is in the middle of an argument with a church that was
ranking itself by which gift a person had, and this is the answer.

Chapter 12 ends *and yet I show you a more excellent way*. Chapter 14 begins *pursue
love*. This sits between them.

## The grammar

English turns them into adjectives — love is patient, love is kind. The Greek has verbs.

Love **waits**, love **acts kindly**, love **does not envy**. It is a description of
conduct, not of temperament.

## The two positives

*Love **waits without complaining**. Love **is kind**.*

The Greek for the first is **makrothumei** — long-tempered, slow to burn. It is used of
God's patience with people.

Only two of the fifteen are positive at the start. The next eight are all negatives.

## The eight things it does not do

- **Is not jealous** — **zeloi**, the same root as *zeal*
- **Does not show itself off** — **perpereuetai**, a rare word for boasting; it occurs
  once in the Bible
- **Is not full of itself** — **phusioutai**, puffed up, inflated. Paul uses it six times
  in this letter and almost nowhere else; it was Corinth's particular problem
- **Is never rude** — **aschemonei**, behaving indecently
- **Is not out for what it can get** — *does not seek its own*
- **Is not quick to take offense** — **paroxunetai**, provoked, stirred
- **Keeps no ledger of wrongs** — **logizetai**, the accounting word. Love does not enter
  it in the book
- **Takes no pleasure in anything wrong**

## The ledger

*Love **keeps no ledger of wrongs**.*

The Greek verb is the one Paul uses eleven times in Romans 4 for God crediting
righteousness and not imputing sin. See the note on Romans 4:6–8.

The same bookkeeping word, applied to what love does with an offence.

## The four "keeps on"

*Love **bears up under anything**. Love **keeps on trusting**. Love **keeps on hoping**.
Love **outlasts everything**.*

The first and last are the same Greek root twice — **stegei** (covers, roofs over) and
**hupomenei** (remains under).

## The claim

*Love **never fails**.*

The Greek is **oudepote piptei** — never falls.

## What does fail

*But **prophecies — they will come to an end; tongues — they will fall silent; knowledge —
it will be finished with**.*

The three gifts Corinth was competing over, and Paul lists all three as temporary.

That is the argument. Not that the gifts are worthless — he has just spent a chapter
saying they come from one Spirit — but that they have an end date and this does not.

## What comes next

*For we know in part and we prophesy in part. But when that which is perfect has come,
then that which is in part will be done away.*

*For now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face.*

## The three that remain

*And now abide **faith, hope, love**, these three; but **the greatest of these is love**.*

Faith and hope also end — you do not need either when the thing is in front of you. Paul
calls love greatest and then, in the next sentence, tells them to chase it.

> **Where the chapter sits.** Between a list of nine spiritual gifts and the instructions
> for using them in a meeting. It is not an interlude. It is the standard the other two
> chapters are measured against.
