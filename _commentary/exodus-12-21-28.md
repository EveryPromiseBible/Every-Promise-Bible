Moses passes the instructions to the elders, and the practical detail is what stands
out.

## The hyssop

*Take **a bunch of hyssop**, dip it in the blood in the basin, and **touch the top and
both sides of your doorframe** with it.*

Hyssop is a small bushy herb, common and worth nothing. It was the applicator — you
could not do it with your hand.

The same plant is used for cleansing a leper, and for the red heifer. David asks for
it: *purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean.* See the note on Psalms 51:3–4.

And at the cross: *they filled a sponge with sour wine, put it on hyssop, and put it
to His mouth.*

## Three sides

Lintel and two doorposts. Top, left and right.

Nothing on the threshold. Nobody was to walk on it.

## The instruction that is easy to miss

*And **not one of you is to step outside his door until morning**.*

The blood is on the outside of the house. The safety is on the inside of it.

They spend the night indoors, eating, dressed to travel, while something goes through
Egypt.

## Who sees the mark

*"When **He sees the blood** on the frame, He will pass over that door."*

Not when you see it. The mark is addressed outward, and the seeing is God's. See the
note on Exodus 12:1–13.

## The destroyer

*"And **not let the destroyer enter** to strike your homes."*

Hebrew **mashchith** — the destroyer, the one who ruins.

The verse describes God passing over the door and, in the same clause, not permitting
something else through it.

## The children's question

*"And when your children ask you, '**What does this ceremony mean to you?**' — tell
them…"*

The instruction assumes the rite will outlive the night and that children will ask
about it.

That question is still asked at the Passover table — *ma nishtanah*, why is this
night different — and it comes from this verse.

## The answer they are given

*"It is the Passover sacrifice to the LORD, **who passed over the houses** of the
Israelites in Egypt, **and spared our homes**."*

The answer is a piece of history, told in the first person plural, by people who were
not there.

## What the people did

*And the people **bowed low and worshiped**. Then the Israelites went and **did exactly
as the LORD had commanded**.*

No questions recorded. It is the first time in the book they simply do it.

> **What was outside that night.** The Egyptians had also been through nine plagues,
> and Exodus notes that by this point *the Egyptians urged the people to hurry out of
> the land.* The same night that emptied Egypt's houses filled Israel's road. See the
> note on Exodus 12:33–36.
