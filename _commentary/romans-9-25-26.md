Paul quotes Hosea twice, and both quotations are the reversal of a name.

## What he is quoting

Hosea's third child was called **Lo-Ammi** — Not-My-People — and God said over
Israel *you are not My people, and I am not your God.*

Paul takes the two verses that reverse it and sets them side by side. See the note
on Hosea 1:8–9.

## The first quotation

*"I will call those who were **not My people** 'My people,' and her who was **not
loved** 'beloved.'"*

Both of Hosea's negative names, both undone. *Lo-Ammi* becomes *Ammi*.
*Lo-Ruhamah* becomes *Ruhamah*.

The prefix is simply dropped. Nothing else changes.

## Who does the calling

*I will call.* The naming is His in both directions — He named them
Not-My-People, and He is the one who renames them.

Nothing in either verse describes them earning the second name. They are called
it.

## The second quotation

*"**In the very place** where it was said to them, 'You are not My people,' there
they will be called **sons of the living God**."*

Not somewhere else. The same place.

And the title is not restored to what it was. They were *My people*; they are now
*sons of the living God*. The reversal overshoots the starting point.

## What Paul is arguing

The chapter's subject is God's freedom in mercy — *I will have mercy on whom I
have mercy* — and Paul applies Hosea's words about Israel to Gentiles being
brought in.

He is showing from the prophets that a people who were not a people becoming
God's own is not a new idea invented by the church. It was written down centuries
earlier, about Israel, and God's method has not changed.

## The word "called"

It runs through the whole chapter. *Not of works but of Him who calls… the
children of the promise are counted as offspring… even us whom He called.*

Calling is what makes the difference in Romans 9, and it belongs to Him.

> **Where the argument ends up.** Two chapters later: *God has not cast away His
> people whom He foreknew… for the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable.*
> Paul spends three chapters on this and does not conclude that anybody was
> discarded.
