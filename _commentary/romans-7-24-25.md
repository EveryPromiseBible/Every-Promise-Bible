He has been asking what is wrong with him for twenty verses. Here the question changes shape.

## The cry

*What a **miserable man** I am.*

The Greek is **talaipōros egō anthrōpos** — wretched, distressed, worn out by struggle.

## What has led to it

*For what I am doing, I do not understand… **the good that I will to do, I do not do; but the evil I
will not to do, that I practise**.* See the note on Romans 7:14–17.

And *I find then a law, that evil is present with me, the one who wills to do good*. See the note on
Romans 7:21–23.

## The count

*I*, *me*, *my* — around forty times in the chapter.

The paragraph is the most inward-facing in Paul, and the most defeated.

## What is missing from it

The Holy Spirit is not mentioned once between verse 7 and verse 24.

In chapter 8 the Spirit appears about twenty times.

## The question

*Who is going to **pull me out of this body of death**?*

The Greek is **tis me rhusetai** — who will rescue me.

## The word

**Tis** — who. Not **ti**, what.

Twenty verses of analysis end by asking for a person.

## The image

**Ek tou sōmatos tou thanatou toutou** — out of the body of this death.

An old tradition holds that some ancient punishments chained a corpse to a living man. Whether or
not Paul had that in mind, the phrase describes something carried that cannot be shaken off.

## The answer

*I thank God — **through Jesus Christ our Lord**.*

The Greek is **charis tō Theō dia Iēsou Christou tou Kuriou hēmōn**.

The answer arrives in the same breath as the question, and it is a name.

## What is not in the answer

No method. No programme.

The rescue is not described; the rescuer is named.

## The last sentence

*So then, with my mind I am **a servant of God's law**; with the old nature I am **a servant of sin's
law**.*

A summary of the whole chapter, placed after the thanksgiving.

## Why it sits there

The verse restates the divided condition after the answer has been given, which has puzzled readers
for centuries.

Read one way, it is a closing summary of the state that has just been escaped.

## The argument about the chapter

Whether Paul is describing his life before Christ or after has been debated since the second
century.

The tenses are present. The Spirit is absent. Both facts are in the text and pull in different
directions.

## What is not disputed

Where it goes.

*There is therefore **now no condemnation** to those who are in Christ Jesus… For **the law of the
Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has made me free** from the law of sin and death.* See the note on
Romans 8:1–4.

## The two laws

Chapter 7 ends with a man serving two laws at once. Chapter 8 introduces a third — the law of the
Spirit of life — and says it is what did the freeing.

## The chapter division

There is no chapter break in Paul's letter. *Therefore* runs straight on.

## The marriage argument earlier

*You also have become dead to the law through the body of Christ, **that you may be married to
another** — to Him who was raised from the dead.* See the note on Romans 7:4–6.

Not the law improved. A different husband.

> **The one-letter change.** Twenty verses of *what is wrong with me*, and then **tis** — *who will
> rescue me*. The answer that comes back is a name, not a method.
