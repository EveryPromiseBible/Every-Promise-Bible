Paul lists them, and the list is longer and more ordinary than people expect.

## The opening

*The works of the flesh are **out in the open, plain for anyone to see**.*

The Greek is **phanera** — manifest, evident.

No detection is required. They surface.

## The word

***Works** of the flesh.*

Plural, and it is set against **fruit** of the Spirit in the next paragraph, which is
singular. Paul chose both.

Works are produced; fruit grows.

## The list

Seventeen items, and they fall into groups:

**Sexual** — *adultery, sexual immorality, uncleanness, indecency*

**Religious** — *idolatry, sorcery*. The Greek for sorcery is **pharmakeia**, the root of
*pharmacy* — it covered drugs and potions as well as spells.

**Social** — *hatred, quarrels, jealousy, outbursts of wrath, selfish ambition,
dissensions, factions, envy*

**Violent and excessive** — *murders, drunkenness, wild parties*

## The proportion

Eight of the seventeen are relational. Quarrels, jealousy, ambition, dissensions, factions
and envy are the ones a church would recognise in itself.

Paul is writing to congregations tearing at each other over circumcision, and he had
already told them: *if you bite and devour one another, beware lest you be consumed by one
another.*

## The catch-all

*And **anything else of the same kind**.*

The list is open. He is describing a category, not compiling a code.

## The warning

*I am telling you about these **ahead of time, just as I told you before**.*

He had said it in person and is saying it again in writing.

## The wording of the verdict

*That those who **make a practice of such things** will not inherit the kingdom of God.*

The Greek is **prassontes** — a present participle, continuous. Those who go on doing.

It describes a habit rather than an incident, and Paul is careful with the tense.

## What comes immediately after

*But **the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness,
faithfulness, gentleness, self-control**. Against such there is no law.* See the note on
Galatians 5:22–24.

**Fruit** is singular in the Greek — one fruit with nine facets, not nine separate
achievements.

## The instruction that governs both lists

Three verses before the works: *I say then: **walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfil
the lust of the flesh**.*

And after the fruit: *if we live in the Spirit, **let us also walk in the Spirit**.* See the
note on Galatians 5:25–26.

The lists are bracketed by the same instruction, twice.

## What Paul does not offer

A method for suppressing the first list. The chapter gives one instruction, and the fruit
is described as growing rather than being assembled.

## The last two items of the fruit

*Gentleness, self-control.* Both are on the second list, and neither is described as
produced by effort.

> **What the list is mostly made of.** Not the spectacular sins. Eight of the seventeen
> are things that happen between people in a room — and Paul was writing to churches doing
> exactly that to each other.
