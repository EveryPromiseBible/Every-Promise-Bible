The end of the call, and God tells a frightened young man what he has been made into.

## Who is being spoken to

*So gather yourself, **Jeremiah**.*

The Hebrew is *gird up your loins* — tie up the long robe to be able to move.

He had objected at the start of the chapter: *"Ah, Lord GOD! **Behold, I cannot speak, for I
am a youth**."*

And the answer: *do not say "I am a youth," for you shall go to all to whom I send you.*

## The instruction

*Stand up and **tell them everything I command you**.*

## The warning

*Do not **fall apart before their faces**, or **I will let you fall apart in front of them**.*

The Hebrew is *do not be dismayed before their faces, lest I dismay you before them*.

The same verb both times, and it is uncomfortable. The protection is attached to the
standing.

## What he is made

*Look, **this very day I have made you**.*

Perfect tense. It is stated as done before he has preached a word.

## The three

*A **fortified city**.*

The Hebrew is **ir mivtsar** — a walled city. The one structure in that world designed to
be attacked and not fall.

*An **iron pillar**.*

A pillar holds a roof. Iron is the strongest metal they had.

*A **wall of bronze**.*

Bronze does not rust and does not let water through.

Three images, and every one is defensive rather than offensive. He is not given a weapon.

## Against whom

*Against **the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and its
people**.*

Four groups, from the throne down. Everybody.

Jeremiah is one man told he has been made a city against a country.

## The promise

*They will **fight against you, but they will not win**.*

Both halves. The fighting is guaranteed as much as the outcome.

He is not told he will be popular or believed. He was told at the start: *they will fight
against you*.

## The reason

*For **I am with you to rescue you**.*

The Hebrew is *to deliver you* — **natsal**.

The same clause given at the start of the chapter: *do not be afraid of their faces, for **I
am with you to deliver you**, says the LORD.*

Twice in one chapter, opening and closing the call.

## What happened

Forty years of preaching, and almost nobody listened. He was put in the stocks, thrown in a
cistern and left in the mud, denounced as a traitor, and forced into Egypt at the end.

He saw the city fall and wrote Lamentations over it. See the note on
Lamentations 3:31–33.

The wall did not stop the fighting. It held.

## What he says about the preaching

*Then I said, "I will not make mention of Him, nor speak anymore in His name." **But His word
was in my heart like a burning fire shut up in my bones**; I was weary of holding it back,
and I could not.*

He tried to stop.

## What was said before he was born

*"**Before I formed you in the womb I knew you**; before you were born I sanctified you; I
ordained you a prophet to the nations."*

> **What he is given.** Not eloquence and not an easy hearing. A city wall, an iron pillar
> and bronze — three things whose only function is to be hit and stay up.
