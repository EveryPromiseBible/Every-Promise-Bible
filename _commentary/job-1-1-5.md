The book opens with a man nobody has anything on, and the very first habit it
records about him is worth noticing.

## What he was

*He was a good man, **honest and whole all the way through**. He walked with God
and turned away from evil.*

Four things, and the writer says them flatly. There is no irony in the sentence
and no qualification after it.

The Hebrew behind *honest and whole* is **tam** — complete, entire, without a
crack running through it.

## What he had

*Seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen, five
hundred donkeys… **the greatest man in all the East**.*

The numbers are given so the reader can measure the losses later, and so the
doubling at the end of the book can be checked.

## The one habit recorded

*Job would gather his children and set them right before God. **Early in the
morning** he would rise and offer a burnt offering for each one of them.*

Not for himself. For them. Seven sons and three daughters, one offering each,
after every round of feasting.

## What he said to himself

*"**Perhaps** my children have sinned and cursed God in their hearts."*

That is the reason he gives, and he gives it to himself — the text says he said
it in his heart.

Nothing had happened. Nobody had done anything. There is a *perhaps*, and there is
an early morning and ten burnt offerings.

## "This is what Job always did"

The Hebrew is *thus did Job all the days*. A continuous, unbroken practice.

The book's very first picture of its hero is a man rising before dawn, week after
week, because of something his children *might* have thought.

## What he had no way of knowing

Whether the offerings were needed. Whether they worked. Whether God was pleased.

There is no priest in this book, no tabernacle, no Scripture, no covenant, and no
word from God to Job about any of it. He is doing what he can think of, on his
own, with a *perhaps*, and repeating it every time the feasting comes round.

That is the man the rest of the book is about.

## The habit that is recorded

*He would rise early in the morning and **offer burnt offerings according to the number of
them all**… "It may be that my sons have sinned." **This is what Job always did.**"*

A father sacrificing for children who had not asked him to, against sins he had no evidence of,
every time the round of feasts came round.

The Hebrew is *all the days* — a standing arrangement, repeated because it could not be
finished.

*And every priest stands ministering daily and offering repeatedly the same sacrifices, **which
can never take away sins**. But this Man, after He had offered **one sacrifice for sins
forever**, sat down.*

**Hebrews** contrasts the two by posture. Job rises early; the other One sits down.

> **What the New Testament says about that arrangement.** *Every priest stands
> daily ministering and offering repeatedly the same sacrifices, which can never
> take away sins. But this Man, after He had offered one sacrifice for sins
> forever, sat down.* Job stood up early. There is Somebody who sat down.
