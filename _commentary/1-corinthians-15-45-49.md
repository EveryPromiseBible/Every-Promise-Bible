Two men, and everybody who has ever lived belongs to one of them.

## The quotation

*And so it is written: "**The first man Adam became a living being**."*

Genesis 2:7: *and man became a living soul*.

The Greek is **psuchen zosan** — a living soul, translating the Hebrew **nephesh chayyah**.

Adam received life. The verse describes something happening to him.

## The addition

*The last Adam became **a life-giving spirit**.*

The Greek is **pneuma zoopoioun** — a spirit making alive.

Not *received life*. Gives it.

Paul is not quoting this half. He adds it, and the whole argument turns on the difference between
a receiver and a source.

## The title

**Ho eschatos Adam** — the last Adam.

Not the second Adam here, though Paul says *the second Man* two verses later.

**Last**, because there will not be a third. The line stops.

## The order

*But **the spiritual is not what comes first; the natural comes first, and afterward the
spiritual**.*

Paul is correcting a sequence, probably against a Corinthian view that the spiritual was the
original and the physical a decline.

Genesis first, then the resurrection. Dust, then glory. The order is not reversible.

## The two origins

*The first man was **of the earth, made of dust**; the second Man is **the Lord from heaven**.*

The Hebrew **adam** and **adamah** — man and ground — are the same root. Adam's name is the soil
he came out of.

**Ex ouranou** — out of heaven.

## The two families

*As was the man of dust, **so are those who are made of dust**; and as is the heavenly Man, **so are
those who are heavenly**.*

Everybody resembles their origin.

Paul has already stated the principle: *for as in Adam all die, even so in Christ all shall be
made alive*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:20–22.

## The promise

*And just as we have borne the image of the man of dust, **we shall also bear the image of the
heavenly Man**.*

The Greek is **phoresomen** — future. We shall wear it.

**Eikon** — image. The word from Genesis 1:27: *God created man in His own image*.

The image that was given at creation and damaged is the word used of what is coming.

## What the chapter is arguing

Some in Corinth were saying *there is no resurrection of the dead*.

Paul's answer runs the whole chapter: if there is no resurrection, Christ is not risen, the
preaching is empty, the faith is futile, and *we are of all men the most pitiable*.

## What the resurrection body is like

*It is sown in corruption, it is raised in incorruption. It is sown in dishonour, it is raised in
glory. It is sown in weakness, it is raised in power.*

*It is sown a natural body, it is raised a spiritual body.* See the note on
1 Corinthians 15:42–44.

Four contrasts, and the same body in each — sown and raised.

## The change

*Behold, I tell you a mystery: **we shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed** — in a
moment, in the twinkling of an eye.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:50–52.

## What Paul says about the image now

*But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, **are being
transformed into the same image from glory to glory**.* See the note on
2 Corinthians 3:16–18.

Present tense, and the same noun.

## The last verse of the chapter

*Therefore, my beloved brethren, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the
Lord, **knowing that your labour is not in vain in the Lord**.*

Fifty-seven verses on resurrection, and the conclusion is about what to do on Monday.

> **What Paul adds to the quotation.** Genesis says Adam *became a living being*. Paul supplies
> the other half himself: the last Adam became a spirit that **gives** life.
