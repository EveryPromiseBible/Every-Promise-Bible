Nine months of silence, and this is the first thing he says.

## Why he could not speak

He had asked Gabriel *how shall I know this?* — and been told *you will be mute and not able to
speak until the day these things take place*. See the note on Luke 1:18–20.

He is a priest, in the temple, at the incense altar, and he asked for evidence.

## What breaks the silence

The neighbours wanted the boy named after his father. Elizabeth said *no; he shall be called
John*, and they made signs to the father, who wrote on a tablet *his name is John*.

*Immediately his mouth was opened and his tongue loosed, and he spoke, praising God.*

## The first word

*Blessed be the Lord, the God of Israel.*

The Greek is **eulogetos** — the standard opening of a Jewish berakah, a blessing.

He starts where every synagogue prayer starts. Nine months of thinking, and he opens with the
liturgy.

## The tense

*For **He has come** to His people and **bought their freedom**.*

Aorist. Completed action.

Nothing has happened yet. Jesus has not been born; Mary is three months pregnant. Zechariah is
holding an eight-day-old baby who is not the subject of the first half of the song.

He speaks about it as done.

## The horn

*He has **raised up a horn of rescue** for us in the house of His servant David.*

The Hebrew idiom behind it: the horn of an ox, the concentration of an animal's strength.

*The LORD is my rock and my fortress… the horn of my salvation*, in David's own song.

## The promise

*Just as He said He would, **long ago, through the mouths of His holy prophets**.*

The Greek is **ap' aionos** — from the age, from of old.

## What the rescue is from

*That we would be **saved from our enemies** and out of the hand of everyone who hates us.*

Political language, in a country under Roman occupation, sung by a Jewish priest.

Luke records it without adjustment.

## The covenant

*That **mercy promised to our fathers** would be shown, that **His holy covenant** would be
remembered, **the oath He swore to our father Abraham**.*

Three clauses for the same thing: mercy, covenant, oath.

The oath is Genesis 22, after the ram: *by Myself I have sworn, says the LORD*. See the note on
Genesis 22:15–18.

## What the rescue is for

*To pull us free from the grip of our enemies **so that we could serve Him unafraid, holy and
righteous in His sight, all the days of our lives**.*

The Greek is **latreuein** — to serve as a priest serves, the temple word.

A priest sings that the point of deliverance is priestly service without fear.

## The turn to the child

*And you, little one.*

The Greek is **kai su, paidion**.

Halfway through the song he looks down at the baby in his arms and addresses him directly.

## What the boy will do

*You will be called **a prophet of the Most High**, for you will **walk out ahead of the Lord to
make His roads ready**.*

The last prophet before four hundred years of silence had said it: *behold, I send My messenger,
and he will prepare the way before Me*. See the note on Malachi 4:4–6.

## What knowledge he brings

*To let His people know **salvation has come, their sins carried away**.*

Not a military knowledge. The song has spent eight lines on enemies and now defines the salvation
as forgiveness.

## The reason for all of it

*All because of **the tender mercy of our God**.*

The Greek is **splanchna eleous** — the bowels of mercy. The gut.

## The image

*The **sunrise breaking over us from heaven**.*

The Greek is **anatole** — the rising, the dayspring. The same word used in the Septuagint for
the Branch.

*To shine on **everyone sitting in darkness and in the shadow of death**, and to **guide our feet
into the path of peace**.*

## The last word

**Eirene** — peace.

Sung by a man who had spent nine months unable to say anything, over a boy who would be beheaded
in a fortress by the Dead Sea.

> **The tense.** Aorist throughout — *He has come, He has bought, He has raised up* — sung before
> the child in the other womb was born.
