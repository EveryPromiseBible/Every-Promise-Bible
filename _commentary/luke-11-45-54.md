A lawyer objects to being included, and gets three woes of his own.

## The interruption

*One of the experts in the law answered Him, "Teacher, **when You say things like that, You
are insulting us too**."*

He has just pronounced three woes on the Pharisees — over tithing mint and rue while
passing over justice, over the best seats, and over being like unmarked graves people walk
on without knowing.

The lawyer's complaint is that the description reached further than intended.

## The reply

*"**How terrible it is for you experts in the law as well**."*

Three more, addressed to them directly.

## The first

*"You **load people down with burdens too heavy to carry**, and you **will not lift a finger
to help carry them**."*

The Greek is **phortia dusbastakta** — loads hard to bear.

Both halves matter. The loading and the refusal to help are named as one offence.

Matthew's version: *they bind heavy burdens, hard to bear, and lay them on men's shoulders;
but they themselves will not move them with one of their fingers.*

## The other yoke

*"**Take My yoke upon you**… **for My yoke is easy and My burden is light**."* See the note
on Matthew 11:28–30.

The Greek for *burden* is the same word — **phortion**.

## The second

*"You **build fine tombs for the prophets** — and it was **your own ancestors who killed
them**."*

*"So you are **witnesses against yourselves**: **you approve of what your ancestors did**.
They did the killing, and you do the building."*

The monument is read as an endorsement. The argument turns on the fact that the same
family is doing both jobs.

## The span

*"From **the blood of Abel** to **the blood of Zechariah**, who was killed **between the
altar and the sanctuary**."*

Abel is the first murder in Genesis. Zechariah is in 2 Chronicles 24, stoned in the temple
court — and Chronicles is the last book in the Hebrew ordering.

First book to last book. He is naming the whole span of the Scriptures.

Zechariah's dying words there: *the LORD look on it, and repay*.

## The third

*"You have **taken away the key of knowledge**. **You did not go in yourselves**, and **you
got in the way of the ones who were trying to**."*

The Greek is *the key of knowledge* — **ten kleida tes gnoseos**.

A key is for opening a door for somebody. This one has been removed from the lock.

Two failures again: not entering, and blocking.

Matthew's parallel: *you shut up the kingdom of heaven against men; for you neither go in
yourselves, nor do you allow those who are entering to go in.*

## The reaction

*And **the teachers of the law and the Pharisees began pressing Him hard, badgering Him
with question after question**.*

The Greek is **enechein** — to hold a grudge, to be hostile — and **apostomatizein**, to
interrogate, to press for answers.

*Waiting to **catch something out of His mouth** that they could use against Him.*

The Greek is a hunting word — lying in wait.

## Where this happened

At a meal. Verse 37: *a certain Pharisee asked Him to dine with him. So He went in and sat
down to eat.*

The whole passage is dinner-table conversation, and it starts because He did not wash His
hands first.

> **What the three woes have in common.** All three are about what happens to other
> people: burdens laid on them, a monument that endorses their killing, and a door held
> shut in front of them.
