Two organs, named twice, in two different orders — and Paul does that on purpose.

## The order changes

*If your **mouth** confesses the Lord Jesus, and your **heart** believes that God
raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.*

Then immediately:

*For it is with the **heart** that a person believes… and it is with the **mouth**
that confession is made.*

First mouth then heart; then heart then mouth. He is not being careless. He is
tying them together so tightly that neither can be lifted out alone.

Why the first order at all? Because he is quoting Deuteronomy 30 two verses
earlier — *the word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart* — and he keeps
the prophet's sequence before explaining it in his own.

## What is confessed

*The Lord Jesus.* In a Roman province where *Caesar is Lord* was the civic
formula, saying it about a crucified Galilean was not a private sentiment.

And what is believed is specific: *that God raised Him from the dead.* Not a
general trust in God. One historical claim.

## Who it is open to

*There is no difference here between Jew and Greek, for the same Lord is Lord of
all, and He is generous to all who call on Him.*

The word for *generous* is literally *rich toward* — the wealth language Paul
uses everywhere. And then the quotation that closes it: *everyone who calls on
the name of the Lord will be saved.*

Everyone. It is from Joel, and Peter used the same line at Pentecost.

> **What is not in the list.** No requirement to clean up first, no probation, no
> ceremony. A confession and a belief, from anyone, of any nation, calling on a
> name. Paul spends the next verses insisting that someone therefore has to go
> and tell them.
