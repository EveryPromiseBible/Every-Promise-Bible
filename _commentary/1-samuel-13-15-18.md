Samuel walks away, and the chapter starts counting what is left.

## The departure

*Then **Samuel got up and left Gilgal and went on his way**.*

He arrived just after the burnt offering, delivered the verdict, and left. See the note on
1 Samuel 13:11–14.

No farewell is recorded. He does not stay to help.

## The remnant

*Saul counted the men still with him — **about six hundred**.*

The chapter opened with three thousand chosen men, and before that the muster after the Ammonite
crisis had been in the hundreds of thousands.

Six hundred.

The rest are in caves, thickets, rocks, pits and cisterns, or across the Jordan. See the note on
1 Samuel 13:5–7.

## The number

Six hundred is the standing figure for a small band in this narrative.

David's men, in the wilderness, settle at six hundred too. See the note on
1 Samuel 22:1–2.

## The position

*Saul and his son Jonathan and their men **stayed at Gibeah of Benjamin**, while the Philistines
**camped at Michmash**.*

Two hills with a gorge between them — the Wadi Suweinit.

That gorge is where Jonathan climbs. See the note on 1 Samuel 14:1–5.

## The raiders

*Raiding parties went out from the Philistine camp **in three companies**.*

The Hebrew is **hammashchith** — the destroyer, the spoiler.

*One turned toward **Ophrah** in the land of Shual, another toward **Beth-Horon**, and the third
toward **the border overlooking the Valley of Zeboim**, out toward the wilderness.*

North, west, and east.

## What the three directions mean

They are not attacking Saul. They are stripping the countryside in three directions while he
sits on a hill with six hundred men and two swords.

The occupation is being administered, not fought.

## What is not happening

Nothing.

Saul has an army, a position and a son, and the narrative records only that he stayed.

The next paragraph explains the equipment situation — no blacksmith, no swords. See the note on
1 Samuel 13:19–23.

Between them, the two paragraphs make the case for doing nothing look overwhelming.

## What one man does about it

*One day Jonathan, Saul's son, said to his young armour-bearer, "**Come on — let's cross over**."*

And *he didn't tell his father*.

## Where Saul is when it happens

*On the outskirts of Gibeah **under a pomegranate tree** at Migron, with about six hundred men.*

The narrator supplies the tree.

## What the chapter is doing

Setting the arithmetic.

Three thousand down to six hundred. Two swords. Three raiding parties loose in the country.
Samuel gone, and the kingdom already declared forfeit.

Everything in chapter 14 happens against that page, and the narrator wrote it first on purpose.

> **What the chapter counts.** Men lost, weapons absent, and enemy columns out in three
> directions — and then Samuel walks away without being asked to stay.
