Three verses quoted at the first Christian sermon, with an argument attached.

## The gladness

*So my heart is glad, **my whole self sings**, and **my body rests in hope**.*

The Hebrew is **libbi… kevodi… besari** — my heart, my glory, my flesh.

**Kavod** — glory, weight. Used here of the self, and the Septuagint renders it *tongue*, which
Peter quotes.

**Basar** — flesh, body. The physical is named in the list.

## The claim

*For **You will not abandon me to the grave** or **let Your Holy One see decay**.*

The Hebrew is **lo-ta'azov naphshi lishol** — You will not leave my soul to Sheol.

**Shachat** — the pit, corruption, decay.

## What David meant

Read straightforwardly, confidence that God would not let him die.

Read as Peter reads it, something else — because David did die, and everyone knew where he was
buried.

## What Peter does with it

*"Men and brethren, let me speak freely to you of the patriarch David, that **he is both dead and
buried, and his tomb is with us to this day**."*

*"Therefore, being a prophet… **he foresaw this and spoke concerning the resurrection of the Christ,
that His soul was not left in Hades, nor did His flesh see corruption**."* See the note on
Acts 2:29–33.

The argument is empirical: the tomb is down the road, and it has somebody in it.

## What Paul does with it

Preaching at Antioch in Pisidia: *"For David, after he had served his own generation by the will
of God, fell asleep, was buried with his fathers, and saw corruption; **but He whom God raised up
saw no corruption**."* See the note on Acts 13:35–37.

Two apostles, two cities, same verse, same argument.

## The path

*You **make known to me the path of life**.*

The Hebrew is **orach chayyim** — the way of life.

## The fullness

*In Your presence is **fullness of joy**.*

The Hebrew is **sova semachot** — satisfaction of joys, plural.

**Sova** — the same root as *full of years*, said over Abraham. See the note on
Genesis 25:7–10.

## The location

*And at **Your right hand** are pleasures forever more.*

The Hebrew is **bimincha netzach** — at Your right hand, perpetually.

**Ne'imot** — pleasant things, delights.

## Where the right hand is

*Sit at My right hand, till I make Your enemies Your footstool.* See the note on
Psalms 110:1.

Peter quotes both psalms in the same sermon, and links them: *therefore being exalted to the right
hand of God*.

## What the psalm opens with

*Preserve me, O God, for in You I put my trust.*

*O my soul, you have said to the LORD, "You are my Lord. **My goodness is nothing apart from
You**."*

## The other famous line

*The LORD is the portion of my inheritance and my cup… **the lines have fallen to me in pleasant
places**; yes, I have a good inheritance.*

The Hebrew is **chavalim** — measuring lines, the surveyor's cords used to mark out an allotment.

## The title

*A Michtam of David.*

Six psalms carry it, and nobody knows what it means. Possibly *a golden psalm*, possibly an
inscription.

> **The argument.** Peter's use of it is empirical: David wrote *You will not let Your Holy One see
> decay*, and David's tomb was a short walk from where Peter was standing.
