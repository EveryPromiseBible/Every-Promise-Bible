Three verses, and two households come out of them differently.

## The fear

*That day **David was afraid of God**.*

The Hebrew is *David feared God that day*.

A verse earlier he was angry or grieved. Now he is frightened, and Chronicles records both
without smoothing them.

## The question

*"**How can I ever bring the ark of God home to me?**"*

A real question, and the right one.

He does not know the answer. Two chapters later he does, and he says so out loud to the
Levites: *we did not seek Him about the proper way*. See the note on
1 Chronicles 15:11–15.

The interval is what the question produces.

## The diversion

*So he **did not take the ark into the City of David**; instead he had it **turned aside to the
house of Obed-edom the Gittite**.*

Six miles short. Thirty thousand men standing in a field, and the procession stops.

## The name

**Obed-edom** — from **eved**, servant, and **Edom**.

The Chronicler lists him among the Levites, and the epithet **the Gittite** — a man of Gath —
probably records where his family had lived.

Gath is Goliath's town.

## The duration

*The ark of God stayed with the family of Obed-edom in his house **three months**.*

## The result

*And **the LORD blessed his household and everything he had**.*

The Hebrew is *his house and all that he had*.

Samuel has the same sentence. Both accounts stop to record it.

## The point the two verses make together

Uzzah touched it and died. Obed-edom housed it for three months and prospered, with everyone
under his roof.

The same object, six miles apart, within the same season.

The ark was never the danger. The handling was, and Chronicles has already said so.

## What made David try again

*Then word reached David: "The LORD has blessed Obed-edom's whole household."* See the note on
2 Samuel 6:12–15.

The blessing is visible enough to be reported to a king.

He had asked *how can the ark come to me?* — and the answer arrives as gossip about a
neighbour's good fortune.

## What Obed-edom does afterwards

He does not go back to farming.

*And he left **Obed-edom and his sixty-eight relatives** to serve with them, while Obed-edom
son of Jeduthun and Hosah kept the gates.* See the note on
1 Chronicles 16:37–38.

He follows the ark to Jerusalem and takes a post.

## What happened to his family

*Obed-edom had sons too… **for God had truly blessed him**.*

Eight named sons, and sixty-two descendants listed as *capable men, strong for the work*. See
the note on 1 Chronicles 26:1–5.

The Chronicler, writing centuries later, is still counting them.

## What the three months were

An interruption that nobody planned, in a house nobody had chosen, and it is the only part of
the first attempt that went well.

> **What the two households prove.** Same ark, same season, six miles apart. One man died
> touching it and one family was blessed housing it — and the difference was never the object.
