The church at Ephesus is commended for a great deal and then told one thing is
wrong, and the one thing outweighs the list.

## What they had

Read the verses just before: hard work, perseverance, intolerance of evil, they
had tested false apostles and found them out, they had endured hardships for His
name and not grown weary.

By any external measure this is a strong church. Nothing on that list is
withdrawn.

## The charge

*That love you had at the beginning — you have walked away from it.*

The Greek is *your first love*, and the verb is to leave or abandon. Not that it
cooled. That they walked off from it.

Doctrine intact, effort intact, discernment intact — and the thing that made it
all worth doing has been left somewhere behind.

## The remedy

Three imperatives, and the middle one is the practical one:

- **remember** how high you stood before you fell
- **turn back**
- **do again what you did then** — the first works

He does not tell them to work up a feeling. He tells them to do the things they
did when the love was fresh. The affection is recovered by returning to the
actions that belonged to it.

The word for *remember* is present continuous — keep remembering, not one act of
recollection.

## The warning

*I will take your lampstand away from where it stands.*

The lampstand is the church itself, as chapter 1 explains. Not a threat to
individuals' salvation — a warning that a congregation which keeps everything
except the love stops being a light in that place.

## What "remember" is doing there

The first word of the remedy is not *try*. It is ***remember** therefore from where you have
fallen*.

**Mnemoneue** — a present imperative: keep remembering. He sends them backwards before He sends them
anywhere else, because first love is not a thing that can be worked up. It only ever appeared as a
reaction, and it reappears the same way.

*We love Him **because He first loved us**.*

That is the mechanism, and it has no other setting. A church trying to manufacture affection for
Jesus Christ by resolution is attempting the one thing the New Testament never asks for. What is
asked for is that they go back and look again at what was done for them, and the works follow the
way they did the first time.

And notice what is at stake in the warning: the lampstand, not the salvation. A church that loses
love does not stop belonging to Him. It stops giving light.

> **What is being asked for.** The whole letter is written by the One who chapter
> 1 describes as walking among the lampstands. He is not asking for more output
> from a church that is already working hard. He is asking to be loved by it.
