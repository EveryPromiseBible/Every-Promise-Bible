The army before the battle, and the account is entirely about where people went to hide.

## The odds

*Thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and troops **as countless as the sand on the
seashore**.*

The chariot figure is very large and some manuscripts read three thousand. Either way the
comparison is the standard Hebrew one for a number nobody counts.

## What Israel had

The chapter says a few verses later: *there was no blacksmith to be found throughout all the
land of Israel, for the Philistines said, "Lest the Hebrews make swords or spears."*

They had to go down to the Philistines to sharpen their ploughshares.

*So it came about, on the day of battle, that **there was neither sword nor spear found in the
hand of any of the people** — but only with Saul and Jonathan his son.*

Two swords in the whole army.

## The place

*They came up and camped at **Michmash**.*

A pass with a steep gorge, about seven miles north of Jerusalem.

## What the men did

*When the men of Israel saw how desperate their situation was, they **scrambled to hide in
caves and thickets, among the rocks, in pits and cisterns**.*

Five hiding places listed. The Hebrew piles them up.

The word for their situation is **tsar** — pressed, narrow, in a tight place.

## The deserters

*Some Hebrews even **slipped across the Jordan into Gad and Gilead**.*

Over the river, out of the country.

## The king

*But **Saul stayed at Gilgal**, and his troops with him were **trembling with fear**.*

The Hebrew is **charedu** — they trembled, they were terrified.

Saul stays where he was told to. Samuel had said *wait seven days, till I come to you and show
you what you should do*.

## What happens next

He waits seven days, the people start slipping away from him, and on the seventh day — before
the day is out — he offers the burnt offering himself.

*And it happened, as soon as he had finished presenting the burnt offering, that Samuel came.*

Minutes.

## Saul's explanation

*"When I saw that **the people were scattered from me**, and that you did not come within the
days appointed, and that the Philistines gathered together at Michmash, then I said, 'The
Philistines will now come down on me at Gilgal.'… **So I felt compelled, and offered a burnt
offering**."*

Three reasons, all of them factually correct.

The Hebrew is *I forced myself*.

## The verdict

*"You have done foolishly. You have not kept the commandment of the LORD your God… **now your
kingdom shall not continue. The LORD has sought for Himself a man after His own heart**."*

## What one man does about the odds

Jonathan, in the next chapter, with his armour-bearer and no army:

*"Come, let us go over to the garrison of these uncircumcised; **it may be that the LORD will
work for us. For nothing restrains the LORD from saving by many or by few**."*

They climb the gorge on hands and feet and kill twenty men, and the panic spreads through the
Philistine camp — *and the earth quaked; so it was a very great trembling*.

The same word as verse 7. The trembling changes sides.

## Where Gilgal is

Where the twelve stones from the Jordan were set up. See the note on Joshua 4:19–24.

Saul is standing on the site of a memorial to a crossing that did not need an army, waiting
for a prophet.

> **What the chapter counts.** Not soldiers. Caves, thickets, rocks, pits and cisterns — and
> two swords in the entire nation.
