Jesus says *I am the good shepherd* twice in five verses, and between the two He
describes the alternative.

## The word "good"

**Kalos** — not merely morally good but fine, admirable, the right one for the
job. It is the word used of a good tree, a beautiful thing, a work well done.

## What makes him good

*The good shepherd lays down His life for the sheep.*

Not his skill or his knowledge of the country. The definition offered is a
willingness to die, and it is stated before anything else.

Ordinary shepherds risked their lives — David fought lions and bears. But risking
and *laying down* are different, and John's Gospel keeps the distinction: no one
takes it from Me, I lay it down of My own accord.

## The hired hand

*The sheep are not his own, so when he sees the wolf coming he abandons them and
runs… he is only hired, and the sheep mean nothing to him.*

Nothing is said about him being wicked. He is not accused of stealing — that was
the thief, two verses earlier. His failure is ownership: they are not his, so at
the moment it costs something he leaves.

## The knowing

*I know My own, and My own know Me — just as the Father knows Me, and I know the
Father.*

The comparison is astonishing and easy to read past. The mutual knowledge between
Christ and His people is set on the same scale as the knowledge between the Son
and the Father.

## The repetition

He says *I lay down My life for the sheep* twice in the passage — once as the
definition and once as the conclusion, with the knowing in between.
