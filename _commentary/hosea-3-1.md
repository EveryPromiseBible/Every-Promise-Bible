The second command to Hosea, and it is harder than the first.

## What the first one was

*"Go. Marry a woman who will not be faithful to you."*

That was chapter 1, and he did it, and she bore three children with names that were sentences.

## What this one is

*"**Go again. Love a woman who is loved by another** and gives herself to unfaithfulness."*

The Hebrew is **od** — again, still, yet more.

The first command was to marry somebody who *would* be unfaithful. This one is to go and love
somebody who **is** — presently, in another man's house.

## The word

*Love her.* **Ehav.**

Not take her back. Not forgive her. The imperative is the affection itself, and it is issued as an
order.

## The comparison

*"**Love her the way the LORD loves the people of Israel**, even as they turn to other gods and to
their cakes of raisins."*

The clause is the reason the book exists. Hosea's marriage is not an illustration chosen
afterwards to explain the theology; the marriage is the assignment, and the theology is the
explanation given for it.

*"Even as they turn"* — present tense on both sides. God is not described as loving them after they
came back.

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that **while we were still sinners, Christ died
for us**.*

*In this is love, **not that we loved God, but that He loved us** and sent His Son to be the
propitiation for our sins.*

## The cakes of raisins

**Ashishei anavim** — pressed raisin cakes, used at festivals and, in Canaanite practice, in
fertility worship.

Hosea specifies what she preferred. The detail is deliberately small and deliberately cheap.

## What Hosea does

*So **I bought her back** for myself.* See the note on Hosea 3:2–3.

## What it costs the prophet

Nothing in the chapter registers Hosea's feelings, and the book never has him complain.

*"A disciple is not above his teacher… **It is enough for a disciple that he be like his
teacher**."*

The prophets are repeatedly made to live the message: Ezekiel is forbidden to mourn his wife,
Jeremiah is forbidden to marry, Isaiah walks barefoot for three years.

*And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us.*

The last of them does not act out the message. He is it.

> **The word at the front.** *Again.* The first command was to marry a woman who would be
> unfaithful. This one is to go, while she is in another man's house, and love her — and the
> reason given is that this is what God is doing at the same moment.
