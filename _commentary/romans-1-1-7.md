The longest greeting Paul ever wrote, to the one church he had never visited.

## The self-description

*From Paul, **a servant of Jesus Christ**.*

The Greek is **doulos** — a slave.

Not a hired servant. Owned property.

He puts it before the apostleship.

## The three descriptions

**Doulos** — slave.

**Kletos apostolos** — called an apostle.

**Aphorismenos** — set apart, separated.

**Aphorizo** is the root behind the word *Pharisee* — the separated ones. Paul was one, and uses
the word of a different separation.

## The subject

*Set apart for **the good news of God**.*

The Greek is **euangelion Theou**.

## Its age

*The news He **promised long ago through His prophets in the holy Scriptures**.*

The gospel is described as something previously announced. Paul opens a letter to Gentiles by
grounding it in Jewish Scripture.

## The two clauses about the Son

*Who was **born from the line of David according to the flesh**.*

*And was **marked out as the Son of God with power**, according to the Spirit of holiness, **by the
resurrection from the dead**.*

The Greek is **horisthentos** — appointed, designated, marked out.

## What the pair does

Descent and declaration. Flesh and Spirit. Birth and resurrection.

Paul is probably quoting an existing confession here; the balanced phrasing does not read like his
usual prose.

## The commission

*It is through Him that we received **grace, and a commission**.*

The Greek is **charin kai apostolen** — grace and apostleship.

Grace first, and the sending second — in that order, as in Ephesians: *this grace was given*.

## The purpose

*To call people of every nation to **the obedience that comes from trusting Him**.*

The Greek is **eis hupakoen pisteos** — unto obedience of faith.

## The phrase

**Hupakoe pisteos**.

The genitive can mean *the obedience that faith is*, *the obedience faith produces*, or *obedience
to the faith*. Commentators divide.

It appears at the start of Romans and again in the last sentence of the letter, framing the whole
argument.

## The readers

*And **you are among those nations**, you who have been called to belong to Jesus Christ.*

The Greek is **kletoi Iesou Christou** — called of Jesus Christ.

## The address

*To everyone in Rome **whom God loves and has called to be His holy people**.*

The Greek is **agapetois Theou, kletois hagiois** — beloved of God, called saints.

Not a description of their conduct. Two things done to them.

## The greeting

*Grace to you and peace.*

The Greek is **charis kai eirene**.

Paul's standard opening — a Greek greeting word turned into **charis**, and the Hebrew **shalom**.

## What he says next

*First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for you all, **that your faith is spoken of throughout
the whole world**.*

*For **I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ, for it is the power of God to salvation**.* See the
note on Romans 1:16–17.

The thesis of the letter, four verses after the greeting ends.

> **The order.** Slave, then apostle. Grace, then commission. Paul puts what he is under before
> what he was sent to do.
