Two men climb a cliff, and by the end of the paragraph a whole army is running.

## The showing

**14:11** *So the two of them **let the Philistine garrison see them**.*

Deliberately, as agreed. See the note on 1 Samuel 14:6–10.

## The taunt

**14:11** again: *"Look! **The Hebrews are crawling out of the holes they've been hiding in!**"*

Accurate. The previous chapter had the army hiding in caves, thickets, rocks, pits and
cisterns. See the note on 1 Samuel 13:5–7.

The Philistines know exactly what the situation is, and are enjoying it.

## The invitation

**14:12** *"**Come on up here — we've got something to teach you!**"*

The Hebrew is *and we will show you a thing*.

Contempt. They are not forming up; they are calling two men up a cliff for sport.

And it is the exact wording Jonathan had set as his sign.

## The reading

**14:12** again: *"**Climb up behind me — the LORD has handed them over to Israel**."*

Perfect tense. Has handed over, before anything has happened.

The *maybe* of verse 6 is gone. He had said he would take that answer as the sign, and he does.

## The climb

**14:13** *Jonathan **scrambled up on his hands and feet** with his armour-bearer right behind.*

The Hebrew is *on his hands and on his feet*.

Both hands occupied. No shield, no weapon ready, and a garrison at the top watching him
come.

This is the most defenceless a man can be in a fight, and it is the only route.

## The fighting

**14:13** again: *Enemies fell before Jonathan, and **his armour-bearer finished them off behind him**.*

Two-man work, and the division of labour is stated.

## The count

**14:14** *In that first strike Jonathan and his armour-bearer struck down **about twenty men in an area of
about half an acre**.*

The Hebrew is difficult — *within about half a furrow of an acre of land*, an agricultural
measure.

Twenty men, in a space you could walk across.

## The collapse

**14:15** *Then **terror swept through the whole camp — the field, the garrison, the raiders — the ground
shook**.*

The Hebrew is **charadah** — trembling.

The same root as chapter 13, where Saul's troops *were trembling*. See the note on
1 Samuel 13:5–7.

The trembling changes sides.

## The verdict

**14:15** again: *It was **a panic sent by God**.*

The Hebrew is **cherdat Elohim** — a trembling of God.

The narrator names the cause. Twenty men fell to two; the rest is not attributed to them.

## What Saul does

His watchmen see the enemy camp, **14:16–17**: *melting away and going here and there*, and he calls a roll to
find out who is missing.

Then he starts to consult the priest, and interrupts him, **14:18–19**: *withdraw your hand* — because the
noise is increasing and there is no time.

The king is asking for guidance about a battle his son started.

## How it ends

**14:23** *So the LORD saved Israel that day, and the battle shifted to Beth Aven.*

And the Israelites who had been hiding in the hill country came out and joined in, and the
Hebrews who had defected to the Philistines changed sides again.

Everybody who had run comes back.

## What Saul does next

Puts the army under an oath not to eat until evening, **14:24**: *cursed is the man who eats any food
until I have taken vengeance on my enemies*.

Jonathan, who did not hear it, eats honey and says, **14:29**: *my father has troubled the land*.

The oath nearly costs him his life, and the people refuse to let the king kill him, **14:45**: *shall
Jonathan die, who has accomplished this great deliverance in Israel? Certainly not!*

> **How he went up.** On hands and feet, both hands occupied, no shield, with the garrison
> watching — and the invitation to do it had been an insult.
