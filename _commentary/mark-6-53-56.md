A summary passage, four verses long, and Mark packs a whole region into it.

## Where they land

*They crossed over and came to land at **Gennesaret** and tied up the boat there.*

They had spent the night on the lake in a storm, and Jesus had walked out to them on
the water. See the note on Mark 6:45–52.

This is the morning after.

## Recognised

*And **the moment they stepped out, people recognized Him**.*

No announcement and no preaching. He is identified getting off a boat.

## What the region did

*They **ran all through the countryside** and began **carrying the sick to Him on their
mats**, wherever they heard He was.*

Whole villages mobilised. People are carried on the pallets they were lying on, over
open country, to intercept a man who kept moving.

## Every place

*Every **village**, every **town**, every **farm** He came to.*

Mark lists three sizes of settlement to say the same thing three ways: everywhere.

## Where they put them

*They **laid the sick out in the marketplaces**.*

The market was the one place in a village everyone passed through. They put the sick in
the road He would have to walk down.

## What they asked

*And **begged Him to let them touch even the edge of His clothes**.*

The Greek is **kraspedon** — the tassel on the corner of the garment, the **tzitzit**
commanded in Numbers.

They are asking for the least possible contact: not His hand, not a word, not a visit
to the house. The hem.

## Where they got the idea

Two chapters earlier a woman had done exactly this in a crowd and been healed, and
Jesus had stopped the procession to find out who it was and tell her *your faith has
made you well.* See the note on Mark 5:25–29.

She had been meant to stay anonymous. Because He would not let her, a whole district
now knows what to reach for.

## The result

*And **everyone who touched Him was made well**.*

The Greek is **esozonto** — imperfect, continuous: they *were being* made whole, one
after another, as long as it went on.

Mark records no exceptions and no failures in this passage.

## The contrast a page earlier

In his own town, *He could do no mighty work there, except that He laid His hands on a
few sick people and healed them. And He marvelled because of their unbelief.*

Same chapter. Nazareth got a few; Gennesaret got everyone who touched Him.

## What He had just done

Fed five thousand men with five loaves, sent the disciples ahead, prayed alone on a
mountain, walked on the sea, and now this. Mark 6 has no gaps in it.

> **What was on offer.** Not a service, not a queue, not a set of conditions. A man
> walking through a marketplace with people lying along the edges of it, reaching for
> a tassel.
