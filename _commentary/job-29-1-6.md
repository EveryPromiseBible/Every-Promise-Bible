The friends have stopped talking, and Job describes what he has lost.

## Where this is

The three cycles of speeches are over. Bildad's last effort was six verses long. See the note on
Job 25:1–6.

Job now speaks for three uninterrupted chapters: what he was, what he is, and his final defence.

## The opening

*Job **took up his words again**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyosep Iyyov se'et meshalo** — and Job again lifted up his discourse.

**Mashal** — a proverb, a parable, an oracle. The word for elevated speech.

## The wish

*"Oh, if only **the old days would come back to me**, those months when **God kept watch over my
life**."*

The Hebrew is **kiyrachei-qedem** — as in the months of old.

**Yishmereni** — He guarded me, kept watch over me.

## What he misses first

Not the wealth. Not the children.

The first thing named is being watched over.

## The lamp

*"**His lamp used to shine above my head**, and **by that light I walked safely through the dark**."*

The Hebrew is **behillo nero alei roshi** — when His lamp shone over my head.

The image is of a light carried above a traveller — enough to see by, moving with him.

*Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.* See the note on
Psalms 119:105–112.

## The days

*"Those were **the days of my ripeness**."*

The Hebrew is **bimei chorpi** — in the days of my autumn, my harvest-time.

Not youth. The season when things come in.

## The friendship

*"When **the friendship of God rested warm over my tent**."*

The Hebrew is **besod Eloah alei ahali** — the intimate counsel of God over my tent.

**Sod** — a confidential circle, a council, the private conversation of friends.

*The secret of the LORD is with those who fear Him.*

## The children

*"When the Almighty was still near me and **my children gathered close around**."*

Seven sons and three daughters, killed when a wind struck the house where they were eating.

He mentions them in a subordinate clause.

## The cream

*"In those days **my steps were washed in cream**."*

The Hebrew is **birchotz halichai bechemah** — when I washed my steps in butter.

Curds so plentiful you could wade in them.

## The oil

*"And **the rock beside me poured out rivers of oil**."*

Olive presses were cut into rock. Oil running out of stone is the image of production so heavy
the rock itself seems to give it.

## What follows in the chapter

*"When I went out to the gate by the city… the young men saw me and hid, and the aged arose and
stood."*

*"**Because I delivered the poor who cried out, the fatherless and the one who had no helper**… I
was eyes to the blind, and I was feet to the lame."*

*"I broke the fangs of the wicked, and plucked the victim from his teeth."*

He describes a public life spent on people with no standing.

## What the next chapter says

*"**But now they mock at me, men younger than I**, whose fathers I disdained to put with the dogs of
my flock."*

*"And now my soul is poured out because of my plight; the days of affliction take hold of me."*

The reversal is total, and Job sets the two chapters side by side himself.

## What he never learns

The reader was told in chapter 1. Job never is.

The answer he gets is four chapters of questions about wild goats, ostriches, the horse, and
Leviathan — and *I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees You*. See the
note on Job 42:1–6.

## The three clauses before the property

Not the herds, and not the children.

*"Oh, that I were as in months past, as in the days **when God watched over me**; when **His
lamp shone upon my head**… when **the friendly counsel of God was over my tent**."*

Three clauses about nearness before a word about property.

## The Hebrew of the third one

**Sod** — intimate counsel, the confidence between friends. *The secret of the LORD is with
those who fear Him.*

*"**No longer do I call you servants**, for a servant does not know what his master is doing;
but I have called you friends, for all things that I heard from My Father I have made known to
you."*

**Jesus** describes the same thing and gives it as permanent.

## What he does not know

That he never lost it. The book opens with God calling him *My servant Job* to the accuser's
face, and closes with the same title said four times in one paragraph.

*"**I will never leave you nor forsake you.**"*

The lamp Job says has gone out was never put out. He is grieving an absence the reader knows
is not there.

> **What he misses first.** Not the herds and not the children — *those months when God kept watch
> over my life*. The list starts with a lamp carried above his head.
