The sentence puts God inside the trouble rather than above it.

## The opening

*Surely **they are My people**, He said. **Children who will not be false**.*

The Hebrew is **banim lo yeshaqqeru** — sons who will not deal falsely.

## What it sounds like

Hope, stated in advance, about people who will in fact deal falsely.

Isaiah records the expectation without softening what followed it.

## The consequence

*And so **He became their Savior**.*

The Hebrew is **vayehi lahem lemoshia**.

## The line

*In all their distress, **He too was distressed**.*

The Hebrew is **bekol-tzaratam lo tzar** — in all their affliction, to Him affliction.

## The textual crux

The Masoretic text has two readings side by side.

The written consonants — the **ketiv** — give **lo** with an aleph: *not*. Read that way: *in all
their affliction He was not afflicted*.

The **qere**, the reading tradition, gives **lo** with a vav: *to Him*. In all their affliction, He
was afflicted.

The two words are pronounced identically.

## Which way it is taken

Most translations follow the **qere**, and the Illumination does.

The ancient versions divide. It is one of the best-known variants in the book.

## The next clause

*And **the angel of His presence saved them**.*

The Hebrew is **umal'ak panav** — the messenger of His face.

## Who that is

*Behold, I send an Angel before you… **My name is in Him**.* See the note on Exodus 23:20–21.

And *My Presence will go with you, and I will give you rest*. See the note on Exodus 33:14–15.

The Hebrew there is **panai**, My face.

## The three verbs

*In His love and in His mercy **He redeemed them**. **He lifted them up. He carried them**, all the days
of old.*

The Hebrew is **ge'alam** — He acted as **go'el**, the kinsman-redeemer, the relative with the right
and duty to buy back.

And **yenattelem** — He lifted. And **yenasse'em** — He bore.

## The picture

*As a man carries his son*, in Moses' phrase, *in all the way that you went*.

## What the passage does next

*But they rebelled and grieved His Holy Spirit; so He turned Himself against them as an enemy.*

The Hebrew is **itztzevu et-ruach qodsho** — they grieved His Holy Spirit.

One of the few Old Testament sentences that names the Spirit and attributes grief to Him.

## Where Paul takes the verb

*And **do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God**, by whom you were sealed for the day of redemption.*
See the note on Ephesians 4:30–32.

The Greek is **lupeite** — the Septuagint's word for the Hebrew here.

## The chapter's opening

*Who is this who comes from Edom, with dyed garments from Bozrah… **I have trodden the winepress
alone**.* See the note on Isaiah 63:1–3.

The chapter opens with a solitary figure in bloodied clothes and turns immediately to a God who
shares the affliction of His people.

## Where the sharing ends up

*Inasmuch then as the children have partaken of flesh and blood, **He Himself likewise shared in the
same**.* See the note on Hebrews 2:14–16.

The Greek is **meteschen** — He took a share of.

> **The variant.** *He was not afflicted* and *He was afflicted* differ by one silent letter, and
> the two words sound the same. The reading tradition puts Him inside it.
