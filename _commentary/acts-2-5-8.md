The crowd's question is not about the miracle. It is about the accents.

## Who is in the city

*Now there were **devout Jews staying in Jerusalem, from every nation under heaven**.*

The Greek is **eulabeis** — reverent, careful.

It is Pentecost, one of the three pilgrim feasts. Jerusalem's population multiplied for the
festivals, and Jews from across the diaspora were resident or visiting.

## What draws them

*When **this sound went out**, a crowd came together.*

The Greek is **phones tautes** — this sound, or this voice.

Verse 2 called it *a sound from heaven, as of a rushing mighty wind*. See the note on
Acts 2:1–4.

Something audible from outside the building.

## The bewilderment

*And **they were bewildered**.*

The Greek is **sunechuthe** — poured together, confounded.

The same verb the Septuagint uses at Babel: *let Us go down and there **confuse** their
language*.

Luke uses Babel's word, at the moment the confusion runs backwards.

## The reason

*Because **each of them was hearing his own language being spoken**.*

The Greek is **te idia dialekto** — in his own dialect.

**Dialektos** — not just the language, but the local form of it. The particular speech of a
region.

## The astonishment

*They were astonished, and they kept saying to one another, "**Are not all these men who are
speaking Galileans?**"*

Galileans were known for a distinctive accent. It gave Peter away in the courtyard: *surely you
are one of them, for **your speech betrays you***.

The crowd is identifying provincial fishermen by ear.

## The question

*"Then **how is it that every one of us is hearing them in the language we were born to?**"*

The Greek is **te idia dialekto hemon en he egennethemen** — in our own dialect, in which we were
born.

## What that phrase means

Not the trade language. Not Greek, which everyone in the empire could manage.

The speech of home — the one learned before school, the one a mother uses.

## The list that follows

*Parthians and Medes and Elamites, those dwelling in Mesopotamia, Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and
Asia, Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the parts of Libya adjoining Cyrene, visitors from Rome,
both Jews and proselytes, Cretans and Arabs.* See the note on Acts 2:9–11.

Fifteen regions, running east to west. Luke names them all.

## What was being said

*"**We hear them speaking in our own tongues the wonderful works of God**."*

The Greek is **ta megaleia tou Theou** — the great things of God.

Not preaching. Not instruction. The content is praise, and the sermon has not started yet.

## Why the languages

The message could have been delivered in Greek and understood by nearly everybody present.

The miracle is not about comprehension. It is about which language it arrived in.

## Babel

*Therefore its name is called Babel, **because there the LORD confused the language of all the
earth**.*

One language became many, and the people scattered.

Here the many hear one message, and three thousand are added in a day.

## What some in the crowd conclude

*Others mocking said, "**They are full of new wine**."*

And Peter, standing up with the eleven: *these are not drunk, as you suppose, since it is only the
third hour of the day*.

Then Joel, and David, and *this Jesus God has raised up, of which we are all witnesses*.

> **The phrase.** *The language we were born to* — not the trade language everyone could manage,
> but the one learned at home. That is what the crowd notices.
