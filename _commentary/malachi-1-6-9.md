---
key: Malachi 1:6–9
title: "Try offering them to your governor"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The first charge in the last book of the Old Testament, and it is about what is being put on the
altar.

## The question

*"A son honours his father. A servant respects his master. **So if I am your Father, where is the
honour due Me? If I am your Master, where is the respect?**"*

Two ordinary relationships, and God asks which one He is in.

*"But **as many as received Him, to them He gave the right to become children of God**, to those who
believe in His name."*

*"**No longer do I call you servants**… but I have called you friends."*

## Who is addressed

*"You **priests** who treat My name as though it were nothing."*

Malachi's whole book is a dialogue, and the pattern is fixed: God makes a charge, the audience
answers with a question, and God produces the evidence. It happens seven times.

*"And you say, '**How have we treated Your name that way?**'"*

They are not defiant. They cannot see it.

## The evidence

*"**By laying defiled food on My altar.**" And still you ask, "How have we defiled You?" **By saying
the LORD's table is of no account.**"*

The Hebrew is **shulchan YHWH nivzeh hu** — the table of the LORD, it is despicable.

Not said out loud. Said by what is put on it.

## The animals

*"When you bring **a blind** animal to sacrifice, is that no wrong? When you bring **the lame and the
sick**, is that no wrong?"*

The law is specific: *whatever has a defect, you shall not offer, for it shall not be acceptable
on your behalf.*

Blind, lame and sick are the three categories a farmer loses nothing by giving away.

## The test

*"**Try offering them to your governor. Would he be pleased with you? Would he welcome you?**"*

The Hebrew is **haqrivehu na lephechatekha** — present it, please, to your governor.

The **pechah** was the Persian administrator. Malachi's argument is not theological. He asks
whether they would risk it with a man who could have them dismissed.

## What was offered instead

*Knowing that you were not redeemed with corruptible things, like silver or gold… but with the
precious blood of **Christ**, as of **a lamb without blemish and without spot**.*

**Peter**'s two words are **amomou kai aspilou** — the Greek Old Testament's vocabulary for exactly
what Malachi's priests were not bringing.

*How much more shall **the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered Himself without
spot to God**, cleanse your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?*

**Hebrews** 9, and the phrase is **amomon to theo** — spotless, to God. The whole complaint of
Malachi 1 is answered by one offering, and the one bringing it is the one being offered.

## Who came to the blind and the lame

*Then **the blind and the lame came to Him in the temple, and He healed them**.*

**Matthew** 21, in the courts where those animals had been refused. The categories Malachi names as
unfit for the altar are the ones **Jesus** receives at the same address.

*"Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city, and bring in here **the poor and the maimed
and the lame and the blind**."*

## The last line

*"So now, **plead with God to be gracious to you. With gifts like these in your hands, will He
welcome any of you?**"*

The question is rhetorical and the answer is no. Malachi will not let them buy the favour with
what they are holding.

*Therefore let us come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy and **find grace to
help in time of need**.*

Empty-handed, and welcomed. **Hebrews** 4 answers Malachi's closing question by removing the gifts
from the transaction.

## Two verses on

*"**For from the rising of the sun, even to its going down, My name shall be great among the
Gentiles**; in every place incense shall be offered to My name, and a pure offering."*

The chapter that opens by rejecting Israel's sacrifices ends by promising an acceptable one from
everybody else.

> **The test he proposes.** Not a doctrine — an experiment. *Try it on the governor.* And what
> finally went on the altar is described by Peter and Hebrews with the exact word the priests'
> animals were missing: **without blemish**.
