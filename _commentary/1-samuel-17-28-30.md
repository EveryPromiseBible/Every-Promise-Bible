The first opposition David meets is not the giant.

## Who it is

**17:28** *When **Eliab, David's oldest brother**, heard David talking with the men.*

The brother Samuel had looked at first and been told *I have not chosen him*. See the note
on 1 Samuel 16:6–7.

Eliab was in the room when the oil went on his youngest brother's head.

## The reaction

**17:28** again: *He **burned with anger**.*

The Hebrew is *his anger was kindled*.

## The four accusations

**17:28** again: *"**Why did you even come down here?**"*

*"And **who's watching those few sheep in the wilderness?**"*

*"**I know how proud you are, and how wicked your heart is**."*

*"**You only came to watch the battle**."*

Each one is aimed lower than the last: his presence, his job, his character, his motive.

**Those few sheep** — the Hebrew is *those few sheep*. It is contempt for the work and for
the size of it.

David had been sent by their father with bread and cheese, on their father's instructions.

## The irony in the accusation

**17:28** again: *I know how proud you are, and how wicked your heart is.*

The chapter before has just established the standard, **1 Samuel 16:7**: *the LORD does not see the way people
see… the LORD looks at the heart*.

Eliab makes a confident claim about a heart, and he is the man that sentence was spoken
over.

## David's answer

**17:29** *"**What have I done now?**"*

The Hebrew is *what have I done now?* — the *now* implies a history.

**17:29** again: *"**Can't I even ask a question?**"*

The Greek and Hebrew both come out as a mild protest. He does not defend himself and does
not raise it.

## What he does

**17:30** *Then he **turned to someone else and asked the same thing**, and **got the same answer**.*

He steps around it.

The question he was asking, **17:26**: *what shall be done for the man who kills this Philistine?* —
he asks again, of the next man.

He does not argue with his brother, does not appeal to the anointing, and does not leave.

## What he had been told about himself

Nothing that he could use here. The anointing had happened *in front of his brothers* and
Samuel had gone home to Ramah without explaining anything. See the note on
1 Samuel 16:11–13.

## What happens next

Saul hears about it, sends for him, and the exchange about the lion and the bear follows.

Then the armour, and the five stones from the brook, and the giant's contempt. See the
note on 1 Samuel 17:41–44.

Two sneers in one chapter — one from his brother and one from the Philistine — and David
answers the second one at length and the first one in six words.

## Where Eliab turns up later

Chronicles lists his daughter as married into the royal line, and 2 Chronicles 11 names
her as the mother of Rehoboam's wife.

The narrator never mentions the quarrel again.

## Who objects first

Not Goliath. His eldest brother.

**17:28** again: *"Why did you come down here? And with whom have you left **those few sheep** in the
wilderness? I know your pride and the insolence of your heart."*

Four accusations, and every one of them is about motive. The giant has not said a word to
David yet.

John, of Jesus, **John 7:5**: *for **even His own brothers did not believe in Him**.*

**Mark 6:4**: *"A prophet is not without honor except in his own country, **among his own relatives**, and
in his own house."*

## What David does about it

**17:30** again: *And he turned from him toward another and said the same thing.*

He does not answer the four accusations, defend his motives, or explain about the sheep. He
turns and asks the question again.

> **The order of the opposition.** The brother comes before the giant, and the accusation
> is about the heart — which is the exact category the previous chapter said only God can
> see.
