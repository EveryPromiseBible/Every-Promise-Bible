They argue about rank at the last supper, and He answers by telling them what they have already done.

## When this happens

*And then **an argument started among them** about which of them ought to be considered the
greatest.*

The bread has been broken. The cup has been given. He has just said *the hand of My betrayer is
with Me on the table*.

And then this.

## The Greek

**Philoneikia** — a love of strife, contentiousness. A rivalry.

Luke places it here deliberately. Matthew and Mark record a similar argument earlier in the
ministry; Luke puts one at the table.

## What kings do

*"**The kings of the nations lord it over their people**, and the men who hold power over them
**get themselves called benefactors**."*

The Greek is **euergetai** — benefactors.

It was a real title. Ptolemy III of Egypt was Euergetes; the honorific was voted to rulers by
cities across the Greek world, sometimes in gratitude and sometimes under pressure.

The system was not a fiction. It was how power presented itself.

## The instruction

*"**It is not to be that way with you**."*

The Greek is **humeis de ouch houtos** — but you, not so.

## The inversion

*"**The greatest among you should become like the youngest**, and **the one who leads like the one
who serves**."*

The Greek is **ho neoteros** — the younger. In a household, the one with the least standing and
the most errands.

## The question

*"Think about it — **who is greater, the one sitting at the table or the one waiting on him?** The
one at the table, surely."*

He grants the ordinary answer. He does not pretend the world works differently.

## The claim

*"**And yet here I am among you as the one who serves**."*

The Greek is **ho diakonon** — the one serving.

Said at a table, by the host, on the night John records Him with a towel round His waist. See the
note on John 13:4–5.

## What He says about them

*"**You are the ones who have stayed with Me** through everything I have been put through."*

The Greek is **hoi diamemenekotes** — perfect participle. Those who have remained throughout.

## When He says it

Hours before every one of them runs.

Peter's denial is predicted in the next paragraph — *before the rooster crows, you will deny three
times that you know Me*.

He commends their constancy immediately before naming its collapse.

## The grant

*"**And I am giving you a kingdom, just as My Father has given one to Me**."*

The Greek is **diatithemai** — I covenant, I assign by will. The verb behind **diatheke**,
covenant or testament.

He is bequeathing it, the way a man disposes of an estate.

## What it comes with

*"So that you may **eat and drink at My table in My kingdom**, and **sit on thrones, ruling the
twelve tribes of Israel**."*

They wanted to know who was greatest. He gives all of them thrones.

The argument is not settled by demotion.

## The twelve

Twelve thrones, and one of the twelve has already gone out. Luke has recorded the betrayal in the
same paragraph.

## What follows immediately

*"Simon, Simon! Indeed, **Satan has asked for you, that he may sift you as wheat**. But I have
prayed for you, that your faith should not fail; and **when you have returned to Me, strengthen
your brethren**."* See the note on Luke 22:31–34.

*When*, not *if*.

## The sentence He answers with

*"**I am among you as the One who serves.**"*

The Greek is **ho diakonon** — the one waiting at table, which is precisely the role being argued
over.

*Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into His hands, and that He had come from God
and was going to God, **rose from supper and laid aside His garments, took a towel and girded
Himself**.*

**John** 13 records what **Luke** does not, at the same table on the same night. The credentials and
the towel are in one sentence.

*Who, being in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal with God, but **made Himself
of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant**… He humbled Himself and became obedient to the
point of death.*

## The moment He says it

*"**You are the ones who have stayed with Me through everything.**"*

Said hours before they scatter, in the same conversation as the prediction that they would, and to
men who are at that moment arguing about rank.

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that **while we were still sinners, Christ died for
us**.*

## What He grants

*"**And I bestow upon you a kingdom, just as My Father bestowed one upon Me.**"*

**Diatithemai** — the verb for making a covenant or a will. He is not promising them promotion; He
is settling an estate, mid-argument, on the people having the argument.

*And if children, then heirs — **heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ**.*

*"**Do not fear, little flock, for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom.**"*

> **The timing.** *You are the ones who have stayed with Me through everything.* Said hours before
> all of them scattered, and in the same paragraph as the prediction that they would.
