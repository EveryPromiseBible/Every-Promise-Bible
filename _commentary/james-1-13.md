James shuts a door, and he shuts it hard.

## What is forbidden

*When the pull toward evil comes, **nobody gets to say, "God is doing this to me."**"*

The Greek is *let no one say, I am tempted by God*.

He is not correcting a misunderstanding gently. He rules the sentence out of the
believer's mouth.

## Why it needed saying

Eleven verses earlier he told them to count it all joy when they land in trouble —
using the noun **peirasmos**. See the note on James 1:2.

Here he uses the verb from the same root and says God does not do it.

The reader who has just been told that trials are worth something might reasonably ask
whether God sends the pull toward evil as well. James answers before it is asked.

## Two things, one word family

Greek uses **peirazo** for both testing and enticing. English separates them into *trial*
and *temptation*; the Greek does not.

James separates them by argument instead. Circumstances that test faith come, and they
work endurance. Solicitation to evil is a different thing with a different source, and
he names the source in the next verse. See the note on James 1:14.

## The reason given

*Evil **has no hold on God whatsoever**.*

The Greek is **apeirastos** — untemptable, incapable of being solicited by evil. The word
appears once in the New Testament, here, and is rare in all Greek.

There is nothing in Him for it to fasten to.

Jesus says the same about the ruler of this world: *he has nothing in Me.* See the note
on John 14:28–31.

## The second half

*And **He pulls no one toward it**.*

Two claims: nothing pulls Him, and He pulls nobody.

James closes it from both sides.

## What this rules out

The oldest instinct in Scripture is to relocate the blame. Adam: *the woman whom You
gave to be with me.* The pointing runs past Eve and lands on God.

James is writing to people under real pressure — scattered, poor, dragged into court by
the rich — and the sentence he takes away from them is the one that would have been
easiest to say.

## What it does not say

He does not say the pressure is imaginary, or that suffering has no purpose, or that
God is absent from it.

The claim is narrow and total: God is not the one doing the enticing.

## What follows the whole paragraph

*Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and comes down from the Father of
lights, **with whom there is no variation or shadow of turning**.*

Four verses later, and it is his closing argument. The one who cannot be pulled toward
evil is also the one who does not change.

> **The shape of the paragraph.** James removes one explanation, supplies the true one
> in the next verse, and then says where the good things come from instead.
