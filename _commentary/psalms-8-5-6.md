Two verses in the middle of a psalm about the night sky.

## The question they answer

*When I consider Your heavens, the work of Your fingers, the moon and the stars, which You
have ordained — **what is man that You are mindful of him, and the son of man that You visit
him?**"*

David is looking up and asking why anybody down here registers.

## The answer

*Yet You made him **just a little lower than the angels**.*

The Hebrew is **me'at me'elohim** — a little lower than **Elohim**.

Which is genuinely difficult. The word normally means God. The Greek Old Testament renders
it *angels*, and Hebrews follows that reading.

Older English versions have *angels*; several modern ones have *God* or *the heavenly
beings*.

**A little** — the Hebrew can also mean *for a little while*, which is how Hebrews takes
it.

## The crowning

*And **crowned him with glory and honor**.*

The Hebrew is **kavod** and **hadar** — weight, and majesty.

Both words are used elsewhere of God and of kings. They are applied here to humanity as
such.

## The commission

*You gave him **charge over the works of Your hands**. You **laid everything beneath his
feet**.*

This is Genesis 1 restated: *let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds
of the air, and over the cattle, over all the earth.*

The psalm lists them in the next verse — sheep and oxen, beasts of the field, birds, fish,
and *whatever passes through the paths of the seas*.

## The problem

Nothing in the observable world looks like this. Man does not visibly have everything under
his feet.

## What Hebrews does with it

The writer quotes all of it and then says exactly that:

*For in that He put all in subjection under him, He left nothing that is not put under him.
**But now we do not yet see all things put under him**.*

He concedes the gap in the middle of the argument.

## Where he takes it

*But **we see Jesus**, who was made a little lower than the angels, for the suffering of
death crowned with glory and honour.*

The writer applies the psalm to Christ — and the phrase *a little lower* becomes *for a
little while lower*, which the Greek allows.

The commission given to humanity in Genesis, unfulfilled in observation, is read as
fulfilled in one man.

## Where Paul uses it

*For He has put all things under His feet.* — 1 Corinthians 15, about the reign that
continues until the last enemy. See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:23–28.

And Ephesians: *and He put all things under His feet, and gave Him to be head over all
things to the church.* See the note on Ephesians 1:20–23.

Three New Testament writers use this psalm, and all three read it messianically.

## What the psalm is bracketed by

The same line, opening and closing:

*O LORD, our Lord, **how excellent is Your name in all the earth**!*

## Who quotes verse 2

*Out of the mouth of babes and nursing infants You have ordained strength.*

Jesus quotes it in the temple, to the chief priests objecting to children shouting
*Hosanna to the Son of David*. See the note on Matthew 21:12–17.

> **What the psalm claims and what Hebrews concedes.** Everything under his feet — and the
> writer of Hebrews quotes it, admits nobody can see it, and points at one man.
