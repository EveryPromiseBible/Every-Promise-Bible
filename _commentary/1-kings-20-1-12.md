A siege, and a negotiation that shows exactly what conceding to a bully buys.

## The first demand

*"**Your silver and your gold are mine, and the best of your wives and children are mine.**"*

And Ahab's answer: *"**As you say, my lord the king. I and all I have belong to you.**"*

Total capitulation, in one sentence, before a shot is fired.

## The second

*"I told you to hand over your silver and gold, your wives and children. **But by this time
tomorrow I'll send my men to search your palace**… and they'll seize everything you treasure
and carry it off."*

The first demand was for delivery. The second is for access — his men will come in and go
through the house themselves and take whatever they want.

Ben-Hadad did not want the gold. He wanted to find out whether the door would open.

## What conceding bought

A larger demand, a day later.

*Neither **give place to the devil**.*

The Greek is **topos** — a foothold, a place to stand.

*Therefore **submit to God. Resist the devil and he will flee from you**.*

James puts the two halves in that order, and the second half is the one Ahab skipped.

## Where the line finally is

Not at his gold, and not at his wives. At the search.

*"I accepted your first demand, **but this one I cannot do**."*

Even the elders can see it: *"Don't listen to him. Don't give in."*

## The boast

*"May the gods deal with me, and worse, **if there's enough of Samaria left to give each of my
men a handful of dust**."*

And Ahab's reply, which is the best line he ever says: *"**The one putting on his armour
shouldn't boast like the one taking it off.**"*

## Where Ben-Hadad is when he hears it

*Ben-Hadad heard this while he and the kings were **drinking in their tents**.*

Said twice in the chapter. He gives the order to attack drunk, and he is drunk again when the
counter-attack comes. See the note on 1 Kings 20:13–21.

> **What the second demand was for.** Not the treasure — he had already been promised that.
> It was for the door to be opened. The concession did not satisfy the demand; it produced the
> next one.
