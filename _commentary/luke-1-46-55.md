A teenager in a hill-country house sings ten verses of Old Testament.

## Where it comes

Elizabeth has just called out *blessed are you among women*, and *how happy you are, because
you believed*. See the note on Luke 1:39–45.

Mary answers with this.

## The opening

*My whole soul **lifts up the Lord**, and my spirit is **dancing in God my Saviour**.*

The Greek is **megalunei** — magnifies, makes great. It is where the song's Latin name,
*Magnificat*, comes from.

**My Saviour** — she includes herself among those who need one.

## The reason

*Because **He noticed me — a servant girl, nobody of consequence**.*

The Greek is **tapeinosin** — low estate, humiliation.

She uses **doule** of herself — a female slave, the same word she used to the angel: *I am
the Lord's servant*. See the note on Luke 1:34–38.

## The prediction

*From now on, **every generation will call me blessed**.*

Said by a village girl in Nazareth, and it has been true for two thousand years.

## What the song is made of

Almost every line comes from the Old Testament. The closest model is Hannah's song, which
it follows in structure and content. See the note on 1 Samuel 2:1–10.

Two women, a thousand years apart, both told they would have a son, both singing about God
turning the order of things over.

Mary knows the Scriptures well enough to compose out of them.

## The turn

*He has **flexed the strength of His arm**.*

The Greek is *He has shown strength with His arm* — the standard Old Testament image for
the exodus.

## The six reversals

*He has **scattered the proud**, tangled in the schemes of their own hearts.*

*He has **pulled the powerful down off their thrones** and **lifted up the lowly**.*

*He has **filled the hungry with good things** and **sent the rich away with empty hands**.*

Three pairs, and each is a swap.

## The tense

Every verb is aorist — completed action.

Nothing has happened yet. She is a few weeks pregnant, no thrones have fallen, and the
child will not be born for months.

She sings about it as done.

## The last section

*He has **taken hold of Israel His servant**, remembering to be merciful, **just as He
promised our fathers long ago — to Abraham, and to his children, forever**.*

The song ends where the covenant started.

## What is not in it

Anything about herself after the first four lines. The personal note is brief and the rest
is national.

## Where she was

The verse before: *Mary got ready and went quickly up into the hill country.*

She had walked eighty miles to the one other person who would understand, and this is what
she says when she arrives.

## How long she stayed

*And Mary remained with her about three months, and returned to her house.*

## What waits at home

An unmarried pregnancy in a village of a few hundred people, and a betrothed husband
entitled to end it.

Matthew records what Joseph decided before the angel came to him: *not wanting to make her a
public example, was minded to put her away secretly*.

> **What she sings about.** Not the child. Six reversals, in the past tense, before any of
> them has happened — and the personal part is four lines about being noticed.
