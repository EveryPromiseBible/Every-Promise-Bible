A complaint quoted back to the people who made it, and the answer is a question about what they
already know.

## The complaint

*"**My way is hidden from the LORD, my cause is overlooked by my God.**"*

The Hebrew for *cause* is **mishpat** — a legal case, a judgment due. It is not vague neglect
being alleged. It is a file that has not been opened.

## Who is saying it

*O Jacob… O Israel.* Both names, as in Isaiah 43. See the note on Isaiah 43:1–4.

Written to exiles, and the sentence is a fair description of what their situation looked like.

## The answer

*Do you not know? Have you not heard?*

Not new information. Isaiah answers a complaint about being unseen by asking whether they have
forgotten something they were told.

*The LORD is the everlasting God, the Creator of the ends of the earth. **He does not grow tired,
He does not grow weary**. His understanding no one can search out.*

## The God who did grow tired

*Now Jacob's well was there. **Jesus therefore, being wearied from His journey, sat thus by the
well**.*

**John** 4, and the Greek is **kekopiakos** — worn out by labour. The One Isaiah says does not
grow weary sat down because He could not walk any further, and the first person to arrive was a
woman with a complaint about where God could be found.

*And He was asleep… **and they woke Him**, saying, "Teacher, do You not care that we are
perishing?"*

**Mark** 4. Somebody else saying *my cause is overlooked*, to a man who was tired enough to sleep
through a storm.

## What is said about the seeing

*"Are not two sparrows sold for a copper coin? And not one of them falls to the ground apart from
your Father's will. But **the very hairs of your head are all numbered**. Do not fear therefore;
you are of more value than many sparrows."*

*"For **your Father knows the things you have need of before you ask Him**."*

**Matthew** answers Isaiah's complaint at the smallest available scale. Isaiah replies with the
ends of the earth; **Jesus** replies with a bird and a headcount.

## What follows in the chapter

*He gives power to the weak, and to those who have no might He increases strength.* See the note
on Isaiah 40:29–31.

The answer to *my way is hidden* is not a defence of God's attention. It is an offer of strength
to the people who complained.

> **The exchange.** They say their case has not been looked at. He asks whether they have
> forgotten who He is — and then, without waiting for an answer, starts handing out strength to
> the ones who said it.
