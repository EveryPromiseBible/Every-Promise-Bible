title: A gentle opening turns sharp
updated: 2026-08-09

The first friend to speak takes six verses to get from sympathy to accusation.

## How he begins

*"Job, **may I say a word? Would it wound you if I tried?**"*

Careful, and genuinely so. He has sat on the ground for seven days without speaking, and he
asks permission before he starts.

## What he says next

*"**Think back: you were the one who steadied so many. When hands hung weak, you took them in
yours.** Your words lifted people who were stumbling; you put strength back into failing
knees."*

All of it true, and Job's own chapter 29 confirms every line. See the note on
Job 29:14–17.

## The turn

*"**And now the trouble has come to your own door, and you fall apart.**"*

The compliment was the run-up. Everything before this exists to make the contrast land.

## The two questions

*"**Wasn't your reverence for God your confidence? Wasn't your blameless life your hope?**"*

Read them slowly. Eliphaz is asking whether Job's confidence was in his own record — and
suggesting that if it had been sound, it would be holding.

That is a whole theology in two rhetorical questions, and it is the one the book spends forty
chapters dismantling.

## What the New Testament puts confidence in

*Having therefore, brethren, **boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus**… let us
draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith.*

Not a blameless life. **Hebrews** names the ground and it is somebody else's blood.

*For **we are the circumcision**, who worship God in the Spirit, rejoice in Christ Jesus, and
**have no confidence in the flesh**.*

**Paul** had the blameless record Eliphaz is appealing to — *concerning the righteousness which
is in the law, blameless* — and calls it loss.

## What God says about the speech

*"**You have not spoken of Me what is right**, as My servant Job has."* See the note on
Job 42:7–8.

The gentlest opening in the book, from the man who ends up needing seven bulls and seven rams
and Job's prayer to be forgiven for it.

> **The two questions he ends on.** *Wasn't your reverence your confidence? Wasn't your
> blameless life your hope?* He means them kindly. They are also exactly what the book is
> written to answer.
