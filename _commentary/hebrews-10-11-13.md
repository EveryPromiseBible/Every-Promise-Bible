The whole argument of the letter turns on a piece of furniture that was not there.

## The old arrangement

*Every priest **stands** ministering daily and offering **repeatedly the same
sacrifices**, which **can never take away sins**.*

Four words doing the work: stands, daily, repeatedly, never.

## What was missing from the tabernacle

There were no chairs in it.

The lampstand, the table of showbread, the altar of incense, the ark — the
specifications in Exodus are exhaustive, down to the tongs and the snuffdishes, and
no seat is mentioned anywhere.

A priest's work was never finished, so there was nothing to sit on.

## The contrast

*But this Man, after He had offered **one sacrifice for sins forever**, **sat down** at
the right hand of God.*

**One**, and **forever**, and then the posture.

The sitting is the proof. You sit when the job is done.

## What He is doing now

*From that time waiting till **His enemies are made His footstool**.*

Psalm 110, which Jesus had quoted at the Pharisees Himself: *the LORD said to my Lord,
sit at My right hand until I make Your enemies Your footstool.* See the note on
Matthew 22:41–46.

## The verse that follows

*For by **one offering He has perfected forever** those who are being sanctified.*

Two tenses in one sentence, and both are true at once. **Perfected forever** — done,
complete, permanent. **Being sanctified** — in progress.

The finished thing and the ongoing thing are not in competition.

## And what the Spirit witnesses

The writer quotes Jeremiah 31: *their sins and their lawless deeds **I will remember no
more**.*

Then his own conclusion: *now where there is remission of these, **there is no longer
an offering for sin**.*

If the debt is forgotten, there is nothing left to pay.

## What comes next in the chapter

*Therefore, brethren, having boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus… **let
us draw near**.* See the note on Hebrews 10:19–21.

The sitting down is what makes the drawing near possible.

## The comparison the letter keeps making

Standing priests, repeated sacrifices, and a room nobody could enter except one man
once a year. See the note on Hebrews 9:6–7.

Against one offering, one seat, and *let us draw near* addressed to everybody.

> **Where the same posture turns up.** *And He was received up into heaven, and **sat
> down** at the right hand of God.* Mark's last chapter. And in Revelation, John sees
> Him standing among the lampstands — seated at the throne, walking among the churches.
