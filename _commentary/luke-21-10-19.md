He lists the end of the world and then says something happens before all of it.

## Where this is said

On the temple mount, looking at the building. Someone had just remarked how it was *adorned
with beautiful stones and donations*, and He answered that not one would be left on
another.

Everything from here to the end of the chapter is the reply to *when will these things be?*

## The list

*"**Nation will rise up against nation**, and **kingdom against kingdom**."*

*"There will be **great earthquakes**, and **famines** and **plagues** in place after
place, and **terrible things**, and **great signs from the sky**."*

Luke has two items Matthew and Mark do not: plagues, and signs from heaven.

The Greek for *plagues* is **loimoi**, and it sits one letter away from **limoi**, famines
— the pair is nearly a pun in Greek.

## The interruption

*"**But before any of that**, they will lay hands on you."*

The Greek is *before all these things*. He has just described the sequence and now steps
back in front of it.

The persecution is not one of the signs. It is what precedes the signs.

## The venues

*"They will hand you over to **the synagogues** and put you **in prison**, and you will be
dragged in front of **kings and governors** because of My name."*

Four, and the book of Acts works through the list in order — the synagogue, the prison, and
then Paul before Felix, Festus and Agrippa.

## The turn

*"And that will be **your opening to bear witness**."*

The Greek is *it shall turn out to you for a testimony* — **apobesetai**, it will result
in.

The courtroom is called an opportunity. Not survived, not endured — used.

## The instruction

*"So **settle it in your hearts now** that you will **not rehearse your defense ahead of
time**."*

The Greek for *rehearse your defence* is **promeletan** — to premeditate, to prepare a
speech in advance. The root of *meditate*.

And *settle it in your hearts* is a legal word too: put it in your hearts, decide it now,
before the hearing.

The one preparation He asks for is the decision not to prepare.

## The reason

*"Because **I Myself will give you words and a wisdom** that none of your accusers will be
able to **stand against or argue down**."*

Matthew's version says the Spirit of your Father will speak. Luke's says *I Myself*.

The Greek for *argue down* is **antipein** — to contradict, to speak against. Luke uses the
same word of Stephen's opponents: *they were not able to resist the wisdom and the Spirit
by which he spoke.*

## The betrayal

*"You will be handed over **even by parents and brothers, by relatives and friends**, and
**some of you they will put to death**."*

Four categories, closing in from parents to friends.

## The two sentences that follow

*"**Everyone will hate you** because of My name. **And yet not a hair of your head will be
lost**."*

Side by side, three words apart from *some of you they will put to death*.

Both are true at once, and the second is not a promise about the body's safety — some of
them are killed in the previous line. It is a promise about what is finally lost, which is
nothing.

## The last line

*"By your **steady endurance** you will **gain your lives**."*

The Greek is **hupomone** — remaining under. And **ktesasthe**, to acquire, to gain
possession of.

## What comes after in Luke

Jerusalem surrounded by armies, and *let those who are in Judea flee to the mountains* —
which the church did, in AD 70. See the note on Luke 21:20–24.

Then the Son of Man coming in a cloud, and *look up and lift up your heads, because your
redemption draws near*.

> **What the hearing is for.** He names the synagogue, the prison and the governor's court,
> and then calls the whole sequence an opening — and tells them the only preparation is to
> decide now not to prepare.
