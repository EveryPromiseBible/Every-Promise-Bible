They ask why they failed, and the answer names the smallest measurable quantity of faith as
sufficient.

## Where He has come from

*When they came back to the crowd.*

Down from the mountain, six days after Peter's confession, having been transfigured in front of
three of them. See the note on Matthew 17:1–8.

Nine disciples stayed at the bottom, and this is what they were doing.

## The father

*A man approached Jesus and **knelt in front of Him**.*

## The son

*"He has **seizures and suffers terribly**. He has often **fallen into the fire, and often into the
water**."*

The Greek is **selēniazetai** — literally *moonstruck*, the ordinary word for epilepsy.

## The detail

Fire and water. The two ways a child alone can die in a village.

Mark adds the father's clause: *from childhood*.

## The complaint

*"I brought him to Your disciples, but **they could not heal him**."*

They had been given authority to do exactly this — *He gave them power over unclean spirits, to
cast them out*.

## The response

*"**You unbelieving and twisted generation**, how long must I be with you? How long must I bear with
you?"*

The Greek is **apistos kai diestrammenē** — faithless and distorted.

## Who is addressed

The generation, not the father and not only the nine.

The language echoes Moses' song: *a perverse and crooked generation*.

## The healing

*Then Jesus **spoke sharply to the demon**, and it came out of the boy, and he was **healed from that
very moment**.*

The Greek is **epetimēsen** — rebuked.

## The private question

*Later the disciples came to Jesus **privately** and asked, "**Why could we not drive it out**?"*

They had done it before, which is why the failure is a question.

## The answer

*"**Because of your unbelief**."*

The Greek is **dia tēn oligopistian humōn** — because of your little-faith.

**Oligopistia** — the noun. Matthew's word, used of the disciples in the boat and of anxiety about
clothing.

## The apparent contradiction

He diagnoses *little faith*, and then says a mustard seed's worth would have been enough.

## The resolution in the sentence

*"If you have faith **the size of a mustard seed**, you will say to this mountain, '**Move from here to
there**,' and it will move."*

The problem is not quantity. **Oligopistia** in Matthew is not a small amount of faith — it is faith
directed at the wrong thing.

## The seed

The smallest seed a Galilean farmer handled. See the note on Matthew 13:31–32.

The point of the image is that the amount is not the variable.

## The mountain

Standing at the foot of one, having just come down it.

## The clause

*"**Nothing will be impossible for you**."*

The Greek is **ouden adunatēsei humin**.

The angel used the same construction to Mary: *with God nothing will be impossible*.

## The last verse

*"But **this kind does not come out except by prayer and fasting**."*

The verse is absent from the earliest manuscripts of Matthew and present in Mark, and most modern
editions bracket it here.

## What it adds

If original, it does not contradict the mustard seed. It names where such faith is kept in working
order.

## What follows immediately

*"The Son of Man is about to be betrayed into the hands of men, and they will kill Him, and the
third day He will be raised up." **And they were exceedingly sorrowful**.*

And then the temple tax, and a coin in a fish's mouth. See the note on Matthew 17:24–27.

> **The measure.** He names their little faith as the cause and then says a mustard seed's worth
> would have done it. The variable in the sentence is not the size.
