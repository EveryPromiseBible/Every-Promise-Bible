Two tenses in one sentence, and the first one is present.

## The address

*Beloved.*

The Greek is **agapetoi** — loved ones. John's habitual name for his readers; he uses it
six times in this short letter.

## The tense

*We **are** sons of God **right now**.*

Present indicative. Not becoming, not hoping to be, not on probation.

The verse before it is the reason: *behold what manner of love the Father has bestowed on
us, that we should be called children of God!* — and John adds *and we are*.

## What is not settled

*What we are **going to be** has not come out into the open yet.*

The Greek is **oupo ephanerothe** — it has not yet been manifested, made visible.

The status is decided. The appearance is pending.

## The one thing known

*But **we know this much**: when He does come out into the open, **we will be like Him**.*

The same verb again — **phanerothe**, when He is manifested.

Two manifestations in two lines, and they are the same event. His showing is our showing.

## The mechanism

*We will be **looking straight at Him, at what He actually is**.*

The Greek is *we shall see Him as He is* — **kathos estin**.

The seeing is the cause of the likeness, not the reward for it. Paul says it the same way:
*we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being
transformed into the same image*. See the note on 2 Corinthians 3:16–18.

Looking is how the change happens, in both apostles.

## The hope

*And **everyone with that hope set on Him** makes himself pure, **the way He is pure**.*

The Greek for *purifies* is **hagnizei**, and the standard given is not a rule but a
person: **kathos ekeinos hagnos estin** — as He is pure.

The purity is measured against Him, and the one doing the purifying is the one who already
knows he is a son.

## The order

Sonship first, in verse 2. Purifying second, in verse 3, and only for those who have the
hope.

John never runs it the other way round.

## The other beholding

Moses came down from the mountain with a face he could not see himself, and had to be told
about. *The skin of his face shone while he talked with Him.*

He had been looking at something for forty days.

## What John says next

*Whoever commits sin also commits lawlessness… and you know that **He was manifested to
take away our sins**, and **in Him there is no sin**.*

The same verb a third time. Manifested — at the incarnation, to remove sin; and manifested
again at the end, so we can be looked at Him and be like Him.

## Where the letter opens

*That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which **we have seen with our
eyes**, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled.*

John begins the letter with what he saw, and gets to what we will see.

## Where the paragraph begins

The sonship in these verses does not start as an argument. It starts as astonishment, one sentence
earlier:

*Behold **what manner of love** the Father has bestowed on us, that we should be called children of
God!*

**Potapen** — an old word that originally asked *from what country?* It is the noise a person makes
when something arrives that has no local origin: what kind of love is this, and where on earth is it
from?

And John answers the question in the same letter: *In this is love… that He loved us and sent His
Son to be the propitiation for our sins.* The love has an address, and it is not here: it arrived in Jesus Christ.

Which is why *now we are children of God* can be stated as a settled fact in the present tense while
everything about the future is left open. The status did not come from us and does not depend on
what has not yet been revealed.

> **The order in two verses.** Sons now — that part is finished. Like Him later, and the
> way it happens is that we will be looking at Him.
