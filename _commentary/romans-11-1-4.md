Paul asks the hardest question of the letter and answers it with himself, then with a
prophet.

## The question

*Has God **pushed His people away**?*

The Greek is **aposato** — thrust away, repudiated.

Three chapters of argument about Israel's unbelief have led here, and Paul puts the
obvious conclusion as a question.

## The answer

*Certainly not.*

The Greek is **me genoito** — Paul's strongest negative, which he uses ten times in this
letter. *May it never be.*

## The first evidence

*I am an Israelite myself, from the seed of Abraham, **from the tribe of Benjamin**.*

He is the exhibit. If God had cast off Israel, Paul would not be writing the letter.

**Benjamin** — the smallest tribe, and the one Saul the king came from. Paul had been
named after him.

## The ground

*Whom **He knew beforehand**.*

The Greek is **proegno** — foreknew. Paul has used it in chapter 8 of individuals; here
it is of a nation.

## The second witness

*Do you not know what the Scripture says **in the passage about Elijah**?*

The Greek is *in Elijah* — the rabbinic way of citing a section by its subject, the way
one might say *in the bush* for Exodus 3.

## The complaint

*How **he pleads with God against Israel**.*

The Greek is **entunchanei kata** — intercedes **against**.

The word for intercession, pointed the wrong way. That is Paul's own description of what
Elijah is doing.

## What Elijah said

*"Lord, **they have killed Your prophets and torn down Your altars, and I am the only one
left**, and they are after my life."*

He said it twice at Horeb, word for word, before and after the wind, the earthquake, the
fire and the whisper. See the note on 1 Kings 19:9–18.

Every fact in it is true except the last.

## The reply

*But **what was God's answer to him?**"*

The Greek is **chrematismos** — a divine response, an oracle. It occurs once in the New
Testament.

Paul asks what the answer was, which means he expects the reader to have noticed that one
was given.

## The seven thousand

*"**I have kept for Myself seven thousand men** who have not bowed the knee to Baal."*

The Greek is *I have left for Myself* — the reserving is God's act, stated in the first
person.

They had been there the whole time, through the drought, through Jezebel's purge, while
Elijah was on Carmel and while he was under the broom tree.

Obadiah's hundred prophets in two caves were among them. See the note on
1 Kings 18:1–6.

## What Paul does with it next

*In the same way, at this present time also, there is **a remnant chosen by grace**.* See
the note on Romans 11:5–6.

He takes the number and turns it into a doctrine.

> **What Paul notices about the passage.** Not that Elijah was wrong about the facts. That
> the direction of his intercession was *against* — and that God answered by naming seven
> thousand men Elijah had never met.
