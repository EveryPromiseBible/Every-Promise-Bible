Chapter 13 has just finished — faith, hope and love, and the greatest of these is
love. Then, without a break, this: *pursue love.*

## Two different verbs

Paul uses one word for love and a different one for the gifts, and the pair is
deliberate:

- **pursue** love — *dioko*, to chase, to hunt down, to run after. The word used
  of persecutors pursuing their target.
- **desire earnestly** the spiritual gifts — a strong word for wanting, but not
  the same as running someone down.

Love is the thing you chase. The gifts are things you want. Nobody reading this
in Corinth could have missed which one Paul put in the hunting verb.

## The measure he applies

Everything after this is judged by one question: does it build up the church?

- the one who speaks in a tongue *builds himself up*
- the one who prophesies *builds up the church*

Paul is not dismissive — he says he wishes they all spoke in tongues, and
elsewhere that he does so more than any of them. His argument is about the
gathering: in a room full of people, the thing that everyone can understand does
more good than the thing only one person understands.

> **What "prophesy" means here.** Paul defines it in the passage rather than
> leaving it to be assumed: speaking *to build them up, to urge them on, and to
> comfort them*. That is a wider and more ordinary thing than predicting the
> future, and all three of its stated purposes are aimed at other people.
