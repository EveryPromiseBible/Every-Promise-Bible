The only miracle in all four Gospels, and John keeps a detail the others share
and nobody quite explains.

## "As much as they wanted"

Not a ration. John says the fish were handed out *as much as they wanted*, and
that everyone *ate their fill*.

The verb for eating their fill is a strong one — it was used of feeding animals
to satisfaction. Five thousand men, plus the women and children the other
accounts mention, all eating until they stopped wanting more.

## The instruction afterwards

*Gather up the pieces that are left over, so that nothing is wasted.*

This is the sentence worth stopping on. The man who has just produced an
unlimited supply out of five loaves tells His disciples to pick up the crumbs.

Abundance and waste are treated as different things. The miracle does not make
the fragments unimportant.

## Twelve baskets

They end with more bread than they started with, and the number is exact —
twelve, one for each disciple. The men who had said *what are five loaves among
so many* each end the day carrying a full basket.

John does not comment on that either. He just records the number.

## What the leftovers were for

Twelve baskets, and twelve men who had said it could not be done.

*"**How many loaves do you have? Go and see.**"* Each of them carries away more than they started
with, and the arithmetic is a rebuke and a kindness at once.

*And **my God shall supply all your need according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus**.*

*And God is able to make all grace abound toward you, that you, **always having all sufficiency in
all things**, may have an abundance for every good work.*

## What He said about it the next day

*"**Most assuredly, I say to you, you seek Me, not because you saw the signs, but because you ate of
the loaves and were filled.**"*

*"**I am the bread of life. He who comes to Me shall never hunger**, and he who believes in Me shall
never thirst."*

**John** 6 is the only place a miracle is followed by a whole chapter explaining it, and the
explanation is a claim about His own person.

*"**And the bread that I shall give is My flesh, which I shall give for the life of the world.**"*

The bread multiplied on a hillside is a sign, and the thing signified is broken later in the same
gospel.

## As much as they wanted

**Hoson ethelon** — as much as they were wanting.

*"**The one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out.**"*

*And let him who thirsts come. Whoever desires, let him **take the water of life freely**.*

> **What happens next.** The crowd tries to make Him king by force, and He
> withdraws to a mountain alone. The following morning He tells them they are
> looking for Him because they ate the loaves, and then calls Himself the bread
> of life — a claim so hard that most of them stop following Him by the end of the
> chapter. The feeding is the beginning of a much longer argument.
