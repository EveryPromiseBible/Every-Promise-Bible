A summary paragraph, and it is one of the largest healing scenes in the Gospels.

## Where He had come from

*Jesus **moved on from there**.*

From Tyre and Sidon — Gentile country — where the Canaanite woman had just argued Him into
healing her daughter with the line about crumbs under the table.

*And walked along the Sea of Galilee. Then He **went up on a mountainside and sat down**.*

He sits. The posture of a teacher, and He does no teaching in these three verses.

## What arrived

*Great crowds came to Him, bringing **the lame, the blind, the crippled, those who could not
speak, and many others**.*

Four categories named and a fifth left open.

The Greek for *crippled* is **kullous** — maimed, mutilated. A limb that is not there or not
usable.

## What they did with them

*And they **laid them at His feet**.*

The Greek is **erripsan** — flung, threw down. Not placed carefully.

A whole day of carrying stretchers up a hill, and the last action in the sentence is to
put the person down and step back.

Nobody in the paragraph says anything. No request is recorded.

## The three words

*And **He healed them**.*

That is the entire account of the healing. Matthew gives four verbs to the carrying and
three words to the cure.

## What the crowd saw

*The people were **astonished** when they saw **those who could not speak now speaking, the
crippled made whole, the lame walking, and the blind seeing**.*

The list is repeated in the same order it arrived in, reversed item by item.

**The crippled made whole** — the Greek is **hugieis**, sound, healthy. For a maimed limb,
which is the one item on the list that requires something to be there that was not.

## Who they praised

*And they gave glory to **the God of Israel**.*

Matthew's phrasing, and it is the phrasing of outsiders. He has just spent a chapter in
Gentile territory, and the Decapolis was on this side of the lake.

People who would not normally say *our God* say *the God of Israel*.

## What happens next

Three days on that mountainside, and then: *I have compassion on the multitude, because they
have now continued with Me three days and have nothing to eat.*

Seven loaves, a few small fish, four thousand men besides women and children, and seven
baskets left over.

The healing runs straight into a meal on the same hill.

## What Isaiah had said would happen on a mountain

*And on this mountain the LORD of hosts will make for all peoples **a feast**.* See the note
on Isaiah 25:6–9.

Rich food, aged wine, and *for all peoples* — and the crowd on this hill glorifies the God
of Israel from outside it.

## The other summary

*And His fame went throughout all Syria; and they brought to Him all sick people who were
afflicted with various diseases and torments… **and He healed them**.*

Matthew writes the same sentence in chapter 4. It bookends the ministry.

> **What the paragraph records.** People carried, dropped and left, and a sentence with no
> conditions in it. Matthew spends his words on the queue and three on the outcome.
