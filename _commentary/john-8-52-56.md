An argument about Abraham, and the last line moves it two thousand years.

## The accusation

*"**Now we are sure You have a demon**. Abraham died, and so did the prophets, and You say, 'If
anyone keeps My word, he will never taste death.'"*

They had said it once already in the chapter — *are we not right in saying that You are a
Samaritan and have a demon?*

The second time it is presented as settled: *now we are sure*.

## What they heard

*He will never taste death.*

He had said *if anyone keeps My word he shall never **see** death*, and they repeat it as
*taste*.

They take it as a claim about dying, which is the only category available.

## The question

*"**Are You greater than our father Abraham?** He died, and the prophets died. **Who do You make
Yourself out to be?**"*

The Greek is **tina seauton poieis** — whom do You make Yourself.

The same question the Samaritan woman asked at the well: *are You greater than our father
Jacob?* See the note on John 4:10–15.

John records both, and both are answered by something larger than the comparison.

## The answer about glory

*"**If I glorify Myself, My glory means nothing**. It is My Father who glorifies Me — **the very One
you call your God**."*

He does not answer the *greater than Abraham* question directly.

## The charge back

*"**You have never known Him, but I know Him**."*

The Greek uses two verbs — **egnokate** for what they have not done, and **oida** for what He
does.

*"If I were to say that I do not know Him, **I would be a liar like you**."*

The sharpest sentence in the chapter, and it is a claim about honesty: He will not deny the
knowledge to defuse the argument.

## The turn

*"**Your father Abraham rejoiced that he would see My day. He saw it, and he was glad**."*

The Greek is **egalliasato** — leaped for joy, exulted. The word Mary uses: *my spirit has
rejoiced in God my Saviour*.

## The two verbs

*Rejoiced that he **would see**… **He saw it**, and he was glad.*

Anticipation, then sight, then gladness. Three stages in one sentence.

## When

The text does not say.

Genesis records Abraham laughing — *then Abraham fell on his face and laughed* — when told Sarah
would have a son.

And on the mountain: *God will provide for Himself the lamb*, and then a ram in a thicket, and
the place named *the LORD will provide*. See the note on Genesis 22:13–14.

*On the mountain of the LORD it will be seen*, said as a proverb about something still future.

## Their reply

*"**You are not yet fifty years old, and have You seen Abraham?**"*

They hear a claim about age, which is the smaller misunderstanding of the two.

## The answer

*"Most assuredly, I say to you, **before Abraham was, I AM**."* See the note on
John 8:57–59.

Not *I was*. **Ego eimi** — the present tense, and the name from the bush.

*Then they took up stones to throw at Him.*

They understood that one.

## What Hebrews says about the same seeing

*These all died in faith, **not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off** were
assured of them, embraced them.* See the note on Hebrews 11:13–16.

The verb is **idontes** — having seen.

## What the chapter opened with

*"**I am the light of the world**. He who follows Me shall not walk in darkness."*

And the argument runs from there to a claim about Abraham and a handful of stones.

> **The three stages.** *Rejoiced that he would see… He saw it… and he was glad.* Anticipation,
> sight, gladness — said of a man dead two thousand years.
