The aim is that they will not sin. The provision is for the case where they do.

## The address

*My **little children**.*

The Greek is **teknia mou** — my little born-ones. From **teknon**, child, in the diminutive.

John uses it seven times in the letter. He is an old man writing to people he considers his own.

## The purpose

*I am writing this to you **so that you will not sin**.*

The Greek is **hina mē hamartēte** — aorist subjunctive.

The letter states its aim before it states its provision. The order matters.

## What the previous chapter was doing

*If we say we have no sin in us, we are lying to ourselves.* See the note on 1 John 1:8.

And *if we own up to our sins, He keeps His word and He does the just thing.* See the note on
1 John 1:9.

John has just spent a paragraph refusing to let anyone claim sinlessness — and immediately says he
writes so they will not sin.

## The second half

*And **if anyone does sin** — we have someone speaking for us in front of the Father.*

The Greek is **kai ean tis hamartē**.

Not *when you fall away*. A third-person conditional, handled without alarm.

## The word

*Someone **speaking for us in front of the Father**.*

The Greek is **paraklēton pros ton Patera** — an advocate with the Father.

**Paraklētos** — one called alongside. In legal usage, counsel for the defence.

## Where else the word appears

Four times in John's Gospel, all of the Holy Spirit: *I will pray the Father, and He will give you
**another Helper***. See the note on John 14:15–17.

**Allon paraklēton** — another of the same kind. Which implies a first one.

This verse names Him. One Advocate is with us; one is with the Father.

## The preposition

**Pros ton Patera** — with, toward, face-to-face with the Father.

The same construction as the Gospel's opening: *the Word was **with God***.

## Who the advocate is

*Jesus Christ, **the righteous One**.*

The Greek is **dikaion**.

The same adjective used of the Father in the previous verse — *He is faithful and **just***.

Counsel and judge share the description.

## What He argues

Not the client's record.

*And He Himself is the propitiation for our sins.* See the note on 1 John 2:2.

The defence is His own blood, not the defendant's conduct.

## What the accuser does

*The accuser of our brethren, who accused them before our God day and night, has been cast down.*
See the note on Revelation 12:10–11.

*And they overcame him **by the blood of the Lamb**.*

Two voices before the Father. They are not making the same kind of case.

## Where He is while doing it

*He always lives to make intercession for them.* See the note on Hebrews 7:24–25.

The Greek is **pantote zōn** — always living.

## What John says next about the writing

*I am writing to you, little children, **because for the sake of His name your sins are forgiven
you**.* See the note on 1 John 2:12.

The Greek is **apheōntai** — perfect tense. A settled state, given as the reason for the letter,
not its goal.

> **The other one.** *Another Helper*, Jesus called the Spirit — **allon paraklēton**, another of the
> same kind. This verse names the first: He is with the Father, and He is the righteous One.
