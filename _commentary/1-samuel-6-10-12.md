The Philistines design an experiment, and the experiment works.

## Why they are doing it

They had captured the ark at Aphek, and it had been a disaster for them.

Dagon face down twice, the second time with head and hands broken off on the threshold. Tumours
in Ashdod, then Gath, then Ekron, and a plague of rats.

Seven months of it, and their priests and diviners are asked what to do.

## The test they design

*"Take **two cows that have calved, on which there has never come a yoke**, and hitch them to
the cart, and **take their calves home, away from them**."*

Two variables loaded against the result.

Milk cows with unweaned calves will not leave them. Untrained cattle will not pull straight.

*"And watch: **if it goes up the road to its own territory, to Beth-shemesh, then He has done us
this great evil. But if not**, then we shall know that it is not His hand that struck us; it
happened to us by chance."*

They state the null hypothesis before running it.

## The cart

*They took two cows that had calved and **hitched them to the cart**, and **penned up their
calves**.*

A new cart, which was the Philistines' own idea and the only method available to them.

Israel copies it later, with a man dead as a result. See the note on
2 Samuel 6:6–8.

## The cargo

*The ark of the LORD… along with the chest holding **the gold rats and the models of the
tumors**.*

Five golden tumours and five golden rats, one for each Philistine city.

A guilt offering, designed by pagan priests, on the advice that *perhaps He will lighten His
hand from you*.

## What happened

*Then the cows **went straight up the road toward Beth-shemesh, keeping to the highway, lowing
all the way, not turning to the right or the left**.*

The Hebrew is *they turned not aside to the right hand or to the left*.

**Lowing all the way** — the detail that makes it. They are calling for their calves the entire
journey and walking in the opposite direction.

Every instinct is pulling one way and they go the other.

## The escort

*The Philistine rulers followed them **as far as the border of Beth-shemesh**.*

The five lords walk behind their own experiment to the frontier and then stop.

They watch the result and go home.

## Where it arrives

Beth-shemesh — *house of the sun* — a Levitical town, the nearest Israelite settlement across
the valley.

*Now they of Beth-shemesh were reaping their wheat harvest in the valley; and they lifted their
eyes and saw the ark, and rejoiced to see it.*

The cows are sacrificed on the spot, using the wood of the cart.

## What happens next

Men of Beth-shemesh look into the ark, and there is a slaughter. See the note on
1 Samuel 6:19–21.

*"**Who is able to stand before this holy LORD God?** And to whom shall it go up from us?"*

The ark comes home and the town it comes home to asks for it to be taken away.

## Where it goes

Kirjath Jearim, to the house of Abinadab, for twenty years. See the note on
1 Samuel 7:2–4.

## What the Philistines proved

Their own test. Untrained cattle, separated from their calves, walking a straight road to the
border of another country without a driver.

They set out to find whether it was chance, and they got their answer.

> **The detail that carries it.** *Lowing all the way.* The cows call for their calves for the
> whole journey and do not turn round — and five Philistine lords walk behind to see if they
> will.
