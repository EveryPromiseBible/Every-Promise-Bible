The most quoted line in the psalm, and it is usually read as being addressed to the
wrong people.

## The verse before it

*He makes wars cease to the end of the earth; **He breaks the bow and cuts the spear
in two**; He burns the chariot in the fire.*

That is the immediate context. Weapons being destroyed.

## The command

*"**Be still, and know that I am God.**"*

The Hebrew is **raphah** — to let go, to sink down, to drop, to cease. It is not
primarily about quietness.

It is used of hands going slack, and of abandoning an effort.

## Who is being told

Read it with verse 9 attached and the addressee changes.

He has just broken the bow and the spear and burned the chariot, and then says
**stop**.

The command is addressed to the nations who were making the war — *the nations raged,
the kingdoms were moved* in verse 6. It is a cease-fire order shouted at combatants.

That does not make it useless to an anxious reader. It means the calm being offered
is the calm of somebody who has watched the weapons being broken, not the calm of
somebody being told to relax.

## What follows

*"**I will be lifted high among the nations. I will be lifted high in the earth.**"*

Twice, and both are His own intention rather than anything anybody does for Him.

## The refrain

*The LORD of hosts is with us. **The God of Jacob is our high tower.**"*

The same two lines close the first half of the psalm as well.

**LORD of hosts** — Yahweh Sabaoth, the God of armies, in a psalm about wars ceasing.

**The God of Jacob** — not the God of Abraham, the friend of God, or Israel the
prince. Jacob: the supplanter, the man who cheated his brother and wrestled all
night.

## What the psalm has said

*God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore we will
not fear, **even though the earth be removed**, and though the mountains be carried
into the midst of the sea.* See the note on Psalms 46:1–3.

And in the middle: *there is a river whose streams shall make glad the city of God…
**God is in the midst of her, she shall not be moved**.*

Roaring seas and toppling mountains in verse 3, and a quiet river in verse 4.

## Selah

The psalm pauses three times, and one of them is right here at the end.

> **What Luther made of it.** *A Mighty Fortress Is Our God* is this psalm, written in
> the 1520s during plague and upheaval. Psalm 46 has a long record of being reached
> for when things are moving.
