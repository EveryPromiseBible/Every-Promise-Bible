Three examples, and the middle one is the strangest paragraph in the New
Testament.

## "You know all of this already"

*You know all of this already, but **I am going to walk you back through it**.*

Jude's method throughout the letter. He is not informing them; he is reminding
them, and he says so.

## Egypt

*The Lord brought a whole people up out of Egypt and rescued them — **and afterward
He destroyed the ones among them who would not believe**.*

Being brought out was not the end of the matter. The generation that walked through
the sea is the generation that fell in the wilderness.

Paul makes the same point in 1 Corinthians 10, from the same history.

## The angels

*And there were angels who **would not stay in the place that was theirs; they
walked out of their own home**.*

Two clauses, and both are about leaving a position.

The Greek behind *the place that was theirs* is **arche** — their rule, their
domain, their principality. And *their own home* is **oiketerion**, a dwelling —
the word Paul uses in 2 Corinthians 5 of the body as a habitation.

Something was abandoned that had been assigned to them.

## Where they are

*He has them now in **chains that will never break, down in the dark**, and He is
**keeping them there for the judgement of the great day**.*

Present tense. They are not free and not yet sentenced — held.

Peter says it in almost the same terms: *He handed them over to chains and to the
dark, and there they are being kept until the judgement.* See the note on
2 Peter 2:4–6.

## "Sodom and Gomorrah did the same"

*Sodom and Gomorrah **did the same**, and so did the towns around them. They handed
themselves over to sexual sin and **went after flesh that was not theirs**.*

That connective is what makes the paragraph what it is. Jude says the cities did
the same **as the angels** — and the phrase he uses of the cities is *strange
flesh*, flesh of a different kind.

Genesis 19 records what the men of Sodom demanded, and who the visitors actually
were.

Whatever Jude means, he means the two cases to be read together, and he has put them
in that order deliberately.

## The exhibit

*They are **set out in plain sight as an example**.*

The Greek is **deigma** — a specimen, something displayed.

## Why Jude is doing this

The letter's purpose is stated in verse 3: *contend earnestly for the faith which
was once for all delivered to the saints*, because *certain men have crept in
unnoticed.*

The three examples are all cases of something that was inside a position and left
it. That is the pattern he wants his readers to see.

> **How the letter ends.** After all of that — chains, darkness, fire — the last two
> verses are the warmest doxology in Scripture: *now to Him who is able to keep you
> from stumbling, and to present you faultless before the presence of His glory with
> exceeding joy.* Kept, and presented faultless. Jude spends a page on people who
> did not stay, and closes on the One who keeps — *to God our Saviour, who alone is wise, be
> glory and majesty, dominion and power, both now and forever*, and Jude gets there **through
> Jesus Christ our Lord**.
