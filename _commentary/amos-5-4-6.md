title: "Seek Me and live"

Three words in the middle of a funeral lament, and they are the only imperative in the book that
is not a warning.

## Where it sits

The chapter opens *hear this word, this lament I take up over you: fallen is Virgin Israel, never
to rise again.*

A **qinah** — a funeral dirge, with the limping metre the Hebrew used for grief. Amos sings the
nation's funeral while it is still standing.

And then, in the middle of it: *Seek Me and live.*

## The two words

**Dirshuni vichyu** — seek Me, and live.

No object of the seeking is named except the pronoun. Not seek forgiveness, not seek the covenant,
not seek righteousness.

*"**You search the Scriptures**, for in them you think you have eternal life; and these are they
which testify of Me. **But you are not willing to come to Me that you may have life.**"*

**John** 5, and it is the same distinction Amos draws — between the places and the Person.

## The three places

*"Do not go looking to **Bethel**. Do not journey to **Gilgal**. Do not cross over to **Beersheba**."*

Every one of them is a real sanctuary with a genuine history.

**Bethel** is where Jacob saw the ladder and said *surely the LORD is in this place.* **Gilgal** is
where Israel first camped in the land and the reproach of Egypt was rolled away. **Beersheba** is
where Abraham planted a tamarisk and called on the name of the LORD.

Amos does not tell them to avoid pagan shrines. He tells them to stop going to the three places
where God had actually met their ancestors.

## Why

Because the meeting was never in the place.

*"Woman, believe Me, **the hour is coming when you will neither on this mountain, nor in Jerusalem,
worship the Father**… the true worshippers will worship the Father in spirit and truth."*

**John** 4, to a woman who had raised exactly this question about which site was the right one.

## The repetition

*"**Seek the LORD and live**, or He will sweep like fire through the house of Joseph."*

Said twice in three verses, and again at verse 14: *seek good and not evil, that you may live.*

## What the seeking finds

*So that they should seek the Lord, in the hope that they might grope for Him and find Him, **though
He is not far from each one of us**.*

*"**Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find**; knock, and it will be opened to
you."*

*"For **the Son of Man has come to seek and to save that which was lost**."*

**Luke** 19, in the house of a tax collector who had climbed a tree to get a look. The command in
Amos is answered by somebody who came looking first.

## The verse in between

*"He is the One who made **the Pleiades and Orion**, who turns deep darkness into morning."*

Amos puts the constellations in the middle of the appeal. The one being sought is named by what He
made, and the one thing said about His work is that He turns dark into morning.

> **What is ruled out.** Not idolatry — three shrines with impeccable pedigrees, where Jacob,
> Joshua and Abraham had each met God. Amos says go past all of them: *seek Me*.
