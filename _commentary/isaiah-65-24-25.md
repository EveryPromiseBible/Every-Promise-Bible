Two clauses about timing, and both of them run ahead of the request.

## The first

*"**Before they call, I will answer.**"*

The answer precedes the asking. Not *as soon as*, and not *while* — before.

## The second

*"**While they are still speaking, I will hear.**"*

The Hebrew is that He hears mid-sentence. There is no interval between the request
leaving the mouth and its arriving.

The two lines together remove any lag at either end.

## Where the same thing happened

Daniel, three chapters into his own vision: *"At the beginning of your supplications
the command went out, and I have come to tell you… for you are greatly beloved."*

Gabriel says the order was issued when Daniel started praying, and the travel time
accounts for the rest.

And Abraham's servant at the well: *before he had finished speaking*, Rebekah came
out. See the note on Genesis 24:12–14.

## The animals

*"**The wolf and the lamb will feed together**, and **the lion will eat straw like the
ox**."*

The predator and the prey at the same trough, and the carnivore on a vegetarian
diet.

Isaiah 11 has the same picture at more length — the wolf with the lamb, the leopard
with the young goat, a child leading them, and an infant playing at a cobra's hole.
See the note on Isaiah 11:1–3.

## The one exception

*"**But dust will be the serpent's food.**"*

Everything else in the picture is reconciled. One creature is not.

Genesis 3: *on your belly you shall go, and **you shall eat dust** all the days of
your life.* The sentence in the garden is the one thing this passage leaves standing.

## The result

*"**They will neither harm nor destroy** on all My holy mountain."*

The Hebrew is that they will do no evil and no ruin.

## What the chapter is

Isaiah 65 opens with *I was sought by those who did not ask for Me; I was found by
those who did not seek Me* — which Paul quotes in Romans 10 about the Gentiles.

And in the middle of it: *behold, **I create new heavens and a new earth**, and the
former shall not be remembered.*

Revelation 21 opens with the same sentence.

## What is also promised in it

*"They shall not build and another inhabit; they shall not plant and another eat…
**they shall not labour in vain**, nor bring forth children for trouble."*

The same reversal Amos and Psalm 128 describe — a man eating what he grew. See the
note on Amos 9:13–15.

> **What is said about the length of a life.** *No more shall an infant from there live
> but a few days, nor an old man who has not fulfilled his days… **as the days of a
> tree, so shall be the days of My people**.*
