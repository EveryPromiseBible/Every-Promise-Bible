Two dangers named, and both of them are the kind you do not see coming.

## The snare

*He will lift you free of **the hidden snare**.*

The Hebrew is *the snare of the fowler* — a bird-catcher's trap, laid on the ground
and covered, with bait on top.

A bird walks into it because nothing about it looks like a trap. That is the whole
design.

## The sickness

*Free of **the sickness that stalks in silence**.*

The Hebrew is **deber** — pestilence, plague. The older rendering is *the noisome
pestilence*.

Both dangers in the verse share one feature: they are concealed. A snare you cannot
see and a sickness you cannot hear.

## The feathers

*He will **cover you with His feathers**, and **under His wings** you will find your
hiding place.*

The image is a bird over a nest. Not a fortress or a shield — the softest covering
available.

Deuteronomy uses it of the exodus: *as an eagle stirs up its nest, hovers over its
young, spreading out its wings, taking them up, carrying them on its wings.*

Boaz says it to Ruth: *the LORD repay your work, and a full reward be given you by
the LORD God of Israel, **under whose wings you have come for refuge**.*

And Jesus, over Jerusalem: *how often I wanted to gather your children together, **as
a hen gathers her chicks under her wings**, but you were not willing.* See the note on
Matthew 23:37–39.

A hen is not a fighter. Her one method is to open her wings and stay put over what is
underneath her.

## The turn

*His faithfulness is **a shield around you**, a wall you can lean your whole life
against.*

The Hebrew for *shield* here is **tsinnah** — the large body shield, the one that
covered a soldier entirely, as against the small round buckler.

And the image changes without warning. Feathers in one line and armour in the next.

## What the shield is made of

*His **faithfulness**.*

Not His power, and not your faith. The Hebrew is **emeth** — truth, reliability,
steadiness. The root behind *amen*.

## Where the verses sit

Immediately after the opening two, which name the condition: *whoever makes a home in
the secret shelter of the Most High.* See the note on Psalms 91:1–2.

Then these — the snare, the plague, the feathers, the shield — and then *a thousand may
fall at your side.* See the note on Psalms 91:5–8.

> **What God says at the end of the psalm.** The last three verses switch to first
> person: *because he has set his love upon Me, therefore I will deliver him… **I will
> be with him in trouble**.* In it, not instead of it. See the note on
> Psalms 91:14–16.
