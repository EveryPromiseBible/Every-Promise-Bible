Four men, two boats, and one sentence each.

## Where He is

*Walking beside the **Sea of Galilee**.*

He has just moved: *leaving Nazareth, He came and dwelt in Capernaum, which is by the sea*.

Matthew attaches a prophecy to the move — *Galilee of the Gentiles: the people who sat in
darkness have seen a great light*.

## The first two

*Two brothers, **Simon who is called Peter and his brother Andrew**, throwing a net into the
water.*

The Greek is **amphiblestron** — a circular casting net, thrown by hand from the shore or a
small boat.

*For they were fishermen.*

Matthew states it as the reason for what they were doing, and it becomes the reason for what
He says next.

## The call

*"**Come, follow Me**," He said to them, "**and I will make you fishers of people**."*

The Greek is **deute opiso mou** — come after Me.

**Halieis anthropon** — fishers of men.

He does not tell them to stop being what they are. He takes the trade and changes what it is
aimed at.

## The one thing He undertakes

*I will make you.*

The Greek is **poieso** — I will make, I will do.

The only promise in the sentence, and He is the one doing the work.

## The response

*They **left their nets right then** and followed Him.*

The Greek is **eutheos** — immediately.

## The second pair

*James son of Zebedee and his brother John, **in a boat with their father Zebedee, mending
their nets**.*

The Greek is **katartizontas** — repairing, putting in order. The same verb Paul uses of
restoring somebody caught in a fault.

One pair is fishing; the other is doing maintenance.

## What they left

*And at once they left **the boat and their father** and followed Him.*

Matthew names both. The equipment and the man.

Zebedee had hired servants, Mark notes — so it is a business rather than subsistence, and the
two sons walking off leave him short-handed.

## What John's Gospel adds

They had met Him before. Andrew was one of the Baptist's disciples and had already brought his
brother: *we have found the Messiah*.

Luke adds the night of fishing and the sinking boats, and *depart from me, for I am a sinful
man*. See the note on Luke 5:8–11.

Matthew tells it flat, without the back-story, because he is writing about the calling rather
than the men.

## What fishermen were

Not the bottom of the social order. A working trade with boats, staff, partners and tax
obligations.

They are not scholars. Acts records the council's assessment: *when they saw the boldness of
Peter and John, and perceived that they were **uneducated and untrained men**, they marvelled.
And they realised that they had been with Jesus.*

## Where the nets turn up again

*Fishermen will stand along the shore… there will be **places for spreading nets**.* See the
note on Ezekiel 47:7–10.

Ezekiel's vision has men drying nets on the shore of a lake that had never held a fish.

## Where they end up

*And after that, He was seen by James, then by all the apostles.*

Peter preaching to three thousand at Pentecost. James executed by Herod, the first apostle to
die. John writing a Gospel, three letters and Revelation.

And Andrew, who is mostly recorded bringing people to Him — his brother, the boy with the
loaves, and the Greeks who asked to see Jesus.

> **The one promise in the sentence.** *I will make you.* He does not ask them to become
> anything; He takes what they already do and says He will do the making.
