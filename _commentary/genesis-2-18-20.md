Seven times in Genesis 1 something is good. Here, for the first time, something is not.

## The verdict

*"**It is not good for the man to be alone**."*

The Hebrew is **lo tov**.

Nothing has gone wrong. There is no sin in the account yet, no serpent, no fruit eaten.

The first deficiency named in Scripture is loneliness in a perfect garden.

## The remedy

*"I will make **a companion who is just right for him**."*

The Hebrew is **ezer kenegdo**.

**Ezer** — a help, a helper. The word is used about twenty times in the Hebrew Bible, and
most of the others are about God: *the LORD is my helper*, *our help is in the name of the
LORD*. It is not a word for a subordinate.

**Kenegdo** — corresponding to him, opposite him. Facing him.

A help that matches, standing across from him.

## The interruption

*So out of the ground the LORD God **formed every animal of the field and every bird of the
sky**.*

The Hebrew is **yatsar** — the potter's verb, the same one used of the man two verses
earlier. See the note on Genesis 2:4–7.

Made of the same material, by the same method.

## The parade

*And **He brought them to the man to see what he would call them**.*

The Hebrew is *to see what he would call it*.

God brings them, and waits.

## The authority

*Whatever the man named each living creature, **that became its name**.*

No corrections are recorded. The naming stands.

Naming is the exercise of dominion in the ancient world — a king renames a vassal, a father
names a child. Adam has already been told to have dominion, and this is the first thing he
does with it.

## What God had done with naming

Day, night, heaven, earth, seas. Five namings in Genesis 1, all by God.

After Adam, God does not name anything else in the chapter.

## The result

*He gave names to all the livestock, the birds, and the wild animals — but among them all,
**there was no true partner to match him**.*

The Hebrew repeats the phrase from verse 18: *there was not found a helper corresponding to
him*.

The search is real and the search fails.

## What the exercise was for

Adam has to see it. God knew the answer before the first animal was brought over.

A man who has named every creature on earth and found none of them a match is ready for what
happens next.

## What happens next

The deep sleep, the rib, and the first recorded human speech:

*"This is now **bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh**; she shall be called Woman, because
she was taken out of Man."*

The first thing a human being says is a poem about somebody else.

And the naming continues — he names her too.

## The other naming

*And Adam called his wife's name **Eve**, because she was the mother of all living.*

That one happens after the fall, immediately after the curse, and the name means *life*. See
the note on Genesis 3:20–24.

## What Paul does with the order

*For Adam was formed first, then Eve.*

And *neither was man created for the woman, but woman for the man* — followed immediately by
*nevertheless, neither is man independent of woman, nor woman independent of man, in the
Lord*.

## The verdict reversed

*Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and **they
shall become one flesh**.*

Quoted by Jesus on marriage, and by Paul on Christ and the church. See the note on
Ephesians 5:28–30.

> **The first *not good*.** Not a fault, and not a failure. A man by himself in a finished
> garden — and the fix involved letting him look at every animal alive first.
