Paul argues from contract law, and then from a singular noun.

## The everyday example

*Let me speak **the way people speak in ordinary matters**. Even a covenant made
between human beings, once it has been **ratified**, is not set aside by anyone, and
**nothing is added to it**.*

He starts with what anybody in that world knew about wills and contracts. Once
witnessed and sealed, a document stands. You cannot append to it later.

## The singular

*The promises were spoken to Abraham **and to his Seed**. It does not say, **"and to
seeds," as though to many**, but as of one: **and to your Seed, who is Christ**.*

The whole weight is on a grammatical number.

The Hebrew **zera** is a collective — like the English *offspring*, it can mean one
descendant or a nation. Paul knows this; he uses it collectively himself four verses
later: *if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's seed.*

His point is not that the collective reading is impossible. It is that the singular
is available, and that it is the one that resolves the promise.

## The arithmetic

*The law, which came **four hundred and thirty years afterward**, cannot cancel a
covenant that was **confirmed beforehand** by God.*

That is the argument in one line. The promise is older.

A later document does not annul an earlier ratified one, and Paul has just
established that from ordinary practice.

## What is at stake

*So as to leave the promise **with nothing in it**.*

The Greek is to make void, to nullify.

If keeping the law were the condition, the promise would have been quietly converted
into a contract with terms — and the terms would be the thing that mattered.

## The two systems

*For **if the inheritance comes by the law, then it no longer comes by promise**; but
**God gave it to Abraham by a promise**.*

Paul treats them as mutually exclusive. Not two routes to the same place — two
different kinds of thing.

An inheritance and a wage cannot be the same transaction.

## What Abraham had

No law, no tabernacle, no priesthood, no commandments. A promise, a night sky, and
a covenant cut while he was asleep. See the notes on Genesis 15:6 and
Genesis 15:12–16.

## What the law was for, then

Paul asks it himself in the next verse: *what purpose then does the law serve?*

His answer: *it was added because of transgressions, till the Seed should come to
whom the promise was made… **the law was our tutor to bring us to Christ**, that we
might be justified by faith.*

A guardian escorting a child somewhere, and the escort's job ends on arrival.

> **How closely Paul reads.** A tense settled the resurrection for Jesus against the
> Sadducees; a singular settles the inheritance for Paul against the Judaisers. Both
> arguments would collapse if the words were approximate. See the note on
> Matthew 22:23–33.
