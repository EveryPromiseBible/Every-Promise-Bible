A staff roster, and the Chronicler stops in the middle of it to explain a family's size.

## What the chapter is

Duty rosters for the gatekeepers, assigned by lot to the four sides of the sanctuary.

Chapters 23 to 27 are David's organisation of the temple staff before he dies: the Levites, the
priests, the musicians, the gatekeepers, the treasurers, and the army divisions.

Lists. Names and numbers.

## The first family

*From the **Korahites**: Meshelemiah son of Kore, from the line of Asaph.*

Seven sons, named in birth order — *Zechariah the firstborn, Jediael the second*, and so on.

The Korahites are descendants of the Korah who led the rebellion in Numbers and was swallowed
by the ground.

Numbers notes what happened to his family: *nevertheless the children of Korah did not die*.

Eleven psalms carry their name, including *as the deer pants for the water brooks*.

## The second family

*Obed-edom had sons too.*

Eight, named the same way: *Shemaiah the firstborn, Jehozabad the second, Joah the third, Sacar
the fourth, Nethanel the fifth, Ammiel the sixth, Issachar the seventh, and Peullethai the
eighth*.

## The clause

*For **God had truly blessed him**.*

The Hebrew is *for God blessed him*.

The Chronicler is running a list and stops to add a reason for the number.

Nowhere else in the roster does he explain why a man had a lot of sons.

## Where the blessing started

Three months with the ark in his house, after the procession stopped six miles short of
Jerusalem. See the note on 1 Chronicles 13:12–14.

*And the LORD blessed his household and everything he had.*

The Chronicler wrote that sentence thirteen chapters earlier and is still tracking the
consequences.

## What the eighth son's name means

**Peullethai** — from **pe'ullah**, work or wages. Something like *my wages* or *my reward*.

## Who these people were

Doorkeepers.

Not priests, not singers. The men who opened up in the morning, controlled who came in, and
locked the gates at night.

*They were trusted, and were in charge of the storerooms and treasuries.*

## What the next paragraph says about them

*All these were descendants of Obed-edom — they, their sons, and their brothers — **sixty-two of
them, capable men, strong for the work**.* See the note on 1 Chronicles 26:6–11.

## The psalm about it

*Blessed is every one who fears the LORD… your children like olive plants all around your
table.* See the note on Psalms 128:3–4.

And *behold, children are a heritage from the LORD*.

## What the man himself asked for

Nothing.

The procession stopped at his house because David was frightened and needed somewhere to leave
it. Obed-edom is not recorded volunteering, negotiating, or saying a word.

He housed it for three months, and a chronicler counting doorkeepers four hundred years later
is still explaining the size of his family.

> **The clause that does not belong in a roster.** *For God had truly blessed him.* The
> Chronicler is listing names and numbers, and stops once, for one man, to say why the number
> was what it was.
