The verse **Paul** quotes about the ascension, and the one word he changes.

## The mountain that is envied

*O mighty mountain, mountain of Bashan… **why do you look with envy** at the mountain God
desired for His dwelling?*

Bashan is high country — Hermon rises to nine thousand feet. Zion is a low ridge with a spring.
The psalm has the great mountain looking jealously at the small one, and the reason is not
altitude.

*But **God has chosen the foolish things** of the world to put to shame the wise, and God has
chosen the weak things of the world to put to shame the things which are mighty.*

## What He desired

*The mountain **God desired for His dwelling**, where the LORD will live forever.*

**Chamad** — to desire, to take pleasure in. The choice is described as preference, not
strategy.

## The ascent

*You went up on high, **You led a train of captives**, **You received gifts among men**.*

## What Paul does with it

*Therefore He says: "**When He ascended on high, He led captivity captive, and gave gifts to
men**."*

**Received** becomes **gave**. Paul reads the psalm as describing Christ going up and handing
down what He took — and then names the gifts: *He Himself gave some to be apostles, some
prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers.*

And he adds a clause: *now this, "He ascended" — what does it mean but that **He also first
descended into the lower parts of the earth**?*

## Who is among the captives

*Even among **the rebellious**, that the LORD God might dwell there.*

The psalm has rebels in the procession, and the stated purpose of the whole ascent is that God
would live among people.

*And the Word became flesh and **dwelt among us**.*

*Behold, **the tabernacle of God is with men**.*

## The chariots

*The chariots of God are **twice ten thousand**, thousands upon thousands.*

Twenty thousand, and the Lord among them. Elisha's servant saw a hillside of them at Dothan.
See the note on 2 Kings 6:15.

> **The word Paul changes.** The psalm says *received*. Ephesians says *gave*. He reads the
> whole procession as a King going up with the spoil and sending it back down.
