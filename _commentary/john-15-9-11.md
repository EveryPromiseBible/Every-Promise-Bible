Three verses, and He tells them what the whole vine passage was for.

## The measure

*"**As the Father has loved Me, so I have loved you**."*

The Greek is **kathos** — in the same way, to the same degree.

He gives the amount by naming a love nobody in the room could quantify. There is no smaller
unit offered.

## The instruction

*"**Make your home in My love**."*

The Greek is **meinate** — abide, remain, stay put. The same verb He has used ten times in
the eight verses before this about the vine and the branches.

Not *get into* His love. Stay where you already are.

## What abiding is not

A branch does not work at being attached. It hangs there, and the sap arrives.

He has just said it: *the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the
vine.* See the note on John 15:4–8.

## The keeping

*"**If you keep My commands you will remain in My love**."*

And immediately the pattern: *"**just as I have kept My Father's commands and remain in His
love**."*

The second clause defines the first. Nobody thinks the Son's obedience earned the Father's
love — it is the way He stayed in it.

## What the command turns out to be

Two verses later: *"**This is My commandment, that you love one another as I have loved
you**."*

The commands, plural, resolve into one, and it is the one thing He has just been doing to
them at the table.

## The purpose

*"**I have told you these things so that** My joy may be in you."*

The Greek is **tauta lelaleka humin** — a formula John uses through the discourse. He says
it four times, and each time the stated reason is for their benefit.

*That your joy may be full.* *That you should not be made to stumble.* *That in Me you may
have peace.*

## Whose joy

*"**My joy** may be in you."*

Not joy He gives, or joy about Him. His own.

And this is said on the night of the arrest, hours from Gethsemane.

Hebrews accounts for it: *who **for the joy that was set before Him** endured the cross,
despising the shame.*

## The fullness

*"And **your joy may be full**."*

The Greek is **plerothe** — filled up, brought to the brim.

John writes the same word in his letter about the same thing: *these things we write to you
that your joy may be full.*

## Where the joy comes from in the sentence

The order is: His love, stayed in — then His joy in them — then their joy filled.

Joy is third in the sequence and never the thing worked at.

## What He says a few verses on

*"**No longer do I call you servants**, for a servant does not know what his master is
doing; but **I have called you friends**, for all things that I heard from My Father I have
made known to you."*

The word for *friends* is **philous** — the ones He is fond of. See the note on
John 16:25–28, where He tells them the Father is fond of them too.

> **What the vine chapter was for.** He says it plainly in verse 11. Not productivity —
> His joy in them, and theirs filled to the brim.
