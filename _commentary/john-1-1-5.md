John opens his Gospel with the first three words of the Bible and then goes back
further.

## The three clauses

*Before anything at all began, **the Word was already there**. The Word **was with
God**, and the Word **was God**.*

Three statements about the same subject, and each one adds something the last did
not have.

He existed. He was in company. He was God.

The second and third do not obviously fit together, and John does not soften either
to make room for the other.

## "The Word"

The Greek is **Logos** — a word with a long history in Greek philosophy, where it
meant the rational principle ordering the universe, and in Jewish thought, where the
word of the LORD does things: *by the word of the LORD the heavens were made.*

John picks a term both audiences already had and attaches it to a person.

## "In the beginning"

The same three words that open Genesis, in the Greek Old Testament.

Genesis 1:1 starts at the beginning. John says that when the beginning began, the
Word was already there.

## The universal claim

*Everything that exists came into being through Him, and **in all that has been made
there is not one thing** that came into being **without Him**.*

Stated positively and then negatively, which is John's habit throughout this Gospel.

Nothing is left outside the claim.

Paul says the same in Colossians: *all things were created through Him and for Him.
And He is before all things, and **in Him all things hold together**.* See the note on
Colossians 1:15–20.

## Life, then light

*In Him was **life**, and that life was **the light** of everyone alive.*

The order matters. Life first, and the light is what the life is to people.

## The last clause

*The light **keeps shining** in the darkness, and the darkness has **never once been
able to swallow it**.*

The Greek verb is present tense — it goes on shining.

And **katalambano** carries both senses: to overcome, and to grasp or comprehend.
The darkness neither put it out nor worked out what it was.

Both readings are true in this Gospel, and translators have never fully settled which
John meant.

## Where the prologue lands

Nine verses later: *and the Word **became flesh** and dwelt among us.* The Greek is
*pitched His tent* — **eskenosen**, the tabernacle word.

The one who was there before the beginning ends up camping.

> **What John does not include.** No genealogy, no Bethlehem, no shepherds, no
> manger. Matthew starts with Abraham, Luke goes back to Adam, and John goes back past
> the first sentence of Genesis.
