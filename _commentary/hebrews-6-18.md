The writer builds a guarantee out of two items and names them both.

## The two

*Now there are **two things that cannot shift**, and God cannot lie in either of
them.*

The verses before identify them: God made a promise to Abraham, and then He swore
an oath to confirm it.

*Because He could swear by no one greater, **He swore by Himself**.*

A promise, and an oath. Both from someone who cannot lie.

## Why the oath at all

*God, determining to show more abundantly to the heirs of promise the
**immutability of His counsel**, confirmed it by an oath.*

The promise was already sufficient. God's word does not require notarising.

The oath was added — the text says so plainly — for the sake of the people receiving
it, so that they would have two immovable things instead of one.

He accommodated human uncertainty.

## The refugees

*And we, **who ran for shelter** and grabbed hold of the hope held out in front of
us.*

The Greek is the language of fleeing for refuge — the image is of somebody running
to the cities of refuge in Numbers 35, where a man who had killed accidentally could
run and not be touched by the avenger.

You did not have to argue your case at the gate. You had to get inside.

## "Grabbed hold"

The Greek is to seize, to take firm hold of.

And note what is grabbed: *the hope held out in front of us* — set before us,
offered. It is extended first and taken second.

## What it does

*We have **something powerful to steady us**.*

The Greek is *strong encouragement* — **parakalesis**, the noun behind *Comforter*.

## The anchor

The next verse: *this hope we have as **an anchor of the soul, both sure and
steadfast**, and which enters the Presence behind the veil.*

An anchor is thrown ahead of the ship into ground the ship has not reached, and it
holds the vessel by being somewhere else.

And this one is described as going *behind the veil* — into the holy of holies, the
room nobody could enter except the high priest, once a year, with blood. See the
note on Hebrews 9:6–7.

The anchor is not in the harbour. It is in the room.

## Who is there

*Where the forerunner has entered for us, even Jesus, having become High Priest
forever according to the order of Melchizedek.*

The Greek for **forerunner** is *prodromos* — the scout who goes ahead of the main
body.

A forerunner is meaningless unless others are following. The word implies the rest
of the fleet.

> **Where the chapter has come from.** Some of the hardest verses in the letter, about
> falling away — and the writer's own comment on them is *we are confident of better
> things concerning you.* Then Abraham, the oath, the anchor and the forerunner. The
> passage that frightens people ends in a room they cannot be got out of.
