Paul tells them how bad it got, and then uses one verb three times in three tenses.

## The disclosure

*We would rather you knew, brothers and sisters, about **the trouble that came on us in
Asia**.*

He chooses to tell them. He never says what it was — the riot at Ephesus, an illness, a
sentence, a shipwreck; the letters do not specify.

What he describes is the weight rather than the event.

## How far past

*The weight of it was **far past anything our strength could carry**, so far past it that
**we gave up on life itself**.*

The Greek is **kath' huperbolen** — beyond all measure, hyperbolically. He piles the
intensifiers up.

**Gave up on life** is *we despaired even of living*. The word is **exaporeomai** — to be
utterly without a way out.

This is the man who wrote *I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me*.

## The verdict

*Deep inside ourselves we had already **heard the verdict read out: death**.*

The Greek is *we had the sentence of death in ourselves* — **apokrima**, a legal ruling,
the official answer of a court.

He had stopped expecting to survive.

## The reason given

*But that was so we would **stop resting on ourselves** and **rest instead on the God who
raises the dead**.*

He names the purpose and it is a transfer of weight, from one place to another.

**The God who raises the dead** is a deliberate choice of title. A man who has heard a
death sentence in himself needs a God described that way and no other.

## The three tenses

*He **pulled us out** of so deadly a danger, and **He is pulling us out still**, and it is
on Him that our hope is set, **that He will go on pulling us out**.*

Past, present, future — one verb, **rhuomai**, to drag out of danger, to rescue by
force.

Paul argues from what has happened to what will. The past deliverance is the evidence
he uses for the next one.

## What they can do about it

*While **you work alongside us by praying for us**.*

The Greek is **sunupourgeo** — to work together underneath, to lend a hand from below.
It occurs once in the New Testament.

Paul asks the Corinthians — the church that questioned his credentials, tolerated
immorality and split into factions — to hold him up by praying.

## The purpose of the many

*So that **the gift given to us through the prayers of many people** may have **many
people giving thanks** for it on our behalf.*

Many pray, one is delivered, many give thanks. Paul builds the circle deliberately.

## How the letter opens

Five verses earlier: *the Father of mercies and God of all comfort, who comforts us in
all our tribulation, **that we may be able to comfort those who are in any trouble**,
with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God.*

The word for comfort appears ten times in five verses.

> **What he did with the worst thing that happened to him.** Wrote it down and sent it
> to a church that had been criticising him — including the part where he gave up.
