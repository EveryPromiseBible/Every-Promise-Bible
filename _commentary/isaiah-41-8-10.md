Four titles handed out in three lines, and then five promises in a row.

## What He calls him

*The offspring of **Abraham My friend**.*

The Hebrew is *Abraham who loved Me* or *My beloved* — the participle works both
ways, and both readings have been held.

It is said of nobody else. 2 Chronicles repeats it and James repeats it: *he was
called the friend of God.*

## What earned it

Nothing that Abraham did. The verse Scripture attaches to the title is the one
about believing: *Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for
righteousness — and he was called the friend of God.* James puts the two clauses
in one sentence.

He had no law to keep. He lied about his wife twice, took Hagar, and laughed at
the promise. And he is the friend. See the note on Genesis 15:6.

## What He calls Israel

*My **servant**… whom I have **chosen**… the **offspring** of Abraham.*

Chosen, and then the negative that goes with it: *I have chosen you **and not cast
you away**.*

## Where He found them

*I **took you from the ends of the earth**, I **called you from its farthest
corners**.*

The initiative is entirely His in both verbs. Nobody in the passage came looking.

## The five promises

*So **do not fear**, for I am with you. **Do not be dismayed**, for I am your God.
I will **strengthen** you. I will **help** you. I will **uphold** you with My
righteous right hand.*

Two commands and three undertakings, and each command has a reason attached rather
than an exhortation.

Do not fear — *for I am with you*. Not because the situation is manageable.

## The right hand

*With **My righteous right hand**.*

The Hebrew is the right hand of My righteousness. What holds a man up in this
passage is God's own righteousness, not his.

That is the same thing Isaiah 54 says: *you will be established in righteousness…
their righteousness is from Me.* See the note on Isaiah 54:13–14.

## What He calls them a few verses later

*"Fear not, **you worm Jacob**, you men of Israel! I will help you," says the LORD
and your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel.*

The chapter's own title. And in the next breath: *I will make you into a new
threshing sledge with sharp teeth; you shall thresh the mountains and beat them
small.*

A worm, told it will thresh mountains. The transformation is announced in the same
sentence as the insult, and the only thing between them is *I will help you.*

> **What the chapter is arguing.** It is a courtroom scene — God challenging the
> idols to predict anything at all. *Show the things that are to come hereafter,
> that we may know that you are gods.* And in the middle of the trial He turns aside
> to reassure a frightened people that He has hold of their hand.
