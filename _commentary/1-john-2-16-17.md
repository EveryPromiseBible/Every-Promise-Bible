John lists three things and says none of them come from the Father. The list is
not arbitrary — it has been used before.

## The three

- **the craving of the body**
- **the craving of the eyes**
- **the bragging about what a man has**

Now set that beside Genesis 3, where Eve looks at the tree:

- *the fruit seemed **good to eat*** — the body
- ***pleasing to the eye*** — the eyes
- ***desirable for the wisdom it promised*** — the standing it would give her

Same three, in the same order, at the first temptation.

## And in the wilderness

The three temptations of Christ run the same way. Turn the stones into bread —
the body. All the kingdoms of the world shown to Him — the eyes. Throw Yourself
down and let the angels catch You — the display, the proving.

One test taken in a garden with everything available, and one in a desert with
nothing. The first was failed and the second was not.

## What John says about the shelf life

*The world is on its way out, and everything it makes you crave along with it.
But whoever does what God wants stays forever.*

The verb is present continuous — the world **is passing**, in progress, already
under way. He is not predicting an end so much as describing something currently
draining away.

The contrast is not between pleasure and misery. It is between what is going and
what stays.

> **Why it is not a list of forbidden things.** John does not name any object —
> no food, no possession, no specific pleasure. Every item on his list is an
> appetite pointed a particular direction. The problem he identifies is where the
> craving comes *from*, not what it happens to land on.
