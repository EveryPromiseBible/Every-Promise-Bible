Before he says a word, he has to swallow one.

## Where this is

By the river Chebar in Babylon, among the exiles. The visions of the first chapter — the living
creatures, the wheels, the throne above the firmament — have flattened him.

*So when I saw it, I fell on my face.* See the note on Ezekiel 1:25–27.

Then a voice, and *the Spirit entered me and set me on my feet*.

## What was handed to him

At the end of chapter 2: *a hand was stretched out to me, and behold, a scroll of a book was in
it*.

*And He spread it before me; and there was writing on the inside and on the outside, and written
on it were **lamentations and mourning and woe**.*

Scrolls were written on one side. This one is full to the edges.

## The address

*He said to me, "**Son of man**."*

The Hebrew is **ben-adam** — son of Adam, son of humanity.

God calls Ezekiel this ninety-three times in the book, and almost never uses his name. Standing
in front of the throne vision, he is addressed by what he is made of.

## The instruction

*"**Eat what you find here. Eat this scroll, and go, speak to the house of Israel**."*

Three imperatives, in order: eat, go, speak.

The speaking is third.

## The obedience

*So **I opened my mouth, and He fed me the scroll**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyaachileni** — and He caused me to eat it.

Ezekiel opens his mouth. God does the feeding.

## The second instruction

*"Son of man, **feed on this scroll I am giving you, and let it fill you full**."*

The Hebrew is **umeecha temalle** — and fill your inward parts, your belly.

Not a taste. The stomach.

## The taste

*So I ate it, and **in my mouth it was as sweet as honey**.*

The Hebrew is **kidvash lematoq** — like honey for sweetness.

## What was written on it

Lamentations, mourning and woe.

Ezekiel's ministry consists of announcing a siege, the fall of the city, and the departure of the
glory from the temple. His wife dies and he is forbidden to mourn.

The content is bitter and the eating is sweet.

## What the next verses say

*"Son of man, go to the house of Israel and speak with My words to them… **but the house of Israel
will not listen to you, because they will not listen to Me**."*

He is told in advance that it will not work.

*"**I have made your face strong against their faces**… like adamant stone, harder than flint."*

His name, **Yechezqel**, means *God strengthens*.

## Where else the scroll is eaten

*Then I took the little book out of the angel's hand and ate it, and **it was as sweet as honey in
my mouth. But when I had eaten it, my stomach became bitter**.* See the note on
Revelation 10:8–9.

John spells out the second half that Ezekiel's account leaves implicit.

## Jeremiah's version

*Your words were found, and **I ate them**, and Your word was to me the joy and rejoicing of my
heart.* See the note on Jeremiah 15:15–18.

The same image, in the same century, from a prophet with the same reception.

## The psalm

*How sweet are Your words to my taste, sweeter than honey to my mouth!* See the note on
Psalms 119:97–104.

## What the order says

Eat, then go, then speak.

Nothing goes out that has not gone in first, and the going-in is described as a meal rather than
a study.

> **The order.** Eat, go, speak. And the scroll he was told to fill his stomach with had
> *lamentations and mourning and woe* written on both sides.
