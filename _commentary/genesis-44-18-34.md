title: Let me stay in place of the boy

The longest speech in Genesis, made by the brother who first suggested selling Joseph.

## Who is speaking

*Then **Judah came close to him** and said, "Please, my lord, let your servant speak a word."*

Twenty-two years earlier: *what profit is there if we kill our brother and cover up his blood?
Come, let us sell him to the Ishmaelites*. See the note on Genesis 37:25–28.

The same man. He does not know who he is talking to.

## What he is answering

The cup was found in Benjamin's sack and the steward has offered the other ten a clean way
home: *only the one found with it will be my slave; the rest of you are free*. See the note on
Genesis 44:1–13.

Nobody took it. They all came back, and Judah steps forward.

## What the speech is about

Count the subject. He says **father** fourteen times in seventeen verses.

He is not arguing about the cup, the law, or Benjamin's innocence. He never mentions any of
them. The entire case is about what this will do to an old man in Canaan.

## The line he quotes

*"Your servant, our father, said to us, 'You know **my wife bore me two sons**. One went away
from me, and I said, "He's surely been torn to pieces," and **I have never seen him since**.'"*

Judah is quoting Jacob's grief over Joseph, out loud, to Joseph.

He helped cause that grief and he is now using it as his argument, without any idea that the
man across the room is the subject of it.

## The knot

*"**Since his life is bound up with the boy's life** — when he sees the boy is gone, **he will
die**."*

The Hebrew is *his soul is bound to his soul*.

## The offer

*"**So please, let me stay here as my lord's slave in place of the boy**, and let the boy go
home with his brothers."*

**In place of.** The Hebrew is **tachat hanna'ar** — under, instead of, in the position of the
lad.

He is not asking for leniency, a reduced sentence, or a shared punishment. He asks to be
exchanged.

## What he is offering to give up

Everything. He has a home, sons, and a life in Canaan. He proposes to spend the rest of it as a
slave in Egypt so that a younger half-brother, who is guilty as far as anyone in the room can
tell, walks free.

## What he had pledged

*"**Your servant guaranteed the boy's safety to my father.** I said, 'If I don't bring him back
to you, let me bear the blame before you all my life.'"*

He made that pledge in chapter 43 to break a deadlock, and it cost nothing at the time. He is
keeping it at the moment it costs everything. See the note on Genesis 43:1–7.

## The last line

*"**How can I go back to my father if the boy is not with me?** I could not bear to see the
misery that would fall upon my father."*

He stops there. There is no closing argument and no appeal to justice.

## What it does

*Then Joseph **could no longer hold himself back**… "Everyone leave me!"* See the note on
Genesis 45:1–8.

The concealment ends here. Not at the bowing, not at the sight of Benjamin, not at the
confession in the prison — at a man offering to take a sentence he did not earn.

## Whose tribe this is

Judah's. *The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, nor a lawgiver from between his feet, until
Shiloh comes.* See the note on Genesis 49:8–12.

The line that produces the King is founded by the man whose recorded turning point is standing
in front of a throne and asking to be made a slave in a guilty brother's place.

Paul says it of Jesus: *He made Him who knew no sin **to be sin for us**, that we might become
the righteousness of God in Him.*

> **The two words.** *In place of.* Judah does not plead for mercy, dispute the evidence, or ask
> for the sentence to be lightened. He asks for it to be moved onto himself, and Joseph cannot
> keep the secret through it.
