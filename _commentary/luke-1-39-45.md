Two pregnant women in a house in the hills, and the first person to recognise the Messiah
has not been born yet.

## The journey

*Mary got ready and **went quickly** up into the hill country, to a town in Judah.*

The Greek is **meta spoudes** — with haste.

Nazareth to the Judean hills is roughly eighty miles. A young woman travelling that
distance is not a small undertaking, and Luke does not say who went with her.

She goes because Gabriel had told her about Elizabeth. See the note on
Luke 1:34–38.

## What happened at the door

*She walked into Zechariah's house and **greeted Elizabeth** — and **the moment Elizabeth
heard Mary's voice, the baby inside her leaped**.*

The Greek is **eskirtesen** — leapt, jumped.

Elizabeth is six months along; John is the one who moves.

## The order

The baby leaps, and **then** Elizabeth is filled with the Spirit and speaks.

Luke gives the sequence deliberately. The recognition comes from inside her before she
knows anything.

## The volume

*She **called out at the top of her voice**.*

The Greek is **anephonesen** — a loud cry. The word occurs once in the New Testament, and
it is used in the Greek Old Testament of the Levites' shout at the ark's procession.

## What she says

*"**You are blessed among women**, and **blessed is the child you are carrying**!"*

Two blessings, and the second one is what the first depends on.

## The question

*"And **why should this come to me — that the mother of my Lord should walk through my
door?**"*

**My Lord.** An older woman, married to a priest, six months pregnant with the forerunner,
calls her young relative's unborn child that.

Nobody has told her anything. Mary has said nothing beyond a greeting.

## The explanation

*"**The instant your greeting reached my ears, the baby inside me jumped for sheer joy**."*

She reports what her own body did and reads it correctly.

Gabriel had said of John: *he will be filled with the Holy Spirit, even from his mother's
womb.*

## The last sentence

*"**How happy you are, because you believed** that what the Lord said to you would actually
be done!"*

The Greek is **makaria** — blessed, happy. It is the word that opens the Beatitudes.

The thing Elizabeth commends is the believing.

Her own husband is standing somewhere in the house, unable to say a word, because he did
not. See the note on Luke 1:18–20.

Luke puts the two in the same building and does not comment.

## What Mary says next

The Magnificat: *my soul magnifies the Lord, and my spirit has rejoiced in God my
Saviour.*

It is stitched almost entirely out of the Old Testament — Hannah's song in 1 Samuel 2 above
all — and it is sung by a teenager.

*He has put down the mighty from their thrones, and exalted the lowly. He has filled the
hungry with good things, and the rich He has sent away empty.*

## How long she stayed

*And Mary **remained with her about three months**, and returned to her house.*

Long enough to be there for the birth, or nearly.

> **Who recognises Him first.** Not a priest, not a prophet, and not an adult. An unborn
> child, at the sound of a voice through a doorway — and his mother says so out loud
> before anybody has explained anything.
