Two ways of praying are ruled out, and both of them are about the audience.

## The first

*When you pray, **do not be like the play-actors**.*

The Greek is **hupokritai** — the word for stage performers, actors who spoke through a mask.

It is the word English took as *hypocrite*, but its first sense is the theatre.

## What they do

*They love to **stand and pray in the synagogues and on the street corners where everyone can
watch them**.*

Nothing in the content is criticised. The prayers may be good ones.

The location is the problem: places chosen because they are visible.

## What they get

*I tell you the truth, **that is the whole of their reward**.*

The Greek is **apechousin** — they have in full. It is the word written on receipts in the
papyri: *paid in full*, nothing further owing.

They wanted to be seen. They were seen. The transaction is closed.

## The alternative

*But you — when you pray, **go into your room, shut the door behind you**, and pray to your Father
**who is there in the secret place**.*

The Greek is **tameion** — the storeroom, the inner chamber. A house's windowless room, where
the valuables were kept.

*Shut the door* — **kleisas ten thuran**, a decisive act.

## What is promised

*And **your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you**.*

The same clause governs the giving in verse 4 and the fasting in verse 18. Three times in one
chapter: **in secret**, and *your Father who sees in secret*.

## The second thing ruled out

*Do not **pile up words** the way people do who do not know Him.*

The Greek is **battalogesete** — a word that may be onomatopoeic. Stammering, babbling,
repeating.

*They imagine **they will be heard because they said so much**.*

**Polulogia** — much-speaking.

## What the two have in common

The first prays to be seen. The second prays to be heard through volume.

Both are working on an audience — one on people, one on God — and both assume something has to
be gotten past.

## The reason

*Your Father **knows exactly what you need long before you ask Him**.*

Which sounds at first like an argument against praying at all.

It is the opposite. It removes the need to inform, persuade, or wear down. What is left is a
child talking to a Father who already knows.

## What He gives them instead

*In this manner, therefore, pray: **Our Father in heaven**…* See the note on
Matthew 6:9–13.

The model prayer is fifty-two words in Greek, and it comes immediately after the warning about
saying too much.

## The word He starts it with

*Father*. And the first person plural — *our*, *us*, *we* — throughout, prayed by a man alone in
a storeroom with the door shut.

## What Isaiah says about the speed

*It shall come to pass that **before they call, I will answer**; and while they are still speaking,
I will hear.* See the note on Isaiah 65:24–25.

Which is the same claim as *your Father knows before you ask*, made seven hundred years earlier.

## Where the chapter goes

*Therefore do not worry, saying, "What shall we eat?"… **for your heavenly Father knows that you
need all these things**.* See the note on Matthew 6:25–34.

The same verb, **oiden** — knows — used twice in one chapter, once about prayer and once about
food.

## The room, and the one that was opened

*"Go into your room, **shut the door behind you**."*

*And behold, **the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom**.*

**Matthew** 27, and the same gospel that has **Jesus** send people into a locked room to pray
records the one room in Israel nobody could enter being opened from the top down.

*Therefore, brethren, having **boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus**, by a new and
living way which He consecrated for us, through the veil, that is, His flesh… **let us draw near**
with a true heart in full assurance of faith.*

**Hebrews** 10, and **parresia** is the word: freedom of speech, the confidence of somebody with
standing in the room.

## Why the words do not have to be piled up

*"Your Father knows exactly what you need **long before you ask Him**."*

*Likewise the Spirit also helps in our weaknesses. For **we do not know what we should pray for as
we ought, but the Spirit Himself makes intercession for us with groanings which cannot be
uttered**.*

**Romans** 8, and it is the strongest form of the sentence: not only does the Father know before
the asking, the asking itself is helped from inside.

*For you did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of
adoption by whom we cry out, "**Abba, Father**."*

The word **Jesus** gives them to start with is the word **Paul** says the Spirit produces.

## And the priest in the room

*Therefore He is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him, since **He
always lives to make intercession for them**.*

> **The receipt.** **Apechousin** — paid in full, nothing further owing. They wanted to be seen,
> and they were.
