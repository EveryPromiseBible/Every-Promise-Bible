Court wisdom, and it opens by saying God conceals things on purpose.

## The heading

*These also are proverbs of Solomon **which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied**.*

An editorial note. Three centuries after Solomon, Hezekiah's scribes went through the archives and
compiled chapters 25 to 29.

## The two glories

*It is **the glory of God to hide a matter away**; it is **the glory of kings to search it out**.*

The Hebrew is **kevod Elohim haster davar ukhevod melakim chaqor davar**.

**Kavod** — glory, weight. Used of both.

## What is being said

Concealment is not withholding. It is an invitation.

God's honour lies in the hiding; the king's lies in the digging.

The two are not in tension — the second exists because of the first.

## The verb

**Chaqor** — to search out, to explore, to investigate.

The word for prospecting, and for Job's *can you search out the deep things of God?*

## The next proverb

*As the heavens rise high and the earth sinks deep, so **the heart of a king is past all finding
out**.*

Which sits oddly beside the first. Kings search things out and their own hearts cannot be
searched.

The book puts them together and does not resolve it.

## The refining

*Take **the dross away from the silver**, and a vessel comes clean for the smith's hand.*

The Hebrew is **hago sigim mikkaseph** — remove the dross from silver.

## The parallel

*Take **the wicked away from before the king**, and his throne stands firm in what is right.*

The metaphor is applied directly. A court has dross in it, and removing it is refining.

## The seating

*Do not push yourself forward in the king's presence, **do not stand in the place meant for the
great**.*

The Hebrew is **al-tithaddar liphnei-melek** — do not glorify yourself before the king.

## The reason

*For it is better he say to you, "**Come up here**," than to be **lowered before the noble** your own
eyes have seen.*

The Hebrew is **aleh henah** — come up here.

## Where Jesus uses it

*"When you are invited by anyone to a wedding feast, **do not sit down in the best place**… But when
you are invited, go and sit down in the lowest place, so that when he who invited you comes he may
say to you, '**Friend, go up higher**.'"* See the note on Luke 14:7–11.

He tells the proverb as a parable at a dinner, and then generalises it: *for whoever exalts himself
will be humbled*.

## What kind of book Proverbs is

Court literature. Written for young men in training for administration.

The advice is about how to behave in a throne room, and Jesus uses it about a seating plan.

## The rest of the chapter

*A word fitly spoken is like **apples of gold in settings of silver**.*

*Whoever has no rule over his own spirit is like **a city broken down, without walls**.*

*If your enemy is hungry, give him bread to eat… for so you will **heap coals of fire on his head**.*

Paul quotes the last one into Romans 12. See the note on Romans 12:17–21.

## What Paul says about the hiding

*But we speak the wisdom of God in a mystery, **the hidden wisdom which God ordained before the ages
for our glory**.*

*But God has revealed them to us through His Spirit. For **the Spirit searches all things, yes, the
deep things of God**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 2:10–13.

The same verb — searching — attributed to the Spirit.

> **The pair.** God's honour is in the hiding; the king's is in the searching. The concealment is
> described as the reason for the digging, not an obstacle to it.
