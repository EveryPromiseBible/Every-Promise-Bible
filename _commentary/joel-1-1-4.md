The book opens with a question and then describes what happened in four stages.

## The question

*"**Has anything like this ever happened** in your days, or in the days of your
fathers before you?"*

He asks the oldest men in the room, and the expected answer is no.

## The instruction

*"**Tell it to your children**, and let them tell their children, and let their
children pass it down to the ones who come after."*

Four generations of transmission ordered for a locust plague.

Something is being preserved deliberately.

## The four

*What the **gnawing** locust left, the **swarming** locust ate. What the swarming
locust left, the **hopping** locust ate. And what the hopping locust left, the
**destroying** locust finished off.*

Four Hebrew words — probably four stages of the same insect's life, or four kinds of
devastation.

The structure is what matters: each one starts where the last one stopped.

*Wave after wave, until nothing was left.*

## What locusts do

A swarm strips everything green — leaf, bark, and the vine down to the wood. Joel
describes it a few verses on: *he has laid waste My vine and ruined My fig tree; he
has stripped it bare and thrown it away; its branches are made white.*

The trees are left white. The bark is gone.

## What it stopped

*The grain offering and the drink offering **are cut off from the house of the
LORD**.*

There is nothing left to bring. The worship stops for lack of materials.

Joel calls the priests to *gird yourselves and lament… lie all night in sackcloth*,
and the cattle groan because there is no pasture.

## The reversal

The same four words come back in chapter 2, in a promise:

*"I will **restore to you the years** the locusts have eaten, the swarming locust and
the hopping locust, the destroying locust and the gnawing locust, **My great army that
I sent among you**."* See the note on Joel 2:25–27.

The book names the destruction in four stages and then names all four again as the
thing being restored.

## What is asked for in between

*"Now, therefore," says the LORD, "turn to Me with all your heart… **so rend your
heart, and not your garments**."*

The visible sign of grief was tearing your clothes. He asks for the thing the sign
stood for.

## Who Joel was

*The son of Pethuel.* That is all anyone knows.

No date, no king named, no city. The shortest biography of any prophet.

> **What the book runs into.** Locusts in chapter 1, an army in chapter 2, and then
> *I will pour out My Spirit on all flesh* — the passage Peter stood up and quoted at
> Pentecost. The prophet nobody can date wrote the sermon that opened the church.
