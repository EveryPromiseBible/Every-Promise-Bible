Three things said about God in one line, and then the worst thing the psalmist can
imagine.

## The three

*God is our **refuge** and our **strength**, **a help always near** the moment
trouble comes.*

**Refuge** — *machseh*, a shelter you run into from a storm.

**Strength** — the word for might, and it is His rather than borrowed.

**A help always near** — the Hebrew is *a help in troubles, very much found*. He is
described as easily located.

## "The moment trouble comes"

The psalm does not say trouble might come. It assumes it, and the whole claim is
about timing: found, and found immediately.

## What he imagines

*Even if **the earth gives way**, even if **the mountains topple into the heart of the
sea**, though its waters roar and foam, though the mountains shake.*

The most permanent things anybody in that world could point at — the ground and the
mountains — going into the water.

It is the language of creation coming undone. The psalmist reaches for the end of
the world and says *even if*.

## The conclusion

*So **we will not be afraid**.*

Placed before the list, not after it. The fearlessness is stated and then the
catastrophes are named underneath it.

## Selah

The psalm pauses here — and again twice more. Nobody is certain what the word
directs, but it marks a break.

## What is in the middle of the psalm

*There is **a river** whose streams shall make glad the city of God… **God is in the
midst of her, she shall not be moved**; God shall help her, **just at the break of
dawn**.*

The seas roar and the mountains fall into them in verse 3. In verse 4 there is a
river, and it is quiet.

Jerusalem had no river. It had one spring, Gihon, and a tunnel Hezekiah cut through
rock to bring the water inside the walls before a siege.

## The line everybody knows

*"**Be still, and know that I am God**."*

The Hebrew is stronger than *be quiet* — it means let go, cease, drop your hands.

And it is not addressed to the anxious believer in the usual reading. Look at the
verse before: *He makes wars cease to the end of the earth; He breaks the bow and cuts
the spear in two.*

The command is addressed to the nations. **Stop.**

## The refrain

*The LORD of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge.*

Twice, at the end of the two halves. **LORD of hosts** is the military title, and
*the God of Jacob* is the God of the man who cheated his brother and wrestled all
night.

> **What was written on Luther's tombstone territory.** He wrote *A Mighty Fortress
> Is Our God* out of this psalm, in the middle of the plague and the Diet of Speyer.
> The psalm has a long record of being reached for when the mountains are moving.
