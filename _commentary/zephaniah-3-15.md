The turn of the book, and the first clause is a courtroom being cleared.

## Where it sits

Zephaniah has been unrelenting: *I will sweep it all away… I will search Jerusalem with lamps… the
great day of the LORD is near.*

And then, from verse 14, the book changes register completely and does not change back.

*"**Sing, Daughter Zion! Shout aloud, Israel! Be glad and rejoice with all your heart.**"*

## The first clause

*"**The LORD has taken away the sentence against you.**"*

The Hebrew is **hesir YHWH mishpatayikh** — the LORD has removed your judgments.

**Mishpat** is the legal verdict. The plural is the accumulated case against her, and the verb is a
removal.

*There is therefore now **no condemnation** to those who are in **Christ Jesus**.*

*Having wiped out **the handwriting of requirements that was against us**, which was contrary to us.
And He has taken it out of the way, having nailed it to the cross.*

**Paul**'s verb in **Colossians** is **erken** — He has taken it away, lifted it out of the middle.
It is Zephaniah's clause with the mechanism attached.

## The order in the paragraph

The sentence is removed first. The singing is commanded before it in verse 14, and the ground for
the singing is given in verse 15 — which means the rejoicing is not the condition of the acquittal.
It is the response to it.

*Therefore, having been justified by faith, **we have peace with God** through our Lord Jesus
Christ… and **we rejoice** in hope of the glory of God.*

## The second clause

*"**He has turned back your enemy.**"*

**Pinnah oyvekh** — He has cleared away your enemy.

*Having disarmed principalities and powers, He made a public spectacle of them, **triumphing over
them in it**.*

*"Now is the judgment of this world; **now the ruler of this world will be cast out**."*

## The third

*"**The LORD, the King of Israel, is with you.**"*

Two of the great titles in one line, and then the presence.

*Pilate wrote a title and put it on the cross. And the writing was: **JESUS OF NAZARETH, THE KING OF
THE JEWS**… and it was written in Hebrew, Greek and Latin.*

**John** 19, and the chief priests object to the wording and are refused.

*"They shall call His name **Immanuel**," which is translated, "**God with us**."*

## The last clause

*"**Never again will you fear any harm.**"*

The Hebrew is **lo-tir'i ra od** — you shall not see evil any more.

*"And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be **no more death, nor sorrow, nor
crying**. There shall be no more pain, for the former things have passed away."*

## What is not said

That she deserved it. Nothing between verse 13 and verse 14 records a repentance, an offering, or a
change of behaviour.

The people described three verses earlier are *a meek and humble people* who have been **left** —
the survivors of the sweeping, not its solution.

*Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but **according to His mercy He saved us**.*

> **The verb.** *He has taken away your judgments.* Zephaniah puts the acquittal before the singing
> and gives no account of anything the city did in between — and Colossians names where the
> document went.
