David wrote this around 1000 BC. Crucifixion had not been invented.

## When the method appeared

The Persians began impaling people some five centuries after David. The Romans
developed crucifixion into what it became, and it was not in use anywhere in
David's world.

Israel's own method of execution was stoning. Nothing in David's experience
involved piercing a man's hands and feet and leaving him where people could stand
and stare.

## The line

*They have **pierced my hands and my feet**.*

Four words, written by a man describing something he had no category for.

## What else is in the passage

- *Dogs surround me* — the term Jews used for Gentiles, and Romans did the work
- *a band of evildoers has closed in*
- *I can **count all my bones*** — what hanging by the arms does to the ribcage
- *they **stare and gloat** over me* — the crowd standing and watching
- *they **divide my clothes** among them and **cast lots** for my garment*

That last one is two separate actions, and John records both: the soldiers divided
His outer garments four ways, and then came to the tunic, *woven in one piece from
the top*, and said *let us not tear it, but cast lots for it.*

They divided some and gambled for one, because of how the tunic was made. David
wrote both halves of it a thousand years earlier.

## Not one bone broken

*I can count all my bones.* And in the same psalm's world, Exodus 12 said of the
Passover lamb: *nor shall you break one of its bones.*

The soldiers came to break the legs of the crucified men to finish them, and found
Him already dead, and did not break His.

## What the psalm opens with

*My God, my God, why have You abandoned me?* — the sentence He quoted from the
cross at the ninth hour. See the note on Psalms 22:1–5.

Naming the first line of a psalm called up the whole psalm. He was pointing at
this.

## And how it ends

*He has done it.* Three Hebrew words at the close, and the Greek for the same
thought is one: **tetelestai**. It is finished.

> **What is not in the psalm.** Any hint that David is describing his own life.
> Nothing recorded of him matches it. He wrote a first-person account of an
> execution that would not exist for five hundred years, in a country that never
> practised it.
