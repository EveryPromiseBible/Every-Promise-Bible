Samaria is surrounded, the king has already surrendered once, and an unnamed prophet arrives
with a promise.

## Who he comes to

Ahab.

The man introduced two chapters earlier as having *done more evil in the eyes of the LORD than
any who came before him*, with an altar to Baal in his capital and Jezebel in his palace. See
the note on 1 Kings 16:29–34.

Nothing in the chapter records him repenting, praying, or asking. The prophet simply arrives.

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that **while we were still sinners**, Christ
died for us.*

## What is said

*"Do you see this vast army? **Today I will hand it over to you**."*

Perfect confidence, stated before anything happens, to a man with no claim on it whatsoever.

## The reason given

*"**And you will know that I am the LORD.**"*

Not because Ahab deserves it. Not because Israel has turned. The stated purpose is that a king
who has spent his reign serving Baal would find out who the LORD is.

That is grace with a motive, and the motive is disclosure.

*Or do you despise the riches of His goodness, forbearance, and longsuffering, **not knowing
that the goodness of God leads you to repentance**?*

## The three questions

*"**But how?**" Ahab asked.*

He does not object. He asks a logistical question — and gets an answer that makes it worse:
*the young officers under the provincial commanders will do it.*

*"**And who will start the battle?**" "**You will.**"*

Two hundred and thirty-two young officers in front, seven thousand men behind, against an army
of thirty-two kings.

## What Ahab does

Musters them and marches out at noon.

For all his record, he acts on the word. The chapter gives him that without comment.

## Where the enemy is

*While Ben-Hadad and the thirty-two kings allied with him were **in their tents getting
drunk**.*

The attack goes in at midday against a command structure that cannot stand up.

*"Whether they've come for peace or for war, **take them alive**."* His last order before the
rout is a piece of confident housekeeping.

## What the chapter refuses to do

Make the promise conditional on the man.

The victory is given, the reason is stated, and Ahab is exactly who he was the day before.

> **Who the promise is made to.** Not a reformer, not a penitent, and not a good king. The
> worst one in the book — and the reason given is *that you shall know that I am the LORD*.
