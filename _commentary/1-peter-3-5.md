Peter names where a particular kind of beauty came from, and it is not a technique.

## What comes before

*Do not let your adornment be merely outward — **arranging the hair, wearing gold, or putting on
fine apparel**.*

*Rather let it be **the hidden person of the heart**, with the incorruptible beauty of a gentle and
quiet spirit, **which is very precious in the sight of God**.* See the note on
1 Peter 3:3.

The Greek is **ho kruptos tes kardias anthropos** — the hidden person of the heart.

## The verse

*That is how **the godly women of long ago made themselves beautiful**.*

The Greek is **hai hagiai gunaikes** — the holy women.

**Ekosmoun heautas** — they adorned themselves. Imperfect: it was their habit.

**Kosmeo** — to arrange, to order, to decorate. The root of *cosmetic*, and of **kosmos**.

## The two things they did

*They **put their hope in God**, and they **put themselves under their own husbands**.*

The Greek is **hai elpizousai eis Theon** — the ones hoping in God.

**Hupotassomenai tois idiois andrasin** — submitting themselves to their own husbands.

## The order

Hope in God first.

The second clause follows from the first, and Peter does not present it the other way round.

## The word *own*

**Idiois** — their own.

Not to men generally. Peter's instruction is domestic and specific, and the qualifier is in the
Greek every time he uses it.

## Who is being addressed

Wives in a mixed household. The paragraph opens *likewise you wives, be submissive to your own
husbands, that **even if some do not obey the word**, they, without a word, may be won by the
conduct of their wives*.

A woman who has become a Christian and whose husband has not.

In that legal setting she had almost no standing. Peter's counsel is aimed at a real and dangerous
position.

## The word *likewise*

**Homoios** — in the same way.

It refers back to the previous chapter, which was addressed to servants under harsh masters, and
before that to Christ Himself: *when He was reviled, did not revile in return*.

The category is not female. It is the pattern of the one who *committed Himself to Him who judges
righteously*.

## What is said to husbands

*Likewise you husbands, **dwell with them with understanding**, giving honour to the wife… **as being
heirs together of the grace of life, that your prayers may not be hindered**.*

The Greek is **sunkleronomois charitos zoes** — joint-heirs of the grace of life.

The same word Paul uses of believers and Christ.

And the sanction is that prayer stops working.

## The example

*Sarah did it — she did what Abraham said and **called him her master**.*

*You are **her daughters** when you do what is right and **will not let any scare rattle you**.* See
the note on 1 Peter 3:6.

## What Sarah actually did

Laughed at the announcement, and then denied it.

Told Abraham to sleep with Hagar, and afterwards said *my wrong be upon you*.

The one time she calls him **adoni** — my lord — is in the sentence where she is laughing to
herself.

Peter names her anyway.

## The last clause

*Will not let any scare rattle you.*

The Greek is **me phoboumenai medemian ptoesin** — not fearing any terror.

Which is a phrase from the Septuagint of Proverbs 3, and it is the note the paragraph ends on: not
compliance, but fearlessness.

> **The order.** Hope in God, then the household. Peter puts them in that sequence, and the
> paragraph ends on not being frightened rather than on being agreeable.
