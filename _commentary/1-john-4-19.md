Six words in Greek, and the argument of the whole letter is in the last one.

## The word

**Protos** — first, prior, before. It is doing all the work.

John is not saying God loved us more, or better. He is saying He loved us
**earlier** — before there was anything to respond to.

## Why the order matters

Read backwards, the verse becomes an instruction: love God, and He will love you.
That is the reverse of what John spends the chapter arguing.

*In this is love, not that we loved God, but that He loved us and sent His Son.*
He says it four verses earlier in almost the same words, ruling out the wrong
order before he states the right one.

## What it makes love

A response. Not something worked up, and not a duty discharged — the reaction to
something that already happened.

That is why John can say elsewhere that His commands are not burdensome. A
command to love, given to someone who has been loved first, is not the same
weight as a command given cold.

## Where the chapter has just been

*There is no fear in love; perfect love throws fear outside.* And: *what He is,
that is what we are, in this world.*

This verse is the summary of both. The fear goes because the love was there
before we arrived, and it did not wait to see how we would turn out.

> **What John does with it next.** He goes straight to the brother you can see: *if
> anyone says "I love God" and hates his brother, he is a liar.* The love that
> came first is expected to run through and land on somebody. See the note on
> 1 John 4:16.
