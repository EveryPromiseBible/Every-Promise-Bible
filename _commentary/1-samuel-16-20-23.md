The newly anointed king's first job is playing music for the man he is going to replace.

## What has just happened

Samuel has come to Bethlehem, worked through seven sons, asked, **16:11**: *are all the young men
here?*, and anointed the one who was out with the sheep.

**16:13**: *And the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward.*

And the next verse, **16:14**: *but the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul.*

## What Jesse sent

**16:20** *Jesse loaded a donkey with **bread, a skin of wine, and a young goat**, and sent them to
Saul **with David his son**.*

A gift to a king, and a boy alongside it.

Jesse does not know what Samuel did — or if he does, he sends him anyway.

## The post

**16:21** *So David came to Saul and **entered his service**.*

## What Saul felt

**16:21** again: *Saul **loved him dearly** and made him **one of his armor-bearers**.*

The Hebrew is *loved him greatly*.

Saul later throws a spear at him twice, hunts him across the wilderness for years, and
kills eighty-five priests for helping him.

The narrator records the loving first, without irony.

## The request

**16:22** *And Saul sent word to Jesse: "**Let David stay with me, for I'm pleased with him**."*

The Hebrew is *for he has found favour in my eyes* — **chen**, grace.

The same word used over Noah, Joseph in Potiphar's house, and Esther before the king.

## The trouble

**16:23** *Whenever **the harmful spirit came over Saul**.*

Verse 14 says where it came from, **16:14** again: *the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul, and a
distressing spirit from the LORD troubled him.*

The narrator says it plainly and does not explain it.

## The remedy

**16:23** again: *David would **take the harp and play**.*

The Hebrew is **kinnor** — a lyre, played by hand.

## The result

**16:23** again: *Then Saul would **find relief, the darkness would lift, and the tormenting spirit would
leave him**.*

The Hebrew is *Saul was refreshed and was well*.

Nothing is said, sung or prayed in the account. A boy plays and it lifts.

## What David was doing beforehand

Keeping sheep, which is where Samuel found him, and where he learned the instrument.

The psalms attributed to him run to seventy-three, and the one everybody knows opens,
**Psalm 23:1**: *the LORD is my shepherd*.

## What Saul's servants had said about him

**16:18** *A skilful player on the harp, a mighty man of valour, a man of war, prudent in speech, and
handsome; **and the LORD is with him**.*

Five qualities and then the last clause, which is the one that matters and the one they
could not have arranged.

## What happens next

Goliath, in the following chapter — and Saul does not recognise him, **1 Samuel 17:55**: *Whose son is this
youth?*

The two accounts sit awkwardly together and the narrator lets them.

> **The first thing the anointed king does.** Carries a donkey-load of bread and wine to
> the palace and plays the lyre for a man in the dark — and 1 Samuel says the darkness
> lifted every time.
