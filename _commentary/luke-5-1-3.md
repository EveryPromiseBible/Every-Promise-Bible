He needs a pulpit and there are two available.

## The crowd

*The crowd kept pressing in around Him, **hungry to hear the word of God**.*

The Greek is a strong word — pressing, crushing. He is being backed toward the
water by people who want to hear.

## Two boats

*He noticed **two boats** pulled up at the water's edge, **empty**.*

Both were there. Both were free. Neither owner was in them — the fishermen had
climbed out and were washing their nets after a night that produced nothing.

## The one He got into

*He stepped into **the one that belonged to Simon**.*

Luke names the boat by its owner. Of the two available, He picks one, and it is
the boat of a man who had worked all night and caught nothing.

Simon is not asked whether he minds. He is asked to *push out a little way from
the land* — a small thing, an inconvenience at the end of a bad shift.

## What Simon gave

The loan of a boat for an hour, and the trouble of pushing it out and holding it
steady while somebody else talked.

That is the whole of his contribution.

## What he got back

The next paragraph: *take her out into the deep water* — and the nets tear, and
both boats fill until they are settling low in the water.

The other boat, still empty on the shore, ends up loaded too. Simon's partners
are waved over and they share it. See the note on Luke 5:4–7.

He handed over an empty boat and it came back sinking.

## The order

The teaching happens first. He sits down in the boat and *taught the crowds from
the water* — and only after He has finished speaking does He say anything about
fish.

Simon heard the whole sermon from a few feet away, sitting in his own boat, before
he was asked to let the nets down.

> **What He was doing with a fishing boat.** Using the tool of a man's trade, on
> the water he had worked all night, to preach. And then filling it. Simon's
> business was not set aside for the ministry — it was pushed out a little way
> from the land and taught out of.
