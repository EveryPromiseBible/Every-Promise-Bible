Two verses before the end, and the strongest man in Israel is being led by a servant.

## Where he is

The temple of Dagon in Gaza. The Philistine lords have gathered *to offer a great sacrifice to
Dagon their god*, and their song has already been recorded:

*"Our god has delivered into our hands Samson our enemy!"*

*So it happened, when their hearts were merry, that they said, "Call for Samson, that he may
perform for us."*

## The state he is in

Blind, in bronze fetters, brought out of a prison where he had been grinding grain. See the
note on Judges 16:21–22.

## The one who leads him

*Samson said to **the young man who held his hand**.*

The Hebrew is **na'ar** — a boy, a servant, a young man. The word covers a wide range and
usually means somebody young enough to be sent on errands.

The last human being to help Samson is a boy holding his hand.

## The request

*"**Let me feel the pillars that hold up this temple, so I can lean against them**."*

Reasonable, from a blind man who has been made to perform. He wants somewhere to rest.

The boy has no reason to refuse and does it.

## The building

*Now the temple was **crowded — men and women, all the Philistine rulers**.*

Excavated Philistine temples of the period have been found with a roof supported on two
central pillars set on stone bases, close enough together for one man to reach both.

## The roof

*And **on the roof alone some three thousand men and women**, watching Samson be mocked.*

The roof is the gallery. Three thousand people up there, and everybody who mattered in
Philistia below.

## The word

*Watching Samson be mocked.*

The Hebrew is **tsachaq** — to laugh, to play, to make sport.

It is the word used of Ishmael in Genesis 21, and the root of Isaac's name.

The entertainment is a blind man being made to stumble.

## The prayer

*"O Lord GOD, **remember me, I pray! Strengthen me, I pray, just this once**, O God, that I may
with one blow take vengeance on the Philistines for my two eyes!"*

The only recorded prayer in his life that is not about his own thirst.

*Remember me* — the same request Hannah made, and the thief on the cross.

## What he did

*And Samson took hold of the two middle pillars which supported the temple, and he braced
himself against them, one on his right and the other on his left.*

*Then Samson said, "**Let me die with the Philistines!**" And he pushed with all his might.*

## The count

*So the dead that he killed at his death were **more than he had killed in his life**.*

The account gives no total, and does not comment.

## What the chapter is

A man who cannot keep a vow, cannot keep a secret, and cannot stay away from the wrong woman.
Twenty years of judging Israel and almost nothing to show for it.

And the last thing he does is ask to be led to a pillar.

## Where his name is

Hebrews 11: *Gideon and Barak and **Samson** and Jephthah, also David and Samuel and the
prophets — who through faith subdued kingdoms, worked righteousness, obtained promises,
stopped the mouths of lions*.

He is on the list, between Barak and Jephthah, and the list mentions lions.

## Where he is buried

*And his brothers and all his father's household came down and took him, and brought him up and
buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of his father Manoah.*

The same ground where, in chapter 13, *the Spirit of the LORD began to move upon him*.

> **Who leads him to the pillars.** A boy, holding his hand, doing what he was asked. The last
> act of the strongest man in Israel required somebody to walk him to the spot.
