Three days after the battle, a man arrives with his clothes torn and a version of events.

## Where David is

*After David came back from cutting down the Amalekites, he settled in at **Ziklag** for two
days.*

Ziklag is the town that had just been burned to the ground with his wives and children taken —
the day he strengthened himself in the LORD. See the note on 1 Samuel 30:6.

He has been home two days. He does not yet know there is no longer a king in Israel.

## The gap

*On **the third day**, a man came stumbling in.*

Somewhere north, on Gilboa, Saul and Jonathan have been dead for three days and David has been
recovering his family and dividing spoil.

## What the man is wearing

*His **clothes torn, dust on his head**, the marks of grief and disaster all over him.*

The costume of a mourner, correctly assembled. Everything about his appearance is right.

He is an Amalekite, and the next verses have him claiming a killing that 1 Samuel 31 says did
not happen that way. See the note on 2 Samuel 1:5–10.

## The two questions

*"**Where have you come from?**"* and *"**Tell me — what happened?**"*

David asks where before he asks what.

## The answer

*"The army broke and ran. Many fell. Many are dead. **Saul is dead too, and Jonathan his
son**."*

Four sentences, in ascending order of cost, and the name David cared about is last.

## What he does not do

Celebrate.

Everything he has wanted for years arrives in this paragraph. The man hunting him is dead, the
throne is open, and the next thing recorded is that he tore his own clothes, and wept, and
fasted until evening — for Saul, for Jonathan, and *for the people of the LORD*.

*Do not rejoice when your enemy falls, and do not let your heart be glad when he stumbles.*

## What the chapter is really about

Two responses to the same news, side by side. One man sees an opening and comes to be paid.
One man sees a disaster and writes a song.

*"**Blessed are those who mourn**, for they shall be comforted."*

> **The three days.** Saul and Jonathan lay dead on Gilboa while David was recovering
> everything he had lost at Ziklag. He is being handed a kingdom in the same week, and the
> first thing he does with it is grieve.
