The ark sits in a house on a hill for twenty years and nobody comes for it.

## Where it is

*The ark stayed at **Kiriath-jearim**.*

The men of Beth-shemesh had asked for it to be taken away after the deaths there, and Kirjath
Jearim came and fetched it. See the note on 1 Samuel 6:19–21.

*And they brought it into the house of Abinadab on the hill, and consecrated Eleazar his son to
keep the ark.*

A private house. Not a sanctuary.

## The duration

*A long time — **twenty years in all**.*

And that is only until the events of this chapter. It stays there through the whole of Saul's
reign — the Chronicler notes that *we did not enquire of it in the days of Saul*.

The total is closer to a century before David goes to get it.

## What was happening meanwhile

The tabernacle of Moses was at Nob, and later at Gibeon, with the altar and the priesthood
functioning.

The system ran. The sacrifices continued. The one thing missing was the ark, and nobody sent
for it.

## The mourning

*And **all the people of Israel mourned and longed for the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyinnahu** — they lamented after the LORD.

Twenty years of it. The verb suggests a long, low grieving rather than a crisis.

## The condition

*"**If you are truly returning to the LORD with all your hearts**, then get rid of the foreign
gods and the Ashtoreths among you."*

Samuel puts the inward first and the disposal second.

**Ashtoreth** — the Canaanite fertility goddess, Astarte. The plural in the Hebrew covers the
local versions.

They had been keeping them. Twenty years of longing for the LORD with the idols still in the
house.

## The instruction

*"**Commit yourselves to the LORD and serve Him only**, and He will rescue you from the hand of
the Philistines."*

The Hebrew is *prepare your hearts* — **hakinu levavkhem**.

## The obedience

*So the Israelites **put away their Baals and Ashtoreths and served the LORD alone**.*

The narrative does not pad it. Samuel says it and they do it.

## What follows

Mizpah, and water poured out before the LORD, and fasting.

The Philistines hear the assembly and attack. *And the LORD thundered with a loud thunder upon
the Philistines that day, and so confused them that they were overcome before Israel.*

## The stone

*Then Samuel took a stone and set it up between Mizpah and Shen, and called its name
**Ebenezer**, saying, "**Thus far the LORD has helped us**."*

**Even ha-ezer** — stone of help.

The same name as the place where the ark was captured twenty years earlier. Israel had lost the
battle at Ebenezer in chapter 4.

He puts the name back on a stone at a victory.

## What Samuel does with the rest of his life

*And he went from year to year on a circuit to Bethel, Gilgal, and Mizpah, and judged Israel in
all those places.*

*But he always returned to Ramah, for his home was there.*

## What David does about the ark

Takes Jerusalem, and immediately goes for it. See the note on
2 Samuel 6:9–11.

The first thing he does with a capital city is fetch the box nobody had wanted for a
generation.

And the psalm about it remembers where it had been: *we heard of it in Ephrathah; we found it
in the fields of the wood*. See the note on Psalms 132:6–7.

> **What twenty years of longing had not produced.** The idols were still in the houses. Samuel
> names the condition and the disposal happens in one sentence.
