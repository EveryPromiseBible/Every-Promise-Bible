A man walks for the first time in thirty-eight years and is stopped for a paperwork
offence.

## The objection

*"**This is the Sabbath. You are not permitted to carry your mat**."*

Nothing about the walking. Nothing about the legs.

The Greek is **ouk exestin** — it is not lawful.

## Where the rule came from

Not from Moses. Jeremiah has *bear no burden on the Sabbath day*, and Nehemiah shut the
gates of Jerusalem against traders.

By the first century the rabbinic tradition had worked out thirty-nine categories of
prohibited work, and carrying from one domain to another was one of them.

A man with a mat under his arm was a walking violation of a clause.

## What they did not notice

They are talking to a man who could not stand that morning.

John has already established the crowd at that pool — *blind, lame, paralysed* — and these
are the people who administered the place.

## His answer

*"**The man who made me well** is the one who said to me, '**Pick up your mat and walk**.'"*

He does not defend the carrying. He points at the authority behind it.

The Greek is *the one who made me whole, that one said to me*.

His logic is that whoever could do the first thing has the right to instruct the second.

## Their reply

*"**Who is this man** who told you to pick it up and walk?"*

They quote him back — but only the second half. *Who told you to pick it up and walk*, not
*who made you well*.

The healing is not asked about. It is not even acknowledged.

## What he did not know

*But the healed man **had no idea who it was**.*

He had been cured by a stranger and had not asked His name.

Contrast the blind man in chapter 9, who is asked the same question and ends up arguing
theology with the Sanhedrin, and finally worshipping.

## Why he did not know

*Because **Jesus had slipped away into the crowd** that was in that place.*

The Greek is **exeneusen** — turned aside, withdrew. Some read it as a swimming metaphor:
He slipped out.

He heals and leaves before anyone can identify Him. John reports this pattern more than
once — after the loaves, *He departed again to the mountain by Himself alone*.

## What happens after

Jesus finds him later, in the temple, and tells him who did it — and then the man goes and
tells the authorities. See the note on John 5:14–18.

## What the chapter turns into

This exchange is the hinge. The rest of John 5 is the longest speech Jesus makes about His
relationship to the Father — *the Son can do nothing of Himself*, *as the Father raises the
dead and gives life, even so the Son gives life*.

It all comes out of a question about a mat.

> **What was asked and what was not.** A man who had been on the ground for thirty-eight
> years is standing in front of them, and the only question is who authorised the bedding.
