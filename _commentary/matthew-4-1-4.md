Read what happened immediately before this. At the end of chapter 3, the Father's
voice says *this is My Son, the One I love. My delight is in Him.*

Then the first word out of the tempter's mouth is **if**.

## The attack

*If You are the Son of God, tell these stones to turn into bread.*

He does not attack the hunger. He attacks the sonship — and he attacks it by
suggesting it needs demonstrating. Do something, and the question will be
settled.

Jesus had nothing to prove. The Father had already said it out loud, in public,
before a single miracle had been performed. See the note on Matthew 3:16–17.

## What He answers with

*It is written: a person does not live on bread alone, but on every word that
comes from the mouth of God.*

He quotes Deuteronomy 8 — Moses telling Israel why God let them hunger in the
wilderness and fed them with manna.

That is the deliberate parallel. Israel spent forty years in the desert and
failed the tests. Jesus spends forty days in one and does not, and He answers
each time with a line from the book about the first forty years.

## The word He lives on

*Every word that comes from the mouth of God.* He has one in hand already: *this
is My Son, the One I love.*

The temptation is answered by holding onto what has been said rather than
producing evidence for it.

> **Who led Him there.** *Then the Spirit led Jesus up into the wilderness.* The
> desert is not a detour or a failure of guidance. Matthew puts the Spirit's
> leading and the testing in the same sentence, immediately after the dove
> descends.
