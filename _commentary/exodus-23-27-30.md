He gives the reason for the slow pace, and it is not that they are being tested.

## What goes first

*I will send **My terror ahead of you** and **throw into confusion** every nation you meet.*

The Hebrew is **eimati** — my dread. And **vehammoti** — I will discomfit, throw into panic. The
verb used of what happened to the Egyptians in the sea.

## The result

*And I will make **all your enemies turn their backs and run**.*

The Hebrew is **oref** — the neck, the back of the neck. I will give you the neck of your enemies:
the view you have of a man running away.

## The hornet

*I will send **the hornet** before you to drive out the Hivites, Canaanites, and Hittites.*

The Hebrew is **hatzir'ah**.

Read as the insect, or as a figure for panic, or — an old suggestion — for Egyptian military
campaigns through Canaan, the hornet being a royal emblem of Egypt.

The text does not settle it. What it settles is who sends it.

## The refusal

*I will **not drive them all out in a single year**.*

He could. The paragraph has just said so twice.

## The reason

*Or **the land would become desolate** and **the wild animals too many for you**.*

The Hebrew is **shemamah** — a waste, a desolation.

## What that means

Empty land does not stay empty. Clear the population faster than Israel can occupy it, and the
fields go back to scrub and the predators move in.

The delay is not about Israel's readiness of heart. It is about the size of the country against
the size of the nation.

## The pace

*Little by little I will drive them out.*

The Hebrew is **me'at me'at** — a little, a little. The phrase doubled.

## The condition

*Until **you have grown** and can take possession of the land.*

The Hebrew is **ad asher tifreh** — until you are fruitful, until you increase.

## What the sentence assumes

That they will grow. The slow pace is calibrated to a growth God expects to happen.

## What is not said

Nothing here about discipline, or about them deserving the delay.

The reason given is agricultural and demographic. It is a logistics answer, and it is the only
answer offered.

## What it is set inside

*Behold, **I send an Angel before you** to keep you in the way… **My name is in Him**.* See the note
on Exodus 23:20–22.

The paragraph on the pace of the conquest sits directly under the promise of the Angel who goes
ahead.

## The other side of it

*And **the LORD your God will drive out those nations before you little by little**; you will be
unable to destroy them at once, lest the beasts of the field become too numerous for you.* See the
note on Deuteronomy 7:22–24.

Moses repeats it, with the same reason, forty years later.

## What Judges records

*So the LORD left those nations, without driving them out immediately.*

The nations that remained became the test the book of Judges is about — which is a later
development, not the reason given here.

## Where the same pace turns up

*But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are **being
transformed into the same image from glory to glory**.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 3:16–18.

The Greek is **metamorphoumetha** — a present passive. Being changed, continuously, by something
being looked at.

> **The reason given.** Not that they were not ready, and not that they had to earn it. Clear the
> land faster than they could fill it and the wild animals move in.
