David opens by talking to himself.

## Who is being addressed

*Bless the LORD, **O my soul**.*

Not God, and not the congregation. He turns to his own inner life and gives it an
instruction.

The psalm does this four times — twice at the start and twice at the end, closing the
whole thing in the same words it opened with.

## The verb

**Barak** — to bless, and the root sense is to kneel.

To bless God is not to confer something on Him. It is a posture.

## Everything

*And **all that is within me**, bless His holy name.*

The Hebrew is *all my inward parts*.

Nothing exempted. He is calling the whole interior to do one thing.

## The name

*Bless **His holy name**.*

Not His gifts. The psalm gets to those in the next verse and spends the rest of itself on
them, but it starts at the name.

## The second command

*Bless the LORD, O my soul, and **do not forget a single one of His kindnesses**.*

The Hebrew is *forget not all His benefits* — **gemul**, a dealing, a recompense, what
someone has done for you.

## Why it needs saying

Forgetting is the Old Testament's standing charge against Israel. Deuteronomy warns of
it repeatedly: *beware, lest you forget the LORD who brought you out of the land of
Egypt.*

Psalm 106 states it flatly: *they soon forgot His works.*

David does not assume he is immune. He instructs his own soul against it.

## What the psalm does next

It lists them, so the forgetting has something to work against:

*Who **forgives** all your iniquities, who **heals** all your diseases, who **redeems**
your life from destruction, who **crowns** you with lovingkindness and tender mercies,
who **satisfies** your mouth with good things.*

Five participles in a row, all present tense, all God's. See the note on
Psalms 103:3–5.

## Where the psalm goes

*As far as the east is from the west, so far has He removed our transgressions from us.*

*As a father pities his children, so the LORD pities those who fear Him. For **He knows
our frame; He remembers that we are dust**.*

The psalm that begins by telling the soul not to forget says that God remembers what we
are made of.

## How it ends

*Bless the LORD, all you His angels… Bless the LORD, all you His hosts… Bless the LORD,
all His works, in all places of His dominion. **Bless the LORD, O my soul!**"*

It widens out to the angels, the hosts and the whole creation, and then comes back to the
one soul it started with.

> **Who gives the order.** He does, to himself. The psalm's first move is a man deciding
> what his own inside is going to do.
