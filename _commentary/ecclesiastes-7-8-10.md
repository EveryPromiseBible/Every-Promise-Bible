Three proverbs, and the last one names a question people ask without noticing.

## The end

*The **end of a thing is better than its beginning**.*

The Hebrew is **tov acharit davar mereshito**.

**Acharit** — the end, the outcome, the latter part.

Beginnings are easy to admire. The book has spent six chapters watching things start well.

## The patience

*And **patience is better than pride**.*

The Hebrew is **tov erek-ruach miggevah-ruach** — long of spirit is better than high of spirit.

Two spirits, one long and one high. The idiom for patience is *long*; the idiom for arrogance is
*high*.

The same construction as **erek appayim** — long of nostrils — used of God at Sinai.

## The anger

*Do not be quick to anger, for **anger settles down in the laps of fools and makes its home
there**.*

The Hebrew is **ka'as becheiq kesilim yanuach** — vexation rests in the bosom of fools.

**Nuach** — to rest, to settle. The root of Noah's name.

Anger is described as a lodger. It arrives, sits down, and stays.

## The question

*And do not sigh, "**Why were the old days better than now?**"*

The Hebrew is **meh hayah shehayyamim harishonim hayu tovim me'elleh** — what was it that the
former days were better than these?

## The verdict

*That is **not a wise question**.*

The Hebrew is **ki lo mechokmah sha'alta al-zeh** — for not out of wisdom have you asked about
this.

## Why not

The Illumination gives the reason: *you ask it out of longing, not out of understanding*.

The question presents itself as analysis and is actually grief.

It also assumes its own premise. Qoheleth has already established that *there is nothing new
under the sun*, and that *no one remembers the people who lived before*. See the note on
Ecclesiastes 1:4–11.

If nothing was ever new, the old days were the same days.

## What the book has just been doing

*A good name is better than precious ointment, and **the day of death than the day of one's
birth**.*

*It is better to go to the house of mourning than to go to the house of feasting.*

*Sorrow is better than laughter, for by a sad countenance the heart is made better.* See the note
on Ecclesiastes 7:1–4.

A run of hard comparisons, and this one belongs to it.

## What the elders did

*But many of the priests and Levites and heads of the fathers' houses, **old men who had seen the
first temple, wept with a loud voice** when the foundation of this temple was laid before their
eyes.*

*So the people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping.*

## What God said to them

*"**Who is left among you who saw this temple in its former glory? And how do you see it now?**"*

And then: *"The glory of this latter temple shall be greater than the former."* See the note on
Haggai 2:6–7.

The question is granted and answered in the other direction.

## What Isaiah says

*"**Do not remember the former things, nor consider the things of old. Behold, I will do a new
thing**."* See the note on Isaiah 43:18–19.

## What Paul says he does

*Forgetting those things which are behind and reaching forward to those things which are ahead, **I
press toward the goal**.* See the note on Philippians 3:12.

> **The verdict.** *Not a wise question.* It looks like analysis and is grief, and it assumes a
> premise the book has already dismantled.
