One of the most quoted promises in Paul, and it is a thank-you note.

## Who he is writing to

The Philippians had just sent him a gift, carried by Epaphroditus, while he was in
prison.

The verses immediately before: *you sent aid once and again for my necessities…
**I have all and abound. I am full**, having received from Epaphroditus the things
sent from you.*

The promise is Paul's response to people who had given him something.

## The measure

*My God shall supply all your need **according to His riches in glory** by Christ
Jesus.*

The preposition is the whole verse. **According to**, not *out of*.

A rich man giving *out of* his wealth might hand over a coin. Giving *according to*
it means the size of the gift is set by the size of the fortune.

## "All your need"

The Greek is **chreia** — need, necessity, what is required. Not desire.

Paul had just distinguished the two in the same paragraph: *I have learned, in
whatever state I am, **to be content**… I know how to be abased, and I know how to
abound.*

The man promising supply for every need had already said he had learned to do without.

## "In glory"

The riches are located. Not in a national economy or a treasury, and not in the
generosity of the Philippians, who were poor themselves — 2 Corinthians describes the
Macedonian churches giving *in a great trial of affliction… their deep poverty
abounded in the riches of their liberality.*

## The channel

*By Christ Jesus.*

Everything Paul promises anywhere runs through that phrase.

## The doxology

*Now to **our God and Father** be glory **forever and ever**. Amen.*

He does not end the sentence with the supply. He ends it with praise, and the
possessive is *our*.

## What Paul had said four verses earlier

*I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me.*

Also usually quoted alone, and also written in a passage about contentment in
poverty. The *all things* he names in context are *being full and being hungry, both
abounding and suffering need.*

## Where he was

A Roman prison, with a case pending, writing the most joyful letter in the New
Testament. *Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice.*

> **Why the Philippians in particular.** *No church shared with me concerning giving
> and receiving but you only. For even in Thessalonica you sent aid once and again.*
> They had been funding him since the beginning — since a riverside prayer meeting and
> a purple merchant's house. See the note on Acts 16:14–15.
