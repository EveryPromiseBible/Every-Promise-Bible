key: John 20:30–31
title: "Why this book was written"
updated: 2026-08-09

**John** stops and says what the gospel is for, and every clause of the sentence is deliberate.

## What is left out

*"**There were many other signs that Jesus did in front of His disciples that are not written down in
this book.**"*

**John** records seven signs before the resurrection and says he had more. He is not writing a
chronicle; he is making a case, and the material is selected.

*And there are also many other things that Jesus did, which if they were written one by one, I
suppose that **even the world itself could not contain the books** that would be written.*

The last verse of the gospel says it again, larger.

## The purpose

*"**But these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God.**"*

Two titles, and they are the two on trial in the book. **Ho Christos** — the Anointed, which is
Israel's question. **Ho huios tou Theou** — which is the charge the Sanhedrin convicts Him on.

**John** names both and says the signs were chosen to establish them.

## The tense that is argued over

*"**That you may believe.**"*

The manuscripts divide between **pisteuete**, present — that you may go on believing — and
**pisteusete**, aorist — that you may come to believe.

The gospel works either way, and has been used for both since the second century: as an
evangelistic book and as one that steadies people who already believe.

## The result

*"**And that by believing you may have life in His name.**"*

**Zoen echete en to onomati autou.**

Not *will have* — **echete**, present. **John** uses **zoe** thirty-six times, more than the other
three gospels together, and *eternal life* in his usage begins now:

*"**Most assuredly, I say to you, he who hears My word and believes in Him who sent Me has
everlasting life, and shall not come into judgment, but has passed from death into life.**"*

*These things I have written to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, **that you may know
that you have eternal life**.*

**John**'s first letter states its purpose in almost the same words, for people who already believed
and needed to know it.

## What is immediately before it

Thomas, eight days after the resurrection, with his hand out.

*"**Because you have seen Me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have
believed.**"*

And then the purpose statement. **John** puts his readers into the sentence: they are the ones who
have not seen, and what they are given instead is a book.

> **The one condition in the sentence.** Believing. Not understanding, not qualifying, not
> improving — and the life it produces is in the present tense, in a name.
