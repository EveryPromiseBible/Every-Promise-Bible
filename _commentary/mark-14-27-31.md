Peter's most famous sentence before the courtyard, and what is striking is what
he skips over to say it.

## The promise he walks past

Jesus says two things. First, *every one of you is going to fall away tonight* —
with a quotation from Zechariah about the shepherd struck and the sheep
scattered.

Then: *but after I am raised, I will go on ahead of you into Galilee.*

That second sentence is a plan for after the failure. Before anyone has denied
anything, a meeting place has been arranged. Peter answers the first sentence and
says nothing about the second.

## What his answer reveals

*Even if all of them fall away, I will not.*

The comparison is the tell. Peter's confidence is measured against the other
eleven, in front of the other eleven. And when Jesus tells him precisely how far
he will go, he says it *again, harder*.

Mark, whose Gospel the early church associated with Peter's own preaching, keeps
all of it. Including the last line: *and every one of them said the same thing.*

## The detail Mark alone records

*Before the rooster has crowed twice.* Only Mark has the two crowings, and only
Mark later notes the first one, which Peter did not hear or did not heed.

It is the kind of detail a man remembers about the worst night of his life.

> **What happened to the arrangement.** At the empty tomb the messenger says: go
> and tell the disciples *and Peter* that He is going ahead of you into Galilee.
> The plan made before the denial stands after it, and Peter's name is added to
> the message.
