Eight words, and they are the hinge of the book.

## The line

*So **stop where you are. Give careful thought to your ways**.*

The Hebrew is **simu levavchem al-darkeichem** — set your heart upon your ways.

## The idiom

Not *think about*. **Set your heart on** — the same construction used of paying close attention,
of taking something to heart, of caring.

The Hebrew heart is the seat of thought and decision, not primarily of feeling. The instruction
is to turn the full attention onto something.

## What onto

*Your ways* — **darkeichem**.

Not their theology, their politics, or their opposition. The pattern of what they have actually
been doing.

## Where it appears

Four times in Haggai: here, in verse 7, and twice more in chapter 2 — *from this day forward,
consider it*.

Thirty-eight verses, and the same phrase four times. It is the book's refrain and its method.

## What preceded it

*"Is it truly the time for you to be living in your own panelled houses… while this house lies in
ruins?"* See the note on Haggai 1:3–4.

A question, and then the instruction to sit down with it.

## What follows it

*You have sown much, and bring in little; you eat, but do not have enough… and **he who earns
wages, earns wages to put into a bag with holes**.* See the note on Haggai 1:6.

Five observations, all of them about ordinary life, all of them verifiable by anyone standing
there.

He does not tell them what is wrong. He tells them to look at their own year and notice
something.

## What kind of preaching this is

Not new information. Every person in that crowd knew the harvest had been poor and the wages had
not stretched.

The prophet's contribution is to stop them and ask them to put the facts next to each other.

## What the pause is for

*Stop where you are.*

The excuse in verse 2 was about timing — *the time has not come yet*. See the note on
Haggai 1:2.

An excuse about timing is answered by an instruction to halt.

## The result

*Then Zerubbabel… Joshua… **with all the remnant of the people, obeyed the voice of the LORD their
God**.*

*And they came and worked on the house of the LORD of hosts.*

Twenty-three days between the sermon and the first tool lifted.

## What God says in the gap

*"**I am with you**," says the LORD.*

Spoken after the obedience began and before anything was built.

## What David says about the same examination

*Search me, O God, and know my heart; try me, and know my anxieties; **and see if there is any
wicked way in me**, and lead me in the way everlasting.* See the note on
Psalms 139:23–24.

The same request, made in the first person.

## What Paul says

*But **let a man examine himself**, and so let him eat of the bread and drink of the cup.* See the
note on 1 Corinthians 11:27–29.

> **The idiom.** **Set your heart upon your ways** — not think about them. Turn the whole
> attention onto the pattern of what you have actually been doing.
