Peter asks Him to leave, and He stays and gives him a job.

## What had just happened

A night of catching nothing, a borrowed boat used as a pulpit, and then *launch out into
the deep and let down your nets for a catch*.

Peter's answer was *Master, we have toiled all night and caught nothing; nevertheless at
Your word I will let down the net*.

The nets broke, and both boats began to sink.

## The reaction

*When Simon Peter saw it, he **went down on his knees in front of Jesus**.*

Not in the water, not at a distance. In the boat, in the fish, on his knees.

## The sentence

*"**Go away from me, Lord. I am a sinful man**."*

The Greek is **exelthe ap' emou** — depart from me.

Two things in one breath: *Lord*, and *go away*. He has just correctly identified who is in
the boat with him, and his instinct is distance.

## Why

*He was **shaken to the core** by the size of the catch.*

The Greek is **thambos** — amazement, astonishment, a shock.

Nothing had been said about Peter's sins. A fisherman was shown a boatload of fish, and
what surfaced was what he was.

## Who else was there

*And so were the others with him, including **James and John, the sons of Zebedee**, who
were **Simon's partners** in the fishing.*

The Greek is **koinonoi** — partners, sharers. A business.

## The answer

*"**Do not be afraid**," He said to Simon.*

Not *you are right to feel that*, and not *you are not a sinful man*.

He does not correct the self-assessment and He does not leave.

## The commission

*"**From now on you will be catching people**."*

The Greek is **zogron** — to take alive. Literally *catching men alive*.

A fisherman kills what he catches. The verb He picks is the one that does not.

## What they did

*So they **brought the boats up onto the shore**, **left everything lying there**, and
**went with Him**.*

The biggest catch of their lives, on the beach, walked away from.

## The other boat, later

*"Children, do you have anything to eat?"* — and again the net on the other side, and again
more fish than the net should hold. See the note on John 21:4–8.

Peter's response the second time is the opposite of this one: he puts his coat on and jumps
in the water toward Him.

Between the two mornings sits a courtyard fire and a denial.

## What Luke does with the fear

The line *do not be afraid* is Luke's refrain. Zacharias at the altar, Mary at the
annunciation, the shepherds in the field, Jairus at the news of his daughter.

Every time, it is said to somebody who has just seen something.

> **What He does with the request.** *Depart from me.* He answers by telling him not to be
> afraid, hands him a life's work, and then Peter leaves the boat to follow the man he had
> asked to leave.
