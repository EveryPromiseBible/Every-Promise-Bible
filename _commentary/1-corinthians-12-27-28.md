The conclusion of the body chapter, and the list at the end is not what a modern reader expects.

## What the chapter has argued

The body. One body, many members, and none of them able to resign.

*If the foot should say, "Because I am not a hand, I am not of the body," is it therefore not of
the body?*

*And if the ear should say, "Because I am not an eye, I am not of the body," is it therefore not
of the body?*

Two complaints, both from parts that feel second-rate, and both answered.

## The other direction

*And the eye cannot say to the hand, "I have no need of you"; nor again the head to the feet, "I
have no need of you."*

The first pair is inferiority. The second is contempt. The chapter addresses both.

## The unpresentable parts

*No, much rather, those members of the body which seem to be weaker are necessary. And those
parts of the body which we think to be less honourable, **on these we bestow greater honour**;
and our unpresentable parts have greater modesty.*

*But God composed the body, **giving greater honour to that part which lacks it**.*

The argument runs the opposite way to instinct. The parts nobody sees get more covering, not
less care.

## The purpose

*That there should be **no schism in the body**, but that the members should **have the same care
for one another**.*

The Greek is **schisma** — a tear, a split.

## The consequence

*And if one member suffers, **all the members suffer with it**; or if one member is honoured,
**all the members rejoice with it**.*

## The identification

*Now **you are the body of Christ**, and each of you is a part of it.*

Said to Corinth. The congregation with four factions, a lawsuit problem, drunkenness at the
table and chaos in the meetings.

He does not say *you should be*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 1:1–3.

## The list

*And God has appointed these in the church.*

The Greek is **etheto** — set, placed, appointed.

*First **apostles**, second **prophets**, third **teachers**.*

Numbered, and only these three.

*After that **miracles**, then **gifts of healings**, those who **lend a hand**, those who
**steer things**, different kinds of **tongues**.*

Unnumbered from here.

## The two in the middle

**Antilempseis** — helps, and it occurs once in the New Testament. The word for taking hold of
something to support it.

**Kuberneseis** — administrations, and also once. From the word for a ship's helmsman, and the
root of *governor* and *cybernetics*.

Helping and steering, listed among the gifts, between miracles and tongues.

## Where tongues comes

Last.

Corinth had made it first. The whole of chapter 14 is Paul reordering the meeting, and the
placement here is part of the argument.

## The questions

*Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? Are all workers of miracles?*

Seven rhetorical questions, and the expected answer to every one is no.

## The last line

*But **earnestly desire the best gifts**. And yet **I show you a more excellent way**.*

Which is chapter 13, and the first thing it weighs is tongues. See the note on
1 Corinthians 13:1–3.

> **What is in the middle of the list.** Helping and steering. Two words that occur once each in
> the New Testament, set between miracles and tongues, and neither of them looks like a gift.
