The first decree cannot be revoked. So a second one is written, and it does not
cancel the first — it answers it.

## The problem

Persian law, once sealed with the king's ring, stands. The king says so himself
in the verse before: *what is written in the king's name and sealed with the
king's ring, no one can revoke.*

The order to destroy every Jew on the thirteenth of Adar is still law. Haman is
dead; his decree is not.

## The answer

*By these letters the king granted the Jews in every city **the right to gather
and defend their lives**.*

Not an amnesty. A counter-decree, of equal force, issued through the same
machinery.

## Written the same way, deliberately

Set the two side by side and the writer's care is obvious. Every element of
Haman's decree in chapter 3 is repeated here:

- the king's scribes summoned
- to a hundred and twenty-seven provinces, India to Cush
- each province in its own script, each people in its own language
- written in the name of the king, sealed with the king's signet ring
- sent by couriers, hurried
- for the same single day — the thirteenth of Adar
- with the same clause about *their little ones and their women*
- and the same right to take *the goods of their enemies as plunder*

Word for word, structure for structure. The same ring, the same road, the same
date.

The one difference is who is holding the pen. See the note on Esther 3:12–15.

## "The right to stand"

The Hebrew is to stand for their life — to make a stand. It is defensive
language throughout.

They are not told to go on the offensive on any other day. One day, the day
already fixed against them, and on that day they may stand.

## Eight months' notice

The letters go out in Sivan, the third month. The day is in Adar, the twelfth.

Haman's lot fell on a date eleven months away, and that delay is what gives the
second decree time to reach India.

## The plunder they did not take

The right to take the enemy's goods is granted here in so many words. Chapter 9
then reports, three separate times, that *they did not lay a hand on the
plunder.*

It was theirs by law and they left it. Whatever the day was about, it was not
about that.

> **What one signet ring did twice.** The same ring sealed the sentence and the
> deliverance. Nothing about the empire changed, nothing about the law changed, and
> the king never learned whose hand was on any of it.
