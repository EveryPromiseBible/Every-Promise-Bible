He asks about the money he has already spent, and gets an answer about a different account.

## What he had done

Amaziah counted his own army — three hundred thousand men — and decided it was not enough.

*He also hired **one hundred thousand mighty men of valour from Israel** for one hundred talents of
silver.*

## What he was told

*"O king, **do not let the army of Israel go with you**, for the LORD is not with Israel."*

A prophet arrives before the campaign starts and tells him to send them home.

## The question

*"But **what about the hundred talents I have already paid** to the troops of Israel?"*

The Hebrew is **umah la'asot leme'ah hakkikkar**.

## What the question is

Not defiance. He accepts the instruction and asks about the sunk cost.

A hundred talents of silver is an enormous sum — roughly three and a half tons.

## The answer

*"**The LORD is able to give you much more than this**."*

The Hebrew is **yesh laYHWH latet leka harbeh mizzeh** — there is to the LORD to give you much more
than this.

## What the answer does not do

It does not tell him he will get the hundred talents back.

It changes the subject from the loss to the one who has more.

## What he does

*So Amaziah **set apart the men who had come to him from Ephraim and sent them home**.*

He obeys.

## What it costs immediately

*And **they burned with anger against Judah and went back home in a fierce rage**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyichar appam me'od** — their anger burned exceedingly.

They go on to raid the cities of Judah on the way back, killing three thousand people.

The obedience costs him the money and then costs him more.

## What happens in the campaign

He wins. Ten thousand of Edom's men in the Valley of Salt, and ten thousand more.

## What he does next

*It was so, after Amaziah came from the slaughter of the Edomites, that **he brought the gods of the
people of Seir, set them up to be his gods, and bowed down before them**.*

He carries home the gods of the army he has just defeated, and worships them.

The prophet's question is unanswerable: *why have you sought the gods of the people, **which could
not rescue their own people from your hand**?*

## The chronicler's verdict on him

*And he did what was right in the sight of the LORD, **but not with a loyal heart**.*

The Hebrew is **raq lo belevav shalem** — only not with a whole heart.

The verdict is given at the start of his reign, and the chapter proves it out.

## Where the phrase goes

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us… **much more** then, having now been justified by His
blood, we shall be saved from wrath through Him.* See the note on Romans 5:9–11.

Paul uses the same construction five times in one chapter.

## The other man who was told to let go first

*"**Sell all that you have** and distribute to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and
come, follow Me."* See the note on Luke 18:18–23.

He was sad, and did not.

> **The answer he got.** Not *you will get it back* — *the LORD is able to give you much more than
> this*. The reply moves the question off the loss and onto who is holding the rest.
