The only person in the Gospels who comes to Jesus and leaves worse off, and Mark
records a detail the others do not.

## How he arrives

*A man came **running up** and **dropped to his knees** in front of Him.*

Running and kneeling. Mark alone has both.

Nothing about his approach is grudging. He is eager, public, and respectful.

## The question

*"Good Teacher, **what must I do** to inherit eternal life?"*

The question has a verb in it that decides the whole conversation: **do**.

An inheritance is not something you do anything for. He asks how to earn one.

## The first answer

*"Why do you call Me good? **No one is good except God alone.**"*

Not a denial. A question about what the man meant by the word he used.

## The list

*"Do not commit adultery, do not murder, do not steal, do not lie about anyone, **do
not cheat anyone**, honour your father and your mother."*

Six commandments, and only from the second table — the ones about other people.

*Do not cheat anyone* is not in the ten. It is closer to *do not covet*, and Mark
alone has it in this form. Coveting is the one commandment nobody can verify from
outside.

## His reply

*"**I have kept every one of these since I was a boy.**"*

He is not being flippant. Externally, it may well have been true.

## The line only Mark has

*Then Jesus **looked straight at him, and He loved him**.*

Matthew and Luke both tell this story and neither includes it.

Whatever follows is not a rebuke. Mark tells you the expression on His face first.

## The one thing

*"**There is one thing you are missing.** Go and sell everything you own, and give the
money to the poor… **Then come, take up the cross, and follow Me**."*

He asks for what the man will not give — and there is no evidence He asks anyone else
in the Gospels for this.

Zacchaeus, a chapter later in Luke, gives away half voluntarily and is not asked for
anything. See the note on Luke 19:1–10.

## What happened

*At those words **the man's face fell**, and he walked away with a heavy heart,
**because he owned a great deal**.*

The Greek for *face fell* is used elsewhere of the sky darkening before a storm.

He came running and went away slowly.

## What Jesus says afterwards

*"How hard it is for those who have riches to enter the kingdom of God!" And the
disciples were astonished.*

They were astonished because wealth was read as a sign of blessing. When they ask
*who then can be saved?*, the answer is the point of the whole passage:

*"**With men it is impossible, but not with God; for with God all things are
possible.**"*

> **What he asked for and what he was offered.** He asked what to *do* to inherit. He
> was offered *treasure in heaven* and *come, follow Me* — and both are things you
> receive. The only sentence in the exchange with a *do* in it was his.
