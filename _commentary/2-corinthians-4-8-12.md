Four pairs, and the second half of each one is what makes the sentence.

## The four

*We are **pressed hard from every direction, but never squeezed into a corner with no way
out**.*

The Greek is **thlibomenoi** (pressed, squeezed) against **stenochoroumenoi** (confined in a
narrow space). Two words from the same field, and the second is denied.

*We are **at a loss, but never at a loss for hope**.*

The Greek is a deliberate pun: **aporoumenoi** but not **exaporoumenoi** — perplexed but not
utterly perplexed.

Paul had used the second word about himself one chapter earlier, about Asia: *we despaired
even of living*. See the note on 2 Corinthians 1:8–11.

*We are **hunted, but never left on our own**.*

**Diokomenoi** — pursued, persecuted — but not **egkataleipomenoi**, abandoned.

*We are **knocked to the ground, but never finished off**.*

**Kataballomenoi** — thrown down — but not **apollumenoi**, destroyed.

## What is granted

Everything in the first half of each pair. Paul concedes the pressure, the confusion, the
pursuit and the falling.

The list is not a denial that any of it happens.

## The carrying

*Wherever we go we **carry in our body the dying of the Lord Jesus**.*

The Greek is **nekrosin** — the putting to death, the process of dying.

## The purpose

*So that **the life of Jesus may be seen in our body too**.*

Paul gives the reason for the whole list, and it is display.

## The restatement

*For we who are alive are **handed over to death again and again for Jesus' sake**, so that
**the life of Jesus may be seen in this mortal flesh of ours**.*

He says it twice, and the second version specifies **mortal flesh** — the ordinary, dying
body.

## The last line

*So **death is at work in us, and life in you**.*

The Greek is **energeitai** — is operating, is at work.

It is one of the strangest sentences Paul wrote. The two clauses are set against each
other and he does not soften the trade.

## What he had listed elsewhere

*In labours more abundant, in stripes above measure, in prisons more frequently, in deaths
often. From the Jews five times I received forty stripes minus one. Three times I was
beaten with rods, once I was stoned, three times I was shipwrecked.*

The four pairs are not rhetorical.

## What comes before

*But **we have this treasure in earthen vessels**, that the excellence of the power may be of
God and not of us.*

Clay pots, and the whole point is that nobody credits the container.

## What comes after

*Therefore we do not lose heart. Even though our outward man is perishing, yet **the inward
man is being renewed day by day**.*

*For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, is working for us **a far more
exceeding and eternal weight of glory**.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 4:16–18.

**Light affliction** — written by the man who had just listed the stripes.

## The word for the glory

**Baros** — weight. Paul weighs one against the other, and calls his own list light.

> **What each pair does.** Concedes the first thing and denies the second. Pressed, yes.
> Cornered, no. Paul never argues that the pressure is imaginary — he argues about where
> it stops.
