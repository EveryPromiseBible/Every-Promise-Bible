Paul warns against something and then, instead of arguing point by point, states
a fact that makes the alternative unnecessary.

## The warning

*Take care that no one carries you off as his prize.*

The verb is used of kidnapping — carrying someone off as plunder. Not persuading;
taking.

And what does the carrying: *philosophy and hollow deceit… traditions handed down
by men… the elementary lessons the world teaches.* Colossae was being sold a
package of rules, visions and observances as an upgrade.

## The fullness

*For in Him all the fullness of what God is lives in bodily form.*

**Pleroma** — fullness, the whole content. The word was in use among the
teachers Paul is opposing, and he takes it off them: the fullness is not spread
across a hierarchy of spiritual beings. It is in Christ, and it is *bodily*.

## The sentence that ends the argument

*And in Him **you have been made complete**.*

The Greek uses the same root. The fullness is in Him; you have been filled full in
Him. Perfect tense — done, and standing done.

That is why the warning works. Anyone offering an addition is offering to add to
something already complete. Nothing on the market can be a next step from here.

## The last clause

*He who is the head over every ruler and every authority.*

The powers the false teaching was anxious about — angelic hierarchies, elemental
spirits — are put under the same head the reader is joined to.

> **What Paul does not do.** He never refutes the philosophy in detail. He states
> what Christ is and what the reader has in Him, and lets the offer collapse under
> the comparison. It is the same move as Ephesians 1: prayer that they would see
> what they already have, rather than instruction on getting more.

## The verse before this one

The warning does not stand on its own. Paul has just written *just as you received
Christ Jesus the Lord, keep on walking in Him* — rooted, built up, made firm.

That is what makes the kidnapping possible to resist. A man who is rooted can be
approached by a persuasive teacher and stay where he is. See the note on
Colossians 2:4–7.
