The first of two test questions, and the priests get it right.

## The question

*"If someone carries **consecrated meat in the fold of his garment**, and that fold touches some
bread or stew, some wine or oil or any other food, **does the food become holy?**"*

The Hebrew is **besar-qodesh** — holy flesh. Meat from a sacrifice, which the priests were
entitled to eat.

**Bikenaph bigdo** — in the wing, the corner, the fold of his garment. The loose fold of an outer
robe used as a carrier.

## Why the question is not hypothetical

Priests carried sacrificial meat home this way. It happened daily.

Haggai picks an ordinary scene from the questioner's own working life.

## The chain

Holy meat → the fold of a garment → bread, stew, wine, oil.

Two removes. The meat touches the cloth; the cloth touches the food.

## The answer

*The priests answered, "**No**."*

Correct. Leviticus 6 says *whatever touches its flesh must be holy*, which covers the first
contact — but the transfer stops there. Holiness does not pass at second hand.

## What the law does say

*Whatever touches the altar shall be holy*, and *whatever touches them shall be holy*, of the
consecrated vessels.

Direct contact, yes. Contact with something that touched something, no.

## What the second question asks

*"If someone made unclean by a dead body touches any of these things, **does it become
unclean?**"*

*"**Yes. It becomes unclean**."* See the note on Haggai 2:13.

## The asymmetry stated plainly

Under that arrangement, contamination is transmissible and consecration is not.

Holiness has a range of one. Defilement does not.

## What Haggai does with it

*"So is this people, and so is this nation before Me… **and what they offer there is unclean**."*

The people had been sacrificing on a rebuilt altar for years while the house lay in ruins. The
ruling says those offerings were not making the rest of their work holy.

The direction of travel was the other way.

## The garment fold

**Kanaph** — wing, corner, hem.

The same word in Malachi: *the Sun of Righteousness shall arise **with healing in His wings***.
See the note on Malachi 4:1–3.

And the word for the corner of a garment where the tassels were commanded to be worn.

## What happens to a garment's hem in Mark

*She had heard about Jesus, and came behind Him in the crowd **and touched His garment**.*

An unclean woman, twelve years unclean, reaching for a hem in a crowd — and *immediately the
fountain of her blood was dried up*. See the note on Mark 5:25–29.

Under this ruling, that contact should have run the other way.

## The other direction

*Then He came and touched the open coffin*, of the widow's son at Nain. And *He took the child by
the hand*, of Jairus's daughter. And *Jesus put out His hand and touched him*, of the leper.

Three contacts that should have defiled Him, and each time the traffic goes the wrong way.

## What Paul says about the same asymmetry

*For **the unbelieving husband is sanctified by the wife**… otherwise your children would be
unclean, but now they are holy.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 7:12–14.

Which is the reverse of the priests' ruling, stated as a fact about a household.

> **The answer.** *No.* Holiness had a reach of one contact; uncleanness did not — and the priests
> stated their own law correctly before Haggai turned it on them.
