Elisha's last recorded act, and it ends in anger.

## The setting

*Now Elisha had fallen sick — **the illness that would take his life**.*

The prophet who raised a boy, cured Naaman, floated an axe head and fed a hundred men from
twenty loaves, dies of an illness in bed.

## Who comes

*And **Jehoash king of Israel** came down to him, **weeping over him**.*

A king of the northern kingdom, of whom the record says *he did evil in the sight of the
LORD*.

He weeps anyway.

## What he says

*"**My father! My father! The chariots and horsemen of Israel!**"*

The exact words Elisha himself had shouted when Elijah went up in the whirlwind, watching
the chariot of fire.

Jehoash is quoting the man in the bed back at him.

The phrase means: you are the real defence of this country. Not the army.

## The bow

*"**Get a bow and some arrows**." The king took them up.*

*"**Now draw the bow**," Elisha told him, and the king drew it.*

## The hands

*And Elisha **laid his own hands over the king's hands**.*

A dying man's hands on the hands of a king who is doing the pulling.

The strength in the draw is Jehoash's. The hands on top are Elisha's.

## The window

*"**Open the east window**," he said, and the king swung it open.*

East is where Aram is. Damascus, and the territory Israel had been losing for two
generations.

*"**Shoot!**" And he shot.*

One arrow, out of a window, at nothing.

## The declaration

*"**The LORD's arrow of victory! The arrow of victory over Aram!** You will strike Aram at
Aphek until you have finished them off."*

The Hebrew is *the arrow of the LORD's deliverance* — **teshuah**.

The word is unqualified. Nothing is conditional in this sentence.

## The second instruction

*"**Take the arrows**." The king took them. "**Strike the ground**."*

No number is given, and no explanation.

## What he did

*He struck it **three times and stopped**.*

The Hebrew is *and he stopped*.

He was not told when to stop. He decided.

## The anger

*And **the man of God was angry with him**.*

The one time in the Elisha narratives that he is angry with a king who has come to him
respectfully.

## The verdict

*"**You should have struck five or six times**," he said. "Then you would have **beaten Aram
down completely. But now you will only defeat Aram three times**."*

The outcome is set by the number of strikes on a floor.

Three victories are recorded later in the chapter: *Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz recaptured
from Ben-Hadad the son of Hazael the cities… **three times Jehoash defeated him** and
recovered the cities of Israel.*

Exactly three. The account keeps the count.

## What the test was

Nobody told him to be enthusiastic. The instruction was *strike the ground*, and the number
was left open.

The floor-striking was the measure, and he had no idea he was being measured.

## What happens after Elisha dies

Raiders throw a body into his tomb in a hurry. *When the man was let down and touched the
bones of Elisha, he revived and stood on his feet.*

The last miracle in his ministry happens after he is dead.

## The other open-ended vessel

*Bring vessels — **empty vessels; do not gather just a few**.* And the oil stops when the
last jar is full. See the note on 2 Kings 4:1–7.

Same prophet, same principle: the supply matched the number of containers, and the number of
containers was the borrower's decision.

> **What ended the sequence.** Not the arrows running out, and not an instruction. He
> stopped, and the record of the wars afterwards counts exactly three.
