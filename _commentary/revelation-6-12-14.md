key: Revelation 6:12–14
title: "The question the sixth seal asks"
updated: 2026-08-09

The sky comes apart, and then the passage does something unexpected: it stops describing and asks a
question.

## What John reaches for

*The sun went black, **the black of rough woven haircloth**. The whole moon turned the color of
blood.*

**Sakkos** — sackcloth, the coarse goat-hair fabric people wore in mourning. The sun does not merely
go dark; it goes the colour of grief.

And the stars fall *the way a fig tree drops its unripe fruit when a gale gets hold of it* — an image
from a hillside, used for the collapse of the heavens. John keeps reaching for ordinary things
because there is nothing else to reach for.

*The sky **peeled back and rolled itself up**.* Isaiah had said it: *all the host of heaven shall be
dissolved, and the heavens shall be rolled up like a scroll.*

## The phrase that should not exist

The passage runs on to what the survivors say, and it contains three words that no other religion
could produce:

*Hide us from the face of Him who sits on the throne, and from **the wrath of the Lamb**.*

A lamb has no wrath. That is the entire point of a lamb — it is the animal chosen for sacrifice
precisely because it does not fight back, and John's first sight of Him in heaven was *a Lamb as
though it had been slain*.

The one thing in the universe that could make a lamb terrifying is what was done to it. This is not
wrath despite the sacrifice; it is wrath because a sacrifice was made and refused.

## The question

*For the great day of His wrath has come, and **who is able to stand**?*

The chapter asks it and does not answer. The question is left hanging at the end of chapter six.

It is not new. It was asked once before, by frightened men at Beth Shemesh looking at the ark: *who
is able to stand before this holy LORD God?* Scripture keeps asking and keeps declining to answer on
the spot.

## Where the answer is

Turn the page. The very next scene is a crowd *out of every nation and every family line and every
people and every language*, and the first thing said about them is that they are ***standing** in
front of the throne and in front of the Lamb*.

They are the answer, and the reason they can stand is given four verses later: *they washed their
robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.*

> **The same Lamb, twice.** In one chapter men are hiding from His wrath; in the next a countless
> crowd is standing in front of Him in white. The difference between the two groups is not their
> record — it is what they did with the blood. *Who is able to stand?* Chapter seven answers with a
> multitude nobody could count.
