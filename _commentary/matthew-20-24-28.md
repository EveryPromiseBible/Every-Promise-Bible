Two brothers have just asked for the best seats, the other ten are furious about
it, and Jesus responds by redefining the whole idea of position.

## What He says about the world

*You know how it works among the nations. Their rulers throw their weight around,
and the great ones make their authority felt.*

Both verbs in the Greek are compounds with *kata* — lording it **down** over,
exercising authority **against**. The preposition does the work: it is power
exerted downward onto people.

Then: *it must not be that way among you.*

## The inversion

*Whoever wants to be great among you must be the one who serves you.*

Notice He does not say wanting to be great is wrong. He does not rebuke the
ambition — He redirects it. The route to greatness is not blocked; it runs the
other way from what everyone assumes.

The word for servant is **diakonos**, the ordinary word for someone who waits at
a table.

## The reason

*Just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His
life as a ransom for many.*

The pattern is not offered as a principle but as a biography. He is describing
what He is doing, in a chapter where He has just told them for the third time
that He is going to Jerusalem to be killed.

**Ransom** is *lutron* — the price paid to release a captive or free a slave. It
is the noun behind the redemption word in Ephesians 1:7.

> **What the brothers had actually asked for.** Seats at His right and left in His
> kingdom. When Matthew's Gospel finally shows two people at His right and left,
> it is at the crucifixion, and they are criminals. The Gospel answers the request
> without comment, several chapters later.
