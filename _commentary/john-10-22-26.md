John gives the season, the feast and the exact colonnade.

## The feast

*It was winter, and **the Festival of Dedication** had come to Jerusalem.*

**Hanukkah.** Not one of the feasts commanded in Leviticus — it commemorates the
cleansing of the temple by Judas Maccabeus in 164 BC, after Antiochus Epiphanes had
defiled it by sacrificing a pig on the altar.

Eight days, in the ninth month, with lamps.

## What the feast is about

A defiled temple, cleaned out and rededicated.

The same thing Hezekiah did in sixteen days, seven centuries earlier. See the note on
2 Chronicles 29:12–17.

## "It was winter"

John says it and he does not need to. Hanukkah is always in winter.

He tends to do this — *and it was night*, when Judas goes out — and readers have long
taken the weather as part of the scene.

## The place

*Jesus was walking in the temple courts, in **Solomon's Colonnade**.*

A covered portico along the east side of the outer court, sheltered from the winter
weather. It was where people gathered when it was cold.

The early church met there daily after Pentecost, and the healed man at the Beautiful
Gate ran into it holding on to Peter and John. See the note on Acts 3:9–11.

## The question

*"**How long are You going to keep us in suspense? If You are the Messiah, tell us
plainly**."*

The Greek is *how long do You lift up our soul?* — hold us in tension.

The demand is for a plain statement, and it sounds reasonable.

## The answer

*"**I did tell you, and you do not believe. The works I do in My Father's name — those
are the witness, and they speak for Me**."*

Two answers: He has already said it, and the works are saying it.

John's Gospel has recorded the works — water into wine, the official's son, the man at
the pool, the five thousand, the walking on water, and a man born blind, one chapter
back.

## The reason given

*"**But you do not believe, because you are not My sheep**, just as I said to you."*

He does not say they are not His sheep because they do not believe. The order in the
sentence runs the other way.

He had said it in the same chapter: *My sheep hear My voice, and I know them, and they
follow Me.* See the note on John 10:16–18.

## What He says next

*"**I and My Father are one**."*

And they pick up stones. When He asks which good work they are stoning Him for, the
answer is: *for blasphemy, and because You, being a Man, make Yourself God.*

They asked Him to tell them plainly, and He did.

## What is not in dispute

Not the works. The charge they bring is about who He is saying He is, and both sides
agree about what He said.

> **What the feast in the background is.** A rededication — a building cleared out and
> given back to God. He walks through it in winter and tells them the works are already
> speaking.
