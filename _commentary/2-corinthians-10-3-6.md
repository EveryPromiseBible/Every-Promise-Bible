Military language from start to finish, and Paul names the target so there is no
guessing.

## The setting

Paul is defending himself against critics at Corinth who said his letters were
weighty but his presence was unimpressive. The whole chapter is about that
quarrel.

So the warfare he describes is not abstract spiritual combat. It is what he is
doing in the letter — dealing with what people were saying and thinking about the
gospel and about him.

## What gets pulled down

*We pull down **arguments**, and every high thing that lifts itself up against
the knowledge of God.*

- **arguments** — reasonings, calculations, the conclusions people reach
- **every high thing** — the word for a rampart or a proud elevation, something
  raised up

The fortresses in this passage are made of ideas. That is what the weapons are
for.

## "Take every thought prisoner"

The image completes the siege: the walls come down, and then the occupants are
led out as captives.

*And bring it into the obedience of Christ.* The destination is not destruction —
it is submission. A captured thought is brought somewhere and put to work.

## The weapons

*Not the weapons of the flesh at all; through God they have power to pull down
fortresses.*

Paul does not list them here, and the omission is deliberate; the point is what
they are not. In Ephesians he does list them, and the offensive one is the word
of God.

> **The verse people skip.** *We are holding ourselves ready to punish every act
> of disobedience, once your own obedience has been brought to the full.* Paul
> deals with his own house first. The order matters — the readiness to address
> others waits on the readiness of the people he is writing to.
