Three verses, and everything in them is a costume.

## What Pilate does

*Then Pilate took Jesus and **had Him flogged**.*

The Greek is **emastigosen** — scourged.

Roman flogging used a **flagrum**, leather thongs weighted with bone or metal. Jewish law
limited a beating to forty strokes and the practice was thirty-nine; Rome had no limit.

John places it in the middle of the trial rather than at the end. Pilate is trying to produce
something pitiful enough to satisfy the crowd — *I will therefore chastise Him and release
Him*.

It does not work.

## The crown

*The soldiers **twisted together a crown of thorns** and set it on His head.*

The Greek is **plexantes stephanon ex akanthon** — having plaited a wreath out of thorns.

**Stephanos** is the victor's wreath at the games, not the royal **diadema**. See the note on
1 Peter 5:4.

They are mocking a triumph, not a coronation.

## Where thorns come from

*Cursed is the ground for your sake… **both thorns and thistles it shall bring forth for
you**.* See the note on Genesis 3:17–19.

The first thing the ground produces after the curse.

And in the parable of the sower, thorns are what chokes the word — *the cares of this world,
the deceitfulness of riches*. See the note on Luke 8:11–15.

## The robe

*And they put **a purple robe** on Him.*

Purple was imperial. The dye came from murex shellfish and was ruinously expensive; Roman law
restricted who could wear it.

Mark says scarlet, which is what a soldier's cloak was. Either way it is a discarded military
cloak being used as a costume.

## The greeting

*And they **kept saying, "Hail, King of the Jews!"**.*

The Greek is an imperfect — they went on saying it.

**Chaire** — the formal salutation. *Ave, Caesar* is the same word in Latin.

## The blows

*And they **struck Him with their hands**.*

The Greek is **rapismata** — blows with the flat of the hand, slaps.

Matthew adds the reed sceptre put into His hand and then taken back to hit Him with, and the
kneeling.

## What the whole scene is

A mock investiture. Crown, robe, sceptre, salute, homage.

The soldiers assembled every element of a coronation and got every one of them right in form
and wrong in intent.

## What Pilate does with it

Brings Him out: *behold the Man!*

And later, from the judgment seat: *behold your King!* — and the chief priests answer *we have
no king but Caesar*. See the note on John 19:12–16.

## The one thing they were not wrong about

The title.

Pilate writes it and refuses to change it: *Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews*, in three
languages.

*What I have written, I have written.*

## Where the wreath goes

*And I looked, and behold, a white horse. He who sat on it had a bow; and **a crown was given
to him**.*

And at the end: *on His head were **many crowns** — **diademata**, the royal ones.

The wreath in this passage is **stephanos**. The crowns at the return are the other word.

## What Isaiah had said about the face

*His visage was marred more than any man.* And *I gave My back to those who struck Me, and My
cheeks to those who plucked out the beard; **I did not hide My face from shame and
spitting**.*

See the note on Isaiah 53:1–3.

> **What the soldiers assembled.** Crown, robe, sceptre and salute — a complete coronation, put
> together as a joke, out of a thornbush and a cast-off cloak.
