Paul writes to a church he had to leave in a hurry, and tells them their reputation
arrived before he could.

## The sound

*The word of the Lord **rang out from you**.*

The Greek is **exechetai** — from the root behind *echo*. It is used of a trumpet
sounding, or thunder rolling out.

## How far

*And **not only through Macedonia and Achaia**; your trust in God has **gone out
everywhere**.*

Macedonia and Achaia are the two Roman provinces of Greece. Then Paul widens it to *every
place*.

Thessalonica sat on the Via Egnatia, the main road from the Adriatic to Byzantium, and it
had a busy port. What was said there travelled.

## Paul's position

*So that **there is nothing left for us to say about it**.*

He arrives somewhere to talk about the Thessalonians and finds the story already told.

## Who is reporting

*People **tell us themselves** what kind of welcome we found among you.*

Strangers are telling Paul about his own visit.

## The two verbs

*And how you **turned away from your idols** and **turned to God**.*

One movement with two directions, and Paul puts them in that order.

The Greek for *turned* is **epestrepsate** — the ordinary word for turning round.

## What God is called

*To serve **the God who is living and real**.*

The Greek is *the living and true God* — **alethinos**, genuine as against a copy.

Said to people who had just stopped serving statues.

## The two things they do now

*To **serve** the God who is living and real, and to **wait for His Son from heaven**.*

Serving and waiting, together.

Both Thessalonian letters are dominated by the return of Christ — every chapter of the
first one ends on it — and Paul had been with them only a few weeks.

## The Son described

*The Son He **raised from the dead**, Jesus, who **rescues us from the wrath that is
coming**.*

The Greek for *rescues* is **rhuomenon** — present participle, the one who is rescuing.
It is the same verb Paul uses of his own deliverance in Asia. See the note on
2 Corinthians 1:8–11.

## What their welcome had cost

Acts 17: three sabbaths in the synagogue, then a mob, then Jason's house attacked, then
Jason dragged before the city officials and made to post bond, and Paul and Silas sent
away by night.

Weeks, and then an evacuation.

*You became followers of us and of the Lord, **having received the word in much
affliction, with joy of the Holy Spirit**.*

## What Paul says about them at the top of the letter

*Remembering without ceasing **your work of faith, labour of love, and patience of
hope**.*

The three, in that order, with a genitive attached to each.

> **What travelled.** Not a report of Paul's preaching. Paul says what went out was their
> faith — and that he keeps meeting people who tell him about it before he can open his
> mouth.
