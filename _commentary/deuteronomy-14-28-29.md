Every third year the tenth does not go to the sanctuary at all. It stays in town.

## Where it is kept

*Gather a tenth of that year's produce and **store it up in your towns**.*

Not carried to the place God chose. Left locally, where the people who need it are.

## Who eats it

Four groups, and the passage names them in order:

- **the Levite**, *who has no land of his own*
- **the foreigner**
- **the fatherless**
- **the widow**

The four categories of person in Israel with no inheritance and no legal means of
supporting themselves. A man without land in an agricultural economy had nothing.

The law names them together repeatedly — *the fatherless and the widow, and the
stranger within your gates* — and builds provision for them into the calendar.

## "Eat their fill"

*May come and **eat their fill**.*

The Hebrew is to be satisfied, to eat to satiety. Not a ration and not a token.

## The reason attached

*"**So that the LORD your God may bless you in all the work of your hands.**"*

The blessing is stated as the consequence, and it is on the work rather than on the
tithe.

## What the three tithes cover

Reading the law together, the tenth does three different jobs:

- **Numbers 18** — support for the tribe with no land
- **Deuteronomy 14:22–27** — a feast, eaten by the giver, at the sanctuary. See the
  note on that passage.
- **this one** — the poor, every third year

One of the three funds ministry. One is a celebration. One is welfare.

Anybody arguing about the tithe from the Old Testament is arguing about a system
that fed the widow and paid for a party.

## The same instinct all through the law

Gleaning: *when you reap the harvest of your land, you shall not wholly reap the
corners of your field… you shall leave them for the poor and the stranger.*

The sabbath year: whatever grows of itself is for *the poor of your people.*

The jubilee: land returns, debts release.

None of it is charity in the modern sense. It is written into how the economy runs.

> **Where it turns up in the New Testament.** *Pure and undefiled religion before God
> and the Father is this: **to visit orphans and widows in their trouble**, and to
> keep oneself unspotted from the world.* James picks two of the four groups from
> this passage and calls attending to them the definition of religion.
