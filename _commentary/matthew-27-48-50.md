Three verses, and the verb at the end of them is not the ordinary one for dying.

## The sponge

*"One of them ran and got a sponge, **soaked it in sour wine**, put it on a stick, and held it up for
Him to drink."*

**Oxos** — sour wine, the cheap ration drunk by soldiers and labourers.

*They also gave me gall for my food, and **for my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink**.*

Psalm 69:21. **John** makes the quotation explicit: *after this, Jesus, knowing that all things were
now accomplished, that the Scripture might be fulfilled, said, "I thirst."*

## What He had refused earlier

*They gave Him **sour wine mingled with gall** to drink. But when He had tasted it, **He would not
drink**.*

Nineteen verses earlier, at the start. The first cup was drugged — gall as a sedative — and He
tasted it and refused. This one is not, and He takes it.

He declines the anaesthetic and accepts the drink.

## The taunt

*"Leave Him. **Let us see whether Elijah comes to save Him.**"*

They have misheard **Eli, Eli** — *My God, My God* — as a call for Elijah. The last recorded
sentence of the bystanders is a mistake.

*"He trusted in God; **let Him deliver Him now** if He will have Him."*

## The cry

*"Then **Jesus** cried out again **with a loud voice**."*

**Krazas phone megale.**

Men dying of crucifixion die of exhaustion and asphyxia; the one thing they cannot do at the end is
shout. **Mark** records that the centurion's conclusion was drawn from exactly this: *when the
centurion saw that He cried out like this and breathed His last, he said, "Truly this Man was the
Son of God!"*

**John** records what the shout was: *"**It is finished!**"* — **tetelestai**, the word written across
a paid bill.

## The verb

*"And **gave up His spirit**."*

The Greek is **apheken to pneuma** — He released, dismissed, let go the spirit.

**Matthew** does not write *he died* and does not write *his spirit left him*. The verb is
transitive and He is its subject.

*"Therefore My Father loves Me, because I lay down My life that I may take it again. **No one takes
it from Me, but I lay it down of Myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it
again.**"*

**John** 10, and **Matthew**'s verb is that claim carried out.

## What happened in the same minute

*And behold, **the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom**, and the earth quaked, and
the rocks were split, and the graves were opened.*

The next verse. The curtain that shut the Holy of Holies is torn — and the direction is specified,
because a tear from the top is not done by hands.

*Therefore, brethren, having **boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus**, by a new and
living way which He consecrated for us, **through the veil, that is, His flesh**.*

## What the centurion said

*So when the centurion and those with him, who were guarding Jesus, saw the earthquake and the
things that had happened, they feared greatly, saying, "**Truly this was the Son of God!**"*

The first person in **Matthew** to say it after the crucifixion is the officer in charge of it.

> **The verb.** *Apheken* — He dismissed it. Matthew will not write that He died; the sentence has
> Him as the subject and the spirit as the object, which is John 10 in one word.
