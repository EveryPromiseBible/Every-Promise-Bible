The third of four rescues in the psalm, and all four have the same shape.

## The psalm's pattern

Psalm 107 tells four stories: travellers lost in a desert, prisoners in iron chains, sick
men who could not eat, and sailors in a storm.

Each one repeats the same two lines:

*Then they **cried to the LORD in their trouble**, and He **saved them out of their
distress**.*

And then: *Oh, that men would give thanks to the LORD for His goodness, and for His
wonderful works to the children of men!*

Four groups, four crises, one response, one deliverance.

## The third group

The verses before describe them: *fools, because of their transgression, and because of
their iniquities, were afflicted. **Their soul abhorred all manner of food, and they drew
near to the gates of death**.*

The psalm does not soften how they got there. It says their own way brought it.

## The cry

*Then they cried to the LORD **in their trouble**.*

Not after sorting themselves out. In it.

The Hebrew is the ordinary verb for shouting for help.

## What was sent

*He **sent out His word** and healed them.*

The Hebrew is **davar** — word, matter, thing.

Not a doctor, a remedy, or an instruction. A word, sent.

The psalm uses the same construction elsewhere for creation: *by the word of the LORD the
heavens were made.*

## The lifting

*And **lifted them clear of the pit**.*

The Hebrew is *delivered them from their destructions* — **shechitah**, the pit, the
grave.

They had drawn near the gates of death in verse 18, and this is the reverse motion.

## The two verbs

Healed, and lifted out. The psalm gives both.

## Where the sending goes

*"**He sent His word to Jacob**"*, and John's opening: *In the beginning was the Word…
**and the Word became flesh**.*

The psalm's phrase is what John's Gospel makes a person.

## What the centurion said

*"Only **speak a word**, and my servant will be healed."*

Jesus called it faith greater than any He had found in Israel — and the man's argument
was that he understood how orders work at a distance.

## The chorus

After each rescue: *Oh, that men would **give thanks** to the LORD.*

And after this one specifically: *let them **sacrifice the sacrifices of thanksgiving**,
and declare His works with rejoicing.*

## How the psalm opens

*Oh, give thanks to the LORD, for He is good! For His mercy endures forever. **Let the
redeemed of the LORD say so**, whom He has redeemed from the hand of the enemy.* See the
note on Psalms 107:1–3.

## How it ends

*Whoever is wise will **observe these things**, and they will understand the
lovingkindness of the LORD.*

> **The one thing all four groups do.** Cry out. The psalm does not distinguish between
> the ones who were lost, imprisoned, sick by their own fault, or caught in weather — it
> gives them the same sentence, four times.
