The whole fight takes one verse, and the detail worth noticing is the direction
David is moving.

## He ran at him

*As Goliath moved in to attack, David **broke into a run toward** the battle line
to meet him.*

Forty days the army had stood still while the giant shouted. The first thing that
changes is a shepherd running the wrong way.

He closes the distance himself. A sling needs room, and he gives up room to
shorten it.

## The weapons

*So David won with a sling and a stone. Without a sword in his hand.*

The narrator says it twice, which is how Hebrew underlines. He had refused
Saul's armour a few verses earlier — it was untested and he could not walk in it.

The sling was not a toy. Judges mentions seven hundred left-handed Benjaminites
who could sling a stone at a hair and not miss. It was the ranged weapon of a man
with no armour, and David had used it on lions and bears where nobody was
watching. See the note on 1 Samuel 17:34–37.

## The forehead

*He struck the Philistine on the forehead. The stone sank in.*

The one place the champion was not covered. Verse 5 describes the bronze helmet,
the coat of scale armour weighing about fifty kilos, greaves, a javelin — and a
gap.

## The sword

*Then David ran and stood over him, drew Goliath's own sword from its sheath, and
cut off his head with it.*

He finishes with the giant's own weapon. The thing everyone had been terrified of
becomes the instrument of the ending.

> **What David had already said.** *The battle is the LORD's, and He will give all
> of you into our hands.* He announced the outcome to Goliath's face before he
> picked up a stone, and the account records the running, the sling and the sword
> as the working out of a sentence already spoken.
