**Luke** gives the reason for the parable before telling it, which he rarely does.

## Why He tells it

*"**Because He was close to Jerusalem now, and because they were expecting the kingdom of God to
appear at any moment.**"*

The parable is a correction of a timetable. They think the arrival is imminent; the story is about a
long absence.

## The historical shape

*"**A nobleman set out for a distant country to have himself appointed king and then return.**"*

This is not a fable. Archelaus, Herod's son, had sailed to Rome to be confirmed as king of Judea,
and a delegation of fifty Jews followed him there to petition against it.

**Luke** keeps the detail: *but his citizens hated him and sent a delegation after him, saying, "We
do not want this man to reign over us."*

**Jesus** tells a parable using recent local politics, and the crowd would have known exactly which
journey He meant.

## What is handed out

Ten servants, ten minas, **one each**.

That is the difference from the talents in **Matthew** 25, where the amounts vary. Here everybody gets
the same, and the returns differ — ten, five, and one buried in a handkerchief.

Equal endowment and unequal outcome, where the other parable has the reverse.

## What the mina was

About three months' wages. A serious sum, and small compared with a talent.

*"**Do business till I come.**"*

**Pragmateusasthe** — trade with it. One instruction, and it covers the whole absence.

## The delay

*"**And it happened that when he returned, having received the kingdom.**"*

**Luke** writes the going and the returning into the same sentence and puts the entire parable in the
gap between them.

*"For **the kingdom of heaven is like a man travelling to a far country**."*

*The Lord is not slack concerning His promise, as some count slackness, but is **longsuffering toward
us**.*

## Where it sits

Immediately after Zacchaeus — *today salvation has come to this house* — and immediately before the
entry into Jerusalem on the colt.

*"For **the Son of Man has come to seek and to save that which was lost**."*

The verse before the parable begins. He corrects their timetable and, one sentence earlier, tells
them what He came for.

> **What Luke supplies first.** The reason. They thought the kingdom was about to appear, and the
> parable is set inside a long absence — told by a man five days from a cross, using a journey to
> Rome the crowd could remember.
