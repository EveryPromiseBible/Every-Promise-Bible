Paul opens the letter without the thing he always opens letters with.

## What is missing

Every other letter of Paul's begins with thanksgiving. *I thank my God always concerning
you*, *we give thanks to God always for you all*, *I thank my God upon every remembrance
of you.*

Even Corinth, with its factions and its lawsuits and its immorality, gets one.

Galatia gets this.

## The word

*I am **astonished**.*

The Greek is **thaumazo** — to marvel, to be amazed. It was a standard opening in
first-century letters, but used to express pleasure.

Paul takes the polite formula and turns it.

## The speed

*At **how quickly** you are walking away.*

The Greek is **tacheos** — soon, hastily.

Not that they left, but how fast.

## What they are leaving

*Walking away from **the One who called you in the grace of Christ**.*

Not from a doctrine. From a person.

Paul does not say they are deserting the gospel first. He says they are deserting the one
who called them, and the calling is described as *in grace*.

## The verb

**Metatithesthe** — to transfer, to change sides. It was used of soldiers deserting and
of a person changing political party.

And it is present tense. It is happening while he writes; it is not finished.

## What they are going to

*Walking off toward **a different good news** — **which is not another good news at
all**.*

Paul uses two different Greek words in one sentence:

- **Heteron** — another of a different kind.
- **Allo** — another of the same kind.

*A **different** gospel, which is not **another** gospel.* Different in kind, and not a
second one of the same species.

There is one, and this is not a variant of it.

## Who is behind it

*There are simply **certain people unsettling you**.*

The Greek is **tarassontes** — agitating, stirring up, throwing into confusion. It is the
word for water being troubled.

He does not name them. Later he calls them *false brethren secretly brought in*, and *he
who troubles you shall bear his judgment, whoever he is.*

## What they want

*What they want is to **twist the good news of Christ into something else**.*

The Greek is **metastrepsai** — to turn about, to reverse.

## What the twist was

Circumcision. The letter names it repeatedly, and the council in Acts 15 dealt with the
same teaching: *unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot
be saved.* See the note on Acts 15:6–9.

Nothing immoral was being proposed. An addition was.

## What he says next

*But even if we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel to you than what we
have preached to you, **let him be accursed**.*

And then he says it a second time, in the next verse, in case it was read as heat: *as we
have said before, so now I say again.*

## Where the letter lands

*It is **for freedom that Christ has made us free**. Stand fast therefore, and do not be
entangled again with a yoke of bondage.*

> **What alarms him.** Not the arrival of teachers and not an argument being lost. The
> word in the sentence is *quickly* — and what they are described as leaving is a
> person, not a position.
