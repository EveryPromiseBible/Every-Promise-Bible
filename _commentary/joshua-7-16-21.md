One man's theft, found by a narrowing process, in a valley that gets a name it does not keep.

## The narrowing

*Tribe by tribe… family by family… household by household… man by man.*

Four rounds, each one closing in. Israel stands there through all of it.

*And **Achan son of Carmi, son of Zabdi, son of Zerah, of the tribe of Judah**, was taken.*

## The tribe

Judah. The tribe of the sceptre, of David, and of the Lion — and the man whose sin stops the
whole conquest is out of it. See the note on Genesis 49:8–12.

Scripture does not tidy its genealogies.

## How Joshua speaks to him

*"**My son**, give glory to the LORD, the God of Israel, and confess to Him."*

The commanding general, whose army has just been beaten for the first time and lost
thirty-six men, calls the man responsible *my son*.

## The confession

*"I **saw** among the plunder a beautiful robe from Babylon, two hundred pieces of silver, and
a bar of gold… I **wanted** them, and I **took** them."*

Saw, wanted, took. Three verbs, in that order.

*So when the woman **saw** that the tree was good for food, that it was **pleasant to the
eyes**… **she took** of its fruit and ate.* See the note on Genesis 3:6–7.

The same three, in the same order, on the first page of the Bible.

## Where it was

*"They're buried in the ground inside my tent, **the silver underneath**."*

Under the floor of a tent in the middle of a camp that had been told everything in Jericho was
devoted.

## The name of the place

**Achor** — trouble. The valley is named for what happened in it.

## What is done with that name

*"I will give her her vineyards from there, and **the Valley of Achor as a door of hope**. She
shall sing there, as in the days of her youth."*

Hosea, seven centuries later, takes the worst place-name in the conquest narrative and makes
it the doorway.

The valley of trouble is not renamed. It is left called trouble, and turned into a door.

## What one man's act did, and what one man's act did

*For as by **one man's disobedience** many were made sinners, so also by **one Man's obedience**
many will be made righteous.*

Israel lost a battle because of one man in a tent. Paul builds the same arithmetic and runs it
the other way.

> **The name that was kept.** Achor — trouble. Hosea does not clear it. He calls it a door of
> hope and leaves the name on it.
