The longest description in the book, and it starts with the same sentence twice.

## The opening

*How beautiful you are, my love, **how beautiful you are**.*

The Hebrew is **hinnak yaphah ra'yati hinnak yaphah** — behold, you are fair; behold, you are fair.

He says it, and then says it again, before any detail.

## What she had said about herself

*I am dark, but lovely… **do not look upon me, because I am dark, because the sun has tanned me**.*

*My mother's sons were angry with me; they made me the keeper of the vineyards.*

Her first statement about her own appearance is an apology, with a reason: outdoor work.

He does not argue with the reason. He states his own verdict.

## The eyes

*Your eyes are **doves behind your veil**.*

The Hebrew is **einayik yonim** — your eyes are doves.

The dove is the book's recurring image for her: *O my dove, in the clefts of the rock*.

**Mibba'ad letzammatek** — from behind your veil. She is partly hidden, and the description says
so.

## The hair

*Your hair flows down like **a flock of goats streaming from Mount Gilead**.*

Black goats moving down a hillside at a distance, which is what dark hair looks like in motion.

The images in this passage are all taken from a shepherd's landscape.

## The teeth

*Your teeth are like **a flock newly shorn, come up washed from the water**, each one **with its twin,
not one of them alone**.*

The Hebrew is **shekullam mat'imot** — all of them bearing twins. And **veshakkulah eyn bahem** —
none bereaved among them.

He uses the same lines again in chapter 6. See the note on Song of Songs 6:6–7.

Washed, paired, complete.

## The lips

*Your lips are **a scarlet thread**, and your mouth is lovely.*

The Hebrew is **kechut hashani** — like a thread of scarlet.

The same word for the scarlet cord Rahab hung from her window, and for the scarlet in the
tabernacle's curtains.

## The cheeks

*Your cheeks behind your veil are **halves of a pomegranate**.*

Cut fruit — deep red inside a covering. See the note on Song of Songs 6:6–7.

## The neck

*Your neck is like **the tower of David**, built in rows of stone, hung with **a thousand shields, the
shields of mighty men**.*

The only architectural image in the sequence, and the only military one.

Shields were hung on tower walls as display. Her bearing is compared to a fortification in dress
order.

## The last image

*Your two breasts are like **two fawns, twins of a gazelle**, that graze among the lilies.*

The Song is frankly a love poem, and the Hebrew canon includes it without apology.

## What he says at the end of the description

*You are **all fair**, my love, and **there is no spot in you**.*

The Hebrew is **umum eyn bach** — and no blemish in you.

**Mum** — the technical word for the defect that disqualified a sacrificial animal.

## What Paul does with that

*That He might present her to Himself a glorious church, **not having spot or wrinkle or any such
thing**, but that she should be holy and **without blemish**.* See the note on
Ephesians 5:25–27.

## What Colossians says

*To set you before Him holy, **with no flaw in you and no charge against you in His sight**.* See the
note on Colossians 1:21–23.

The Greek is **amomous** — without blemish. The Septuagint's word for **mum**.

> **The doubled line.** *How beautiful you are* — said twice before any detail, to a woman whose
> first words about herself were an apology for how she looked.
