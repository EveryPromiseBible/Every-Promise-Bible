---
key: 2 Timothy 1:3–5
title: "Faith with nothing put on for show"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul is writing his last letter from a cell, and the first thing he does is remember a household.

## A conscience with nothing on it

*I thank God, whom I serve **with a conscience that has nothing on it**.*

**En kathara syneidesei** — in a clean conscience. Said by the man who called himself the chief of
sinners and never took it back.

Both are true at once, and only the gospel can hold them together: the record is dreadful and the
conscience is clean, because *the blood of Christ… cleanses your conscience from dead works to serve
the living God.* A clean conscience is not a good memory. It is a settled account.

## The tears

*I remember **the tears on your face**, and remembering them leaves me aching to see you.*

Somewhere there had been a parting, and Timothy had cried. Paul, facing execution, does not mention
it to toughen him up. He mentions it because he misses him.

This letter is the most tender thing Paul wrote, and it was written by a man who knew he had weeks.

## Faith with no mask on

*The faith you carry, **the kind with nothing put on for show**.*

**Anypokritos** — un-hypocritical. **Hypokrisis** is stage-acting, the word Paul used of Peter at
Antioch when fear made him play a part. Timothy's faith is described by the absence of that.

*It **made its home** first in your grandmother Lois and then in your mother Eunice.* **Enokesen** —
took up residence, moved in and stayed.

## Three generations, and a house divided

Luke tells us the rest: Timothy's mother was a Jewish believer and *his father was Greek*. So this
faith did not come down through a tidy household. It came through the women of a mixed marriage, in
a town where that was awkward, and it reached the boy anyway.

Paul had written to Corinth that in such a house *the unbelieving husband is set apart through his
wife… otherwise your children would be unclean, but as it is they are holy*. Here is the letter
where you see it happen to somebody with a name.

## What he says he is sure of

*And I am **certain** it has made its home in you as well.*

**Pepeismai** — I stand persuaded. A perfect tense: convinced, and still convinced.

He says it about the man he will shortly have to tell not to be ashamed, not to be afraid, and to
fan the gift back into flame. Paul's confidence about Timothy plainly does not rest on Timothy's
nerve.

> **What was handed down was not a temperament.** Lois and Eunice could not give the boy courage,
> and evidently did not. What lived in the house was faith in Jesus Christ, unmasked, and it moved
> in and stayed — which is the only inheritance in the world that survives being passed to a
> frightened person.
