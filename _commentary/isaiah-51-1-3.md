A discouraged people are told to look backwards at two very old people.

## The address

*Listen to Me, you who **chase after what is right**, you who **seek the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **rodephei tzedeq** — pursuers of righteousness.

Not the indifferent. The ones already trying.

## The instruction

*Look to **the rock you were cut from**, to **the quarry you were dug out of**.*

The Hebrew is **tzur chutzavtem** — the rock you were hewn from. And **maqqevet bor** — the hollow
of the pit.

Quarry language. Blocks cut out of a face and lifted from a hole.

## Who the rock is

*Look to **Abraham, your father**, and to **Sarah, who gave you birth**.*

## Why Sarah is named

She is mentioned by name in the Old Testament outside Genesis exactly once, here.

The prophet is talking to people who feel too few and too weak to be a nation, and he names the
woman who was barren until ninety.

## The point

*When I called him, **he was only one man**.*

The Hebrew is **ki-echad qerativ** — for one I called him.

One. Childless, seventy-five, in Ur, with no land and no son.

## The result

*But **I blessed him and made him many**.*

Two verbs, both first person. *I blessed*, and *I multiplied*.

The arithmetic between *one* and *many* is entirely God's, and that is the argument.

## Who is being addressed

Exiles. A remnant in Babylon, looking at their own numbers and concluding it is over.

The answer is not encouragement about their prospects. It is a fact about where they came from.

## The comfort

*For the LORD **will comfort Zion**. He will comfort **all her ruins**.*

The Hebrew is **nicham** — to comfort, to console.

The verb that opens chapter 40: *comfort, yes, comfort My people*. See the note on
Isaiah 40:1–2.

## The reversal

*He will make her **wilderness like Eden**, her **desert like the garden of the LORD**.*

**Midbar** — wilderness. **Eden** — delight.

The only two places in Isaiah where Eden is named, and both are here.

## What is found there

*Joy and gladness will be found in her, **thanksgiving and the sound of singing**.*

The Hebrew is **todah veqol zimrah** — thanksgiving and the voice of melody.

## What the chapter goes on to say

*"Lift up your eyes to the heavens, and look on the earth beneath. For **the heavens will vanish
away like smoke**, the earth will grow old like a garment… **but My salvation will be forever**."*

*"**Do not fear the reproach of men**, nor be afraid of their insults."*

## The waking

*Awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of the LORD!*

*Awake, awake! Stand up, O Jerusalem.*

The chapter and the next are full of the same imperative, aimed alternately at God and at the
city.

## What Paul says about the one man

*Therefore from **one man**, and him as good as dead, were born as many as the stars of the sky in
multitude — innumerable as the sand which is by the seashore.* See the note on
Hebrews 11:8–12.

*And him as good as dead* — **nenekromenou** — having been deadened.

## The other rock

*And all drank the same spiritual drink. For **they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them,
and that Rock was Christ**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 10:1–5.

> **The argument.** Not encouragement about their numbers — a fact about their origin. *When I
> called him, he was only one man*, and both verbs in the next clause are first person.
