Joshua gives an order to the solar system, out loud, in front of everybody.

## Where it happens

Five Amorite kings had attacked Gibeon — the city that had tricked Israel into a
treaty a chapter earlier. Israel is defending people who had deceived them.

Joshua marched all night from Gilgal to get there.

## The command

*Joshua spoke to the LORD, and **in the sight of all Israel he cried aloud**: "**Sun,
stand still** over Gibeon; and **Moon**, over the Valley of Aijalon."*

He addresses the LORD, and then addresses the sun.

The Hebrew for *stand still* is **dom** — be silent, be still, cease.

And note the setting: *in the sight of all Israel*, out loud. If nothing had
happened, everybody would have watched nothing happen.

## What is recorded

*And the sun stood still, and the moon stopped… **The sun halted in the middle of the
sky and did not hurry to set for about a whole day**.*

## The citation

*Is this not written in **the Book of Jashar**?*

A source the writer expects his readers to have. It is quoted once more in Scripture,
for David's lament over Saul and Jonathan, and it has not survived.

The writer of Joshua is pointing at an external record.

## The sentence

*There has never been a day like it, before or since — **a day when the LORD listened
to the voice of a man**.*

That is the writer's summary, and it is not about astronomy.

He does not say *a day when the sun stood still*. He says a day when God listened to
somebody.

## And the clause after it

*For **the LORD Himself was fighting for Israel**.*

Both halves are in the same verse. God listened to a man, and God was doing the
fighting.

Joshua's shout was not what won the battle — the chapter has already recorded
hailstones killing more of the enemy than the sword did. What the long day did was
finish it.

## What was at stake

A treaty with people who had lied to them. Israel could have let Gibeon fall and
been rid of an embarrassment.

Joshua marched all night instead, because he had given his word.

## Where the same idea turns up

*Elijah was a man with a nature like ours, and **he prayed earnestly** that it would
not rain, and it did not rain on the land for three years and six months.*

James makes the point deliberately — the man was ordinary, and the sky obeyed.

> **The other thing that stood still.** *Jesus **stood still**, and commanded him to
> be called.* Mark 10, on the road out of Jericho, for a blind beggar shouting.
> The same word in Greek that the Old Testament used of the sun. See the note on
> Mark 10:46–52.
