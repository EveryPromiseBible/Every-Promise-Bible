The last of the twelve, and it is one line about a wolf and two halves of a day.

## The blessing

*"Benjamin is a **ravenous wolf**; **in the morning** he devours the prey, and **in
the evening** he divides the spoil."*

The same animal, two different activities, split by the hours of one day.

Morning: tearing. Evening: distributing.

## The name

Rachel died bearing him and called him **Ben-Oni**, son of my sorrow. His father
renamed him **Benjamin**, son of the right hand.

The only son of Jacob born in the land, and the only one whose name his father
overruled.

## The most famous Benjamite

*Circumcised the eighth day, of the stock of Israel, **of the tribe of Benjamin**,
a Hebrew of Hebrews.*

Paul says it about himself twice — in Philippians and in Romans.

## The morning

*Saul was consenting to his death… **he made havoc of the church**, entering every
house, and dragging off men and women, committing them to prison.*

The Greek for *made havoc* is the word used of a wild animal mangling a body.

And *breathing threats and murder against the disciples of the Lord*, going to
Damascus with letters, to bring back any he found *bound to Jerusalem.*

That is the wolf in the morning, and Luke's vocabulary for it is not accidental.

## The evening

The same man, later, writing from a cell: *I have learned, in whatever state I am,
to be content.*

Fourteen letters distributed to churches across the empire. Collections carried
from Macedonia and Achaia to the poor saints at Jerusalem. Timothy, Titus, Luke,
Onesimus, Philemon, Priscilla and Aquila.

*In the evening he divides the spoil.*

And what he divides is what he took: *what things were gain to me, these I have
counted loss for Christ.*

## Standing before Agrippa

*"And now I stand here on trial for **the hope of the promise God made to our
fathers**."*

The man who dragged people to prison is in chains himself, on trial, and calls it
standing for a hope. He asks the king a question rather than mounting a defence:
*why should it be judged something incredible by any of you that God raises the
dead?*

By the end of that hearing he has told Agrippa he wishes every man in the room were
*as I am, except for these chains.* See the note on Acts 26:4–8.

## What it took

One morning on the Damascus road, and a light above the brightness of the sun.

Nothing in Jacob's line says the wolf became a different animal. It says what the
same animal did with the second half of the day.

> **The other famous Benjamite.** King Saul — the first king of Israel, from this
> tribe, who hunted David and died on Gilboa. And then Saul of Tarsus, from the
> same tribe, who hunted the Church and was renamed. Two men called Saul, one
> tribe, and only one of them let go of the sword.
