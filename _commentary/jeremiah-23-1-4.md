The shepherds scattered the flock, and the sentence on them is that the owner will come and do the
job Himself.

## Who is addressed

*Woe to **the shepherds** who destroy and scatter the sheep of My pasture.*

The Hebrew is **ro'im** — shepherds. In the prophets it means the kings, princes and priests: the
whole governing class.

Not a rural image. A political one.

## Whose sheep

*The sheep of **My** pasture.*

The possessive is the charge. They were managing somebody else's flock.

## The three failures

*You have **scattered** My flock. You have **driven them away** and **have not cared for them**.*

The Hebrew is **hafitzotem**, **vattaddichum**, and **velo peqadtem otam**.

## The verb that turns

**Paqad** — to visit, to attend to, to see to.

*You have not **cared for** them. So now **I will care about you**, and about the evil of your deeds.*

The same verb twice. They did not visit the flock, so He will visit them.

English needs two different words for it; the Hebrew uses one and lets the sentence close like a
trap.

## What He does about the flock

*I **Myself** will gather the remnant of My flock.*

The Hebrew is **va'ani aqabbetz** — and I, I will gather. The pronoun is added for emphasis where
the verb already carries it.

Having sacked the shepherds, He does not immediately appoint replacements. He gathers them
personally.

## Where He gathers them from

*Out of all the lands **where I have driven them**.*

The scattering is attributed to Him in the same breath as the shepherds who did it.

Both are true in the sentence and neither cancels the other.

## What follows

*And they will be **fruitful and increase**.*

The Hebrew is **ufaru veravu** — the words spoken over the first man and over Noah. See the note on
Genesis 1:26–31.

## Then the replacements

*I will **raise up shepherds** over them who will **truly shepherd them**.*

New ones, after the gathering, not instead of it.

## The four promises

*They will **fear no more**, they will **not be dismayed**, and **not one of them will be missing**.*

The Hebrew is **velo yippaqedu** — and they shall not be visited, i.e. not be found lacking on
inspection.

**Paqad** a third time. The word that condemned the shepherds is the word that guarantees the sheep.

## What comes next in the chapter

*Behold, the days are coming, says the LORD, that **I will raise to David a Branch of
righteousness**… and this is His name by which He will be called: **THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS**.*
See the note on Jeremiah 23:5–6.

The Hebrew is **YHWH Tzidqenu**.

The passage about failed shepherds runs straight into the naming of one who will not fail.

## The shepherd who says it Himself

*"I am the good shepherd. **The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep**."* And *"he who is a
hireling… **sees the wolf coming and leaves the sheep and flees**."* See the note on
John 10:11–15.

The hireling in that parable is doing what Jeremiah's shepherds did.

## And the counting

*"Of those whom You gave Me **I have lost none**."* See the note on John 18:8–9.

> **One verb, three times.** They did not *visit* the flock; He will *visit* them; and not one sheep
> will be found missing at the *visitation*. The Hebrew closes it like a trap.
