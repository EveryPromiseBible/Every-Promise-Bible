The passage Jesus read out in Nazareth, and the most important thing about it is
where He closed the scroll.

## The commission

*The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on me, because the LORD **has anointed me**.*

The Hebrew is **mashach** — the root of *Messiah*.

## The five things

- **bring good news to the poor**
- **bind up the brokenhearted**
- **proclaim freedom for the captives**
- **release from darkness for the prisoners**
- **proclaim the year of the LORD's favour**

Every one is a verb of announcement or repair.

## The year of favour

The Hebrew is *the year of the LORD's acceptance*, and it points at the jubilee — every
fiftieth year, when debts were released, slaves went free and land returned to the
family that lost it.

## And then

*…**and the day of our God's vengeance**.*

The two clauses sit in one sentence: a **year** of favour, and a **day** of vengeance.

## What Jesus did with it

In the synagogue at Nazareth He read from this scroll — and Luke records the exact
point He stopped:

*"To proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD." **Then He closed the book**, gave it
back to the attendant, and sat down.*

He stops mid-sentence, at the comma.

*And He began to say to them, "**Today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing**."*

See the note on Luke 4:16–21.

## The rest of the sentence

The day of vengeance is not deleted. It is left unread, on the same line, in the same
breath of the prophet.

Isaiah wrote them together. Jesus read the first half and said *today*.

## What follows in Isaiah

*To comfort all who mourn… to give them **a crown of beauty instead of ashes**, the
**oil of joy instead of mourning**, a **garment of praise instead of a spirit of
despair**.*

Three exchanges, each a swap of one thing for another.

Ashes were what mourners put on their heads. A crown goes where the ashes were.

## The last line

*They will be called **oaks of righteousness**, a planting of the LORD, **for the
display of His splendour**.*

Not saplings. Oaks — the slowest-growing, longest-standing tree in that country.

## What happened next in Nazareth

He mentioned two Gentiles — the widow at Zarephath and Naaman the Syrian — and the
congregation took Him to the brow of the hill to throw Him off. See the note on
Luke 4:28–30.

> **What the scroll would have looked like.** He would have unrolled it to find the
> place, read aloud standing, rolled it up, handed it back, and sat down — sitting
> being the posture for teaching. Luke records every step, and the sermon is one
> sentence long.
