This is the most argued-over paragraph about the Lord's return, and Paul ends it
with an instruction that tells you what he wrote it for.

## The three sounds

*With a shout, with the voice of the archangel, with the trumpet of God.*

Nothing hidden or quiet. The word for shout is a **command** shouted aloud — used
of an officer to troops, or a captain to rowers. Whatever else this is, it is not
secret.

## "Caught up"

The Greek **harpazo** means to seize, to snatch away by force. It is used of the
Spirit carrying Philip off, and of a child snatched up to God in Revelation. The
Latin translation of it, *rapturo*, is where the English word rapture comes from
— which is why that word is not in most English Bibles: it came in through Latin,
not from a missing Greek term.

The picture is not a gentle ascent. It is being grabbed.

## The order Paul gives

- the Lord comes down
- the dead in Christ rise **first**
- then the living are caught up **together with them**
- and so we will be with the Lord always

He is still answering the question from the verses before: their dead are not
left behind. The sequence exists to settle that worry, and *together* is the word
carrying it.

> **The last line is the point.** *Comfort one another with these words.* Not
> "chart these events," not "work out the timing." Paul wrote this to be said to
> grieving people, and the destination of the whole passage is the plainest thing
> in it: *we will be with the Lord always.* Everything else in the paragraph is
> the transport.
