Four people leave a city, and none of them walks out under their own power.

## The urging

*At dawn the angels **urged Lot on**.*

The Hebrew is **va'ya'itsu** — they pressed, they hurried him.

The same verb Lot had used on them the evening before, pressing them to come indoors. See the
note on Genesis 19:1–3.

The pressing has reversed direction.

## The instruction

*"**Quick — take your wife and your two daughters who are here**, or you will be swept away."*

*Who are here* — the phrase implies others who are not. The sons-in-law stayed. See the note on
Genesis 19:12–14.

## The hesitation

*When he **hesitated**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyitmahmah** — he lingered, he delayed.

One word, and it is the most human thing in the chapter.

He has a house here, a position in the gate, two daughters engaged to local men, and a life. It
is dawn, and he is being told to leave all of it in the next few minutes.

## The hands

*The men **grabbed his hand and the hands of his wife and daughters**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyachaziqu** — they seized, took firm hold.

Four wrists, taken. Nobody in the party is walking out voluntarily at this point.

Isaiah has the same word for God: *I, the LORD your God, will hold your right hand*.

## The reason given

*For **the LORD was merciful to them**.*

The Hebrew is **bechemlat YHWH alav** — in the LORD's compassion toward him.

Genesis supplies the motive for the dragging, and it is not impatience.

## The delivery

*And **led them safely out of the city**.*

## The three commands

*"**Run for your life!**"*

*"**Don't look back, and don't stop anywhere in the plain**."*

*"**Flee to the hills**, or you will be swept away."*

Escape, do not look, do not stop, and go up.

## What Lot says to that

He argues.

*"Please, no, my lords! Indeed now, your servant has found favour in your sight… **but I cannot
escape to the mountains, lest some evil overtake me and I die**. See now, this city is near
enough to flee to, and it is a little one. Please let me escape there."*

He is standing outside a city about to be destroyed, having been carried out by the wrist,
negotiating the destination.

And it is granted: *see, I have favoured you concerning this thing also*.

The town is called **Zoar** — *little*, and it is named for his argument.

## The delay clause

*"**Hurry, escape there, for I cannot do anything until you arrive there**."*

The angel says the destruction is waiting on him.

## What happens to the one who looked

*But Lot's wife **looked back**, and she became a pillar of salt.* See the note on
Genesis 19:23–26.

The instruction had been given to all four.

## What Jesus does with it

*"**Remember Lot's wife**. Whoever seeks to save his life will lose it, and whoever loses his
life will preserve it."*

Three words in the middle of the Olivet material, and it is the whole reference.

He also uses the day itself: *they ate, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they
built; but on the day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven*.

Nothing on that list is a sin. They are the ordinary activities of a Tuesday.

## Who is remembered when it happens

*So when God destroyed the cities of the plain, **He remembered Abraham**, and He brought Lot
out.* See the note on Genesis 19:27–29.

> **How the four of them left.** By the wrist. Genesis names the reason for the dragging in the
> same sentence — *the LORD was merciful to them* — and the man being dragged spends the next
> paragraph negotiating.
