The address is to a Phoenician monarch, and halfway through the description stops fitting one.

## The form

*Raise **a lament** over the king of Tyre.*

The Hebrew is **qinah** — a funeral dirge, in the limping metre used for the dead.

The chapter is a funeral song for a man who is still alive.

## The two oracles

Verses 1–10 address **the prince** of Tyre — **nagid**. A man: *you are a man, and not a god,
though you set your heart as the heart of a god*.

Verses 11–19 address **the king** — **melek**. The language changes with the title.

## The opening

*You were **the seal of perfection**, full of wisdom and **perfect in beauty**.*

The Hebrew is **chotem toknit** — a signet of proportion, of measure.

## The location

*You were **in Eden, the garden of God**.*

The historical king of Tyre was not.

## The stones

*Every precious stone was your covering: **ruby, topaz, and emerald; chrysolite, onyx, and jasper;
sapphire, turquoise, and beryl**.*

Nine stones in the Hebrew. The Septuagint has twelve.

## What they belong to

The high priest's breastpiece carried twelve stones, and the Hebrew list here matches nine of them.
See the note on Exodus 28:15–21.

Priestly language, applied to a covering rather than a garment.

## The workmanship

*Your settings and mountings were worked in gold, **prepared for you on the day you were created**.*

The Hebrew is **beyom hibbara'aka** — in the day of your being created.

Created, not born.

## The title

*You were **the anointed cherub who guards**, for so I appointed you.*

The Hebrew is **keruv mimshach hassokek** — the anointed covering cherub.

**Sakak** — to hedge in, to cover, to screen. The verb used of the cherubim over the mercy seat,
whose wings covered it.

## The place

*You were **on the holy mountain of God**, and you **walked among the fiery stones**.*

## The verdict

*You were **blameless in your ways from the day you were created**.*

The Hebrew is **tamim** — complete, without blemish. The sacrificial word.

## The turn

*Until **wickedness was found in you**.*

The Hebrew is **ad-nimtza avlatah bak**.

Six words in English, and the whole hinge of the passage.

## The cause

The next verses: *your heart was lifted up because of your beauty; **you corrupted your wisdom for
the sake of your splendour***.

## How the passage is read

Two ways, and they are not exclusive.

As hyperbolic court language for a Phoenician king, in the style ancient monarchs were addressed in.

Or as an oracle that begins with a man and moves behind him to the power he images.

The Eden setting, the created day and the cherub title are what make the first reading strain.

## The parallel oracle

*"**How you are fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning**!… For you have said in your
heart: **'I will ascend into heaven… I will be like the Most High.'**"* See the note on
Isaiah 14:12–15.

Addressed to the king of Babylon, with the same doubled focus.

Five *I wills* in that passage.

## What Jesus said

*"**I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven**."* See the note on Luke 10:17–20.

## What he does now

*The **accuser of our brethren**, who accused them before our God day and night, has been cast
down.* See the note on Revelation 12:10–11.

*And they overcame him **by the blood of the Lamb** and by the word of their testimony.*

> **The hinge.** *Blameless from the day you were created, until wickedness was found in you.* One
> clause, and everything before it is past tense from there on.
