Three quotations are produced to prove one point: He says *brothers* about them.

## The common origin

*The One who makes them holy and the ones being made holy **all come from a single source**.*

The Greek is **ex henos pantes** — of one, all.

The noun is left out. Of one what — Father, stock, origin — the letter does not say.

## The two participles

**Ho hagiazōn** — the one sanctifying. **Hoi hagiazomenoi** — the ones being sanctified.

Active and passive of the same verb. One does it; the others are having it done.

## The conclusion

*That is why **He is not embarrassed to call them brothers**.*

The Greek is **ouk epaischunetai** — He is not ashamed.

## What that phrasing admits

The word only makes sense where shame would be understandable.

The letter does not say it would be absurd for Him to be ashamed. It says He is not.

## The first quotation

*"**I will tell My brothers Your name; in the middle of the gathered assembly I will sing Your
praise**."*

Psalm 22. The psalm that opens *My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?* See the note on
Psalms 22:1–5.

## Where in the psalm

The turn. After the piercing, the counted bones and the divided garments, the psalm changes and the
sufferer starts speaking to a congregation.

The verse quoted is the first line of the second half.

## What He does in it

Sings.

The letter's proof that He calls them brothers is a line in which He is leading the singing among
them.

## The second quotation

*"**I will put My trust in Him**."*

From Isaiah 8, in the Septuagint. A prophet's line in a time when the nation would not listen.

## What it proves

Trust is what a creature does. A quotation in which the speaker trusts God places Him alongside
them, which is the argument.

## The third

*"**Here I am, and the children God gave Me**."*

The next verse of Isaiah 8. Given as spoken by Him.

The Greek is **ta paidia ha moi edōken ho Theos**.

## The echo

*"I have manifested Your name to the men whom **You have given Me** out of the world."* See the note
on John 17:6–8.

The same construction, in the prayer the night before.

## What the passage has just said

*For it was fitting for Him… in bringing many sons to glory, to make **the captain of their salvation
perfect through sufferings**.* See the note on Hebrews 2:9–10.

The Greek is **archēgon** — the pioneer, the one who goes first and opens the way.

## What follows

*Inasmuch then as the children have partaken of flesh and blood, **He Himself likewise shared in the
same**, that through death **He might destroy him who had the power of death**.* See the note on
Hebrews 2:14–16.

The Greek is **katargēsē** — render inoperative, put out of work.

## Why He had to be a brother

*Therefore, in all things He had to be made like His brethren, that He might be **a merciful and
faithful High Priest**.*

The letter's whole argument for the priesthood runs through the word *brothers*.

## What He calls them after the resurrection

*"**Go and tell My brethren** to go to Galilee, and there they will see Me."*

Said about men who had run.

> **The word that admits it.** *Not ashamed* only makes sense where shame would have been
> understandable. The letter does not deny that; it reports the fact.
