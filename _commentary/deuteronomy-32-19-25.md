The Song of Moses, taught to Israel before they entered the land, and it tells
them in advance what will happen and what it will look like.

## When it was written

Moses is about to die. He has not yet let them cross the Jordan, and God tells
him to write a song and teach it to the people so that *when many troubles come
upon them, this song will testify against them, for it will not be forgotten.*

It is a song about the future, given to be memorised before any of it happened.

## The phrase

*"**I will hide My face** from them," He said, "and see what their end will be."*

That is the sentence, and it is the sentence the whole exile is described by.

Not *I will destroy them*. Not *I will leave them*. **Hide My face** — the
Hebrew is to conceal, to cover over.

## What hiding is not

The rest of the song is the proof. Read on past this passage and it goes to *the
LORD will vindicate His people and have compassion on His servants… He will
avenge the blood of His servants… and make atonement for His land and His people.*

The face is hidden and He is still working. That is the season Hosea named
*Lo-Ammi*, and it is the season the book of Esther is set inside — no name of
God in ten chapters, and His hand on every one of them. See the notes on
Hosea 1:8–9 and Esther 1:1–4.

## The mirror

*"They made Me jealous with **what is no god**… so I will make them jealous with
**what is no people**."*

The two halves are built to match. *No-god* answers *no-people*.

Paul picks up exactly this line in Romans 10 and applies it to the Gentiles being
brought in — the *no-people* who become *sons of the living God*. See the note on
Romans 9:25–26.

## "Children who have no faith"

The Hebrew is children in whom there is no **emun** — no faithfulness, no
steadiness, from the root behind *amen*.

Not children who did wrong. Children with nothing dependable in them.

## What the song is doing

*The LORD saw it and turned away, **grieved by His sons and daughters**.*

He is grieved and they are still called sons and daughters. The relationship is
named in the same verse as the turning away.

> **How the song ends.** Not with the arrows. *Rejoice, O nations, with His
> people, for He will avenge the blood of His servants… and provide atonement for
> His land and His people.* Moses taught them the whole thing, and the last note in
> it is atonement.
