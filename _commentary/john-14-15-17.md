He is leaving, and He tells them what is coming instead — and the word He picks for it is
the one He uses for Himself.

## The condition

*"If you love Me, **keep My commands**."*

The order runs love first, keeping second. John says the same in his letter: *this is the
love of God, that we keep His commandments. And His commandments are not burdensome.*

## The asking

*"And **I will ask the Father**, and **He will give you** another Helper."*

Jesus asks and the Father gives. The whole arrangement is described as a request granted.

## "Another"

The Greek is **allos** — another of the same kind, as against **heteros**, another of a
different kind.

Not a substitute of a lesser sort. The same kind of person.

## The word

**Parakletos** — from *para*, alongside, and *kaleo*, to call. One called to stand
beside you: an advocate, a counsel for the defence, a supporter in court.

English versions render it Comforter, Helper, Advocate, Counsellor. No single word
carries all of it.

John uses it once more, in his letter, of Jesus: *we have an **Advocate** with the Father,
Jesus Christ the righteous.*

**Another** Helper. The first one is the man saying it.

## The duration

*"To be with you **forever**."*

The Greek is *into the age*.

He had been with them three years and was about to be arrested.

## The name

*"**The Spirit of truth**."*

John uses the title three times in these chapters. Later: *when He, the Spirit of truth,
has come, **He will guide you into all truth**.*

## What the world cannot do

*"**The world cannot receive Him**, because **it does not see Him and does not know
Him**."*

Not *will not*. Cannot. The reason given is a lack of recognition rather than a refusal.

## What they can

*"**But you know Him**, because **He lives with you and will be in you**."*

Two prepositions, and the change between them is the whole point.

**With** — present tense, already true.

**In** — future tense, from Pentecost onward.

## What He says immediately after

*"**I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you**."*

The Greek is **orphanos** — the word for a child left without a father, and also for
disciples left without their teacher.

He has just told them He is going, and the next sentence denies the thing they would
conclude from it.

## Where this sits

The upper room, after the supper, before the arrest. The same discourse that ends *get
up now; let us go from here*. See the note on John 14:28–31.

> **What is promised.** Not a replacement and not an influence. Another of the same kind
> as Himself, permanently, and moving from beside them to inside them.
