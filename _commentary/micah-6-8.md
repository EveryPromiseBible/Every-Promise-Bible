The answer to a question somebody asks in a panic three verses earlier.

## The setting

Micah 6 is written as a courtroom scene. God calls the mountains as witnesses and puts
His case: *O My people, what have I done to you? In what have I wearied you? Testify
against Me.*

Then He recites what He did — brought them out of Egypt, redeemed them from the house of
bondage, sent Moses, Aaron and Miriam.

## The panicked question

The people answer, and the answer escalates:

*With what shall I come before the LORD? **With burnt offerings, with calves a year
old**? Will the LORD be pleased with **thousands of rams**, with **ten thousand rivers of
oil**? Shall I give **my firstborn** for my transgression?*

Four bids, each bigger than the last, ending at child sacrifice — which the law
forbade absolutely.

That is the mood the verse answers.

## The reply

*"**He has shown you**, mortal, **what is good**."*

The Hebrew is *He has told you* — it is already stated, already given.

The question assumed the requirement was unknown and had to be guessed at with
increasingly extravagant offers. The answer says it was never hidden.

## "Mortal"

The Hebrew is **adam** — man, human being, the word for the first one.

## The three

*"Only this: **to act justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God**."*

- **Act justly** — *mishpat*, right judgment. Do the right thing.
- **Love mercy** — the Hebrew is **chesed**, covenant loyalty, steadfast love. Not
  *do* mercy. **Love** it.
- **Walk humbly with your God** — one Hebrew word, **hatsnea**, found only twice in the
  Old Testament. It carries the sense of walking carefully, modestly, in step with
  somebody.

## What is not in the list

Nothing is brought. No rams, no oil, no calves, no firstborn.

The question was about what to carry to the altar and the answer is about how to walk.

## The scale of it

Three items against *ten thousand rivers of oil*. The reply is deliberately smaller than
the question.

## Where the same word turns up

*Chesed* is the word the psalms translate *mercy* and *lovingkindness* — *His mercy
endures forever*, twenty-six times in Psalm 136.

The word describing what God is like is the one the people are told to love.

## "With your God"

The last two words. Not *before* Him and not *for* Him.

Enoch is the man Genesis says *walked with God*, and Hebrews explains it as faith. See
the note on Hebrews 11:4–7.

> **What is being answered.** A man in a panic, bidding upward, asking what will be
> enough. The reply names three things, none of which cost anything, and opens by
> saying he was told long ago.
