Two short sentences, and the tense changes between them.

## The first

*Everything born from God **gets the better of the world**.*

The Greek is **nika ton kosmon** — overcomes the world. Present tense: goes on overcoming.

And the subject is neuter — **pan to gegennemenon**, everything that has been born. Not
*everyone*.

John describes the birth itself as the thing that wins, rather than the person's performance.

## What *the world* means in John

Not the planet, and not people in general.

*All that is in the world — the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of
life — is not of the Father but is of the world.*

The system, running on those three engines.

## The second sentence

*And **the thing that got the better of the world is our trust**.*

The Greek is **he nike he nikesasa** — the victory that has overcome. An aorist participle:
a completed act, in the past.

The tense shifts. The first sentence is ongoing; this one is finished.

## What is named

*Our faith.*

Not our effort, our holiness, or our record.

And the next verse defines it further: *who is he who overcomes the world, but **he who
believes that Jesus is the Son of God**?*

The victory is located in an object, not in a quantity. It is not strong faith that wins; it
is faith in Him.

## Where the word comes from

*"In the world you will have tribulation; but be of good cheer, **I have overcome the world**."*

The last sentence of the upper-room discourse, in the same Gospel, from the same writer.

The Greek there is **nenikeka** — a perfect: I have overcome and it stands overcome.

Said hours before the arrest.

John's letter uses the disciple's version of his Master's verb.

## What comes before it

*By this we know that we love the children of God, when we love God and keep His commandments.
For this is the love of God, that we keep His commandments. **And His commandments are not
burdensome**.*

The Greek is **bareiai** — heavy.

The overcoming sentence is the explanation of that clause. The commands are not heavy because
of what the birth has already done.

## What comes after

*This is He who came by water and blood — Jesus Christ; not only by water, but by water and
blood. And it is the Spirit who bears witness, because the Spirit is truth.*

And then: *he who has the Son has life; he who does not have the Son of God does not have
life*.

And the letter's stated purpose: *that you may know that you have eternal life*. See the note
on 1 John 5:13.

## Where the verb ends up

Seven times in Revelation, at the end of each letter to the churches:

*To him who overcomes I will give to eat from the tree of life.*

*He who overcomes shall not be hurt by the second death.*

*To him who overcomes I will give to eat of the hidden manna.*

And the last: *to him who overcomes I will grant to sit with Me on My throne, as I also
overcame and sat down with My Father on His throne.*

The same verb He used in the upper room, promised to the reader.

## The other overcoming

*And they overcame him **by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony**.* See
the note on Revelation 12:10–11.

> **The change of tense.** Born-of-God keeps on overcoming, present. And the victory itself is
> in the aorist — already done, and named as faith.
