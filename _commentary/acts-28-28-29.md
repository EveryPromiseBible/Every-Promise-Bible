The last sentence Luke records from Paul, and it is a forecast.

## The announcement

*"So **let it be known to you** that this salvation of God **has been sent to the
Gentiles**."*

The Greek is **apestale** — an aorist passive. It has been sent.

**Apostello** — the verb behind *apostle*. The salvation is described as having been
commissioned and dispatched.

## The clause

*"And **they will listen**."*

The Greek is **autoi kai akousontai** — they also will hear.

Two words of prediction, and Luke's whole second half is the evidence for it: Cornelius'
house, Antioch, Cyprus, Galatia, Philippi, Thessalonica, Athens, Corinth, Ephesus.

## The contrast

He has just quoted Isaiah — *you will hear and hear and never understand*. See the note on
Acts 28:25–27.

Then, in the next breath, *they will hear*.

The same verb, negated in the quotation and affirmed in the forecast.

## Why Rome matters

The commission was *in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth*.

Rome is not the end of the earth geographically. It is the centre of the world Luke is
writing in, and from Rome the roads go everywhere.

Paul had told the Romans his real plan: *whenever I journey to Spain, I shall come to you*.

## The three times this happens

It is Paul's pattern, stated three times in Acts and each time after a synagogue rejects him.

Antioch in Pisidia: *it was necessary that the word of God should be spoken to you first; but
since you reject it… **we turn to the Gentiles***. See the note on Acts 13:38–39.

Corinth: *your blood be upon your own heads; I am clean. From now on I will go to the
Gentiles.*

And Rome, here, the third and last.

## What he wrote about the order

*For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ, for it is the power of God to salvation for
everyone who believes, **for the Jew first and also for the Greek**.* See the note on
Romans 1:16–17.

First, not only. Paul kept the order in every city.

## The departure

*And when he had said these words, **the Jews left, arguing among themselves**.*

The Greek is **pollen echontes en heautois suzetesin** — having much dispute among
themselves.

They do not leave united. Luke has already said *some were persuaded*.

## What is not in Acts

Paul's trial before Caesar. His release, if there was one. His death.

Luke was in Rome and wrote the book, and stops without telling us.

The traditional explanation is that Acts was finished before the outcome was known.

## The last two verses

*Then Paul dwelt two whole years in his own rented house, and **received all who came to
him**, preaching the kingdom of God and teaching the things which concern the Lord Jesus
Christ, **with all confidence, no one forbidding him**.*

The Greek is **parresias** — the boldness word, and **akolutos**, unhindered.

## What was written from that house

Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians and Philemon are usually dated to these two years.

*I want you to know, brethren, that the things which happened to me have actually turned out
for the furtherance of the gospel, so that **it has become evident to the whole palace guard**
that my chains are in Christ.*

A soldier chained to Paul for four hours at a stretch, replaced by another, around the clock,
for two years.

> **The last word in the book.** *Unhindered.* Luke ends an account of arrests, floggings,
> shipwrecks and a chain with an adverb saying nothing was in the way.
