An announcement of a birth, and the messenger goes to the woman.

## Where the book is

*Again the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the LORD, and **the LORD delivered them into
the hand of the Philistines for forty years**.*

The longest oppression in Judges, and the only one where the people do not cry out. Nobody asks
for a deliverer.

The angel arrives unrequested.

## The couple

*In the town of **Zorah** there lived a man named **Manoah**, from the clan of **Dan**.*

Dan — the tribe that never took its allotment and later migrated north and set up an idol.

## The condition

*His wife had no children — **year after year, an empty cradle, a quiet ache she had learned to
carry**.*

The Hebrew is **aqarah velo yaladah** — barren, and had not borne.

She is not named anywhere in the chapter. Manoah is named repeatedly; his wife is *the woman*.

## Who is visited

*Then one day **the angel of the LORD came and stood before her**.*

Her, not him.

The pattern in Scripture is announcements to the father — Abraham, Zacharias. Here it is the
mother, twice, and Manoah has to ask for the visit to be repeated.

## The announcement

*"**You have known only barrenness**," he said, "**but that is about to change**."*

The angel names her situation before changing it.

## The instructions

*"So take care of yourself: **no wine, no strong drink, nothing unclean** must touch your lips."*

The Nazirite restrictions, given to the mother.

She is to keep the vow while carrying him. The consecration starts before the child does.

## The child

*"For the boy will **belong to God from his mother's womb — a Nazirite, set apart**."*

The Hebrew is **nezir Elohim** — a Nazirite of God.

**Nazir** — separated, consecrated. Numbers 6 sets out the vow: no wine, no razor, no contact with
the dead.

## The difference

Numbers 6 is a vow somebody takes for a period.

This one is from the womb and for life, and the person concerned is never asked.

## The hair

*"**No razor is ever to touch his head**."*

The one part of the vow Samson keeps, until Delilah.

He drinks at a feast, takes honey from a lion's carcass, and touches corpses repeatedly. The
razor is the last item to go, and when it does, *he did not know that the LORD had departed from
him*.

## The commission

*"And he will be the one to **begin rescuing Israel** from the grip of the Philistines."*

The Hebrew is **yachel lehoshia** — he shall begin to save.

**Begin.** Not finish.

Samson kills more at his death than in his life and never delivers the nation. See the note on
Judges 16:28–30.

The angel says so at the start.

## What the wife does

Goes and tells her husband: *a Man of God came to me, and His countenance was like the countenance
of the Angel of God, very awesome*.

And Manoah prays that He would come again *and teach us what we shall do for the child*.

*And God listened to the voice of Manoah.*

## What happens on the second visit

The offering on the rock, and the angel going up in the flame. See the note on
Judges 13:19–20.

*"We are going to die! We have seen God!"* — and his wife's answer. See the note on
Judges 13:21–23.

## Who else was told first

*But **the Angel of the LORD appeared to the woman** and said to her, "Indeed now, you are
barren and have borne no children, but you shall conceive and bear a son."*

A barren woman, visited by a messenger, told she will have a son, given instructions about
how he is to be raised, and told what he will do.

*Now in the sixth month the angel Gabriel was sent by God to a city of Galilee named
Nazareth… "Do not be afraid, Mary… you will conceive in your womb and bring forth a Son, and
shall call His name JESUS."*

The husband is not the one addressed in either account.

## The word in the commission

*"**He shall begin to deliver Israel** out of the hand of the Philistines."*

**Begin.** Not finish.

Samson kills more at his death than in his life and the Philistines are still there. Judges
ends with them in place; Samuel is still fighting them; David is the one who finally settles
it.

The book is honest that its strongest man is a start.

*Being confident of this very thing, that **He who has begun a good work in you will complete
it** until the day of Jesus Christ.*

## What the consecration is

*"**No razor shall come upon his head**, for the child shall be a Nazirite to God **from the
womb**."*

Set apart before he existed, by a vow he did not take and never chose.

> **Who is visited.** The wife, and the vow is given to her first. The consecration starts before
> the child does, and the commission says *begin* rather than finish.
