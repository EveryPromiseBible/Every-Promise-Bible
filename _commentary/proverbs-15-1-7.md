The most quoted proverb about speech, and the word behind *gentle* is not what
most readers assume.

## The word

The Hebrew is **rak** — soft, tender. It is used of tender meat, of a delicate
child, and of Leah's eyes.

Not weak, and not evasive. Soft in texture.

## What it does

*Turns away wrath.* The verb is about causing something to go back the way it
came — deflection rather than absorption.

The proverb is not saying a soft answer makes you feel better about the abuse. It
says the anger goes back down.

And the second half gives the alternative: *a harsh word stirs the anger up.* The
Hebrew for *harsh* means painful, hurtful — a word that causes grief.

Both halves describe an answer. The choice is not between speaking and staying
silent.

## What the paragraph sits with

Every proverb in the run is about the mouth:

- *the tongue of the wise makes knowledge sweet*
- *a healing tongue is a tree of life; a twisted tongue breaks the spirit*
- *the lips of the wise scatter knowledge like seed*

The tree of life image is the strongest. It appears four times in Proverbs, and
one of them is a way of speaking.

## The line in the middle

*The eyes of the LORD are everywhere, watching the evil and the good.*

An odd insertion in a run about speech — until you notice what it does to the
rest. All these exchanges happen in front of somebody.

> **Where a soft answer is hardest.** The examples in Scripture are unflattering.
> Peter, told the cross was coming, argued. James and John proposed fire. Paul
> called a high priest a whitewashed wall and then apologised when told who he
> was. The proverb is not describing a personality type — it is describing a
> choice made in the second after being provoked.
