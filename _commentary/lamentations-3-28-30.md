title: "Let him offer his cheek"

Three lines of counsel in the middle of the turn, and the last one is what somebody actually did.

## The posture

*Let him **sit alone and be still**, for God has laid it upon him. Let him **bow his face to the
dust**; there may yet be hope.*

The Hebrew of the second is *let him put his mouth in the dust*.

## The clause in the middle

*There may yet be hope.*

**Ulai yesh tiqvah** — perhaps there is hope. The poet will not overclaim. He is three verses
from *great is Your faithfulness* and here he says *perhaps*.

## The instruction

*Let him **offer his cheek to the one who strikes**, and take his fill of the reproach.*

The Hebrew is **yitten lemakkehu lechi** — let him give his cheek to the one striking him.

## Who gave it

*"**I gave My back to those who struck Me, and My cheeks to those who plucked out the beard**; I
did not hide My face from shame and spitting."*

Isaiah 50, the third Servant song, and it is the same construction: to *give* the cheek.

*Then **they spat in His face and beat Him**; and others struck Him with the palms of their hands,
saying, "Prophesy to us, Christ! Who is the one who struck You?"*

*Who, when He was reviled, **did not revile in return**; when He suffered, He did not threaten,
but committed Himself to Him who judges righteously.*

**Peter** describes it as a series of things not done, and the letter he puts it in is about
suffering unjustly.

## And who taught it

*"But I tell you not to resist an evil person. But **whoever slaps you on your right cheek, turn
the other to him also**."*

**Matthew** 5. It is the one instruction of the Sermon on the Mount that the same gospel records
Him carrying out in person, twenty-one chapters later.

## What the counsel is not

It is not resignation, and the poem does not leave it there. Four verses on: *for the Lord will
not cast off forever… He does not wound from His heart.* See the note on
Lamentations 3:31–33.

The silence is held for a stated length of time, and the reason given for holding it is that the
one who laid it on is not doing it from the heart.

*Who for **the joy that was set before Him endured the cross, despising the shame**.*

**Hebrews** 12 gives the same shape: something endured, on account of something ahead of it.

> **The line.** *Let him give his cheek to the one who strikes.* Isaiah 50 has a Servant say it in
> the first person, Matthew 5 has it taught from a hillside, and Matthew 26 has it done in a
> courtyard by the one who taught it.
