Three questions on a beach, and Peter had denied Him three times beside another fire.

## When it happens

*When they had **finished breakfast**.*

Bread and fish, cooked on *a fire of coals* — the Greek is **anthrakia**, a charcoal fire.

The word occurs twice in the New Testament. The other is the courtyard where Peter warmed
himself and said *I am not*.

John chose the word.

## The name

*"**Simon, son of Jonas**."*

Not Peter. Three times, He uses the name the man had before.

## The first question

*"**Do you love Me more than these do?**"*

The comparison is in it. Peter had said, hours before the arrest, *even if all are made to
stumble because of You, I will never be made to stumble*.

He is asked about that claim once, and never again.

## The two Greek verbs

Jesus asks with **agapao** the first two times and **phileo** the third.

Peter answers with **phileo** all three times.

Readers have divided over whether the change is significant since the early centuries.
John uses the two words interchangeably elsewhere in the Gospel, and the whole conversation
would have been in Aramaic.

What is not in doubt is that Peter does not raise his answer to meet the question, and that
the third time Jesus comes down to Peter's word.

## What Peter appeals to

*"Yes, Lord, **You know** that I love You."*

Three times he appeals to what Jesus knows rather than to what he can promise.

He does not make a claim about his own reliability. He had done that once.

## The third time

*And **it grieved Peter that He asked him a third time**.*

The Greek is **elupethe** — was pained, distressed.

Nobody in the account needs the number explained.

*"Lord, **You know everything. You know that I love You**."*

## The three commissions

*"**Feed My lambs**."*

*"**Take care of My sheep**."*

*"**Feed My sheep**."*

Two different verbs — **bosko**, to feed, and **poimaino**, to shepherd — and two different
nouns.

Each answer is met with a job, and the job is not about Peter.

## Whose sheep

**My** lambs. **My** sheep. Three times.

Peter picks the word up in his own letter: *shepherd the flock of God which is among you…
**and when the Chief Shepherd appears**, you will receive the crown of glory.*

## What comes next

*"Most assuredly, I say to you, when you were younger, you girded yourself and walked where
you wished; but **when you are old, you will stretch out your hands**, and another will gird
you and carry you where you do not wish."*

John adds: *this He spoke, signifying by what death he would glorify God.*

And then: *"**Follow Me**."*

The same two words He had said on the shore three years earlier. See the note on
Mark 1:16–20.

## What Peter does immediately

Turns round, sees John, and asks *but Lord, what about this man?*

*"If I will that he remain till I come, what is that to you? **You follow Me**."*

## Where they were

Back at the lake, fishing. *Simon Peter said to them, "I am going fishing."* See the note on
John 21:1–3.

They had caught nothing all night.

> **What is not asked.** Whether he is sorry, and whether he will do better. Three
> questions about one thing, and Peter answers all three by pointing at what Jesus already
> knows.
