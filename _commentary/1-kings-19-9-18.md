The same question is asked twice, the same answer is given twice, and in between the
mountain comes apart.

## The question

*"**What are you doing here, Elijah?**"*

Not an accusation and not a summons. A question, and it is the first thing said to him
after forty days of walking.

## The answer

*"**I have been very zealous** for the LORD God Almighty. The Israelites have **abandoned
Your covenant, torn down Your altars, and killed Your prophets** with the sword. **I'm
the only one left** — and now they're trying to kill me too."*

Four complaints and a conclusion. Every fact in it is true except the last one.

## The instruction

*"**Go out and stand on the mountain** in the presence of the LORD, for **the LORD is
about to pass by**."*

The same mountain, and nearly the same words, as Moses in the cleft of the rock: *while
My glory passes by, I will put you in the cleft of the rock.*

## The three

*A great and powerful **wind** tore through the mountains and shattered the rocks — **but
the LORD was not in the wind**.*

*After the wind came an **earthquake** — **but the LORD was not in the earthquake**.*

*After the earthquake came a **fire** — **but the LORD was not in the fire**.*

Three times the clause repeats.

Every one of them is how God had shown up before. Sinai had all three when the law was
given, in this exact place. Elijah had watched fire fall on Carmel eight verses ago.

He is shown the whole repertoire and told God is not in any of it.

## The fourth

*And after the fire came **a sound. A low, gentle whisper**.*

The Hebrew is **qol demamah daqqah** — a voice of thin silence. **Daq** is what is used
of something ground fine, and **demamah** is stillness, hush.

## The response

*When Elijah heard it, he **pulled his cloak over his face**, went out, and stood at the
mouth of the cave.*

The wind, the earthquake and the fire did not move him out of the cave. The whisper
did — and he covered his face for it.

Moses hid his face at the bush.

## The question again

*Then a voice came to him: "**What are you doing here, Elijah?**"*

Identical. Nothing has been explained.

## The answer again

Word for word the same complaint, minus one clause.

He is not talked out of it. He says it twice and God lets him.

## What he is given instead

*"**Go back the way you came**."*

Three anointings: **Hazael** over Aram, **Jehu** over Israel, and **Elisha** to take his
place.

He asked to die and is given work — including, in the third item, a successor. The
request under the broom tree is answered in a way, and by a man rather than a grave. See
the note on 1 Kings 19:1–8.

Of the three, Elijah personally does only the last: he finds Elisha ploughing with twelve
yoke of oxen and throws his cloak on him.

## The seven thousand

*"Yet **I have kept seven thousand in Israel** — every knee that has **not bowed to
Baal**, every mouth that has **not kissed him**. **You are not alone**."*

The Hebrew is *I have left*, *I have reserved*.

Elijah said it twice — *I'm the only one left* — and the correction comes at the end,
after the anointings, not before.

They had been there through the whole drought. Obadiah's hundred prophets were among
them, in two caves, being fed. See the note on 1 Kings 18:1–6.

## Where Paul quotes it

Romans 11: *"LORD, they have killed Your prophets… and I alone am left." But what does
the divine response say to him? "**I have reserved for Myself seven thousand men who have
not bowed the knee to Baal**." Even so then, at this present time there is a remnant
**according to the election of grace**.*

Paul takes the answer under the mountain and calls it grace.

> **What was never said to him.** No rebuke for running, no reference to Jezebel, no
> mention of Carmel. He was fed, allowed to sleep, asked a question, allowed to answer
> it twice, and then told he was not the only one.
