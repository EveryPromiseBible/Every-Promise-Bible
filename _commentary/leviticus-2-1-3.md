The one offering in Leviticus with no blood in it, and every element is
specified.

## What goes in

- **fine flour** — the Hebrew is *solet*, the finest grade, sifted until there is
  no coarseness left anywhere in it. Nothing is uneven.
- **oil** — poured over it and mixed through it
- **frankincense** — the fragrant resin, laid on top

And the two things that are excluded, a few verses later: no yeast, and no
honey. Nothing that ferments, and nothing that sweetens artificially.

## The memorial portion

*The priest takes a handful of the flour and oil, together with **all** the
frankincense, and burns this memorial portion on the altar.*

A handful of the flour goes into the fire — and all of the incense. The fragrant
part is entirely God's; none of it is kept back.

## The rest is food

*The rest of the grain offering belongs to Aaron and his sons.*

That is the shape of this offering: a portion given, and the remainder becoming
a meal for the priests. It is the only sacrifice in which the bulk of the gift
feeds people.

## What it was

A grain offering was the produce of ordinary work — a farmer's crop, ground and
brought. There is no death in it and no atonement language attached.

Set among the sin offering and the burnt offering, it stands for something
different: a life given over, sifted fine, with oil through it and a fragrance
that rises.

> **Why the details are worth reading.** Christian readers have long seen a
> portrait here — the flour with no unevenness in it, the oil mixed all the way
> through, no leaven and no artificial sweetness, and the whole of the fragrance
> going up to God. The chapter never says so. It simply specifies the recipe, and
> the recipe is exact.
