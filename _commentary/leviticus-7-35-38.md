The end of the sacrificial manual, and it signs off with an inventory.

## The portion

*This is **the portion set apart for Aaron and his sons** from the offerings made by fire.*

The Hebrew is **mishchat** — a word connected to anointing. Some read it as *the anointing
portion*: what came to them by virtue of being anointed.

## The date it started

*Given on **the day they were presented to serve the LORD as priests**. On the day they were
anointed.*

The income began with the office. The oil and the portion are attached to the same day.

## The duration

*For **every generation to come**.*

The Hebrew is **choq olam** — a perpetual statute, and it recurs through the whole book.

## The list

*The **burnt offering**, the **grain offering**, the **sin offering**, the **guilt offering**,
the **ordination offering**, and the **peace offering**.*

Six named, and the peace offering is last.

Five are usually counted as the standing offerings. The sixth, the ordination offering, was
for the priests' installation and happens once.

## What each one was

**Olah**, the burnt offering — wholly consumed, nothing eaten. See the note on
Leviticus 1:3–9.

**Minchah**, the grain offering — flour, oil and frankincense, no blood.

**Chattat**, the sin offering — for unintentional sin, with the highest holiness grade. See
the note on Leviticus 6:24–30.

**Asham**, the guilt offering — with a valuation and restitution attached, and the one Isaiah
names for the Servant. See the note on Leviticus 5:14–19.

**Millu'im**, the ordination offering — literally *fillings*, from the idiom *to fill the
hands*, which is what consecration to the priesthood was called.

**Shelamim**, the peace offering — the only one the worshipper eats. See the note on
Leviticus 3:1–5.

## The order

Leviticus 1 to 7 gives them in this order twice: once to the people, once to the priests.

The peace offering comes last in the summary and third in the original run. The manual does
not explain why.

## Where the instructions came from

*The commands the LORD gave Moses **on Mount Sinai**.*

## Where they were given to the people

*There in **the Wilderness of Sinai**.*

Two locations in one sentence — the mountain where Moses received them and the desert floor
where he passed them on.

The book began with the same distinction: a voice out of the tent, and a message for the
Israelites. See the note on Leviticus 1:1–2.

## What the manual is

Seven chapters of procedure: which animal, which hand, where the blood goes, what burns, who
eats, and how long they have to finish it.

Nothing in it is left to judgment.

## What the next chapter does

Consecrates Aaron. Blood on the right ear, the right thumb, the right big toe. Seven days
inside the door of the tabernacle.

And then chapter 9: fire out from before the LORD, the people shouting and falling on their
faces.

And chapter 10: two of the sons dead the same week. See the note on
Leviticus 10:12–15.

## What Hebrews says about the whole apparatus

*For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and goats could take away sins.* See the note
on Hebrews 10:3–4.

And *by that will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ
once for all*.

Six offerings, listed here as a perpetual statute, and a letter written to Hebrews arguing
that one of them happened once.

> **What the sign-off contains.** Six names and a date. The priests' income started on the day
> they were anointed, and the manual closes by listing everything it has covered.
