Three verses on why an argument fails to land, and the reason is not intelligence.

## The man

*The person who **lives only by his natural life**.*

The Greek is **psuchikos anthropos** — soulish man. From **psuche**, soul, life.

Paul's three-way vocabulary in this letter: **sarkikos** (fleshly), **psuchikos** (natural), and
**pneumatikos** (spiritual).

**Psuchikos** does not mean stupid or immoral. It means operating on the equipment everybody
has.

## What he does

*Does **not welcome the things of the Spirit of God**.*

The Greek is **ou dechetai** — does not receive, does not accept.

The word is about welcoming a guest. Not *cannot hear*. Does not take in.

## The verdict he reaches

*For to him **they are foolishness**.*

**Moria** — folly. The word Paul used two chapters earlier: *the message of the blood covenant is
foolishness to those who are perishing*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 1:18–25.

## The reason

*And **he is not able to know them**, because they are **discerned in a spiritual way**.*

The Greek is **ou dunatai gnonai** — is not able to know.

**Pneumatikos anakrinetai** — spiritually examined.

## The word

**Anakrino** — to examine, to sift, to investigate. A legal word for a preliminary hearing.

It is used three times in these two verses.

The claim is not that the natural man lacks information. It is that the instrument is wrong for
the material.

## The reversal

*But **the one who is spiritual weighs all things, while he himself is weighed by no one**.*

Same verb both times.

## What that does not mean

Not that a spiritual person is above correction. Paul submits himself to examination constantly:
*with me it is a very small thing that I should be judged by you or by a human court*, and then
*He who judges me is the Lord*.

The sentence is about category, not immunity. Someone using a different instrument cannot assess
the result.

## The quotation

*For **who has known the mind of the Lord, that he should instruct Him?**"*

Isaiah 40:13: *who has directed the Spirit of the LORD, or as His counsellor has taught Him?* See
the note on Isaiah 40:12–14.

The answer expected is nobody.

## The turn

*Yet **we have the mind of Christ**.*

The Greek is **hemeis de noun Christou echomen**.

Isaiah's question had no answer. Paul quotes it and then answers it.

## The pronoun

**Hemeis** — we. Plural, and emphatic.

Not *I*, and not *some believers*. He is writing to a divided congregation with sexual scandal,
lawsuits between members, drunkenness at the Lord's table, and factions named after teachers.

He tells them they have the mind of Christ.

## Where the chapter started

*And I, brethren, when I came to you, did not come with excellence of speech or of wisdom… **I was
with you in weakness, in fear, and in much trembling**.*

*And my speech and my preaching were not with persuasive words of human wisdom, **but in
demonstration of the Spirit and of power**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 2:1–5.

## What the Spirit does

*But God has revealed them to us through His Spirit. For **the Spirit searches all things, yes, the
deep things of God**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 2:10–13.

*For what man knows the things of a man except the spirit of the man which is in him?*

## What follows

*And I, brethren, could not speak to you as to spiritual people **but as to carnal, as to babes in
Christ**.*

Chapter 3 opens by telling the same people they are not behaving like it.

Paul states the possession and then addresses the behaviour, in that order.

> **The verb.** **Anakrino** — to examine, to sift. Three times in two verses. The claim is about
> which instrument the material answers to, not about how clever anybody is.
