Paul is writing to a church he has never met, and the thing he is guarding them
against is not immorality. It is persuasive speech.

## "Talk you around"

*I say this so that no one may **talk you around** with words that sound
persuasive.*

The Greek verb is a reckoning word — to miscount, to defraud someone by faulty
arithmetic, and so to beguile with plausible reasoning.

The danger named is not error that sounds wrong. It is error that adds up.

## Good order and firm faith

*I am glad to see the good order among you and how firm your faith in Christ
stands.*

Both nouns are military. **Taxis** is a rank drawn up in formation; **stereōma**
is a solid front, a bulwark.

Paul, writing from prison to people he has never seen, describes them as a line
that is holding.

## The rule

*Just as you **received** Christ Jesus the Lord, **keep on walking** in Him.*

Two verbs, and the second is present tense — continuous. However you got in is
how you go on.

You received Him — you did not achieve Him, negotiate Him, or qualify for Him.
Walk the same way.

## The four participles

Then four pictures stacked in one sentence, and they do not come from one world:

- **rooted in Him** — agricultural, and the tense is a completed act; the root
  went down once
- **being built up in Him** — architectural, and this one is ongoing
- **made firm in the faith exactly as you were taught** — legal, the word used of
  a contract being confirmed
- **overflowing with thanksgiving** — a river over its banks

Rooted is done. Built up is happening. That order is deliberate.

> **What the chapter goes on to name.** Philosophy and empty deceit, human
> tradition, food rules, festivals, new moons, sabbaths, angel worship. All of it
> answered by one sentence: *in Him all the fullness of the Deity lives in bodily
> form, and you have been made full in Him.*
