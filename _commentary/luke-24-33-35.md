They had just persuaded Him to stay the night, and then they walk seven miles in the dark.

## The hour

*They got up **that same hour**.*

An hour earlier they had said *abide with us, for it is toward evening, and the day is far
spent*. See the note on Luke 24:28–32.

The reason they gave for stopping is still true. It is now darker.

## The distance

Emmaus is sixty stadia from Jerusalem — about seven miles.

They had walked it once already that day, going the other way, *and they stood still,
looking sad*.

## What they had said on the road

*"We were hoping that it was He who was going to redeem Israel."*

Past tense. They were leaving.

## Who they found

*And they found **the eleven and the others gathered together**.*

The eleven — Judas is dead, and Luke keeps the count exact.

## What the room says first

*"**The Lord has really risen, and He has appeared to Simon**."*

The Greek is **ontos** — really, actually.

The two arrive bursting with news and are told the news before they can give it.

## The appearance to Simon

Nothing else is recorded about it. Luke mentions it in one clause and never returns to it.

Paul has it in his list: *He was seen by Cephas, then by the twelve*. It is the first
resurrection appearance he names.

Somewhere between the empty tomb and this evening, Jesus met Peter alone, and neither Luke
nor Paul says a word about what passed between them.

The last thing recorded before it is Peter going out and weeping bitterly. See the note on
Matthew 26:69–75.

## Their turn

*And the two of them told **what had happened on the road**, and **how they had known Him
when He broke the bread**.*

Two things: the walk, and the moment.

The Greek is **egnosthe** — He was made known, passive.

## What the walk had been

*Beginning at Moses and all the Prophets, **He expounded to them in all the Scriptures the
things concerning Himself**.* See the note on Luke 24:25–27.

The longest Bible study in the New Testament, and no one wrote it down.

## The moment

*Now it came to pass, as He sat at the table with them, that **He took bread, blessed and
broke it, and gave it to them**. Then their eyes were opened and they knew Him; and **He
vanished from their sight**.*

Four verbs — took, blessed, broke, gave — the same four as the feeding of the five thousand
and the upper room.

He is recognised in the action, and is gone in the same sentence.

## What happens next

*Now as they said these things, **Jesus Himself stood in the midst of them**.*

He arrives while they are still comparing notes, and asks for something to eat. See the note
on Luke 24:36–43.

## Who these two were

One is named: **Cleopas**. The other is not.

Neither is one of the eleven. The first extended resurrection appearance in Luke is given to
two people outside the inner circle, one of whom nobody can name.

## What they did with the evening

Walked seven miles back, at night, to tell people who already knew.

Luke reports it without remarking on it.

> **What was said before they could speak.** They ran seven miles with news, and the room
> announced it to them first — *He has appeared to Simon*, and that is the whole record of
> it.
