Paul has just spent twelve verses listing what these believers possess. Then he
prays — and he does not ask for a single additional thing.

## What he asks for

*The Spirit of wisdom and of unveiling, in coming to know Him — that the eyes of
your understanding would be flooded with light.*

The word behind *unveiling* is **apokalypsis**, the uncovering of something
already there. Not new information. The removal of whatever is in the way of
seeing it.

And *the eyes of your understanding* is literally *the eyes of your heart* — the
seeing organ Paul is worried about is not the intellect alone.

## The three things he wants them to see

- **what the hope is that He called you into**
- **how glorious and rich a share He has among His holy people**
- **how far past measuring His power is toward us who believe**

The second one is worth reading twice. The Greek most naturally means **His**
inheritance in the saints — God's own share, and the people are it. Paul has
already said in verse 11 that we have obtained an inheritance; now he prays they
would grasp that they *are* one.

## The prayer is for perception, not supply

That is the whole shape of it. Everything in chapter 1 has already been given —
chosen, adopted, redeemed, forgiven, sealed. Paul's response is to pray that they
would know it.

He does the same thing in chapter 3, where the prayer is that they would have
power to grasp the dimensions of a love that surpasses knowledge.

> **The measure of the power.** *Power in step with the working of His mighty
> strength* — and then the next verse says what strength that was: the force that
> raised Christ and seated Him above every authority. Paul's answer to "how much
> power is available" is to point at the resurrection. See the note on Ephesians
> 1:20–23.
