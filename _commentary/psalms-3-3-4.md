The first psalm with a title, and the situation behind it is the worst week of David's life.

## The heading

*A psalm of David when he fled from Absalom his son.*

His son had spent four years standing at the gate telling people the king would not hear them,
and then declared himself king at Hebron.

*So David said, "Arise, and let us flee… lest he overtake us suddenly and bring disaster upon
us."*

He went up the Mount of Olives barefoot, weeping, with his head covered.

## What he says first

*LORD, **how they have increased who trouble me!** Many are they who rise up against me.*

*Many are they who say of me, "**There is no help for him in God**." Selah*

The taunt is theological. They are not saying he will lose. They are saying God has finished with
him.

Which, after Bathsheba and Uriah and Nathan's *the sword shall never depart from your house*, is
an argument with something behind it.

## The turn

*But **You, LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **ve'attah YHWH** — and You, LORD.

Two words, and they carry the whole psalm.

## The shield

*Are **a shield all around me**.*

The Hebrew is **magen ba'adi** — a shield about me.

Not a shield in front. Around.

## The glory

***My glory**.*

The Hebrew is **kevodi**.

He is walking out of his own capital with his head covered and dust on it, and he calls God his
glory.

## The head

*And **the One who lifts my head**.*

The Hebrew is **umerim roshi** — and the lifter of my head.

## What the idiom means

Covering the head was the posture of mourning and shame — *David went up… and had his head
covered*.

Lifting the head is the reversal. Pharaoh's butler had his head lifted and was restored; the baker
had his lifted off.

## The cry

*I **cried out loud** to the LORD, and **He answered me** from His holy hill.*

The Hebrew is **qoli el-YHWH eqra** — with my voice I call to the LORD.

Aloud. Not silently.

## Where the answer comes from

*From **His holy hill**.*

Mount Zion, which David has just been driven off.

He is on the run from the city, and the answer comes from the hill he is running away from.

## What he does that night

*I lay down and slept; **I awoke, for the LORD sustained me**.*

The Hebrew is **ani shachavti va'ishanah heqitzoti** — I lay down, I slept, I woke.

Three verbs, and every one of them is unremarkable. A man being hunted by his son's army sleeps
through the night and gets up.

## The number

*I will not be afraid of **ten thousands of people** who have set themselves against me all
around.*

The same word — **saviv**, all around — used of the shield in verse 3.

They are around him, and so is the shield.

## The last line

*Salvation belongs to the LORD. **Your blessing is upon Your people**. Selah*

The Hebrew is **laYHWH hayeshu'ah**.

A man fleeing for his life ends the psalm by asking for a blessing on the nation, part of which is
currently chasing him.

## What happens to Absalom

Caught by the head in the branches of a terebinth, and killed by Joab against orders.

*O my son Absalom — my son, my son Absalom — if only I had died in your place!*

> **The three verbs.** *I lay down, I slept, I awoke.* Written by a man whose son's army was
> hunting him, and the only remarkable thing about them is that they are unremarkable.
