The last five verses of Matthew, and there is a clause in the middle nobody quotes.

## The place

*The eleven disciples went to **Galilee, to the mountain where Jesus had told them to go**.*

Not Jerusalem. Home ground — where He had called them, taught the crowds, and fed the five
thousand.

He had said it twice: on the walk to Gethsemane, *after I have been raised, I will go before you
to Galilee*, and at the tomb through the angel.

## The number

*The eleven.*

Matthew keeps the count exact. Judas is dead and not replaced yet.

## The worship

*And when they saw Him they **fell down and worshiped Him**.*

The Greek is **prosekunesan** — prostrated themselves.

## The clause

*Though **some of them were still unsure**.*

The Greek is **hoi de edistasan** — but they doubted.

**Distazo** occurs twice in the New Testament, and the other is Peter on the water: *O you of
little faith, **why did you doubt?***

The word is closer to hesitation than to disbelief. Divided, wavering, in two minds.

Matthew records it and does not resolve it. The men receiving the commission include some who
are not sure.

## The claim

*"**All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Me**."*

The Greek is **pasa exousia** — all authority.

*Has been given* — a passive, and Daniel is behind it: *to Him was given dominion and glory and
a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve Him*.

It is also what was offered on a mountain three years earlier, for one act of worship, and
refused. See the note on Matthew 4:8–11.

Same offer, different mountain, different price.

## The one imperative

*"**So go — go and make disciples of all the nations**."*

The Greek has one main verb: **matheteusate**, make disciples.

*Go*, *baptising* and *teaching* are participles hanging off it. The older translations make
*go* an imperative; the grammar makes it circumstantial — *as you go*.

## The name

*"Baptizing them in **the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit**."*

**The name**, singular, with three genitives.

The clearest trinitarian formula in the Gospels, and it is in a baptism instruction rather than
in a doctrinal passage.

## The teaching

*"And teaching them **to live out everything I have commanded you**."*

The Greek is **terein** — to keep, to observe, to guard.

Not to know it. To keep it.

## The promise

*"And remember, **I am with you always, right to the very end of the age**."*

The Greek is **ego meth' humon eimi** — I, with you, am. The pronoun is emphatic.

## Where Matthew started

*"They shall call His name **Immanuel**," which is translated, "**God with us**."*

First chapter and last chapter, and the same three words.

The Gospel opens with a name and closes with the thing the name means, said in the first
person.

## Who it is said to

Eleven men on a hill, some of whom are still hesitating.

The commission is not conditional on the hesitation clearing up.

> **The clause in the middle.** *Some doubted.* Matthew puts it between the worship and the
> commission, and does not come back to it.
