Peter says three times in three verses that Lot was righteous, which nothing in Genesis
prepares you for.

## The list Peter is working through

Angels who sinned, cast down.

The ancient world, and Noah *a preacher of righteousness* with seven others.

Sodom and Gomorrah, turned to ashes. See the note on 2 Peter 2:4–6.

Three examples of judgment, and in the middle of the third he stops for Lot.

## The rescue

*And **He pulled Lot out**.*

The Greek is **errusato** — delivered, dragged out of danger.

Genesis is literal about it: *while he lingered, the men took hold of his hand, his wife's
hand, and the hands of his two daughters… and they brought him out and set him outside the
city*. See the note on Genesis 19:15–17.

He had to be pulled by the wrist.

## The verdict

*A man **in the right**.*

The Greek is **dikaion**.

Peter says it three times: righteous Lot, his righteous soul, and *the godly*.

Nothing in the Genesis account says it. Lot chooses the well-watered plain, pitches toward
Sodom, ends up sitting in its gate, offers his daughters to a mob, argues with the angels about
the destination, and ends the chapter in a cave.

Peter reads the same chapters and files him under righteous.

## What it cost him

*Worn down to nothing by **the filth those people lived in**.*

The Greek is **kataponoumenon** — worn out, exhausted, oppressed to the point of collapse.

## The daily part

*He **saw it, he heard it**, and **day after day their lawlessness scraped away at his soul**.*

The Greek is **ebasanizen** — tormented, tortured. The word for putting somebody to the
question.

And **hemeran ex hemeras** — day out of day.

Two senses named, and the erosion is described as continuous.

He was not enjoying it. That is Peter's point, and it is the only evidence he offers.

## The conclusion

*So **the Lord knows exactly how to lift out the people who live toward Him when trials close
in**.*

The Greek is **oiden kurios eusebeis ek peirasmou ruesthai** — the Lord knows how to rescue
the godly out of trial.

**Oiden** — knows how. It is a statement about competence.

## The other half

*And He knows exactly how to **hold the unjust under guard until the day of judgment**.*

The Greek is **tereien** — to keep, to guard, to reserve.

Both halves are the same verb of knowing. He knows how to get one out and how to hold the other.

## What Genesis says about why Lot got out

*And it came to pass, when God destroyed the cities of the plain, that **God remembered
Abraham, and sent Lot out** of the midst of the overthrow.* See the note on
Genesis 19:27–29.

Genesis credits Abraham's intercession. Peter credits Lot's own state.

Both are in the text and neither cancels the other.

## Where Abraham had got to

Down to ten. Six requests, six grants, and he stopped. See the note on
Genesis 18:27–33.

There were not ten. There was one, and one was enough for a rescue if not for a city.

## Why Peter is making the argument

The chapter is about false teachers — *they promise them liberty, while they themselves are
slaves of corruption*.

The point of the Lot paragraph is the first half of verse 9. If God got that man out of that
city, the readers under pressure can be got out too.

## Where a verdict like that comes from

Peter calls Lot **righteous** three times in three verses — righteous Lot, his righteous soul, that
righteous man — about someone whose recorded conduct in Genesis includes pitching toward Sodom,
sitting in its gate, offering his daughters to a mob, having to be dragged out by the hand, and
ending in a cave.

Genesis never once calls him righteous. Peter does, repeatedly, and the New Testament explains where
a verdict like that comes from:

*But to him who **does not work but believes on Him who justifies the ungodly**, his faith is
accounted for righteousness.*

That is Paul's sentence about grace, and Lot is its illustration. The righteousness is imputed, not observed —
credited by God to a man who did nothing to earn the description and quite a lot to contradict it.

Which is exactly why the passage can promise that *the Lord knows how to deliver the godly*. If the
category depended on performance, Lot would not be in it, and neither would anybody reading.

> **The only evidence Peter offers.** Not Lot's decisions, which were poor. That living there
> hurt him, every day — and Peter builds the word *righteous* on it three times.
