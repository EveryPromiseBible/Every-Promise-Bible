Not everything brought to the tabernacle was owned. Some of it was made.

## The spinners

*Every woman with skill **spun with her own hands** and brought what she had spun.*

The Hebrew is **kol-ishah chakhmat-lev** — every woman wise of heart.

**Chokmah** — wisdom. The same noun used of Bezalel two chapters later: *filled with the Spirit of
God, in wisdom and understanding*.

The word for wisdom in Hebrew covers craft skill. It is not divided into intellectual and
practical.

## What they made

*Blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine linen.*

The four materials of the tabernacle's inner curtains, the veil, and the priestly garments.

**Tekhelet, argaman, tola'at shani, shesh** — the colours that recur through the specification
every time something holy is described.

## What it cost to make them

Blue came from a sea creature, purple from murex shells, scarlet from a crushed insect.

Linen from flax, retted, beaten, combed, and spun by hand into thread fine enough for temple
cloth.

Nobody bought this. It was months of work.

## The second group

*And all the women **whose hearts moved them and who had the gift** spun the goat hair.*

Goat hair was the second covering, over the fine curtains — the outer, rougher layer nobody would
see once the tent was up.

The text names those women separately, and uses the same two clauses about the heart.

## The leaders

*The leaders brought **onyx stones and other gems** to be set in the ephod and the breastpiece.*

**Nesi'im** — the tribal chiefs.

The stones with the twelve names engraved on them, worn on the priest's shoulders and over his
heart whenever he went in.

## What else

*Along with **spices and olive oil for the light, for the anointing oil, and for the fragrant
incense**.*

Three uses: the lamp that burned continually, the oil that consecrated, and the incense burned
morning and evening.

## The summary

*So the Israelites — **every man and woman whose heart made them willing** — brought a freewill
offering to the LORD.*

The Hebrew is **nedavah** — freewill offering.

The phrase about the heart appears five times in this chapter.

## What is being recorded

Two kinds of giving, and the text does not rank them.

Some people brought what they owned — the jewellery, the yarn, the acacia wood. See the note on
Exodus 35:20–24.

Some brought what they could do. The spinners had skill, and their skill is called wisdom.

## The named craftsmen

*See, **the LORD has called by name Bezalel**… and I have filled him with the Spirit of God, in
wisdom, in understanding, in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship.* See the note on
Exodus 31:1–5.

The first person in the Bible said to be filled with the Spirit of God is a metalworker.

*And I have put wisdom in the hearts of all who are gifted artisans.*

## What was made from it

A tent, in a desert, by ex-slaves, using materials they carried out of Egypt and skills they must
have learned there.

*Then the cloud covered the tabernacle of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the
tabernacle.*

## The final sentence of the book

*For the cloud of the LORD was above the tabernacle by day, and fire was over it by night, **in the
sight of all the house of Israel, throughout all their journeys**.*

> **What the spinners brought.** Not something they owned — something they made. The Hebrew calls
> their skill **wisdom**, the same word used of the man who built the ark.
