Abram turns down a fortune in a single sentence, and gives his reason.

## The offer

*"**Give me the people and keep the goods for yourself.**"*

By the war conventions of the time everything was Abram's — he had won it. The king
of Sodom is asking for a favour and calling it a deal.

It is a fortune, offered legitimately, with no strings visible.

## The oath

*"**I have raised my hand to the LORD, God Most High, Creator of heaven and earth,
and sworn an oath.**"*

He had already sworn — the Hebrew is past tense. This was decided before the king
opened his mouth.

And notice the title he uses: *God Most High, Creator of heaven and earth* — word
for word what Melchizedek had just called Him in the blessing three verses earlier.

He takes the priest's language and swears by it.

## The refusal

*"I will accept nothing belonging to you, **not even a thread or the strap of a
sandal**."*

The two most worthless items he can name. A loose thread and a sandal lace.

## The reason

*"**So that you can never say, 'I made Abram rich.'**"*

That is the whole of it. Not that the money was dirty, or that he did not need it.

He would not have anyone else able to claim credit for his prosperity — and Sodom
in particular.

## What he did take

*"I will take nothing but **what my men have eaten** and **the share belonging to
the men who went with me** — Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre. **Let them have their
share**."*

He refuses on his own behalf and refuses to impose the decision on anyone else. His
allies are Amorites; they get their portion.

The conviction is his, and he does not legislate it for other people.

## The two transactions, in order

Within the same hour:

- **gave** a tenth to the priest of God Most High
- **refused** everything from the king of Sodom

He gave to one and would take nothing from the other, and the two decisions are the
same decision seen from both ends: about who he wanted to be indebted to.

See the note on Genesis 14:17–20.

## What God says next

The very next verse — Genesis 15:1:

*"Do not be afraid, Abram. **I am your shield, your exceedingly great reward.**"*

He turned down a reward and was told who his reward was. The Hebrew for *great
reward* is the word for wages.

> **What happened to the goods he refused.** Sodom is destroyed five chapters later,
> and Lot — who went back to live in it — comes out with nothing but his daughters
> and the clothes he stands in. Abram, who took not a thread of it, dies *an old
> man and full of years*, rich in livestock, silver and gold.
