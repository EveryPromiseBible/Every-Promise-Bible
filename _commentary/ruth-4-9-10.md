The transaction is done in public, at the gate, in front of the whole town.

## Where it happens

The gate — the courthouse of an Israelite town, where elders sat and business was witnessed.

The same place Abraham weighed out four hundred shekels for a field with a cave in it, in
front of all the Hittites, so that it could never be undone. See the note on
Genesis 23:17–20.

## What he says he has bought

*"I have bought from Naomi **everything that belonged to Elimelech and to Chilion and
Mahlon**."*

The whole estate of a dead family. Not the profitable part.

## And who comes with it

*"**And Ruth the Moabite, Mahlon's widow, I have taken to be my wife.**"*

The field and the widow are one transaction. That is what the nearer kinsman balked at — he
would take the land and not the woman, and the law would not let him separate them. See the
note on Ruth 3:10–13.

## Why

*"**So that the dead man's name will live on with his inheritance** and not vanish from among
his family or from the gate of his town."*

The child will legally be Mahlon's. Boaz pays the full price for an inheritance that will be
recorded under another man's name.

## What the office required

**Goel** — near enough, able to pay, and willing. All three, and no law could supply the third.

*For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, **yet for your sakes
He became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich.*

## The purchase

*In whom we have **redemption through His blood**, the forgiveness of sins, according to the
riches of His grace.*

*For **you were bought at a price**; therefore glorify God in your body.*

And the same public shape: *this thing was not done in a corner.*

## The witnesses

*"**You are witnesses today.**"*

Said twice in two verses, at the beginning and the end.

*Whom God set forth as a propitiation by His blood, through faith, **to demonstrate His
righteousness**.*

The redemption of a Moabite widow was transacted where everybody could see it, and so was the
other one.

> **What was bought.** Not the field on its own. The nearer kinsman wanted that and refused
> the rest; Boaz took the whole estate of a dead family and the foreign widow with it, and
> paid for a name that would not be his.
