Four things happen at the moment Jesus dies, and the first one is reported with
a detail that carries the whole meaning.

## The curtain

*The curtain of the temple was torn in two, **from the top all the way down to
the bottom**.*

The veil separated the Most Holy Place from everything else. Behind it was the
place a single man entered once a year with blood, in smoke, having first atoned
for himself. See the note on Leviticus 16:11–14.

It was not opened. It was **torn**, and torn from the end nobody could reach.

Matthew does not explain it. He records the direction and moves on.

## The earthquake

*The ground shook and the rocks split apart.*

Matthew has a pattern of noting the earth's reaction — there is another quake at
the resurrection two chapters on, and it is the centurion's response to this one
that produces the confession *truly this was the Son of God.*

## The tombs

*Tombs broke open, and the bodies of many of God's people who had died were
raised to life.*

The strangest paragraph in the Gospel, and Matthew alone reports it. He is
careful about the timing: the tombs open at the death, but the people do not come
out until **after** the resurrection.

Even here, Christ is kept as the firstfruits — nobody precedes Him out of a
grave.

## What Matthew does not do

He offers no interpretation of any of it. Four events, reported flat, in two
sentences.

The reader is left to notice that at the moment the sacrifice was finished, the
barrier came down, the ground broke, and death started letting people go.

> **Where the letter to the Hebrews takes it.** *We have confidence to enter the
> holy places by the blood of Jesus, by the new and living way that He opened for
> us through the curtain, that is, through His flesh.* The torn veil and the torn
> body are put together, and the conclusion drawn is an invitation to come in. See
> the note on Hebrews 4:14–16.
