---
key: Exodus 28:31–35
title: Bells and pomegranates
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The high priest's robe has a hem that makes a noise, and the reason given is so the people
outside know he is still alive.

## The colour

*"Weave the robe that goes under the ephod **entirely of blue**."*

The Hebrew is **kelil tekhelet** — wholly blue. One colour, no pattern, no variation.

**Tekhelet** is the blue of the tabernacle's inner curtains and of the cord on the tassel every
Israelite was to wear. It is the colour of the sky, and the dye came from a sea creature at
enormous cost.

## The collar

*"Give it an opening for the head in the center, with a woven collar around it, **like the neck
of body armor, so it won't tear**."*

The Hebrew notes the reinforcement specifically: **lo yiqqarea** — it shall not be torn.

The garment is woven, not cut and sewn, and the neck is finished so that the whole thing holds
together in one piece.

*Now the tunic was **without seam, woven from the top in one piece**. They said therefore among
themselves, "**Let us not tear it**, but cast lots for it, whose it shall be."*

Roman soldiers, at the foot of a cross, looking at a seamless garment and deciding not to tear
it.

## The hem

*"Around the hem, sew **pomegranates** of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, with **gold bells**
between them all the way around — a golden bell and a pomegranate, a golden bell and a
pomegranate."*

Alternating, the whole way round. Sound and fruit, one after the other.

A pomegranate is full of seed. A bell is empty, and it is the emptiness that lets it ring.

## What the sound was for

*"Aaron wears it when he ministers, and **its sound will be heard as he enters the Holy Place
before the LORD and as he comes out — so that he will not die**."*

The priest goes in alone and nobody can follow him. The congregation stands outside in silence
and listens to the hem.

While the bells are moving, he is alive and the work is going on. The sound is the only
evidence they have, and it is enough.

## What happened when he came out

He came out. That is the part the bells report.

*But Christ came as High Priest of the good things to come… **not with the blood of goats and
calves, but with His own blood** He entered the Most Holy Place once for all, having obtained
eternal redemption.* See the note on Hebrews 9:11–12.

He went in, and He came out, and the people standing outside did not have to listen for a bell
to know it — He was seen by more than five hundred at once.

## What was torn instead

Not the robe.

*And behold, **the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom**.*

The seamless garment was kept whole and the curtain that kept everybody out was ripped, and
after that nobody had to stand in the courtyard listening for a hem.

> **What the congregation could hear.** A golden bell and a pomegranate, all the way round —
> and the reason Exodus gives for the noise is so that the people outside would know the man
> inside was still alive.
