Job tells his three friends exactly what they are, and then tells them what he
would have done in their place.

## The name he gives them

*"**Comforters? You are a misery, every one of you.**"*

The Hebrew is a play on words — *comforters of trouble*, comforters who are
themselves the trouble. It is the phrase that gave English the expression *Job's
comforters*.

They came to comfort him. Chapter 2 says so: they *made an appointment together to
come and mourn with him and comfort him.* That was the intention.

## "I have heard all this before"

*"I have heard all this before, and heard it many times. **Is there no end to your
windy words?**"*

The Hebrew for *windy* is words of wind — air.

And there is a question after it: *what is it that stirs you up to keep
answering?* He is asking what is driving them. Nobody has asked them that.

## What he could do

*"I could talk the way you talk, **if it were you sitting where I sit**. I could
pile up fine speeches against you and shake my head over you."*

The whole argument collapses into that clause. If the positions were reversed, he
could produce the same material.

Their theology costs them nothing to hold. It costs him everything to be under.

## What he says he would do instead

*"**But no. I would use my mouth to steady you**, and the comfort of my lips would
ease your pain."*

That is the sentence. A man scraping himself with a piece of broken pot, having
buried ten children, describing what he would have said to them.

The Hebrew for *steady* is to strengthen, to make firm — and *ease your pain* is
the verb for restraining, holding something back.

He is telling them the thing they are for, and none of the three does it.

## And then the honest line

*"Yet whether I speak, my grief is not eased, and if I stay silent, it does not
leave me either."*

There is no position he can take that helps. Speaking does not relieve it; silence
does not either.

That is where he is, and the book leaves it on the page without softening it.

## What he wants

*"**Oh, that one might plead for a man with God**, as a man pleads for his neighbour!"*

He says it a few verses later, and it is the cry the whole book is reaching for. Earlier:
*"nor is there **any mediator** between us, who may lay his hand on us both."*

*For there is one God and **one Mediator between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus**.*

Job asks for the office by name. **Paul** says it exists and names who holds it.

## What he already believes

*"Even now, behold, **my witness is in heaven, and my evidence is on high**."*

He has three men in front of him calling themselves comforters, and he says his real witness
is somewhere else.

*My little children, these things I write to you, so that you may not sin. And if anyone sins,
**we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous**.*

**John**'s word is **parakletos** — one called alongside, a defence counsel.

## What he says he would do in their place

*"**I also could speak as you do**, if your soul were in my soul's place… but I would
strengthen you with my mouth, and the comfort of my lips would relieve your grief."*

He does not claim he would have better arguments. He says he would use them differently.

> **What they had already done right.** Seven days on the ground with him and not a
> word, *for they saw that his suffering was very great.* The best thing the three
> of them managed was the week before any of them opened their mouths.
