The second attempt, and everything about it is different except the destination.

## What started it again

*Then word reached David: "**The LORD has blessed Obed-edom's whole household**… because of the
ark of God."*

Three months of a household visibly prospering, and the news travels. See the note on
2 Samuel 6:9–11.

The report is what gets David moving.

## The difference

*So David went down and brought the ark of God up from Obed-edom's house **into the city of
David — this time with rejoicing**.*

The Chronicler supplies what the phrase covers:

*"**No one may carry the ark of God but the Levites**… because you did not do it the first
time, the LORD our God broke out against us, **because we did not consult Him about the proper
order**."*

*And the children of the Levites **bore the ark of God on their shoulders, by its poles**, as
Moses had commanded according to the word of the LORD.*

Shoulders and poles. No cart.

## The six steps

*And whenever those carrying the ark had gone **six steps**, David stopped and **sacrificed an
ox and a fattened calf**.*

Six steps.

Read strictly, that is a sacrifice every few yards for six miles, which is not what happened —
the Hebrew most likely means when they had gone six steps at the start, David sacrificed.

Either way, the first act after the ark moves is blood.

The last time it moved, the first thing that happened was a death. This time the death happens
first and it is an animal's.

## The dancing

*And David **danced before the LORD with all his might**.*

The Hebrew is **mekarker bekol-oz** — whirling with all strength.

A king of Israel, dancing in the street.

## What he was wearing

*A simple **linen ephod**.*

Not royal dress. A linen ephod is priests' clothing — a plain garment, and the Chronicler adds
that he also wore *a robe of fine linen*.

He takes off what marks him as king and puts on what marks a servant.

## The noise

*So David and all Israel brought up the ark of the LORD with **shouts of joy and the blast of
the ram's horn**.*

## The window

*Now as the ark of the LORD came into the City of David, **Michal, Saul's daughter, looked
through a window and saw King David leaping and whirling before the LORD; and she despised him
in her heart**.*

And afterwards: *"How glorious was the king of Israel today, uncovering himself today in the
eyes of the maids of his servants, as one of the base fellows shamelessly uncovers himself!"*

David's answer: *"It was before the LORD… **I will be even more undignified than this**, and
will be humble in my own sight."*

And the chapter's last line: *therefore Michal the daughter of Saul had no children to the day
of her death*.

## Where the ark ends up

*In its place, in the midst of the tabernacle that David had erected for it.*

Not the tabernacle of Moses, which was at Gibeon with the altar and the priests. A tent David
pitched in Jerusalem.

For roughly forty years the ark sat in a tent in the capital with no veil in front of it, and
singers around it day and night, while the sacrifices continued six miles away.

That arrangement is what Amos calls *the tabernacle of David that is fallen*, and what James
quotes at the Jerusalem council. See the note on Acts 15:13–18.

## What David does at the end of the day

*And he distributed among all the people, the whole multitude of Israel, both the women and the
men, to everyone a loaf of bread, a piece of meat, and a cake of raisins.*

Everybody goes home with food.

> **What the second attempt cost.** Three months of not moving, and then blood before the first
> six steps — and a king who took off the crown and danced in a linen ephod.
