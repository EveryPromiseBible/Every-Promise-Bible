He proves He is not a ghost by asking for supper.

## When this happens

*While they were **still talking about it**.*

The two from Emmaus had just walked seven miles back to Jerusalem to report that they had
seen Him, and were mid-sentence.

## The arrival

*Jesus Himself **stood there among them**.*

John adds that the doors were shut.

## The greeting

*"**Peace be with you**."*

**Shalom aleichem** — the ordinary Jewish greeting, said in the least ordinary circumstances
in history.

The last thing most of them had done was run away from His arrest.

## The reaction

*They were **startled and frightened**, and thought they were **seeing a spirit**.*

The Greek is **pneuma** — a ghost.

They had just been told He was alive, and their reaction to His being alive is terror.

## The questions

*"**Why are you shaken? Why are these doubts coming up in your hearts?**"*

The Greek for *doubts* is **dialogismoi** — reasonings, arguments running in the mind.

He treats the doubt as something happening in the heart rather than as a moral failure, and
He answers it with evidence.

## The evidence offered

*"**Look at My hands and My feet — it is I Myself. Touch Me and see**."*

Three senses invited in one sentence: look, touch, see.

The hands and feet are the identifying marks, which means the wounds are still there in the
risen body.

## The argument

*"**A spirit does not have flesh and bones**, as you can see that I have."*

**Flesh and bones** — not *flesh and blood*, which is Paul's phrase for what *cannot inherit
the kingdom of God*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:50–52.

Luke, a physician, records the distinction.

## The reason they still could not believe

*They **still could not believe it, for sheer joy**, and they were amazed.*

The Greek is *while they still disbelieved for joy and marvelled*.

Luke gives the most sympathetic account of unbelief anywhere in Scripture. They did not
doubt because they were hard. They doubted because it was too good.

## The request

*"**Do you have anything here to eat?**"*

The resurrected Christ asks for food.

## What He was given

*A piece of **broiled fish**.*

*And **He took it and ate it in front of them**.*

The Greek is **enopion auton** — in their presence. He eats where they can watch Him
swallow.

Peter cites it later, to Cornelius: *God raised Him up on the third day… to witnesses chosen
before by God, even to us **who ate and drank with Him after He arose from the dead**.* See
the note on Acts 10:34–43.

## The other fish

Breakfast on the shore of Galilee, and Peter in the water. See the note on John 21:4–8.

Twice after the resurrection, He is at a meal with fish on it.

## What He does next

*"These are the words which I spoke to you while I was still with you, that all things must
be fulfilled which were written in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms
concerning Me."*

*And He **opened their understanding**, that they might comprehend the Scriptures.*

The same verb He used on the road to Emmaus, where *He expounded to them in all the
Scriptures the things concerning Himself*. See the note on Luke 24:25–27.

## What the body is like

Doors do not stop it, and it eats fish. It can be handled and it can arrive.

Luke reports both without reconciling them.

> **The proof He picked.** Not an explanation. He held out His hands, invited them to touch,
> and then asked whether there was anything to eat.
