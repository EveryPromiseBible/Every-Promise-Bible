David is leaving Jerusalem barefoot, and a foreigner refuses to be sent home.

## What is happening

Absalom's revolt. The king walking out of his own capital with the household, up the Mount of
Olives, weeping, head covered.

**15:13** *And a messenger came to David, saying, "**The hearts of the men of Israel are with Absalom**."*

Everybody is leaving. The question in the chapter is who is going with him.

## Who Ittai is

**15:19** *Ittai **the Gittite**.*

A man of Gath. A Philistine, from Goliath's city.

**15:19** again: *You're **a foreigner, an exile far from your own home**.*

He has six hundred men with him, and the number is the same as David's own band in the
wilderness years.

Whatever brought him to Jerusalem, he is a professional soldier in a foreign king's service.

## How long he has been there

**15:20** *"**You only came yesterday**."*

The Hebrew is *yesterday you came*, and it need not be literal — it means recently.

## What David offers him

**15:19** again: *"**Go back and stay with the new king**."*

He calls Absalom *the king*, plainly, without bitterness.

**15:20** again: *"Should I **drag you all over the country with me today, when I don't even know where I'm
going**?"*

The most honest sentence in the passage. He does not know where he is going and says so.

## The blessing

**15:20** again: *"Go back, and take your countrymen with you. **May kindness and faithfulness go with you**."*

The Hebrew is **chesed ve'emet** — steadfast love and truth. The covenant pair.

He releases him with the best words in the language.

## The refusal

**15:21** *"**As surely as the LORD lives, and as my lord the king lives — wherever my lord the king may
be, whether it means life or death, there your servant will be**."*

A Philistine swearing by the LORD.

The sentence has one condition in it and it is not a condition: *wherever*.

## What it echoes

**Ruth 1:16**: *"Entreat me not to leave you, or to turn back from following after you; for **wherever you go,
I will go**… your people shall be my people, and your God, my God."*

Ruth, to Naomi. A Moabite, on a road, refusing to be sent home.

Two foreigners, two roads out of a country, and nearly the same words.

## The answer

**15:22** *"**March on, then**," David said.*

Two words in Hebrew: **lekh va'avor** — go and pass over.

## The crossing

**15:22** again: *So Ittai the Gittite marched on, **with all his men and all the families who were with him**.*

Families. He is not just committing soldiers.

**15:23** *The whole countryside **wept aloud** as the people passed by. **The king crossed the Kidron
Valley**.*

## What happens at that valley

**John 18:1**: *Then Jesus, when He had said these things, **went out with His disciples over the Brook
Kidron**, where there was a garden.*

A thousand years later, a king leaving the city at night, over the same brook, with his own
people about to scatter.

## What became of Ittai

Three chapters on, David divides the army for the battle in the wood of Ephraim:

**2 Samuel 18:2**: *A third under the hand of Joab, a third under Abishai, and **a third under the hand of Ittai
the Gittite**.*

The man who had been in the country a matter of weeks is given a third of the army.

> **What he was offered and refused.** A release, with the best blessing in the language
> attached — from a king who admitted he did not know where he was going.
