Paul applies the allegory, and the application has a hard edge on it.

## Who the readers are

*Now we, brothers, are **children of promise, just as Isaac was**.*

Not children of Abraham in general. He picks the son and names him.

Isaac's distinction is not his character. It is how he came to exist. See the note on
Galatians 4:21–27.

## The friction

*But as **the one born in the ordinary way of the flesh persecuted** in those days **the one
born according to the Spirit**, so it is now.*

The Genesis account is short about it: at the weaning feast Sarah saw Ishmael *mocking*.
See the note on Genesis 21:8–11.

The Hebrew is **metsacheq** — playing, laughing, mocking. It is the same root as Isaac's
own name, which means *laughter*.

Paul reads it as persecution, and says the pattern is still running.

## "So it is now"

The Galatian churches were being pressured by teachers insisting on circumcision. Paul had
been driven out of Antioch, stoned at Lystra, and hounded from city to city on that first
journey.

He treats the current pressure as the old one continuing.

## The quotation

*Yet **what does the Scripture say? Cast out the slave woman and her son**.*

Genesis 21, and the words are Sarah's. Paul quotes a family quarrel as Scripture speaking.

He drops one word: Sarah said *cast out this bondwoman **and her son**, for the son of this
bondwoman shall not be heir with my son, **namely with Isaac***.

## The reason

*For **the son of the slave woman shall never be heir together with the son of the free
woman**.*

The point is inheritance, not affection. Genesis records that Abraham loved Ishmael and
that the demand *cut Abraham deeply*.

The two cannot share the estate. That is the whole argument.

## What Genesis does with Ishmael

God tells Abraham to do what Sarah says, and then: *also of the son of the bondwoman I will
make a nation, because he is your seed.*

And in the desert, when the water runs out, *God heard the voice of the lad* — and Hagar is
shown a well.

Ishmael is provided for. He is not made the heir.

## The conclusion

*So then, brothers, **we are not children of the slave woman but of the free**.*

Stated as a fact about the readers, not as a goal.

## What follows immediately

*It is **for freedom that Christ has made us free**. Stand fast therefore, and **do not be
entangled again with a yoke of bondage**.*

The chapter break hides it. The famous sentence of Galatians 5 is the *therefore* of this
paragraph.

## What is being cast out

Not people. Paul is arguing about a system — Sinai in the Hagar column, and the reader in
the other one.

The letter's own summary: *you have become estranged from Christ, you who attempt to be
justified by law; you have fallen from grace.*

> **Why the two cannot both stay.** Not because one son was worse. Because an estate has
> one heir, and Paul says the two ways of getting a son cannot share it.
