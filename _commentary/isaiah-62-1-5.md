Two names are taken away and two are given, and the new ones are the vocabulary of a
wedding.

## The refusal to be quiet

*For Zion's sake **I will not keep silent**. For Jerusalem's sake **I will not stay
quiet**.*

The chapter opens with God declining to say nothing.

## Until what

*Until her **righteousness shines out like the dawn**, her salvation like a blazing
torch.*

The Hebrew for *shines out* is the ordinary verb for going out — as the sun goes out
in the morning. Nothing is added to the dawn to make it bright; it arrives.

## Who sees it

*The nations will see **your righteousness**, and all the kings your glory.*

Her righteousness, on display to outsiders. The chapter never says she produced it.

## The new name

*You will be given **a new name**, a name **the mouth of the LORD will choose**.*

Not a name she picks. Naming rights belong to whoever gives it, and in Scripture the
one who names is the one with authority — Adam over the animals, God over Abram,
Jacob, Simon.

## The crown

*You will be **a crown of splendor in the hand of the LORD**, a royal diadem in the
palm of your God.*

Not on His head. In His hand — held up, turned in the light, shown.

## The two names removed

*No longer will they call you **Forsaken**, no longer name your land **Desolate**.*

The Hebrew is **Azubah** and **Shemamah**. Forsaken is what a wife is called when her
husband has left; desolate is what land is called when nobody works it.

## The two names given

*You will be called **Hephzibah**, My Delight Is in Her, and your land **Beulah**,
Married.*

**Chephtsi-bah** — *my delight is in her*. **Beulah** — *married, owned, possessed*.

Isaiah supplies the translations inside the verse, which he rarely does. He wants the
meanings heard, not just the sounds.

Hephzibah was also a real woman's name — Hezekiah's wife, the mother of Manasseh, in
2 Kings. Isaiah's readers knew it as an ordinary name a man gave his wife.

## The reason attached

*For **the LORD takes delight in you**, and your land will be joined to Him.*

The name is given because the delight is already there. The naming reports a fact
rather than creating one.

## The wedding

*As a young man marries a young woman, so will **your Builder** marry you. As a
bridegroom rejoices over his bride, **so will your God rejoice over you**.*

The Hebrew for *rejoices* is **sus** — to be glad, to exult. It is the word for the
groom's face at the wedding, applied to God, with the reader in the bride's place.

## Who is not silent, and who came instead

*For Zion's sake I will not keep silent.*

*God, who at various times and in various ways spoke in time past to the fathers by the prophets,
**has in these last days spoken to us by His Son**.*

**Hebrews** opens by saying the refusal to stay quiet ended in somebody rather than something.

## Who chooses the name

*"To him who overcomes I will give some of the hidden manna to eat. And **I will give him a white
stone, and on the stone a new name written which no one knows except him who receives it**."*

**Revelation** 2 keeps Isaiah's rule exactly, and adds that the stone is given to somebody who
did not name themselves.

## The bridegroom's voice

*"**He who has the bride is the bridegroom**; but the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and
hears him, rejoices greatly because of the bridegroom's voice. Therefore **this joy of mine is
fulfilled**."*

**John** the Baptist, identifying **Jesus** by the same metaphor and putting himself in the
best man's place.

*Husbands, love your wives, just as **Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her**…
that He might present her to Himself a glorious church.*

*Then I, **John**, saw the holy city, New Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, **prepared
as a bride adorned for her husband**.*

## Hephzibah

*My delight is in her.* Given because the delight is already there — the naming reports a fact
rather than creating one.

*"This is My beloved Son, **in whom I am well pleased**."*

*Having predestined us to adoption as sons by **Jesus Christ** to Himself, **according to the good
pleasure of His will**.*

> **What the chapter is written over.** Jerusalem after the fall — walls down, the
> land unworked, the people gone. God renames it in the middle of the ruin, and the
> names He gives are about how He feels rather than how it looks.
