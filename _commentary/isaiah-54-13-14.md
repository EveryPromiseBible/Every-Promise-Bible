Two verses, and everything in the second one hangs off a single condition.

## The children

*All your children will be **taught by the LORD**, and great will be their peace.*

The Hebrew is *taught of the LORD* — not taught about Him. He is the teacher.

Jesus quotes it in John 6: *it is written in the prophets, "And they shall all be
taught by God." Therefore everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes
to Me.*

And *great will be their peace* — **shalom**, which is not the absence of noise.
Wholeness, soundness, nothing missing.

## The condition

*You will be **established in righteousness**.*

The Hebrew for *established* is to be set firm, fixed, made to stand — the word used
of a foundation.

And what a person is established in is named: righteousness. Not effort, not
vigilance, not track record.

The chapter has already said whose righteousness it is — verse 17: *this is the
heritage of the servants of the LORD, and **their righteousness is from Me**, says
the LORD.*

## What follows from it

Three things, all of them consequences of the establishing:

- *Cruelty will be far from you*
- *you will have nothing to fear*
- *Terror will be far from you, and it will not come near*

The Hebrew for *far* is distance — put away, removed to a distance.

Fear is not managed here. It is relocated.

## What the chapter is

Isaiah 54 is the chapter immediately after Isaiah 53 — the suffering servant,
wounded for our transgressions, by whose stripes we are healed.

And 54 opens *Sing, O barren, you who have not borne!* The whole chapter is the
result of chapter 53, and it is addressed to somebody who had nothing.

## The rest of the promise

*"For the mountains shall depart and the hills be removed, but **My kindness shall
not depart from you**, nor shall My covenant of peace be removed," says the LORD,
who has mercy on you.*

And He names the guarantee: *this is like the waters of Noah to Me; for as I have
sworn that the waters of Noah would no longer cover the earth, so have I sworn that
I would not be angry with you.*

He points at a rainbow and says the two promises are the same kind.

> **The last verse.** *No weapon formed against you shall prosper, and every tongue
> which rises against you in judgement you shall condemn.* It is the most quoted
> line in the chapter, and the sentence it ends on is *their righteousness is from
> Me.* The verse is anchored to whose righteousness it is.
