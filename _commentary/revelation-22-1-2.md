The last chapter of the Bible, and the two things in it are the two things Genesis 2
opened with.

## The river

*And he showed me **a river of the water of life**, pure, **clear as crystal**.*

Genesis 2: *now a river went out of Eden to water the garden, and from there it parted and
became four riverheads.*

The Bible opens with a river in a garden and closes with one in a city.

## Where it comes from

*Running out from **the throne of God and of the Lamb**.*

One throne, two occupants. The construction is deliberate and John uses it again in the
next verse.

The river has a source rather than a catchment. It does not collect; it proceeds.

## The street

*Down the middle of the city's street, **with the river on both sides of it**.*

The geometry is difficult to picture, and John does not help. A street with a river on
either side, and a tree in the middle of both.

Ezekiel saw something similar — water from under the temple threshold, deepening as it
went, *and on the bank of the river, on this side and that, will grow all kinds of trees for
food*.

## The tree

*Stood **the tree of life**.*

Genesis 2: *the tree of life was also in the midst of the garden.*

Genesis 3 closes the access: *He drove out the man; and He placed cherubim at the east of
the garden of Eden, and a flaming sword which turned every way, **to guard the way to the
tree of life**.*

Nothing between Genesis 3 and Revelation 22 gets back to it. Proverbs uses the phrase
figuratively — wisdom is *a tree of life to those who take hold of her* — and the letters to
the seven churches promise it: *to him who overcomes I will give to eat from the tree of
life*.

Here the guard is gone.

## The fruit

*It carries **twelve kinds of fruit** and gives its fruit **every month**.*

Twelve, and monthly. There is no season in which it is not producing.

## The leaves

*And **the leaves of the tree are for the healing of the nations**.*

The Greek is **therapeian** — the root of *therapy*.

**The nations** — **ethnon**, the peoples. The word that runs through the whole Bible as
the outsiders, from the promise to Abraham that *in you all the nations shall be blessed*
to the great multitude *from every nation, tribe, people and tongue*.

## What the next verse says

*And **there shall be no more curse**.*

The Greek is **katathema**.

The curse in Genesis 3 fell on the ground: *cursed is the ground for your sake; in toil you
shall eat of it all the days of your life.* See the note on Genesis 3:17–19.

## What else is gone

*There shall be **no night there**: they need no lamp nor light of the sun, for the Lord God
gives them light.*

And earlier: *God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be **no more death,
nor sorrow, nor crying**.*

## The last invitation

*And the Spirit and the bride say, "Come!"… **and let him who thirsts come. Whoever desires,
let him take the water of life freely**.*

The Greek is **dorean** — freely, as a gift, for nothing. The same word Paul uses of being
justified.

The Bible's last offer is a drink, at no cost.

> **What the two chapters share.** A river and a tree. Genesis puts a sword between the man
> and one of them; Revelation puts it in the middle of a street with leaves for the
> nations.
