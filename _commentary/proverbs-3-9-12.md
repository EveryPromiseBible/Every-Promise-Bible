Four verses, and the second pair is not what the first pair leads you to expect.

## The instruction

*Honour the LORD with all you have, with **the first and best of everything you
gather**.*

The Hebrew is **reshith** — the beginning, the first, the choicest. It is the word
for firstfruits, and it is the first word of the Bible: *in the beginning*.

Two things are specified: that it is **first**, and that it is **best**. Not what is
left over, and not the ordinary portion.

## What follows

*Then your **barns will overflow**, your **vats will brim with new wine**.*

The Hebrew for *overflow* is to be filled to bursting. And the vats *burst out* with
new wine — the verb is used of a wall breaking.

Grain and wine, the two staples of that economy, and both described as more than the
container can hold.

## The turn

And then, without any transition:

*My son, **do not despise the LORD's discipline**, and do not grow weary of His
correction.*

That is the next verse. Overflowing barns, and then a word about correction.

Proverbs sets them side by side deliberately. Both are how a father treats a son —
the provision and the correcting come from the same relationship.

## The word for discipline

**Musar** — instruction, chastening, the whole business of forming a child. It is
the Hebrew equivalent of the Greek **paideia** that runs through Hebrews 12.

Hebrews quotes this exact passage: *my son, do not despise the chastening of the
LORD, nor be discouraged when you are rebuked by Him; for whom the LORD loves He
chastens.* See the note on Hebrews 12:5–11.

## The reason given

*For the LORD corrects **the one He loves**, as a father the son **in whom he
delights**.*

Both halves name affection. The correction is evidence of the relationship, not a
withdrawal of it.

## Why the two halves belong together

A man who reads only the first two verses gets a formula: give the first and best,
receive overflow.

The chapter will not let him stop there. The same Father who fills the barns also
corrects, and the son is told not to despise either.

## What the chapter opens with

*Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding; in
all your ways acknowledge Him, and He shall direct your paths.*

Five verses before this one. The honouring with firstfruits is one instance of the
acknowledging.
