Four words of instruction and then a sentence about where the supply comes from — and the order is
the whole point.

## Out of

*God is **where love comes from**.*

**He agape ek tou theou estin** — love is *out of* God. **Ek** is the preposition of origin: the
thing came from there and is still arriving from there.

John does not say God approves of love, or commands it, or is pleased by it. He says love has a
source and names it. Which means anyone being told to love is being told to draw rather than to
generate.

## The tense that settles it

*Everyone who loves **has been born from God** and knows God.*

**Gegennetai** — perfect passive. Has been born, and stands born. Something happened to the person,
it was done by Another, and the result is permanent.

Read the direction of the sentence carefully, because it is easy to reverse. John does not say
loving produces the birth. He says loving is the evidence of a birth that has already taken place.
The love is the symptom, not the fee.

## Where he goes three verses later

In case anybody still had it the wrong way round, John states it flatly:

*In this is love, **not that we loved God, but that He loved us** and sent His Son to be the
propitiation for our sins.*

That is a definition, and it excludes our contribution by name. The love that the paragraph is about
did not originate at our end at any point, and its clearest expression is not a feeling but a
sending.

## Why the command is not heavy

*Dear friends, **let us** love each other.*

The form is a plural exhortation — John puts himself inside it — and it is addressed to people he
has just told are born of God.

You can ask that of people whose supply is *ek tou theou*. It would be cruelty to ask it of anybody
else.

> **Draw, do not manufacture.** The instruction to love one another is never given in this letter to
> people who have to find it in themselves. It is given to people born of God, whose love is a
> reaction: *we love Him because He first loved us*, and He proved it by sending Jesus Christ before
> anybody had asked Him to.
