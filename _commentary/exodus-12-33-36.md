They went in as guests, spent four centuries as slaves, and walked out carrying
the treasury.

## Who was doing the hurrying

*The **Egyptians urged the people to hurry out** of the land, for they said, "We
will all die."*

Egypt is pushing them toward the door. Nobody has to negotiate a release any more.

## The dough

*The people took their dough **before the yeast was added**, wrapping their
kneading bowls in their cloaks and carrying them on their shoulders.*

That is where unleavened bread comes from. Not a symbol first and a practicality
second — they left before it could rise, and the feast keeps the memory of the
speed.

## "Just as Moses had told them"

*And **just as Moses had told them**, the Israelites asked the Egyptians for
articles of silver and gold and for clothing.*

It was instructed in advance, in chapter 11, and before that it was promised to
Abram four hundred years earlier: *afterward your people will come out with great
wealth.* See the note on Genesis 15:12–16.

Nobody improvised this.

## The word

The Hebrew for *asked* is the ordinary verb for making a request. And the Egyptians
*gave them whatever they asked.*

This is the settlement of an account. Four hundred years of building
store-cities — Pithom and Raamses — with no wages paid, and on one morning the
books are balanced.

## Whose doing it was

*The **LORD had made the Egyptians so favourable** toward His people.*

The Hebrew is *gave the people favour in the eyes of the Egyptians* — **chen**,
grace, the same word used over Esther in a Persian palace and over Noah before the
flood.

The Israelites did not persuade anybody. They asked, and the disposition of a
nation had already been changed.

## What it was for

Some of it went into a golden calf a few chapters later. Most of it went into the
tabernacle — the gold of the ark, the lampstand, the mercy seat, the boards
overlaid, the silver sockets, the linen and the blue and purple.

Egypt's wages built the tent God met them in.

> **How the psalm sums it up.** *He brought His people out with silver and gold, and
> not one of their tribes stumbled.* Wealthy and well, in one line — the morning
> after the lamb. See the note on Psalms 105:37–38.
