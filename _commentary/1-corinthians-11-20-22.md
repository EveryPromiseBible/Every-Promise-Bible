Paul says something startling about a meeting that thought it was taking
communion.

## What it is not

*So when you assemble together in one place, **it is not the Lord's Supper that
you eat**.*

Not *you are taking it badly*. It is not that meal at all. Whatever they were
doing, the name did not apply to it.

## What was happening

*Each one goes ahead with his own supper, and one is left hungry while another is
drunk.*

The Corinthian gathering was a full meal. Wealthier members arrived earlier —
they were not working — and ate what they had brought. Slaves and labourers came
when they could get away, to nothing.

The Greek for *goes ahead* is about taking yours first, before the others.

## The charge

*Do you not have houses to eat and drink in? Or do you **despise the church of
God**, and **shame those who have nothing**?*

Paul does not accuse them of bad theology about the bread. He accuses them of
humiliating poor people at the table.

The Greek behind *shame* is to put to shame, to disgrace — a public thing done to
somebody's face.

## "In this I do not praise you"

He has praised them a few lines earlier for holding to what he passed on. Here he
withdraws it, in the same formula, so the reversal is unmissable.

## What follows

The famous words come next — *the Lord Jesus, on the night He was betrayed, took
bread.* Paul recites the institution **as the correction** to what he has just
described.

And a few verses on, *not discerning the body* — which in this context is a
gathering that cannot see the people it is eating with. See the note on
1 Corinthians 11:27–30.

> **The point of the whole passage.** A meal about a body given for everyone was
> being eaten in a way that divided the room by income. Paul's remedy at the end
> is two words long: *wait for one another.*
