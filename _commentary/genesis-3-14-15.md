The first announcement of a rescue in the Bible is not addressed to the people
being rescued. It is spoken to the enemy, over their heads.

## Who is being talked to

God curses the serpent, and in the middle of the sentence describes a future
conflict — and its outcome. Adam and Eve are standing there listening.

They are not consulted, promised anything directly, or asked to do anything. They
overhear their own hope while being sentenced.

## "Her seed"

*Between your offspring and hers. Her seed will crush your head.*

The phrase is unusual. Descent in Hebrew genealogy runs through the man, and
Scripture speaks of the seed of Abraham, of David, of Israel. The seed **of a
woman** is not how anyone talked.

Christian readers have seen a virgin birth signalled from the first pages
because of it, and Paul's phrasing in Galatians follows the same track: *God sent
forth His Son, born of a woman.*

## The two wounds

*Her seed will crush your head, and you will strike his heel.*

Both parties are injured, and the injuries are not equal. A crushed head ends the
fight. A struck heel is real damage that heals.

Everything from here to the cross is the working out of that asymmetry.

## Where the promise sits

*Even here, in the wreckage, God spoke a promise.*

Before the curse on the ground, before the exile from the garden, before the
garments of skin. The first thing God says after the fall is that it will not
stand.

> **What the wound cost.** *You will strike his heel* is not a small thing.
> Whatever the crushing of the head would take, the promise made in Genesis 3
> already includes the injury to the one doing it. See the note on Isaiah 53:4–6.
