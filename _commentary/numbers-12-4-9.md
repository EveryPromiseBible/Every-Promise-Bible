A family quarrel, settled by God appearing at the tent door.

## What started it

*Then Miriam and Aaron **spoke against Moses because of the Ethiopian woman whom he had
married**.*

And then the real grievance: *"**Has the LORD indeed spoken only through Moses? Has He not
spoken through us also?**"*

Aaron is the high priest. Miriam is a prophetess, and had led the women with tambourines
after the sea.

Their claim is not baseless. God had spoken through both.

## What Moses said

Nothing. The narrator supplies the only comment:

*Now the man Moses was **very humble, more than all men who were on the face of the
earth**.*

Written by Moses, according to the traditional authorship, which readers have found
awkward for a very long time.

## The summons

*Suddenly the LORD said to Moses, Aaron, and Miriam, "**The three of you, come out to the
meeting tent**."*

The Hebrew is *suddenly*. There is no delay and no hearing.

## Who appears

*The LORD came down **in a pillar of cloud**, stood at the entrance of the tent, and called
out, "**Aaron and Miriam**."*

Two names. Moses is summoned with them and then left out of the address.

## What God says about prophets

*"When there is **a prophet among you**, I reveal myself to him **in visions** and speak to
him **in dreams**."*

The Hebrew is **mar'ah** and **chalom**.

This is the clearest statement in the Old Testament of how prophetic revelation normally
worked. God names His own method.

Both are indirect — something seen, and something dreamed.

## The exception

*"**But not so with My servant Moses**. He is **faithful in all my house**."*

The Hebrew is *he is entrusted with all My house*.

Hebrews quotes it: *Moses indeed was faithful in all His house as a servant… but Christ as a
Son over His own house.* See the note on Hebrews 3:7–11.

## The difference

*"With him I speak **face to face, plainly and not in riddles**, and **he sees the very
likeness of the LORD**."*

The Hebrew is *mouth to mouth*, and *not in dark sayings* — **chidot**, riddles, enigmas.

Exodus says the same: *the LORD spoke to Moses face to face, as a man speaks to his
friend.*

## The question

*"So **why were you not afraid to speak against my servant Moses?**"*

## The departure

*The LORD's anger burned against them, **and he departed**.*

The Hebrew is that He went. The cloud lifts.

## What was found

*And when the cloud departed from above the tabernacle, suddenly **Miriam became leprous, as
white as snow**.*

Aaron is not struck. He is the high priest, and a leprous high priest would have ended the
priesthood.

## What Aaron does

*"Oh, my lord! Please **do not lay this sin on us**… please do not let her be as one dead."*

He calls Moses *my lord*, which is the whole argument conceded.

## What Moses does

*So Moses cried out to the LORD, saying, "**Please heal her, O God, I pray!**"*

Four words in the Hebrew. The shortest prayer in the Old Testament, and it is for the woman
who had just attacked him.

## The outcome

Seven days shut out of the camp, and *the people did not journey till Miriam was brought in
again*.

The whole nation waits a week for her.

> **What God says about His own method.** Visions and dreams, to prophets, as the norm —
> and then names one exception, and the exception says nothing in his own defence.
