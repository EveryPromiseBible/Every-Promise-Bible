Israel is trapped between an army and a sea, Moses is praying, and God tells him
to stop.

## The question

*Why are you crying out to Me? Tell the Israelites to move forward.*

Nothing in the chapter suggests Moses was praying wrongly. He had just told the
people to stand still and see the salvation of the LORD, which is a fine thing to
say.

But there was a word already given. Back at the burning bush, God had said He was
bringing them out. The situation had not changed the instruction — it had only
made it look impossible.

## What he is told to do instead

*Lift up your staff, stretch out your hand over the sea, and divide it.*

The staff is the one that had already turned into a snake, already struck the
Nile, already brought hail. He is not being handed something new.

The prayer is not rebuked so much as redirected: there is a time to ask, and a
time to pick up what you were already given and use it.

## Who does the dividing

The instruction says *divide it* — spoken to Moses. Two verses on, the account
says the LORD drove the sea back with a strong east wind all night.

Both are true, and neither cancels the other. The man stretches out his hand; God
moves the water. That is the pattern the whole book runs on.

## Why God says He is doing it

*I will gain honour through Pharaoh and all his army… Then the Egyptians will
know that I am the LORD.*

The reason given is not only Israel's rescue. God names His own honour and
Egypt's knowledge as the point — the rescue is real, and it is also a
demonstration aimed at people watching.

> **The line just before it.** *The LORD will fight for you; you need only to be
> still.* Moses says it, and God's next words are *tell them to move forward*.
> Both are in the same passage, four verses apart. Stillness and forward movement
> are not opposites here — the stillness is about who does the fighting.
