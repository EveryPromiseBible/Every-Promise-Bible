Peter reaches for a word nobody else in Greek literature seems to have used, and it comes from a
night he could not forget.

## The apron

*Tie humility on **like an apron**.*

**Enkombosasthai** — from **kombos**, a knot. The **enkomboma** was the white overall a slave knotted
over his clothes before he started the dirty work; the verb means to tie that on.

It occurs once in the Bible. Peter had a particular evening in mind.

*Jesus rose from supper and laid aside His garments, took a towel and **girded Himself**… and began
to wash the disciples' feet.*

Peter is the man who said *You shall never wash my feet* and then, in the same minute, asked to be
washed all over. Thirty years later he tells the whole church to put on the apron he had tried to
take off his Lord.

## Drawn up in battle order

*Because **God sets Himself against the proud**.*

**Antitassetai** — a military term. To draw up troops in formation against an opposing line.

It is a startling thing to say. God does not merely disapprove of pride; He arranges Himself
opposite it. And Peter is quoting a proverb that James quotes too — both of them reached for the
same sentence when writing to churches with friction in them.

## And hands grace to the low

*And **hands grace to the low**.*

**Didosin charin** — He gives grace. Not *rewards* the humble; gives.

Which is the whole reason this is not a new rung to climb. Humility is not a qualification you
achieve in order to earn a supply. It is simply the shape a person has to be in to receive something
that is being handed down — grace runs to low ground the way water does, and the only thing that can
keep it out is being higher than it.

## Why "younger ones" first

Peter starts with the young men falling in behind the elders and then widens it: *and all of you*.
The instruction is not a hierarchy. Everyone is told to get underneath everyone.

That is only possible in a room where nobody has anything to defend — and nobody does, because the
next sentence in the letter is about casting your anxiety on Him, and the one after that names the
Chief Shepherd who is coming with a crown that *does not fade away*.

> **The one who wore it first.** The apron in this verse was tied on by Jesus Christ in an upstairs
> room hours before the cross, and He did it for men who had spent the walk there arguing about
> which of them was the greatest. Nobody is being asked to go lower than He went, and grace is
> already down there waiting.
