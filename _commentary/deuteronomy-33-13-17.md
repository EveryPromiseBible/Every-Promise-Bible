The longest and richest of the twelve blessings Moses gives before he climbs the mountain to
die.

## When it is said

Deuteronomy 34 is Moses' death. Chapter 33 is the last thing he does.

He blesses the tribes one at a time, and Joseph gets more lines than anyone except Levi.

## The water

*With the **precious dew of heaven above** and the **deep waters that lie below**.*

The Hebrew for *deep* is **tehom** — the same word as the deep in Genesis 1, and the springs
that burst open in the flood.

Water from the sky and water from under the ground. Both directions.

This is Isaac's blessing on Jacob, repeated: *may God give you of the dew of heaven, of the
fatness of the earth*.

## The seasons

*With **the best the sun brings forth** and the **finest the months can yield**.*

The Hebrew is *the precious fruits brought forth by the sun, and the precious produce of the
months*.

**Months** — the Hebrew is **yerachim**, from **yareach**, moon. Sun crops and moon crops:
what ripens in heat and what ripens on the lunar calendar.

## The hills

*With the **choicest gifts of the ancient mountains** and the **bounty of the everlasting
hills**.*

The Hebrew is *the chief things of the ancient mountains* — **harerei qedem**, mountains of
old.

Habakkuk uses the phrase of God's own coming: *the everlasting mountains were scattered, the
perpetual hills bowed*.

## The signature

*And the **favor of Him who dwelt in the burning bush**.*

The Hebrew is *the good will of Him who dwelt in the bush* — **soneh**, the thornbush of
Exodus 3. See the note on Exodus 3:1–3.

Moses names God, in his final speech, by the shrub He appeared in when this whole thing
started. Sixty years earlier, on the back side of the desert.

## Where the blessing lands

*Let all this rest **on the head of Joseph**, on the brow of **the prince among his
brothers**.*

The Hebrew is **nezir echav** — the separated one of his brothers. The same root as
Nazirite: one set apart.

Jacob had used the same phrase over him: *the blessings of your father… shall be on the head
of Joseph, and on the crown of the head of him who was separate from his brothers*.

Separated is the accurate word for what happened to him. Sold, and gone for twenty-two
years.

## The animal

*In majesty he is like **a firstborn bull**; his horns are the **horns of a wild ox**.*

The Hebrew is **reem** — the untameable one. The same beast Balaam used of Israel. See the
note on Numbers 24:3–9.

## The reach

*With them he will **gore the nations, driving them to the ends of the earth**.*

## The two sons

*Such are **the ten thousands of Ephraim**; such are **the thousands of Manasseh**.*

Ephraim first, and the larger number, though Manasseh was the elder.

Jacob had crossed his hands to do exactly that, and Joseph had tried to move them: *not so,
my father, for this one is the firstborn*. The answer was *I know, my son, I know*.

Moses records the outcome as settled fact two generations later.

## What Joseph got

The double portion. The birthright went to him rather than to Reuben, and his two sons were
counted as tribes in their own right — which is why the list of twelve has no Joseph in it,
and has Ephraim and Manasseh instead.

## What he said about it himself

*God has made me forget all my toil* — that is Manasseh — and *God has caused me to be
fruitful in the land of my affliction*, which is Ephraim.

He named his sons after what had been done to him and what came out of it.

> **How Moses signs it.** *The favour of Him who dwelt in the bush.* His last recorded
> blessing reaches back to a burning thornbush on the far side of the wilderness.
