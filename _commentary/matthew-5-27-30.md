The second of the six, and it does the same thing to adultery that the first did
to murder.

## The move

*"You have heard… 'Do not commit adultery.' **But I say to you**, anyone who looks
at a woman with wanting in his eyes **has already committed adultery with her in
his heart**."*

The seventh commandment concerned an act. He relocates it to a glance.

**Already.** The Greek is emphatic — it is done, past tense, before anything
happened.

## Who is left standing

That is the question the passage is designed to raise, and the answer is nobody.

The first paragraph took murder down to a temper. This one takes adultery down to a
look. Between them they clear the hillside.

Anyone who leaves the Sermon on the Mount feeling he has done reasonably well has
not been listening. That is the whole point of it: *by the law is the knowledge of
sin*, and the law was *our tutor to bring us to Christ.*

He is not making the law harder to keep. He is showing what it always said, and
what it always said cannot be kept.

## The eye and the hand

*"If your right eye is what trips you up, **tear it out**… if your right hand is
what trips you up, **cut it off**."*

The Greek verbs are violent and specific.

And the arithmetic is stated: *better to lose one part of you than to have your
whole body thrown into hell.* On His own terms that is simply correct — if a hand
could be traded for a life, the trade would be worth making.

But a blind man can still want, and a one-handed man can still hate. The surgery
does not reach the organ that is actually the problem, and the passage has already
named it: *in his heart.*

## What has to be dealt with

Nothing removable is the trouble. That is why the sentence about the heart comes
first and the amputations after.

Ezekiel had the only remedy that reaches it: *I will give you a new heart, and put
a new spirit within you; I will take the heart of stone out of your flesh and give
you a heart of flesh.*

An operation nobody can perform on himself.

## What the Sermon ends with

Two houses, and one of them *fell — and great was its fall.*

He finishes His most demanding sermon with a collapse, and the crowds are
*astonished at His teaching, for He taught them as one having authority.*

> **Where this leaves a reader.** Exactly where it left the hearers: with a standard
> that is God's own and no way to reach it, and a Man on the hillside who will go
> on to keep it entirely and then hand what He earned to people who did not. That is
> the sequence the Sermon is written into.
