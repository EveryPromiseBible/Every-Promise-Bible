Luke was there for this one — the *we* in the passage is his — and he tells it
with a doctor's eye and a slight glint of humour.

## The setting

*On the first day of the week, when the disciples came together to break bread.*

One of the earliest indications of Christians meeting on Sunday, and of what they
met for.

*There were many lamps burning in the upper room.* Luke mentions it because it
matters: many oil lamps in a crowded upper room, at night, burning the air. A
young man in a window is looking for cooler air.

## Paul talking

*He was leaving the next day, and he kept on talking until midnight.*

Luke does not defend him. The word he uses for the sinking sleep is a medical
term — being borne down, overcome by degrees. Eutychus does not doze off
carelessly; he is gradually overwhelmed.

## The fall

*He fell from the third floor, and **they picked him up dead**.*

Luke, a physician, states it flatly. Not *as though dead*. Dead.

## What Paul does

*Paul went down, bent over him, and took him in his arms.*

The posture is Elijah's over the widow's son, and Elisha's over the Shunammite's
boy — stretching himself on the child. Paul repeats an old gesture.

*"Do not be alarmed. His life is in him."*

## What happens next

*Then he went back up, broke bread and ate, and talked with them a long while,
until daybreak, and then he left.*

He resumes. The meeting continues, the bread is broken, and the preaching runs
till dawn.

The boy is mentioned once more, in the last line: *they brought the young man
home alive, and were greatly comforted.*

> **What Luke leaves out.** Any comment. No sermon on the meaning of it, no
> rebuke of the boy, no reflection on the length of the sermon. A death, a
> raising, and then breakfast — reported by a man who was in the room.
