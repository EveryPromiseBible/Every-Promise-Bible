The first of the seven letters, and it opens with a page of commendation.

## Who is speaking

The verse before: *these things says **He who holds the seven stars in His right hand, who
walks in the midst of the seven golden lampstands**.*

Every letter opens with a description of the speaker taken from the vision in chapter 1.

## The opening

*"**I know what you have been doing**."*

The Greek is **oida** — settled knowledge. It opens six of the seven letters.

## The list

Seven things are commended, and they are all real:

- *"I know **how hard you have worked**"* — **kopos**, labour to the point of exhaustion
- *"and **how much you have taken without giving way**"* — **hupomone**, remaining under
- *"You **cannot stomach wicked men**"*
- *"When people turned up claiming to be apostles, you **put them to the test and caught
  them out as liars**"*
- *"You have **carried the weight**"*
- *"You have **held out**"*
- *"You have **worn yourself down for the sake of My name**, and **you have not run out of
  strength**"*

## What kind of church this is

Doctrinally careful, tireless, intolerant of frauds, and still standing after decades of
pressure.

Paul had spent three years there and warned the elders that *savage wolves* would come in
and that *from among your own selves men will rise up*. See the note on
Acts 20:28–31.

They did what he told them to. They tested and they caught them.

## The endurance

The Greek **hupomone** appears twice in two verses. Peter uses it of the same period, and
James says testing produces it. See the note on James 1:3.

## The next verse

*"**Nevertheless I have this against you, that you have left your first love**."*

The Greek is **aphekes** — you left, you let go. Not *lost*.

The whole commendation is intact and this is added to it.

## The instruction

*"**Remember therefore from where you have fallen; repent and do the first works**."*

Three imperatives: remember, repent, do.

**The first works** — not new ones. The ones they were doing at the start.

## What is at stake

*"Or else I will come to you quickly and **remove your lampstand from its place** — unless
you repent."*

The lampstand is the church itself; chapter 1 says so.

## What they still had

*"But this you have, that **you hate the deeds of the Nicolaitans, which I also hate**."*

He agrees with them again, in the same breath as the warning.

## How it ends

*"To him who overcomes I will give **to eat from the tree of life**, which is in the midst
of the Paradise of God."*

The tree nobody has reached since Genesis 3.

## The order of the letter

Praise, one thing, instruction, warning, praise again, promise.

The complaint occupies one verse out of seven.

> **What the commendation contains.** Work, endurance, doctrinal vigilance, and stamina —
> all confirmed by the speaker. And the one thing named against them is not on that axis
> at all.
