Pharaoh has stopped saying no. That is what makes this passage worth reading
slowly — the refusals have become offers.

## The question he asks

*Go, worship the LORD your God. But tell me — who exactly is going?*

He is not blocking the worship any more. He is negotiating the guest list, which
is a different and more effective tactic.

## Moses' answer leaves nothing out

*We will go with our young and our old, our sons and daughters, our flocks and
herds.*

Four categories, and he names them deliberately: both ends of the generations,
both sexes of the children, and the livelihood. There is nothing left in Egypt to
come back for.

## Where Pharaoh draws the line

*Only the men may go.*

The children are the sticking point, and Pharaoh knows exactly what he is doing.
A generation of men can go and worship in the desert and still leave everything
behind that would make the departure permanent. Keep the children and you keep
the future.

> **The rest of the pattern.** This is the third of four attempted compromises
> across the chapters: worship inside Egypt; do not go far; leave the children;
> and finally, leave the livestock. Each one concedes more than the last, and
> each keeps hold of something. Moses' answer to the fourth is the one that ends
> it — *not a hoof will be left behind.*
