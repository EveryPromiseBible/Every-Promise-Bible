David's last public speech, and it is a series of narrowings.

## The occasion

*Now David assembled at Jerusalem **all the leaders of Israel**: the officers of the tribes, the
captains of the divisions, the captains over thousands, the stewards over all the substance and
possessions of the king and of his sons, with the officials, the valiant men, and all the mighty
men of valour.*

Everybody who mattered, and he *rose to his feet*.

An old man standing up to talk about a building he will not see.

## What he says first

*"I had it in my heart to build a house of rest for the ark of the covenant of the LORD… **but
God said to me, "You shall not build a house for My name, because you have been a man of war and
have shed blood."***

He tells the assembly the refusal before anything else.

## The chain

*"Yet the LORD, the God of Israel, **chose me out of my whole family** to be king over Israel
forever."*

*"**Out of Judah** He chose the leaders."*

*"And **out of Judah my father's house**."*

*"And **out of my father's sons** He was pleased with me."*

Four narrowings, and each one is inside the last: the tribe, the clan, the household, the man.

The Hebrew verb is **bachar** — chose — used at every stage.

## What it corrects

Judah was not the firstborn. Reuben was, and the birthright went to Joseph's sons.

The Chronicler says so a few chapters earlier: *the birthright was Joseph's… **yet Judah
prevailed over his brothers**, and from him came a ruler*.

And Jesse's household was not distinguished. And David was the youngest of eight, left with the
sheep when the prophet came to dinner: *there remains yet the youngest, and there he is, keeping
the sheep*.

## The fifth narrowing

*"And **out of all my sons** — for the LORD gave me many — He chose **Solomon** to sit on the
throne."*

Solomon was not the eldest either. Amnon, Absalom and Adonijah all came first, and all three
died badly.

And he is Bathsheba's son, which the Chronicler does not mention and Samuel does.

## The throne

*"To sit on the throne of **the LORD's kingdom** over Israel."*

Not David's kingdom. The Chronicler consistently calls it God's throne with a man sitting on
it: *Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king instead of David his father*.

## What David hands over

The plans.

*Then David gave his son Solomon the plans for the vestibule, its houses, its treasuries…
**by the Spirit**, of the courts of the house of the LORD.*

*"**All this," said David, "the LORD made me understand in writing, by His hand upon me, all the
works of these plans**."*

He was not allowed to build it and he was given the drawings.

## The charge

*"As for you, my son Solomon, **know the God of your father, and serve Him with a loyal heart
and with a willing mind**… **If you seek Him, He will be found by you; but if you forsake Him,
He will cast you off forever**."*

*"Be strong and of good courage, and do it; **do not fear nor be dismayed, for the LORD God — my
God — will be with you. He will not leave you nor forsake you** until you have finished all the
work."*

The same words Moses gave Joshua, from a father to a son.

## What he had already done

Stockpiled the materials with his own resources: *because I have set my affection on the house
of my God, I have given to the house of my God, over and above all that I have prepared, my own
special treasure of gold and silver*.

## Where the narrowing ends up

*The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the Son of David, the Son of Abraham.*

Judah, Jesse, David, Solomon — the first fourteen names of Matthew's list run straight through
this speech.

> **The shape of the sentence.** Out of the family, out of Judah, out of my father's house, out
> of my father's sons — and then out of all my sons. Five narrowings, and every one uses the
> word *chose*.
