---
key: Zephaniah 2:1–3
title: "Perhaps you will be sheltered"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The only appeal in the first half of the book, and it ends on a word most prophets avoid.

## Who is addressed

*"Gather yourselves, gather together, **you nation with no longing left for Him**."*

The Hebrew is **goy lo niksaph** — a nation not longing, not yearning. **Kasaph** is the verb for
pale desire, the ache in *my soul longs for the courts of the LORD.*

They are not accused of hating God. They are accused of not wanting anything.

And **goy** — the general word for a nation, the one normally used of Gentiles. Zephaniah calls
Judah by the outsiders' noun.

## The urgency

*"**Do it before** the decree takes flight, **before** the day passes like blown chaff, **before** the
burning anger of the LORD comes upon you, **before** the day of the LORD's anger falls."*

Four times. The Hebrew is **beterem** — before, not yet.

*"Behold, **now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation**."*

## The three imperatives

*"**Seek the LORD**, all you humble of the land, all who do what He commands. **Seek righteousness;
seek humility.**"*

**Baqqeshu** three times.

The second is the striking one. *Seek righteousness* — **tzedeq** — addressed to people who are
already described as doing what He commands.

*But **seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness**, and all these things shall be added to
you.*

*Blessed are those who **hunger and thirst for righteousness**, for they shall be filled.*

**Matthew** 5, and the qualification is the appetite. Zephaniah's nation is one *with no longing
left*, which is precisely the condition the beatitude does not describe.

*And be found in Him, **not having my own righteousness**, which is from the law, but that which is
through faith in Christ.*

**Paul** tells them where the seeking ends.

## The third

*"**Seek humility.**"*

**Anavah** — from **anav**, the poor, the bowed-down. The same word in the line before: *all you
humble of the land.*

They are told to seek what they are already called.

*"Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for **I am gentle and lowly in heart**."*

**Matthew**'s word is **praus** — the Greek Old Testament's rendering of **anav**. It is the one
adjective **Jesus** ever applies to His own character, and He tells people to learn it from Him
rather than work it up.

## The word at the end

*"**Perhaps** you will be sheltered on the day of the LORD's anger."*

The Hebrew is **ulai** — perhaps, it may be.

Zephaniah will not promise. He is not withholding grace; he is describing a day nobody had yet seen
survived, and he is honest that he cannot see past it.

*"**Who knows?** God may yet relent."* The king of Nineveh, in Jonah 3, with the same word.

## What the *perhaps* became

*There is therefore now **no condemnation** to those who are in **Christ Jesus**.*

*Much more then, having now been justified by His blood, **we shall be saved from wrath through
Him**.*

*Love has been perfected among us in this: that **we may have boldness in the day of judgment**.*

**John**'s word is **parresia** — the confidence of somebody with standing in the room. Zephaniah
says *perhaps*; the New Testament says *boldness*, and the difference between the two is a name.

> **The last word of the appeal.** *Perhaps.* The prophet will not go further than that, and
> nothing in the Old Testament lets him — and 1 John, on the same day, says *boldness*.
