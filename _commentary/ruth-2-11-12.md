---
key: Ruth 2:11–12
title: Under whose wings
updated: 2026-08-07
---

He blesses her by naming a shelter, and the word he uses is the one she will use on him.

## What prompts it

She has asked why he noticed her — *why have I found favour in your eyes, seeing I am a foreigner?*

The Hebrew is **nokriyyah** — a foreign woman. Not even a **ger**, a resident alien. An outsider.

## The answer

*"**I've heard all about it** — everything you've done for your mother-in-law since your husband
died."*

The Hebrew is **huggad huggad li** — it has surely been told me. The verb doubled.

## The three things

*"How you **left your own father and mother** and **the land of your birth**, and **came to live among a
people you had never known**."*

## What that echoes

*"Get out of your country, from your family and from your father's house, to a land that I will
show you."* See the note on Genesis 12:1–3.

The words spoken to Abraham, used of a Moabite widow.

She did it without a promise attached.

## What she had said

*"Wherever you go, I will go… **your people shall be my people, and your God, my God**."* See the note
on Ruth 1:16–17.

## The blessing

*"May the LORD **repay you for what you've done**."*

The Hebrew is **yeshallem YHWH po'olek** — may the LORD complete, make whole, your work.

**Shalam** — the root of **shalom**. To pay in full.

## The second half

*"May you be **richly rewarded by the LORD, the God of Israel**."*

The Hebrew is **maskurtek shelemah** — your wages full.

## The image

*"**Under whose wings you have come to find shelter**."*

The Hebrew is **tachat kenafav** — under His wings. And **lachasot** — to take refuge.

## Where the picture comes from

*He spread His wings and caught them, carrying them on His outstretched wings.* See the note on
Deuteronomy 32:10–14.

And *He shall cover you with His feathers, and under His wings you shall take refuge*. See the note
on Psalms 91:3–4.

## The word

**Kanaf** — a wing. It also means the corner or skirt of a garment.

## What she does with it

*"**Take your maidservant under your wing**, for you are a close relative."*

The Hebrew is **uparasta kenafeka al-amateka** — spread your skirt over your maidservant. See the
note on Ruth 3:8–9.

She uses his own word back at him, on the threshing floor, at midnight.

## What she is asking for

The gesture is a proposal.

Boaz prayed that she would be sheltered under God's wings. She asks him to be the wings.

## What he says to that

*"Blessed are you of the LORD, my daughter! For **you have shown more kindness at the end than at the
beginning**."*

The Hebrew is **chesed**, twice in the book applied to her.

## The office

*"**I am a close relative**; however, there is a relative closer than I."*

The Hebrew is **go'el** — the kinsman-redeemer. See the note on Ruth 3:10–13.

The nearer one declines at the gate, and Boaz buys the field and the widow with it.

## Where it goes

*So Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife… and she bore a son… **and they called his name Obed. He
is the father of Jesse, the father of David**.* See the note on Ruth 4:16–17.

## And further

*Salmon begot Boaz **by Rahab**, Boaz begot Obed **by Ruth**.* See the note on Matthew 1:5–6.

Two foreign women, four generations apart, named in the genealogy.

> **The word returned.** He prays that she will find shelter under God's wings. She asks him, weeks
> later, to spread his over her — the same noun, and he says yes.
