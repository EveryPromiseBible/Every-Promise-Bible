Three warnings in a row, and then a definition that takes the other side's word away from
them.

## The break in the letter

Philippians is the warmest thing Paul wrote — *I thank my God upon every remembrance of
you*, and *rejoice in the Lord always*.

Chapter 3 opens *finally, my brethren, rejoice in the Lord*, and then this.

## The three

*Keep your eyes open for **the dogs**. Keep your eyes open for **the workers who do harm**.
Keep your eyes open for **the knife-cutters**.*

The Greek repeats **blepete** three times — look, watch, beware.

## The first

**Dogs.** In that world, not pets. Scavengers in the street, and a standard Jewish term of
contempt for Gentiles.

Paul turns it round and applies it to the party insisting on Jewish observance.

## The second

**Workers who do harm** — the Greek is **kakous ergatas**, evil workers.

They would have called themselves workers. Paul keeps the noun and changes the adjective.

## The third

**Knife-cutters** — the Greek is **katatome**, a cutting-down, a mutilation.

It is a pun. The word for circumcision is **peritome**, a cutting-around. Paul coins a
near-identical word meaning butchery.

Leviticus forbids cutting the flesh: *you shall not make any cuttings in your flesh.* Paul
puts the practice in that category.

## The definition

*For **we are the circumcision**.*

He takes the term itself. The people insisting on the operation are the mutilation; the
people not having it are the thing it was meant to signify.

Moses had already located it: *circumcise the foreskin of your heart, and be stiff-necked no
longer.* And Jeremiah, and Paul in Romans: *circumcision is that of the heart, in the
Spirit.*

## The three marks

*We **who serve God by His Spirit**, who **make our boast in Christ Jesus**, and who **put no
trust at all in what human flesh can offer**.*

Three clauses answering the three warnings — a positive for each negative.

## What Paul does next

He lists what he would have had to boast about if the third clause were not true:

*Circumcised the eighth day, of the stock of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of
the Hebrews; concerning the law, a Pharisee; concerning zeal, persecuting the church;
concerning the righteousness which is in the law, blameless.*

Seven items — four by birth and three by achievement.

## The verdict on them

*But **what things were gain to me, these I have counted loss for Christ**.*

And: *I also count all things loss for the excellence of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my
Lord… and count them as **rubbish**.*

The Greek is **skubala** — refuse, dung. It is a coarse word and Paul chose it.

## What he wants instead

*And be found in Him, **not having my own righteousness, which is from the law, but that
which is through faith in Christ** — the righteousness which is from God by faith.*

## Where the chapter ends

*Our citizenship is registered in heaven.* See the note on Philippians 3:20–21.

> **What Paul does with the word.** Takes it. The party demanding circumcision is renamed
> the cutting — and the people refusing it are called the circumcision, defined by three
> things none of which involve a knife.
