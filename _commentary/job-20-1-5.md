The third friend's second speech, and the reason he gives for making it is that he is
upset.

## Who is speaking

*Then **Zophar from Naamah** answered.*

The third of the three. The shortest-tempered of them, and the only one who does not get
a third speech.

## Why he is speaking

*"**My uneasy thoughts drive me to reply**, because of **the turmoil churning inside
me**."*

He states his own motive and it is agitation.

Not a word about Job's condition, Job's argument, or anything he has learned. He is
answering because he cannot sit still.

## What has stung

*"**I have heard a rebuke that shames me**, and now my understanding urges me to speak."*

Job had just said, at the end of chapter 19: *be afraid of the sword for yourselves; for
wrath brings the punishment of the sword, that you may know there is a judgment.*

Zophar takes it personally, and the speech that follows is about how the wicked come to
nothing.

## What Job had said just before

The most famous sentence in the book: *For **I know that my Redeemer lives**, and He shall
stand at last on the earth; and after my skin is destroyed, this I know, that **in my
flesh I shall see God**.*

Zophar's answer to that is *the triumph of the wicked is short*.

He is replying to a confession of faith with a lecture about the fate of the godless.

## The appeal to what everybody knows

*"**Surely you know how it has always been**, ever since man was first set on the
earth."*

The stock move of all three friends. They argue from the way things generally go and
apply it to a case they have not understood.

## The thesis

*"**The triumph of the wicked is short. The joy of the godless lasts only a moment**."*

Which is true, and it is in Proverbs and the Psalms.

It is also, in this room, an accusation. Job has lost everything, so on Zophar's
reasoning the category is obvious.

## What the book has already told the reader

Chapter 1: *that man was blameless and upright, and one who feared God and shunned
evil.*

And the conversation in heaven that none of the men on the ground know about. See the
note on Job 1:6–12.

Every speech of the friends is made by men working from a rule that does not fit the
case in front of them.

## What God says about them at the end

*"**My wrath is aroused against you and your two friends, for you have not spoken of Me
what is right**, as My servant Job has."*

They are told to bring seven bulls and seven rams, and that Job will pray for them — *for
I will accept him.*

The man they had been correcting is the one appointed to intercede for them.

## What the speech does not contain

Any question. Zophar does not ask Job anything in the whole chapter.

> **The reason he gives for speaking.** The turmoil inside himself. Job's real answer,
> when it comes, is out of a whirlwind and consists almost entirely of questions.
