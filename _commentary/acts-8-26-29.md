Philip is in the middle of a revival and is sent to an empty road.

## What he was doing

The chapter before this: Philip in Samaria, *and the multitudes with one accord
heeded the things spoken by Philip… and there was great joy in that city.*

A city-wide response, and he is told to leave it.

## The instruction

*"**Get up and go south**, to the road that runs down from Jerusalem to Gaza."*

No reason given. No name, no destination, no explanation.

And Luke adds his own note: ***It is a desert road.***

He is sent from a crowd to a place with nobody on it.

## The obedience

*So he **got up and went**.*

Four words. No question is recorded.

## Who is on the road

*A man from Ethiopia, **a court official of Candace** the queen of the Ethiopians,
**in charge of all her treasury**.*

The finance minister of a kingdom, in a chariot, on the way home.

**Candace** is a title rather than a name — the queen mother of the Nubian kingdom.

## What he had been doing

*He had **come to Jerusalem to worship** and was now on his way home.*

A journey of some fifteen hundred miles, each way, to worship at a temple that would
not have let him past the outer court.

He is a God-fearer from the ends of the known world, going home with a scroll.

## What he is doing in the chariot

*Sitting in his chariot and **reading aloud from the prophet Isaiah**.*

Reading aloud was normal in the ancient world — texts were read out even in private.

A scroll of Isaiah was an enormous expense. He had bought one, and he is reading it
in a moving vehicle in a desert.

## The second instruction

*The Spirit said to Philip, "**Go over to that chariot and stay near it.**"*

The angel gave the road. The Spirit gives the chariot.

Guidance arrives in two stages, and Philip had to act on the first before he got the
second.

## What he does

*Philip **ran** up alongside.*

He runs, beside a moving chariot, and hears what is being read.

The passage the man had reached is Isaiah 53 — *led like a sheep to the slaughter*.
See the note on Acts 8:30–35.

> **Where it ends.** *The Spirit of the Lord caught Philip away… but Philip was found
> at Azotus.* Luke reports it flatly, and the next thing recorded is that he kept
> preaching through every city until he came to Caesarea. Twenty years later he is
> still there, with four daughters who prophesied, hosting Paul.
