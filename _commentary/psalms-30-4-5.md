Two lines built as a pair, and the pairing is the argument.

## The comparison

*For **His anger lasts only a moment**, but **His favour lasts a lifetime**.*

The Hebrew for *a moment* is the blink of an eye — the shortest measure the language
has.

Against it: **chayyim**, life, a lifetime.

They are not the same size, and the psalm is not saying they balance. It is saying
one is a blink and the other is the whole of the days.

## The second pair

*Weeping may **stay for the night**, but **joy comes with the morning**.*

The Hebrew for *stay* is to lodge, to spend the night — the word for a traveller
taking a room.

Weeping is a guest. It checks in, and it does not have a permanent address.

## The order of the Hebrew day

Evening, then morning — the way Genesis 1 counts every one of the six days.

Whatever the night is, the direction of travel in that calendar is always toward the
light.

## What is being addressed

*Sing to the LORD, **O you His faithful**. Give thanks to His holy name.*

The two lines about anger and weeping are addressed to His own people. It is not a
general description of how life goes; it is what the covenant faithful are told about
their own experience.

## What David had been through

The psalm's heading is *a song at the dedication of the house of David*, and the body
of it is a man recounting a rescue:

*O LORD my God, I cried out to You, and You healed me. O LORD, You brought my soul up
from the grave; You have kept me alive.*

And earlier: *in my prosperity I said, "I shall never be moved"… You hid Your face,
and I was troubled.*

He had been confident, then undone, then healed, and this psalm is written from the
far side.

## How it ends

*You have turned for me **my mourning into dancing**; You have put off my sackcloth
and clothed me with gladness — to the end that my glory may sing praise to You and
not be silent.*

The Hebrew for *turned* is the word for overturning, converting a thing into
something else.

Not mourning followed by dancing. Mourning made into it.

> **The word Isaiah uses for judgement.** *His strange work… His alien task* —
> foreign, not native to Him. This psalm is the same claim in a song: the anger is a
> blink, the favour is the lifetime. See the note on Isaiah 28:20–22.
