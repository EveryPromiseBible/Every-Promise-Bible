Four attributes of God, personified, meeting each other in a field.

## The meeting

*Steadfast love and faithfulness **have met**.*

The Hebrew for *steadfast love* is **chesed** — covenant loyalty, the word that runs
through the whole Psalter. And **emeth**, truth, firmness, reliability.

The verb is *have met together* — **nifgashu**, encountered one another.

## The kiss

*Righteousness and peace **have kissed**.*

**Tsedeq** and **shalom**.

These two are the pair with a problem. Righteousness requires the account to be settled;
peace requires it to be dropped. The psalm has them kissing.

Everything in the Bible about how that becomes possible is downstream of this line.

## The direction of each

*Faithfulness **springs up from the ground**, and righteousness **leans down from the sky**.*

The Hebrew is *truth shall spring out of the earth, and righteousness shall look down from
heaven*.

One grows up; the other bends down. They arrive from opposite ends.

## Where the two directions meet elsewhere

Paul, quoting Deuteronomy: *do not say in your heart, "Who will ascend into heaven?" (that
is, to bring Christ down) or "Who will descend into the abyss?"… **the word is near you, in
your mouth and in your heart***. See the note on Romans 10:6–8.

Righteousness that came down is not to be fetched. It has already made the journey.

## The giving

*The LORD will **give what is good**, and our land will **yield its harvest**.*

The Hebrew is *the LORD shall give what is good, and our land shall yield its increase*.

His giving, then the land's yielding. The order is not reversible.

## The last image

*Righteousness **walks ahead of Him**, **clearing a path for His feet**.*

The Hebrew is *righteousness will go before Him and shall make His footsteps a way*.

Righteousness is the outrider, not the escort. It goes first and the road is what it leaves
behind.

## Where the psalm starts

*LORD, You have been favourable to Your land; You have brought back the captivity of
Jacob. You have **forgiven the iniquity of Your people**; You have **covered all their
sin**.*

Past tense, all of it. The psalm opens with what has already been done and then asks *revive
us again*.

## The turn in the middle

*I will hear what God the LORD will speak, for **He will speak peace to His people**.*

The listening comes just before the four attributes arrive. The psalmist stops asking and
waits for a sentence.

## Where the kiss happened

*Being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus… **that
He might be just, and the justifier** of the one who has faith in Jesus.* See the note on
Romans 3:23–26.

Paul's word for it is *demonstrate His righteousness*. The problem the psalm poses — how
both can be satisfied at once — is the thing Paul says the cross was for.

And *having been justified by faith, **we have peace with God***.

> **The line the whole Bible has to solve.** Righteousness and peace, kissing. One of them
> insists the debt be paid and the other insists it be over, and the psalm puts them face
> to face as though it were nothing.
