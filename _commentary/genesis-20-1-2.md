Two verses, and the man doing this has already done it once.

## Where he goes

*From there Abraham journeyed toward the region of the Negev and **settled between Kadesh and
Shur**.*

South. He moves after the destruction of the plain — the smoke had been visible from his camp
at Mamre. See the note on Genesis 19:27–29.

**Shur** is on the way to Egypt. Hagar had been found at a spring on that road.

## The place

*While staying in **Gerar**.*

Philistine country, on the coastal side.

## The sentence

*Abraham said of his wife Sarah, "**She is my sister**."*

The same words as chapter 12, in Egypt, to Pharaoh. See the note on
Genesis 12:10–20.

Twenty-five years apart, and the second time is worse.

## Why it is worse

Since the first occasion he has been given the covenant, been renamed, been circumcised, and
been told the son would be born within the year.

Sarah is pregnant, or about to be. Chapter 21 opens with Isaac's birth, and the promise in
chapter 18 had a date on it. See the note on Genesis 18:9–15.

He hands her over at the point in the story when the promise is physically at stake.

## The result

*So **Abimelech king of Gerar sent for Sarah and took her**.*

Sarah is around ninety. The taking is presumably political — a marriage alliance with a
wealthy chief — which is what makes the lie work.

## What Abraham says when confronted

Three defences, and he uses all of them.

*"Because I thought, surely **the fear of God is not in this place**; and they will kill me on
account of my wife."*

*"But indeed **she is truly my sister**. She is the daughter of my father, but not the daughter
of my mother."*

*"And it came to pass, when God caused me to wander from my father's house, that I said to her,
'This is your kindness that you should do for me: **in every place we go, say of me, "He is my
brother."**'"*

It was true, it was a standing arrangement, and he was frightened. The third defence is the
most damaging: he had made it a policy.

## What God says about him

*"Now therefore, restore the man's wife; for **he is a prophet, and he will pray for you and
you shall live**."*

Said to Abimelech, about the man who has just endangered his wife.

The first use of the word **navi**, prophet, in the Bible, and it is applied here.

## Who comes out well

Abimelech.

*"Lord, will You slay a righteous nation also? Did he not say to me, 'She is my sister'?…
**In the integrity of my heart and innocence of my hands I have done this**."*

And God agrees: *yes, I know that you did this in the integrity of your heart*.

The pagan king behaves better than the patriarch, and the text says so.

## The rebuke

*Then Abimelech called Abraham and said to him, "**What have you done to us? How have I
offended you, that you have brought on me and on my kingdom a great sin?**"*

*"**You have done deeds to me that ought not to be done**."*

## What happens next

*So Abraham prayed to God; and **God healed Abimelech, his wife, and his female servants**.
For the LORD had closed up all the wombs of the house of Abimelech.*

The prophet prays, and the wombs open — in the chapter immediately before the one where his
own wife's does.

## Why Genesis includes it

Nothing requires this chapter. The story would run without it.

It is here because Genesis does not tidy up its people. The man Paul builds the doctrine of
justification by faith on. See the note on Romans 4:1–3.

Righteousness had been credited to him five chapters earlier, and it was not credited for
this.

> **The worst of the three defences.** Not that it was half true and not that he was afraid.
> That he had arranged it in advance, as a standing policy, *in every place we go*.
