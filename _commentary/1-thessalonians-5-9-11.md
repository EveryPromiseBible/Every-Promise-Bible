Paul has spent a chapter and a half on the Lord's return, and this is the
conclusion he draws from it.

## The appointment

*God never marked us out for wrath. He marked us out to come into salvation
through our Lord Jesus Christ.*

The verb is about setting or appointing — placing someone in a category. Paul
denies one appointment and states another, and both are God's doing rather than
the reader's.

The Thessalonians had been anxious. The letter has already reassured them about
their dead; now it settles the other question, which is what is coming for the
living.

## "Awake or asleep"

*So that awake or asleep, we are alive with Him, and alive together.*

Paul has used *asleep* for the Christian dead all through chapter 4. Here he uses
both words in one clause and says the outcome is identical.

Whether the Lord finds a believer alive or already buried makes no difference to
the result — *alive with Him, and alive together.* It is the same reassurance he
gave earlier, compressed to a phrase.

## What he tells them to do about it

*So keep encouraging one another, and keep building one another up.*

The practical instruction attached to the second coming is not preparation, not
calculation, and not vigilance about dates. It is to speak to each other.

He said the same at the end of chapter 4 — *comfort one another with these
words*. Twice, and both times the teaching about the return is handed over as
material for encouraging somebody else.

> **The last clause.** *Which is exactly what you are already doing.* Paul adds
> it almost in passing, and it is characteristic — he tells a church to do the
> thing and then notes that they are doing it. The instruction arrives as
> confirmation rather than correction.
