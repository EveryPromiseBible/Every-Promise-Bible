The crowd works out that He left without a boat, and then asks the wrong question about it.

## The arithmetic

*The next day the crowd that had stayed on the other side of the sea noticed that **there had been
only one boat there**, and that **Jesus had not gotten into it** with His disciples — they had gone
off alone.*

They have counted the boats.

He fed them, withdrew to the mountain alone when they tried to crown Him, and now He is not on
this side and He did not sail.

## The detail John adds

*Other boats came in from Tiberias and landed **near the place where the people had eaten the bread
after the Lord had given thanks**.*

The Greek is **eucharistesantos tou Kuriou** — the Lord having given thanks.

John names the location by what happened there, and the event he names it by is the thanksgiving,
not the multiplication.

## The pursuit

*So… they **climbed into the boats themselves and went over to Capernaum, looking for Jesus**.*

They cross a lake to find Him. It is not indifference.

## The question

*"**Rabbi, when did You get here?**"*

Which is the question about the boat.

They have the makings of a genuine puzzle — He crossed water without transport — and they ask
about the timetable.

## What they do not ask

How.

The disciples know: *they saw Jesus walking on the sea and drawing near the boat, and they were
afraid*.

## The answer

*"I tell you the truth, **you are looking for Me not because you saw the signs, but because you ate
the loaves and had your fill**."*

The Greek is **echortasthete** — you were filled, satisfied. The word for feeding animals to
fullness.

## What He does not do

Answer the question.

He tells them their motive instead, to their faces, having just fed them.

## The distinction

**Semeia** — signs. Things that point.

They saw a sign and took the bread. The pointing did not register.

## The instruction

*"**Do not work for the food that spoils, but for the food that lasts on into eternal life**."*

The Greek is **ergazesthe me ten brosin ten apollumenen** — work not for the perishing food.

Not *do not work*. Do not work **for** that.

## The manna behind it

The food that would not keep, gathered fresh every morning, breeding worms if held overnight. See
the note on Exodus 16:19–21.

The crowd raises it themselves two verses later: *our fathers ate the manna in the desert*.

## Who supplies it

*"**The food the Son of Man will give you**."*

The Greek is **dosei** — future. He will give it.

Which sits oddly against *work for* in the same sentence, and John lets both stand.

Their next question is exactly that: *what shall we do, that we may work the works of God?*

*"**This is the work of God, that you believe in Him whom He sent**."*

## The seal

*"God the Father has **put His own mark of approval on Him**."*

The Greek is **esphragisen** — sealed.

The word for a seal pressed into wax to certify ownership and authenticity. See the note on
Revelation 7:1–3.

## Where the chapter goes

*"**I am the bread of life**. He who comes to Me shall never hunger."*

And then the discourse hardens, and the crowd that crossed a lake to find Him leaves. See the note
on John 6:66–71.

> **The name of the place.** John identifies the site as *where they ate the bread after the Lord
> had given thanks* — naming it by the thanksgiving rather than by the miracle.
