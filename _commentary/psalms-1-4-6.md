The psalm has spent three verses on a tree, and now says what the other man is like.

## The contrast

*The wicked are **nothing like this**.*

The Hebrew is **lo-ken haresha'im** — not so the wicked.

The Septuagint doubles it: *not so, not so*.

## What the first half said

*He shall be like **a tree planted by the rivers of water**, that brings forth its fruit in its
season, whose leaf also shall not wither; and whatever he does shall prosper.* See the note on
Psalms 1:1–3.

Planted, watered, fruiting on schedule, green.

## The image

*They are only **chaff, loose husks the wind scatters away**.*

The Hebrew is **kammotz asher-tiddephennu ruach** — like chaff which the wind drives.

## What chaff is

The husk left after threshing. The grain is beaten loose on the floor, then thrown into the air —
the heavy kernel drops and the light casing blows off.

It is not evil. It is weightless, and it was attached to something that mattered.

## The pairing

A tree with roots in water, against something with no root and no weight.

The whole contrast is about whether a thing is anchored.

## The judgment

*So when **the reckoning** comes, the wicked **will not stand**.*

The Hebrew is **bammishpat** — in the judgment.

**Lo-yaqumu** — will not rise, will not stand up.

## The assembly

*Nor **sinners find a place among the gathered righteous**.*

The Hebrew is **ba'adat tzaddiqim** — in the congregation of the righteous.

The image is a court or an assembly with standing membership. Chaff cannot be counted in it
because chaff is not there when the wind has finished.

## The reason

*For **the LORD keeps watch over the way of the righteous**.*

The Hebrew is **yodea YHWH derek tzaddiqim** — the LORD knows the way of the righteous.

**Yada** — knows, in the sense of relationship rather than information. The word used of Adam and
Eve, and of *you only have I known of all the families of the earth*.

## The other way

*But **the way of the wicked simply fades and is gone**.*

The Hebrew is **toved** — perishes.

The two ways are not given symmetrical treatment. One is known; the other is not destroyed by an
active verb — it simply ends.

## What the two verbs do

The psalm's whole argument is in the last verse. The difference between the tree and the chaff is
not primarily behaviour. It is that one way is known and one is not.

## Where the chaff turns up again

*"His winnowing fan is in His hand, and He will thoroughly clean out His threshing floor, and
gather His wheat into the barn; **but He will burn up the chaff with unquenchable fire**."*

John the Baptist, at the Jordan, to the crowds coming out to him.

## What the psalm is for

Psalm 1 has no title, no author, and no petition. It is not addressed to God.

It stands at the front of the book as a doorway: two ways, and the reader has to walk through it
to get to the other hundred and forty-nine.

## What the second psalm does

*Why do the nations rage… against the LORD and against His Anointed?*

*Blessed are all those who put their trust in Him.* See the note on Psalms 2:10–12.

Psalm 1 opens with **ashrei** — blessed — and Psalm 2 closes with the same word. The two are a
pair.

> **The image.** Chaff is not wicked; it is weightless. The contrast with the tree is not about
> behaviour but about whether a thing has roots in water.
