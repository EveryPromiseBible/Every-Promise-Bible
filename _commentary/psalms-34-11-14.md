The psalm turns into a lesson halfway through, and the teacher gathers the children.

## The setting

*A Psalm of David when he pretended madness before Abimelech, who drove him away, and he
departed.*

He had fled to Gath — a Philistine city, carrying Goliath's sword — was recognised, and *changed
his behaviour before them, feigned madness, scratched on the doors of the gate, and let his saliva
fall down on his beard*.

The psalm that opens *I will bless the LORD at all times* was written out of that.

## The call

*Come close, **children**, and listen.*

The Hebrew is **banim** — sons.

The address of a wisdom teacher. Proverbs opens the same way: *my son, hear the instruction of your
father*.

## What he will teach

*I will teach you **to walk in awe of the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **yir'at YHWH alammedchem** — the fear of the LORD I will teach you.

It is presented as a subject that can be taught. Not a temperament.

## The question

*Who among you **loves being alive**, who wants **long days full of good**?*

The Hebrew is **mi-ha'ish hechaphetz chayyim** — who is the man delighting in life?

**Ohev yamim lir'ot tov** — loving days to see good.

He asks for a show of hands before giving the answer.

## The first instruction

*Then **keep your tongue from evil**, keep your **lips from telling lies**.*

The Hebrew is **netzor leshonecha mera** — guard your tongue from evil.

**Natzar** — to watch, to guard as a sentry guards a post.

## Why speech first

The answer to a question about long life is about the mouth.

Not diet, not danger, not work. The psalm names the tongue as the first item.

## The second

*Turn away from wrong and **do what is good**.*

Two movements, negative and positive. Departure and action.

## The third

*Go after peace, **chase it down**.*

The Hebrew is **baqqesh shalom veradpehu** — seek peace and pursue it.

**Radaph** — to chase, to hunt, to run after. The verb used of an army pursuing a retreating enemy.

## What that implies

Peace does not stay put.

The psalm does not say *keep the peace*. It says run after it, which assumes it is moving away.

## What follows

*The eyes of the LORD are on the righteous, and **His ears are open to their cry**.*

*The LORD is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves such as have a contrite spirit.*

## The line about bones

*He guards all his bones; **not one of them is broken**.*

John quotes it at the cross: *for these things were done that the Scripture should be fulfilled,
"Not one of His bones shall be broken."* See the note on John 19:31–34.

## Where Peter quotes it

*For "**He who would love life and see good days, let him refrain his tongue from evil**… let him seek
peace and pursue it."* See the note on 1 Peter 3:10.

Four verses, quoted whole, into a letter to persecuted congregations.

## The other line the psalm is known for

*Oh, **taste and see that the LORD is good**; blessed is the man who trusts in Him!* See the note on
Psalms 34:8–10.

Quoted by Peter as well, two chapters earlier.

> **The answer to the question.** Asked who wants a long life, the psalm's first instruction is
> about the tongue — and the last one is a hunting verb.
