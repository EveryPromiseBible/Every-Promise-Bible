The exception first, and then the orchard.

## The exception

*"But its **swamps and marshes will not be made fresh. They are left for salt**."*

The Hebrew is *they shall be given over to salt*.

The vision does not claim everything changes. Some ground stays as it is, and the text gives a
reason rather than an apology: salt was needed.

Every grain offering was seasoned with it — *with all your offerings you shall offer salt* —
and the phrase for a permanent agreement was **berith melach**, a covenant of salt.

The Dead Sea salt pans supplied it.

## The trees

*"Along the river, **on both banks, every kind of tree for food will grow**."*

The Hebrew is *every tree for food*.

Not ornamental. Everything on the bank is edible.

## The leaves

*"**Their leaves will not wither**."*

The Hebrew is **lo yibbol** — shall not fade.

Psalm 1 has the same clause of a man: *he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water,
that brings forth its fruit in its season, whose **leaf also shall not wither***. See the note
on Psalms 1:1–3.

Same image, same phrase, and the psalm's tree is planted by water channels.

## The fruit

*"And **their fruit will not fail. Every month they will bear fresh fruit**."*

The Hebrew is *it shall bring forth new fruit according to its months*.

Fruit trees have a season. That is what a season is for.

These do not.

## The reason

*"Because **the water that flows to them comes from the sanctuary**."*

The Hebrew is *because their waters issued out of the sanctuary*.

The vision explains the abnormality by naming the source. Nothing about the trees is
different; the water is.

## The two uses

*"Their **fruit will be for food**, and **their leaves for healing**."*

The Hebrew for *healing* is **terufah** — a word that occurs once in the Bible.

Two products from one tree, and neither is wasted.

## Where it ends up

*In the middle of its street, and on either side of the river, was **the tree of life**, which
bore twelve fruits, **each tree yielding its fruit every month**. **The leaves of the tree were
for the healing of the nations**.*

Revelation 22, and it is Ezekiel's paragraph almost word for word — the river, the trees on
both banks, monthly fruit, and leaves for healing.

John adds one thing: the tree is named. It is the tree of life, last seen in Genesis 3 with a
flaming sword turning every way to guard the path to it.

## The three rivers

Eden: *a river went out of Eden to water the garden, and from there it parted and became four
riverheads*.

Ezekiel: out from under the threshold, deepening with no tributary.

Revelation: *proceeding from the throne of God and of the Lamb*.

The Bible opens with a river in a garden and closes with one running down a street.

## What Jesus said about it

*"He who believes in Me, as the Scripture has said, **out of his heart will flow rivers of
living water**."*

And John's note: *this He spoke concerning the Spirit*. See the note on John 7:37–39.

He says *as the Scripture has said*, and does not say which. This chapter is the strongest
candidate.

> **Why the trees are abnormal.** Nothing about the trees. *Because their water comes out of
> the sanctuary* — and the fruit arrives every month because of where the river started.
