*Wherever your treasure is, that is where your heart will be living.* Most people
quote it the other way round — put your heart somewhere and your money follows.
Jesus says the reverse.

## The order He gives

Treasure first, heart after. The heart is described as taking up residence
wherever the valuables already are.

That makes it a diagnosis and a lever at once. A diagnosis, because a person can
find out what they actually treasure by looking at where their resources have
gone. A lever, because moving the treasure is presented as the thing that moves
the affection.

## The three thieves

*Moths eat it and rust eats it and thieves break in and carry it off.*

Three ways to lose it, covering the three forms wealth took: cloth, metal, and
anything storable. The Greek behind *rust* is broader than iron corrosion — it is
eating away, decay of any kind.

Jesus is not moralising about wealth here. He is making a practical argument
about where things keep.

> **The verse after it.** He goes straight on to the eye being the lamp of the
> body, and then to serving two masters. The passage is a run: where your money
> is, what your eye is fixed on, who you actually work for. All three ask the same
> question from a different angle, and Luke's version of the third one is in the
> note on Luke 16:9–13.
