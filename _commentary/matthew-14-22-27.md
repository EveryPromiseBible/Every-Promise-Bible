He sends them into the storm, and then comes to them at the worst hour of the
night.

## Who put them there

*Right away Jesus **had the disciples get into the boat** and go on ahead of Him.*

The Greek is stronger than *had* — He compelled them, made them get in.

They are on that lake because He put them there. It is not a wrong turn.

## Where He is

*He climbed the mountainside **by Himself to pray**. Evening fell, and He was there
alone.*

This is the evening after the feeding of the five thousand. He dismisses the crowd,
sends the disciples off, and goes up alone.

## The wind

*The boat was already out in the middle of the sea, **thrown about by the waves, for
the wind was against it**.*

The Greek is that the wind was contrary — against them.

Mark adds that He *saw them straining at rowing*, from the mountain, in the dark.
They could not see Him; He could see them.

## The fourth watch

*In **the fourth watch of the night** Jesus came toward them.*

The Romans divided the night into four watches. The fourth runs roughly from three
until six in the morning — the last stretch, the coldest, the darkest before it
lightens.

They had been rowing since evening. He comes at the end of it, not the beginning.

## What frightens them

*When the disciples saw Him walking on the water **they were terrified**. "**It is a
ghost!**" they said, and they cried out in fear.*

The storm had not made them scream. He did.

The thing that had come to rescue them was the most frightening thing that happened
that night, because it did not arrive in a shape they recognised.

## What He says

*"**Take heart. It is I. Do not be afraid.**"*

Three short sentences, and the middle one is two words in Greek: **ego eimi** — *I
am*.

It is the ordinary way of saying *it's me*. It is also the name from the burning
bush, and John's Gospel builds seven sayings on it. See the note on
Exodus 3:13–15.

## What He was going to do

Mark records something Matthew leaves out: *He came toward them, walking on the sea,
**and would have passed them by**.*

Whatever that means, He was not going to force Himself into the boat. They *cried
out* — and then He spoke.

## What happens next

Peter asks to come, walks, looks at the wind, sinks, and *immediately Jesus stretched
out His hand and caught him.*

And then: *when they got into the boat, the wind ceased.*

> **Where He had just been.** Praying, alone, on a mountain, all night — and the
> chapter opens with Him hearing that John the Baptist had been beheaded, and trying
> to withdraw to a deserted place before the crowds followed Him.
