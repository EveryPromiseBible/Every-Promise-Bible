God gives the history from the beginning, and the beginning is an oath sworn in Egypt.

## The occasion

*It came to pass in the seventh year, in the fifth month, on the tenth day of the month, that
certain of the elders of Israel came to inquire of the LORD, and sat before me.*

They are in Babylon. The elders come to Ezekiel with a question, and God's answer is *will you
judge them? Then make known to them the abominations of their fathers.*

What follows is a long recital of the nation's history, and it starts here.

## The choosing

*On the day **I chose Israel**, **I lifted My hand and swore** to the offspring of the house of
Jacob.*

The Hebrew is **nasati yadi** — I lifted My hand. The gesture of taking an oath.

The idiom appears four times in these two verses and repeatedly through the chapter. It is
Ezekiel's signature for God binding Himself.

## Where He made Himself known

*I made Myself known to them **in the land of Egypt**.*

Not at Sinai. In Egypt, before the exodus, while they were still slaves.

The self-disclosure comes before the deliverance.

## What He swore

*I lifted My hand and swore, '**I am the LORD your God**.'*

The Hebrew is **ani YHWH Eloheichem**.

The same sentence that opens the ten commandments — *I am the LORD your God, who brought you out
of the land of Egypt* — is here sworn as an oath before any commandment exists.

## The promise

*On that day **I promised to bring them out of Egypt into a land I had searched out for them**.*

The Hebrew is **tarti lahem** — which I explored, spied out, searched for them.

**Tur** is the verb used of the twelve spies: *send men to spy out the land of Canaan*. See the
note on Numbers 13:17–20.

## What the verb does

God uses the scouts' word of Himself.

Before Moses sent twelve men to survey it, God says He had already surveyed it — and chosen it
for them.

Ten of the twelve came back and said *we are not able*. See the note on
Numbers 13:26–33.

## The description

*A land **flowing with milk and honey**, **the loveliest of all the lands**.*

The Hebrew is **tzevi hi lekol-ha'aratzot** — the beauty, the ornament, the glory of all lands.

**Tzevi** — glory, splendour, and also the gazelle. A word for something both beautiful and
swift.

## Where else Ezekiel uses it

*The beauty of their ornament* — of the temple, when he speaks of what will be profaned.

And Daniel uses it of the same territory: **eretz hatzevi**, the Glorious Land.

## What the chapter goes on to say

*Then I said to them, "**Each of you, throw away the abominations which are before his eyes**"…
**but they rebelled against Me and would not obey Me**.*

In Egypt. Before the Red Sea, before the wilderness, before the golden calf.

The chapter records four generations of the same pattern and the same refrain: *I acted for My
name's sake, that it should not be profaned*.

## What God says He did instead of ending it

*Nevertheless **I withheld My hand and acted for My name's sake**.*

The same hand that was lifted in the oath is the hand withheld.

## Where the chapter ends

*"**I will accept you as a sweet aroma when I bring you out from the peoples**… and you shall know
that I am the LORD, when I have dealt with you for My name's sake, **not according to your wicked
ways nor according to your corrupt doings**."*

A long catalogue of failure, and that is the conclusion drawn from it.

## Before the deliverance

*I made Myself known to them **in the land of Egypt**.* Not at Sinai. While they were still
slaves, before a commandment existed and before the sea opened.

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that **while we were still sinners, Christ died
for us**.*

*We love Him because **He first loved us**.*

**John**'s adverb is **protos** — first, in order. The whole chapter of Ezekiel 20 is a recital of
Israel failing, and its opening move is God introducing Himself to them before they had done
anything at all, in the country they were enslaved in.

## The land He had searched out

**Tur** — the scouts' own verb. God says He had surveyed it and chosen it before twelve men were
sent to look.

*"In My Father's house are many mansions; if it were not so, I would have told you. **I go to
prepare a place for you**. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and receive
you to Myself."*

**John** 14, and the same order: the place is found and made ready before anybody is brought to
it.

*To an inheritance incorruptible and undefiled and that does not fade away, **reserved in heaven
for you**.*

**Peter**'s verb is **tetremenen** — kept, guarded. Ezekiel's **tzevi**, the glory of all lands,
was scouted in advance; **1 Peter**'s is being held.

## For My name's sake

*"I have dealt with you **for My name's sake, not according to your wicked ways nor according to
your corrupt doings**."*

That is the conclusion the chapter draws from four generations of failure, and it is the shape of
the New Testament's own sentence:

*Who has saved us and called us with a holy calling, **not according to our works, but according
to His own purpose and grace** which was given to us in **Christ Jesus** before time began.*

*Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but **according to His mercy**.*

**2 Timothy** and **Titus**, both built on the same *not… but*, and both putting the reason on
God's side of the sentence exactly where Ezekiel puts it.

> **The verb.** **Tur** — the scouts' own word for surveying a land. God says He had already done
> it, for them, before twelve men were ever sent.
