Seven words of title, and the grammar of them is the strongest construction Hebrew has.

## The construction

*The **song of all songs**, the loveliest ever sung.*

The Hebrew is **shir hashirim** — the song of songs. It is how Hebrew makes a superlative: not
by an adjective, but by putting a noun in charge of its own plural.

The same shape gives **qodesh haqodashim** — the holy of holies. And **elohei ha'elohim**, God of
gods. And **melekh hammelakhim**, king of kings.

## What Akiva said

*All the writings are holy, but the Song of Songs is **the Holy of Holies**.*

He was making a pun on the title. The book that is *the song of songs* is, in the same
grammatical breath, the *holy of holies* — the room past the veil.

## What the New Testament does with the same construction

*And He has on His robe and on His thigh a name written: **KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS**.*

**Revelation** puts the superlative on a rider whose robe is dipped in blood. It is the Song's
own grammar, applied to a Person.

## And it belongs to Solomon

*Asher lishlomo* — which is Solomon's.

*"The queen of the South… came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and
indeed **a greater than Solomon is here**."*

**Matthew** puts the comparison on record. The finest song in the language belongs to a king;
the New Testament says a greater one than that king was standing in the temple courts.

## What kind of book follows

Eight chapters of dialogue, in two voices and a chorus, with no narrator, no plot, and no
mention of God's name until a flame at the end carries a syllable of it. See the note on
Song of Songs 8:6–7.

It is a love poem, and it is not embarrassed about being one.

## Where the New Testament takes it

*"For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two
shall become one flesh." **This is a great mystery, but I speak concerning Christ and the
church**.*

**Paul** says the marriage was always describing something else. That is the licence the whole
Christian reading of this book rests on, and it is given by an apostle about the institution the
Song is written for.

*"Let us be glad and rejoice and give Him glory, for **the marriage of the Lamb has come**, and
His wife has made herself ready."*

## The new song

The Song of Songs is the loveliest ever sung, and the Bible ends with a different one being
learned.

*And they sang **a new song**, saying: "You are worthy… **for You were slain, and have redeemed
us to God by Your blood** out of every tribe and tongue and people and nation."*

**Revelation** 5. The reason given for the new song is not the singer's love. It is the price the
one being sung to paid.

> **The grammar of the title.** *Shir hashirim* — the same construction as *holy of holies* and
> *King of kings*. Akiva heard the pun and said so: the song of songs is the room past the veil.
