Jesus is at a dinner party and says the hardest thing in Luke. The sentence at
the centre of it is a correction of proportion, not a cancellation.

## What He accuses them of

*You carefully tithe your mint and your rue and every garden herb you grow, and
you step straight over justice and the love of God.*

The precision is the joke. The law asked for a tenth of the crop; these men were
counting leaves out of the kitchen garden while walking over the two largest
things in the law.

## The line He does not say

He does not say *stop tithing your herbs*. He says:

> *These are the things you should have been doing, **without letting go of the
> others**.*

Both. Justice and the love of God taken up, and the giving not put down. Jesus
had every opportunity in that sentence to abolish the practice and instead He
kept it in place while re-ranking it.

That is a correction of what had gone missing, not of what they were doing with
the mint.

## The remedy He gives

*You Pharisees scrub the outside of the cup and the plate, but inside you are
full of greed and cruelty… Give away what is inside as a gift to the poor, and
see — everything about you is clean.*

Having diagnosed greed under a polished exterior, His prescription is not more
careful measuring. It is generosity — giving the contents away. He treats open-
handedness as the thing that reaches what scrubbing never could.

> **Why the meal setting matters.** This is said at a table, to a host, after
> Jesus declined to perform the ritual handwashing. Everything in the passage is
> about outsides and insides — clean hands and grasping hearts, polished dishes
> and unmarked graves. The tithing line belongs to that argument: a right
> practice, carried out by a heart that had never been dealt with.
