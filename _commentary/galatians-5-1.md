One sentence, and it is the hinge of the letter.

## Where it comes

The chapter division hides it. This is the *therefore* of the allegory that ends chapter
4 — Hagar and Sarah, the slave woman cast out, and *we are not children of the slave woman
but of the free*. See the note on Galatians 4:28–31.

## The purpose

*So stand firm **in the freedom by which Christ has set us free**.*

The Greek is *for freedom Christ has made us free* — **te eleutheria**, in the dative, which
can be read as the purpose.

Freedom is named as the point of the setting free, not as a by-product of it.

## The verb

*Stand firm.*

The Greek is **stekete** — stand fast, hold your ground.

Paul uses it in Ephesians of the armour — *having done all, to stand* — and in
Philippians: *stand fast in the Lord*.

Nothing is to be advanced or won. The instruction is to remain where they were put.

## What is already done

*By which **Christ has set us free**.*

The verb is aorist. It happened.

Paul is not asking them to get free. He is telling free people not to go back in.

## The warning

*And **do not let yourselves be harnessed again into a yoke of slavery**.*

The Greek is **zugo douleias** — a yoke of bondage. A yoke is the crossbar laid over
working animals.

**Again** — the Greek is **palin**. They had been under one and had come out.

## What the yoke was

Circumcision and the calendar. Paul says so in the next verse: *if you become circumcised,
Christ will profit you nothing.*

Peter used the same image at the council in Jerusalem: *why do you test God by putting a
yoke on the neck of the disciples which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear?* See
the note on Acts 15:6–9.

## The other yoke

*"Take **My yoke** upon you and learn from Me… **for My yoke is easy and My burden is
light**."* See the note on Matthew 11:28–30.

Two yokes in the New Testament. One is described as slavery and one as rest.

## What follows

*You have become estranged from Christ, you who attempt to be justified by law; **you have
fallen from grace**.*

The Greek is **exepesate** — fallen out of.

Paul's own definition of the phrase is in the clause before it: *you who attempt to be
justified by law*. See the note on Galatians 5:2–4.

## What the freedom is not

*For **you were called to liberty; only do not use liberty as an opportunity for the
flesh**, but through love serve one another.*

The Greek for *opportunity* is **aphorme** — the bridgehead word, the same one he used of
sin using the commandment as a launching point. See the note on Romans 7:7–8.

He names both ditches in the same chapter.

## The summary

*For **all the law is fulfilled in one word**, even in this: "**You shall love your
neighbour as yourself**."*

> **The shape of the instruction.** Not go, not fight, not achieve. Stand — and the only
> thing named as a danger is being put back under something they had already come out
> from.
