Four verbs at the end of the sentence, and every one of them is God's.

## The title

*And **the God all grace comes from**.*

The Greek is *the God of all grace*. Peter has used the word grace throughout the letter
— *the grace that was to come to you*, *stewards of the manifold grace of God*, *God
gives grace to the humble*, four verses back.

He names Him by it here, at the point where he is describing suffering.

## The calling

*The One who **called us into His forever glory** through Christ Jesus.*

The destination is stated before the trouble is mentioned. Glory, and it is called
**eternal** — set against the *little while* in the next clause.

**Through Christ Jesus.** Peter puts the route in.

## The measure

*After you have suffered **your little while**.*

The Greek is **oligon** — a little, a small amount. It can mean brief or slight; here it
is set against *eternal*, so the sense is duration.

Peter does not say it is small in itself. He says it is small next to the other thing in
the sentence.

He has said it before in the letter: *though now for **a little while**, if need be, you
have been grieved by various trials.*

## The four verbs

*Will **put you back together**, **hold you up**, **make you strong**, and **set you on
rock**.*

- **Katartizo** — to mend, to repair, to set a bone, to refit a ship. It is the word used
  of the disciples *mending their nets*, and of restoring a fallen brother in Galatians.
- **Sterizo** — to fix firmly, to establish. It is the word Jesus used to Peter: *when
  you have turned back, **strengthen** your brothers.* See the note on Luke 22:31–34.
- **Sthenoo** — to make strong, to fill with strength. It occurs once in the New
  Testament, here.
- **Themelioo** — to lay a foundation, to ground.

Four different repairs, and the tense is future indicative. Peter states them as
facts, not possibilities.

## Who does them

All four verbs have the same subject: **the God of all grace**.

Nothing in the sentence is asked of the reader. Verses 8 and 9 gave the instructions —
be sober, watch, resist, stand. This verse has none.

## What restoration means

The first verb is the one used for a broken bone or a torn net. Peter assumes damage.

He does not promise that nothing will break. He promises who does the mending.

## The doxology

*To Him be the glory and the dominion forever and ever. Amen.*

Peter finishes the argument and then stops arguing.

## Who is writing it

A man who had collapsed completely and been put back together on a beach over
breakfast. He knows the word for mending nets because he had mended them.

> **The order of the sentence.** Grace first, glory second, the suffering third and
> called little, and then four things God does. The suffering is the shortest item in
> the verse.
