Luke records the first sentence spoken from the cross, and it is a prayer for the people
holding the hammer.

## The company

*Two others were led out with Him as well, **both of them criminals**.*

Luke's word is **kakourgoi** — evildoers. Matthew and Mark call them robbers.

*One on His right and one on His left* — the position James and John had asked for.

## What Luke does not describe

*They crucified Him there.*

Four words. No description of the nails, the raising, or the pain. All four Gospels do
this.

## The prayer

*And Jesus said, "**Father, forgive them; they do not know what they are doing**."*

The Greek verb is imperfect — He **kept saying** it. Not once.

## Who "them" is

Luke does not say. The soldiers driving the nails are nearest. The rulers who arranged it
are standing there watching. The crowd is there too.

He leaves it open, and the reason He gives applies to all of them: **they do not know**.

## The category

Ignorance is exactly the condition the cities of refuge were built for — a death caused
*without meaning to*. See the notes on Numbers 35:9–15 and Numbers 35:16–21.

Peter says it to Jerusalem a few weeks later: *"I know that you did it **in ignorance**, as
did also your rulers."*

And Paul: *"had they known, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory."*

## The clothes

*Then they **divided His clothes among themselves, throwing lots** for them.*

Psalm 22: *they divide My garments among them, and for My clothing they cast lots.*

Written a thousand years earlier by a man describing an execution method that did not
exist yet.

## Who watched

*The people **stood there watching**.*

Luke separates the crowd from the mockers. The people watch; the rulers sneer.

## The three mockeries

Each has the same shape — *save Yourself*:

- *The **rulers** sneered: "He saved other people. Let Him save Himself, **if He is the
  Messiah of God, the chosen One**."*
- *The **soldiers** mocked Him too… "**If You are the king of the Jews**, save
  Yourself."*
- And one of the criminals, in the next verse: *"**If You are the Christ**, save Yourself
  and us."*

Three times *if*, and it is the same word the wilderness temptation opened with: *if You
are the Son of God.*

## What the rulers admitted

*"**He saved other people**."*

They concede it in the middle of the taunt. Nobody in the account disputes the works.

## The notice

*A notice fixed above Him, written in **Greek and Latin and Hebrew**: This is the King of
the Jews.*

The three languages of the empire's east, its law, and its religion. Pilate wrote it, and
when the chief priests objected he said *what I have written, I have written.*

## What happens next

The second criminal turns on the first, says *we receive the due reward of our deeds, but
this Man has done nothing wrong*, and asks to be remembered.

*"Assuredly, I say to you, today you will be with Me in Paradise."*

> **The order of it.** He prays for their forgiveness before anyone asks, before anyone
> is sorry, and while the work is still being done to Him.
