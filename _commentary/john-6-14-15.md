The crowd draws the right conclusion and wants the wrong thing done about it.

## The sign

*When the people saw **the sign He had done**.*

Five thousand men fed from five barley loaves and two small fish, with twelve baskets of
fragments gathered afterwards. See the note on John 6:10–13.

John calls the miracles **semeia** — signs. Things that point somewhere.

## The conclusion

*They began saying, "**This really is the Prophet who is to come into the world**."*

Deuteronomy 18: *the LORD your God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from your midst… Him you
shall hear*.

**The Prophet** — a fixed expectation. The priests sent to question John the Baptist asked *are you
the Prophet?* and he said no.

## Why bread produced that conclusion

Moses gave them manna in the wilderness.

The crowd have just watched somebody feed a multitude in a wilderness, and they name him with the
title reserved for the one who would be like Moses.

The reasoning is sound.

## What they do with it

*Jesus knew they meant to **come and seize Him and make Him king by force**.*

The Greek is **harpazein** — to snatch, to seize violently. The verb used of a wolf catching sheep,
and of being caught up to meet the Lord.

## The plan

Coronation, by mob.

Galilee was the region where revolts started. A prophet who could feed an army is a supply chain.

## What He does

*So He **withdrew again to the mountain, alone**.*

The Greek is **anechoresen** — withdrew, retreated.

**Palin** — again. He had already gone up the mountain once in this chapter, before the feeding.

**Autos monos** — He Himself alone.

## What Matthew adds

*Immediately Jesus **made His disciples get into the boat** and go before Him to the other side, while
He sent the multitudes away.*

The Greek is **enankasen** — compelled, forced.

He has to make the twelve leave. Mark records the same verb.

The disciples did not want to go, which suggests what they thought of the crown.

## What He does on the mountain

*And when He had sent the multitudes away, **He went up on the mountain by Himself to pray**.*

The night ends with Him walking on the water toward a boat in a storm.

## What the crowd does next

Finds Him on the other side the next morning.

*"Rabbi, when did You come here?"*

*"Most assuredly, I say to you, **you seek Me, not because you saw the signs, but because you ate of
the loaves and were filled**."*

He tells them their motive to their faces, and then the bread discourse begins.

## Where it ends

*From that time on, **many of His disciples turned back and no longer walked with Him**.*

*"Do you want to leave too?"* See the note on John 6:66–71.

The chapter that opens with a crowd trying to crown Him ends with almost all of them gone.

## What He says about the crown later

*"**My kingdom is not of this world**. If My kingdom were of this world, My servants would fight."*

Said to Pilate, who had the authority to give Him one.

## The one crown He takes

*And the soldiers twisted a crown of thorns and put it on His head.*

*And they put a purple robe on Him, and said, "Hail, King of the Jews!"*

The only coronation recorded is a mockery, and John writes the title over the cross: **JESUS OF
NAZARETH, THE KING OF THE JEWS**.

> **The verb.** **Harpazein** — to seize by force. They reached the right conclusion about who He
> was and moved to make it happen, and He went up a mountain by Himself.
