The ninth plague is the strangest of them, and the last one before the death of
the firstborn.

## Darkness that could be touched

The Hebrew phrase is unusual — a darkness that may be *felt*, groped at. Not
night. Something with substance to it.

For three days *no one could see anyone else, and no one moved from where he was*.
Egypt stops. Nobody travels, nobody works, nobody visits. A whole civilisation
sits still in the dark.

## The separation

*But all the Israelites had light where they lived.*

The plagues have been dividing Egypt from Goshen for several chapters now — the
flies, the livestock, the hail — and the text keeps saying so plainly. Here the
division is at its most visual: one people cannot see their hand, and a few miles
away the lamps are on.

## Why this plague, and why now

Egypt's chief deity was the sun. The plague before this stripped the land bare;
this one puts out the god. And it comes immediately before the night when the
firstborn dies and Israel walks out — a rehearsal in the dark for what is
coming.

> **The last exchange.** Pharaoh tells Moses to get out and never show his face
> again, and Moses agrees: *you have spoken well; I will never see your face
> again.* It is the end of the negotiations that ran through the whole cycle —
> the compromises about who could go and what they could take are over, and the
> next thing in the book is the Passover.
