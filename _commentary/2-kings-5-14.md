A general with an army behind him ends up standing in a muddy river doing
something undignified seven times, and comes out with the skin of a child.

## What he had brought

Naaman arrived with horses, chariots, ten talents of silver, six thousand shekels
of gold and ten changes of clothes — a fortune, and a letter from his king.

Elisha did not even come outside. He sent a servant with a message: go and wash
in the Jordan seven times.

Naaman's fury is recorded in detail. He had expected a ceremony. He had better
rivers at home — and he names them, Abana and Pharpar, clean water against
Jordan's brown.

## What actually healed him

The Illumination puts it plainly: *it was not his greatness that healed him, nor
his gold, nor the rivers he preferred. It was the goodness of God, freely given,
and received at last like a child.*

Everything he brought was useless. The one thing required was to go down into the
water he thought beneath him, the full seven times, and come up.

## "Clean and fresh as the flesh of a little child"

The Hebrew is exactly that — his flesh returned like the flesh of a small boy.
Not merely cured. Made new.

There is a nice detail in who talked him into it. His own servants — the people
with no standing at all — reason with him: if the prophet had asked something
great, you would have done it. Why not this?

## What he had to do

Go down into a muddy river seven times, in front of his own retinue, having arrived with
horses, chariots, ten talents of silver, six thousand shekels of gold and ten changes of
clothing.

None of it was wanted. The instruction was free, humiliating, and delivered by a servant at
the door — Elisha did not even come out.

*For **by grace you have been saved through faith**, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift
of God, **not of works, lest anyone should boast**.*

## What he had wanted instead

*"**Indeed, I said to myself, He will surely come out to me, and stand and call on the name of
the LORD his God, and wave his hand over the place**, and heal the leprosy."*

He had scripted the ceremony in advance, and the objection is that the real thing was too
plain.

*"Are not the Abanah and the Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of
Israel?"*

## Whose idea it was that he go at all

A captured Israelite girl, working as a slave in his house, who told his wife where to send
him.

## What the flesh came back as

*And **his flesh was restored like the flesh of a little child**, and he was clean.*

*"**Most assuredly, I say to you, unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of
God.**"… "How can a man be born when he is old?"*

Naaman, a grown man and a general, comes up out of the water with the skin of a child.

## Who else pointed at this

*"And many lepers were in Israel in the time of Elisha the prophet, **and none of them was
cleansed except Naaman the Syrian**."*

Jesus, in Nazareth, in the sermon that ended with them trying to throw Him off a cliff. See
the note on Luke 4:16–21.

> treasure, and Elisha refuses every coin of it. The healing was free at both
> ends: nothing bought it, and nothing was accepted for it. Gehazi chasing after
> the money in the next paragraph, and catching the leprosy, is the chapter's own
