Paul asks them to copy him, and then describes the people he is warning them about.

## The request

*Brothers, all of you together, **follow the way I have walked**.*

The Greek is **summimetai** — joint-imitators. Paul appears to have coined it.

He says the same elsewhere: *imitate me, just as I also imitate Christ.*

It sounds immodest until you read what he has just written about himself — everything he
had counted as gain, counted as loss, and *not that I have already attained*. See the note
on Philippians 3:2–3.

## The second half

*And **keep your eye on those who walk by the pattern you have seen in us**.*

The Greek for *pattern* is **tupos** — a stamp, a mould, the impression a die leaves.

Not only him. Anyone walking the same way.

## The warning

*For there are **many who walk otherwise**.*

## The frequency

*Of whom **I have told you often**.*

He had said it before, in person, more than once.

## The manner

*And now tell you **with tears running down**.*

The Greek is *weeping*.

Paul cries in three places in his letters and speeches: over these people, over the
Ephesian elders, and in the letter to Corinth *out of much affliction and anguish of heart
I wrote to you, with many tears*.

He is describing opponents and weeping while he does it.

## The name he gives them

*They are **enemies of the cross of Christ**.*

Not enemies of God, or of the church, or of him. Of the cross.

## The four descriptions

*The end of that road is **ruin**.*

The Greek is **apoleia** — destruction.

*Their **god is their appetite**.*

The Greek is **koilia** — the belly.

*Their **glory is in what should shame them**.*

*Their **whole mind is fixed on the earth**.*

The Greek is *who mind earthly things* — **ta epigeia phronountes**.

## Who they are

Paul does not identify them. The letter has already warned about *the dogs… the
knife-cutters* in the same chapter, which points at the circumcision party.

The four descriptions here read more like indulgence than legalism, and readers have
divided over whether these are the same people.

Paul does not say.

## The contrast

The very next sentence: *for **our citizenship is registered in heaven**. And it is from
there that we are watching eagerly for the Saviour.* See the note on
Philippians 3:20–21.

*Their whole mind is fixed on the earth* against *our citizenship is in heaven*. The two
verses are built as a pair.

## Where the paragraph ends

*Who will **change these lowly bodies** of ours so that they are **formed like His own
glorious body**.*

The belly-god and the transformed body, in consecutive verses.

## What he says immediately after

*Therefore, my beloved and longed-for brethren, **my joy and crown**, so stand fast in the
Lord, beloved.*

He calls them his crown in the sentence after describing enemies in tears.

> **How the warning is delivered.** Weeping. Paul gives four descriptions, each harder
> than the last, and tells the readers he is crying while he writes them.
