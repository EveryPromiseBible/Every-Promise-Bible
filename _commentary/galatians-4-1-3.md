Paul draws a picture from a wealthy household and says it is a picture of the reader's
own past.

## The situation

*As long as **the heir is a child**, he is **no different from a slave**, though
**everything belongs to him**.*

Three clauses, and the third is the one that makes it strange.

The estate is legally his. Nothing is in doubt about the ownership.

## The word

*A **child**.*

The Greek is **nepios** — an infant, literally *non-speaking*. Paul uses it of the
immature: *when I was a child, I spoke as a child*, and of those still on milk.

The writer of Hebrews uses the same word of readers who should be teaching by now. See the
note on Hebrews 5:11–12.

## The comparison

*No different from a slave.*

Not worse than a slave and not better. In terms of what he can actually do with the
property, indistinguishable.

## The supervision

*He is **under guardians and stewards**.*

Two words: **epitropous**, guardians of the person, and **oikonomous**, managers of the
property.

Roman law had exactly this arrangement. A minor heir had both, and neither answered to
him.

## The end date

*Until **the day his father has appointed**.*

The Greek is **prothesmias** — a set term, a legally fixed date.

The father sets it. Not the boy, not the guardians, and not the calendar.

## The application

*And **so it was with us**.*

Paul turns the picture on his readers.

## What they were under

*When we were children, we were **held as slaves under the elemental things of the
world**.*

The Greek is **stoicheia tou kosmou** — the elements, the rudiments. It is the word for
the letters of the alphabet, and for the basic constituents of a thing.

Paul uses it again at the end of the chapter, about the Galatians turning back: *how is it
that you turn again to **the weak and beggarly elements**, to which you desire again to be
in bondage? **You observe days and months and seasons and years**.*

## What comes next

*But **when the fullness of the time had come, God sent forth His Son**, born of a woman,
born under the law, **to redeem those who were under the law**, that we might **receive the
adoption as sons**.*

The appointed day arrived. See the note on Galatians 4:4–7.

## The word for adoption

**Huiothesia** — from **huios**, son, and **tithemi**, to place. The placing of a son.

In Roman law it was a legal act that transferred a person into a family with full
inheritance rights, and it could not be undone.

## The proof

*And because you are sons, **God has sent forth the Spirit of His Son into your hearts,
crying out, "Abba, Father!"**"*

Paul says the Spirit is sent **because** they are sons, not in order to make them so.

## Where the chapter lands

*Therefore you are **no longer a slave but a son**, and if a son, **then an heir of God
through Christ**.*

## What the illustration does not say

That the child was mistreated, or that the guardians were cruel, or that the estate was
ever at risk.

The whole difference in the picture is the date the father set.

> **What was always true and what changed.** The estate belonged to him the entire time.
> What changed is that he came of age — and Paul says the arrangement before that was
> indistinguishable from slavery.
