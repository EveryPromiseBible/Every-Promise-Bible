God describes what a ruined country is going to look like, and the order of
events is the point.

## What comes first

*On the day I cleanse you from all your sins, I will settle people in your towns
again, and the ruins will be rebuilt.*

The cleansing is stated before the rebuilding, and the rebuilding is dated from
it: **on the day** I cleanse you.

The chapter has already said how — *I will sprinkle clean water on you… I will
give you a new heart and put a new spirit in you.* The restoration of the land
follows the restoration of the people, not the other way round.

## The comparison the neighbours make

*People will say, "This land that was ruined has become like the garden of Eden."*

The observation is put in the mouths of outsiders. God does not describe the
country that way Himself; He says that is what will be said about it.

Eden is the highest comparison available in Hebrew — the garden before anything
went wrong.

## What the towns were

*Rubble, deserted and torn down*, and now *walled and full of life*. Ezekiel is
writing to people who had watched Jerusalem fall and had walked away from the
smoke.

To them, this is not poetry about an abstract future. It is their own streets.

## Why God says He does it

*Then the nations left around you will know that I, the LORD, have rebuilt what
was torn down.*

The stated audience is the surrounding nations. Earlier in the chapter He says it
plainly: this is not for Israel's sake but for the sake of His name, which they
had profaned among the nations.

## The last line

*I, the LORD, have spoken, and I will do it.*

Two verbs, and the second guarantees the first. Ezekiel ends the promise the way
Isaiah ends his — with the fact that it was said out loud by someone who finishes
what He says. See the note on Isaiah 55:10–11.

## What Eden turns out to be called

*People will say, "This land that was ruined has become **like the garden of Eden**."*

The Greek Old Testament renders the garden **paradeisos**.

*And **Jesus** said to him, "Assuredly, I say to you, **today you will be with Me in Paradise**."*

**Luke** 23, said on a cross to a man being executed beside Him who had just admitted *we receive
the due reward of our deeds*. The comparison the neighbours make about a rebuilt country is given
to a criminal, by name, on the day.

*To him who overcomes I will give to eat from **the tree of life, which is in the midst of the
paradise of God**.*

## The order the chapter keeps

*On the day I cleanse you from all your sins, I will settle people in your towns again.*

The cleansing is dated first, and the rebuilding runs from it.

*Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, **having our hearts sprinkled from
an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water**.*

**Hebrews** 10 uses the chapter's own image — *I will sprinkle clean water on you* — and puts the
drawing near on the far side of it, in that order.

*Therefore, if anyone is in **Christ**, he is **a new creation**; old things have passed away;
behold, all things have become new.*

## And who does the rebuilding

*"Then the nations left around you will know that **I, the LORD, have rebuilt what was torn
down**."*

*"**I will build My church**, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it."*

*I, the LORD, have spoken, and I will do it.*

*Being confident of this very thing, that **He who has begun a good work in you will complete it**
until the day of Jesus Christ.*

> **The date on the rebuilding.** *On the day I cleanse you.* The chapter will not let the
> restored country come first — and the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the garden the
> neighbours compare it to is the word Jesus used on a cross, to a criminal, about that afternoon.
