Four sentences, and the Holy Spirit is named in three of them — unusually dense
even for Luke, who mentions the Spirit more than the other Gospel writers
combined.

## The three movements

- the Spirit **was on him**
- it had been **shown to him** by the Spirit that he would not see death until
  he had seen the Messiah
- **led by the Spirit**, he came into the temple

A standing presence, a specific word, and then a prompting on a particular
morning. All three are needed for the scene to happen, and Luke lists them in
order.

## The timing

*Just as the parents carried the child Jesus in.* Simeon walks into the temple
courts at the moment one poor family arrives with a baby to do what the law
required — one of many families doing the same thing that week.

Nothing marked them out. Luke gives no star, no crowd, no announcement. An old
man crosses a courtyard and takes a child out of a stranger's arms.

## What he had been promised, exactly

Not that he would be spared death. That he *would not see death until* — a
deferral, not an exemption. The promise had an end built into it, and Simeon
plainly knew it: the first thing he says after this is that his master can now
let him go in peace.

> **What he was waiting for.** *The comfort of Israel* — the Greek is
> *paraklesis*, from the same root as the name Jesus gives the Spirit in John,
> the one called alongside. Simeon was waiting for the consolation of his people
> and was carrying it in his arms before he finished the sentence.
