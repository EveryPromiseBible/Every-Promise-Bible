The psalm opens by telling a particular group of people to speak.

## The reason

*Give thanks to the LORD, **for He is good. His steadfast love never runs out**.*

**Chesed** — the covenant word, loyal love.

*Never runs out* is the Hebrew *endures forever*, the refrain of Psalm 136, said
twenty-six times.

## Who is instructed

*Let **the rescued ones** say it out loud, **the ones He bought back**.*

The Hebrew is *the redeemed of the LORD* — **ga'al**, the kinsman-redeemer's word, the
relative whose duty was to buy back what a family member had lost.

The instruction is not addressed to everybody. It is for people who have been got out
of something.

## "Say it out loud"

The Hebrew is simply *let them say so*.

The psalm's entire method rests on this line: what follows is four accounts of rescue,
and the reader is told at the top to be one of the voices.

## Where they came from

*Gathered from the countries, **from sunrise and sunset, from the far north and the
sea in the south**.*

Four directions, and the phrasing is not the usual compass list — east and west by the
sun, and then north and the sea.

They are being brought back from everywhere they were scattered to.

## What the psalm does next

Four stories, each with the same structure:

- **the wanderers** — lost in a desert, hungry and thirsty, no city to live in
- **the prisoners** — in darkness and irons, because they rebelled
- **the sick** — fools, afflicted because of their transgressions, near the gates of
  death
- **the sailors** — in a storm, with every skill swallowed up. See the note on
  Psalms 107:25–27.

And each one gets the same two refrains: *then they cried out to the LORD in their
trouble, and He delivered them*, and *oh, that men would give thanks to the LORD for
His goodness.*

## What the four have in common

Two of the four are explicitly their own fault — the prisoners *rebelled against the
words of God*, and the sick are *fools, because of their transgression*.

The psalm does not sort them. All four cry, and all four are brought out.

## Where it is placed

Psalm 107 opens Book Five of the Psalter, straight after the end of Book Four, and it
reads as a homecoming song for people back from exile.

## What the redeemed are told to do

*Let **the redeemed of the LORD say so**, whom He has redeemed from the hand of the enemy.*

Not feel it. Say it.

*For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, and **with the mouth confession is made
unto salvation**.*

*And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb and **by the word of their testimony**.*

## Who is gathered

*And gathered out of the lands, **from the east and from the west, from the north and from the
south**.*

Four directions. The psalm is about exiles coming home, and the rest of it is four case
studies — the lost in the desert, the prisoners, the sick, and men in a storm at sea. Each ends
with the same refrain: *then they cried out to the LORD in their trouble, and He saved them.*

*"And they will come **from the east and the west, from the north and the south**, and sit down
in the kingdom of God."*

**Jesus** uses the same four directions, and the people arriving are not the ones expected.

## The word

**Goel** — redeemed. Bought back by a kinsman with the right and the willingness to do it.

*In Him we have **redemption through His blood**.*

> **The last line.** *Whoever is wise will observe these things, and **they will
> understand the lovingkindness of the LORD**.* The psalm says the way to understand
> **chesed** is to look at four rescues and notice what they have in common.
