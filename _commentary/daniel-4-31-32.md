He is mid-sentence about his own greatness when the sentence is answered.

## What he was saying

*"Is this not great Babylon, that **I have built** for a royal dwelling **by my mighty power** and for
**the honour of my majesty**?"*

Three possessives in one line.

## The timing

*The words were **still on his lips** when a voice fell from heaven.*

The Aramaic is **od millta befum malka** — the word yet in the king's mouth.

## The delay before it

A year.

Daniel interpreted the dream twelve months earlier and told him: *break off your sins by
righteousness*. See the note on Daniel 4:24–25.

Nothing happened for twelve months. The narrative records the gap.

## The address

*"**To you it is spoken, King Nebuchadnezzar**. The kingdom has departed from you."*

The Aramaic is **adat minnak** — has passed away from you.

The perfect tense. Not *will depart*.

## The sentence

*"You will be **driven away from among men**, and your home will be **with the beasts of the field**.
You will **eat grass like an ox**."*

## What the condition is

Boanthropy — the delusion of being an ox — is a recognised, if rare, condition. The text describes
the symptoms without naming a cause.

His hair grows like eagles' feathers, his nails like birds' claws.

## The duration

*"And **seven times will pass over you**."*

The Aramaic is **shiv'ah iddanin**. **Iddan** — a period, a set time.

Usually read as seven years. The text does not specify the unit.

## The purpose clause

*"**Until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdoms of men and gives them to whomever He
will**."*

The Aramaic is **ad di-tinda** — until you know.

## What the chapter is

*I, Nebuchadnezzar…* — the account is written in the first person, by the king, as a public
proclamation to all peoples.

He published it himself. The document is a pagan emperor's circular describing his own insanity.

## What he had already been told

*"You are that head of gold."* And *the God of heaven has given you the kingdom, the power, the
strength, and the glory*. See the note on Daniel 2:36–38.

The sentence in chapter 4 is the same clause enforced: God *gives them to whomever He will*.

## The stump

*"**Leave the stump and roots in the earth, bound with a band of iron and bronze**."* See the note on
Daniel 4:13–14.

The tree comes down. The root is deliberately left.

## Where it goes

*And when those days had passed, **I lifted my eyes to heaven, and my understanding returned to
me**.* See the note on Daniel 4:34–35.

The first thing the restored mind does is look up. The second is bless the Most High.

## The last line of the chapter

*And those who walk in pride He is able to put down.*

Written by a man who had been on grass.

> **The gap.** A year between the warning and the voice. Chapter 4 records both, and puts the
> boast and the answer in the same sentence.
