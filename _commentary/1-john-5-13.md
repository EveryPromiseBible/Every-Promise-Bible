John states, near the end, why he wrote the letter.

## The purpose statement

*I have written all this to you… **so that you can know**.*

The Greek is **hina eidete** — a purpose clause.

**Oida**, not **ginosko**. The knowing that is settled rather than the knowing that is
acquired by process.

## Who it is for

*To you **who trust the name of the Son of God**.*

Not to the uncertain, and not to the outsider. The letter is addressed to believers, and the
stated aim is that they should know what they have.

Somebody was telling them they might not. The letter has been fighting it for five chapters.

## What they are to know

*That **the life that never ends is yours**.*

The Greek is **zoen echete aionion** — you *have* eternal life. Present tense.

Not will have. Not may have if you finish well.

**Zoe** is John's word — the life itself, as opposed to **bios**, the span or livelihood.

## The verse in front of it

*He who has the Son has life; he who does not have the Son of God does not have life.*

Two sentences with no third option, and the possession of the life is identical to the
possession of the Son.

## The second purpose

*And so that you **go on trusting** the name of the Son of God.*

The Greek is a present subjunctive — continue believing.

Assurance is given as the fuel for the continuing, not as a reward at the end of it.

## The parallel in the Gospel

*These are written **that you may believe** that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and
that believing you may have life in His name.* See the note on John 20:30–31.

The Gospel is written that they may believe. The letter is written that those who believe
may know.

## What comes immediately after

*Now this is the **confidence** that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to
His will, He hears us.*

The Greek is **parresia** — freedom of speech, the boldness of somebody with the right to
be there.

Knowing comes first in verse 13, and the boldness in prayer is built on it in verse 14.

## The three certainties John ends on

*We know that whoever is born of God does not sin.*

*We know that we are of God.*

*We know that the Son of God has come, and has given us an understanding.*

Three times, **oidamen** — we know. Plus the *know* of verse 13, four in the closing
paragraphs.

## The tests through the letter

John has given them plenty: love for the brothers, keeping His commandments, confessing that
Jesus has come in the flesh.

He is not writing to make them anxious about the tests. He says so here, and the sentence
he chooses is *that you may know*.

## What the letter opened with

*That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes,
which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled… **that your joy may be full**.*

Two purpose statements bracket the letter, and both are about the reader's certainty.

> **What he wrote it for.** Not so they would try harder. *That you may know that you have
> eternal life* — present tense, addressed to people who already believe.
