Two men go home from the same parade, and the writer records both journeys in one
sentence.

## Mordecai goes back to work

*Afterward **Mordecai returned to the king's gate**.*

Robe off, horse gone, back to his seat. Nothing is recorded of what he said or
felt.

He had not asked for the parade, had not campaigned for it, and does not appear
to have adjusted his life because of it. He goes back to the gate.

## Haman goes home

*But Haman hurried home, **his head covered**, mourning and shaken.*

Covering the head was the posture of grief and shame. In 2 Samuel it is what
David does going up the Mount of Olives, and what the mourners do for the dead.

He was leading a parade an hour ago.

## The advisers change their tune

The same wife and the same friends who suggested the gallows the previous evening
now say this:

*"If Mordecai, before whom you have **begun to fall**, is **one of the Jewish
people**, you will not overcome him. You will surely fall before him."*

They give a reason, and the reason is his nation. Pagan advisers in a Persian
house state the principle the book never states outright — that there is
something about this people you cannot win against.

They cannot name what they are describing. They can see it working.

## "Begun to fall"

The Hebrew has the verb twice: you have *begun to fall* before him, you will
*surely fall*. Once it starts, they say, it does not stop.

Nothing has happened yet except a robe and a horse. There has been no trial, no
accusation, and Haman is still the second man in the empire.

They are reading the direction of things off one afternoon, and they are right.

## The interruption

*They were still speaking when the king's messengers arrived, **hurrying Haman
away** to the banquet Esther had prepared.*

He does not get to finish the conversation. He is taken, mid-sentence, to the
room where he will be exposed and sentenced within the hour.

> **The pace of the chapter.** Everything in Esther 6 happens in a single day, and
> the verbs are all urgent — *hurry*, *hastened*, *hurrying away*. Eleven months of
> waiting under a death decree, and then one day in which the whole thing turns
> over.
