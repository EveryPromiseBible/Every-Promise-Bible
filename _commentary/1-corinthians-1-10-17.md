title: "Was Paul crucified for you?"

The first problem in the letter is not immorality or lawsuits or the Lord's Supper. It is fan
clubs — and Paul settles it with a question nobody can answer twice.

## Tears, and the word for mending them

*That there be no **splits** among you, but that you be **knit together** in the same mind.*

**Schismata** — tears, rips in cloth. And the cure is **katertismenoi**, from **katartizo**: the
word a physician used for setting a broken bone and a fisherman used for mending nets. Matthew uses
it of James and John *mending their nets* the day they were called.

Paul does not ask for uniformity of opinion. He asks for the thing you do with a break — you set
it, and it knits, and it holds weight again.

## The four parties, and the fourth is the worst

*"I belong to Paul," or "I belong to Apollos," or "I belong to Cephas," or **"I belong to Christ."***

The fourth one is on the list of things being rebuked. That is worth sitting with. A party can form
around His name — the group that is right, that has the pure position, that does not go in for
personalities — and the moment His name is used to be superior to a brother it has become a faction
like the others.

## The question that ends it

*Is Christ divided? **Was Paul crucified for you?** Or were you baptized into the name of Paul?*

There it is. The test of whose you are is not who taught you, who baptised you, who first made the
gospel make sense to you. It is who died for you.

Every one of these teachers was good. Apollos was eloquent and mighty in the Scriptures; Peter had
walked with Him; Paul had written half the New Testament. And not one of them had a cross with a
Corinthian's name on it.

There is only one name in that category, and it is not on the list of parties. It is the answer to
them.

## Emptied

*For Christ did not send me to baptize but to announce the good news — and not with clever,
wise-sounding words, **or the cross of Christ would be emptied of its power**.*

**Kenothe** — hollowed out, made void. The same root as Philippians 2, where He *emptied* Himself.

The cross does not lose its power to opposition. Paul says it can lose its power to decoration —
to preaching so accomplished that people leave admiring the preacher. A sermon can be so good that
nobody sees Him.

> **Nobody has your name on a cross but Him.** Whoever led you to Christ, whoever pastors you now,
> whoever you would name if asked where your faith came from: they were servants through whom you
> believed, and Paul's own verdict on them is that neither the planter nor the waterer is anything.
> One Man was crucified for you. Everything you have belongs to Him and comes free.
