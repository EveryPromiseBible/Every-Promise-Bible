They ask for credentials and He gives them one they cannot use.

## What they are asking about

He has just cleared the temple — the money changers, the tables, the doves, the whip
of cords.

*"**What sign will You show us** to prove You have the right to do all this?"*

A fair question by their lights. Somebody disrupting temple business ought to be able
to say who authorised it.

## The answer

*"**Tear this temple down, and in three days I will raise it up again.**"*

He offers a sign that cannot be produced on demand, and can only be verified after
the fact.

The Greek verb is imperative — *destroy it* — which is not an instruction so much as
a condition. Do that, and watch.

## Their arithmetic

*"It has taken **forty-six years** to build this temple, and You are going to raise it
up in three days?"*

Herod began the reconstruction around 20 BC, and work on it continued for decades —
it was finally finished in the AD 60s, a few years before the Romans destroyed it.

Their figure dates this scene to about AD 27.

## What He meant

*But **the temple He was speaking about was His body**.*

John supplies the explanation, and he is the only one who does.

The Greek for temple here is **naos** — the sanctuary, the inner house, not the whole
complex.

Paul uses the same word of believers: *do you not know that you are the temple of
God?* See the notes on 1 Corinthians 3:16–17 and 1 Corinthians 6:18–20.

## When they understood

*So **when He was raised from the dead**, His disciples remembered that He had said
this, **and they believed** the Scripture and the word Jesus had spoken.*

Not at the time. Years later.

John says it about several things in this Gospel — *these things His disciples did
not understand at first; but when Jesus was glorified, then they remembered.*

They were in the room, heard it, and it meant nothing until Sunday morning.

## Who else remembered

The chief priests did. At the trial: *we heard Him say, "I will destroy this temple
made with hands, and within three days I will build another made without hands."*

And at the cross, passers-by shook their heads: *You who destroy the temple and build
it in three days, save Yourself.*

His enemies quoted it back at Him for three years. His friends worked it out
afterwards.

And after the burial the chief priests went to Pilate: *we remember, while He was
still alive, how that deceiver said, "**After three days I will rise**."* They asked
for a guard on the tomb.

They took it more literally than the disciples did.

> **What the sign turned out to be.** He was asked for authority to clear a building
> and pointed at His own body. The one He was standing in came down forty years
> later, and has not been rebuilt.
