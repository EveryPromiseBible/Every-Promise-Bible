A proverb about interest that turns out to be about who ends up holding the money.

## The line

*Whoever **swells his wealth by interest and gain gathers it for one who is kind to
the poor**.*

He is not stopped, and he is not impoverished. He goes on swelling the pile.

The proverb simply tells him whose pile it is.

## What the law said about interest

*You shall not lend him your money for interest, nor lend him your food at a
profit* — Leviticus 25, about a brother who has become poor.

Taking interest from the destitute was the specific practice the law forbade, and
it is the practice in view here.

## Who receives it

*One who is **kind to the poor**.*

The Hebrew is the one who shows favour, who is gracious to the poor — the root of
**chen**, grace.

The man who squeezed the poor for interest is described as a collector working on
behalf of a man who gives to them.

## The same idea elsewhere

*The wealth of the sinner is stored up for the righteous* — Proverbs 13:22.

*To the one who misses the mark, God gives the task of gathering and piling up,
only to hand it over to the one who pleases God* — Ecclesiastes 2:26. See the note
on Ecclesiastes 2:24–26.

Three places, same claim. The gathering and the keeping are two different jobs.

## The prayer that offends

*If a man **turns his ear from hearing the law**, even his prayer becomes an
offence.*

The Hebrew is that his prayer is an abomination. He is still praying — the proverb
does not say he stops.

## The pit

*Whoever leads the upright down an evil road **falls into his own pit**, but the
**blameless will inherit good**.*

The pit is his. He dug it for somebody else.

## The rich man's eyes

*The rich man is **wise in his own eyes**, but a poor man with sense **sees right
through him**.*

The Hebrew is that the poor man searches him out. Money buys the appearance of
judgement, and it does not fool a discerning man with nothing.

## And the last one

*Whoever **hides** his sins will not prosper, but whoever **confesses and forsakes**
them will find mercy.*

Two verbs on each side, and the outcome named at the end is mercy.

## The verse the chapter ends on

*Whoever **hides** his sins will not prosper, but whoever **confesses and forsakes** them will
find mercy.*

*If we confess our sins, **He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us
from all unrighteousness**.*

**John** keeps the proverb's verb — **homologomen**, to say the same thing — and adds the two
adjectives it could not: *faithful*, and *just*. Mercy in Proverbs is found; in 1 John it is
owed, because somebody has already paid.

*My little children, these things I write to you, so that you may not sin. And if anyone
sins, **we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous**.*

## And the prayer that was an offence

*If a man turns his ear from hearing the law, **even his prayer becomes an offence**.*

*"God, I thank You that I am not like other men… I fast twice a week, I give tithes of all that
I possess."*

*"God, be merciful to me a sinner!"… "**I tell you, this man went down to his house justified
rather than the other**."*

**Luke** puts both prayers in one parable. The man who kept the law recited it and went home
unheard; the man who confessed went home justified — which is the proverb's own arithmetic,
stated by **Jesus** with the two men named.

## Who is kind to the poor

*Whoever swells his wealth by interest gathers it **for one who is kind to the poor**.*

**Chonen dallim** — the one who is gracious to the poor, from **chen**.

*For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes
He became poor.*

> **What connects the chapter.** Hiding. The man hides his sins, the wealthy man
> hides behind his own good opinion, the people hide when the wicked rise. And the
> one line about not hiding ends in mercy.
