The saddest of the twelve, and it describes a man who could have had rest and
chose a load instead.

## The blessing

*"Issachar is a **strong donkey**, resting among the sheepfolds. When he sees how
good his resting place is and how pleasant the land, **he will bend his shoulder
to the load and submit to hard labour**."*

Read the logic of it. He sees the rest is good. He sees the land is pleasant.

And that is the reason he takes up the burden — the Hebrew is that he becomes a
servant to forced labour, a slave gang.

He had the rest in front of him and put his shoulder under a load.

## The name

**Issachar** means *there is hire*, or *he brings wages*. Leah named him that.

A man whose name means wages, ending up in forced labour.

## The ground

Issachar's portion was the Jezreel valley — some of the best farmland in the whole
country, which is what makes the *pleasant land* line so exact.

And in that valley sits a town called **Nain**, which means pleasant.

## What happened at that gate

*As He came near the town gate, a dead man was being carried out — the only son
his mother had, and she was a widow.*

A funeral procession, in the tribe whose blessing is a donkey between two burdens.
Two dead men in one town — the son on the frame, and the mother's whole future
with him.

He touched the frame, the bearers stood still, and He said *young man, I say to
you, get up.*

## What He did not ask for

*And Jesus **gave him back to his mother**.*

Nothing is requested. No follow, no serve, no condition. He raises the boy and
hands him over.

Issachar submitted to hard labour to keep a pleasant land. In the pleasant town in
Issachar's own valley, a man is given his life back and is asked for nothing at
all. See the note on Luke 7:11–17.

## The two burdens

The older rendering has him *couching down between two burdens* — the two packs a
donkey carries, one on each side.

Whatever the Hebrew word is exactly, the picture is a strong animal lying down
under weight it did not have to carry.

> **What the tribe was later known for.** *The sons of Issachar, who had
> understanding of the times, to know what Israel ought to do.* Two hundred chiefs
> of them came to David at Hebron. The tribe that bowed its shoulder to a load
> became the tribe that could read a season.
