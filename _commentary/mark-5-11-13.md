Three verses, and the striking thing is who is doing the requesting.

## The begging

*The spirits begged Him.* Mark uses the verb twice in this scene — earlier they
had begged not to be sent out of the region.

Whatever they are, they cannot move without permission. The whole exchange is
them asking and Him deciding.

## What the request reveals

*Send us into the pigs. Let us go into them.*

They want somewhere to be. The parallel account in Luke has them begging not to
be sent into the abyss.

It is the same thing the returning spirit is looking for in Matthew 12 —
somewhere to settle, a house to occupy. See the note on Matthew 12:43–45.

## "He let them go"

Two words, and Mark does not explain them. The permission is granted without a
recorded reason.

Readers have argued about the pigs for centuries — whose they were, what the loss
meant, why Jesus allowed it. Mark says nothing about any of that.

What he does record is the count: *about two thousand of them*. It is the only
indication in the passage of the scale of what had been living in one man.

## What was in the man

Earlier in the chapter he gives his name as Legion, *for we are many* — a Roman
legion being some thousands of soldiers.

Nobody could bind him, not even with chains. He had been breaking irons and
cutting himself with stones among the tombs.

## The result

*The pigs stampeded down the steep bank into the lake and were drowned.*

The destruction the spirits had been working on the man goes into the herd, and
the man is left sitting, dressed, in his right mind.

> **What the region asks for afterwards.** Not more healings. They *begged Jesus to
> leave*, and He got into the boat. The third begging in the chapter comes from
> the townspeople, and it is the only one about which He does what is asked
> without a word. See the note on Mark 5:18–20.
