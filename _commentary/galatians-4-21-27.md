Paul takes a family story out of Genesis and says it was always about two systems.

## The opening

*Tell me, **you who want to live under the law: do you not listen to what the law itself
says?**"*

He is about to argue against law-keeping from the law itself.

## The two sons

*Abraham had **two sons, one by the slave woman and one by the free woman**.*

Ishmael and Isaac. Hagar and Sarah.

## The difference

*The son of the slave woman was **born in the ordinary way of the flesh**, and the son of
the free woman was **born through the promise**.*

Ishmael was conceived by Abram and Hagar on Sarai's suggestion, when the couple decided
the promise needed help. He was born to a man of eighty-six by ordinary means.

Isaac was born to a man of a hundred and a woman of ninety past childbearing. Hebrews says
of Abraham *as good as dead*. See the note on Hebrews 11:8–12.

Paul's line is between what a couple can manage and what only a promise produces.

## The claim

*These things **carry a deeper meaning**.*

The Greek is **allegoroumena** — spoken allegorically.

Paul is not denying the history. He treats Genesis as an account of things that happened
and as carrying a second sense.

## The identification

*The **two women are two covenants**.*

- **Hagar** — *the one from **Mount Sinai**, bearing children into slavery*
- **Sarah** — unnamed here, but *the Jerusalem above is free*

## The geography

*For this Hagar is **Mount Sinai in Arabia**, and she **answers to the Jerusalem that now
is**, and she is **in slavery along with her children**.*

The Greek for *answers to* is **sustoichei** — stands in the same column, corresponds in a
list.

Paul aligns Sinai with the current Jerusalem — the city with the functioning temple, the
priesthood and the sacrifices.

That is a startling thing to write to Jewish readers, and it is the sharpest sentence in
the letter.

## The other city

*But **the Jerusalem above is free**, and she is **the mother of us all**.*

Hebrews uses the same phrase: *you have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living
God, **the heavenly Jerusalem***. See the note on Hebrews 12:18–24.

Two mountains again: Sinai and Zion.

## The quotation

*Rejoice, **barren woman**, you who never bore a child; **break out and shout aloud**, you
who were never in labor — for **the desolate woman has many more children than she who has
a husband**.*

Isaiah 54, the chapter directly after the suffering servant. See the note on
Isaiah 54:1–3.

Paul quotes it because it is about a woman with no children being told to prepare for a
crowd — which is Sarah's situation, and which he says is the reader's.

## Which side the Galatians were choosing

They were being told to take on circumcision. Paul's argument places that on the Hagar
side of the table — the side that produced a son by ordinary means because somebody could
not wait.

## What follows

*Now we, brothers, are **children of promise, just as Isaac was**.* See the note on
Galatians 4:28–31.

> **What Paul does with the story.** Reads it as two ways of getting a son — one a couple
> can arrange and one only a promise produces — and puts the current temple city in the
> first column.
