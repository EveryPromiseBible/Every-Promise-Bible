A description of things falling apart, and then a command that runs against every
instinct the description produces.

## What the passage describes

Signs in sun, moon and stars. Nations at their wits' end. The sea roaring. People
fainting from fear and dread. *The powers that hold the heavens in place will be
shaken.*

Luke does not soften any of it. The reaction he reports is collapse — people
literally giving out from fear of what is coming.

## The instruction

*When all of this begins to happen, stand up straight and lift up your heads.*

Two postures, and both are the opposite of what a frightened crowd does. The
first word means to straighten up — used in this same Gospel of a bent woman
being made straight. The second is lifting the head that fear drives down.

Note the timing: *when all of this begins*. Not after it resolves. At the start,
while it is still getting worse.

## The reason

*Because the day of your rescue is drawing near.* The word is **apolutrosis** —
the ransom-release word, the same one in Ephesians 1:7 for being bought out of
captivity.

So the sign that terrifies everyone else is read by these people as the sound of
the rescue arriving. Same events, opposite posture, because of a different
expectation about what they mean.

## The word at the end

*"**Look up and lift up your heads, because your redemption draws near.**"*

The Greek is **apolytrosis** — the buying back of a slave or a prisoner of war, and the noun is the
one **Paul** uses of the cross.

*In Him we have **redemption through His blood**, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of
His grace.*

*Being justified freely by His grace **through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus**.*

The same noun, and here it is put in the future. What was paid for at Calvary is described as still
arriving, which is why the instruction attached to it is a posture rather than an action.

*Not only that, but we also who have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within
ourselves, eagerly waiting for the adoption, **the redemption of our body**.*

## The one instruction

*Look up.* **Anakypsate** — straighten up, unbend.

Every other reaction in the paragraph is downward: men's hearts failing them for fear, the powers
shaken, the sea roaring. The response asked for is the opposite of the room's.

*Looking for the blessed hope and **glorious appearing of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ**.*

**Titus** 2 calls it the **blessed** hope. The event that makes the nations faint is, for the people
told to look up, the thing they were waiting for.

*"**And then they will see the Son of Man coming in a cloud with power and great glory.**"*

> **On "the powers of the heavens shaken."** This is the phrase people connect
> with modern weaponry, and the words behind it are the ordinary Greek for the
> starry host and for shaking — the same phrase Isaiah 34:4 already used
> centuries earlier of judgement on a nation. It is a stock prophetic image for
> the cosmos being disturbed, not a coded reference to anything later.
