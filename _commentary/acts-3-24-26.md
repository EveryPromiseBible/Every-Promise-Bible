key: Acts 3:24–26
title: "To you first"
updated: 2026-08-09

The end of **Peter**'s second sermon, and the last word of it is a surprise.

## All the prophets

*"**Yes, and all the prophets, from Samuel and those who follow, as many as have spoken, have also
foretold these days.**"*

**Peter** does not cite them. He asserts that the whole line points one way, and **Luke** records
**Jesus** doing the same on the Emmaus road: *beginning at Moses and all the Prophets, He expounded
to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself.*

## The sons of the covenant

*"**You are sons of the prophets, and of the covenant which God made with our fathers, saying to
Abraham, 'And in your seed all the families of the earth shall be blessed.'**"*

Genesis 12, and **Peter** quotes it to a crowd in Solomon's porch as their own inheritance.

*Now to Abraham and his Seed were the promises made. He does not say, "And to seeds," as of many,
but as of one, "**And to your Seed," who is Christ**.*

**Paul** in **Galatians** 3 argues from the singular noun.

## To you first

*"**To you first, God, having raised up His Servant Jesus, sent Him to bless you.**"*

**Hymin proton** — to you first.

The word **first** implies a second, and **Peter** does not say who. **Paul** spells it out years
later — *to the Jew first and also to the Greek* — but the sequence is already in this sentence, in
the temple, weeks after Pentecost.

## Who is being addressed

The same crowd. Two sermons earlier **Peter** had said *you have taken by lawless hands, have
crucified, and put to death*, and in this one: *you denied the Holy One and the Just, and asked for
a murderer to be granted to you, and killed the Prince of life.*

And then *to you first*.

*"**Yet now, brethren, I know that you did it in ignorance, as did also your rulers.**"*

*Then Jesus said, "**Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do.**"*

**Peter** offers them the ignorance defence **Jesus** had prayed for them from the cross.

## The blessing

*"**In turning away every one of you from your iniquities.**"*

The blessing promised to Abraham, applied by **Peter**, turns out to be a change of direction rather
than a change of circumstances — and God is the subject of the verb. He sent Him *to bless you, in
turning you away*.

*Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law… **that the blessing of Abraham might come upon the
Gentiles in Christ Jesus**.*

> **The two words.** *To you first.* Said to the crowd he has just accused of killing the Prince of
> life — and the word *first* leaves the rest of the sentence open for everybody else.
