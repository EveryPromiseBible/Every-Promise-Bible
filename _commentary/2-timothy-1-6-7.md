Paul's last surviving letter, written from a Roman prison to a younger man who
appears to be losing his nerve.

## What is already there

*God **placed a gift inside you** the day I laid my hands on you.*

Past tense, and settled. Nothing in the passage asks Timothy to obtain anything.

The whole instruction assumes the gift is present and has been present for years.

## "Fan it back up into open flame"

The Greek is **anazopureo** — *ana* (again) + *zoos* (alive) + *pur* (fire). To
rekindle; to stir a fire back up.

You do not fan an ashtray. The verb takes for granted that there are coals in
there, banked and covered, still hot.

Timothy is not told to light something. He is told to stir something.

## "Was never a small, cowering thing"

*What God breathed into us was never a small, cowering thing.*

The Greek noun is **deilia** — cowardice, timidity. It appears nowhere else in
the New Testament, and it is not a neutral word; the Greeks used it as the
opposite of courage, a fault rather than a temperament.

Paul does not say Timothy is not afraid. He says where the fear did not come
from.

## Three things it was

*He gave us **power**, and He gave us **love**, and He gave us **a mind that stays
clear and steady**.*

- **power** — *dunamis*, the plain word for ability and force
- **love** — *agape*
- **a clear and steady mind** — **sophronismos**, a word about soundness of mind,
  self-command, a head that is in one piece

The third is the one that gets flattened in translation. It is built from the
words for *safe* and *mind*: a mind that is intact.

Paul lists it as something given, alongside power and love — not as a
disposition Timothy has to manufacture under pressure.

> **What Paul asks for at the end of the letter.** His cloak, the books, and
> especially the parchments. A man writing about power, love and a sound mind
> from a cell, and asking for a coat because winter is coming.
