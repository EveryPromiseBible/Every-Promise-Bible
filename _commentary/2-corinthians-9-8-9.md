One sentence with the word *all* in it four times.

## Where it sits

In the middle of a fundraising letter.

Paul is organising a collection for the poor believers in Jerusalem, and chapters 8 and 9 are
the longest treatment of giving in the New Testament.

He has just said *God loves a cheerful giver* — **hilaron**, the word behind *hilarious*.

## The claim

*And **God is able** to make every grace overflow toward you.*

The Greek is **dunatei ho Theos** — God is powerful, God has the power.

Paul does not say God will. He says God is able, which is what a man raising money for someone
else's poor needs to be sure of first.

## The overflow

**Perisseusai** — to abound, to exceed, to have more than enough.

The verb runs through both chapters. It is the word used of the twelve baskets left over.

## Every grace

*Every grace* — **pasan charin**.

**Charis** is the word for grace, and in these two chapters Paul uses it for the collection
itself, for the Macedonians' generosity, for God's gift, and for thanks.

The same noun carries the whole transaction.

## The four alls

*So that **always**, in **everything**, having **all** you need, you may have plenty over for
**every** good work.*

The Greek: **pantote pasan autarkeian en panti… eis pan ergon agathon**.

Four occurrences of *pas* in one clause. It is deliberate piling.

## The word in the middle

**Autarkeia** — sufficiency, having enough.

It is a Stoic technical term: self-sufficiency, the philosopher's ideal of needing nothing
outside himself.

Paul takes the word and attaches it to a supply that comes from somewhere else. The Stoic
generated his own; Paul's is given.

He uses the adjective of himself: *I have learned, in whatever state I am, to be content*. See
the note on Philippians 4:11–13.

## What the sufficiency is for

*That you may have **plenty over for every good work**.*

The purpose clause is not consumption. The overflow is directed.

Paul is writing to people he is asking for money, and the sentence bends back toward the giving
he is asking for.

## The quotation

*As it stands written: "**He has scattered widely, He has given to the poor; His righteousness
lasts forever**."*

Psalm 112, describing the man who fears the LORD.

The Greek is **eskorpisen** — scattered, the word for broadcasting seed by hand.

## Who the *He* is

In the psalm it is the generous man. In Paul's argument it has become ambiguous, and the next
verse makes God the supplier: *He who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food*.

## The seed

*Now may **He who supplies seed to the sower**, and bread for food, supply and multiply the seed
you have sown, and increase the fruits of your righteousness.*

Two things in the sower's hand: bread to eat, and seed to plant. The passage distinguishes them
and says both are supplied.

## Where the chapter ends

*While, through the proof of this ministry, they glorify God… **Thanks be to God for His
indescribable gift!***

The Greek is **anekdiegeto** — indescribable, beyond narration. A word Paul appears to have made
up.

A chapter about a cash collection ends there.

> **The word.** **Autarkeia** — the Stoics' self-sufficiency, the state of needing nothing from
> outside. Paul keeps the word and moves the source.
