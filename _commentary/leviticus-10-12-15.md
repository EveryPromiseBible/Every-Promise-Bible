Moses gives instructions about lunch on the worst day of Aaron's life.

## What has just happened

*Then Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, each took his censer and put fire in it, put incense
on it, and offered **profane fire before the LORD, which He had not commanded them**.*

*So fire went out from the LORD and devoured them, and they died before the LORD.*

*And Aaron held his peace.*

Their bodies have been carried out of the camp by their cousins. Aaron and his two remaining
sons have been told not to mourn publicly.

## Who is addressed

*Moses said to Aaron and his **remaining sons, Eleazar and Ithamar**.*

The word *remaining* is in the text.

## The instruction

*"**Take the grain offering that is left over**… and **eat it beside the altar**."*

Life continues. The offerings still have to be handled and the priests still have to eat.

## The grain offering

*Prepared **without yeast**, for it is **most holy**. Eat it in **a holy place**.*

The Hebrew is **qodesh qodashim**. It stays inside the sanctuary courtyard.

## The reason

*"Because it is **your share and your sons' share** of the offerings made by fire to the
LORD; **this is what I was commanded**."*

Moses does not argue for it. He reports the command.

## The other portion

*"But the **breast that was waved and the thigh that was presented you may eat in a clean
place**."*

The peace offering's portions. See the note on Leviticus 7:28–34.

## The difference

The grain offering is eaten in a **holy** place. The peace offering portions are eaten in a
**clean** place.

Two different categories. Most holy things stay in the courtyard; the peace offering's share
can go anywhere ritually clean — a house, a tent, a table.

## Who eats them

*You and your sons **and daughters**.*

The grain offering is for the males of the priesthood. The peace offering's share is for the
whole household.

Leviticus is precise about it, and the peace offering is the one that widens.

## What they do with it first

*The thigh that was presented and the breast that was waved, together with the fat portions,
are to be **lifted up as a wave offering** before the LORD.*

The Hebrew is **tenuphah** — a waving, a to-and-fro motion. And **terumah**, a heaving,
lifted up.

The portions are moved in front of God before anybody takes them home. Presented, and then
handed back.

## The term

*They belong to you and your children as **a lasting share**.*

The Hebrew is **choq olam** — a perpetual due.

## The chapter's other half

Right after this, Moses discovers the goat of the sin offering has been burned rather than
eaten, and is angry with Eleazar and Ithamar.

Aaron answers: *"Look, this day they have offered their sin offering and their burnt offering
before the LORD, and such things have befallen me! **If I had eaten the sin offering today,
would it have been accepted in the sight of the LORD?**"*

*So when Moses heard that, he was content.*

A father whose two sons died that morning explains why he could not eat, and Moses accepts
it.

## What sits between the two halves

The instructions about the priests' portion, and the sentence about a clean place where the
daughters can eat too.

> **The two locations.** Most holy things stay in the courtyard. The peace offering's share
> goes home — to a clean place, with the daughters included.
