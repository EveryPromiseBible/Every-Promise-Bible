The first place in Ecclesiastes where the Preacher stops complaining and says
something plain.

## Where he has been

Two chapters of trying everything. Wisdom, pleasure, wine, houses, vineyards,
gardens, pools, servants, herds, silver and gold, singers, every delight — *whatever
my eyes desired I did not keep from them.*

And then: *I hated life… I hated all my labour, because I must leave it to the man
who comes after me, and who knows whether he will be wise or a fool?*

## The turn

*There is nothing better for a person than to **eat and drink and find joy in his
work**. And this, I saw, is **from the hand of God**.*

Bread, a drink, and satisfaction in the day's work. That is the whole list.

And the second sentence is what makes it more than resignation — he names where it
comes from. Ordinary contentment is described as a gift handed over.

## "Apart from Him"

*For **apart from Him, who can eat, who can find any real enjoyment?**"*

Not who can afford to eat. Who can eat and get anything out of it.

He has just proved by experiment that a man can own everything in that list and
enjoy none of it.

## The three given

*To the one who **pleases Him**, God gives **wisdom and knowledge and joy**.*

None of the three is money.

## And the fourth

*But to the one who misses the mark, He gives **the task of gathering and piling
up, only to hand it over** to the one who pleases God.*

The Hebrew is *to gather and to heap up*. It is described as an assignment — a
**task** given.

He does the collecting. Somebody else does the keeping.

Proverbs says the same thing twice: *the wealth of the sinner is stored up for the
righteous*, and *whoever swells his wealth by interest gathers it for one who is
kind to the poor.* See the note on Proverbs 28:7–13.

## "This too is vapour"

*This too is vapour, a chasing after the wind.*

The Hebrew **hebel** — breath, vapour, what you can see on a cold morning and
cannot hold.

He applies it to the gathering, not to the gift. The eating and drinking and joy
are *from the hand of God*; the heaping up is wind.

> **Where the book ends.** *Fear God and keep His commandments, for this is the
> whole duty of man.* Twelve chapters of a man with unlimited means testing every
> avenue, and the conclusion is two clauses long.
