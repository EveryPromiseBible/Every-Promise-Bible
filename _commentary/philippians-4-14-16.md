The warmest paragraph about money in the New Testament, and it is written by a man in prison to the
one church that had never let him go hungry.

## Fellowship in the trouble

*You did a beautiful thing in **sharing my hardship** with me.*

**Synkoinonesantes** — you had **koinonia** with my affliction. The great word for fellowship,
attached here not to a meal or a meeting but to somebody else's trouble.

They could not get into the prison. What they could do was make his hardship partly theirs, and
Paul counts that as having been there.

## Paul keeps books

*Not one church joined me in **the give and take of giving and receiving** — only you.*

**Eis logon doseos kai lempseos** — into an account of giving and receiving. It is commercial
language: a ledger with two columns, debit and credit, open between two parties.

Paul says only one church had an account open with him. He had been at this for years, planted
churches all over the Aegean, and the books show one name.

## Twice in a few weeks

*Even while I was in Thessalonica you sent something for my needs, **and then sent again**.*

Paul was in Thessalonica for something like three weeks before he was run out of town. In that
window, a poor church a hundred miles back down the road got money to him twice.

Macedonia is the same region he later describes to Corinth as giving *out of poverty right down to
the bottom of them*. These were not wealthy patrons. They were the people who had least and moved
fastest.

## Which side of the ledger he wants

*Not that I seek the gift, but I seek **the fruit that abounds to your account**.*

He keeps the accounting metaphor and turns it round. He is not chasing income. He wants the entry
on their side of the page — because in this economy the giver is the one who ends up richer, and
Paul would rather they had the fruit than that he had the money.

> **Who the famous promise was written to.** Five verses later comes *my God shall supply all your
> need according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus.* It is not a promise floating free. It is
> said to the church that had just emptied its pockets for a prisoner, and the measure named is not
> the size of what they gave — it is **His riches in glory**, which is the only supply that never
> runs low.
