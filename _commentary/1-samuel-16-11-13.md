Seven sons have passed and none of them is it, so Samuel asks a question.

## The question

*"**Are these all the sons you have?**"*

He has to ask. Nobody volunteers the information.

## The answer

*"**There's still the youngest**," Jesse said. "**But he's out in the fields, keeping the
sheep**."*

The Hebrew is **haqqaton** — the smallest, the youngest.

The *but* is Jesse's. A prophet has come to town, a sacrifice has been called, seven sons
have been paraded, and the eighth was not thought worth fetching.

He is looking after the animals — which in a family with eight sons is the job given to
the one who counts for least.

## The instruction

*"**Send for him. We won't sit down until he's here**."*

The Hebrew is *we will not sit around until he comes*.

The whole ceremony stops. Nobody eats.

## The description

*He was **ruddy and bright-eyed, with a fine face**.*

The Hebrew is *ruddy, with beautiful eyes, and good to look at*.

Which is the joke of the chapter. God has just told Samuel not to look at the outside, and
then the one He picks is the handsome one.

The point was never that appearance disqualifies. It was that it does not decide.

## The word

*And the LORD said, "**This is the one. Get up and anoint him**."*

Samuel had to be told. He had asked for the boy without knowing, and he waits for the
sentence before pouring.

## The anointing

*So Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him **right there in front of his brothers**.*

In front of the seven who were called first.

Nothing in the chapter says the family understood what it meant. Samuel had told them he
came to sacrifice.

## What came with it

*And from that day on, **the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon David and stayed**.*

The Hebrew is **tsalach** — rushed, came mightily.

**And stayed.** The next verse says the opposite of Saul: *but the Spirit of the LORD
departed from Saul.*

Two clauses, back to back, and the chapter turns on the contrast.

## What Samuel did next

*Then Samuel **went home to Ramah**.*

No instruction, no training, no schedule. He anoints a teenager and leaves.

David goes back to the sheep, and the next thing recorded of him is carrying bread and
cheese to his brothers at the front. See the note on 1 Samuel 16:20–23.

## How long the wait was

Years — through Goliath, Saul's spear, the wilderness, Ziklag, and Saul's death. Roughly
fifteen by most reckonings.

He is anointed here and crowned in 2 Samuel 5.

## What Paul says about the choosing

*God has chosen the foolish things of the world to put to shame the wise; and God has
chosen the weak things of the world to put to shame the things which are mighty.* See the
note on 1 Corinthians 1:26–31.

> eighth was in a field — and the prophet stopped the whole proceeding and refused to sit
> down until he came in.
