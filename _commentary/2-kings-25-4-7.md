The end of the kingdom of Judah, reported in four verses without comment.

## The siege

*Then **the city wall was breached**.*

Eighteen months of it. The chapter opens with Nebuchadnezzar arriving in the ninth year of
Zedekiah's reign and building siege works all round.

By the fourth month of the eleventh year *the famine had become so severe in the city that
there was no food for the people of the land*.

Lamentations describes what that meant.

## The escape

*In the night, with the Babylonians ringed around the city, **the soldiers all fled through
the gate between the two walls, by the king's garden**, and made for the Arabah.*

A postern gate at the southern end, near the pool of Siloam. Out through the garden, in the
dark, heading for the Jordan valley.

## The capture

*But the Babylonian army pursued the king and **overtook him in the plains of Jericho**, and
**all his soldiers scattered from him**.*

Jericho. Where Israel's occupation of the land began, and where its monarchy ends.

His army leaves him in the open.

## The sentence

*They captured the king and brought him to the king of Babylon **at Riblah**, where **sentence
was passed** against him.*

Riblah is in Syria, on the Orontes, about two hundred miles north — Nebuchadnezzar's forward
headquarters.

Zedekiah is walked there under guard to be tried.

## The two things

*There his **sons were killed before his eyes**; then **his own eyes were put out**.*

The order is the point, and the Babylonians knew what they were doing. The last image he
carried was arranged.

## The chains

*They **bound him in bronze chains** and took him to Babylon.*

The same phrase used of Samson in Gaza. See the note on Judges 16:21–22.

Blinded, in bronze, in a foreign city, grinding out the rest of his life.

## The two prophecies

Jeremiah had told him: *you shall not escape from his hand, but shall surely be taken and
delivered into his hand; **your eyes shall see the eyes of the king of Babylon**, he shall
speak with you face to face, and you shall go to Babylon.*

Ezekiel, in exile, had said the opposite-sounding thing: *I will bring him to Babylon, to the
land of the Chaldeans; **yet he shall not see it**, though he shall die there.*

Both were true. He met Nebuchadnezzar face to face at Riblah, and never saw Babylon.

## What Zedekiah had done

*He did evil in the sight of the LORD.* And he had asked Jeremiah for a word repeatedly, in
private, and had not acted on any of them.

Jeremiah's last offer to him: *if you surely surrender to the king of Babylon's princes, then
your soul shall live*.

He was afraid of what his own officials would say.

## What follows in the chapter

The temple burned, the palace burned, every great house burned. The bronze pillars broken up
and carried off. The walls pulled down.

And *the captain of the guard left some of the poor of the land as vinedressers and farmers*.

## The last paragraph of Kings

Thirty-seven years later, in Babylon, Jehoiachin is released from prison, given new clothes,
and *he ate bread regularly before the king all the days of his life*.

The book that ends with a burnt city ends with a man at a table.

## What was still standing

*This is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, says the
LORD: **I will put My law in their minds, and write it on their hearts**.*

Jeremiah wrote that during the siege.

> **What the Babylonians arranged.** The order of the two acts. His sons first, and then his
> eyes — so that the last thing he ever saw was chosen for him.
