Paul's first recorded sermon, and he tells the story before he makes the point.

## Where he is

The synagogue at Antioch in Pisidia, on the first missionary journey, invited to speak after
the readings: *men and brethren, if you have any word of exhortation for the people, say on*.

He starts with the exodus and works forward.

## The judges

*"And after that, for about **four hundred and fifty years**, He gave them judges, **until
Samuel the prophet**."*

The period from Joshua's death to Samuel — Othniel, Ehud, Deborah, Gideon, Jephthah, Samson,
and the rest.

The book's refrain: *in those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in
his own eyes*.

## The request

*"Then **they asked for a king**."*

Paul's phrasing puts the initiative with them, which is how Samuel records it: *now make us a
king to judge us like all the nations*.

And God's reading of it: *they have not rejected you, but **they have rejected Me**, that I
should not reign over them*.

## The first one

*"And God gave them **Saul son of Kish, a man of the tribe of Benjamin, for forty years**."*

He was what they had asked for: *from his shoulders upward he was taller than any of the
people*.

They wanted a king like the nations, and they got the best-looking man in the country.

## The removal

*"When He had **removed him**."*

The Greek is **metastesas** — set aside, deposed.

Saul's disqualification is dated to Gilgal, where he could not wait seven days for Samuel. See
the note on 1 Samuel 13:11–14.

*"You have acted foolishly… but now your kingdom shall not continue. **The LORD has sought for
Himself a man after His own heart**."*

## The successor

*"He **raised up David** to be their king."*

## The testimonial

*"**I have found David son of Jesse, a man after My own heart, who will do everything I want
done**."*

Paul is quoting, and he is quoting two places at once.

*The LORD has sought for Himself a man after His own heart* — Samuel, at Gilgal.

*I have found My servant David* — Psalm 89.

He splices them.

## What the phrase means

Not that David was better behaved. The record includes adultery, an arranged killing, and a
census that cost seventy thousand lives.

Saul's disqualifying sins look smaller: an offering made early, and sparing an enemy king and
the best of the livestock.

The difference the narrative draws is in what each man did afterwards.

Saul: *I have sinned; yet **honour me now, please, before the elders of my people***.

David: *against You, You only, have I sinned* — and Psalm 51.

## What the phrase describes

A man who wanted what God wanted.

The Hebrew is *a man according to His heart*, and the clause Paul adds spells it out: *who will
do all My will*.

## Where the sermon goes

*"**From this man's seed, according to the promise, God raised up for Israel a Saviour —
Jesus**."*

Everything before this is the run-up. Paul spends fifteen verses on the history to land on one
descendant.

## How it ends

*"Therefore let it be known to you that **through this Man is preached to you the forgiveness of
sins**; and by Him everyone who believes is justified from all things from which you could not
be justified by the law of Moses."* See the note on Acts 13:38–39.

## What happened next

*And the next Sabbath almost the whole city came together to hear the word of God.*

And then the opposition, and *we turn to the Gentiles*.

> **What the title is not.** Not a verdict on conduct. The man it was taken from lost the throne
> over an early sacrifice; the man it was given to committed adultery and arranged a killing.
