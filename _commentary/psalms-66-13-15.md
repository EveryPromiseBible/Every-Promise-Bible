---
key: Psalms 66:13–15
title: The vows my lips spoke when I was in trouble
updated: 2026-08-09
---
A man pays what he promised in a crisis, and the psalm records that he remembers what he said.

## What he is bringing

*I will come into Your house with offerings, **I will keep the promises I made to You**, the
vows my lips spoke, the vows my mouth uttered **when I was in trouble**.*

The clause is honest about when the vow was made. He is not pretending it came out of settled
devotion.

*When He slew them, then they sought Him; and they returned and sought earnestly for God.* The
psalms are not naive about this.

## What he does about it

Keeps it anyway.

*When you make a vow to God, **do not delay to pay it**… Better not to vow than to vow and not
pay.*

## What he brings

*The best of my offerings, with the smoke of rams rising up, I will offer **bulls and goats**.*

The most expensive list in the Psalter. A man who promised something in a bad week arrives with
livestock.

## What the New Testament does with bulls and goats

*For **it is not possible that the blood of bulls and goats could take away sins**.*

**Hebrews** says it flatly, in the chapter that quotes Psalm 40 — *sacrifice and offering You
did not desire, but a body You have prepared for Me*. See the note on Psalms 40:1–3.

Psalm 66's offerings were real and they were accepted, and they could not do the thing the
worshipper most needed.

## What is asked for instead

*Therefore by Him let us continually offer **the sacrifice of praise to God**, that is, the
fruit of our lips, giving thanks to His name.*

**Hebrews** keeps the vocabulary of the altar and changes what is on it — and the psalm's own
next section is exactly that: *come and hear, all you who fear God, and I will declare what He
has done for my soul.*

## The verse that follows

*If I regard iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear. **But certainly God has heard me**.*

The psalm raises the disqualification and then reports that it did not apply.

> **What he does not do.** Pretend the vow was made calmly. *The vows my lips spoke when I was
> in trouble* — and he brings the bulls anyway.
