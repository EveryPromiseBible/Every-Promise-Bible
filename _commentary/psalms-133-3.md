The last line of a three-verse psalm, and it moves water from one end of the country to the other.

## The psalm

*Behold, how good and how pleasant it is **for brethren to dwell together in unity**!* See the note
on Psalms 133:1.

*It is like the precious oil upon the head, running down on the beard, the beard of Aaron, running
down on the edge of his garments.* See the note on Psalms 133:2.

Three verses, two images: oil, and dew.

## The dew

*It is like **the dew of Hermon** falling on **the hills of Zion**.*

The Hebrew is **ketal-Chermon sheyyored al-harerei Tziyyon**.

## Where Hermon is

The far north. A mountain over nine thousand feet, snow-capped most of the year, on the border
of Lebanon and Syria.

Its slopes are famously wet. The runoff feeds the Jordan.

## Where Zion is

A hundred and twenty miles south, in the Judean hills, dry for half the year.

## The problem with the line

Hermon's dew does not fall on Zion.

The psalm knows that. It is not making a claim about weather.

## What the image does

Takes the wettest place in the country and puts its abundance on the driest.

The oil in verse 2 ran down from the head to the hem — top to bottom. The dew runs from north to
south. Both images are about something travelling a distance it would not naturally travel.

## The verb

**Yored** — descending, running down.

The same participle used of the oil in the previous verse. The psalm uses one word for both
images.

## What dew was

In a country with no rain from April to October, dew was the difference between a crop and none.

*Then God give you of the dew of heaven, of the fatness of the earth* — Isaac's blessing on
Jacob.

And Gideon's fleece: *there was dew on the fleece only, and it was dry on all the ground*.

## The commanded blessing

*For **there the LORD has given His blessing, life forevermore**.*

The Hebrew is **ki sham tzivvah YHWH et-habberakhah** — for there the LORD commanded the blessing.

**Tzivvah** — commanded. The blessing is issued as an order.

## Where *there* is

The word points back to the whole psalm: where brothers dwell together.

Not a location. A condition.

## What is commanded

*Life forevermore.*

The Hebrew is **chayyim ad-ha'olam** — life to the age.

A three-verse psalm about getting along ends on eternal life.

## What kind of psalm this is

*A Song of Ascents.*

One of fifteen, sung by pilgrims going up to Jerusalem for the feasts. The road climbs the whole
way.

The psalm before it is one line long. The one after it is the last of the set: *behold, bless the
LORD, all you servants of the LORD, who by night stand in the house of the LORD*.

## Who wrote it

*A Song of Ascents. Of David.*

The man whose own household produced Absalom, Amnon and Adonijah, and whose sons killed each
other.

He is not writing from an easy family.

## What Paul asks for

*Now may the God of patience and comfort grant you **to be like-minded toward one another**, according
to Christ Jesus, that you may **with one mind and one mouth glorify God**.*

> **The geography.** Hermon's dew does not reach Zion, and the psalm knows it. It takes the
> wettest place in the country and puts it on the driest.
