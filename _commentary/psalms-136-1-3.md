The psalm with one line in it, repeated twenty-six times.

## The structure

Every verse in Psalm 136 has two halves. The first varies; the second never does.

*His steadfast love endures forever.*

Twenty-six verses, twenty-six repetitions.

## How it was used

The form is antiphonal. A leader sang the first half and the congregation answered with
the second.

The psalm is built to be said back.

## The word

The Hebrew is **chesed** — covenant loyalty, steadfast love, mercy. It is one of the
hardest words in the Old Testament to translate, and English versions render it a dozen
ways.

It means the kind of love that holds because of an agreement rather than because of a
feeling — and then exceeds the agreement.

## The three titles

*Give thanks to **the LORD**, for He is good.*

*Give thanks to **the God of gods**.*

*Give thanks to **the Lord of lords**.*

The Hebrew is **YHWH**, then **Elohei ha-elohim**, then **Adonei ha-adonim** — a Hebrew
superlative construction, the same shape as *king of kings* and *holy of holies*.

## What the psalm goes on to list

Creation — the heavens, the earth on the waters, the great lights, the sun for the day and
the moon and stars for the night.

Then the exodus — the firstborn of Egypt, the outstretched arm, the sea divided *into
parts*, Israel through the middle, Pharaoh overthrown.

Then the wilderness and the kings — Sihon, Og, the land given as a heritage.

Then: *who **remembered us in our lowly state**… and **rescued us from our enemies**… who
**gives food to all flesh**.*

Cosmology, national history, and dinner, and after every one of them the same line.

## What the repetition does

The listed events are all different. The refrain is the constant, which is the point of the
form.

Whatever the verse is about, the answer is the same.

## Where it turns up in the history

2 Chronicles, at the dedication of Solomon's temple: the trumpeters and singers *as one, to
make one sound to be heard in praising and thanking the LORD… saying, "**For He is good, for
His mercy endures forever**" — that the house was filled with a cloud.*

And at Jehoshaphat's battle, where the singers went out in front of the army with the same
line. See the note on 2 Chronicles 20:20–21.

And at the laying of the second temple's foundation, in Ezra — where the old men who
remembered the first one wept while everyone else shouted.

## The name it carries

Jewish tradition calls Psalm 136 **the Great Hallel**, and it is sung at Passover.

Which means it was very likely sung in the upper room. Matthew records the end of the
supper: *and when they had sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives.*

> **What the psalm is.** One sentence, said twenty-six times, with the whole of creation
> and the whole of Israel's history hung between the repetitions.
