The parable turns on one thing, and it is not the sleeping.

## The setting

A Jewish wedding. The bridegroom comes to the bride's house at an hour nobody publishes, and
the party escorts him back to his father's house with lamps.

Waiting is built into the arrangement. See the note on John 14:1–4.

## The ten

*Ten bridesmaids who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom.*

All ten go out. All ten have lamps. All ten are expecting him.

*Five of them were **foolish** and five were **wise**.*

The Greek is **morai** and **phronimoi** — the same pair as the two builders at the end of the
Sermon on the Mount.

## The difference

*The foolish ones **took their lamps but took no oil** with them. The wise ones **took jars of
oil** along with their lamps.*

One item. Everything else about the ten is identical.

The foolish are not lazy or hostile. They turned up. They simply brought nothing to keep the
light going.

## The delay

*The bridegroom was **a long time coming**.*

The Greek is **chronizontos** — taking a long time.

The delay is in the parable on purpose. It is what exposes the difference, and nothing else
would have.

## The sleeping

*And **they all grew drowsy and fell asleep**.*

All ten. The wise sleep too.

Whatever the parable is warning about, it is not sleeping — the ones who are commended do it.

## The cry

*At **midnight** the cry went up: "**Here is the bridegroom! Come out to meet him!**"*

## The trimming

*Then **all ten** bridesmaids woke up and **trimmed their lamps**.*

All ten wake instantly and all ten reach for the same task.

The difference does not appear until the lamps have been burning a minute.

## The request

*"Give us some of your oil; **our lamps are going out**."*

The Greek is a present tense — they are being extinguished, now.

## The refusal

*"**No** — there may not be enough for us and for you. **Go to those who sell oil and buy some
for yourselves**."*

It reads harshly. It is the parable's point: this is not transferable.

Nobody can lend it, and it cannot be acquired in the last five minutes.

## The door

*But **while they were away buying it, the bridegroom arrived**. The ones who were ready went
in with him to the wedding feast, and **the door was shut**.*

## The sentence

*"Sir, sir, open the door for us." But he answered, "**I tell you the truth, I do not know
you**."*

The Greek is **ouk oida humas**.

The same words as the end of the Sermon on the Mount: *many will say to Me in that day, "Lord,
Lord, have we not prophesied in Your name?"… and then I will declare to them, "I never knew
you."*

Both times the address is doubled, and both times the answer is about knowing rather than
doing.

## The moral He attaches

*"So **stay awake**, because you do not know the day or the hour."*

The Greek is **gregoreite** — be watchful.

Which is odd, since all ten slept and five were fine.

The watching He asks for is not staying conscious. It is the state of being supplied before
the night starts.

## Where it sits

Between the Olivet discourse and the parable of the talents, in the last week.

Three parables in a row about the interval: the servant whose master delays, the ten lamps,
and the talents. All three are about what people do while nothing is happening.

> **The one variable.** Not attentiveness — all ten slept. Five of them had brought oil before
> the waiting started, and the delay is what made the difference show.
