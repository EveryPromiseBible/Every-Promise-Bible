Paul describes two kinds of people by what their minds are set on.

## The word

*Have their **minds set on** what that nature wants.*

The Greek is **phronousin** — from **phroneo**, to have a mindset, to be disposed toward.

It is not a word about thinking occasionally. It describes a settled orientation, the
direction a mind faces by default.

Paul uses the noun **phronema** four times in these four verses and almost nowhere else.

## The two groups

*People who **live according to the old nature** have their minds set on what that nature
wants; people who **live according to the Spirit** have their minds set on **what the Spirit
wants**.*

The living and the mindset are described as one thing. Paul does not say the mindset
produces the life or the reverse — the sentence puts them together.

## The two outcomes

*A mind set on the old nature **ends in death**; a mind set on the Spirit is **life and
peace**.*

The Greek is simply *is death* and *is life and peace*.

Not *leads to*. Paul equates them.

**Peace** is added and not paralleled. There is no opposite named for it.

## The hostility

*For the mind set on the old nature is **hostile toward God**.*

The Greek is **echthra** — enmity.

Not indifferent, and not weak. Set against.

## The two clauses

*It **does not submit to God's law**, and **in fact it cannot**.*

The Greek is *neither indeed can be*.

Paul goes past unwillingness to inability. It is the sharpest thing he says about the
flesh, and he says it twice in one line.

## The conclusion

*Those who are **living out of that nature cannot please God**.*

Which is the end of the road for any programme of self-improvement, and Paul has spent
Romans 7 demonstrating it from the inside. See the note on Romans 7:18–20.

## What comes immediately after

*But **you are not living out of the old nature; you are living in the Spirit**, if indeed
the Spirit of God lives in you.* See the note on Romans 8:9–11.

The whole paragraph is diagnostic, and the next sentence tells the readers it does not
describe them.

Paul does this repeatedly — states the condition in full and then names where his readers
stand.

## Where the chapter opened

*So then, **there is no condemnation now — none at all** — for those who are in Christ
Jesus.* See the note on Romans 8:1–4.

And the reason: *what the law could not accomplish, weakened as it was by the old nature,
God accomplished Himself.*

The inability in verse 7 is the same inability named in verse 3. Paul is consistent about
where the failure is.

## What he is not describing

Two kinds of Christian. The categories in the paragraph are people in whom the Spirit
dwells and people in whom He does not — verse 9 makes it explicit: *if anyone does not have
the Spirit of Christ, that person is not His.*

## Where the chapter goes

*If the Spirit of Him who raised Jesus from the dead lives in you, then the One who raised
Christ from the dead **will bring life to your mortal bodies as well**.*

And then sonship, the Spirit's intercession, and *all things work together for good*.

> **The word the paragraph runs on.** Not what a mind thinks about — what it is set toward.
> Paul uses the noun four times in four verses and attaches death to one direction and life
> and peace to the other.
