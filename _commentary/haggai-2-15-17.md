He asks them to remember the arithmetic of the years before the building started.

## The instruction

*"Now **give careful thought to this, from this day on**."*

The fourth time the phrase appears in this short book. See the note on Haggai 1:5.

**From this day on** — the twenty-fourth of the ninth month, the date the oracle carries.

## The marker

*"Think back to **how things were before one stone was laid on another** in the temple of the
LORD."*

The Hebrew is **miterem sum-even el-aven** — before stone was placed on stone.

He gives them a fixed point in the calendar and asks them to compare the two sides of it.

## The first count

*"When anyone came to a heap **expecting twenty measures, there were only ten**."*

A heap of harvested grain, and half of what the eye had estimated.

## The second

*"When anyone went to the wine vat to draw off **fifty measures, there were only twenty**."*

Worse. Less than half.

The Hebrew word for the vat is **yekev** — the collecting trough beneath the winepress.

## Why the specifics

Because everyone standing there had done the counting themselves.

Haggai is not describing a famine. He is describing the ordinary experience of expecting a
figure and finding a smaller one, year after year, which is a much more familiar thing.

## The three blows

*"**I struck all the work of your hands with blight and mildew and hail**."*

The Hebrew is **shiddaphon** — scorching, the hot east wind that withers standing grain.
**Yeraqon** — mildew, yellowing. **Barad** — hail.

Two are slow and one is sudden. Three ways for a harvest to fail.

## Where the list comes from

Deuteronomy 28, in the warnings: *the LORD will strike you with… blight and mildew*.

And Solomon's prayer at the dedication of the first temple: *when there is famine in the land,
pestilence or blight or mildew… whatever prayer is made by anyone**, then hear in heaven*.

Haggai's audience would have recognised the vocabulary.

## The sentence at the end

*"**Yet you did not turn to Me**," declares the LORD.*

The Hebrew is **ve'eyn-etchem elai** — and you were not toward Me.

The harvests failed and nobody drew the connection. That is the charge, and it is the last line
of the paragraph.

## What the point of the striking was

To be noticed.

The same complaint runs through Amos: *I gave you cleanness of teeth in all your cities… **yet you
have not returned to Me**, says the LORD* — five times in one chapter.

## The bag with holes

*He who earns wages, earns wages **to put into a bag with holes**.* See the note on
Haggai 1:6.

The image from the first sermon. Not that there were no wages. That they did not stay in.

## The turn

*"**Is the seed still in the barn?** As yet the vine, the fig tree, the pomegranate, and the olive
tree have not yielded fruit."*

December. Nothing sown has come up, and the four fruit trees named are all still bare.

*"**From this day I will bless you**."*

The promise is dated to a day when the evidence is entirely absent.

## What had changed

Three months of work on a building that was nowhere near finished, and would not be for four
more years.

The blessing is not attached to completion.

> **The last line.** *Yet you did not turn to Me.* The harvests had been failing for years and
> nobody put the two things next to each other — which is what the whole book asks them to do.
