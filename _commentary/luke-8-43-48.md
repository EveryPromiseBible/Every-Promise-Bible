He is on His way to a dying child and stops the whole procession for a question He already knows the answer to.

## Where He is going

Jairus's house. His only daughter, twelve years old, is dying, and the crowd is nearly crushing
Him. See the note on Luke 8:40–42.

## The woman

*A woman **having a flow of blood for twelve years**.*

Twelve years — the same number as the girl's age. Luke does not comment.

## Her condition under the law

*If a woman has a discharge of blood for many days… **she shall be unclean**… every bed on which
she lies shall be unclean, and everything on which she sits shall be unclean.*

Twelve years outside. She cannot go to the temple, and anyone she touches becomes unclean.

She is in a crowd pressing against a man, which is not permitted.

## What she had spent

*Who **had spent all her livelihood on physicians and could not be healed by any**.*

Luke is a physician. He records that the physicians failed.

Mark is blunter: *she had suffered many things from many physicians, spent all that she had, and
was no better, but rather grew worse*.

## The approach

*Came from behind and touched **the border of His garment**.*

The Greek is **tou kraspedou** — the hem, and specifically the tassel.

Numbers 15 commanded a tassel on the corner of the garment, with a blue thread, *that you may
look upon it and remember all the commandments of the LORD*.

She reaches for the part of His clothing that carried the law.

## The result

*And **immediately her flow of blood stopped**.*

## The question

*And Jesus said, "**Who touched Me?**"*

## Peter's objection

*"Master, **the multitudes throng and press You**, and You say, 'Who touched Me?'"*

Which is entirely reasonable. Dozens of people are in contact with Him.

## The distinction

*"**Somebody touched Me**, for I perceived power going out from Me."*

The Greek is **hepsato mou tis** — someone touched Me.

Luke distinguishes two verbs. **Sunthlibo** — the crowd's pressing. **Haptomai** — her touching.

The difference is not physical. Everybody was in contact; one person drew.

## Why He asks

Not to identify her. He knows.

She could have gone home healed and anonymous, still legally unclean, with no one told. The
question is what stops that.

## What she does

*Now when the woman saw that she was not hidden, **she came trembling**; and falling down before
Him, she declared to Him **in the presence of all the people** the reason she had touched Him.*

In public, in front of a crowd, a woman states twelve years of a condition nobody discussed.

## What He calls her

*"**Daughter**, be of good cheer; your faith has made you well. Go in peace."*

The Greek is **thugater**.

He does not use the word of anybody else in the Gospels.

Jairus's **thugater** is dying up the road. This woman is given the same word.

## The verb

**Sesoken se** — has saved you. The wide word, covering healing and salvation.

## The cost of the stop

*While He was still speaking, someone came from the ruler of the synagogue's house, saying to him,
"**Your daughter is dead. Do not trouble the Teacher**."*

The interruption and the death are in consecutive verses.

## What He says to Jairus

*"**Do not be afraid; only believe, and she will be made well**."* See the note on
Luke 8:49–56.

The same verb He has just used of the woman.

Jairus has watched the delay that cost him, and been given a demonstration of what the delay can
do.

> **The two verbs.** The crowd **pressed**. She **touched**. Luke uses different words and says the
> difference was not physical.
