This is the most quoted sentence in the letter and the most often quoted
half-sentence in the Bible. Almost nobody reads the next line, and the next line
is where Paul says what he means by *good*.

## He defines his own term

> everything, all of it together, is working toward good … **for** those God knew
> beforehand, He also marked out beforehand **to be shaped into the likeness of
> His Son**

The *for* is doing the work of a definition. The good being worked toward is not
a comfortable outcome, a resolved situation, or a happy ending. It is a person
coming to look like Christ.

That is why the verse holds up in situations where a promise of good outcomes
would be an insult. Paul is not saying the thing that happened was good. He is
saying nothing is wasted in what God is making.

## "Shaped into the likeness"

The Greek word is *symmorphous* — sharing a form with. It is not imitation from
the outside. Paul uses the same root when he speaks of a body being transformed,
and of being conformed to Christ's death. The picture is of something taking a
shape rather than copying a model.

And the purpose given is not the reader's improvement: *so that His Son would be
the firstborn among many brothers and sisters.* The goal of the whole process is
a family with Christ at the head of it. The reader's transformation is how that
family comes to exist, not a self-improvement project with a spiritual label.

## The five links, and where they land

Paul then does something unusual: he runs the whole thing end to end in one
breath.

- knew beforehand
- marked out beforehand
- called
- put right with Himself
- **glorified**

Every verb is past tense, including the last one. Glorified — a thing no reader
has experienced yet — is spoken of as done. Paul is not confused about the
timeline. He is describing the chain from God's side, where it is finished, and
that is why he can start the paragraph with *we know*.

## What it is safe to say with this verse

Not: everything that happens is good. Paul does not say that, and the chapter
around it is full of groaning, weakness and suffering.

What he says is that all of it together is being worked toward one end, that the
end is Christ's likeness in a person, and that the sequence which gets them there
is already complete in the mind of the One running it.

> **On "those who love God."** It reads like a qualifier a person might fail, and
> anxious readers use it that way. But Paul immediately restates the group by
> another name — *those who have been called according to His purpose* — and
> everything after that is God's action, not theirs. The love is how he describes
> them, not a threshold they maintain.
