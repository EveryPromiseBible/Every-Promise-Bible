The third time the king makes the same offer, and this time she takes it.

## The offer, again

*"**What is your petition, Queen Esther? It will be granted you. What is your request? Even to
half the kingdom, it will be done.**"*

Word for word what he said in the inner court and again at the first banquet. Three times, and
she has let two of them pass. See the note on Esther 5:6–8.

## Who is at the table

Three people. A king who does not know his wife is Jewish, a queen who is about to say so, and
the man who signed the order to kill her nation — invited by her, twice.

*"**You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies.**"* See the note on
Psalms 23:5.

Not away from them. In front of them, and Esther set both places herself.

## What the wine is for

*Again on this second day, **over the wine**, the king said to her…*

The book keeps its business at tables. Vashti is dismissed at a drinking party, the decree
against the Jews is sealed while the king and Haman *sat down to drink*, and it is undone over
wine at a dinner for three.

## The title he uses

*"What is your petition, **Queen Esther**?"*

He calls her queen. She is about to tell him she is one of the people he has already agreed to
have destroyed.

*To Him who loved us and washed us from our sins in His own blood, and **has made us kings and
priests to His God and Father*** — **John**, of **Jesus**, at the opening of Revelation.

The standing is given before the disclosure, not after it.

## What she has been doing

Feeding him. Two banquets, prepared and hosted, before a single word of the petition.

Nothing in the book is rushed and nothing in it is explained — and the sequence is the only
argument it makes.

*And we know that **all things work together for good** to those who love God, to those who are
the called according to His purpose.*

> **The same sentence, three times.** *Even to half the kingdom.* She let two of them go, and
> the night in between is the one the whole book turns on.
