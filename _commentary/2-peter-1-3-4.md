Two verses at the front of Peter's last letter, and both of the main verbs are past tense.

## The power

*God has **power that belongs to nobody else**, and He has **already spent it on us**.*

The Greek is **tes theias dunameos autou** — His divine power.

**Dedoremenes** — perfect passive participle. Has been given, and stands given.

## What has been given

*Everything a person needs **in order to live, and to live toward God** — He has **handed the whole of
it over**.*

The Greek is **panta ta pros zoen kai eusebeian** — all things pertaining to life and godliness.

**Eusebeia** — godliness, reverence toward God. The word runs through the letter.

## The tense

Perfect. Completed, with results standing.

Peter is not describing a supply that will be issued. He is describing a delivery already made.

## How it came

*It came **through knowing the One who called us**.*

The Greek is **dia tes epignoseos** — through the full knowledge.

**Epignosis** — knowledge with the prefix intensified. Peter uses it four times in this short
letter.

## What we were called to

*And what He called us to is **splendor and goodness**.*

The Greek is **idia doxe kai arete** — by His own glory and virtue, or *to* them.

**Arete** — excellence, moral vigour. A Greek philosophical word, and rare in the New Testament.

## The promises

*That is where the promises come from, and they are **enormous**, and **there is nothing else like them
anywhere**.*

The Greek is **ta timia kai megista epangelmata** — the precious and very great promises.

**Megista** — a superlative. The greatest.

## What they are for

*Through those promises you **come to share in the nature of God Himself**.*

The Greek is **theias koinonoi phuseos** — partakers of the divine nature.

**Koinonos** — a partner, a sharer. The word used of business partners: James and John were
Simon's *partners* in the fishing.

## What that does not mean

Not absorption into deity. Peter is Jewish and monotheist.

**Koinonos** is the language of participation, not identity. The same root as **koinonia**,
fellowship.

## The escape

*You are **out of the rot that runs through this world** — the rot that **wanting breeds**.*

The Greek is **apophugontes tes en to kosmo en epithumia phthoras**.

**Phthora** — corruption, decay, ruin.

**En epithumia** — in lust, in craving. Peter names the mechanism: the decay is driven by wanting.

## What follows

*But also for this very reason, giving all diligence, **add to your faith virtue, to virtue
knowledge, to knowledge self-control**, to self-control perseverance, to perseverance godliness, to
godliness brotherly kindness, and to brotherly kindness love.* See the note on
2 Peter 1:5–7.

Seven things to add, and they come after two verses saying everything has already been given.

The order matters. The adding is not to make up a shortfall.

## Why he is writing

*Knowing that shortly I must put off my tent, just as our Lord Jesus Christ showed me.*

*Moreover I will be careful to ensure that **you always have a reminder of these things after my
decease**.*

He is writing a last letter, and had been told how it would end: *when you are old, you will
stretch out your hands, and another will gird you*.

## What he saw

*For we did not follow cunningly devised fables… but **were eyewitnesses of His majesty**.*

*And we heard this voice which came from heaven **when we were with Him on the holy mountain**.* See
the note on 2 Peter 1:16–18.

> **The tenses.** *Has given*, perfect — a delivery already made and standing. The seven things to
> add come two verses later, and not to make up a shortfall.
