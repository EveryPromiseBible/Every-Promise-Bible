A shadow tells you something real is standing there. It is not the thing.

## The instruction

*So **let no one pass judgment on you** about what you eat or what you drink.*

The Greek is **mē tis humas krinetō** — let no one be judging you.

Present imperative. Stop an ongoing practice.

## The list

*Or about **a festival**, or **a new moon**, or **sabbath days**.*

Yearly, monthly, weekly.

The whole Jewish calendar, in descending order of interval. Nothing is left out of it.

## The verdict

*These are **a shadow of what was coming**; **the body that casts it is Christ's**.*

The Greek is **skia tōn mellontōn** — a shadow of the things coming. And **to de sōma tou
Christou** — but the body is of Christ.

## What a shadow is

Evidence of a solid object, in the right shape, with no substance in it.

Paul does not call the calendar false. He calls it a silhouette.

## The word

**Sōma** — body. He could have said **eikōn**, image, or the truth, or the reality.

He chose the word that means the object with mass. And it also means the church.

## What the letter says elsewhere

*In Him **dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily**.* See the note on Colossians 2:8–10.

The Greek is **sōmatikōs**.

## The second warning

*Let no one **cheat you of your prize**.*

The Greek is **katabrabeuetō** — from **brabeus**, the umpire who awards the prize. To rule
against, to disqualify.

Somebody at Colossae was acting as referee.

## What they were selling

*Taking his pleasure in **a humility of his own making** and in **the worship of angels**.*

The Greek is **thelōn en tapeinophrosunē** — delighting in humility.

Self-appointed lowliness, which is a contradiction, and Paul writes it as one.

## The phrase

*Pushing his way into **things he has not seen**.*

The Greek is **ha heoraken embateuōn** — entering into what he has seen. The manuscripts differ
over the negative.

**Embateuō** was used of initiates entering a shrine. The language of mystery religion.

## The diagnosis

*Swollen with pride over nothing, **by the mind of his flesh**.*

The Greek is **phusioumenos** — puffed up, inflated. From **phusaō**, to blow.

Air, not weight. The opposite of **sōma**.

## The real charge

*And **not holding on to the head**.*

The Greek is **ou kratōn tēn kephalēn**.

Everything before this was symptom. This is the fault.

## What the head does

*From whom **the whole body, supplied and bound together by its joints and its ligaments, grows with
the growth that comes from God**.*

The Greek is **epichorēgoumenon kai sumbibazomenon** — supplied and knit together.

**Epichorēgeō** — to underwrite, to bear the cost of. The word came from funding a chorus at a
festival.

## The growth

**Tēn auxēsin tou Theou** — the increase of God.

A body does not grow by effort at the joints. It grows because the head is feeding it.

## What this is set against

Everything in the passage is something a person does: keeping days, refusing food, manufacturing
humility, entering visions.

Against it, one participle that is not: *supplied*.

## Where the argument started

*Beware lest anyone cheat you through philosophy and empty deceit… and not according to Christ.*
See the note on Colossians 2:8–10.

And it ends: *these things have an appearance of wisdom in self-imposed religion, false humility,
and neglect of the body, but are of no value against the indulgence of the flesh*. See the note on
Colossians 2:20–23.

> **The shadow.** He does not say the calendar was a lie. He says it was the outline — and the thing
> standing in the light is Christ.
