Four promises in a row, and the chapter is careful about what they are attached
to.

## "Do this"

The promises begin with a condition, and the chapter has just spelled it out at
length. Israel had been fasting and complaining that God took no notice. His
answer describes the fast He actually wants:

loose the chains of injustice, share your bread with the hungry, bring the
homeless poor into your house, clothe the naked, do not hide from your own flesh
and blood.

*Do this*, and then these four.

## The four

- **your light will break forth like the dawn** — sudden, and unstoppable once
  begun
- **your healing will come quickly** — the Hebrew word is about new flesh
  sprouting over a wound; it is a recovery word, used of a nation's restoration
  and of a body's
- **your goodness will walk ahead of you**
- **the glory of the LORD will be your rear guard**

## The formation

The last two are military. Something goes in front and something covers the
back — the same picture as the exodus, where the pillar of cloud moved from
before Israel to behind them, between the camp and Egypt's army.

A person in that formation is covered at both ends.

## The answer

*Then you will call, and the LORD will answer. You will cry out, and He will say,
"Here I am."*

The last phrase is the same one Abraham and Moses and Samuel say to God —
*hinneni*, here I am. Here it comes back the other way, from God to the person
calling.

> **Why the condition matters.** The chapter is not describing a technique for
> getting light and healing. It is God telling a religious people that the
> observance they were proud of had left the hungry hungry — and that the
> breakthrough they wanted was on the other side of feeding them.
