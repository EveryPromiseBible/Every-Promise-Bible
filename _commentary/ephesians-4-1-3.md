The hinge of the letter. Three chapters of what God has done, and then the first
instruction — and it is about how to treat each other.

## What is not being asked for

*Working hard to **guard** the oneness the Spirit gives you.*

Not to create it. Not to achieve it. The unity already exists — it is the
Spirit's, and it is given. The job is to keep it.

That distinction changes the work entirely. Nobody is being asked to manufacture
agreement. They are being asked not to break something that is already there.

## The four qualities

*Never above anyone, gentle in your handling of people, patient, putting up with
one another because you love one another.*

- **never above anyone** — the Greek word for lowliness of mind. In classical
  Greek it was an insult, a term for a grovelling nature. The New Testament picks
  it up and makes it a virtue.
- **gentle** — the word for strength under control, used of a broken horse
- **patient** — long-tempered, the same word used of God in 2 Peter 3:9
- **putting up with one another** — bearing with, enduring

Every one of them is a quality you only need when someone is being difficult.
This is not a description of people who naturally get along.

## "The tie of peace"

The Greek is a bond, a ligament — the thing that holds a joint together. Paul
uses the same image later in the chapter for the body being knit together.

Peace is not the absence of friction here. It is the connective tissue.

> **"I, the Lord's prisoner."** Paul opens the practical half of his letter by
> naming where he is writing from. The man asking them to be patient with each
> other is in chains, and he mentions it once, without complaint, as his
> credential for asking.
