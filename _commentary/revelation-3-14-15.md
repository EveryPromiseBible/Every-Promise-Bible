The last of the seven letters, and the speaker names Himself with a word nobody had used
as a title before.

## The address

*Write this to **the angel of the church of the Laodiceans**.*

Laodicea was a banking centre in the Lycus valley, known for a black wool industry and a
medical school that produced an eye ointment.

All three turn up in the letter: *buy from Me gold refined in the fire… white garments…
and anoint your eyes with eye salve*.

He speaks to a city in its own vocabulary.

## The first title

*Here is who is speaking: **the Amen**.*

**Amen** is a Hebrew word meaning *firm, sure, so be it*. It is what a congregation says
back.

Nobody had used it as a name. Here it is a person.

Isaiah comes closest: *he who blesses himself in the earth shall bless himself in **the God
of truth*** — and the Hebrew there is *the God of Amen*.

## The second and third

*The witness who is **faithful**, the witness who **tells the truth**.*

The Greek is **ho martus ho pistos kai alethinos**.

**Martus** — witness, the root of *martyr*.

## The fourth

*The **beginning of everything God made**.*

The Greek is **he arche tes ktiseos tou theou** — the beginning, the origin, the ruling
principle of God's creation.

**Arche** carries both senses: what something starts from, and what governs it. Colossians
uses the same idea: *He is before all things, and in Him all things consist… **the
beginning**, the firstborn from the dead.*

## Where Paul takes the Amen

*For **all the promises of God in Him are Yes, and in Him Amen**, to the glory of God through
us.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 1:15–20.

God says Yes; the Amen comes back through the people who trust it. Paul puts both halves in
one sentence, and here the Amen is a name.

## The assessment

*"**I know what you have been doing**. You are **not cold and you are not hot**."*

The Greek is **psuchros** and **zestos** — cold, and boiling.

Every one of the seven letters opens *I know*.

## The preference

*"**I wish you were cold, or else hot**."*

Which is a startling sentence. Cold is named as preferable to what they are.

## The local detail

Laodicea had no water of its own. It was piped in — hot mineral water from Hierapolis six
miles north, cold spring water from Colossae ten miles east.

By the time either arrived it was lukewarm.

The hot springs were medicinal; the cold water was refreshing. Tepid water is the one that
is good for nothing, and the next verse says what it provokes: *I will vomit you out of My
mouth*.

## What they said about themselves

*"Because you say, '**I am rich, have become wealthy, and have need of nothing**' — and do
not know that you are **wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked**."*

## What is said last

*"Behold, **I stand at the door and knock**. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, **I
will come in to him and dine with him**, and he with Me."*

Addressed to a church, from outside it.

The harshest of the seven letters ends with a meal being offered through a door.

> **The title He opens with.** The Amen — the word people say back. Paul says every promise
> gets its Yes in Him and its Amen through us; here the Amen is the one doing the
> speaking.
