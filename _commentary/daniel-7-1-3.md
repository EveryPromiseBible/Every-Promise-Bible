The book turns over here. For six chapters Daniel has interpreted other men's dreams; from
this one on, he has his own.

## The date

*In the **first year that Belshazzar ruled Babylon**.*

Which places it before chapter 5 — the writing on the wall, on the night Belshazzar died.

The book is not in chronological order. Chapters 7 and 8 belong before 5 and 6, and Daniel
has arranged the material by kind rather than by date.

Chapters 2 to 7 are in Aramaic — the language of the empire. Chapters 1 and 8 to 12 are in
Hebrew.

## Whose dream it is

*Daniel had **a dream**.*

For six chapters he has been the man who explains. Now he is the one who needs explaining
to — and inside the vision he asks: *I came near to one of those who stood by, and asked
him the truth of all this.*

## Where it happened

*Visions moved through his mind **as he lay on his bed**.*

An old man asleep in his own room. Nothing about the setting is remarkable.

## What he did next

*When he woke, **he wrote it all down**.*

The Aramaic is *he wrote the dream, telling the main facts*.

Habakkuk had been told to do exactly this: *write the vision, carve it clear on tablets*.
See the note on Habakkuk 2:2–3.

## The clause

*This is where **the account begins**.*

The book says so about itself.

## The sea

*In the night I looked, and I saw **the four winds of heaven churning the great sea**.*

Four winds — from every direction at once.

The sea in Hebrew imagery is the place of chaos, the thing God set boundaries on at
creation. Revelation ends with *there was no more sea*.

## What comes out

*And **up out of that sea came four huge beasts, each one unlike the others**.*

A lion with eagle's wings, a bear with three ribs in its mouth, a leopard with four heads
and four wings, and a fourth *dreadful and terrible, exceedingly strong* with iron teeth and
ten horns.

The same four empires as the statue in chapter 2 — and where Nebuchadnezzar saw gold,
silver, bronze and iron, Daniel sees animals.

A king dreams of a gleaming statue. A prophet dreams of what comes out of the sea.

## Where the chapter goes

Thrones set in place, the Ancient of Days seated, *His garment white as snow*, and *a
thousand thousands ministered to Him*.

And then: *behold, **One like the Son of Man, coming with the clouds of heaven**… to Him was
given dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages should
serve Him.*

**Son of Man** is the title Jesus uses of Himself more than any other, and it comes from
here.

He quotes this verse at His trial, which is what the high priest tears his clothes over.

## What it did to Daniel

*I, Daniel, was grieved in my spirit within my body, and **the visions of my head troubled
me**.*

And at the end: *my thoughts greatly troubled me, and my countenance changed; **but I kept
the matter in my heart**.*

## The last line of the chapter

He does not explain it or publish it. He writes it down and keeps it.

> **The turn in the book.** The interpreter becomes the one who needs interpreting — and
> the first thing he does on waking is reach for something to write with.
