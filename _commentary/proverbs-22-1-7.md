*Train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not turn
from it.* A promise with a long fuse, and the Hebrew has more in it than the
English lets through.

## "According to his way"

The phrase is **al pi darko** — literally *on the mouth of his way*. There is no
word for *should* in the Hebrew at all.

The natural sense is *according to his way*: this child, his bent, his grain, the
particular shape of him. It turns the instruction from a method applied to
children in general into an act of attention paid to one child in particular.

You train the child you actually have.

## The verb

**Chanak** is used elsewhere of dedicating a house or a temple — setting
something apart for the purpose it was made for. The related noun gives us
Hanukkah, the feast of dedication.

So the training is not primarily drilling. It is dedicating: putting a child on
the path he was made for, early.

## The promise attached

*When he is old he will not turn from it.*

The timescale is the striking part. Not next year. When he is old. The promise
reaches past the years a parent has any influence at all, and past the stretch
where it may look as if nothing took.

That is what makes it worth having. A promise that only covered the compliant
years would be no comfort to anybody.

> **What the chapter opens with.** *Choose a good name over great riches.* The
> chapter that contains the great parenting promise begins by saying what is
> worth handing on, and it is not money. Four verses later: *the reward of
> humility and the fear of the LORD is riches and honour and life.* The
> inheritance being described runs through character.
