Paul is describing a change of clothes.

## The word

*This body that decays **must be clothed** with what cannot decay.*

The Greek is **endusasthai** — to put on, as a garment. The same verb Paul uses for putting
on Christ, and for the armour in Ephesians 6.

Not *replaced*. Put on over.

## Said twice

*This body that **decays** must be clothed with what **cannot decay**, and this body that
**dies** must be clothed with **life that cannot die**.*

Then immediately again, word for word, as a *when* clause.

Two pairs — corruptible/incorruptible, mortal/immortal — stated, and then restated as the
condition for what comes next.

## What must

The Greek is **dei** — it is necessary. Flesh and blood *cannot inherit the kingdom of
God*, Paul has just said three verses earlier. See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:50–52.

The change is not an upgrade offered. It is a requirement of the destination.

## The trigger

*Then **the words that stand written will come true**.*

The Greek perfect — *has been written and stands written*. Isaiah's sentence has been on
the page for seven hundred years waiting for the event that makes it true.

## The first quotation

*"**Death is swallowed up in victory**."*

Isaiah 25:8, from the chapter about the feast on the mountain. See the note on
Isaiah 25:6–9.

The Hebrew verb there is **bala** — to swallow down. Isaiah uses it twice: once of the veil
over the nations, once of death.

## The second quotation

*"**Death, where is your sting? Grave, where is your victory?**"*

Hosea 13:14. In Hosea the line is spoken by God as a summons — *O Death, I will be your
plagues; O Grave, I will be your destruction*.

Paul turns it into a taunt shouted at an enemy already beaten.

## The sting

The Greek is **kentron** — the goad of an ox-driver, or the sting of a bee or scorpion.

And Paul explains it in the next verse: *the sting of death is sin, and the strength of sin
is the law.*

Two verses later he answers his own question: *thanks be to God, who gives us the victory
through our Lord Jesus Christ.*

## What is being described

A generation of believers who never die. *We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be
changed* — in a moment, at the last trumpet.

The dead rise incorruptible; the living are clothed without ever taking the garment off.

## Where else Paul says it

*Not that we want to be unclothed, but **further clothed**, that mortality may be swallowed
up by life.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 5:1–5.

Same verb, same swallowing, and there he adds that God *has given us the Spirit as a
guarantee*.

## What the chapter ends with

*Therefore, my beloved brethren, **be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of
the Lord**, knowing that your labour is not in vain in the Lord.*

Fifty-seven verses on resurrection, and the application is: keep working.

> **Which direction it is swallowed.** Not life absorbed into death, which is what everyone
> watching a funeral would assume. Death, gulped down by victory — and the word is Isaiah's,
> the one he had already used for the veil.
