The end of the opening vision, and what is on the throne is the point of the whole chapter.

## The approach

Four living creatures, four faces each, wheels within wheels full of eyes, and an expanse over
their heads *like the colour of an awesome crystal*.

And then a voice, and the wings come down.

*When they stood, **they let down their wings**.*

The noise stops before the throne is described. Ezekiel has spent twenty-four verses on the
machinery and the vehicle, and it all goes quiet.

## The throne

*Above the expanse over their heads was **the likeness of a throne, like the appearance of
sapphire**.*

The Hebrew is **even-sappir** — sapphire, or more likely lapis lazuli.

*Then Moses went up… and they saw the God of Israel. And there was under His feet as it were **a
paved work of sapphire stone**, and it was like the very heavens in its clarity.*

Exodus 24. The same stone, under the same feet, seven centuries earlier.

## What is on it

*And high upon the likeness of the throne was **a likeness with the appearance of a man**.*

The Hebrew is **demut kemar'eh adam** — a likeness like the appearance of a man.

Ezekiel is a priest, writing in a culture that forbade images, in a book that will spend chapters
attacking idols. He piles up hedging nouns — *likeness*, *appearance* — four and five deep, and
still says what he saw.

There is a man on the throne of God.

## What he looked like

*From what looked like His waist upward, **a gleam like glowing metal, like fire enclosed all
around**; and from what looked like His waist downward, the likeness of fire, and brightness all
around Him.*

The Hebrew is **chashmal** — an unknown word, usually taken as amber or a glowing alloy.

*And in the midst of the seven lampstands **One like the Son of Man**, clothed with a garment down
to the feet… **His feet were like fine brass, as if refined in a furnace**, and His voice as the
sound of many waters… and **His countenance was like the sun shining in its strength**.*

**Revelation** 1, and **John** describes the same figure with the same materials — a man, in
glowing metal, in fire.

*And He was transfigured before them. **His face shone like the sun**, and His clothes became as
white as the light.*

## Who Ezekiel saw

*These things Isaiah said **when he saw His glory and spoke of Him**.*

**John** 12 says Isaiah's throne vision in chapter 6 was of **Christ**. Ezekiel's is the same
throne, and the figure on it is described as a man.

*Who being the brightness of His glory and **the express image of His person**.*

**Hebrews** 1, and the Greek is **charakter tes hypostaseos** — the exact impression, as a seal
leaves in wax.

*"**He who has seen Me has seen the Father.**"*

## What Ezekiel does about it

*So when I saw it, **I fell on my face**, and I heard a voice of One speaking.*

The next verse, and it is the same response **John** has: *and when I saw Him, I fell at His feet
as dead. But He laid His right hand on me, saying to me, "**Do not be afraid**."*

Both men go down. In **Revelation** somebody reaches out and picks the man up, and the first thing
He says is not to be frightened.

## What he is called from there on

*"**Son of man**, stand on your feet, and I will speak to you."*

Ezekiel is addressed as **ben-adam** ninety-three times in the book — more than anybody else in
Scripture.

*"The **Son of Man** has power on earth to forgive sins."*

**Jesus** uses the title of Himself about eighty times in the gospels, and it is the one He chose.
Ezekiel is called it while lying on the ground in front of a man on a throne.

> **What the hedging does not hide.** A priest forbidden images stacks up *likeness* and
> *appearance* four deep and still reports it: the thing on the throne, above the sapphire, has
> the appearance of a man.
