Two verses of setup, and they produce the only place in Revelation where John cries.

## Where this is

The throne room of chapter 4 — the sea of glass, the four living creatures, the twenty-four
elders.

Nothing has happened yet. The scene has been described, and now something is noticed in a
hand.

## What He is holding

*The One on the throne was holding something in **His right hand**: a **scroll**.*

The Greek is **biblion** — a book or roll.

The right hand is the hand of authority. It is held out, not put away.

## The writing

*With writing **on the inside of it and writing on the back as well**.*

Papyrus was normally written on one side, along the grain. Writing on both means the
document is full — nothing more can be added to it.

Ezekiel is given one like it: *a scroll of a book was in it, and He spread it before me; and
there was writing on the inside and on the outside, and written on it were lamentations and
mourning and woe*.

## The seals

*Shut with **seven seals**.*

Roman wills were sealed with seven seals, one for each witness, and could only be opened in
the presence of all of them or by the rightful heir.

Jeremiah's title deed to a field at Anathoth was sealed the same way — *I signed the deed
and sealed it, took witnesses* — and it was a purchase made while the Babylonian army was
outside the city, as a sign that fields would be bought again.

A sealed scroll is a document of ownership.

## The challenge

*And there was a **mighty angel calling out at the top of his voice**.*

The Greek is *proclaiming with a loud voice*. A herald.

## The question

*"**Who deserves to break these seals and open this scroll?**"*

The Greek is **axios** — worthy, of sufficient weight.

Not who is *able*. Who is qualified.

## What happens next

*And no one in heaven or on the earth or under the earth was able to open the scroll, or to
look at it.*

Three realms, searched, and nothing.

*So **I wept much**, because no one was found worthy.*

The only recorded weeping in the book, and it is over an unopened document.

## The answer

*"**Do not weep. Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has prevailed**
to open the scroll and to loose its seven seals."*

The elder announces a Lion.

*And I looked, and behold, in the midst of the throne… **stood a Lamb as though it had been
slain**.*

John hears *Lion* and turns to look, and sees a Lamb with its throat cut, standing.

The Greek is **arnion** — a little lamb — and **esphagmenon**, slaughtered.

## What qualifies Him

*"**You are worthy** to take the scroll and to open its seals, **for You were slain, and
have redeemed us to God by Your blood**."*

The worthiness the angel asked about is answered by the killing. Nothing else in the chapter
is offered as the credential.

## What the new song says

*"And have made us kings and priests to our God, and we shall reign on the earth."*

Then the number: *ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands*.

And the last voice: *every creature which is in heaven and on the earth and under the earth
and such as are in the sea* — the same three realms that had nobody worthy in them, now all
singing.

## What the Lamb has

*Having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent out into all the
earth.*

Horns are power and eyes are sight, and both are at seven — the number of completeness.

> **What the herald asked.** Not who is strong enough. *Who is worthy* — and the answer,
> when it came, was a Lamb that had been killed and was standing.
