One verse, and it answers the second objection in the chapter.

## The question

*Then **did something good become death to me?**"*

He has just said the commandment *meant for life* turned out to bring death, and then
called the law *holy and just and good*. See the note on Romans 7:9–12.

The two statements sit awkwardly, and Paul knows it.

## The answer

*Certainly not.*

**Me genoito** again — the second time in seven verses, and the tenth in the letter.

## The culprit

*It was **sin**.*

One word, and the whole weight of the chapter rests on it.

## What sin was doing

*Sin **showing itself for exactly what it is**, producing death in me **through what is
good**.*

The Greek is *that it might appear sin*.

The commandment was the instrument, and Paul says so plainly — **through what is good**.
He does not soften the mechanism to protect the law; he protects the law by naming the
other party.

## The purpose

*So that **through the commandment sin might be exposed as utterly, immeasurably
sinful**.*

The Greek is **kath' huperbolen hamartolos** — sinful beyond measure, hyperbolically
sinful.

Paul stacks the intensifiers, as he does elsewhere: *far beyond anything we ask*, and *the
weight of it was far past anything our strength could carry*.

## What the law is for, in this verse

Exposure. Not correction, not enablement, and not condemnation of the person.

The commandment is described as the thing that makes sin visible in its true size — and
Paul says that was the intent.

He said it in chapter 5: *the law entered that the offence might abound.* See the note on
Romans 5:20–21.

## The two words for what the law reveals

**Epignosis** in chapter 3 — full recognition: *by the law is the knowledge of sin*.

And here, that it might *appear* — **phane**, be shown, come to light.

## What Paul is not arguing

That the law made him worse. He is arguing that it made something already there
identifiable.

The verse before: *I would not have known covetousness unless the law had said, "You shall
not covet."*

## Where the chapter goes next

*For **we know that the law is spiritual, but I am carnal, sold under sin**.*

Then the long passage about doing what he does not want, and the cry. See the note on
Romans 7:18–20.

## Why the chapter exists

The writer of it had been *blameless* concerning the law's righteousness by his own
account, and describes himself here as a man who found out what was underneath.

## What the exposure is for

Nothing in Romans 7 offers a remedy. That is the point of the chapter — it ends *O
wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me?*

And then: *I thank God — through Jesus Christ our Lord!*

> **What is on trial in the verse.** Not the law. Paul takes the charge, refuses it, names
> sin instead, and says the exposure was the object — that sin be shown at full size,
> using something good to do it.
