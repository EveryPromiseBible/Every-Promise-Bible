The gentlest line in the chapter, and it is about something being let go.

## The blessing

*"Naphtali is **a doe set free**, that bears **beautiful fawns**."*

The Hebrew is a hind loosed, released. Something that was tied is not tied any
more, and what comes out of it is beautiful.

The second half can be read as beautiful fawns or as beautiful words — the Hebrew
carries both, and older translations render it *he gives goodly words*.

**Naphtali** means my wrestling. Rachel named him after the struggle with her
sister.

## The ground

Naphtali's territory was the north-west shore of the Sea of Galilee — Capernaum,
Bethsaida, Chorazin, the Mount of Beatitudes.

That is where Jesus based Himself. *Leaving Nazareth, He came and dwelt in
Capernaum, which is by the sea, in the regions of Zebulun and **Naphtali**.*

Matthew quotes Isaiah over it: *the land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali… the
people who sat in darkness have seen a great light.*

Most of the miracles happened on that shoreline, and the Sermon on the Mount was
preached on that hillside. Beautiful words, on Naphtali's ground.

## The loosing

In a synagogue in that country there was a woman bent double for eighteen years,
who could not straighten up at all.

*"Woman, you are **loosed** from your infirmity."* And He laid His hands on her,
and immediately she was made straight.

When the ruler of the synagogue objected about the Sabbath, He used the word
again — *ought not this woman, being a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan has bound
for eighteen years, be **loosed** from this bond on the Sabbath?*

Loosed, twice, over a woman who had been tied for eighteen years, in the tribe
whose blessing is a doe set free. See the note on Luke 13:10–17.

## Who did the binding

He says it plainly in that synagogue: *whom Satan has bound.*

Not a discipline, not a lesson. A binding, and He unties it in a sentence.

> **What Moses said over Naphtali.** *O Naphtali, satisfied with favour, and full
> of the blessing of the LORD.* The word is *ratson* — good pleasure, acceptance.
> Satisfied with favour, on the shore where He did most of His work.
