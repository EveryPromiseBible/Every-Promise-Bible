Luke's summary of what the early weeks looked like, and he does not hedge the
numbers.

## The scale

*People even carried the sick out into the streets and laid them on cots and
mats… Crowds came in from the towns around Jerusalem too, bringing the sick and
those troubled by unclean spirits, and **every one of them was healed**.*

Luke uses the same phrase Matthew used of the evening at Peter's house: all of
them. He is a careful writer — he says elsewhere that he investigated everything
closely — and this is what he reports.

## The shadow

*Hoping that as Peter came by, at least his shadow might fall across some of
them.*

Luke records what the people hoped. He does not say the shadow healed anybody,
and no apostle is described as teaching it.

What the detail conveys is the expectation in the city at that moment. People
were carrying relatives into the street on the chance of being in the right place
when Peter walked past.

## The two reactions

*No one else dared to attach himself to them, though the people held them in high
regard. And still more were added to the Lord, crowds of men and women alike.*

Both at once — a wariness about joining, and steady growth. Luke has just
recorded the deaths of Ananias and Sapphira, and *great fear came on the whole
church*. He puts the fear and the crowds in the same paragraph without resolving
the tension.

## Where they met

*Solomon's Colonnade* — the covered walkway on the east side of the temple
courts. They were meeting in public, at the temple, in the open.

Two paragraphs later the apostles are arrested there.

> **What Luke says was happening among them.** *The believers all gathered with
> one heart.* The healings, the growth and the unity are reported in one
> sentence, and Luke keeps making that connection through the early chapters —
> see also Acts 2:40–41.
