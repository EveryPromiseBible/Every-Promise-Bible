---
key: Philippians 1:3–6
title: The One who began it will finish it
updated: 2026-08-07
---

The confidence at the end of the sentence rests on who started, not on how it is going.

## The thanks

*I thank my God **every time you come to mind**.*

The Greek is **epi pasē tē mneia humōn** — at every remembrance of you.

## Where he is

In custody. *My chains are in Christ*, he says a few verses on.

## The prayers

*And every prayer I make for you — **for all of you** — I make **with joy**.*

The Greek is **meta charas tēn deēsin poioumenos**.

**Chara** and its verb run through this letter more than any other: joy, rejoice, sixteen times.

## The insistence

*For all of you.*

He says it four times in the first chapter. The letter has a quarrel in the background — *I implore
Euodia and I implore Syntyche to be of the same mind*. See the note on Philippians 4:2–3.

The repeated *all* is not filler.

## The reason

*Because of **the share you have had with me in the good news** from the very first day until now.*

The Greek is **koinōnia** — partnership, fellowship, a joint stake.

## What the partnership was

Money, among other things.

They sent to him twice while he was in Thessalonica, and once more by Epaphroditus. *No church
shared with me concerning giving and receiving but you only.* See the note on Philippians 4:15–16.

## The first day

*From the very first day.*

Lydia, a jailer, and a slave girl. The Philippian church began with a business woman, a prison
officer and a former fortune-teller. See the note on Acts 16:13–15.

## The confidence

*And of this one thing **I am certain**.*

The Greek is **pepoithōs auto touto** — having been persuaded of this very thing.

Perfect participle. A settled conviction, not a hope.

## The subject

*The One who **began a good work in you**.*

The Greek is **ho enarxamenos en humin ergon agathon**.

**Enarchomai** — to begin, to make a beginning. The word was used of the opening rite of a
sacrifice.

## Who is doing what

Every verb in the clause is God's. The Philippians are the location, not the agent.

## The two verbs

*Will **carry it through and finish it**.*

The Greek is **epitelesei** — will complete, bring to its end.

**Epiteleō** is the sacrificial word for finishing what was begun. The pair **enarchomai /
epiteleō** was a set phrase for a rite carried from start to completion.

## The deadline

*Right up to **the day of Jesus Christ**.*

Not until they improve, and not until they die.

## What that does to the timescale

The completion is dated to an event outside their lifetime, which means nothing in their remaining
years can constitute the verdict.

## What he asks for next

*And this I pray, that **your love may abound still more and more** in knowledge and all discernment.*
See the note on Philippians 1:9–11.

The confidence does not replace the prayer. Both are in the same paragraph.

## The other side of the same coin

*Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; **for it is God who works in you** both to will
and to do for His good pleasure.* See the note on Philippians 2:12–13.

The Greek is **energōn** — the one energising.

Command and guarantee in one sentence, with the guarantee giving the reason for the command.

> **The grammar of the confidence.** Every verb in the clause belongs to God — began, carries
> through, finishes. The Philippians are where the work happens, not who does it.
