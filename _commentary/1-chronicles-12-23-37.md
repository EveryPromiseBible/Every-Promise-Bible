A muster roll, tribe by tribe, and one entry in it is not counted the same way as
the others.

## What the list is

*The armed men who came to David at Hebron **to turn Saul's kingdom over to him**,
just as the LORD had said.*

Saul is dead. David has been king over Judah alone for seven years, and this is the
moment the other tribes come across.

The chronicler counts them.

## How the others are described

Every tribe gets a number and a military note:

- Judah — *carrying shield and spear*: 6,800
- Simeon — *brave warriors ready for war*: 7,100
- Zebulun — *seasoned soldiers with every weapon*: 50,000
- Asher — *seasoned for war*: 40,000
- and 120,000 from beyond the Jordan, *armed with every kind of weapon*

Roughly three hundred and forty thousand men in total, described by their weapons and
their readiness.

## And then Issachar

*From Issachar, **men who understood the times and knew what Israel should do**: 200
chiefs, with all their kinsmen under their command.*

Two hundred. It is the smallest number of leaders in the entire list.

And they are the only tribe whose contribution is not military. No shields, no
spears, no seasoning for battle.

They knew what time it was, and what to do about it.

## The two halves of it

**Understood the times** — the Hebrew is discernment of the season, the moment.

**Knew what Israel should do** — knowledge that issues in action. Not analysis for
its own sake.

Plenty of people can read a situation. The verse credits them with both.

## Who Issachar was

Jacob's blessing over him was the saddest of the twelve: *a strong donkey, resting
among the sheepfolds… he will bend his shoulder to the load and submit to hard
labour.* See the note on Genesis 49:14–15.

The tribe that put its shoulder under a burden it did not have to carry produced the
men who could read a season.

## Zebulun's line

*Standing by David with **undivided hearts**.*

The Hebrew is *not of a double heart*. Fifty thousand of them.

## Benjamin's

*Saul's own kinsmen: 3,000, **though most of them had stayed loyal to the house of
Saul until then**.*

The chronicler is honest about it. Three thousand out of Saul's own tribe, and he
notes that the rest had not come.

## What the chapter ends with

*All these men of war, who could keep ranks, came to Hebron with a loyal heart, to
make David king over all Israel… and there was joy in Israel.*

And they *were with David three days, eating and drinking, for their brethren had
prepared for them.*

A three-day feast, with food sent down from as far as Naphtali on donkeys, camels,
mules and oxen.

> **What David did not know how to read.** *When spring came around, the season when
> kings ride out to war… **but David stayed behind in Jerusalem**.* One sentence, and
> the chapter that follows is Bathsheba. See the note on 2 Samuel 11:1.
