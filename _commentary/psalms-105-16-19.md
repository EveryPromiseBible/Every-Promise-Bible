---
key: Psalms 105:16–19
title: Until the word tested him
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Four verses on Joseph, and the last line is the one nobody expects.

## Who sent the famine

*He **called down a famine** on the land. He cut off every supply of bread.*

The psalm assigns it to God directly. Not permitted, not foreseen — *called*, as you would call
a servant. See the note on 2 Kings 8:1.

## Who sent Joseph

*He **sent a man ahead of them**, Joseph, sold as a slave.*

The brothers sold him. The psalm says God sent him, in the same sentence, and does not
reconcile the two.

*"**It was not you who sent me here, but God.**"* Joseph's own words. And: *you meant it for
evil, but God meant it for good.* See the note on Genesis 45:1–8.

## What happened to him

*They **shackled his feet**, they **clamped iron on his neck**.*

The Hebrew of the second clause is *his soul came into iron*. Genesis does not record either
detail; the psalm supplies them.

## The clause the passage turns on

*Until the time his word came true, **until the LORD's own promise had tested him**.*

The Hebrew is **tzerafathu** — refined him. It is the metallurgist's word, used of separating
silver from ore.

The dreams did not comfort him through those years. They **tested** him. He had a word from
God and thirteen years in which nothing about it was true, and the psalm says the word was
doing something to him the whole time.

*That the genuineness of your faith, being much more precious than gold that perishes, **though
it is tested by fire**, may be found to praise, honour and glory at the revelation of **Jesus
Christ**.*

## What comes next in the psalm

*The king sent and released him… **he made him lord of his house**.*

Three verses of prison and one of promotion, in a psalm about God keeping a promise to Abraham.

> **What the word did.** Not carried him. *Refined* him — the metallurgy word. He had a promise
> and thirteen years in which nothing about it looked true, and the psalm says the promise was
> the furnace.
