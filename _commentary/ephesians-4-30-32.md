Three verses: a warning, a clear-out, and a standard.

## The warning

*And **do not bring sorrow to the Holy Spirit of God**.*

The Greek is **lupeite** — to grieve, to cause pain. It is the ordinary word for making
somebody sad.

Not *do not anger*. Grief is what you cause someone who is attached to you.

Isaiah says the same of the wilderness generation: *they rebelled and **grieved His Holy
Spirit***.

## What the Spirit is called

*The Spirit who **sealed you and marked you as God's own** for the day when **He sets you
free for good**.*

The Greek is **esphragisthete** — sealed. A seal in that world marked ownership and
guaranteed that something would arrive intact.

Paul has already used it twice in the letter: *you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of
promise, who is the guarantee of our inheritance.* See the note on Ephesians 1:7–10.

**For the day** — the sealing points forward. The warning sits inside a statement of
security.

## The list

*Put it all away from you: **bitterness, rage, anger, shouting, and words that tear a
person's name down** — **every last trace of spite** along with it.*

Six items, and the Greek runs from the inside out:

- **Pikria** — bitterness, the settled kind
- **Thumos** — rage, a flare
- **Orge** — anger, the slower-burning sort
- **Krauge** — clamour, shouting
- **Blasphemia** — slander, speech that damages a name
- **Kakia** — malice, ill-will, and Paul adds *with all*

The first two are internal, the middle two come out of the mouth, and the last is the
root the rest grow from.

## The three positives

*Be **kind** to one another instead, **soft-hearted**, **forgiving** one another.*

- **Chrestoi** — kind, useful, good to be around
- **Eusplanchnoi** — from **splanchna**, the guts. Tender-hearted, moved in the inward
  parts. The same root as the compassion Jesus felt looking at the crowds. See the note on
  Matthew 9:35–38.
- **Charizomenoi** — from **charis**, grace. To forgive as a gift, to cancel freely

## The measure

*Just as **God, in Christ, forgave you**.*

Not *so that* God will forgive. **Just as** He already did.

The Greek is aorist — a completed act. The forgiving named as the standard is in the past
tense.

Paul says it the same way in Colossians: *forgiving one another… **even as Christ forgave
you**, so you also must do.*

## The verb Paul chooses

**Charizomai**, not the courtroom word. It is what you do with a debt you have decided to
hand over rather than collect — the verb Luke uses of the two debtors, and Paul uses of
God cancelling the note nailed to the cross. See the notes on Luke 7:40–43 and
Colossians 2:11–15.

## What comes next

*Therefore **be imitators of God, as dear children**. And **walk in love**, as Christ also
has loved us and given Himself for us.* See the note on Ephesians 5:1–2.

The chapter break hides the connection. The forgiving is the last item before the
imitating, and the imitating is the *therefore*.

> **Where the standard is set.** Not at a level of effort. Paul names an act that has
> already happened to the reader and says the forgiving is to match it.
