God quotes His own private thought, and it is about a name He hoped to be called.

## The construction

*I had said **to Myself**.*

The Hebrew is *I said*, and what follows is an interior sentence. Jeremiah records it as
overheard.

## The intention

*"**How gladly would I set you among My children** and **give you a pleasant land, the
finest inheritance of any nation**."*

The Hebrew is *how shall I put you among the children*.

**A pleasant land** — the Hebrew is *a land of desire*, **eretz chemdah**.

## The expectation

*"And **I thought you would call Me 'My Father'** and **never turn away from Me**."*

The Hebrew is *you shall call Me My Father*.

The whole weight of the passage is on a form of address.

## Why that is unusual

God is called Father in the Old Testament, but rarely, and almost always of the nation
rather than by an individual.

Isaiah: *doubtless You are our Father, though Abraham was ignorant of us*. And: *You, O
LORD, are our Father, our Redeemer from Everlasting is Your name.*

It is not a common form of address in those pages. Here God says it is what He was hoping
for.

## The turn

*"But **as a wife betrays her husband, so you betrayed Me**, house of Israel."*

The Hebrew is **bagad** — to act treacherously, to deal falsely.

The whole of Jeremiah 3 runs on this image: Israel as an unfaithful wife, and the chapter
opens with the law about a man who cannot take back a wife who has married another.

## The two metaphors together

The passage moves from father-and-child to husband-and-wife in one sentence, and does not
smooth the transition.

Both are used because both are covenant relations that can be broken from one side only.

## What God says immediately after

*"**Return, you backsliding children**, and I will heal your backslidings."*

And two verses on: *"Return, O backsliding Israel," says the LORD; "**I will not cause My
anger to fall on you**. For I am merciful," says the LORD; "I will not remain angry
forever."*

The Hebrew for *merciful* is **chasid** — from **chesed**, covenant loyalty.

## What He asks for

*"**Only acknowledge your iniquity**, that you have transgressed against the LORD your
God."*

One thing. The chapter does not set terms beyond it.

## Where the Father-name goes

*"**Our Father in heaven**, hallowed be Your name."* See the note on Matthew 6:9–13.

And **Abba**, in Gethsemane and in two of Paul's letters, left untranslated both times.
See the note on Galatians 4:4–7.

Paul says the Spirit puts it in the mouth: *God has sent forth the Spirit of His Son into
your hearts, crying out, "Abba, Father!"*

## Who Jeremiah was writing to

Judah in its last decades, before Babylon. He was told at his call that nobody would
listen, and he preached for forty years anyway.

> **What God says He was hoping for.** Not obedience, in this sentence. A name — that they
> would call Him Father — and He says it as a thought He had to Himself.
