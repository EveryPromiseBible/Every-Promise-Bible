Four contrasts, in identical grammar, about the same body.

## Where this comes

Somebody in Corinth had asked *how are the dead raised up? And with what body do they come?*

Paul's first answer is blunt: *foolish one, what you sow is not made alive unless it dies.*

Then the wheat: *you do not sow that body that shall be, but mere grain.*

## The verb

*What is **sown**.*

Four times, and the word is **speiretai** — sown, as seed is.

Burial is called sowing. Nobody plants a seed intending to leave it there.

## The first pair

*What is sown **decays**; what is raised is **beyond the reach of decay**.*

The Greek is **phthora** and **aphtharsia** — corruption and incorruption.

The same pair Peter uses of the seed the believer was born from. See the note on
1 Peter 1:23.

## The second pair

*What is sown goes into the ground **in dishonor**; what is raised comes up **in glory**.*

The Greek is **atimia** and **doxa**.

Dishonour is an honest word for a corpse. Paul does not soften what a burial is.

## The third pair

*What is sown is **weak**; what is raised is **full of power**.*

**Astheneia** and **dunamis**.

## The fourth pair

*What is sown is a **natural body**; what is raised is a **spiritual body**.*

The Greek is **soma psuchikon** and **soma pneumatikon**.

**Psuchikos** is from **psuche**, soul — a soul-animated body. The older versions have
*natural*.

## What *spiritual body* does not mean

Not immaterial. Both nouns are **soma**, body, and Paul repeats the sentence to make sure:
*there is a natural body, and there is a spiritual body*.

The adjective describes what animates it, not what it is made of.

Luke's account has Him settling exactly this: *a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you
can see that I have* — and then He eats a piece of fish. See the note on Luke 24:36–43.

## What continues

Paul's whole image is agricultural, which means continuity. The wheat that comes up is
related to the grain that went in, and is not identical to it.

*God gives it a body as He pleases, and to each seed its own body.*

## The two men

*The first man Adam became a living being; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit.*

*As was the man of dust, so also are those who are made of dust; and as is the heavenly Man,
so also are those who are heavenly.*

*And as we have borne the image of the man of dust, we shall also bear the image of the
heavenly Man.*

## What comes after

*Now this I say, brethren, that flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God.* See the
note on 1 Corinthians 15:50–52.

And *behold, I tell you a mystery: we shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed*.

## Where the chapter ends

*Therefore, my beloved brethren, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the
Lord, knowing that **your labour is not in vain in the Lord**.*

Fifty-seven verses on resurrection bodies, and the application is about work.

## The parallel elsewhere

*Who will **transform our lowly body that it may be conformed to His glorious body**.* See
the note on Philippians 3:20–21.

The pattern is a body that has already been raised, and Luke says it could be touched.

## Whose body the pattern is

*And as we have borne the image of the man of dust, **we shall also bear the image of the heavenly
Man**.*

Five verses later, and it names the model. The four contrasts — corruption to incorruption,
dishonour to glory, weakness to power, natural to spiritual — are not abstractions; **Paul** is
describing a body that already exists.

*Who will transform our lowly body that it may be conformed to **His glorious body**.*

*Beloved, now we are children of God; and it has not yet been revealed what we shall be, but we know
that **when He is revealed, we shall be like Him**, for we shall see Him as He is.*

## The firstfruits

*But now **Christ is risen from the dead, and has become the firstfruits of those who have fallen
asleep**.*

**Aparche** — the first sheaf, waved as a pledge that the harvest is coming. The chapter's whole
argument rests on one resurrection having already happened.

*And if **Christ** is not risen, your believing is futile; you are still in your sins.*

## What the chapter ends on

*"O Death, where is your sting?"… The sting of death is sin, and the strength of sin is the law. But
**thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ**.*

> **What the four pairs describe.** Not a replacement. Paul's word for burial is *sown* —
> and nothing about sowing implies that what comes up is a different thing.
