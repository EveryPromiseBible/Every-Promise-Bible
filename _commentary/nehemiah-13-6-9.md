He comes back, finds out what has happened, and empties the room himself.

## Where he had been

*In the **thirty-second year of Artaxerxes** king of Babylon I **had returned to the
king**.*

He had said he would. Back in chapter 2 the king asked how long he would be gone and
*I set him a time*.

Twelve years as governor, and then he went back to his post at court, exactly as
promised.

## The leave

*After some time I **asked his leave and came back to Jerusalem**.*

He asks a second time. The whole enterprise runs on permissions correctly obtained.

## What he found

*There I learned **the wrong that Eliashib had done for Tobiah**, making him a room in
the courts of the house of God.*

The Hebrew for *wrong* is **ra'ah** — evil.

He names the priest and he names the beneficiary. No euphemism for either.

## His reaction

*It **grieved me deeply**.*

The same construction used of Sanballat and Tobiah when they heard he was coming — *it
grieved them exceedingly*. See the note on Nehemiah 2:9–10.

The book uses the identical phrase for both sides.

## What he did

*So I **threw all of Tobiah's belongings out of the room**.*

The Hebrew is *I cast out*. Personally, by hand, into the courtyard.

No hearing, no negotiation with Eliashib, no committee. The furniture goes out the door.

Jesus does the same thing to the same courts five centuries later, with a whip of cords
and the tables of the money changers. See the note on John 2:13–17.

## The cleansing

*Then I gave orders, and **they cleansed the rooms**.*

The Hebrew is the ritual word — **taher**, the term for purifying what has been defiled.

Occupation by an Ammonite made the storeroom unclean, and it had to be made fit before
anything could go back.

## What he brought back

*And I brought back into them **the vessels of the house of God, with the grain
offering and the incense**.*

The same items listed in verse 5 as having been displaced, restored in the same order.

He reverses the transaction item by item.

## What he does next

He finds the Levites gone to their fields, confronts the officials — *why is the house
of God forsaken?* — gathers the people back, and *all Judah brought the tithe of the
grain and the new wine and the oil to the storehouse.*

Then he appoints treasurers over the stores, *for they were considered faithful*, so it
cannot happen again while he is away.

## The refrain

*Remember me, O my God, concerning this, and **do not wipe out my good deeds** that I
have done for the house of my God.*

He says a version of it four times in this chapter. It is how the book ends.

> **What the whole chapter turns on.** A room. Not the wall, not an army, not a siege —
> a storeroom reassigned while the governor was out of the country, and the singers
> going home because there was nothing in it.
