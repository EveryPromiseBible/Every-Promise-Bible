Abram is asleep, and God tells him four hundred years of his family's history
before any of it exists.

## The darkness

*A deep sleep fell over Abram, and **a thick, terrifying darkness** settled on
him.*

The Hebrew for *deep sleep* is **tardemah** — the same word used of Adam when the
woman was taken out of his side.

He is unconscious for the covenant. What follows in the chapter is a smoking
firepot and a burning torch passing between the divided animals, and only one party
walks the path. Abram is asleep for the whole thing.

## The four hundred years

*"Your descendants will live as strangers in a land not their own. **They will be
enslaved and mistreated for four hundred years**."*

He is told in advance. The slavery is not a surprise God responds to later; it is
announced to Abram while Isaac is not yet born.

## And the sentence after it

*"**But I will judge the nation that enslaves them, and afterward your people will
come out with great wealth**."*

**Great wealth** — the Hebrew is a large substance, heavy possessions.

Four hundred years of unpaid work, and God says at the start how the account will
be settled. What happened on the night of the exodus was not a windfall; it was the
back wages, promised centuries before the first brick was laid.

Exodus records it plainly: *the LORD had made the Egyptians so favourable toward
His people that they gave them whatever they asked. And so Israel walked out
carrying the wealth of Egypt.* See the note on Exodus 12:33–36.

## Where it sits in Abram's life

Two chapters earlier, Abram had come back from rescuing Lot, met Melchizedek, and
*gave him a tenth of everything.*

He refused a penny of the king of Sodom's — *I will not take a thread or a
sandal-strap, lest you should say, "I have made Abram rich."* He tithed to the
priest and turned down the king.

And then, in chapter 15, God says *I am your shield, your **exceedingly great
reward*** — and lays out the four hundred years and the great wealth on the far
side of it.

## "You will go to your ancestors in peace"

In the middle of a prophecy about slavery, one line addressed to the man himself:
*you will be buried in a good old age.*

He is not going to see any of it. He is told anyway.

## The Amorites

*"In the fourth generation your descendants will return here, **for the wrongdoing
of the Amorites is not yet complete**."*

The reason for the delay is not about Israel at all. Another people is being given
four centuries.

> **Who walked between the pieces.** In that culture both parties to a covenant
> passed between the divided animals, invoking the same fate on themselves if they
> broke it. Abram was asleep. Only the fire went through, which means the covenant
> was cut on one signature.
