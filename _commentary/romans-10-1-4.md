Paul says what he wants, and it is not an argument won.

## The desire

*The **desire of my heart** and **my prayer to God** for Israel is **that they may be
saved**.*

The Greek for *desire* is **eudokia** — good pleasure, the thing he would choose.

He has just written three chapters about Israel's unbelief, and he opens the fourth by
saying what he prays.

## What he had said a chapter earlier

*I have great sorrow and continual grief in my heart. For **I could wish that I myself
were accursed from Christ for my brethren**, my countrymen according to the flesh.*

He is not arguing against people he dislikes.

## The credit he gives

*I can **testify about them that they have a zeal for God**.*

The Greek is **zelos** — zeal, fervour. Paul is not accusing them of indifference, and he
knew the zeal from the inside: *concerning zeal, persecuting the church.*

## The qualification

*But it is **a zeal that runs ahead of knowledge**.*

The Greek is *not according to **epignosis*** — full or accurate knowledge.

The energy is real and the information is missing.

## The diagnosis, in three clauses

*Not **recognizing the right standing that comes from God**.*

*And busy **establishing one of their own**.*

*They **did not submit themselves** to the righteousness of God.*

The Greek for *establishing* is **stesai** — to set up, to make stand. It is construction
work.

## The word "submit"

**Hupetagesan** — to place oneself under, to be subordinated.

Receiving a righteousness that is not yours is described as a submission. Building one of
your own is described as the alternative.

Nothing in the three clauses is about immorality.

## The conclusion

*For **Christ is the end of the law**, so that there is **a right standing with God for
everyone who believes**.*

The Greek is **telos** — the end, and also the goal, the finish line, the point a thing
was aiming at.

Both senses are live. Where the road stops, and what it was going toward.

## The word "everyone"

Paul repeats it through the chapter: *everyone who believes*, *whoever calls on the name
of the LORD shall be saved*, and *there is no distinction between Jew and Greek, for the
same Lord over all is rich to all who call upon Him.*

## What follows

The passage about the word being near: *the word is near you, in your mouth and in your
heart… **if you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart** that
God has raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.*

Which he quotes out of Deuteronomy 30 — the law testifying to a righteousness apart from
it. See the note on Romans 3:21–22.

## The questions at the end

*How then shall they call on Him in whom they have not believed? And how shall they
believe in Him of whom they have not heard? And how shall they hear without a preacher?*

The chapter that opens with a prayer ends by asking who is going to tell them.

> **What is wrong in the passage.** Not the zeal, and not the sincerity. The clause Paul
> keeps circling is *establishing one of their own* — and he calls receiving the other
> one a submission.
