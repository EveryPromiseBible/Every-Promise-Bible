---
key: Ephesians 2:14–16
title: He Himself is our peace
updated: 2026-08-07
---

Not that He makes peace. That He is it.

## The sentence

*For **He Himself is our peace**.*

The Greek is **autos gar estin hē eirēnē hēmōn**.

Not *He brings* peace, and not *He gives* it. The predicate is a person.

## The two

*He has made **the two into one**.*

The Greek is **ta amphotera hen** — the both, one. Neuter.

Jew and Gentile, which the passage has just described: *you were at that time without Christ… having
no hope and without God in the world*. See the note on Ephesians 2:11–13.

## The wall

*And He has **torn down the dividing wall that stood between them**.*

The Greek is **to mesotoichon tou phragmou** — the middle wall of the fence.

## What wall

The temple had a low barrier separating the Court of the Gentiles from the inner courts.

Inscriptions on it, two of which have been recovered, warned in Greek that any foreigner passing
beyond it had only himself to blame for the death that followed.

Paul was arrested for allegedly taking a Greek past it.

## What was abolished

*Doing away **in His own flesh** with **the hostility, the law with its commands and its rules**.*

The Greek is **ton nomon tōn entolōn en dogmasin** — the law of commandments in decrees.

## What that means

Not the moral demand as such. The regulatory system whose function was to keep the two groups
apart: circumcision, food, calendar, purity.

The barrier was doing its job. The job is over.

## The location

*In His own flesh.*

The wall does not come down by legislation. It is dismantled in a body.

## The purpose

*So that **in Himself He might make out of the two one new man**.*

The Greek is **hena kainon anthrōpon** — one new human.

**Kainos** — new in kind, not merely recent.

## What is not made

Not a Gentile absorbed into Israel, and not a Jew becoming a Gentile.

A third thing that did not exist before.

## The second purpose

*And so that He might **bring the two of them back to God in one body through the cross**.*

The Greek is **apokatallaxē** — a double-compound of reconcile. To thoroughly change back.

## The last clause

*Killing the hostility there.*

The Greek is **apokteinas tēn echthran en autō** — having killed the enmity in it.

The enmity is the thing that dies at the cross, in the sentence's grammar.

## What follows

*And He came and **preached peace to you who were afar off and to those who were near**.* See the note
on Ephesians 2:17–18.

*For through Him we both have access by one Spirit to the Father.*

**Prosagōgē** — introduction, right of approach.

## And then

*Now, therefore, you are **no longer strangers and foreigners**, but fellow citizens with the saints
and members of the household of God.* See the note on Ephesians 2:19–20.

The building image follows the demolition image, in consecutive verses.

## Where the word comes from

*How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who **brings good news, who proclaims peace**.*
See the note on Isaiah 52:7.

> **The predicate.** *He Himself is our peace* — not the one who arranges it. The peace between the
> two groups is identified with a person, and the wall came down in His body.
