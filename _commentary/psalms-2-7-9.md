The verse the New Testament quotes about the resurrection.

## The decree

*Let me tell you what the LORD decreed. He said to me, "**You are My son. This very day I have
become your Father.**"*

The Hebrew is **beni attah, ani hayyom yelidtikha** — You are My son; today I have begotten
You.

## Who quotes it, and about what

*God has fulfilled this for us their children, in that **He has raised up Jesus**. As it is also
written in the second Psalm: "**You are My Son, today I have begotten You**."*

**Paul**, in Antioch, applies *today* to the resurrection.

*For to which of the angels did He ever say: "**You are My Son, today I have begotten You**"?*

**Hebrews** opens with it to prove the Son is not an angel — and quotes it again in chapter 5
to prove He did not appoint Himself High Priest: *so also Christ did not glorify Himself to
become High Priest, but it was He who said to Him, "You are My Son."*

It is the most-quoted verse of the Psalter in the New Testament after Psalm 110.

## What the Son is told to ask for

*"**Only ask**, and I will hand you the nations for your inheritance, the ends of the earth to
be your own."*

The inheritance is not seized. It is asked for, and the asking is the whole condition.

*"**Ask of Me**"* — and *"go therefore and **make disciples of all the nations**"*.

## The iron sceptre

*"You will rule them with an iron sceptre, break them like a potter's clay jar."*

**Revelation** takes the line three times, and once hands it over: *he who overcomes and keeps
My works until the end, **to him I will give power over the nations** — he shall rule them with
a rod of iron.*

## What the psalm is doing here

Psalm 1 describes a man; Psalm 2 describes a King. The two open the Psalter together and have
no titles, and the New Testament reads the second as a coronation.

> **What *today* turned out to mean.** Paul stood up in Antioch, quoted this verse, and dated
> it — not to a birth, but to a morning outside a tomb.
