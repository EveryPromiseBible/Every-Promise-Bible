Two lines that say the same thing twice, with the object raised.

## The first

*It is **better to shelter in the LORD than to lean on any human**.*

The Hebrew is **tov lachasot baYHWH mibbetoach ba'adam**.

**Chasah** — to take refuge, to flee for shelter. The verb of a small animal getting under
something.

**Batach** — to trust, to lean on, to feel secure in.

Two different verbs. Not the same word repeated.

## The second

*It is better to shelter in the LORD than to **lean on princes**.*

**Nedivim** — nobles, princes, the willing and generous ones.

The step up from **adam**, mankind generally, to the people who actually have power to help.

## What the pair does

The psalm does not say people are useless. It says that even the ones who could genuinely do
something are the lesser option.

Raising the object and keeping the verdict identical is the whole argument.

## Where the verse sits

Traditionally described as the middle verse of the Bible by chapter count, though the arithmetic
depends on which chapters are counted.

The position is a coincidence of arrangement. The line stands on its own.

## What the psalm has just said

*The LORD is on my side; **I will not fear. What can man do to me?***

*It is better to trust in the LORD…*

The question and the verse answer each other.

## Where the psalm has been

*Oh, give thanks to the LORD, for He is good! For **His mercy endures forever**.*

The refrain repeated four times at the start, the same line that runs twenty-six times through
Psalm 136. See the note on Psalms 136:4–9.

*I called on the LORD in distress; the LORD answered me and **set me in a broad place**.*

The Hebrew is **merchav** — a wide space, room to move.

## Where the psalm goes

*All nations surrounded me, but in the name of the LORD I will destroy them.*

*You pushed me violently, that I might fall, **but the LORD helped me**.*

*I shall not die, but live, and declare the works of the LORD.*

## The stone

*The stone which the builders rejected **has become the chief cornerstone**. This was the LORD's
doing; it is marvellous in our eyes.* See the note on Psalms 118:22–24.

Quoted by Jesus at the end of the parable of the vineyard, by Peter before the Sanhedrin, and by
Peter again in his letter.

## The day

*This is the day the LORD has made; we will rejoice and be glad in it.*

The verse follows immediately from the cornerstone. It is not a general observation about
mornings — it is about the day the rejected stone was set in place.

## What was sung at the entry

*Blessed is he who comes in the name of the LORD!*

The crowd on the road into Jerusalem with palm branches was quoting this psalm.

## What was sung at the table

Psalm 118 closes the Hallel, sung at Passover.

*And when they had sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives.*

The last thing recorded of Jesus singing is likely this psalm, ending: *Oh, give thanks to the
LORD, for He is good! For His mercy endures forever.*

> **The two verbs.** **Chasah** — to take shelter under something. **Batach** — to lean your
> weight on it. The psalm does not use the same word for both halves.
