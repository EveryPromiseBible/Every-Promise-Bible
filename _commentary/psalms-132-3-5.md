A vow, quoted in a pilgrim song, and it is about accommodation.

## What the psalm is

One of the Songs of Ascents — Psalms 120 to 134, sung going up to Jerusalem.

The longest of the fifteen, and the only one that is a piece of history.

It opens: *LORD, remember David and all his afflictions; how he swore to the LORD, and vowed to
the Mighty One of Jacob*.

## The vow

*I will **not enter my house**, nor **climb onto my bed**.*

Four negatives, escalating.

*I will give **no sleep to my eyes**, **no rest to my eyelids**.*

The Hebrew is **tenumah** — slumber, drowsiness. The lighter word.

Not just no sleep. Not even dozing.

## The condition

*Until I **find a place for the LORD**, a **dwelling for the Mighty One of Jacob**.*

The Hebrew is **maqom** — a place. And **mishkanot**, dwellings, from the root **shakan**, to
settle — the word behind **shekinah**.

## Where the vow is not recorded

Nowhere in Samuel or Chronicles.

The narrative records the intention — *see now, I dwell in a house of cedar, but the ark of God
dwells inside tent curtains* — and Nathan's answer, and God's reply: *would you build a house
for Me to dwell in?*

The psalm preserves the oath itself, which the histories do not.

## What David was living in

*A house of cedar.* Hiram of Tyre had sent carpenters, masons and timber and built it for him.

The contrast is the thing that started it. He is in a permanent building and the ark is under
canvas.

## What God says to the offer

*"**The LORD will make you a house**."*

The offer is refused and reversed. David proposes to build God a house; God announces He will
build David one.

*"And your house and your kingdom shall be established forever before you. Your throne shall be
established forever."*

## What David does instead

Buys the site — Araunah's threshing floor, *I will not offer burnt offerings to the LORD my God
with that which costs me nothing* — and stockpiles the materials.

*Now I have prepared with all my might for the house of my God.*

He gathers gold, silver, bronze, iron, timber and stone for a building he is told he will not
put up.

## Why he could not

*"You have shed much blood and have made great wars… **you shall not build a house for My
name**."*

And: *"**Solomon your son shall build My house**."*

The vow in the psalm is kept by somebody else.

## What he did put up

A tent in Jerusalem, and the ark in it. See the note on
2 Samuel 6:12–15.

Not the tabernacle of Moses, which stayed at Gibeon. A second tent, with singers around the
ark day and night, and no veil in front of it.

## Where the psalm goes next

*We heard of it in Ephrathah. We found it in the fields of Jaar.* See the note on
Psalms 132:6–7.

The search, and then the finding — in a house on a hill where it had sat for a generation.

## The answer to the vow

*For the LORD has **chosen Zion**. He has **desired it for His home**. **This is My resting
place forever**.* See the note on Psalms 132:13–14.

David swore he would not rest until he found God a resting place.

The psalm's second half is God saying He has found one.

## What he swore

*"**I will not give sleep to my eyes** or slumber to my eyelids, **until I find a place for the
LORD**, a dwelling place for the Mighty One of Jacob."*

David swears off sleep until God has a house. It is the most generous vow in the Psalter and
God does not accept it — *you shall not build a house for My name*. See the note on
2 Samuel 7:1–3.

## What he is told instead

*The LORD will build you a house.*

The whole exchange turns on **bayit** meaning both a building and a dynasty. The man who
offered to give is given to.

*Or who has first given to Him and it shall be repaid to him? **For of Him and through Him and
to Him are all things**.*

## The other sleepless night

*And He was in the stern, asleep on a pillow.*

*"Foxes have holes and birds of the air have nests, but **the Son of Man has nowhere to lay His
head**."*

David could not sleep until God had a place to live. **Jesus** had none Himself.

## Where the house ends up

*And the Word became flesh and **dwelt among us**.* The Greek is **eskenosen** — pitched His
tent.

*Do you not know that **you are the temple of God**?*

> **What the vow asks for.** Not victory, not a dynasty, not an heir. A place for somebody else
> to live — and the man swearing it was sleeping in a cedar house at the time.
