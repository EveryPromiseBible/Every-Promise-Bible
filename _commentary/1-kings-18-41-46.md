Elijah announces rain to the king before anything has changed in the sky, and
then goes up the mountain to pray it in.

## The order

*"Go on up — eat and drink, for **I hear the sound of a heavy rain coming**."*

There is no rain. There has been none for three and a half years, and the sky is
clear. He says he hears it anyway, tells the king to go and eat, and only then
climbs Carmel to pray.

The announcement comes before the answer, and the praying comes after the
announcement.

## The posture

*He bent down to the ground and put his face between his knees.*

Not a dignified stance. The man who had just called fire out of heaven in front
of a nation folds himself onto the ground and stays there.

## Seven times

*"Go look toward the sea"… "There's nothing"… Seven times Elijah said, "Go back
and look again."*

Six times the report comes back empty. He does not adjust the announcement or
conclude he misheard. He sends the servant again.

And what finally arrives is *a cloud as small as a man's hand* — the least
impressive possible confirmation, and enough. He immediately sends word to Ahab
to get down the mountain before the rain stops him.

## What happened to him afterwards

*The power of the LORD came on Elijah — he tucked his cloak into his belt and ran
ahead of Ahab's chariot all the way to Jezreel.*

Roughly seventeen miles, on foot, ahead of a horse-drawn chariot, by a man who
had been awake since before dawn and had fasted through a contest and a
slaughter. The chapter records it in one sentence.

> **What he was praying for.** God had already told him, at the start of the
> chapter, *I will send rain on the land.* The promise was given first; Elijah
> announced it, then prayed it down, then sent seven times for the evidence. The
> praying was not to persuade God of something. It was to bring in what had
> already been said.
