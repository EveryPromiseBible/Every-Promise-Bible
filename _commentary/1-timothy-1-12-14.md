---
key: 1 Timothy 1:12–14
title: "Reckoned trustworthy before he was"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul introduces himself to Timothy's church by reading out his own charge sheet, and the verb in the
middle of it is the surprising one.

## He counted me faithful

*Christ Jesus our Lord… **for counting me worth trusting** and setting me down in the middle of His
work.*

**Piston me hegesato** — He reckoned me faithful.

Now read the next clause: *me, who had **cursed His name, hunted His people and handled them with
violence***. Blasphemer, persecutor, and **hybristes** — the violent aggressor, the man who enjoys
the damage.

Christ reckoned him trustworthy while that was the true description. The reckoning came first and
the trustworthiness followed it, which is the same order as everything else in Paul's gospel: God
*calls those things which do not exist as though they did*.

## Mercy, and the reason given

*But **mercy found me**, because I did it all in ignorance, outside of faith.*

He does not offer the ignorance as an excuse — two verses later he calls himself the chief of
sinners in the present tense. He offers it as the reason mercy had room to move.

And **eleethen** is passive. Mercy was done to him. Nobody obtains mercy by qualifying for it;
the word means the opposite of that.

## The word he had to build

*And **the grace of our Lord came flooding over me**.*

**Hyperepleonasen** — a verb with two prefixes stacked on it. **Pleonazo** is already *to abound*;
**hyper** puts it over the top; and Paul apparently could not find anything on the shelf that would
carry the load, so he welded them together.

It occurs once in the Greek Bible. He made it for this sentence.

## Even the faith was not his

*And with it **the faith and the love that are in Christ Jesus**.*

The grace arrived carrying them. Paul does not say the grace met his faith halfway; he says faith
and love came in the same delivery, and locates them **en Christo Iesou** — they were already in
Christ, and he received them by being put there.

That closes the last door. If a man could produce the faith himself, there would be one item on the
inventory he could boast about.

> **The chief of sinners, present tense.** *Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, **of
> whom I am chief**.* Years into an apostleship, writing Scripture, and he says *am* rather than
> *was* — not because the mercy is uncertain but because he never moved off the ground he was saved
> on. Nobody graduates from grace onto some steadier footing. There isn't one.
