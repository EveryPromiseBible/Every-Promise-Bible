A foreign widow gleaning at the edge of a field is called over to the table.

## Where she is

Boaz's field, in Bethlehem, at the start of the barley harvest.

She had asked permission to glean — *let me glean and gather after the reapers* — which the law
permitted to the poor, the widow and the foreigner.

*And she happened to come to the part of the field belonging to Boaz.* See the note on
Ruth 2:1–3.

## What he had already said to her

*"Do not go to glean in another field… **stay close by my young women**."*

*"Have I not commanded the young men not to touch you? And when you are thirsty, go to the vessels
and drink from what the young men have drawn."*

*"**The LORD repay your work, and a full reward be given you by the LORD God of Israel, under whose
wings you have come for refuge**."* See the note on Ruth 2:11–12.

## The invitation

*When it was time to eat, Boaz called to her, "**Come here. Have some bread**."*

The Hebrew is **goshi halom** — draw near here.

A gleaner ate what she brought, at the edge, apart. He calls her in.

## The dish

*"**Dip your piece in the wine vinegar**."*

The Hebrew is **chometz** — sour wine, vinegar.

The harvesters' drink. Thin, sharp, and what field labourers had at noon in the heat.

The soldiers offered the same thing at the cross: *they filled a sponge with sour wine*.

## Where she sits

*So she **sat down beside the harvesters**.*

The Hebrew is **mitzad haqqotzerim** — at the side of the reapers.

Not at the edge of the field. Beside the workers, with the people who belong there.

## What he does

*And **he handed her roasted grain**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyitzbot-lah qali** — and he held out to her parched grain.

The verb **tzavat** occurs once in the Hebrew Bible, here. Its sense is to reach out and hand
across.

He passes it to her himself. Not through a servant.

## The result

*She **ate until she was satisfied — and still had some left over**.*

The Hebrew is **vattochal vattisba vattotar**.

Three verbs: she ate, she was satisfied, she had left over.

## What she does with the surplus

*And she brought out and gave to her mother-in-law what she had kept back after she had been
satisfied.*

Naomi is at home. Ruth carries the leftovers back.

## What Boaz does next

*"**Let her glean even among the sheaves**, and do not reproach her. Also **let grain from the bundles
fall purposely for her**, and leave it that she may glean, and do not rebuke her."* See the note on
Ruth 2:15–16.

The Hebrew is **shol tasholu lah** — pulling out, you shall pull out for her.

Instructions given to the young men, out of her hearing.

## The measure

*So she gleaned in the field until evening, and beat out what she had gleaned, and it was **about an
ephah of barley**.* See the note on Ruth 2:17–18.

An ephah is around thirty pounds — perhaps two weeks' food, from one day's gleaning.

## What Naomi says

*"**Blessed be he of the LORD, who has not forsaken His kindness to the living and the dead**!"*

And: *"**This man is a relation of ours, one of our close relatives**."*

The Hebrew is **miggo'alenu** — of our redeemers. See the note on
Leviticus 25:23–28.

Naomi, who came home saying *call me Mara*, blesses somebody for the first time.

> **The three verbs.** She ate, she was satisfied, and she had some left. The last one is what she
> carries home to a woman who said she came back empty.
