Paul has just listed his own credentials — circumcised the eighth day, tribe of
Benjamin, a Pharisee, blameless as to the law — and called the whole file rubbish.
This verse is what he wants instead.

## The two righteousnesses

*Not wearing a righteousness of my own that comes from the law, but the one that
comes through faith in Christ, the righteousness that comes from God and rests on
faith.*

He sets them side by side:

| his own | the other |
|---|---|
| from the law | from God |
| produced | received |
| worn as his own | found in Him |

Paul does not say his own record was fake. By the law's own measure he says he was
blameless. He says it is the wrong kind of thing to be standing in.

## "Found in Him"

The verb is about being discovered in a place — where you turn out to be when
somebody looks.

Paul's ambition is that whenever the inspection comes, he is located inside
Christ rather than inside his own record. That is a statement about position, not
about effort.

## The word for the credentials

The term he used two verses earlier is deliberately coarse — refuse, what is
thrown to dogs. He is not saying his advantages were worthless in themselves; he
is describing what they became once he had something to compare them with.

*So that I may gain Christ* is the sentence they were traded for.

> **What he wants next.** The verse immediately after: *that I may know Him and
> the power of His resurrection, and share in His sufferings.* Having settled that
> the standing is received rather than earned, the thing he then reaches for is
> not more standing. It is knowing the person.
