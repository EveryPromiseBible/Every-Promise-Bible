Job remembering what his life used to be, and the memory is about how people
listened to him.

## The image

*They waited for me as for the rain, and opened their mouths wide as for the
spring showers.*

The **spring** showers — the latter rain, the one that filled out the grain just
before harvest. See the note on Deuteronomy 11:13–15.

Mouths open, upturned, waiting for something that only comes from above and
cannot be summoned. That is how he says people used to receive what he said.

## What his words did

*My words fell gently on them.*

The verb is the dripping or distilling of dew — the same picture Moses uses in
Deuteronomy 32 of his own teaching. Not a downpour. Something that settles.

And the effect: *after I had spoken they had nothing to add.*

## What he did with the standing

*I chose the road for them and sat at their head. I lived like a king among his
troops, **like one who comforts those who mourn**.*

The last phrase turns the whole passage. He has been describing authority — men
falling silent, princes deferring, a seat at the head — and he ends it by
comparing himself to someone consoling the grieving.

That is his own account of what the position was for.

## Why the chapter exists

Job is speaking in the past tense throughout. The next chapter begins *but now
they mock me* — men younger than him, whose fathers he would not have put with
his sheepdogs.

This is a man remembering, out loud, in the middle of losing everything.

> **What he was actually like.** The verses just above this passage are his own
> account: he rescued the poor, was eyes to the blind and feet to the lame, took
> up the case of the stranger, and broke the jaws of the wicked. The book's
> assessment of him at the start — blameless and upright — is not contradicted by
> anything he says here.
