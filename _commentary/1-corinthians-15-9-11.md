Paul says three things about himself in a row, and two of them cancel.

## The self-assessment

*For **I am the least of the apostles**.*

The Greek is **ho elachistos ton apostolon**.

The earliest of three such statements. Later it becomes *less than the least of all the saints*,
and later still *sinners, of whom I am chief*. See the note on Ephesians 3:8–9.

## The disqualification

*And I am **not even worthy to be called an apostle**, because **I persecuted the church of God**.*

The Greek is **ouk eimi hikanos** — I am not sufficient, not competent.

He does not soften the reason. **Edioxa** — I persecuted, hunted.

## What has just been said

The resurrection appearances, in order: Cephas, the twelve, five hundred at once, James, all the
apostles.

*Then last of all He was seen by me also, **as by one born out of due time**.*

The Greek is **to ektromati** — the miscarriage, the abortion.

He calls himself that.

## The turn

*But **by the grace of God I am what I am**.*

The Greek is **chariti de Theou eimi ho eimi**.

Six words in Greek. It is the whole answer to the previous two verses.

## What grace does not do

*And **His grace toward me was not in vain**.*

The Greek is **kene** — empty, void, without result.

Grace that produced nothing would have been empty. It did not.

## The claim

*I **laboured more abundantly than all of them**.*

The Greek is **perissoteron auton panton ekopiasa**.

**Kopiao** — to labour to exhaustion.

A comparative claim against every other apostle, made by the man who has just called himself a
miscarriage.

## The correction

*And yet **it was not I, but the grace of God which was with me**.*

The Greek is **ouk ego de alla he charis tou Theou he sun emoi**.

The sentence takes back the boast in the same breath.

## The three statements together

I am the least. I worked harder than all of them. It was not me.

The middle claim stands — he does not withdraw it — and it is placed between two denials of any
credit for it.

## The word

**Sun emoi** — with me.

Not *in me* or *through me*. Alongside. Grace is described as a co-worker.

## The conclusion

*So then, whether it was I or whether it was they, **this is what we preach, and this is what you
believed**.*

The Corinthians had been sorting themselves by teacher — *I am of Paul, I am of Apollos*. See the
note on 1 Corinthians 1:10–13.

Paul closes the paragraph by making the question of which apostle irrelevant.

## What the message was

*That Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, and that He was buried, and that **He
rose again the third day** according to the Scriptures.* See the note on
1 Corinthians 15:3–8.

## What he says elsewhere about the same thing

*I can do all things through Christ **who strengthens me**.* See the note on
Philippians 4:11–13.

*Not that we are sufficient of ourselves… but **our sufficiency is from God**.*

> **The middle claim.** *I laboured more than all of them* — placed between *I am the least* and
> *it was not I*. He does not withdraw the boast; he brackets it.
