Paul is dealing with a scandal, and he argues from what they already are.

## What the chapter is about

A man in the congregation living with his father's wife, and the church proud of how
tolerant it was being about it.

*And you are puffed up, and have not rather mourned.*

## The rebuke

*This **boasting of yours is not a good thing**.*

The Greek is **kauchema** — the thing boasted in.

## The proverb

*Do you not know that **a little yeast works its way through the whole batch of dough**?*

The Greek is **zume** — leaven. And **phurama**, the lump.

Paul quotes the same line in Galatians, about a different problem entirely — there it is
circumcision being smuggled back in.

## What leaven is doing in the argument

At Passover every scrap of leaven had to be searched out of the house before the feast
began. The seven days that follow are the Feast of Unleavened Bread.

Paul is writing to Gentiles in Corinth and expects them to follow the picture.

## The instruction

*Clear out the old yeast, then, so that you may be **a fresh batch — bread with no yeast in
it at all**.*

The Greek is **ekkatharate** — clean out thoroughly.

## The clause everything turns on

*Which is **what you already are**.*

The Greek is **kathos este azumoi** — as you are unleavened.

He tells them to become what he has just said they are. The command and the fact sit in the
same sentence, and the fact is the reason for the command.

Paul writes this to the church with more disorder in it than any other he corresponded
with — divisions, lawsuits, drunkenness at the Lord's table, chaos in the meetings.

He still calls them unleavened.

## The reason

*For **Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed for us**.*

The Greek is **etuthe** — a perfect passive. It has been sacrificed and the sacrifice
stands.

The blood on the doorposts is why the house is clean. See the note on Exodus 12:1–13.

And the Baptist, pointing: *behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world*.
See the note on John 1:29–34.

## The feast

*So **let us keep the feast** — not with the old yeast, nor with the **yeast of malice and
wickedness**, but with **bread made without yeast, the bread of sincerity and truth**.*

The Greek for *sincerity* is **eilikrineias** — from *judged by sunlight*. Held up to the
light with nothing hidden in it.

## The two leavens named

Malice and wickedness — **kakias** and **ponerias**.

Not the sexual sin the chapter is about. Paul names the attitudes of the congregation
watching it, not the act.

## What he tells them to do

Hand the man over — and the reason given is *that his spirit may be saved in the day of the
Lord Jesus*.

The second letter is generally read as the sequel: *this punishment which was inflicted by
the majority is sufficient… **now you ought rather to forgive and comfort him**, lest
somehow such a one be swallowed up with too much sorrow.* See the note on
2 Corinthians 7:10–11.

The discipline in the first letter has a reinstatement in the second.

> **The order of the sentence.** *Clear out the old yeast, so that you may be a fresh batch
> — which is what you already are.* The status comes last in the sentence and first in the
> logic.
