Two dreams, told out loud to the worst possible audience.

## The result stated first

*Then Joseph had a dream, and when he told it to his brothers, **they hated him all the
more**.*

Genesis reports the reaction before the content. It does this twice in this passage.

## The invitation

*"**Listen to this dream I had**."*

He gathers them for it.

Nothing in the text suggests he was told to tell them.

## The first dream

*"We were out in the field **binding sheaves of grain**, and suddenly **my sheaf rose and
stood upright**, and **your sheaves gathered around and bowed down to it**."*

A harvest image, given to a family of shepherds. They do not farm.

It fits Egypt, where he ends up running a granary, better than it fits Canaan.

## Their reading

*"**So you think you will reign over us? You think you will rule us?**"*

Two verbs — reign and have dominion — and they supply the interpretation themselves.

He did not say what it meant. They did.

## The second reaction

*And **they hated him still more for his dreams and his words**.*

Both. The dreams, and the telling of them.

## The second dream

*"I dreamed again — this time **the sun and the moon and eleven stars were bowing down to
me**."*

Eleven stars, and he is the twelfth. The count is exact and everyone in the room can do the
arithmetic.

## Who he tells

*When he told **his father as well**.*

The first dream went to the brothers. This one goes to the whole household.

## The rebuke

*His father **rebuked him**. "What is this dream? **Am I and your mother and your brothers
really to come and bow to the ground before you?**"*

Jacob does the same as the brothers — supplies the interpretation while objecting to it.

And he names himself as the sun. He knows exactly what it says.

## The problem with the moon

Rachel is dead. She died giving birth to Benjamin two chapters earlier. See the note on
Genesis 35:16–20.

Jacob's objection includes a figure who cannot bow to anyone.

## The two responses

*His brothers were **jealous of him**, but his father **kept the matter in mind**.*

The Hebrew is *his father observed the saying* — **shamar**, kept, guarded, watched.

The same construction Luke uses of Mary: *but Mary kept all these things and pondered them in
her heart*.

Jacob rebukes him in public and files it.

## Where the sheaves come true

*So his brothers came and **bowed low before him, their faces to the ground**.* In an
Egyptian granary, buying grain, twenty-two years later. See the note on Genesis 42:6–17.

The dream was about sheaves, and the fulfilment happens over sacks of corn.

## Where the second one does

*Then Israel said to Joseph, "I had not thought to see your face; but in fact, God has also
shown me your offspring!"*

Jacob comes down to Egypt at the end and Joseph goes up in his chariot to meet him.

The sun does not bow, but he comes.

## What Joseph says at the end

*"You meant evil against me, but **God meant it for good**."* See the note on
Genesis 50:15–21.

Between the dreams and that sentence: a pit, a caravan, a slave market, a false accusation,
a prison, and two forgotten years.

Nothing in the dreams warned him about any of it.

## What they actually refused

*"So you think you will reign over us? You think you will rule us?"*

That is not a misreading. They understood the dreams exactly, and what they would not have
was his reign.

*But his citizens hated him, and sent a delegation after him, saying, "**We will not have
this man to reign over us**."* The parable of the nobleman who went into a far country to
receive a kingdom, and returned.

And over the head of a man his own people had handed across, a placard in three languages:
**THE KING OF THE JEWS**.

## When they bow

*So his brothers came and bowed low before him, their faces to the ground.*

Twenty-two years, a famine, and a journey to Egypt later — and they still do not know who
they are bowing to. See the note on Genesis 42:6–17.

> **What he did with them.** Told them. Genesis records the telling twice and the hatred
> twice, and never says the dreams were wrong.
