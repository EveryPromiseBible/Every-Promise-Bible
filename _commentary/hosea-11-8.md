key: Hosea 11:8
title: "My heart turns over inside Me"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Four questions in a row, and God is asking them of Himself.

## The questions

*"**How can I give you up, Ephraim? How can I hand you over, Israel? How can I treat you like
Admah? How can I make you like Zeboiim?**"*

The Hebrew is **eikh ettenkha** — how shall I give you?

Nobody else is in the room. The chapter has just finished the case against Ephraim — they would
not return, Assyria will rule them — and then the sentence breaks down into four questions with no
addressee.

## The two cities

**Admah** and **Zeboiim** were destroyed with Sodom and Gomorrah. Deuteronomy names all four
together in the same sentence about what a devastated land looks like.

He does not say *how can I treat you like Sodom*. He picks the two nobody remembers — the smaller
cities in the same judgement — which makes the comparison less rhetorical and more exact.

## The line

*"**My heart turns over inside Me. All My compassion is stirred and burning.**"*

The Hebrew is **nehpakh alai libbi** — My heart is overturned within Me. **Haphakh** is the verb
used of what happened to Sodom: *He overthrew those cities.*

The same verb that describes the destruction of the cities describes what happens inside God when
He considers doing it again.

And **nikhmeru nichumai** — My compassions are kindled, grown warm. The verb is used of Joseph in
Genesis: *his heart yearned for his brother, and he sought somewhere to weep.*

## What is not offered

A reason. Ephraim has not repented, has not asked, and does not appear in the paragraph except as
the object.

*Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but **according to His mercy**.*

## The answer

*"**I will not act on the heat of My anger.** I will not turn back to destroy Ephraim. **For I am
God, and not a man.** I am the Holy One in your midst, and I will not come against you in wrath."*

The reason given is His nature, and the specific point of contrast is that a man would.

*God is not a man, that He should lie, nor a son of man, that He should repent.*

Numbers uses the same comparison to say He does not go back on His word. Hosea uses it to say He
does not do what an offended husband would be entitled to do.

## Where the compassion word goes

*But when He saw the multitudes, **He was moved with compassion for them**, because they were weary
and scattered, like sheep having no shepherd.*

The Greek is **esplanchnisthe** — from **splanchna**, the inward parts. It is the exact register of
Hosea's Hebrew: something happening in the body.

It is used of **Jesus** twelve times in the gospels and almost nowhere else, and three of the
parables put it at the hinge — the father who runs, the Samaritan who stops, the king who cancels
the debt.

## And the God who did not spare Himself

*"How can I give you up, Ephraim?"*

*He who did not spare His own Son, but **delivered Him up for us all**.*

**Paul**'s verb is **paredoken** — handed over, which is Hosea's second question. God asks how He
could hand Ephraim over and answers that He will not; **Romans** 8 says who was handed over
instead.

> **The verb.** *My heart is overturned within Me* — **haphakh**, the word for what was done to
> Sodom. The overturning that was going to happen to the cities happens instead inside God, and
> the cities are left standing.
