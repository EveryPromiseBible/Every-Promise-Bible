God looks for somebody to fix it, finds nobody, and dresses for the job Himself.

## What He is looking at

The chapter has been cataloguing it: hands defiled with blood, lips speaking lies, feet running
to evil. *They hatch vipers' eggs and weave the spider's web.*

And the people's own admission of it. See the note on Isaiah 59:12–15a.

## The reaction

*The LORD saw it, and **it grieved Him that there was no justice**.*

The Hebrew is *it was evil in His eyes*.

## The search

*He saw that **there was no one to help**, and **He was astonished that no one stepped in**.*

The Hebrew is **yishtomem** — He was appalled, astonished.

And **maphgia** — one who intervenes, who interposes. The word for an intercessor.

Ezekiel has the same search: *I sought for a man among them who would make a wall, and stand in
the gap before Me on behalf of the land, that I should not destroy it, **but I found no
one**.*

## The conclusion

*So **His own arm brought salvation**, and **His own righteousness held Him up**.*

The Hebrew is *His arm brought salvation for Him, and His righteousness sustained Him*.

Twice: *His own*. No help was available, so nothing external is used.

Isaiah has already asked about that arm: *to whom has the arm of the LORD been revealed?* — the
opening line of chapter 53.

## The armour

*He put on **righteousness like armor** and set the **helmet of salvation** on His head.*

The Hebrew is **shiryon** — a breastplate, a coat of mail. And **kova yeshuah** — helmet of
salvation.

This is where Paul's armour comes from. *The breastplate of righteousness*, and *the helmet of
salvation*. See the note on Ephesians 6:14–17.

Paul takes God's own kit and hands it to the church.

## The other two pieces

*He clothed Himself in **garments of vengeance** and wrapped Himself in **zeal like a cloak**.*

The Hebrew for *zeal* is **qinah** — jealousy, ardour.

Paul leaves these two out of the Ephesians list. The believer gets the breastplate and the
helmet; vengeance and zeal are not distributed.

## What He does in it

*So shall they fear the name of the LORD from the west, and His glory from the rising of the
sun; **when the enemy comes in like a flood, the Spirit of the LORD will lift up a standard
against him**.*

See the note on Isaiah 59:18–20.

## Where it ends

*"**The Redeemer will come to Zion**, and to those who turn from transgression in Jacob," says
the LORD.*

Paul quotes it in Romans 11, at the end of his argument about Israel. See the note on
Romans 11:25–27.

And then the covenant sentence: *My Spirit who is upon you, and My words which I have put in
your mouth, shall not depart from your mouth, nor from the mouth of your descendants*. See the
note on Isaiah 59:21.

## What the next chapter opens with

*Arise, shine, for your light has come!* See the note on Isaiah 60:1–3.

The chapter division falls between the dressing and the dawn.

## The other armour passage

*Put on the whole armour of God, that you may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.*
See the note on Ephesians 6:10–13.

Paul does not invent the equipment. He is quoting what God wore when nobody else would go.

> **Where the armour comes from.** God put it on first — because He looked for somebody to
> intervene and was astonished to find no one. Paul hands two pieces of it to the church.
