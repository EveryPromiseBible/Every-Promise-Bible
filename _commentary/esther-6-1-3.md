The turning point of the book is a man who cannot get to sleep.

## The hinge

*That night **sleep would not come to the king**.*

That is it. No angel, no dream, no prophet, no plague. The most powerful man in
the world lies awake, and from that one ordinary thing everything reverses.

The Hebrew is impersonal — sleep fled from the king. The writer does not say who
took it.

## The timing

Count what has to line up on this one night:

- Haman is at that moment building a gallows for Mordecai
- the king cannot sleep
- of everything he could call for, he calls for the **chronicles**
- of the whole reign's records, they read the page about **Mordecai**
- the reader is told *nothing has been done for him at all*
- and Haman walks into the court at daybreak to ask for Mordecai's death

Every one of those is unremarkable on its own. Any one of them going differently
and the book ends another way.

## Why the record was there

Mordecai had uncovered the plot of Bigthana and Teresh back in chapter 2, four
years earlier, and it was written down and forgotten.

That failure to reward him looked at the time like an injustice. It was a debt
being held until the night it would do the most work.

## "Nothing has been done for him at all"

The servants' answer, and it is the sentence the king cannot leave alone.

He does not send for a treasurer. His next question is *who is in the court?* —
and Haman has just arrived, early, to ask permission to hang the man the king is
about to honour.

## What the chapter is showing

There is no miracle in Esther 6. There is insomnia, an administrative record, and
bad timing for one man.

That is how the whole book works: nothing supernatural on the surface, and every
detail landing exactly where it has to. He is not named in the chapter. He is all
over it.

> **What Haman was doing while the king lay awake.** Standing in his own courtyard
> watching a seventy-five-foot gallows go up. Two men in one city that night — one
> building, one unable to sleep — and only one of them thought he was in control of
> the morning.
