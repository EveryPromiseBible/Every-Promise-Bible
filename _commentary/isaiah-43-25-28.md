One of the plainest statements of forgiveness in the Old Testament, and the reason attached to it
is not the sinner's.

## The doubled pronoun

*"**I, even I, am He** who blots out your transgressions."*

The Hebrew is **anokhi anokhi hu** — I, I, He. The pronoun is said twice and then a third
demonstrative added, which is as emphatic as the language gets.

Nobody else is in the sentence.

## The verb

**Mocheh** — to wipe out, to erase. It is the verb for wiping a dish and for blotting writing off
a scroll.

*Having **wiped out the handwriting of requirements** that was against us, which was contrary to
us. And He has taken it out of the way, **having nailed it to the cross**.*

**Colossians** uses the same picture — a document erased — and adds where the erasing happened.

*Repent therefore and be converted, that **your sins may be blotted out**.*

## The reason

*"**For My own sake**, and remembers your sins no more."*

**Lema'ani** — for My sake.

The motive is stated and it is not their repentance, their record or their promise to improve. He
gives His own name as the reason.

*"For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and **their sins and their lawless deeds I will
remember no more**."*

**Hebrews** quotes Jeremiah's new covenant twice — in chapter 8 and again in chapter 10 — and both
times the not-remembering is the last clause, the thing the whole argument has been building to.

## Remembering no more

Not failing to recall. **Zakar** in Hebrew and **mnesthesomai** in Greek are both about acting on
what is held — to remember somebody is to do something for them.

To remember sins no more is a decision about how He will act, not a gap in the record.

## The courtroom

*"Remind Me of the past. **Let us argue it together. State your case, that you may be proved
right.**"*

He invites the defence and then, in the same breath, the passage lists what the defence would have
to work with: *your first father sinned. Those who spoke for you rebelled against Me.*

There is no case. The invitation is real and the file is empty on their side.

*Who shall bring a charge against God's elect? **It is God who justifies.***

**Paul** convenes the identical hearing in **Romans** 8 and gets the same result: the only one with
standing to accuse is the one who has already acquitted.

## What is around it

*"Behold, I will do a new thing… I will even make a road in the wilderness and rivers in the
desert."* See the note on Isaiah 43:18–19.

And before that: *when you pass through the waters, I will be with you*. See the note on
Isaiah 43:1–4.

The forgiveness sits between a promise about fire and a promise about a road.

> **The reason given.** *For My own sake.* Isaiah states the motive for the blotting out and puts
> it entirely on God's side of the sentence — and then invites them to argue, knowing the file is
> empty.
