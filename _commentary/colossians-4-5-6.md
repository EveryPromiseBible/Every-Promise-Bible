Two verses near the end of the letter, about talking to people who do not believe any of
it.

## Who is in view

*Live wisely toward **those who are outside**.*

The Greek is **hoi exo** — those outside, the standard early-church term for people not
in the congregation.

Paul writes it from a Roman prison, to a church he had never visited.

## The time

*And **buy up every hour you are given**.*

The Greek is **exagorazomenoi ton kairon** — redeeming the time. **Kairos** is not clock
time; it is the opportune moment, the window.

*Buy up* is a market word: purchase it out, take it off the shelf before somebody else
does.

He uses the same phrase in Ephesians and adds *because the days are evil*.

## The speech

*Let your words **always be gracious**.*

The Greek is *in grace* — **en chariti**. Not merely polite. The quality of the message
is to be the quality of the delivery.

## The salt

*With **salt in them**.*

Greek and Roman writers used salt as a metaphor for wit — speech with some bite, not
insipid.

And it carries the Old Testament sense as well: salt was required on every offering, and
it was called the salt of the covenant. See the note on Leviticus 2:11–13.

Salt preserves and it makes thirsty.

## What it is for

*So that **you know the answer that fits each person you speak with**.*

The Greek is *how you ought to answer each one*.

**Each one.** Not a formula to be repeated at everybody. The answer is fitted to the
person asking.

That is the point of the salt: seasoning is measured against what it is going into.

## What is not said

He does not tell them to start conversations, to argue, or to prepare a case.

Peter frames it the same way: *always be ready to give a defence to everyone who **asks**
you a reason for the hope that is in you, with meekness and fear.*

Both apostles describe answering rather than initiating.

## What Paul asks for in the same paragraph

Three verses earlier: *pray for us, that God would **open to us a door for the word**, to
speak the mystery of Christ, **for which I am also in chains** — that I may make it
manifest, as I ought to speak.*

He asks for a door and for the right words, about himself, immediately before telling
them the same two things.

## Where it sits in the letter

Colossians spends three chapters on Christ — *in Him dwells all the fullness of the
Godhead bodily*, *hidden with Christ in God*, *He is all and in all*.

Then the practical section, and this is nearly the last item in it: how to talk to the
neighbours.

> **What is being asked for.** Not a script. Grace in the words, salt in them, and the
> awareness that the person in front of you is one person rather than a category.
