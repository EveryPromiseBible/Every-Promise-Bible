The last instruction before He goes, and it is to stay put.

## When

*While He was with them.*

The forty days between the resurrection and the ascension. Luke has just summarised them:
*to whom He also presented Himself alive after His suffering by many infallible proofs, being
seen by them during forty days and speaking of the things pertaining to the kingdom of God*.

The Greek is **tekmeriois** — demonstrable proofs, evidence. A legal word.

## The instruction

*He told them **not to leave Jerusalem**.*

The city that had just executed Him.

Every instinct would send them home to Galilee, and Galilee is where He had told them to meet
Him after the resurrection. Now they are told to stay in the capital.

## The reason

*But to **wait there for what the Father had promised**.*

The Greek is **perimenein** — to wait around for, to stay expecting.

**The promise of the Father** — the phrase is His. Luke records it at the end of his Gospel
too: *behold, I send the Promise of My Father upon you; but tarry in the city of Jerusalem
until you are endued with power from on high*.

## What they already knew

*"**You have heard Me speak of it**."*

He is not introducing a new subject. The upper-room discourse had four separate passages on
the Helper.

*I will pray the Father, and He will give you another Helper.* See the note on
John 14:15–17.

*It is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to
you.*

## The contrast

*"**John baptized with water, but in a few days you are going to be baptized with the Holy
Spirit**."*

The Greek is **baptisthesesthe** — a future passive. It will be done to them.

**Baptizo** — to dip, to immerse, to plunge. Used in secular Greek of a ship going down and of
cloth being dyed.

## Whose words these are

The Baptist's. He had said it at the Jordan, before any of this:

*I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance, but He who is coming after me is mightier
than I… **He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire**.*

Jesus quotes John, three years later, to men waiting in a city.

## The timing

*"In a few days."*

Ten, as it turns out. The ascension, and then Pentecost — which was already a fixed date in
the calendar, fifty days after Passover.

He does not give them the number.

## What they ask instead

*"Lord, is it at this time **You will restore the kingdom to Israel**?"*

And the answer: *"It is not for you to know times or seasons which the Father has put in His
own authority. **But you shall receive power** when the Holy Spirit has come upon you."* See
the note on Acts 1:6–8.

They ask about a timetable and are handed an assignment.

## What they did with the waiting

*These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women and Mary the
mother of Jesus, and with His brothers.*

About a hundred and twenty of them, in an upper room, for ten days.

And they replaced Judas, which Peter proposed and they settled by lot.

## What arrived

*When the Day of Pentecost had fully come, they were all with one accord in one place. And
suddenly there came a sound from heaven, as of a rushing mighty wind.* See the note on
Acts 2:1–4.

> **The last instruction.** Not go — *stay*. The commission to the ends of the earth is issued
> three verses later, and the first order attached to it is to wait in the city.
