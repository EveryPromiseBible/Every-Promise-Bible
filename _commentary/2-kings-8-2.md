One verse of obedience, and it costs her everything she owns for seven years.

## The verse

*The woman **did as the man of God said**. She and her family went away and **stayed in the land of
the Philistines seven years**.*

## What she was told

*"Go away with your family and stay for a while wherever you can, because the LORD has decreed a
famine in the land that will last seven years."* See the note on 2 Kings 8:1.

## What is not recorded

Any question.

She does not ask where to go, how they will live, what happens to the house, or why God would
call a famine.

The narrator gives the instruction in one verse and the compliance in the next.

## Who she is

A **great woman** — **ishah gedolah** — of Shunem. Wealthy enough to build a room on her roof for
a passing prophet and furnish it.

She has property, standing, and a settled place among her own people: *I dwell among my own
people*, she said, when offered a word to the king on her behalf.

That is what she leaves.

## Where she goes

*The land of the Philistines.*

Not Judah, not further inland. Philistia — the coastal plain, Israel's standing enemy, where
Samson died and where the ark spent seven months.

Gerar in that region is where Isaac was sent during a famine, and where he was told *do not go
down to Egypt*.

## Why there

It had food.

The famine was on *the land* — Israel. The coast, with different rainfall and Egyptian trade, did
not have it.

## The parallel that went wrong

*Now it came to pass in the days when the judges ruled, that **there was a famine in the land**. And
a certain man of Bethlehem, Judah, went to dwell in the country of Moab, he and his wife and his
two sons.*

Elimelech went to Moab in a famine, and he and both sons died there. Naomi came back saying *call
me Mara*.

The book of Ruth begins with a family leaving for the same reason, and it is not presented as
obedience.

## The difference

She was told to go.

Nothing in the text says leaving during famine is right or wrong in itself. What the chapter
records is that she did what the prophet said.

## The word

**Vattelech** — and she went.

The Hebrew names her as the actor. Her husband, old at the start of the story and probably dead
by now, is not mentioned.

She decides, she moves, and she later goes to the king alone to appeal.

## The seven years

The full term, exactly as announced.

The narrator does not describe them. Seven years of living as a resident alien in enemy territory
pass in half a verse.

## What she comes back to

*It came to pass, at the end of seven years, that the woman returned from the land of the
Philistines, and **she went to make an appeal to the king for her house and for her land**.*

The property is gone. Obedience did not preserve it.

## What she gets

*"**Give back everything that belonged to her, including all the produce of her land from the day
she left the country until now**."* See the note on 2 Kings 8:6.

Seven years of harvests off land she did not work, awarded by a king who happened to be hearing
her story at the moment she walked in.

> **The verse.** An instruction, and a compliance, with nothing in between. Seven years in enemy
> territory pass in half a sentence.
