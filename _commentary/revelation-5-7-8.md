He takes the scroll, and the whole throne room goes down — carrying two things.

## What has just happened

A sealed scroll in the right hand of the One on the throne, and an angel asking who is
worthy to open it. Nobody in heaven, on earth or under it could.

*So I wept much, because no one was found worthy.*

Then an elder says *do not weep — the **Lion** of the tribe of Judah has prevailed*, and
John turns to look and sees *a **Lamb** as though it had been slain*.

That is the sentence before this passage.

## The taking

*He **walked up and took the scroll** out of the right hand of the One on the throne.*

No permission is asked and none is recorded as given. The Lamb walks up to the throne and
takes it.

## The response

*And **the moment He took it**, the four creatures and the twenty-four elders **went down
in front of Him**.*

Immediately. The taking is what triggers the worship.

## What each one carried

*Every one of them had **a harp**, and **a gold bowl full of burning incense**.*

Two objects, and both are temple furniture.

The gold bowls are the **phiale** — the shallow libation dishes from the tabernacle
service, listed among the vessels Nehemiah carried back into the storerooms.

## The identification

*And **the incense is the prayers of God's people**.*

John says it flatly, and it is the only time in Scripture the equation is made outright.

## What the incense was

Four spices in equal parts, salted, blended by a perfumer, and forbidden for private
use. Three of the four give off nothing until they are crushed or burned. See the note
on Exodus 30:34–38.

That is what the bowls are full of.

## Who is holding them

Not the people who prayed. The four living creatures and the twenty-four elders — the
nearest beings to the throne.

The prayers have been collected, kept, and are in gold when they arrive.

## What David asked for

*Let my prayer **stand before You like incense**, let my lifted hands be the evening
offering.*

He asked for it as a request. See the note on Psalms 141:1–2.

## Where else the bowl appears

Three chapters on: *another angel, having a golden censer… **and the smoke of the incense,
with the prayers of the saints, ascended before God** out of the angel's hand.*

And then the angel fills the same censer with fire from the altar and throws it to the
earth.

## What they sing next

*"You are worthy to take the scroll and to open its seals, for **You were slain, and have
redeemed us to God by Your blood** out of every tribe and tongue and people and nation."*

The worthiness is stated as a fact about the slaying.

> **What is in the bowls.** The prayers themselves — not a report of them, not a record.
> John sees them in front of the throne, in gold, at the moment the scroll changes
> hands.
