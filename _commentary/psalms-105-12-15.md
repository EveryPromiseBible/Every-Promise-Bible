The psalm looks back at the patriarchs and describes how small and exposed they
were.

## What they were

*When they were still **only a handful, few, and strangers** in it.*

Three words for it: a handful, few, and foreigners.

Abraham, Isaac and Jacob were nomads with tents and livestock, owning no land except
a burial cave, in a country full of established kingdoms.

## What they did

*Wandering **from nation to nation, from one kingdom to another people**.*

Constant movement, through territory belonging to other people.

Every one of those crossings was an occasion to be robbed, absorbed or killed.

## What did not happen

*He **let no one do them harm**.*

The Hebrew is that He did not permit any man to oppress them.

## How He did it

*For their sake **He warned kings**.*

The psalm says the protection worked by God speaking to the men who could have
harmed them.

Genesis records two occasions. Abimelech, warned in a dream about Sarah — *you are a
dead man because of the woman you have taken.* And Laban, overtaking Jacob: *God had
come to Laban the Syrian in a dream by night and said, "Be careful that you speak to
Jacob neither good nor bad."*

Both times the protection is a conversation the patriarch never hears about.

## The words

*"**Do not touch My anointed ones. Do My prophets no harm.**"*

Two titles for men who held neither office in any formal sense.

**Anointed ones** — *meshichai*, the plural of the word behind Messiah. Nobody had
poured oil on Abraham.

**Prophets** — and Genesis does use it once, of Abraham, in God's own words to
Abimelech: *he is a prophet, and he will pray for you and you shall live.*

## What they were being protected for

The psalm's next verses go straight into Joseph, sold as a slave, *his feet hurt with
fetters, he was laid in irons* — *until the time that his word came to pass.*

The protection did not mean an easy passage. Joseph is in the same psalm, in chains.

## What the psalm is

A hundred and forty-five verses of history in Psalms 105 and 106, and this one is the
positive telling — a catalogue of what God did, verb after verb, with almost nothing
about what Israel did.

Its purpose is stated at the top: *remember His marvellous works which He has done.*

> **Where it ends up.** *He brought His people out with silver and gold, and not one of
> their tribes stumbled.* The handful of wandering strangers become a nation walking
> out of Egypt. See the note on Psalms 105:37–38.
