A dying king asks for proof and is offered a choice about which direction time
should go.

## The request

*"**What is the sign** that the LORD will heal me?"*

He had already been told he would live and given a number of years. He asks for a
sign anyway, and nobody in the passage rebukes him for it.

Isaiah simply provides one.

## The offer

*"**Shall the shadow go forward ten steps, or back ten steps?**"*

Two options, and the prophet lets the king pick.

## What Hezekiah chooses

*"**It's easy for the shadow to go forward ten steps** — no, let it **go back** ten
steps."*

He rules out the easy one himself.

A shadow moves forward all day without anybody's help. Forward is what shadows do.

He asks for the one that cannot happen.

## What happened

*So Isaiah the prophet **called on the LORD**, and the LORD **drew the shadow back the
ten steps** it had already gone down on the stairway of Ahaz.*

The Hebrew is *the steps of Ahaz* — a stairway built by his father, evidently
functioning as a sundial, with the shadow marking the hours down the stairs.

Ahaz was the king who shut the temple doors and sacrificed his own son. His stairway
is where the sign is given to the son who reopened them.

## Ten steps

Ten of the marks the shadow had already covered. Time already spent, given back.

## What the chronicler notes

2 Chronicles adds one line about the whole episode: *God withdrew from him, in order
to test him, that He might know all that was in his heart.*

And 2 Chronicles also records who was watching — *the envoys of the princes of
Babylon… sent to inquire about **the wonder that was done in the land**.*

The Babylonians came to ask about the shadow. Astronomy was their profession.

## The other long day

*The sun stood still, and the moon stopped… **there has never been a day like it**,
before or since — a day when the LORD listened to the voice of a man.* See the note on
Joshua 10:12–14.

Joshua's day was lengthened in the middle of a battle. Hezekiah's was reversed at a
sickbed.

## What Isaiah's own account adds

Isaiah 38 records Hezekiah's writing after his recovery — a psalm, in the first
person:

*I said, "In the prime of my life I shall go to the gates of Sheol"… **like a weaver
I have cut off my life**… O LORD, by these things men live… **You have lovingly
delivered my soul from the pit of corruption**, for You have cast all my sins behind
Your back.*

> **The one thing he says he will do with the years.** *The living, the living man, he
> shall praise You, as I do this day; the father shall make known Your truth to the
> children.* Hezekiah's stated plan for the added time is telling his children.
