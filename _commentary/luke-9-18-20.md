---
key: Luke 9:18–20
title: "You are the Messiah of God"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The turning point of the gospel, and **Luke** notes what **Jesus** was doing beforehand.

## Where He was

*"Once, when He had **gone off alone to pray** and only His disciples were with Him."*

**Luke** is the evangelist who records **Jesus** praying at every hinge: at the baptism, before
choosing the twelve, here, on the mountain of transfiguration, in Gethsemane, and from the cross.

**Matthew** and **Mark** put this conversation at Caesarea Philippi. **Luke** puts it after a prayer.

## The first question

*"**Who do the crowds say I am?**"*

John the Baptist, Elijah, one of the old prophets. Every answer is respectful, every answer is a
category from the past, and every answer is wrong.

## The second

*"**But who do you say that I am?**"*

**Hymeis de tina me legete einai** — the pronoun is emphatic and plural. He turns from the survey to
the twelve.

## The answer

*"**The Messiah of God.**"*

**Ton Christon tou Theou** — the Anointed One of God.

*Jesus answered and said to him, "Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, for **flesh and blood has not
revealed this to you, but My Father who is in heaven**."*

**Matthew** records the reply, and it takes the confession out of Peter's hands in the moment he
makes it. He is congratulated for something he is told he did not work out.

*Therefore I make known to you that no one speaking by the Spirit of God calls Jesus accursed, and
**no one can say that Jesus is Lord except by the Holy Spirit**.*

## What He says immediately after

*"**The Son of Man must suffer many things, and be rejected by the elders and chief priests and
scribes, and be killed, and be raised the third day.**"*

The first passion prediction follows the confession within one verse. The two belong together: the
title is accepted, and then defined.

**Mark** records that **Peter** *took Him aside and began to rebuke Him* — and gets *get behind Me,
Satan* for it, minutes after the revelation.

## And the instruction

*"**But He strictly warned and commanded them to tell this to no one.**"*

The right answer, and an order to keep it quiet. **Luke** has Him silencing demons who say it
correctly too.

The confession is not withheld out of modesty. It could not be understood before the cross, which is
what the next sentence is about.

> **What Luke adds.** He was praying first, alone. And Matthew's version of the reply takes the
> confession out of Peter's hands in the same breath as blessing him for it: *flesh and blood has
> not revealed this to you*.
