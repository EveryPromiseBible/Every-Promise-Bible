The last scene of Luke's Gospel, and He gives them a reading lesson.

## What He says first

*"This is what **I was telling you while I was still with you**."*

Not new information. He had said it repeatedly — three times in Luke that the Son of Man
must suffer, and each time Luke notes they did not understand.

## The three divisions

*"Everything written about Me in **the law of Moses and in the Prophets and in the
Psalms** had to be fulfilled."*

The standard Jewish division of Scripture: Torah, Nevi'im, Ketuvim — the Psalms standing
for the writings, as the largest book in them.

He is claiming all three.

## The opening

*Then **He opened their minds to understand the Scriptures**.*

The Greek is **dienoixen** — to open thoroughly, to open up.

Luke uses the same verb three times in this chapter: on the Emmaus road *He **opened** to
us the Scriptures*; at the table *their eyes were **opened***; and here, the mind.

The problem was not that the texts were unclear. Something had to be opened at the
reader's end.

## What stands written

*"That the Messiah would **suffer** and **rise from the dead on the third day**."*

*"And that **a change of mind and the forgiveness of sins would be proclaimed in His
name** to all the nations, **beginning at Jerusalem**."*

Three items, and the third is the mission. He includes the preaching in what the Old
Testament said had to happen.

## "Beginning at Jerusalem"

The city that had just executed Him.

Acts follows the order exactly: *in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the
end of the earth.*

## The commission

*"**You are witnesses of all of this**."*

The Greek is **martures** — the root of *martyr*.

A witness reports what he saw. It is the only qualification named.

## The promise

*"And **I am sending what My Father promised out upon you**."*

## The instruction

*"**Stay here in the city of Jerusalem until you are clothed with power from on high**."*

The Greek for *clothed* is **endusesthe** — to be dressed in, to put on like a garment.

**Until.** They are told to do nothing until it arrives — and they do exactly that:
eleven men and a hundred and twenty people in an upper room for ten days. See the note on
Acts 1:12–14.

## What they had been given and not given

Opened minds, an explanation of the whole Old Testament, the commission, and the title of
witness — and they are still told to sit still.

## The last verses of the Gospel

*And He led them out as far as Bethany, and He lifted up His hands and blessed them. Now
it came to pass, **while He blessed them, He was parted from them** and carried up into
heaven.*

The last thing they saw was His hands up.

*And they returned to Jerusalem **with great joy**, and were continually in the temple
praising and blessing God.*

## How the Gospel opened

With a priest, Zacharias, unable to give the blessing when he came out of the temple
because he had been struck dumb.

Luke ends with a blessing that is completed.

> **What was opened.** Not the book. Luke says He opened *their minds to understand the
> Scriptures* — the same scrolls they had been reading in synagogue their whole lives.
