Jesus places John at a boundary and says which side of it everything falls on.

## The occasion

John is in Herod's prison and has sent two disciples to ask *are You the Coming One, or
do we look for another?*

Jesus answers by listing what is happening — the blind see, the lame walk, the poor have
the gospel preached to them — and then turns to the crowd and speaks about John.

## The pressing

*"From the days of John the Baptist until this very hour, **the kingdom of heaven has been
advancing under force, and forceful people are seizing hold of it**."*

The Greek is **biazetai** and **biastai** — words about violence and force. They can be
read as the kingdom suffering violence or as it forcefully advancing; the grammar allows
both, and readers have divided over it since the beginning.

What is not in doubt is the timing: **from the days of John**.

## The boundary

*"**All the Prophets and the Law spoke their prophecies right up to John**."*

The Greek is *prophesied until John*.

Luke's version is blunter: *the law and the prophets were until John. **Since that time
the kingdom of God has been preached**, and everyone is pressing into it.*

Both put John at the join.

## What that makes John

The last man on one side of the line. Jesus says it two verses earlier: *among those born
of women there has not risen one greater than John the Baptist* — and then, immediately,
*but he who is least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.*

The greatest of the old order, and smaller than the least of the new.

## The identification

*"And **if you are willing to receive it, he is the Elijah who was to come**."*

Malachi's last words before four hundred years of silence: *behold, I will send you
Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD.*

## The qualification

*"**If you are willing to receive it**."*

Jesus attaches a condition to His own identification.

John had denied it himself when the priests asked: *"Are you Elijah?" He said, "I am
not."*

The angel had told Zacharias he would go *in the spirit and power of Elijah*, which is
the resolution — not the man, the office.

## The closing formula

*"**Whoever has ears, let him listen**."*

He uses it after the parables and Revelation uses it to each of the seven churches.

It marks something that will not be understood by everyone hearing it.

## Who Elijah stands with

On the mountain, eight days after the first announcement of the cross, Moses and Elijah
appear and talk about the departure. See the note on Luke 9:28–36.

The law and the prophets, in person, discussing the thing that ends their era.

And when the cloud lifts, *Jesus was there alone*.

> **Where the line is drawn.** *The Law and the Prophets… right up to John.* Everything
> before is one thing, and Jesus says a man alive in the same generation was standing at
> the end of it.
