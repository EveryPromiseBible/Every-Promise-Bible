Paul has just run a chain of questions — how can they call on someone they have
not believed in, believe in someone they have not heard, hear without a preacher
— and this is where it lands.

## The sentence

*So faith comes by hearing, and hearing comes through the word of God.*

Two steps, and the direction is fixed. Faith is not summoned up from inside a
person; it arrives by a route, and the route starts with something said.

Nobody in this chain generates their own believing. Something is spoken, it is
heard, and faith follows.

## The word for "word"

Paul uses **rhema** — a word spoken, an utterance, rather than *logos*. It is the
living, said thing.

That is why the chain has a preacher in it at all. The gospel in this chapter is
something that travels by voice: *how beautiful are the feet of those who bring
good news.*

## The present tense

Both verbs are present. Faith *comes*, hearing *comes* — ongoing, repeatable, not
a single event in the past.

Which means the mechanism does not stop working after conversion. Faith arrives
the same way at every stage: something is heard.

> **The verse before it.** *But not all of them welcomed the good news.* Paul is
> honest that the same word is heard and refused. Hearing in the physical sense
> is not the whole of it — Isaiah's complaint, which Paul quotes, is that the
> message was announced and not believed. The route is reliable; the response is
> still a response.
