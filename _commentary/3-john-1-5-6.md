Hospitality to complete strangers, reported back to the church that sent them.

## The commendation

*"Dear friend, **you can be relied on in everything you do for the brothers, and these are people you
have never met**."*

The Greek is **piston poieis** — you do a faithful thing. And **kai touto xenous** — and these
strangers.

**Xenos** — a foreigner, an outsider. The word behind **philoxenia**, hospitality, which in Greek is
literally *love of strangers*.

## Why it mattered

Inns in the ancient world were dangerous, expensive and disreputable. A travelling preacher with no
letter of introduction had nowhere to sleep, and the whole missionary enterprise of the first
century ran on private houses.

*"**I was a stranger and you took Me in.**"… "Lord, when did we see You a stranger and take You
in?"… "**Whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of Mine, you did for
Me.**"*

**Matthew** 25, and the Greek for *stranger* is **xenos** — the same word Gaius is commended for.

*Do not forget to entertain strangers, for by so doing **some have unwittingly entertained
angels**.*

## The report

*"**They stood up in front of the whole church and told them about your love.**"*

The men he housed went home and said so publicly. **John** has heard it twice — once from the
travellers in verse 3, and once from the congregation.

## The request

*"**Send them on their way in a manner worthy of God.**"*

**Propempsas axios tou Theou** — a technical phrase. **Propempo** meant to equip a traveller for the
next stage: food, money, letters, company for part of the road.

**Paul** uses it repeatedly — *that you may send me on my journey wherever I go* — and it is what he
asks the Romans for on his way to Spain.

The standard given is not *generously* or *as you can afford*. It is **worthy of God**, which is a
strange yardstick for a packed lunch.

## Who they went out for

*"They set out **for the sake of His name**, and **they take nothing from the nations**."*

**Hyper tou onomatos** — on behalf of the Name. **John** does not say which name and does not need
to.

*"Nor is there salvation in any other, for there is **no other name** under heaven given among men by
which we must be saved."*

They refuse support from the people they are preaching to, which is why they need Gaius. The gospel
is not to arrive with an invoice.

*"**Freely you have received, freely give.**"*

## What that makes him

*"So people like that are ours to look after. Do it, and **you are working right alongside the
truth**."*

**Synergoi ginometha te aletheia** — we become fellow workers with the truth.

Not with the missionaries. With the truth itself, which **John** has been personifying since the
first verse.

> **The yardstick.** *Send them on in a manner worthy of God.* It is applied to provisions for a
> journey, and the men being provisioned had refused to take anything from the towns they were
> preaching in.
