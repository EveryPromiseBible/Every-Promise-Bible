One verse, and it contains a comparison people usually skip past.

## What comes before

*This is the message which we have heard from Him and declare to you, that **God is light and
in Him is no darkness at all**.*

The Greek doubles the negative — **skotia ouk estin oudemia**. No darkness, none whatever.

And then the first of John's three *if we say* clauses: *if we say that we have fellowship
with Him, and walk in darkness, we lie and do not practise the truth.*

## The condition

*But **if we walk in the light**.*

The Greek is **peripatomen** — a present subjunctive. Go on walking.

Light in John is a place, not a standard of conduct. In the Gospel: *I am the light of the
world. He who follows Me shall not walk in darkness, but have the light of life.*

## The comparison

*The way **He is in the light**.*

The Greek is **hos autos estin en to photi** — as He is in the light.

Not *as brightly as He is*, which would be an impossible standard and would make the rest of
the verse unnecessary. The comparison is about where, not how well.

He is in the light. Walking in the light is being where He is.

## The first result

*Then **we are in this together**.*

The Greek is **koinonian echomen met' allelon** — we have fellowship with one another.

The horizontal one. The verse before was about fellowship with Him; this one lands on the
company.

## The second result

*And the **blood of Jesus Christ His Son washes us clean of every sin**.*

The Greek is **katharizei** — a present tense. Goes on cleansing.

The cleansing is happening to people who are already in the light. That is the point of the
verse and the reason the clause is there at all.

If walking in the light meant sinlessness there would be nothing left to cleanse.

## Every sin

*From **all sin** — **pases hamartias**.

No category is set aside.

## The verse after

*If we say that we have no sin, **we deceive ourselves**, and the truth is not in us.*

John states the cleansing and immediately forbids the conclusion that there is nothing to
cleanse.

## And the one after that

*If we **confess our sins**, He is **faithful and just to forgive us** our sins and to
cleanse us from all unrighteousness.*

The Greek for *confess* is **homologomen** — to say the same thing. To agree with what has
already been said about it.

And the two adjectives are **pistos kai dikaios** — faithful and righteous. Not *merciful*.
The forgiveness is described as an act of justice, because the ground of it was settled at
the cross.

## The three false claims

John lists them in a row, each starting *if we say*:

*We have fellowship with Him* — while walking in darkness.

*We have no sin.*

*We have not sinned* — which, he says, *makes Him a liar*.

## Where the paragraph ends

*My little children, these things I write to you, **so that you may not sin**. And **if
anyone sins, we have an Advocate with the Father**, Jesus Christ the righteous.*

The Greek is **parakleton** — the same word Jesus uses of the Spirit in the upper room. One
Helper here, one there.

## What the letter is for

*That you may know that you have eternal life.* See the note on 1 John 5:13.

The chapter about sin and light is written by a man whose stated purpose is assurance.

> **The word doing the work.** *As He is in the light.* A comparison of location, not of
> performance — and the blood is described as cleansing the people already standing there.
