Job asks for something that does not exist yet, and names it precisely.

## The speed

*"My days **run faster than a courier**; they flee away and **never see anything good**.
They slip by **like reed boats**, like **an eagle swooping down on its prey**."*

Three images, each faster than the last — a runner, a papyrus skiff on a river, a
stooping eagle.

The fastest things he could name.

## The attempt to cheer up

*"If I say, **I will forget my complaint, I will change my face and smile**, I still
dread all my sufferings."*

He tries it and reports that it does not work.

## Why not

*"For I know **You will not treat me as innocent**. **I will be found guilty anyway**, so
**why should I struggle for nothing?**"*

The Hebrew for *treat me as innocent* is the legal word for acquittal.

His problem is not the suffering. It is that he cannot see a court in which the case
could go his way.

## The washing

*"Even if I **washed myself with snow and scrubbed my hands with lye**, You would plunge
me into the pit, and **my own clothes would loathe me**."*

Snow water and lye were the strongest cleaning agents available.

He is saying that self-cleaning does not reach the problem — and the image at the end is
that even his clothes would find him disgusting afterward.

## The real difficulty

*"**He is not a man like me**, that I could answer Him, **that we could go to court
together**."*

The disparity is the whole obstacle. There is no shared ground, no common footing, no
venue.

## What he asks for

*"**There is no umpire between us, no one to lay his hand on us both**."*

The Hebrew is **mokiach** — an arbiter, a referee, one who decides between two parties.

And the picture he attaches is physical: someone who can put one hand on each. To do
that, a person would have to be able to reach both — to be at home on both sides.

Job does not know of anyone who could.

## What he would do if there were one

*"If only He would **take His rod away from me**, so His terror would stop frightening
me, **then I would speak up and not be afraid of Him**."*

He wants to speak freely, and says he cannot while afraid.

## The last line

*"**But that is not how things stand with me**."*

He closes the speech by conceding it.

## What the New Testament calls it

*For there is **one God and one Mediator** between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus.*

The Greek is **mesites** — the go-between, the one standing in the middle.

And Hebrews adds the other half of Job's request — a High Priest *who can have
compassion, since He Himself is also subject to weakness* — a man, who can be answered,
and God, who can answer. See the note on Hebrews 7:20–22.

## What Job says four chapters later

*"I know that **my Redeemer lives**, and He shall stand at last on the earth… **whom I
shall see for myself, and my eyes shall behold, and not another**."*

The man who said there is no umpire says this in chapter 19.

> **What he asked for.** Not relief, not an explanation, and not vindication. Somebody
> to stand between with a hand on each — and he says outright that no such person is
> available to him.
