The day after the feeding of the five thousand, and by the end of it almost everybody has
gone.

## What they had heard

The bread of life discourse — *I am the living bread which came down from heaven*, and
then the sentences that emptied the room: *unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and
drink His blood, you have no life in you*.

## The complaint

*"**This is a hard teaching. Who can listen to it?**"*

The Greek is **skleros** — hard, harsh, rough. Not *difficult to understand*. Hard to
take.

And it is not the crowd saying it. *Many of **His disciples***.

## What He knew

*Jesus **knew within Himself** that His disciples were grumbling about it.*

The Greek is **gogguzousin** — murmuring. It is the word the Greek Old Testament uses for
Israel in the wilderness, complaining about the manna.

They had just been fed miraculously and are murmuring about bread. John chose the word.

## The question

*"**Does this trip you up?**"*

The Greek is **skandalizei** — from **skandalon**, the trigger of a trap.

## The harder thing

*"Then **what if you were to see the Son of Man going up to where He was before?**"*

If the incarnation offends, the ascension will be worse.

He does not soften the teaching. He raises it.

## The key

*"**It is the Spirit who gives life; human strength on its own counts for nothing**."*

The Greek is *the flesh profits nothing*.

He has spent the chapter talking about eating flesh, and now says flesh is worth nothing.
It is the interpretive key handed over in one line — they had been hearing it at the level
of meat.

Paul uses the same contrast: *the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life.* See the note
on 2 Corinthians 3:4–6.

## What His words are

*"**The words I have spoken to you are spirit, and they are life**."*

## The exception

*"But **there are some of you who do not believe**."*

*For Jesus had known **from the beginning** which of them did not believe, and **which one
would betray Him**.*

Judas is in the room and has been for some time.

## The conclusion

*"This is why I told you that **no one can come to Me unless it has been given to him by
My Father**."*

He had said it earlier in the chapter: *no one can come to Me unless the Father who sent
Me draws him*.

He repeats it at the moment of the exodus.

## What happened

The next verse: *from that time **many of His disciples went back and walked with Him no
more**.*

It is the largest recorded departure in the Gospels, and He does not call them back or
adjust anything.

## The question to the twelve

*"**Do you also want to go away?**"*

And Peter: *"**Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life**. Also we
have come to believe and know that You are the Christ, the Son of the living God."*

An answer with no enthusiasm in it and no alternative either.

## What He says next

*"Did I not choose you, the twelve, and **one of you is a devil**?"*

The chapter that opens with five thousand fed ends with a crowd gone, eleven staying, and
one named.

> **What He does when the room empties.** Nothing. He does not explain further, soften the
> saying, or go after them — and the only question He asks afterward is put to the men who
> are left.
