The answer is given, and then the question turns round and lands on the hearer.

## The instruction

*"**Listen to what that dishonest judge finally said**."*

The Greek is **ho kritēs tēs adikias** — the judge of unrighteousness. A Hebraism: the unjust judge.

## What he had said

*"This widow will not leave me alone. I will see that she gets her justice."* See the note on
Luke 18:1–5.

He grants the case for the sake of quiet.

## The comparison

*"And **will God not bring justice to His own chosen people**?"*

The argument is **qal vachomer** — the rabbinic move from lesser to greater. If the worse case
holds, how much more the better.

Not: God is like this judge. If even this judge acts, how much more one who is not like him at all.

## Who is asking

*"His own chosen people, **who cry out to Him day and night**."*

The Greek is **tōn eklektōn autou tōn boōntōn autō hēmeras kai nuktos**.

**Boaō** — to shout, to cry aloud. The widow's persistence, described in the plural.

## The question about delay

*"**Will He keep putting them off**?"*

The Greek is **makrothumei ep' autois** — a much-argued clause. It can be read as *He is
long-suffering over them*, or as a question about delay.

The ambiguity is in the Greek, not the translation.

## What that ambiguity holds

Either way, the delay is real and is not indifference.

The word for it is the same word Paul uses for God's patience with sinners.

## The answer

*"I tell you, **He will see that they get justice, and He will not be slow about it**."*

The Greek is **en tachei** — quickly, speedily.

## The apparent problem

Crying day and night, and the answer comes speedily.

**En tachei** describes the manner of the action once it starts, not the length of the wait before
it.

## The turn

*"But **when the Son of Man comes, will He find faith on the earth**?"*

The Greek is **tēn pistin** — the faith, with the article.

## What has just been established

That God will act.

## What is now in question

Not whether the answer comes. Whether anyone is still asking when it does.

## Who the question is for

The reader.

The parable answers one question about God and then leaves an open one about us. Luke does not
supply the answer.

## The other place He asks it

*"And because lawlessness will abound, **the love of many will grow cold**."* See the note on
Matthew 24:9–14.

*"But he who endures to the end shall be saved."*

## What follows immediately in Luke

*Also He spoke this parable to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous: two men went
up to the temple to pray.* See the note on Luke 18:9–12.

Two parables about prayer, back to back. The first is about not giving up; the second is about who
goes home justified.

## What the chapter ends with

*"**Lord, that I may receive my sight**."* A blind man on the Jericho road who *cried out all the
more* when the crowd told him to be quiet. See the note on Luke 18:35–39.

Luke gives the parable, and then puts a man in the road doing exactly what it describes.

## Who keeps the faith from failing

The question He leaves open is whether He will find faith on the earth.

*"**Simon, Simon! Indeed, Satan has asked for you, that he may sift you as wheat. But I have prayed
for you, that your faith should not fail**; and when you have returned to your brethren, strengthen
them."*

**Luke** 22, and the same evangelist. The faith **Jesus** wonders about in chapter 18 is the faith He
is recorded praying for in chapter 22 — and He prays for it in advance of the failure, not after
it.

*Looking unto **Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith**.*

**Hebrews** 12's two nouns are **archegon** and **teleioten** — the one who starts it and the one who
completes it. The question in the parable has an answer, and the answer is not about the persistence
of the widow.

## The comparison the parable makes

*"**Hear what the unjust judge said.**"*

The judge is not a picture of God. He is the contrast: he acts to be rid of her, and God is said in
the same breath to *avenge speedily*.

*The Lord is not slack concerning His promise, as some count slackness, but is **longsuffering toward
us, not willing that any should perish**.*

**Peter** answers the same complaint about delay and gives the reason for it, and the reason is
mercy.

## Who the widow is

She has no husband, no advocate and no standing, and the only thing she can do is keep coming.

*My little children… if anyone sins, **we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the
righteous**.*

*Therefore He is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him, since **He
always lives to make intercession for them**.*

The parable's widow has to plead her own case. **Hebrews** 7 says the arrangement changed.

> **The unanswered half.** He settles the question about God — justice will come, and quickly. The
> question he leaves open is whether anyone will still be asking by then.
