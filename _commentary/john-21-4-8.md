The same lake, the same empty nets, the same man — and this time he swims toward Him.

## The setting

Seven of them have gone back to fishing. Peter says *I am going fishing* and the rest say
*we are going with you*.

*That night they caught nothing.*

## The stranger

*When the morning was breaking, **Jesus was standing on the shore**, but the disciples
**did not know that it was Jesus**.*

The third time John reports a resurrection appearance not recognised at first — Mary
thought He was the gardener, and Luke's two on the road walked seven miles with Him.

## The question

*"**Children, do you have anything to eat?**"*

The Greek is **paidia** — little children. And the question is phrased expecting the answer
no.

*They answered Him, "**No**."*

## The instruction

*"Throw the net out on **the right side of the boat**, and you will find some."*

No explanation. A stranger on a beach at dawn telling professional fishermen which side.

## What happened

*So they threw it, and now they **could not haul it back in**, there were so many fish in
it.*

The same event as the morning He called them. See the note on Luke 5:8–11.

One difference: this net does not break. John says so at the end — *and although there were
so many, the net was not broken.*

## Who worked it out

*Then **the disciple Jesus loved** said to Peter, "**It is the Lord**."*

John sees it. Peter moves.

That is the pattern in the whole chapter, and at the tomb too: John got there first, Peter
went in first.

## What Peter did

*And when Simon Peter heard that it was the Lord, he **pulled his fisherman's coat around
him**, since he had **stripped for the work**, and he **threw himself into the water**.*

He puts clothes *on* to swim, which is the wrong order for swimming and the right order for
arriving.

The Greek for *outer garment* is **ependuten**, and for what he did with himself, **ebalen
heauton** — he threw himself.

## The distance

*About **two hundred cubits** out.*

A hundred yards. Close enough to have waited two minutes.

## The others

*The other disciples came on **in the little boat, dragging the net full of fish** behind
them.*

Somebody had to bring the catch in. John notes the number when they land it: a hundred and
fifty-three, counted.

## What is on the beach

*As soon as they had come to land, they saw a fire of coals there, and fish laid on it, and
bread.*

The Greek for *fire of coals* is **anthrakian** — and John uses the word exactly twice. The
other is the fire in the high priest's courtyard, where Peter warmed himself and said *I am
not*.

Breakfast is cooking on the same kind of fire, and the conversation after it is *do you
love Me*, three times.

## The change

Three years earlier, in a boat sinking under fish, Peter said *depart from me, I am a sinful
man.*

Since then he has denied Him three times, which is worse than anything he was aware of on
the first morning.

And this time he is in the water before the boat has turned.

> **The difference between the two catches.** Not the fish, and not the net. The first time
> he asked Him to leave. The second time he had far more reason to, and he swam.
