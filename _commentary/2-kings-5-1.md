One verse of introduction, and the last two words undo everything before them.

## The name

*There was a man named **Naaman**.*

The Hebrew **Na'aman** means *pleasantness*.

## The rank

*Commander of the army of the king of **Aram**.*

Syria — Israel's persistent enemy. The next chapter has Aramean raiding parties in Israelite
territory, and the chapter after that has Samaria under siege by the same army.

He is the enemy's general.

## The standing

*He was **a great man in his master's eyes, held in high honor**.*

The Hebrew is **gadol… venesu panim** — great, and lifted of face.

**Nasa panim** — the idiom for someone whose face is raised, who is received and respected.

## The reason

*Because **through him the LORD had given Aram victory**.*

The Hebrew is **ki-vo natan YHWH teshu'ah la'Aram**.

**YHWH** — the covenant name. The narrator of an Israelite history says the God of Israel gave
victory to Syria, through a Syrian, against — among others — Israel.

Stated without comment or apology.

## The soldier

*He was **a mighty soldier, a warrior**.*

The Hebrew is **gibbor chayil** — mighty of valour. The phrase used of David's champions.

## The turn

*But **he had leprosy**.*

The Hebrew is **metzora** — a leper. Two words at the end of a verse that has spent itself on his
greatness.

## What the Hebrew does

Everything in the sentence is superlative until the last clause, which is introduced by a single
conjunction.

The verse is built to arrive there.

## What kind of leprosy

Syrian leprosy did not carry the Israelite legal exclusion. He is still commanding an army and
still in the king's presence.

But it is progressive and incurable, and everybody knows it.

## Who knows the answer

*And the Syrians had gone out on raids, and had brought back **captive a young girl** from the land
of Israel. She waited on Naaman's wife.*

*Then she said to her mistress, "**If only my master were with the prophet who is in Samaria!** For
he would heal him of his leprosy."*

A slave, taken in a raid conducted by this man's army, tells his wife where to find help.

She is not named. She has the only useful information in the chapter.

## What he does with it

Goes to his king, who writes to the king of Israel, who tears his clothes: *am I God, to kill and
make alive?*

And Naaman arrives at Elisha's door with horses and chariots, ten talents of silver, six thousand
shekels of gold and ten changes of clothing.

## What Elisha does

Does not come out.

Sends a messenger: *go and wash in the Jordan seven times*.

*But Naaman **became furious**, and went away and said, "Indeed, I said to myself, He will surely
come out to me… and wave his hand over the place."* See the note on 2 Kings 5:9–10.

## Who talks him into it

His servants: *if the prophet had told you to do something great, would you not have done it?*

*So he went down and dipped seven times… **and his flesh was restored like the flesh of a little
child**.*

## Who Jesus mentions

*"And **many lepers were in Israel** in the time of Elisha the prophet, and none of them was
cleansed **except Naaman the Syrian**."*

Said in the synagogue at Nazareth, and *all those in the synagogue, when they heard these things,
were filled with wrath*. See the note on Luke 4:25–27.

> **The last clause.** Everything in the verse is superlative until two words at the end, and the
> sentence is built to land on them.
