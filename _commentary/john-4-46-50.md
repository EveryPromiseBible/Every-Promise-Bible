A court official walks about twenty miles uphill to ask Jesus to come back down
with him, and goes home alone.

## What he asked for

*He begged Him to come down and heal his son, who was at the point of death.*

A specific request with a method attached: come, and heal. The verb for *begged*
is continuous — he kept asking.

Capernaum is on the lake shore; Cana is up in the hills. He had made a hard
journey with a dying child behind him.

## The sentence in the middle

*Unless you people see signs and wonders, you simply will not believe.*

It is plural — *you people* — so it is not aimed only at this father. Jesus is
saying something about the crowd's appetite for spectacle before He does anything.

The man does not defend himself. He says: *Sir, please — come down before my
little boy dies.*

## What he is given instead

*Go on home. Your son lives.*

Not what he asked for. He wanted a journey; he gets a sentence. And the tense of
*lives* is present — not *will live*.

## What he does

*The man took Jesus at His word and started for home.*

That is the hinge of the story. He had come for a person and left with a
sentence, and he turned around and walked back down the hill on it.

The next verses have his servants meeting him on the road with the news, and the
fever leaving at the exact hour Jesus spoke. John notes that the man worked it
out and then *he himself believed, and his whole household.*

> **Twice at Cana.** John points out that this is where the water became wine.
> Both signs are at the same village, and in both the person who acts on His word
> before seeing anything — the servants filling jars, and this father walking home
> — is the one who knows what happened.
