Three phrases in this passage are doing more than the English can easily carry.

## "Moved in among us"

The Greek verb is built on the word for a **tent**. Literally: He pitched His
tent among us, He tabernacled with us.

Every Jewish reader knew which tent. The tabernacle in the wilderness was where
the glory settled — and John's next words are *we saw His glory with our own
eyes*. He is saying the presence that filled the tent has taken a body and moved
into the neighbourhood.

## "Grace on top of grace"

The phrase is odd in Greek too — literally *grace instead of grace*, or grace
succeeding grace. The picture is of one wave arriving as the last recedes:
nothing is used up, and what replaces it is more of the same.

Not grace once, and then the reader's effort. Grace, replaced by grace, out of a
fullness John says we have all been drawing from.

## "The law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ"

Two different verbs, and the difference is deliberate. The law was *given* —
handed over, delivered as a deposit. Grace and truth **came into being** through
Jesus Christ: the verb John uses of things happening, coming to pass.

He does not say the law was bad. He says one was delivered and the other arrived
as an event, in a person.

> **The last line.** *No one has ever seen God. The only Son… He is the One who
> has made Him known.* The verb behind *made known* is where the word *exegesis*
> comes from — to lead out, to explain, to set forth in full. Jesus is described
> as God's own exposition of Himself. John has spent seventeen verses building to
> that, and then the Gospel begins.
