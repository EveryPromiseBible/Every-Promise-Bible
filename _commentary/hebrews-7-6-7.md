The writer states a rule nobody disputes and uses it to settle a question about
rank.

## The rule

*And **nobody argues with this: the lesser is blessed by the greater**.*

The Greek is *beyond all contradiction*.

In that world, blessing ran downhill. A father blessed a son, a priest blessed the
people, a patriarch blessed the tribes. The direction of the blessing told you who
outranked whom.

## What it is being applied to

*This man is **not out of their line at all** — and he **took a tenth from Abraham**
and **spoke a blessing over the man who held the promises**.*

Two facts about Melchizedek, and both point the same way: he received from Abraham,
and he blessed Abraham. See the note on Genesis 14:17–20.

## "The man who held the promises"

The writer chooses the biggest possible description of Abraham before making him the
lesser party.

Not *Abraham the shepherd*, or *Abraham the newcomer*. The man who held the promises
— the whole covenant, the land, the nation, the seed.

That man received a blessing, which means somebody greater gave it.

## "Not out of their line at all"

The Levites collected tithes because the law told them to, from brothers who came
out of Abraham exactly as they did. Melchizedek has no genealogy in the record at
all, and he collected from Abraham himself.

## What the argument is for

The whole chapter is establishing that a priesthood exists which is not Aaron's, and
which is better.

*If perfection were through the Levitical priesthood… what further need was there
that another priest should rise according to the order of Melchizedek?*

Psalm 110 promised one, a thousand years after Abraham and centuries after Levi.

## Where the same principle lands

Nobody blesses himself. Blessing is received from somebody higher, by definition —
that is what the rule says.

Which is why the great blessings in Scripture are always given rather than obtained:
Melchizedek to Abraham, Isaac to Jacob, Jacob to the twelve, the priests to Israel
with the Aaronic blessing, and Jesus with His hands raised over the disciples as He
was carried up. See the note on Acts 1:9–11.

## The chapter's conclusion

*Therefore He is also **able to save to the uttermost** those who come to God through
Him, since **He always lives to make intercession for them**.*

The Greek for *to the uttermost* is completely, to the end, all the way through.

> **The one who is greater.** *Here mortal men receive tithes, but there He receives
> them, **of whom it is witnessed that He lives**.* One verse later, and it is the
> writer's own summary of the difference. See the note on Hebrews 7:8.
