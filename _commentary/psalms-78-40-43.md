A long psalm retelling Israel's history, and this is its diagnosis of what went
wrong in the desert.

## The verbs

*They defied Him… they grieved Him… they tested God… they wounded the Holy One of
Israel.*

The third and fourth are the striking ones. **Tested** — they put God through a
trial, repeatedly. And the last, often rendered *limited*, has the sense of
marking out a boundary or causing pain; the older translation *they limited the
Holy One of Israel* is where the familiar phrase comes from.

Either way the psalm attributes something to God that is rarely said aloud: He
was grieved. Not inconvenienced. Pained.

## The cause given

*They did not remember His hand, the day He set them free.*

Forgetting is the root, and it is a strange thing to forget — a sea opening, a
nation walking out of Egypt in a night. The psalm's charge is not that they
disbelieved a promise they had never seen kept. It is that they stopped
recollecting one they had.

## What the whole psalm is for

The opening verses say it: these things were handed down *so that the next
generation would know them*, and set their hope in God rather than repeating the
pattern.

It is a psalm written to be taught to children, and its method is to tell the
story honestly, failures included.

> **What limited Him.** The psalmist puts the cause in the next breath, and it is
> not that they lacked faith for something ahead: *they did not remember His hand,
> the day He set them free*. What they had already been given was sitting behind
> them, and they walked on without it.
