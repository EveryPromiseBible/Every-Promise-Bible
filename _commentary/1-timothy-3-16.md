Six clauses in a row, and they are almost certainly a hymn the churches already sang.

## The opening

*And **beyond all argument**.*

The Greek is **homologoumenos** — by common confession, admittedly. It signals something
everybody agreed on.

## What it is

*The **secret at the heart of a godly life** is a great one.*

The Greek is **to tes eusebeias musterion** — the mystery of godliness.

**Mystery** in Paul is not a puzzle. It is something that could not have been worked out
and had to be disclosed.

**Godliness** — **eusebeia**, the theme word of the letter. See the note on
1 Timothy 4:6–8.

The secret of godliness turns out not to be a practice. It is a person, in six lines.

## Why it reads as a hymn

The Greek has six parallel clauses, each an aorist passive verb followed by a noun, in
three pairs. The rhythm is regular and the structure is too tight for prose.

Paul does the same in Philippians 2 and Colossians 1 — quoting material his readers
already knew.

## The six

*God was **shown to us in a human body**.*

The Greek is *manifested in flesh*. Some manuscripts read *He who*, others *God* — the
difference is one letter in Greek uncials, and it has been argued over since the early
centuries.

*Shown to be **right in the Spirit**.*

The Greek is *justified in spirit* — vindicated, declared in the right.

Romans says where: *declared to be the Son of God with power… by the resurrection from the
dead*.

*Seen by **angels**.*

At the birth, in the wilderness, in the garden, and at the tomb.

*Announced **among the nations**.*

The Greek is *preached among the Gentiles*.

*Believed on **across the world**.*

*Taken up **into glory**.*

## The pattern

The three pairs alternate between the unseen realm and this one:

- flesh / Spirit
- angels / nations
- world / glory

Down, up, out, and up again. It is not chronological — the ascension is last, but the
preaching to the nations came after it.

The order is thematic.

## Where it sits in the letter

Directly after the qualifications for overseers and deacons, and the reason Paul gives for
writing:

*So that you may know **how you ought to conduct yourself in the house of God**, which is the
church of the living God, **the pillar and ground of the truth**.*

Ephesus had the temple of Artemis, one of the seven wonders, famous for its pillars.

Paul calls the congregation the pillar.

## What follows

*Now the Spirit expressly says that **in latter times some will depart from the faith**,
giving heed to deceiving spirits.*

The hymn is placed between a description of church order and a warning about defection.

## Where the same word goes

*People who imagine that **godliness is a way of getting on in the world**.* See the note on
1 Timothy 6:3–5.

Three uses of one noun in one letter: the mystery of it, the training for it, and the
counterfeit.

> **What the secret turns out to be.** Not a discipline. Six lines about one person, in a
> rhythm the church was already singing — and Paul quotes it rather than composing it.
