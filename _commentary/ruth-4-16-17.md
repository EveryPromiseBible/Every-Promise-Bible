The book ends with an old woman holding a baby, and then four names.

## Who holds him

*And **Naomi took the child, laid him on her lap**, and became his nurse.*

She had come back from Moab saying *call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly
with me. I went out full, and the LORD has brought me home again empty.*

The last picture of her is a full lap.

## What the women say

*"**A son has been born to Naomi.**"*

Not to Ruth. The neighbourhood women name him and assign him to the woman who had said she was
empty.

They had said something else to her a few verses earlier: *your daughter-in-law, who loves
you, **who is better to you than seven sons**, has borne him.*

Seven sons was the Hebrew idiom for a complete family. They say a Moabite is worth more.

## The name

**Obed** — *servant*, one who serves.

The child born to end an old woman's emptiness is named for service.

## The four names

*He became the father of **Jesse**, and Jesse the father of **David**.*

The book that opens with a famine, a funeral and two more funerals ends with the great-
grandfather of the king.

## Where the line goes on

*Boaz begot Obed by Ruth, Obed begot Jesse, and Jesse begot David the king… and **Jacob begot
Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus who is called Christ**.* See the note on
Matthew 1:1–3.

Matthew runs the same list and does not stop at David.

## Who is in it

A Moabite, from a nation barred from the assembly to the tenth generation. And four names
earlier, Rahab of Jericho.

*But now in Christ Jesus **you who once were far off have been brought near** by the blood of
Christ.*

## What the book is about

A field, a redeemer with the right and the willingness to buy it, a foreigner with no claim,
and a child.

*God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under the law, **to redeem** those who were
under the law, that we might receive **the adoption as sons**.*

> **The last word of the story.** Not the wedding. An old woman with a baby on her lap, and
> the neighbours saying a son has been born to *her* — about a child who is not hers, borne by
> a Moabite, in the line the King comes out of.
