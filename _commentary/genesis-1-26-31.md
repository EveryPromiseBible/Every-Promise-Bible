The sixth day, and the account changes register for it.

## "Let us"

Everything before this is *let there be*. Here God speaks about the work rather
than commanding it into existence — a deliberation, in the plural.

Christian readers have long heard the Trinity in it, and the New Testament
supports the direction: all things were made through the Son, and the Spirit was
hovering over the waters in verse 2.

## The image

*In the image of God He created them; male and female He created them.*

Said three times in one verse, which is how Hebrew emphasises. And the second
clause is part of the definition, not an afterthought — the image is carried by
humanity as male and female together.

The word for image is used elsewhere of a statue set up to represent a ruler in a
province he could not visit in person. That is the job description: a
representation, placed where the king is not visibly present.

## What they are given

*Have children, and multiply, and fill the earth, and tend it. I am placing it
all in your care.*

The older translations say *subdue* and *have dominion*, and the words are strong.
But they are attached to the image — this is rule exercised on behalf of someone
else, by people made to look like Him.

## "Very good"

*Then God looked over everything He had made — and it was not merely good, it was
very good.*

The refrain through the chapter has been *good*, five times. Only at the end,
with humanity in it, does the assessment change.

> **What the chapter never says.** Nothing here is earned and nothing is
> conditional. The blessing, the food, the care of the earth and the image itself
> are all given before a single human being has done anything at all.
