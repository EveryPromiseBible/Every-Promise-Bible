The doxology that closes the first half of the letter, and Paul piles the words up
because one will not do.

## Where it comes from

The prayer just before it: that they would be *strengthened with might through His Spirit
in the inner man*, that Christ would dwell in their hearts by faith, and that they would
*know the love of Christ which passes knowledge*.

Then this, as the amen on it.

## The two measures

*To Him who can do **far beyond anything we ask**, far beyond **anything we can even
picture**.*

The Greek is **huperekperissou** — a triple compound Paul appears to have built himself:
*huper* (beyond) + *ek* (out of) + *perissos* (abundant).

Beyond, out of, abundantly. He stacks three intensifiers on one word.

## The two things exceeded

**Asking** and **thinking**.

The second is the larger. A person can ask only for what they have thought of, so
imagination is the wider boundary — and Paul says the doing is past that too.

## The manner

*And can do it **lavishly**.*

The Greek is *exceedingly abundantly above*.

## The measure of the power

*According to **the power already at work inside us**.*

The Greek is **energeo** — the root of *energy*. Present participle: currently
operating.

Paul does not measure the promise by God's power in the abstract. He measures it by what
is already running in the reader.

He has already named that power in chapter 1: *the exceeding greatness of His power
toward us who believe… **which He worked in Christ when He raised Him from the dead**.*
See the note on Ephesians 1:20–23.

The resurrection is the unit.

## Where the glory goes

*To Him be the glory **in the church, in Christ Jesus**.*

Two locations, and one of them is a collection of first-century congregations with the
problems Paul's letters keep addressing.

He names them anyway, and puts them first.

## The duration

*Through **every generation, on and on, forever and ever**.*

The Greek is *unto all the generations of the age of the ages* — the most extended
formula for eternity in the New Testament.

Paul is not economising anywhere in the sentence.

## What follows immediately

Chapter 4: *I, therefore, the prisoner of the Lord, beseech you to **walk worthy of the
calling** with which you were called, with all lowliness and gentleness, with
longsuffering, bearing with one another in love.*

The doxology ends and the practical half of the letter begins in the next breath.

## Who is writing

A man in Roman custody. He calls himself *the prisoner of Christ Jesus for you Gentiles*
at the start of the same chapter, and tells them *do not lose heart at my tribulations
for you, which is your glory.*

> **The measure Paul gives.** Not what God could do. What is already at work inside the
> reader — and he says the doing runs past what they would think to ask for.
