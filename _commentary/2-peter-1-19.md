Peter has just described the mountain — the voice, the brightness, the *this is
My beloved Son*. He was standing there. And then he says something surprising
about what is more reliable than that.

## "Firmer now than ever"

*And the word the prophets spoke **stands firmer now than ever**.*

The Greek is a comparative: *more sure*, more confirmed. Peter is comparing the
prophetic word to the transfiguration he has just recounted as an eyewitness.

He does not doubt what he saw. He says the written word is the steadier of the
two things to stand on.

## The room

*Picture a lamp lit in a pitch-black room — that is what it is.*

The Greek word behind *pitch-black* is **auchmēros**: literally dry, parched,
squalid, and so murky. It appears nowhere else in the New Testament.

The lamp is not lighting a pleasant evening. It is the one light in a dry dark
place, and the passage takes for granted that the place is dark.

## Until

*You watch it until the morning breaks through and the star that carries the day
comes up **inside you**.*

Two things happen at the end of the sentence, and they are different in kind:

- **the day dawns** — outside, the whole sky
- **the morning star rises in your hearts** — inside, personal

*Phōsphoros* is the light-bearer, the planet that rises just before sunrise. In
Revelation, Jesus calls Himself *the bright morning star*.

The lamp is for the meantime. The star is the thing the lamp was pointing at all
along.

> **The verse that follows.** Peter's next sentence is his reason: no prophecy of
> Scripture came about by the prophet's own working out of it. The lamp is
> trustworthy in the dark because of where it was lit.
