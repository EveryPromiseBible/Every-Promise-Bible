He identifies the traitor by handing him something to eat.

## The state He is in

*He was **troubled in His spirit**.*

The Greek is **etarachthe** — stirred up, agitated. The same verb John uses of Him at the
tomb of Lazarus and in the temple court before the Greeks.

The Gospel that most insists on His sovereignty is also the one that records the agitation.

## The announcement

*And He said it plainly: "I tell you the truth — **one of you is going to hand Me over**."*

The Greek is **paradosei** — will deliver up, hand over. John's standard word for what Judas
does, and it is not the word for betray.

## The reaction

*The disciples **looked at one another, at a loss** to know which of them He meant.*

Nobody points. Matthew records what they said: *Lord, is it I?* — one after another.

Whatever Judas had been doing for three years, it had not been visible.

## The position

*One of them, **the disciple Jesus loved**, was **leaning back close against Him**.*

The Greek is **en to kolpo** — in the bosom.

The same phrase John used in his prologue of the Son and the Father: *the only begotten Son,
who is **in the bosom of the Father**, He has declared Him*. See the note on John 1:14–18.

He uses the words about himself and Jesus that he had used about Jesus and the Father.

## The name he goes by

**The disciple Jesus loved.** John never gives his own name in his Gospel.

He uses the title five times: here, at the cross, at the tomb, at the lake, and at the end.

## The go-between

*Simon Peter **motioned to him** to ask which one He was speaking about.*

Peter cannot ask. He signals to the man who is close enough to.

The seating means Peter is not near enough to speak quietly, and John is.

## The question

*So, leaning against Jesus, he said to Him, "**Lord, who is it?**"*

## The answer

*"It is **the one I give this piece of bread to, when I have dipped it**."*

The Greek is **psomion** — a morsel, a small piece torn off.

## What the gesture meant

At a Passover meal the host dipped bread in the dish and handed it to the guest he wished to
honour. It is a gesture of favour, and it usually went to the person at the host's left —
the second place at the table.

Which is where Judas must have been sitting for Jesus to reach him.

## Who got it

*And when He had dipped the piece of bread, He gave it to **Judas Iscariot, the son of
Simon**.*

The identification and the courtesy are the same action.

Nobody at the table works it out. Two verses later John says so: *no one at the table knew
for what reason He said this to him.*

## What happens next

*Now after the piece of bread, Satan entered him. Then Jesus said to him, "**What you do, do
quickly**."*

And: *having received the piece of bread, he then went out immediately. **And it was
night**.*

Two words, at the end of a verse, and John means both of them.

## What He had already said about it

*"I do not speak concerning all of you. I know whom I have chosen, but that the Scripture may
be fulfilled, '**He who eats bread with Me has lifted up his heel against Me**.'"*

Psalm 41, quoted a few verses earlier — and the psalm's line is about a table companion.

## What He had just finished doing

Washing their feet. All of them.

*Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into His hands… rose from supper and
laid aside His garments.*

Judas' feet were in that basin.

> **What the gesture was.** Not an accusation. The host's piece of bread, dipped and handed
> to the guest at his side — and it was the answer to *who is it*.
