Moses retells the worst thing that happened to him, forty years later, to a
generation that was not there.

## The forty days

*I stayed on the mountain **forty days and forty nights, eating no bread and
drinking no water**.*

Not a fast he chose. He was in the cloud on Sinai and did not eat or drink for the
whole of it — which is beyond what a body does unaided.

He does it twice in this chapter. Forty days for the tablets, and then another forty
on his face after the calf.

## What he was given

*The two stone tablets, **written with the finger of God**.*

Said twice in the passage. The only thing in Scripture described as written by God's
own finger.

## The interruption

*"**Get up, go down from here quickly**, for your people, the ones you brought out of
Egypt, have corrupted themselves."*

He is handed the tablets and told in the same breath to go down.

Note the pronouns: *your people, the ones **you** brought out of Egypt.* God says it
that way to Moses; Moses says it back the other way in his prayer — *Your people,
whom You brought out.*

## "They have turned so quickly"

The Hebrew is *they have turned aside quickly out of the way.*

Six weeks. They had heard the voice out of the fire and agreed to everything — *all
that the LORD has spoken we will do* — and they lasted six weeks. See the note on
Exodus 19:7–9.

## The offer

*"**Let Me alone**, so that I may destroy them… **and I will make you into a nation
mightier and greater than they are**."*

The same offer made to Moses that was made to nobody else: start over with you.

He turns it down and spends the next forty days face down.

## What he did with the tablets

The verses after this passage: *I took the two tablets and threw them out of my two
hands and broke them before your eyes.*

The covenant was broken before it reached the bottom of the hill. Moses' act simply
made visible what had already happened up the mountain.

## Why he is telling them

The chapter's purpose is stated at the top: *do not think in your heart… "Because of
my righteousness the LORD has brought me in to possess this land."*

He is telling them the calf story to stop them believing they earned the country
they are about to walk into.

*Understand that the LORD your God is not giving you this good land to possess
because of your righteousness, for **you are a stiff-necked people**.*

## The forty days that answer these

Jesus in the wilderness, forty days without food, and the first thing said to Him
is *if You are the Son of God, command that these stones become bread.*

He answers out of this book — *man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word
that proceeds from the mouth of God* — quoting Deuteronomy 8, one chapter earlier. See
the note on Deuteronomy 8:2–5.

> **How Moses ends the chapter.** *So I fell down before the LORD forty days and
> forty nights… because the LORD had said He would destroy you. Therefore I prayed.*
> Eighty days on that mountain without food, and he spends half of them arguing for
> people who had just replaced him with a calf.
