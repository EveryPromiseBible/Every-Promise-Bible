The end of the book of the covenant, and the promises attached to it are domestic.

## Where it sits

The last paragraph of the case law that runs from Exodus 21 — oxen, pits, fires, borrowed
property, the sabbath year and the sabbath day. See the note on Exodus 21:1.

This is the conclusion, and it comes with an angel: *behold, I send an Angel before you to
keep you in the way… **beware of Him and obey His voice**… for My name is in Him.*

## The prohibition

*Do **not bow to their gods** or serve them or **follow their practices**.*

The Hebrew is *nor do after their works*.

Three things: worship, service, and imitation of practice.

## The instruction

*Instead, **tear their idols down and shatter their sacred pillars**.*

The **matzevot** — standing stones, set up at Canaanite shrines.

## The command

*Worship the LORD your God.*

The Hebrew is *serve the LORD your God* — the same verb used of serving their gods two
verses earlier.

The service is not removed. It is redirected.

## The first promise

*And He will **bless your bread and your water**.*

Not a harvest and not wealth. Bread and water — the two things a household needed every
day.

The blessing is put on the ordinary supply.

## The second

*I will **take sickness away from among you**.*

The Hebrew is *I will take sickness away from the midst of you*.

The same promise as Exodus 15, with a name attached: *I will put none of these diseases on
you which I have brought on the Egyptians. For **I am the LORD who heals you**.*

## The third

*No woman in your land will **miscarry or be barren**.*

Both. The Hebrew covers losing a child and never conceiving one.

Barrenness is the recurring grief of the patriarchs — Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, and later
Hannah.

## The fourth

*And I will **give you a full span of days**.*

The Hebrew is *the number of your days I will fulfil*.

Not endless life. A complete one.

## What comes with them

The verses after: *I will send My fear before you… I will send hornets before you… **little
by little I will drive them out** from before you, until you have increased, and you inherit
the land.*

**Little by little** — the reason given is agricultural: *lest the land become desolate and
the beasts of the field become too numerous for you*.

The slowness is deliberate and explained.

## What the parallel promise says

*The LORD will keep every sickness far from you. He will not lay on you any of the terrible
diseases of Egypt that you knew.* See the note on Deuteronomy 7:12–15.

And the same list of blessings: *none among you, man or woman, will be childless, and none
of your livestock barren*.

Two statements of the same terms, forty years apart.

## What the chapter has just finished saying

The sabbath year — *let it rest and lie fallow, so the poor among your people may eat from
it* — and the sabbath day, for the ox and the donkey and the slave's son and the foreigner.
See the notes on Exodus 23:10–11 and Exodus 23:12.

Rest for the land, rest for the household, and then bread, water, health and a full life.

> **What the promises cover.** Not spectacle. Bread, water, no sickness, children, and a
> complete number of days — attached to a case-law code that had just spent three chapters
> on oxen and pits.
