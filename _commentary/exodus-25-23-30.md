A dining table in the holy place, and the food on it is never described as being
for God.

## What it is made of

*A table of **acacia wood**… **overlay it with pure gold**.*

Wood inside, gold outside — the same construction as the ark. The desert timber that
does not rot, wrapped in the metal that does not tarnish.

## The rim

*Build **a rim around it a handbreadth wide**, with its own gold molding on top of
that.*

A raised lip, about four inches, running round the edge, with a border on top of
that as well.

It is there so nothing on the table can slide off. Whatever is set on it stays on
it.

## The poles

*Four gold rings… **to hold the poles for carrying**.*

The table was made to travel. Every piece of tabernacle furniture has rings and
poles, because the whole thing moved whenever the cloud lifted.

## The bread

*And on this table, **always — day after day — keep the bread of the Presence set out
before Me**.*

The Hebrew is *bread of the face* — bread of the Presence, before His face.

Twelve loaves, arranged in two rows, replaced every Sabbath, with frankincense set
alongside. One loaf for each tribe, including the ones that would later be lost.

*Always.* The table was never bare.

## Who eats it

Leviticus says: *it shall be Aaron's and his sons', and they shall eat it in a holy
place.*

The bread sits before God for a week and is then eaten by the priests. Nothing is
burned; nothing is destroyed.

It is a meal in God's presence, with God's people at the table, in the room next to
the ark.

## Where it turns up in the Gospels

David and his men ate it when they were hungry and had nothing — *he entered the
house of God and ate the showbread, which was not lawful for him to eat, nor for
those who were with him, but only for the priests.*

Jesus brings that incident up Himself, to defend His disciples for picking grain on
the Sabbath, and adds: *the Sabbath was made for man, and not man for the Sabbath.*

## What He called Himself

*I am the bread of life.* And: *I am the living bread which came down from heaven.*

The table with bread on it, permanently, before God's face, in a room lit only by
the lampstand. See the note on Exodus 25:31–40.

## The pattern in that room

Three things stood in the holy place: the lampstand, the table of bread, and the
altar of incense.

Light, food, and prayer — and a curtain, with the ark on the other side of it.

> **What Jesus said He was living on.** *My food is to do the will of Him who sent
> Me, and to finish His work.* Said in Samaria, to disciples who had gone to buy
> lunch, while a woman ran back to the town.
