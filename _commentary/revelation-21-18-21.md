title: "Stones a priest used to wear"

A list of jewels that reads like an inventory, until you notice where the list has appeared before.

## The breastplate

Twelve stones, set in the foundations of the city wall — and there is one other place in Scripture
with twelve named stones in order.

*And you shall put settings of stones in it, four rows of stones… **and the stones shall have the
names of the sons of Israel, twelve according to their names**.*

That was Aaron's breastplate, worn over the heart, carried into the holy place whenever the high
priest went in. Exodus says why: *so Aaron shall **bear the names** of the sons of Israel over his
heart before the LORD continually.*

The names went in because the man wearing them could not go in alone.

## What has changed

In the city the stones are not on a priest's chest. They are the foundations, and the names on them
are *the twelve apostles of the Lamb*.

What one man once carried over his heart into a curtained room for a few minutes a year has become
the thing the whole city is built on and stands in permanently.

## Each gate one pearl

*The twelve gates were twelve pearls — **each gate a single pearl**.*

Every other material here is mined or refined. A pearl is the one gem in the list made by something
alive, and it is made in response to an injury — a wound sealed over, layer after layer, until what
was damage has become the thing people cross oceans for.

Every entrance to the city is one of those.

And the merchant in the parable *found one pearl of great price, went and sold all that he had, and
bought it.* Nobody in these gates paid for the way in.

## Gold you can see through

*Pure gold, gold as clear as glass.*

Twice — the city and the street. Gold does not do this. John is describing a substance with gold's
worth and none of its opacity.

The point is at the end of the chapter: *there shall by no means enter it anything that defiles.*
Nothing is hidden anywhere in that city, and nothing needs to be. That is what a place looks like
when *the blood of Jesus Christ His Son cleanses us from all sin* has finished its work.

> **Carried on a heart, then built into a floor.** The stones a priest wore for a nation are the
> footings of the city, and the gates are made of the one jewel that begins as a wound. Everything
> in the architecture is telling you the same thing about how anybody got there — and the Lamb who
> was slain is named on the foundations.
