Nine proverbs, and the first and last are about the same man.

## The sowing

*Whoever **sows injustice will reap disaster**, and the rod of his fury will fail.*

The Hebrew is **zorea avlah yiqtzor-aven** — sowing iniquity, he reaps trouble.

**Shevet evrato** — the rod of his wrath. The instrument he used to oppress with will break.

## The eye

*Whoever has **a generous eye will be blessed**, for **he shares his bread with the poor**.*

The Hebrew is **tov-ayin** — good of eye.

The idiom for generosity. Its opposite is **ra-ayin**, evil of eye — the phrase Jesus uses of the
labourer complaining about the landowner's payments. See the note on
Matthew 20:13–16.

## What the second clause does

*For he shares his bread with the poor.*

The generous eye is defined by what it does. The proverb does not leave it as a disposition.

## The scoffer

*Drive out the scoffer, and **strife goes out with him**; quarreling and insults come to an end.*

The Hebrew is **letz** — the mocker, a fixed character type in Proverbs.

The proverb's claim is that the conflict is not distributed. Remove one man and it goes.

## The friend of the king

*Whoever **loves a pure heart and speaks with grace** will have the king for a friend.*

The Hebrew is **chen sephatav** — the grace of his lips.

Two things together: an interior condition and a manner of speaking. Court literature, addressed
to young men in training.

## The watch

*The eyes of the LORD **keep watch over knowledge**, but **He overturns the words of the
faithless**.*

The Hebrew is **natzeru da'at** — guard knowledge.

## The lion

*The lazy man says, "**There is a lion outside! I will be killed in the streets!**"*

Proverbs returns to this joke three times. The sluggard's excuses are always dramatic and always
about danger elsewhere.

*The lazy man buries his hand in the bowl, and will not so much as bring it to his mouth again.*

## The pit

*The mouth of a forbidden woman is **a deep pit**.*

**Shuchah amuqqah** — a deep pit. The book's standing warning, running from chapter 2 to chapter
9.

## The child

*Folly is **bound up in the heart of a child**, but the rod of discipline drives it far away.*

The Hebrew is **qeshurah belev-na'ar** — tied up in the heart of a boy.

Not learned from outside. Already there.

## The last one

*Whoever **oppresses the poor to enrich himself**, or **gives to the rich**, will only come to want.*

The Hebrew is **oshek dal** — oppressing the weak.

## The two halves

Two ways of getting money wrong, in one sentence.

Squeezing those below you, and paying tribute to those above you.

Both, says the proverb, arrive at the same place: **ach lemachsor** — only to lack.

## What frames the passage

Verse 8: the man who sows injustice.

Verse 16: the man who oppresses the poor to enrich himself.

The paragraph opens and closes on the same figure, and puts the generous eye second from the top.

## The other proverb about the eye

*A man with **an evil eye hastens after riches**, and does not consider that poverty will come upon
him.* See the note on Proverbs 28:21–28.

## What the section opens with

*A good name is to be chosen rather than great riches, and loving favour rather than silver and
gold.*

*The rich and the poor have this in common: **the LORD is the maker of them all**.* See the note on
Proverbs 22:1–7.

> **The idiom.** **Tov-ayin** — good of eye. The proverb does not leave it as an attitude; the
> second half of the line says what such an eye does with bread.
