Paul's instruction to a young man left running a difficult church.

## The command

*Give these instructions, and teach them.*

The Greek is **parangelle** — a military word, to pass on an order.

## The problem

*Do not let anyone **dismiss you because you are young**.*

The Greek is **kataphroneito** — think down on, despise.

**Young** — **neotes** could cover anyone up to about forty in that culture. Timothy had
been travelling with Paul for over a decade and was still the junior man in the room.

## What replaces the credential

*Let them see in you instead **the pattern of what a believer is**.*

The Greek is **tupos** — a stamp, a mould, the impression a die leaves.

The answer to being dismissed is not an argument about authority.

## The six

*In **what you say**, in **the way you live**, in **love**, in **spirit**, in **faith**, in
**purity**.*

Two are outward — speech and conduct. Four are inward.

## The three public duties

*Until I come, give your attention to:*

**The reading of the Scriptures aloud** — the Greek is **te anagnosei**, the public
reading. It was the synagogue practice carried into the church: a passage read out to a
congregation where almost nobody owned a copy.

**Calling people up to what is right** — **paraklesei**, exhortation, the same root as the
word for the Helper.

**Teaching** — **didaskalia**.

Read, exhort, teach. That is the whole job description.

## Where Timothy was

Ephesus. Paul had left him there *that you may charge some that they teach no other
doctrine* — a church he had spent three years in, and had warned that *from among your own
selves men will rise up*. See the note on Acts 20:28–31.

## What comes next

*Do not neglect **the gift that is in you**, which was given to you by prophecy with the
laying on of the hands of the eldership.*

*Meditate on these things; **give yourself entirely to them**, so that **your progress may be
evident to all**.* See the note on 1 Timothy 4:14–16.

The Greek for *progress* is **prokope** — a pioneering word, cutting a way forward.

## What the letter has already dealt with

Myths and endless genealogies, people wanting to be teachers of the law *understanding
neither what they say nor the things which they affirm*, and — in the chapter directly
before this — men *forbidding to marry and commanding to abstain from foods*.

Paul's answer to that: *every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be refused if it
is received with thanksgiving*.

## The second letter

Years later, in his last: *but you — hold your ground in what you learned and came to be
sure of.* See the note on 2 Timothy 3:14–15.

And: *stir up the gift of God which is in you through the laying on of my hands. For God has
not given us a spirit of fear, but of power and of love and of a sound mind.*

Which suggests the timidity was real and persistent.

## What he was actually standing on

Underneath the six qualities and the three duties there is something steadier, and Paul says it
plainly in his last letter to the same man:

*Who has saved us and called us with a holy calling, **not according to our works, but according to
His own purpose and grace** which was given to us in Christ Jesus before time began.*

Timothy's commission did not rest on his age, his temperament, his nerve or his record. It was
handed to him out of a purpose that predates him — and a young man who knows that can afford to be
under-estimated by a roomful of people, because the appointment was never theirs to make.

> **What is offered against being written off.** Not seniority and not volume. A pattern
> visible in six things — and three public duties: read it out, urge them, teach.
