An army marches out to a battle it has been told it will not fight, with the choir
leading.

## Where they are going

*Early in the morning they rose and went out into **the wilderness of Tekoa**.*

Tekoa is Amos's home town, south of Bethlehem, and the road out of it drops toward
the Dead Sea.

Three armies are coming up that way.

## The address

*"Listen to me, Judah and people of Jerusalem. **Trust in the LORD your God, and you
will be held firm. Trust His prophets, and you will succeed.**"*

Two clauses, and the Hebrew has a play on them that does not survive translation:
*ha'aminu… te'amenu* — believe, and you will be made firm. The same root, twice.

The second half is the unusual one. He tells them to believe the prophets, and the
word that had come was *you will not need to fight this battle.* See the note on
2 Chronicles 20:16–19.

## The consultation

*Then, **after talking it over with the people**, he appointed singers.*

The Hebrew notes that he consulted. It was not a royal decree issued over their
heads.

## What he appointed

*Singers for the LORD, to praise Him **in the splendour of holiness**, **as they
marched out ahead of the army**.*

In front. Unarmed men, at the head of the column, between the soldiers and three
armies.

Nobody does this. The front rank is where the shields go.

## What they sang

*"**Give thanks to the LORD, for His love endures forever.**"*

Six words. The refrain of Psalm 136, repeated twenty-six times in that psalm, and it
is what Judah carried into a valley.

Nothing about the enemy. Nothing about the danger. One line about His **chesed** —
the covenant word.

## When it worked

*Now **when they began to sing and to praise**, the LORD set ambushes.*

That is the next verse, and the timing is stated.

## What they found

Three days of collecting spoil, more than they could carry away — and a valley
renamed **Beracah**, blessing.

## Where it started

With a king who prayed *we have no power against this great multitude that is coming
against us, nor do we know what to do — **but our eyes are upon You**.*

He admitted no strength, no plan and no idea, in public, in front of the whole
assembly with their wives and children standing there.

> **What the word had been.** *You will not even need to fight this battle. Take your
> positions, stand still, and watch the salvation of the LORD.* The same instruction
> Moses gave at the sea — *stand still and see*. See the note on Exodus 14:10–14.
