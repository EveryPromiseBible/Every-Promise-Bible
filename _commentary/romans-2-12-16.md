Nobody is judged by a standard they never had, and nobody is without one.

## The symmetry

*All who have sinned **apart from the law** will also perish **apart from the law**, and all who have
sinned **under the law** will be **judged by the law**.*

The Greek is **anomōs** twice, and **en nomō** twice.

## What that establishes

The measure a man is judged by is the measure he had.

The Gentile is not condemned for missing Leviticus. Neither is he acquitted for not having read it.

## The principle

*It is **not those who hear the law** who are right before God; it is **those who do the law** who will
be counted righteous.*

The Greek is **hoi akroatai** — the hearers. And **hoi poiētai** — the doers.

## Who this is aimed at

The man in the first verses of the chapter who judges other people: *you who judge practise the
same things*.

Possession of the standard is not performance of it.

## The evidence

*When **Gentiles, who do not have the law, do by nature the things the law requires**, then they are
**a law to themselves**.*

The Greek is **phusei** — by nature.

## What the argument is

Not that some Gentiles keep the whole law.

That they demonstrably have a standard — they honour parents, punish theft, condemn treachery —
without ever having been given one.

## The inscription

*They show that **the work of the law is written on their hearts**.*

The Greek is **to ergon tou nomou grapton en tais kardiais autōn**.

Not the law itself. The **work** of it — its effect, its requirement, showing up in the
conscience.

## The distinction

*I will put My law in their minds, and **write it on their hearts**.* See the note on
Jeremiah 31:31–34.

That is a promise of the new covenant, and a different thing.

Here, what is inscribed is the demand. There, what is inscribed is the fulfilment.

## The witness

*Their **conscience bearing witness alongside it**.*

The Greek is **suneidēseōs** — from **sun**, with, and **oida**, to know. Knowing-with.

**Summarturousēs** — bearing witness together with.

## The courtroom

*And their **thoughts arguing back and forth, accusing them or else defending them**.*

The Greek is **metaxu allēlōn** — between one another. **Katēgorountōn** — prosecuting.
**Apologoumenōn** — making a defence.

## The picture

A trial held inside a person's head, with counsel on both sides, running continuously.

## The date

*All of it will come to light **on the day when God judges the hidden things of everyone**.*

The Greek is **ta krupta tōn anthrōpōn** — the secret things.

## Through whom

*Through **Jesus Christ**, according to the good news I announce.*

The judge named is the one the gospel is about.

## Where the argument is going

*What then? Are we better than they? Not at all… **There is none righteous, no, not one**.* See the
note on Romans 3:9–12.

The chapter's function is to close the last door. The man without the law has one written inside
him, and it is prosecuting.

## The answer to the courtroom

*Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, **having our hearts sprinkled from
an evil conscience**.* See the note on Hebrews 10:22.

And *how much more shall the blood of Christ… **cleanse your conscience from dead works***. See the
note on Hebrews 9:13–14.

> **The internal trial.** Prosecution and defence, running back and forth, in a man who was never
> given a written law. Paul's case is that the standard was always there.
