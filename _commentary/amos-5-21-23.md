---
key: Amos 5:21–23
title: "I hate your religious festivals"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The most violent thing God says about worship anywhere in Scripture, and He says it about worship
that was being done correctly.

## The verbs

*"**I hate** your religious festivals. **I despise** them. Your solemn gatherings **give Me no
pleasure at all**."*

**Saneti**, **ma'asti**, **lo ariach** — I have hated, I have rejected, I will not smell.

The last is technical. The smoke of a sacrifice going up was described as *a sweet aroma to the
LORD*, and Amos has God refusing to inhale.

## What is being refused

*"Even when you bring Me your **burnt offerings** and **grain offerings**, I will not accept them,
and your **fattened fellowship offerings** I will not so much as look at."*

Three of the five offerings of Leviticus, named by their proper titles. This is not idolatry and
it is not neglect. It is the prescribed liturgy, performed.

## And the music

*"**Take away from Me the noise of your songs.** I will not listen to the music of your harps."*

The Hebrew for *noise* is **hamon** — a roar, a tumult. It is the word for a crowd.

David set the harps up. The psalms command the singing. Amos has God calling it a racket.

## Why

The chapter says so eleven verses earlier: *you trample on the poor… you afflict the just and take
bribes, and divert the poor from justice at the gate.*

And in chapter 8: *you who trample the needy… making the ephah small and the shekel large, and
falsifying the scales by deceit.*

The festivals were kept by people whose scales were rigged.

## The one thing asked for

*"But let **justice roll on like a river**, and **righteousness flow like a stream that never runs
dry**."*

**Mishpat** and **tzedaqah**. And **nachal eitan** — a wadi that does not dry up, which in that
country is rare and is the point of the image. Most streams there run in winter and vanish.

## Who quoted this argument

*"But go and learn what this means: '**I desire mercy and not sacrifice**.' For I did not come to
call the righteous, but sinners."*

**Matthew** 9, said at a dinner table full of tax collectors, to Pharisees who had asked why He was
eating there. He quotes Hosea and makes Amos's point.

*"Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you **pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin**,
and have neglected the weightier matters of the law: **justice and mercy and faith**. These you
ought to have done, without leaving the others undone."*

**Matthew** 23, and the last clause matters — He does not abolish the tithing.

## What the New Testament asks for instead

*Therefore, by the mercies of God, that you **present your bodies a living sacrifice**, holy,
acceptable to God, which is your reasonable service.*

**Paul** puts *by the mercies of God* at the front of it. Eleven chapters of mercy come first, and
the offering is the response rather than the transaction.

*Pure and undefiled religion before God and the Father is this: **to visit orphans and widows in
their trouble**, and to keep oneself unspotted from the world.*

> **What is being refused.** Not pagan worship. The burnt offering, the grain offering and the
> peace offering, by their Levitical names, correctly performed — by people who were rigging the
> scales on the way home.
