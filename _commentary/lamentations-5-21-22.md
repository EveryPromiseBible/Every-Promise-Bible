The last two lines of the book, and the grammar of the first one is the whole point.

## The request

*Restore us to Yourself, LORD, **that we may return**.*

The Hebrew is **hashivenu YHWH elekha venashuvah** — cause us to turn, and we will turn.

The same verb twice, **shuv**. The first is causative and God is the subject; the second is
ordinary and they are.

They do not undertake to repent and ask God to accept it. They ask to be turned, and then say
they will turn.

*"**No one can come to Me unless the Father who sent Me draws him**."*

*"**You did not choose Me, but I chose you.**"*

*For **it is God who works in you both to will and to do** for His good pleasure.*

**Philippians** 2, and it is Lamentations' grammar in Greek: the willing itself is worked in.

## The second request

*Renew our days **as they were of old**.*

**Chaddesh yamenu keqedem**.

*"Behold, **I make all things new**."*

*Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; **behold,
all things have become new**.*

They ask for the old days back. What is finally offered is not a restoration to a previous
condition.

## How the book ends

*Unless You have utterly rejected us and are angry with us beyond measure.*

That is the last sentence. The book does not resolve.

The Hebrew is **ki im-ma'os me'astanu** — for if You have utterly rejected us. In synagogue
reading, verse 21 is repeated after verse 22 so the book does not close on it — a custom it shares
with Isaiah, Malachi and Ecclesiastes.

## What answers it

*I say then, **has God cast away His people? Certainly not!**… God has not cast away His people
whom He foreknew.*

**Paul** takes up Lamentations' unresolved final clause in **Romans** 11 and answers it in two
words — **me genoito**, may it never be.

*"**The one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out.**"*

*For the Lord will not cast off forever.* See the note on Lamentations 3:31–33.

The poet had already said it in the middle of the book, and could not make it hold at the end. The
resolution is not available in the Old Testament, and it is not available because it had not
happened yet.

## The five poems

Four of them are unrelieved. The middle one turns at verse 21 and gives the church *great is Your
faithfulness*. See the note on Lamentations 3:22–24.

*And she will bring forth a Son, and you shall call His name JESUS, **for He will save His people
from their sins**.*

> **The grammar of the last request.** *Hashivenu… venashuvah* — turn us, and we will turn. They
> ask for the repentance before they promise it, and Philippians 2 says that is exactly how it
> works.
