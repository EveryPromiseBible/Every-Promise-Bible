Two ingredients, and the account is careful about which one came from where.

## The verb

*Then the LORD God **stooped down and formed** a man from the dust of the ground.*

The Hebrew is **yatsar** — the potter's word. It is used of a craftsman working clay,
and it implies hands.

Everything else in the creation account is spoken into being. *Let there be light*,
and light was. This one is made by hand.

## The dust

*From **the dust of the ground**.*

**Adamah** is ground, soil. **Adam** is the man. The name and the material are the
same word.

Abraham says it to God: *I who am but dust and ashes.* And Psalm 103: *He knows our
frame; He remembers that we are dust.*

## The breath

*And He **breathed into his nostrils the breath of life**.*

Face to face. Whatever this is, it is not done at a distance.

The Hebrew **neshamah** is breath, and it is used almost exclusively of God's breath
and of human life.

## What the two make

*And the man became **a living soul** — dust and breath, earth and God, joined
together.*

**Nephesh chayyah** — a living being. The whole man is the soul; the account does not
describe a soul being installed in a body.

Dust on its own is dirt. Breath on its own is not a man. The person is the join.

## What was not there yet

*No fields yet green, no plants yet sprung up, for the LORD God had **not sent
rain**, and there was **no one to work the ground**.*

Two absences named, and only one of them is about the man.

*Instead a mist rose up from the earth and watered the whole surface of the soil.*

The ground is watered before there is anybody to farm it. He arrives into a place
already provided for. See the note on Genesis 2:1–3.

## Where the same breathing turns up

*And when He had said this, **He breathed on them**, and said to them, "Receive the
Holy Spirit."*

John 20, in a locked room on the evening of the resurrection. The Greek verb
**emphusao** occurs once in the New Testament, and once in the Greek Old Testament —
here, in Genesis 2:7.

The same word for the first man and the new creation.

## And where it goes back

*Then the dust will return to the earth as it was, and the spirit will return to God
who gave it.* Ecclesiastes 12, taking the two components apart again.

> **What Paul does with the verse.** *"The first man Adam became a living being." **The
> last Adam became a life-giving spirit.*** He quotes Genesis 2:7 and then sets a
> second man beside it — one who received life, and one who gives it.
