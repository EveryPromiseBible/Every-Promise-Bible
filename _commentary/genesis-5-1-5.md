The first genealogy in Scripture, and it is built around a refrain.

## The heading

*This is **the written record** of Adam's descendants.*

The Hebrew is **sepher toledoth** — the book of the generations. Genesis is structured
around this formula; it appears eleven times and divides the book.

## The restatement

*On the day God created humanity, **He made them in the likeness of God**. **Male and
female** He created them, and **He blessed them** and called them "Mankind."*

Moses goes back to chapter 1 and repeats it before starting the list.

Between then and now: a garden, a command, a serpent, an expulsion, and a murder. He
restates the original terms anyway.

## The name

*And **called them "Mankind"** on the day they were made.*

The Hebrew is **adam** — the word for humanity and the man's name. God gives the same
name to both.

## The change of preposition

*He had a son **in his own likeness, in his own image**.*

Verse 1 has Adam made in the likeness of **God**. Verse 3 has Seth in the likeness of
**Adam**.

The same two words — **demuth** and **tselem** — used twice in three verses, with a
different origin each time.

Something is now being transmitted down a line.

## Seth

Chapter 4 explains the name: *God has appointed another seed for me instead of Abel, whom
Cain killed.*

The line does not run through the firstborn.

## The numbers

*Adam lived another **800 years**… Altogether Adam lived **930 years**.*

## The refrain

*And then **he died**.*

It comes at the end of every entry in the chapter. Seth died. Enosh died. Cainan died.
Mahalalel, Jared, Methuselah, Lamech — *and he died*, eight times.

It is the drumbeat of the chapter.

## The one exception

*Enoch walked with God; and he was not, for God took him.*

The only man in the list the refrain is not said of. See the note on Hebrews 11:4–7.

## What was said in the garden

*"In the day that you eat of it **you shall surely die**."* See the note on
Genesis 2:15–17.

The Hebrew is *dying you shall die* — the doubled construction.

He did not fall down that afternoon. He lived nine hundred and thirty years and the
process ran the whole way.

## Where Paul takes it

*Through one man sin entered the world, and death through sin, and **thus death spread to
all men**.*

*For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ all shall be made alive.*

The genealogy is the demonstration. Eight entries, eight identical endings.

## The other genealogy

Luke 3 runs the same list backwards from Jesus, through Seth, and ends *the son of Adam,
the son of God*.

Matthew starts at Abraham and comes down. Luke goes all the way back to this chapter.

> **What the chapter is doing.** Repeating one sentence. Long lives, sons and daughters,
> and then three words at the end of each entry — and one man in the list who does not
> get them.
