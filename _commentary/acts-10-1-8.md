The chapter that opens the church to the world starts with a soldier of the occupying
army.

## Who he is

*In Caesarea there was a man named **Cornelius, a centurion of what was called the Italian
Regiment**.*

Caesarea was the Roman administrative capital of Judea — Pilate's seat, and the garrison
town.

A centurion commanded eighty to a hundred men. Every centurion named in the New Testament
comes off well: the one at Capernaum, the one at the cross, and the one who kept Paul
alive on the ship.

## What Luke says about him

*He was **devout**, and **he and his whole household feared God**.*

**God-fearer** was a recognised category — a Gentile who worshipped the God of Israel,
attended the synagogue, and had not taken circumcision.

*He **gave generously to the people** and **prayed to God continually**.*

Alms and prayer. Both were Jewish devotional practice, and he did them as an outsider.

## The hour

*About **three in the afternoon**.*

The ninth hour — the hour of the evening sacrifice, and the hour Peter and John went up to
the temple in chapter 3.

A Roman officer is keeping Jewish prayer hours in a garrison town.

## The vision

*He **saw plainly in a vision an angel of God** coming in to him and saying, "**Cornelius**."*

Luke says *plainly* — the Greek is *manifestly*, openly.

## The reaction

*He **stared at the angel, frightened**, and said, "**What is it, Lord?**"*

The same **kurie** that can mean *sir*. He does not know what he is speaking to.

## What the angel says

*"**Your prayers and your gifts to the poor have gone up before God, and He has remembered
them**."*

The Greek is *have ascended for a memorial before God*.

The language is sacrificial — the phrase used of the grain offering's memorial portion
burned on the altar.

Prayers and alms from a Gentile soldier, described as an offering God has kept.

## What the angel does not do

Preach. It gives an address.

*"Now **send men to Joppa and bring back a man called Simon, who is also named Peter**. He
is staying with **Simon the tanner, whose house is by the sea**."*

An angel appears and its message is a street direction.

The man is to be told by a man. Peter says the same thing at the council later: *God made a
choice among us: that **by my mouth** the Gentiles should hear the message.* See the note on
Acts 15:6–9.

## The tanner

Luke has already told the reader where Peter is: *Peter stayed on in Joppa for some time
with a tanner named Simon.* See the note on Acts 9:36–43.

A tanner was ceremonially unclean by trade, and tanneries were built outside towns.

The man about to be told *what God has cleansed, you must not call unholy* is already
lodging with one.

## The promise

*"**He will tell you what you ought to do**."*

## What Cornelius did

*He called **two of his household servants and a devout soldier** from among those who
attended him. **He explained everything to them** and sent them to Joppa.*

Thirty miles down the coast. He tells them the whole thing.

## What happens the next day

Peter goes up on the roof to pray at noon, and sees a sheet. See the note on
Acts 10:9–16.

The two visions are a day apart and neither man knows about the other.

> **What the angel is for.** Not the message. It gives a name, a town, a host and a
> street — and says the man in the house will do the telling.
