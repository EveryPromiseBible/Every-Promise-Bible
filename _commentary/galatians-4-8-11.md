title: "Back to the beggarly things"

Paul's argument turns personal here, and the sharpest thing in it is a sentence he starts and then
refuses to finish.

## The correction in mid-air

*Now that you have **come to know God** — or rather, now that **you are known by God**.*

He begins with our knowing and stops himself. It is a habit: the same move appears in the letter to
Corinth — *if anyone loves God, that one is known by Him* — and again in the love chapter, *then I
shall know fully, even as I have been fully known*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 8:1–3.

Every time, Paul takes the weight off our grip on God and puts it on God's grip on us. And he does
it here because he is about to describe people whose grip has visibly slipped. If their standing
depended on their knowing, the paragraph would be an obituary.

## Weak and beggarly

*How is it that you are turning back again to those **weak and beggarly** things?*

**Asthene kai ptocha** — without strength, and destitute. **Ptochos** is the crouching beggar, the
man with nothing at all to hand out.

And **stoicheia** — the elementary things, the ABCs, the first row of the alphabet.

That is Paul's verdict on religious observance as a way of standing before God: it is illiterate,
it has no power, and it has nothing to give you. He says it about the calendar God Himself once
prescribed.

## The days were real

*You watch and keep days and months and seasons and years.*

These were not pagan festivals. Sabbaths, new moons, feasts, sabbatical years — every one of them
commanded in Leviticus, and every one of them a portrait: Passover, firstfruits, the Day of
Atonement, all of them drawn from life before the sitting had happened.

*Which are a shadow of things to come, but **the substance is of Christ**.*

A shadow is not a lie. It is an accurate outline of something real. But once the Man casts it is
standing in the room, going back to trace His outline on the wall is not devotion — it is turning
around.

## Turning back again

The Greek doubles the word: **palin anothen** — again, back to the beginning. Paul is not describing
a stumble. He is describing a return journey to a country they were bought out of.

*Because you are sons, God has sent forth the Spirit of His Son into your hearts, crying, "Abba,
Father." Therefore you are no longer a slave but a son.* That is the sentence immediately before
this paragraph, and it is what makes the turning back so painful to watch.

> **He knows you, and that is the part that holds.** Paul is afraid for them, and says so — and he
> still calls them known by God while they are doing it. That is the ground he keeps putting under
> people's feet when their own footing is poor: not the firmness of your hold on Christ, but His on
> you, which was never a shadow and never a beggar.
