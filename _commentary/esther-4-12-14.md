Mordecai's answer is the theological centre of a book that never mentions God,
and he manages to say everything without using the name.

## "Relief and deliverance will arise from another place"

*If you stay silent now, **relief and deliverance for the Jews will arise from
another place**.*

He is completely certain the rescue is coming. That is not in doubt anywhere in
his sentence.

He does not say *perhaps we will be saved*. He says the deliverance will arrive,
with or without her — and he does not name where from. *Another place* is as close
as this book comes to saying the name.

The question on the table is never whether the Jews will be delivered. It is
whether Esther will be in it.

## "You and your father's house will perish"

The one who stands to lose is the one who stays out.

She had just sent word that going in unsummoned carries a death sentence unless
the golden sceptre is held out. Mordecai answers that the danger she is weighing
is not the real danger.

## "Who knows whether you have come to the palace for such a time as this?"

Every step that put her in that room was somebody else's doing. A queen's
refusal, a drunk king's temper, an adviser's decree, a eunuch's advice, a beauty
contest she did not enter herself.

Mordecai looks at that chain and reads it correctly: she did not arrive there by
accident, and the position was given for a reason that is only now becoming
visible.

*Who knows* — he does not claim a revelation. There is no prophet in this book. He
is reading providence from the shape of it, which is what the whole book asks its
reader to do.

## What she does with it

Her answer is three verses later: gather the Jews, fast three days, *and if I
perish, I perish.*

She goes in. And the position that had been prepared for her turns out to be the
place the whole nation is saved from.

> **What Mordecai is not saying.** He is not telling her the outcome depends on
> her. He has already ruled that out — the deliverance is coming from another
> place. He is telling her there is a part with her name on it, and that she can
> miss it. See the note on Esther 5:1–2.
