The son has not appeared since the mountain. He comes back into the story walking through a
field at evening.

## Where he had come from

*Now Isaac had come from the region of **Beer-lahai-roi**.*

The name means *the well of the Living One who sees me*. It is Hagar's well — the place where
a pregnant runaway slave was found by God in the desert and said *You are the God who sees
me*. See the note on Genesis 16:13–14.

Genesis places the son, on the evening his bride arrives, at the well named for God noticing
somebody nobody else was looking for.

## Where he had last been seen

On Mount Moriah, bound, on wood he had carried up himself. See the note on Genesis 22:9–10.

Between the altar and this field, Genesis records nothing he says or does. He goes up the
mountain with his father and comes back into view here, waiting for a bride.

## The evening

*One evening he **went out to the field to meditate**.*

The Hebrew verb is rare and its sense is to muse, to be occupied in thought — a man out on his
own at dusk with something on his mind.

*And **as he looked up, he saw camels coming**.*

## The other pair of eyes

*Rebekah **also looked up**, and when she saw Isaac, **she got down from her camel**.*

The same phrase for both of them, one verse apart. Each lifts their eyes and sees the other at
the same moment.

She has travelled four hundred miles on the strength of a servant's account, and she gets off
the camel before she is told who he is.

## The question and the answer

*"**Who is that man walking through the field to meet us?**" "**He is my master**," the servant
said.*

The last thing the servant does in the chapter is name the son. It is the only answer he has
given about himself all the way home, and it is not about himself.

## The veil

*So she **took her veil and covered herself**.*

## What has not happened yet

He has not spoken to her. He has not tested her, questioned her, or asked what she brought.

He came out to meet her, and the next verse is *he took Rebekah, and she became his wife, and
he loved her*.

## Where the picture goes

*For the Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a shout… and **we shall be caught up
together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air**.* See the note on
1 Thessalonians 4:16–18.

The Greek for *to meet* is the word used of a city going out to receive an arriving dignitary
and walking back in with him.

He does not wait at the house. He comes out into the field.

> **What they both do.** *He looked up… she also looked up.* The same clause twice, one verse
> apart, at the same moment, from opposite ends of a four-hundred-mile journey.
