This is the last thing the Gospels say about Jesus before He is thirty. Everything
between the temple at twelve and the Jordan is covered by two verses, and what
they choose to record is unexpected.

## What He was doing

*He went back down to Nazareth with them, and He lived under their care and did
as they said.*

He had just told Mary and Joseph He had to be about His Father's business. Then
He goes home and spends eighteen years under theirs. Luke puts the two right next
to each other without comment.

## "Went on growing"

The verb is imperfect — continuous, over time, not a state He arrived at. And
Luke lists four directions at once:

- in wisdom
- in stature
- in favour with God
- in favour with the people around Him

The third is the one that stops people. Whatever it means, Luke was willing to
write it: a real childhood, a real adolescence, real development. Not a divine
adult in a small body waiting for the years to pass.

> **What Mary does.** *She kept every one of these things treasured up in her
> heart* — Luke says something similar after the shepherds' visit. He seems to be
> telling his readers where parts of this account came from, and the answer is a
> woman who had been storing things up for decades.
