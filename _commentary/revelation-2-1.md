The first of the seven letters, and it opens by naming what He is doing.

## The address

*Write this to **the angel of the church at Ephesus**.*

The Greek is **angelos** — messenger. All seven letters are addressed this way and nobody is
certain what it means: the church's guardian angel, its overseer, or the messenger carrying the
letter.

## The city

Ephesus. The largest city in the province, with the temple of Artemis — one of the seven
wonders — and a theatre that seated twenty-five thousand.

Paul spent three years there. It is where the riot broke out over the silversmiths' trade, and
where he found twelve men who had stopped at John's baptism. See the note on
Acts 19:1–7.

Timothy was left there. John is traditionally associated with it too.

## The formula

*Here is who is speaking.*

Every one of the seven letters opens this way, and each takes a description from the vision in
chapter 1.

The vision had eighteen or so features — white hair, eyes like fire, feet like burnished brass,
a voice like many waters, a sword from His mouth. Each church gets the ones it needs.

## The first

*The One who **holds the seven stars in His right hand**.*

The Greek is **kraton** — holding fast, gripping.

Chapter 1 had said *He had in His right hand seven stars*. The verb here is stronger: not
merely holding, but keeping hold of.

And the vision explains the symbol: *the seven stars are the angels of the seven churches*.

## The second

*The One who **walks about in among the seven lampstands of gold**.*

The Greek is **peripaton** — walking around, moving about.

In chapter 1 He was *in the midst of the seven lampstands* — standing. Here He is walking among
them.

And the lampstands are named too: *the seven lampstands which you saw are the seven churches*.

## What the two images say together

The churches are lampstands and He is walking between them. The messengers are stars and He is
holding them.

Nothing in the seven letters is an assessment from a distance. He is described, before the
first commendation or rebuke, as somebody moving about inside the room.

## The lampstand

**Luchnia** — the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the seven-branched menorah in the
tabernacle.

One lampstand with seven branches there. Seven separate lampstands here.

The priest's daily job was to tend it — trim the wicks, top up the oil, and keep it burning.

## What He says to this church

*"**I know your works, your labour, your patience**, and that you cannot bear those who are
evil… and you have persevered and have patience, and have laboured for My name's sake and have
not become weary."*

A hardworking, doctrinally careful, tireless congregation.

*"Nevertheless I have this against you, that **you have left your first love**."*

*"Remember therefore from where you have fallen; repent and do the first works, or else I will
come to you quickly and **remove your lampstand from its place**."*

## The threat

Not that the church will be corrected. That the lampstand will be moved.

Ephesus is a field of ruins now.

## The promise

*"To him who overcomes I will give **to eat from the tree of life, which is in the midst of the
Paradise of God**."*

The first of the seven promises, and it reaches back to Genesis 3.

## The one who holds the stars

The letter to Ephesus is a rebuke, and the description at the top of it is a hand keeping hold.

## What a priest did with lamps

The picture of Someone walking among the lampstands is drawn from a job description. In the
tabernacle the lamps were not left to themselves:

*And Aaron shall tend the lamps… **he shall tend the lamps continually before the LORD**.*

Morning and evening a priest went in, trimmed the wicks, topped up the oil and cleared away what had
burned. The light did not maintain itself, and nobody expected it to.

That is what the risen Christ is doing in the middle of these seven churches. He is not inspecting
them from a distance; He is inside, among them, doing a priest's daily work on lamps that need it.

Which is why the letters can be as searching as they are without being a threat. The One walking
about with the trimming knife is the same One who is described three verses earlier as having *loved
us and washed us from our sins in His own blood*.

> **What He is doing before He says anything.** Walking about among them, with the messengers
> gripped in His right hand — and that is the description attached to the letter with the
> hardest warning in it.
