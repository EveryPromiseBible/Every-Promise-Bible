The firstborn gets the longest list of titles in the chapter and loses everything
in the next line.

## What he was

*"Reuben, you are **my firstborn, my might, the first sign of my strength** —
first in rank and first in power."*

Four titles stacked, and every one of them is genuine. The double portion, the
headship of the family, the priesthood of the household — all of it was his by
right of birth.

Jacob lists it in full before taking it away.

## The turn

*"**Yet** you are as unruly as the churning waters, and you will no longer hold
first place."*

The older rendering is *unstable as water*, and it is the phrase the tribe is
remembered by.

Water takes whatever shape the container gives it. It has no form of its own and
no way of holding a line.

## What he did

*"For you **climbed into your father's bed** and defiled it — you went up onto my
couch."*

Genesis 35 records it in one sentence and moves on: *Reuben went and lay with
Bilhah his father's concubine, and Israel heard about it.*

That is all. No confrontation, no punishment, no conversation — *Israel heard
about it*, and then thirty-five chapters of silence.

The silence ends here, on a deathbed, in front of all eleven brothers.

## What the flesh at its best produces

Reuben is not a villain. He is the one who tried to save Joseph from the pit — *let
us not kill him… throw him into this pit*, intending to come back for him. He is
the one who offered his own two sons as security for Benjamin.

He means well every time, and nothing holds. Good instincts, no strength to carry
them through — which is the definition of the word Jacob uses.

## What became of the tribe

No judge came out of Reuben. No prophet, no king, no leader of any note.

Deborah's song asks about them: *among the divisions of Reuben there were great
searchings of heart… why did you sit among the sheepfolds?* They debated it at
length and did not come.

## The order in the chapter

Reuben first, and he loses the birthright. Simeon and Levi next, and their anger is
cursed. Then Judah, and the sceptre.

Three sons pass before the blessing lands anywhere, and the first three are what
the natural man is: unstable, and then violent.

## What he lost, and how

*"**Unstable as water, you shall not excel.**"*

Reuben was the firstborn — *my might and the beginning of my strength, the excellency of
dignity and the excellency of power*. Jacob lists the standing first and then removes it, in
one verse.

The reason is a single act in chapter 35, recorded in one sentence and never mentioned again
until this deathbed.

## Where it went

*The birthright was given to the sons of Joseph… **yet Judah prevailed over his brothers, and
from him came a ruler**.*

Chronicles spells out the split: the double portion went to Joseph, and the sceptre to Judah.
See the note on Genesis 49:8–12.

Neither of them was the eldest, and neither of them was chosen for seniority.

## The Firstborn who cannot lose it

*For whom He foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son, that He
might be **the firstborn among many brethren**.*

*And He is the head of the body, the church, who is the beginning, **the firstborn from the
dead**, that in all things He may have the preeminence.*

Reuben held the title by birth order and forfeited it by an act. The One the title finally
lands on holds it by resurrection, and there is no act that can put Him out of it — which is
also why the brothers counted in with Him cannot be put out either.

> **Where the birthright went.** *The birthright was given to the sons of Joseph* —
> 1 Chronicles 5 says so outright, and explains why: *because he defiled his
> father's bed.* The double portion went to Joseph, the kingship went to Judah, and
> the priesthood went to Levi. What Reuben held alone was split three ways and
> given to other men.
