key: 1 John 2:2
title: And not ours only
updated: 2026-08-07

The sentence corrects its own scope halfway through.

## The claim

*And **He Himself is the sacrifice that answers for our sins**.*

The Greek is **autos hilasmos estin peri tōn hamartiōn hēmōn**.

## The word

**Hilasmos** — appears twice in the New Testament, both in this letter.

The related **hilastērion** is the mercy seat, the lid of the ark where blood was applied on the
Day of Atonement.

## What it means

The two readings argued for centuries: *propitiation*, which turns away wrath, or *expiation*,
which removes the sin.

The Septuagint uses the word group for the covering of the ark and for the atonement made on it.
Both senses are present in that furniture.

## The predicate again

*He Himself is.*

Not *He made* the sacrifice. The same construction John uses in the verse before — *we have someone
speaking for us* — and the same one Paul uses in Ephesians: *He Himself is our peace*. See the note
on Ephesians 2:14–16.

The advocate and the sacrifice are one person.

## What that means in a courtroom

Counsel for the defence, arguing his own blood.

## The correction

*Ours, and **not ours only, but the whole world's as well**.*

The Greek is **alla kai peri holou tou kosmou**.

## What the clause does

Refuses to let the sentence stop where the readers are.

John's community had just been split by teachers who left, and the letter is full of *us* and
*them*. This line puts a limit on how narrowly the *us* can be drawn.

## The word

**Kosmos** — in John, usually the world in its opposition to God. *Do not love the world.* And *the
whole world lies under the sway of the wicked one*.

The sacrifice is stated to be adequate for exactly that world.

## What the verse does not say

That everyone is thereby saved.

The clause is about the sufficiency of the sacrifice and the size of the offer. John's letter
elsewhere is explicit that belief is required: *who is he who overcomes the world, but he who
believes*.

## Where the same reach appears

*"Behold! **The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world**!"* See the note on John 1:29–31.

And *"God so loved **the world**."* See the note on John 3:16–17.

Same author, same noun, all three times.

## The verse before

*"If anyone does sin — **we have someone speaking for us in front of the Father**: Jesus Christ, the
righteous One."* See the note on 1 John 2:1.

## The verse after

*Now by this we know that we know Him, **if we keep His commandments**.* See the note on
1 John 2:3–5.

The letter's pattern: a statement about what is true, and then a test of whether it has landed.

## The other use of the word

*In this is love, not that we loved God, but that He loved us and sent His Son to be **the
propitiation** for our sins.* See the note on 1 John 4:9–10.

The Greek is the same noun, and the sentence there specifies the direction: not that we loved
first.

> **The clause that widens it.** *And not ours only.* John's community had just split in two, and
> the sentence refuses to let the atonement be measured by who is left in the room.
