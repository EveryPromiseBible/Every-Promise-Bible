Three sentences in the prayer, and the same verb runs through all of them — including one
He applies to Himself.

## The request

*"**Set them apart for Yourself by the truth**."*

The Greek is **hagiason** — sanctify, make holy, set apart for a purpose.

It is the word used of the tabernacle vessels, the priests, and the Sabbath. Something
ordinary designated for one use.

## The means

*"**Your word is truth**."*

Four words, and no article on *truth* in the Greek — *your word **is** truth*, not merely
true.

He names the instrument. Not effort, not separation from the world, not ritual.

He has just asked the Father *not to take them out of the world*. See the note on
John 17:13–16.

## The sending

*"**As You sent Me into the world, so I have sent them into the world**."*

The two sendings are put in the same sentence with the same verb, and the second is
measured by the first.

**Into the world** — the second time in five verses He insists on the direction.

## The last one

*"And **for their sake I set Myself apart**."*

The same verb, **hagiazo**, and now He is both the one doing it and the one it is done to.

He is not being made holy. There is nothing in Him to purify — He had said so an hour
earlier: *the ruler of this world is coming, and he has nothing in Me.* See the note on
John 14:28–31.

The sense is the older one: set apart for a purpose. A priest consecrating himself for
what he is about to do.

## What He was setting Himself apart for

The garden is on the other side of this prayer. John 18 opens *when Jesus had spoken these
words, He went out with His disciples over the Brook Kidron.*

## The purpose

*"**So that they too may be truly set apart by the truth**."*

The Greek is *sanctified in truth*.

His consecration is stated as the ground of theirs. The order in the sentence runs one
way only.

## Who else is in the prayer

Four verses later: *"I do not pray for these alone, but **also for those who will believe
in Me through their word**."*

Everybody reading it is named in it.

## What He asks for them

*"That they all may be one, as You, Father, are in Me, and I in You."*

And at the end: *"Father, I desire that they also whom You gave Me **may be with Me where
I am**, that they may behold My glory."*

## Where this is prayed

Out loud, in front of them, between the supper and the arrest. See the note on
John 17:9–12.

> **The three uses of one verb.** Set them apart; I send them as I was sent; I set Myself
> apart for them. The middle clause is the errand, and the outer two are the same act
> done at both ends of it.
