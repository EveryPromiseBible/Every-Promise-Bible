The only woman in the New Testament called a disciple, and the room is full of the coats
she made.

## The name

*In Joppa there was **a disciple named Tabitha, which in Greek is Dorcas**.*

The Greek is **mathetria** — the feminine of disciple. It occurs once in the New
Testament.

**Tabitha** is Aramaic and **Dorcas** is Greek, and both mean *gazelle*.

Luke gives both, which tells you Joppa was a mixed town.

## What she did

*Her life was **full of good works and acts of mercy to the poor**.*

The Greek is *full of good works and almsdeeds which she did*.

## The death

*In those days she **fell ill and died**, and after they had **washed her** they laid her in
**an upstairs room**.*

The washing is the preparation for burial. In that climate burial followed within a day.

They wash her and then stop.

## The message

*When the disciples heard that Peter was there they sent two men to him, urging him,
"**Please come to us without delay**."*

Lydda is about ten miles from Joppa. They send two men and do not say why.

Nothing in the message asks for anything specific. They simply want him there.

## The room

*When he arrived they took him to the upstairs room, and **all the widows stood around him
weeping, showing him the tunics and other clothes Dorcas had made while she was still with
them**.*

The Greek is *displaying* — holding them up.

They are wearing the evidence. Every woman in the room has something she made.

Widows in that world had no income and no legal standing, and the early church's first
administrative crisis was about their daily distribution.

## What Peter did

*Peter **sent them all outside**, and he **knelt down and prayed**.*

Two actions, in that order.

He had watched Jesus do exactly this in Jairus's house: *He put them all outside, and took
her by the hand.* See the note on Luke 8:49–56.

Peter was one of the three allowed to stay in that room.

## The words

*Then he turned toward the body and said, "**Tabitha, get up**."*

The Aramaic would have been **Tabitha koum**.

Mark records what Jesus said to the girl: **Talitha koum** — *little girl, get up*.

One letter apart.

## What happened

*She **opened her eyes**, and when she saw Peter she **sat up**. **He gave her his hand and
helped her to her feet**.*

Luke, a physician, gives it in stages: eyes, sitting, standing.

## The presentation

*And then he called the believers and the widows and **presented her to them alive**.*

## The result

*Word of it spread through all of Joppa, and **many believed in the Lord**.*

## The last line

*Peter **stayed on in Joppa for some time with a tanner named Simon**.*

A tanner worked with animal skins and was ceremonially unclean by trade. Observant Jews
kept their distance, and tanneries were built outside towns by the sea.

Peter lodges with one.

It is the address the men from Caesarea come to in the next chapter, after the sheet and
the voice. See the note on Acts 11:4–14.

The staying is a small detail and Luke puts it there deliberately — the man about to be
told *what God has cleansed, you must not call unholy* is already living in a tanner's
house.

> **What the widows brought.** Not an argument. They held up the clothes and cried, and
> Peter put them all outside before he prayed.
