An unfinished collection, and Paul handles it without once making it a command.

## How he frames it

*And here is **what I think best** about it.*

The Greek is **gnomen didomi** — I give an opinion.

He says the same thing about another matter in the first letter: *I have no commandment from the
Lord; yet I give judgment*.

He is raising money and explicitly declining to order it.

## Whose benefit

*Since **this is the very thing that will do you good**.*

The Greek is **touto humin sumpherei** — this is profitable for you.

The collection is for the poor in Jerusalem. Paul says finishing it is to Corinth's advantage.

## The history

*A year ago **you were the first not only to act but to want to act**.*

The Greek is **apo perusi** — from last year.

Corinth started it. They were ahead of Macedonia, ahead of everyone. Paul had used their eagerness
to stir up the other churches: *your zeal has stirred up the majority*.

## The gap

Twelve months, and it is not done.

Paul does not speculate about why. He names the date and moves on.

## The instruction

*Now **finish the doing of it**.*

The Greek is **epitelesate** — complete, bring to the end.

## The reason

*So that **the eagerness with which you wanted it may be matched by carrying it through**.*

The Greek is **kathaper he prothumia tou thelein, houtos kai to epitelesai** — as the readiness of
the willing, so also the completing.

Willingness and completion, matched.

The point is not that the intention was worthless. It is that an intention a year old is
supposed to arrive somewhere.

## Out of what you have

*Out of **what you have**.*

The Greek is **ek tou echein** — out of the having.

He caps it before anyone can over-reach. Macedonia gave beyond their ability. Corinth is told to
give out of what is in hand. See the note on 2 Corinthians 8:3–5.

## The principle

*For **where the willingness is there, a gift is welcomed for what a person has, not for what he
does not have**.*

The Greek is **kathe ean eche… ou katho ouk echei**.

**Euprosdektos** — well-received, acceptable.

## What the measure is

The willingness, and then what is actually in hand.

Not the amount. Not a percentage. Two things: is the will there, and what is there.

## Who this exempts

Everyone who has little.

Paul's sentence protects the poor giver from feeling the gift is too small, and closes off the
excuse of the rich one who has plenty.

## What follows

*For I do not mean that others should be eased and you burdened; **but by an equality**.*

*Your abundance may supply their lack, that their abundance also may supply your lack.*

And then the manna: *he who gathered much had nothing left over, and he who gathered little had no
lack*. See the note on 2 Corinthians 8:13–15.

## How the money was handled

Paul sent Titus and two other men, and explained why:

*Avoiding this: that anyone should blame us in this lavish gift which is administered by us —
**providing honourable things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men**.*

He would not carry the money alone. The arrangement was designed to be above suspicion.

## What he never does in two chapters

Name a sum, set a percentage, or command it.

He reports what Macedonia did, reminds Corinth what they started, states the principle, and
appeals to the example of Christ. See the note on 2 Corinthians 8:9.

> **The cap.** *Out of what you have.* Having praised churches who gave past their ability, he
> tells this one to give out of what is in hand — and says a gift is measured by what a person has.
