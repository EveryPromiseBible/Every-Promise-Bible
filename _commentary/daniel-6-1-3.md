A new empire reorganises its administration, and the foreigner comes top again.

## The new regime

*It pleased **Darius** to set over the kingdom **a hundred and twenty satraps**.*

The night before, Belshazzar had seen writing on a wall and been told his kingdom was finished.

*That very night Belshazzar, king of the Chaldeans, was slain. And Darius the Mede received the
kingdom, being about sixty-two years old.*

## The structure

*And over them **three high officials, of whom Daniel was one**.*

The Aramaic is **sarkin telatah** — three chief ministers.

## The purpose

*So that these satraps might **give account to them** and **the king would suffer no loss**.*

An audit layer. The three exist to stop the hundred and twenty stealing.

## Who Daniel is at this point

Somewhere in his eighties.

Taken as a teenager under Nebuchadnezzar, he has outlasted that king, his successors, Belshazzar,
and the fall of the entire Babylonian empire.

The new Persian administration promotes him.

## The distinction

*And this Daniel **outshone the other officials and the satraps**.*

The Aramaic is **hava mitnatzach** — was distinguishing himself, was surpassing.

## The reason

*For **an excellent spirit was in him**.*

The Aramaic is **ruach yattira** — a surpassing spirit, an extraordinary spirit.

The phrase is used of him three times in the book, twice by people who are not Israelites.

Belshazzar's queen said it: *there is a man in your kingdom in whom is the Spirit of the Holy
God… **an excellent spirit, knowledge, understanding**.*

## What it is not

Not diligence, though he is diligent. Not experience, though he has sixty years of it.

The text names something in him, and calls it a spirit.

## The promotion

*And the king planned to **set him over the whole kingdom**.*

The Aramaic is **la'hakamutheh al-kol-malchuta**.

An eighty-year-old Jewish exile about to be made second in the Persian empire.

## What that produces

*So the governors and satraps **sought to find some charge against Daniel** concerning the kingdom;
but they **could find no charge or fault**, because he was faithful.*

*Nor was there any error or fault found in him.*

They audit the auditor and find nothing.

## Their conclusion

*"**We shall not find any charge against this Daniel unless we find it against him concerning the law
of his God**."*

They have to change the law to catch him.

## The trap

A decree that nobody may petition any god or man except the king for thirty days.

*Now when Daniel knew that the writing was signed, **he went home. And in his upper room, with his
windows open toward Jerusalem, he knelt down on his knees three times that day and prayed**.*

*As was his custom since early days.*

He does not increase his prayers or hide them. He carries on.

## What happens

The den, the stone, the king's own seal, and a night in which Darius *went to his palace and spent
the night fasting; and no musicians were brought before him*.

*"**My God sent His angel and shut the lions' mouths**."*

## The first thing the king does

*And the king was exceedingly glad for him, and commanded that they should take Daniel up out of
the den. **So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no injury whatever was found on him, because
he believed in his God**.*

> **The phrase.** **Ruach yattira** — a surpassing spirit. Said of him three times in the book, and
> twice by people who did not share his religion.
