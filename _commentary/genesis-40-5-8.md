A prisoner with a life sentence notices that two other men are having a bad morning.

## Where he is

In prison, on a false accusation, having refused Potiphar's wife.

*But the LORD was with Joseph and showed him mercy, and He gave him favour in the sight of the
keeper of the prison.* See the note on Genesis 39:19–23.

He is running the place, and he is still in it.

## Who is with him

Pharaoh's **butler** and **baker** — the chief cupbearer and the chief baker, both offended their
master and both put under Joseph's charge.

Court officials, and he is a foreign slave with a criminal record.

## The night

*One night, **both men dreamed** — each his own dream, and **each dream carried its own meaning**.*

The Hebrew is **ish chalomo belaylah echad** — each man his dream in one night.

Two dreams, one night, and the narrator says in advance that each had its own interpretation.

## The morning

*In the morning Joseph came to them and **saw that something was wrong; their faces were heavy with
worry**.*

The Hebrew is **zo'aphim** — troubled, sullen, downcast.

## What he does

He notices.

A man who has been sold by his brothers, sold in Egypt, framed by a woman, and forgotten in a
prison, walks in and sees that two other men's faces are wrong.

## The question

*So he asked Pharaoh's officials… "**Why do you look so downcast today?**"*

He has more reason to be downcast than either of them, and he asks them.

## Their answer

*"We've both had dreams, and **there's no one here to tell us what they mean**."*

Egypt had a professional class of dream interpreters. In prison they have none.

## The reply

*Gently Joseph answered, "**Doesn't the meaning of dreams belong to God? Tell me what you saw**."*

The Hebrew is **halo lelohim pitronim** — do not interpretations belong to God?

## What he does with the credit

Deflects it before he has done anything.

He says the same thing later, in front of Pharaoh: *it is not in me; **God will give Pharaoh an
answer of peace***.

## What he had been

The seventeen-year-old with the dreams — the sheaves bowing and the sun, moon and eleven stars —
who told his brothers about them. See the note on Genesis 37:5–11.

He had dreams then, and no interpretation for anyone. Now he interprets and asks nothing for it.

## What he does ask for

*"**But remember me when it is well with you**, and please show kindness to me… for indeed I was
stolen away from the land of the Hebrews."*

One request, to the man he told would live. See the note on Genesis 40:9–15.

## What happens

*Yet the chief butler **did not remember Joseph, but forgot him**.*

The last verse of the chapter.

## How long

*Then it came to pass, **at the end of two full years**, that Pharaoh had a dream.*

Two more years, and then the butler remembers: *I remember my faults this day*.

## What comes of it

*Then Pharaoh sent and called Joseph, and **they brought him quickly out of the dungeon**.*

The Hebrew is **vayritzuhu** — and they made him run.

Thirteen years of waiting, and the last stretch is at a sprint.

## The two men beside him

One is restored to the king's own cup in three days. One is hanged on a tree in three days.

The same prison, the same night, the same interpreter — and the two verdicts are opposite.

*And when they had come to the place called Calvary, there they crucified Him, and the
criminals, **one on the right hand and the other on the left**.*

## "Remember me"

*"But **remember me** when it is well with you."*

That is what Joseph asked of a condemned man who was about to be lifted up — and *the chief
butler did not remember Joseph, but forgot him*.

*Then he said to Jesus, "**Lord, remember me** when You come into Your kingdom."*

Asked of a condemned man in the middle, by a criminal with nothing to offer and no time left
to offer it. The answer came back the same day: *today you will be with Me in Paradise.*

One man asked and was forgotten for two years. One man asked and was answered before
sundown.

> **The question.** *Why do you look so downcast today?* Asked by the man in the room with the
> strongest case for looking downcast himself.
