The famous line closes a run of sayings that are almost all about the mouth, and
that is not an accident.

## Read what it is sitting with

- *with his mouth the godless would ruin his neighbor*
- *by the blessing of the upright a city is lifted high; by the mouth of the
  wicked it is torn down*
- *whoever belittles a neighbor has no sense at all*
- *a gossip goes about giving secrets away; a faithful spirit keeps the matter
  quiet*

Then: *where there is no guidance, a people falls. In many wise counselors there
is safety.*

So the proverb about counsel arrives at the end of a paragraph about how much
damage talk can do — which is a caution built into the promise. The counsellors
being commended are the sort just described as trustworthy with what they are
told.

## The words

**Guidance** is a navigation word — the same root is used of steering a ship, and
elsewhere in Proverbs of a person of understanding acquiring *direction*.

**Safety** is literally deliverance, rescue. Not "a better outcome." Getting out.

> **What the proverb assumes.** Not that a crowd is wiser than one person — this
> book has plenty to say about crowds. It assumes *wise* counsellors, plural, who
> can keep a confidence. A proverb is a general truth well put, not a mechanism,
> and this one carries its own quality control in the verses above it.
