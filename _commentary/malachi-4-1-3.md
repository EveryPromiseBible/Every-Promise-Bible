The last page of the Old Testament, and it ends with a sunrise.

## The sun of righteousness

*But for you who fear My name, the **sun of righteousness** will rise with
**healing in its rays**.*

The Hebrew for *rays* is **kanaph** — wings. The same word used of the wings in
Psalm 36, and the same word for the corner or hem of a garment.

It is the word behind the woman who touched the hem of His garment and was
healed. The sun rising with healing in its wings, and a woman reaching for the
wing of His robe.

## What rises

Not *a* sun. **The** sun of righteousness — righteousness itself coming up over
the horizon like the morning.

The order in the sentence is worth noticing: righteousness rises first, and
healing is what is carried in its wings. The healing is not the sun. It is what
the sun brings with it.

## The calves

*You will go out and frolic like calves let loose from the stall.*

The Hebrew verb is to leap or skip. The picture is stall-fed calves — animals
kept in and well fed — released into open ground.

It is the least solemn image in the prophets, and it is the note the Old
Testament goes out on.

## The furnace

*The day is coming, burning like a furnace… not a root or a branch will be left.*

The same day is a furnace and a sunrise. Malachi does not soften either half.
Which one it is depends on the phrase *for you who fear My name*.

> **What comes next.** Four hundred years of silence, and then a man in the
> wilderness. The book's final lines promise Elijah before the great and dreadful
> day — and the first chapter of Luke says of John that he will go on before Him
> *in the spirit and power of Elijah*.
