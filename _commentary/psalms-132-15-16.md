God's reply, and it answers the prayer item by item with one word changed.

## The provision

*I will **bless her with plenty of food**.*

The Hebrew is *I will abundantly bless her provision* — **tsedah**, the word for supplies laid
in for a journey.

## The poor

*I will **fill her poor with bread**.*

The Hebrew is **evyoneha** — her needy, her destitute.

Not *feed*. **Asbia** — I will satisfy, fill to satisfaction.

The city's poor are the second item on the list, before anything about the priesthood or the
worship.

Mary's song has the same order: *He has filled the hungry with good things, and the rich He has
sent away empty*.

## The clothing

*I will **clothe her priests with salvation**.*

Here is the change.

The prayer, seven verses earlier, was *let Your priests be clothed with **righteousness*** — the
Hebrew **tsedeq**. See the note on Psalms 132:8–10.

The answer says **yesha** — salvation.

Righteousness was requested. Salvation is given.

## What Solomon did with it

When he prayed at the temple dedication he quoted the psalm's request and used the answer's
word:

*"Now therefore, arise, O LORD God, to Your resting place… **let Your priests be clothed with
salvation**."*

He asks for what God had already promised rather than for what the psalm had asked.

## The singing

*And her **faithful ones will sing aloud for joy**.*

The prayer had asked *let Your saints shout for joy*. The answer repeats it and intensifies the
verb.

## The pattern

Four requests in verses 8 to 10, and four answers in 13 to 16, in order.

Rise to Your resting place — *this is My resting place forever*.

Priests clothed with righteousness — *clothed with salvation*.

Saints shout for joy — *they will sing aloud for joy*.

For David's sake — *the LORD has sworn in truth to David*.

Every item is answered, and one is upgraded.

## The last three verses

*"There **I will make the horn of David grow**; I have prepared **a lamp for My Anointed**. His
enemies I will clothe with shame, but upon Himself **His crown shall flourish**."*

Two clothings in the psalm — priests in salvation, enemies in shame.

**A lamp for My Anointed** — the phrase that recurs through Kings whenever a bad king reigns and
Judah is not destroyed anyway: *for David's sake the LORD his God gave him a lamp in
Jerusalem*.

## Where the food promise goes

*I am the bread of life. He who comes to Me shall never hunger.*

And *He has filled the hungry with good things*.

## What the psalm is doing among the pilgrim songs

Fifteen short psalms for walking up to Jerusalem, and this is the one that tells them why the
building at the top is there.

A man made a vow about a resting place, and God answered with a longer speech than the request.

> **The word that changed.** *Righteousness* was asked for. *Salvation* was given — and Solomon,
> praying in the finished temple, quoted the answer rather than the request.
