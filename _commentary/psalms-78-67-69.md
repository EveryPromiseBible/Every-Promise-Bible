The end of a long history psalm, and it finishes on a choice.

## What the psalm is

Seventy-two verses of Israel's record, told as a warning to the next generation.

*I will open my mouth in a parable; I will utter dark sayings of old… **we will not hide them
from their children**, telling to the generation to come the praises of the LORD.*

Matthew quotes the opening line about the parables.

## What it has been rehearsing

The Red Sea, the water from the rock, the manna, the quail, the plagues in Egypt, and the
repeated turning back.

*They tested God in their heart by asking for the food of their fancy.*

*How often they provoked Him in the wilderness… **yes, again and again they tempted God**.*

## What happened at Shiloh

The psalm records it a few verses earlier: *He forsook the tabernacle of Shiloh, the tent He had
placed among men, and **delivered His strength into captivity, and His glory into the enemy's
hand**.*

That is the day the ark was captured at Aphek — the day Eli fell backwards off his seat and his
daughter-in-law named her son **Ichabod**, *the glory has departed*.

Shiloh was in Ephraim's territory, and it had held the tabernacle for centuries.

## The rejection

*He **rejected the tent of Joseph**, He did not choose the tribe of **Ephraim**.*

Ephraim was Joseph's younger son, given the greater blessing by Jacob with crossed hands. See
the note on Deuteronomy 33:13–17.

The northern tribes were called Ephraim collectively, and Shiloh was theirs.

## The choice

*But **He chose the tribe of Judah, Mount Zion, which He loves**.*

The Hebrew is **bachar** — chose.

Judah, the fourth son. Not the firstborn, not the favourite, and the man whose own chapter in
Genesis is the Tamar episode.

## What is not given as a reason

Merit.

The psalm has just spent sixty-five verses on national failure and does not suggest Judah was
better.

Psalm 132 gives the same choice with the same absence of explanation, and the only reason
offered is desire: *the LORD has chosen Zion. He has **desired it** for His home*. See the note
on Psalms 132:13–14.

## The building

*He **built His sanctuary like the heights**, like the earth **He founded forever**.*

The Hebrew is *like high palaces*, or possibly *like the heavens*.

Two comparisons: the height of the sky and the permanence of the ground.

## The last verses

*He also chose **David His servant**, and took him from the sheepfolds; from following the ewes
that had young He brought him, **to shepherd Jacob His people**, and Israel His inheritance.*

*So he shepherded them **according to the integrity of his heart**, and guided them by the
skilfulness of his hands.*

Seventy-two verses of history ending on a boy with sheep.

## Where Shiloh goes

*"Go now to My place which was in Shiloh, where I set My name at the first, and **see what I did
to it** because of the wickedness of My people Israel."*

Jeremiah, standing in the temple gate, telling Jerusalem not to say *the temple of the LORD, the
temple of the LORD* as if the building were a guarantee.

Shiloh had been the place too.

> **What is not offered as a reason.** Anything about Judah. The psalm spends sixty-five verses
> on failure, then names the choice and moves straight to a shepherd boy.
