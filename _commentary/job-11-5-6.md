Zophar is wrong about almost everything, and then says one of the best lines in the book.

## Who is speaking

Zophar the Naamathite, the third and harshest of Job's friends, opening his first speech.

He has just accused Job of babbling: *should a man full of talk be vindicated?*

What he wants is for God to show up and put Job in his place.

## The wish

*"But oh, if only God would **open His lips and speak against you**."*

He gets it, eventually. God does speak — out of a whirlwind, in chapter 38 — and the
speech is against Zophar. *My wrath is aroused against you and your two friends, for you
have not spoken of Me what is right, as My servant Job has.*

## The line

*"If He would show you **the secrets of wisdom**, for **true wisdom has two sides you
cannot see**."*

The Hebrew is **kiphlayim le-tushiyyah** — double, or twofold, to sound wisdom.

**Kephel** is a fold. The word for doubling something over on itself, as cloth is folded.

## What it means

Scripture has a fold in it. There is the plain reading, and there is what the plain reading
is folded over.

Paul says the same of the wilderness generation: *these things happened to them as
examples* — the Greek is **typoi**, types — *and they were written for our admonition*.

And of the Old Testament food laws: *a shadow of things to come, but the substance is of
Christ*.

## Where the doubling is visible

Isaac carrying the wood up the mountain his father would offer him on. See the note on
Genesis 22:1–2.

The bronze serpent on a pole, which Jesus applies to Himself in the third chapter of John.

Jonah, three days inside, which He names as the only sign He would give.

Every one of them is a real event that happened to real people, and every one of them is
folded over something else.

## Zophar's other half

*"Then you would know that **God has actually forgotten some of your guilt. He is giving
you less than your sin deserves**."*

The Hebrew is *God exacts from you less than your iniquity deserves*.

Zophar means it as an insult — *you are getting off lightly*. It is the only true thing in
his speech, and it is true of everyone.

## What he says next

*"Can you search out the deep things of God? Can you find out the limits of the Almighty?
They are **higher than heaven**… **deeper than Sheol**… **longer than the earth and broader
than the sea**."*

Four dimensions. Paul uses the same four of the love of Christ — *the width and length and
depth and height*. See the note on Ephesians 3:17–19.

## And then he ruins it

*"If you would prepare your heart… **if iniquity were in your hand, put it far away**…
then surely you could lift up your face without spot."*

The offer is: fix yourself first. Which is what all three friends say, at length, for
twenty chapters.

Job's answer is *though He slay me, yet will I trust Him* — and later, *I know that my
Redeemer lives*. See the note on Job 19:25–27.

> **The word under the line.** *Kephel* — a fold. Zophar wanted God to speak so Job would be
> crushed, and the sentence he chose says that wisdom is folded over, with a second face
> underneath.
