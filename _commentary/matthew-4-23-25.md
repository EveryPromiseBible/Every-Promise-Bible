A summary verse, and Matthew uses it twice to bracket the whole first section of his Gospel.

## The three things

*Jesus went all through Galilee, **teaching** in their synagogues, **announcing the good news** of the
kingdom, and **healing** every kind of disease and weakness.*

The Greek: **didaskon**, **kerusson**, **therapeuon**.

Three participles, in one sentence, describing one ministry.

## The order

Teaching, preaching, healing.

Matthew states it this way here, and again in chapter 9: *then Jesus went about all the cities and
villages, teaching in their synagogues, preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing every
sickness and every disease among the people*.

Two identical summaries, four chapters apart.

## What they enclose

Between them: the Sermon on the Mount, and then nine miracles in chapters 8 and 9.

The words first, then the works. Matthew arranges his material so the sermon and the miracles sit
inside the same frame.

## The two verbs

**Kerusso** — to herald, to announce as a public crier.

**Euangelion tes basileias** — the good news of the kingdom.

## The scope of the healing

*Every kind of **disease and weakness**.*

The Greek is **noson kai malakian** — sickness and infirmity.

**Malakia** — softness, feebleness. The chronic, wearing conditions rather than acute illness.

## The reach

*Word about Him spread **across all Syria**.*

The Roman province north of Galilee. Gentile territory, well outside the Jewish heartland.

## The four categories

*Those held down by **every sort of illness and pain**, those **tormented by demons**, those who **fell
in seizures**, those who **could not move their limbs**.*

The Greek is **selehniazomenous** — moonstruck, the ancient word for epilepsy.

**Paralutikous** — paralysed.

Matthew lists the conditions nobody could do anything about.

## The result

*And **He healed them**.*

The Greek is **etherapeusen autous**. No qualification.

## Where they came from

*From **Galilee**, from **the Ten Cities**, from **Jerusalem and Judea**, and from **the far side of the
Jordan**.*

**Dekapolis** — the Decapolis, a league of Greek cities east of the Jordan. Gentile.

Four regions, and two of them are outside Jewish territory.

## What has just happened

*From that time Jesus began to preach and to say, "**Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at
hand**."*

And the calling of four fishermen: *follow Me, and I will make you fishers of men*. See the note on
Matthew 4:18–22.

The ministry is weeks old.

## What comes immediately after

*And **seeing the multitudes**, He went up on a mountain, and when He was seated His disciples came
to Him.*

*Then He opened His mouth and taught them, saying: "**Blessed are the poor in spirit**."* See the
note on Matthew 5:1–4.

The crowds gather, and He goes up a hill and starts teaching.

## The pattern

The healing draws the crowd; the crowd gets the sermon.

Matthew's ordering makes the miracles the reason anybody was there to hear the Sermon on the
Mount.

## What Luke's version says

*They had come **to listen to Him and to be healed** of their diseases… because **power was flowing out
from Him**.* See the note on Luke 6:17–19.

The same two purposes, in the same order.

> **The frame.** Teaching, preaching, healing — stated here and again in chapter 9, with the Sermon
> on the Mount and nine miracles between them.
