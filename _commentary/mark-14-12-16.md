Two disciples are sent into a crowded city with no address, and told to follow a man
they have never seen.

## The day

*On the first day of Unleavened Bread, **the day the Passover lamb was killed**.*

Jerusalem at Passover held a population many times its usual size. Every household
needed a room, and rooms had been spoken for weeks earlier.

## The question

*"Where do You want us to go and **get the Passover ready** for You?"*

They are asking a practical question. He answers with a sign.

## The sign

*"**A man carrying a jar of water** will meet you. **Follow him**."*

Water-carrying was women's work in that culture. A man with a water jar on his
shoulder was unusual enough to pick out of a festival crowd without a description,
without a name, and without a street.

## What the two are given

Not the location. A person to follow, and the next instruction only when they arrive.

They are told what to say at the door, and nothing about what happens after.

## The words at the door

*"The Teacher asks: **where is the guest room** where I may eat the Passover with My
disciples?"*

**The Teacher** — no name. The householder knows who is meant.

The Greek for *guest room* is **kataluma**, the same word Luke uses for the inn with
no room in it at the beginning. The first *kataluma* had no room for Him. This one is
kept ready.

## The room

*"He will show you a **large room upstairs, already furnished and ready**."*

The Greek is **estromenon** — spread, laid out, with the couches already in place.

Nothing had to be arranged. It was furnished before the two disciples set out.

## What they found

*The two of them went into the city and found it **exactly as He had told them**.*

Mark says it as flatly as that. Every detail landed.

## What He is doing in the same week

Judas has already been to the chief priests. The arrangement of this room keeps the
supper's location out of Judas's hands until they are in it.

## What happens in that room

The bread and the cup, the announcement of the betrayer, the new covenant in His
blood, and the walk out to the Mount of Olives singing a hymn. See the note on
Mark 14:22–26.

## Which lamb was being prepared

*"On the first day of Unleavened Bread, **when they killed the Passover lamb**."*

**Mark** dates the errand by the slaughter.

*For indeed **Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us**. Therefore let us keep the feast.*

**Paul** in **1 Corinthians** 5, and the sentence has no *like* in it.

*The next day John saw **Jesus** coming toward him, and said, "**Behold! The Lamb of God who takes
away the sin of the world!**"*

*Then I looked, and behold, in the midst of the throne… stood **a Lamb as though it had been
slain**.*

Two disciples are sent to make arrangements for a meal, and the one giving the instructions is what
the meal has been about for fourteen centuries.

## Why the arrangement is discreet

Judas is at the table that night, and he does not know the address in advance. The signal in the
street is how two men find a room without anybody else being told where it is.

## What He already knew

*"Go into the city, and **a man will meet you**… he will show you a large upper room, furnished and
prepared."*

The instructions are given as fact before anything happens, and *they went, and found it just as He
had said to them* — a sentence **Mark** uses again a few chapters earlier about the colt. See the
note on Mark 11:1–6.

*"But **first the gospel must be preached to all the nations**."*

*"For **the Son of Man goes just as it is written of Him**, but woe to that man by whom the Son of
Man is betrayed!"*

Ten verses later, in the room they prepared. Both halves are held in one sentence: the going is
written, and the betrayer is not excused by it.

## What He does in the room

*And as they were eating, **Jesus** took bread, blessed and broke it, and gave it to them and said,
"Take, eat; **this is My body**."*

*"**This is My blood of the new covenant, which is shed for many.**"*

The disciples prepare a lamb. He hands round bread and a cup and identifies both with Himself, and
by the next afternoon the arithmetic is settled.

> **The other errand like it.** Six days before, two disciples were sent for a colt
> nobody had ridden, told what they would find and what to say to the owner — *the
> Lord has need of it* — and found it exactly so. See the note on Mark 11:1–6.
