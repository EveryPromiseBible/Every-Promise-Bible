The story is usually named after the younger son, but Jesus did not stop where
the party started. He told it to people who were annoyed that He ate with the
wrong sort, and the last scene is aimed straight at them. It is the only one of
the three stories in this chapter that does not end with something found.

## Listen to how he talks

Four sentences, and every one of them is worth slowing down for:

- *all these years I have worked for you* — the word is the one used of a hired
  hand's labour, not a son's life at home
- *I have never once disobeyed an order of yours* — an order. Not a word from a
  father; an instruction from a master
- *you never gave me so much as a young goat* — a man who has never asked
- *this son of yours* — not *my brother*

He has lived in the house for years and describes it as a job. The tragedy is
not that he is wrong about his own record — the father never disputes a word of
it — but that a faultless record has left him standing outside his own home,
angry, on the one night the place is full of music.

## The father's answer

*My child, you are always with me, and everything I have is yours.*

Read that against the complaint. He asked for a goat. He is told he owns the
estate. He has been *working for* what was already in his name. And the word the
father uses is not the formal one for a grown son; it is affectionate, the word
you would use for your own boy.

Nothing is being given to him in that sentence. He is being told what has been
true the entire time he was earning it.

---

## The thing he never says

Track the vocabulary. The younger son comes home and says *father* — twice. The
older son opens with *Look*, and never uses the word at all.

The younger one lost everything and knew what he was coming home to. The older
one lost nothing and did not know where he lived.

## Why Jesus ends it unfinished

The story stops. We are not told whether he came in. Every other thread in the
chapter closes — the sheep is carried home, the coin is found, the son is
embraced — and this one is left open, because the people Jesus was telling it to
were standing in exactly that spot while He told it.

> **On the elder son's picture of his father.** Notice that his bitterness is
> not aimed at his brother first — it is aimed at a father he believes has been
> withholding a goat for years. That is not what the father is like, and the
> story has already shown us so. A person's grievance is usually built on a
> picture of God that the facts do not support, and the correction here is not a
> rebuke but a piece of information: *everything I have is yours*.
