Forty years of sheep, and then a shrub.

## Where he is

*Moses was **tending the flock of his father-in-law Jethro**, the priest of Midian.*

The man raised in Pharaoh's household is working for his wife's father.

He had killed an Egyptian, buried him in the sand, and run when it came out. Stephen dates
it: forty years in Egypt, forty in Midian, forty in the wilderness.

Jethro is the same man who later tells him he is doing too much. See the note on
Exodus 18:19–23.

## How far out

*One day he led the sheep out **past the edge of the wilderness** and came to **Horeb, the
mountain of God**.*

The Hebrew is *the back side of the desert*.

Horeb is Sinai. He will bring two million people back to this exact mountain, and the sign
God gives him a few verses later is *when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you
shall serve God on this mountain*.

The proof of the call is only verifiable after obeying it.

## The appearance

*There **the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire, from the heart of a
bush**.*

The Hebrew for *bush* is **seneh** — a thornbush. Not a tree, and not impressive.

The same three-letter root that gives the mountain its other name.

## What he saw

*Moses looked — and the bush was on fire, **burning and burning, yet it was not burning
up**.*

The Hebrew is a participle: burning, continuous, and *the bush was not consumed*.

Fire that does not consume. Every other fire in Exodus does — the plague of hail, the
sacrifices, the mountain itself when God descends on it *in fire*.

## What he said

*"**I have to go closer**," Moses said to himself. "**I have to see this strange thing. Why
doesn't the bush turn to ash?**"*

The Hebrew is *I will now turn aside*.

He was at work with the sheep. The whole sequence turns on a shepherd deciding to walk off
his route.

## What God waited for

*When the LORD saw him **turn aside to look**, God called out to him.*

The voice comes after the turning, not before. See the note on Exodus 3:4–6.

## What is in a thornbush

Thorns are what the ground produced after Genesis 3. *Cursed is the ground for your sake…
both thorns and thistles it shall bring forth for you.*

And there is a crown of them, at the end.

God speaks out of the cursed thing, and the cursed thing is not destroyed by the fire.

## Where Moses' story goes from here

Five objections in a row — *who am I*, *what is Your name*, *they will not believe me*, *I
am slow of speech*, *send somebody else* — and God answers all five.

The name given at this bush is *I AM WHO I AM*.

## What Jesus called it

Arguing with the Sadducees about resurrection: *but even Moses showed in the burning bush
passage that the dead are raised, when he called the Lord "the God of Abraham, the God of
Isaac, and the God of Jacob." For He is not the God of the dead but of the living.*

He treats the bush scene as an argument about who is alive.

> **What made him stop.** Not the fire — a shepherd in that country would have seen scrub
> burn before. The thornbush was still there afterwards.
