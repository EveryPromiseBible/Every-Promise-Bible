Two verses, and grace is the subject of every verb in them.

## The appearing

*The grace of God **has come out into the open**.*

The Greek is **epephane** — appeared, shone out. The root of *epiphany*, and it was used
of a god showing himself or of a king arriving in a city.

Grace is described as an event that happened, at a time, in public.

## What it brought

*Bringing **rescue** with it.*

The Greek is **soterios** — saving, bringing salvation.

## Who saw it

*In **plain sight of everyone alive**.*

The Greek is *to all men*.

## The turn

*And that same grace **takes us in hand and teaches us**.*

The Greek is **paideuousa** — from **pais**, a child. It is the whole business of raising
one: teaching, training, correcting, disciplining.

The same word Hebrews uses of the Father's discipline: *whom the Lord loves He
chastens*.

Grace is the teacher in the sentence, and the teaching is not gentle in the Greek. It
trains.

## What it teaches, negatively

*To **turn our backs on a life with no room for God**, and on **the wanting that belongs to
a world like this one**.*

The Greek is **arnesamenoi** — to deny, to disown. It is the verb used of Peter in the
courtyard.

Two things disowned: **asebeia**, ungodliness, and **kosmikas epithumias**, worldly
desires.

## What it teaches, positively

Three adverbs in the Greek:

- **Sophronos** — with clear heads, self-controlled, sound-minded.
- **Dikaios** — rightly, justly. Toward other people.
- **Eusebos** — turned toward God.

Toward oneself, toward others, toward God. The three directions a life runs in.

## Where

*Here, now, **in the middle of the age we are actually living in**.*

The Greek is *in the present age* — **nun aioni**.

Not in retreat from it and not in the age to come. The training is for the place they are
standing.

## What follows

*Looking for the blessed hope and glorious appearing of our great God and Saviour Jesus
Christ.*

The Greek uses the same root — **epiphaneia**. Grace appeared once and will appear again,
and the verses are held together by the word.

## What it does not say

Grace is not described as the reason a standard can be lowered. It is named as the
teacher of the standard.

Paul does the same thing in Romans: *shall we continue in sin that grace may abound?
Certainly not.* See the note on Romans 6:1–2.

## Who is being written to

Titus, left in Crete *to set in order the things that are lacking*. The chapter before
this quotes a Cretan poet on his own countrymen and says the description is true.

Into that Paul writes that grace has appeared to everyone and is training them.

> **The subject of every verb.** Grace appears, grace brings, grace takes in hand, grace
> teaches. Paul does not make it a disposition God has — he makes it the thing doing the
> work in the sentence.
