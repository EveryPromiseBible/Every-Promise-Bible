**Paul** offers a reading of the whole episode, and he offers it as a guess.

## The word

*"**Perhaps** this is why he was away from you for a while."*

The Greek is **tacha** — perhaps, possibly. It occurs twice in the New Testament.

**Paul** is proposing a providence and will not assert it. He does not tell Philemon that God
arranged the theft.

## The passive

*"**He was away from you** for a while."*

**Echoristhe** — he was separated. A passive, where the active would have been *he ran away*.

**Paul** chooses the verb that does not name the crime, in the sentence where he is offering the
generous reading. He has already conceded the crime plainly, two paragraphs later, and does not need
to do it twice.

## The arithmetic

*"**So that you could have him back, and have him for good.**"*

**Pros horan** against **aionion** — for an hour, and forever.

A few months of loss set against a permanence, and the permanence is not about the household
arrangement. Onesimus and Philemon are going to outlast the institution they are arguing about.

## The sentence

*"**Not a slave any longer, but far more than that — a brother.**"*

**Ouketi hos doulon all' hyper doulon, adelphon agapeton** — no longer as a slave but above a slave,
a beloved brother.

*There is neither Jew nor Greek, **there is neither slave nor free**, there is neither male nor
female; for **you are all one in Christ Jesus**.*

*Where there is neither Greek nor Jew, circumcised nor uncircumcised, barbarian, Scythian, **slave
nor free, but Christ is all and in all**.*

**Colossians** 3, written to the same town, probably read aloud in the same room, and carried by
Onesimus.

## What the letter does not do

Demand abolition.

**Paul** does not order Philemon to free him and does not argue the institution. He writes a private
letter that makes the arrangement impossible to maintain, and then adds *knowing you will go further
than what I have asked.*

*"Both in the flesh and in the Lord."*

The two spheres are named separately, and **Paul** asks for the brotherhood in both.

## Who else stopped being called a slave

*"**No longer do I call you servants**, for a servant does not know what his master is doing; but I
have called you friends, for all things that I heard from My Father I have made known to you."*

**John** 15, and the Greek is **doulous** — the same noun. **Jesus** makes the same reclassification
in the upper room, and gives the same reason **Paul** gives: what the two parties now share.

*Therefore **you are no longer a slave but a son**, and if a son, then an heir of God through
**Christ**.*

> **The word Paul will not drop.** *Perhaps.* He offers the providential reading of a theft and a
> runaway as a possibility, not a verdict — and then makes the household arrangement impossible by
> naming what the man now is.
