Two letters, one purpose, and the purpose is not to tell them anything new.

## The address

*Dear friends.*

The Greek is **agapētoi** — beloved. Four times in this chapter alone.

## The count

*This is **the second letter I have sent you**.*

The Greek is **deuteran… epistolēn**.

The only place in the New Testament where a writer refers to his own previous letter by number.

## The purpose of both

*And **both of them were written for one reason**.*

## What it is

*To **shake awake the clear thinking that is already in you** by putting old truths back in front of
you.*

The Greek is **diegeirō en hupomnēsei tēn eilikrinē dianoian** — to stir up your sincere mind by
reminder.

## The two words

**Diegeirō** — to rouse from sleep. Used of waking Jesus in the boat.

**Eilikrinēs** — from **heilē**, sunlight, and **krinō**, to judge. Tested by holding it up to the
sun; unmixed, without hidden flaw.

## What he grants them

The thinking is already sound.

He does not diagnose confusion. He says the mind is clear and needs waking.

## What he had already said

*So I am going to keep bringing all of this up. I will not let it slide. **You know it already**.*
See the note on 2 Peter 1:12–13.

The same claim, twice in one short letter.

## The two authorities

*Hold on to **what God's own prophets said long before any of this happened**.*

*And hold on to **the charge that came to you from us — the men our Lord and Savior sent out**.*

## What that pairing is

Prophets and apostles. The Old Testament and the apostolic word, set side by side as one deposit.

*Having been built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ Himself being the
chief cornerstone.* See the note on Ephesians 2:19–22.

## Why it matters here

The next paragraph is about people who will deny the second coming: *scoffers will come in the last
days… saying, "**Where is the promise of His coming?**"* See the note on 2 Peter 3:3–4.

The remedy set against them is not a new argument. It is two old ones.

## What the letter says next about time

*But, beloved, do not forget this one thing, that **with the Lord one day is as a thousand years,
and a thousand years as one day**.* See the note on 2 Peter 3:8.

And *the Lord is not slack concerning His promise… but is **longsuffering toward us, not willing that
any should perish***. See the note on 2 Peter 3:9.

Delay is reinterpreted as patience.

## What he says about Paul

*As also in all his epistles, speaking in them of these things, **in which are some things hard to
understand**.* See the note on 2 Peter 3:15–16.

And in the same breath he calls them Scripture.

## The last line of the letter

*But **grow in the grace and knowledge** of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.* See the note on
2 Peter 3:17–18.

> **The stated purpose.** Two letters, and the reason given for both is the same: not to inform, but
> to wake up a mind he says is already clear.
