The hardest story in Acts, and Luke reports it without a word of explanation.

## What was not required

*"Before you sold it, was it not your own? And once it was sold, was the money
not yours to do with as you pleased?"*

Peter states it twice, plainly. The land was theirs. The money was theirs. Nobody
had commanded them to sell anything, and no percentage was owed.

That removes the obvious reading. This is not about how much they gave.

## What it was about

*"Why has Satan filled your heart, so that you would **lie to the Holy Spirit**
and keep back part of the price?"*

The Greek behind *keep back* is **nosphizomai** — to embezzle, to set aside for
yourself out of something already dedicated.

It is the word the Greek Old Testament uses for Achan, who kept back devoted
things from Jericho and hid them under his tent.

## Who was lied to

*"You have not lied to men. You have lied to God."*

Peter says *the Holy Spirit* in one line and *God* in the next, about the same
act. It is one of the clearest statements in Scripture of who the Spirit is.

## The pretence

Chapter 4 has just described Barnabas doing exactly this — selling a field,
bringing the money, laying it at the apostles' feet. Luke names him.

Ananias performs the same scene with a portion, and presents it as the whole. The
sin is not the withholding. It is the performance.

## What Luke records afterwards

*A deep fear came over everyone who heard of it.*

The word is **phobos**, and Luke repeats it a few verses later after Sapphira,
extending it to *the whole church*.

He offers no commentary, no softening, and no moral. The young men wrap him and
carry him out, and the narrative moves on to signs and wonders in the next
paragraph.

> **Where this sits in Acts.** Immediately before it, a community where *there was
> not a needy person among them*. Immediately after it, crowds bringing the sick
> into the streets. The story is set between two descriptions of extraordinary
> grace, and Luke placed it there.
