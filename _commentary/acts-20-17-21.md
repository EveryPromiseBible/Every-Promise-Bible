Paul is in a hurry to reach Jerusalem, so he sends for the elders rather than going to
them.

## The arrangement

*From **Miletus** he sent to **Ephesus** and called for the elders of the church.*

Thirty miles each way. He is skipping Ephesus deliberately — Luke says *he was hurrying
to be at Jerusalem, if possible, on the Day of Pentecost* — and the elders walk to him.

This is the only speech in Acts addressed to a Christian congregation. Every other one is
to a crowd, a court, or a synagogue.

## The record

*"**You know how I lived among you the whole time, from the first day I set foot in
Asia**."*

He appeals to what they saw. He had been in Ephesus about three years, longer than
anywhere else.

## The three descriptions

*"Serving the Lord **with a humble heart**, **with tears**, and **through the trials that
came to me by the plots of the Jews**."*

He mentions the tears three times in this speech — here, in verse 31 about warning them
night and day, and at the end when they weep on his neck.

## What he did not do

*"**I kept back nothing that would help you**."*

The Greek is **hupostello** — to draw back, to shrink from, to withhold. It was used of
furling a sail and of a person keeping quiet out of fear.

He says it again in verse 27: *I have not shunned to declare to you the whole counsel of
God.*

## Where he taught

*"But told it to you and taught you **publicly and from house to house**."*

Two settings. The hall of Tyrannus, where Luke says he reasoned daily for two years, and
the houses.

## Who he taught

*"**Testifying to both Jews and Greeks**."*

The same message to both, which was not obvious in that city.

## The two things

*"About **a change of mind toward God**, and **faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ**."*

He compresses three years of teaching into two items.

**A change of mind** — the Greek is **metanoia**, from *meta*, change, and *nous*, mind.
It is a word about what a person thinks, and the direction is given: *toward God*.

**Faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ** — the second half, and the object is named.

## What he says next

*"And see, now I go bound in the spirit to Jerusalem, **not knowing the things that will
happen to me** there, except that the Holy Spirit testifies in every city, saying that
**chains and tribulations await me**."*

And then: *"**But none of these things move me**; nor do I count my life dear to myself,
so that I may finish my race with joy."*

## How the meeting ends

*He knelt down and prayed with them all. Then they all wept freely, and **fell on Paul's
neck and kissed him**, sorrowing most of all for the words which he spoke, that **they
would see his face no more**.*

Luke was there — the passage is in the *we* sections — and this is one of the few scenes
in Acts he describes as an eyewitness to the emotion.

## The one saying of Jesus

Paul closes with a line found nowhere in the Gospels: *"**It is more blessed to give than
to receive**."*

Acts preserves it and nothing else does.

> **What the speech is.** A man laying out three years of work in front of the people who
> watched him do it, and inviting them to check. The summary of the content is two
> phrases long.
