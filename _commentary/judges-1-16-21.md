*The LORD was with Judah, and they took the hill country. But they could not
drive out those in the valley, for they had iron chariots.*

Two halves, and the second one does not follow from the first.

## The Presence and the chariots

The verse says God was with them. Then it explains a failure by pointing at
enemy equipment.

Those two things cannot both be doing the work. If the LORD was with Judah, then
iron was never the deciding factor — and the book itself proves it a few chapters
later, when Deborah and Barak face Sisera's **nine hundred** iron chariots and
the LORD routs every one of them in a river valley.

Same terrain. Same technology. Opposite outcome. What changed was not the metal.

## What Caleb was doing in the same paragraph

*As Moses had promised, Hebron was given to Caleb, and he drove out the three
sons of Anak from it.*

An eighty-five-year-old walks into a fortified hill town and clears out giants by
name, in the same six verses where a whole tribe stops at flat ground because of
chariots.

The writer puts them side by side without comment. He does not need to say
anything.

## What the chapter is beginning

Read on and the word *but* keeps returning: this tribe did not drive them out,
and neither did that one, and neither did the next. Chapter 2 opens with the
consequence spelled out — the peoples left behind become a snare.

The land had been given. God had said so in the first paragraph of the book:
*I have already handed the land to them.* What is being described here is not a
promise falling short. It is a promise not fully entered.

> **Where the eyes went.** Judah looked at the valley and saw iron. Caleb looked
> at Hebron and said *if the LORD is with me, I will drive them out, exactly as
> He said.* One of them was reckoning with the chariots and one with the promise,
> and the chapter records what each of them ended up with.
