Paul is one long sentence into this letter and shows no sign of stopping. In
Greek, verses 3 to 14 are a single unbroken sentence — the longest in the New
Testament — and this is the middle of it.

## The word behind "bought out of our captivity"

**Apolutrosis.** The root is *lutron*, a ransom — the price paid to free a
prisoner of war or to buy a slave out of ownership. The prefix makes it a
release accomplished by that payment.

It is a commercial word being used about people, and it carries a specific
picture: someone was held, a price was named, the price was paid, and the person
walked out. Paul says where the price came from in the same breath — *by His
blood*.

## "Poured over us without measure"

The phrase for grace here is extravagant even by Paul's standards: the *sheer
wealth* of His grace, and a verb meaning to make something overflow, to have more
than enough of it.

He is not describing a sufficiency. He is describing a surplus.

## What the surplus is for

*It brought with it every kind of wisdom and clear-eyed good sense.*

That is an unexpected turn. The overflow of grace does not arrive as feeling — it
arrives as understanding, and specifically as being let in on something: *He let
us in on the secret of what He wants.*

> **Where the sentence is going.** The purpose Paul names is enormous: that
> everything, in heaven and on earth, would be gathered up under one head in
> Christ. The Greek verb is from arithmetic — adding a column of figures and
> writing the total at the top. All of it, summed up, in Him. That is the end the
> ransom was paid toward, and Paul reaches it without once pausing for a full
> stop.
