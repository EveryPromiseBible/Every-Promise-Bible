A man who has been blind from birth is interrogated twice by the religious
authorities, and he wins.

## What they want him to say

*"**Give the glory to God.** We know this man is a sinner."*

It is a legal formula — an oath, roughly *tell the truth before God*. And they
attach the verdict they want in the same breath.

They are not asking him a question. They are telling him what the answer is.

## What he will not do

*"**Whether He is a sinner, I do not know.**"*

He declines to go past what he has. He is not a theologian and does not pretend to
be one.

## What he does have

*"**One thing I do know: I was blind, and now I see.**"*

There is no argument against that sentence. They can dispute the doctrine; they
cannot get at the fact.

He was blind on that street every day of his life — the neighbours in this chapter
argue about whether it is even the same man. And he is standing there looking at
them.

## The second interrogation

*"What did He do to you? How did He open your eyes?"*

They ask him to go through it again. They had already heard it — verse 15 records
the first telling.

They are looking for a discrepancy.

## His answer

*"I have already told you, **and you would not listen**. Why do you want to hear it
again? **Do you want to become His disciples too?**"*

A beggar, unschooled, being examined by the Sanhedrin, and he asks them if they are
signing up.

It is the funniest line in John's Gospel and it is delivered by a man with
everything to lose.

## What it cost him

*And they threw him out.* Excommunicated — put out of the synagogue, which in that
world meant out of the community.

His own parents had already backed away: *he is of age, ask him*, because *they
feared the Jews.*

## Who came looking

*Jesus heard that they had cast him out, and **when He had found him**, He said,
"Do you believe in the Son of God?"*

He goes and finds him. The man had never seen His face — he had been sent to the
pool to wash while still blind, and had not laid eyes on Him.

*"Lord, I believe." And he worshipped Him.*

> **What He says about the whole thing.** *For judgement I have come into this
> world, that those who do not see may see, and that those who see may be made
> blind.* The Pharisees ask whether He means them, and He answers: *if you were
> blind, you would have no sin. But now you say, "We see." Therefore your sin
> remains.*
