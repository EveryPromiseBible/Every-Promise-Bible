---
key: Titus 1:1–4
title: "God, who cannot lie"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The longest greeting in the shortest of **Paul**'s letters to an individual, and there is a clause in
it he uses nowhere else.

## The two lines of work

*"**My work runs along two lines: the faith of the people God chose, and their knowledge of the
truth** — the truth that fits with a life given over to God."*

Faith and knowledge, and the knowledge is defined by what it produces: **kat' eusebeian**, according
to godliness.

Doctrine that does not touch how somebody lives is not, in this sentence, knowledge of the truth.

## The hope

*"**Resting on the hope of life that never ends.**"*

**Ep' elpidi zoes aioniou.**

## The clause

*"Which **God, who cannot lie**, promised before the beginning of time."*

The Greek is **ho apseudes Theos** — the unlying God. The adjective occurs once in the New
Testament.

*God is not a man, that He should lie.*

*Thus God, determining to show more abundantly to the heirs of promise the immutability of His
counsel, confirmed it by an oath, that by **two immutable things, in which it is impossible for God
to lie**, we might have strong consolation.*

**Hebrews** 6, and the two immutable things are the promise and the oath.

## Before the beginning of time

*"**Promised before the beginning of time.**"*

**Pro chronon aionion** — before eternal times.

Promised to whom, before there was anybody to promise it to? **Paul** uses the phrase twice, and the
other occurrence answers it:

*Who has saved us and called us with a holy calling, **not according to our works, but according to
His own purpose and grace which was given to us in Christ Jesus before time began**.*

The grace was given, in **Christ**, before there was a creation to give it to. The promise is
intra-Trinitarian before it is ours.

*Just as **He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world**.*

## The two occurrences of Saviour

*"**God our Saviour**"… "**Christ Jesus our Saviour**."*

**Paul** uses **soter** six times in this three-chapter letter, and three times of the Father and
three times of the Son, in alternating pairs. He does not distinguish the work.

## Grace and peace

*"**Grace and peace** from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Saviour."*

Every letter **Paul** writes opens with the two words, in that order, and never the other way round.

> **The adjective that occurs once.** *Apseudes* — the God who cannot lie. Paul puts it in the
> opening sentence of a letter to a man managing a church on Crete, whose own poet had said
> *Cretans are liars, every time*.
