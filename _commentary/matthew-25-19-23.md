Two servants, two different results, and one commendation repeated word for word.

## The delay

*"**After a long time** the master of those servants came home and settled accounts."*

The third of three parables in a row about an interval. The bridegroom is late, the master is a
long time, and the point of both is what happens while nothing is happening.

## What the first says

*"Master, **you entrusted me** with five talents. See, I have gained five more."*

He begins with what he was given, not with what he produced. Both servants do — *you entrusted
me*, in both speeches — and neither takes credit for the capital.

## The commendation

*"**Well done, good and faithful servant. You have been faithful with a few things; I will put you
in charge of many things. Come and share your master's joy.**"*

## And again

*"**Well done, good and faithful servant. You have been faithful with a few things; I will put you
in charge of many things. Come and share your master's joy.**"*

Word for word. **Matthew** writes it out twice rather than saying *and he said the same to the
second*, which is how you make sure a reader notices.

One man produced five talents and one produced two, and the sentence does not change by a syllable.

## What is being commended

*Faithful.* **Pistos** — trustworthy, reliable. Not productive, not gifted, not successful.

*Moreover it is required in stewards that one be found **faithful**.*

**1 Corinthians** 4, and **Paul** says it is the only requirement of the office.

## A few things

*"You have been faithful with **a few things**."*

A talent was twenty years' wages. The master calls it *a few*, which tells you the scale of what
comes next rather than the scale of what was handed over.

*For **our light affliction, which is but for a moment**, is working for us a far more exceeding and
eternal weight of glory.*

*For **I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the
glory** which shall be revealed in us.*

## The joy

*"**Come and share your master's joy.**"*

The Greek is **eiselthe eis ten charan tou kyriou sou** — enter into the joy of your lord.

It is not a bonus, a percentage, or a promotion in the ordinary sense. The reward named is
admission to something that already belonged to somebody else.

*Who for **the joy that was set before Him** endured the cross, despising the shame, and has sat
down at the right hand of the throne of God.*

**Hebrews** 12, and the joy the master invites them into turns out, in the letter, to be what
carried Him through the worst of it.

*"These things I have spoken to you, **that My joy may remain in you, and that your joy may be
full**."*

## What is not proportioned

The praise, the promotion and the joy are identical for both men. Only the capital differed, and
the capital was the master's decision. See the note on Matthew 25:14–18.

> **Why Matthew writes it twice.** Five talents and two talents, and the commendation does not
> change by a word. The one thing being measured is faithfulness with somebody else's property, and
> both men led with the same phrase: *you entrusted me*.
