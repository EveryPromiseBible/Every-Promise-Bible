Samson's best-known fight, and it starts with his own countrymen handing him over.

## How he got there

Three thousand men of Judah had come down to the rock of Etam to arrest him.

*"Do you not know that the Philistines rule over us? What is this you have done to us?"*

He agrees to be tied up, on one condition: *swear to me that you will not kill me
yourselves*.

They bind him with two new ropes and bring him out.

The men of Judah are not resisting the occupation. They are managing it, and Samson is the
problem.

## The arrival

*As he came to **Lehi**, the Philistines **rushed at him, shouting**.*

The Hebrew is *shouted against him*.

A bound man, delivered by his own tribe, and a crowd running at him.

## The Spirit

*Then **the Spirit of the LORD came powerfully upon him**.*

The Hebrew is **vattitslach alav ruach YHWH** — rushed upon him.

The same phrase used when he tore the lion, and when he struck the thirty men at Ashkelon. See
the note on Judges 14:5–7.

## The ropes

*The ropes on his arms **became like burnt flax**, and the bonds **simply melted from his
hands**.*

New ropes, and the image is of something that has already been through a fire and only looks
like rope.

## The weapon

*He spotted the **fresh jawbone of a donkey**, snatched it up.*

The Hebrew is **lechi chamor teriyyah** — a moist jawbone of an ass.

*Fresh* matters. A dried bone shatters; a fresh one has some give.

It is also, for a Nazirite, a dead body — one more clause of the vow gone. He had already eaten
honey out of a lion's carcass. See the note on Judges 14:12–14.

## The count

*And with it **struck down a thousand men**.*

## The chant

*"With the jawbone of a donkey, **heaps upon heaps** — with the jawbone of a donkey I have struck
down a thousand men."*

The Hebrew is a pun that does not survive translation: **chamor chamorathayim** — donkey, and
heaps. Same consonants.

And **lechi** means both *jawbone* and is the name of the place.

He composes a couplet about it on the spot.

## The disposal

*When he finished speaking, **he threw the jawbone away**.*

Nothing is kept. It was a bone on the ground twenty minutes earlier and it goes back on the
ground.

Nobody enshrines it, which is the opposite of what happened to the bronze serpent — Israel kept
that one and burned incense to it for centuries until Hezekiah broke it up.

## The name

*And he named that place **Ramath-lehi, the Hill of the Jawbone**.*

## What happens immediately after

*Then he became very thirsty; so he cried out to the LORD and said, "**You have given this great
deliverance by the hand of Your servant; and now shall I die of thirst?**"*

*So God split the hollow place that is in Lehi, and water came out.*

The man who has just killed a thousand men nearly dies of thirst twenty minutes later, and says
so out loud.

The passage records both without embarrassment.

## The verse after that

*And he judged Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines.*

## Where it ends

Gaza, blind, at a mill, and a boy holding his hand. See the note on
Judges 16:26–27.

> **What he did with it afterwards.** Threw it away. A fresh bone off the ground, a thousand men,
> a couplet, and then he drops it and complains about being thirsty.
