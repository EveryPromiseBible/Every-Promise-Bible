A rule about corporal punishment, and the reason given for the limit is about dignity.

## The court

*When two men have a dispute, they are to **bring it to court**, and the judges will
decide, **clearing the innocent and condemning the guilty**.*

The Hebrew is *justifying the righteous and condemning the wicked* — **tsadaq** and
**rasha**, the two verbs Proverbs pairs. See the note on Proverbs 17:15–21.

That is the definition of a working court, stated in one line.

## The sentence

*If the guilty one deserves a beating, the judge shall **make him lie down and be flogged
in his presence**.*

**In his presence** — the judge who passed the sentence has to watch it carried out.

The Hebrew is *before his face*. There is no delegating it to somewhere out of sight.

## The measure

*The number of lashes **fitting his offense**.*

The Hebrew is *according to his fault, by number*.

Proportion is required, and the total is to be counted.

## The ceiling

*He may give **forty, but no more**.*

## The reason

*To go beyond that would **degrade your brother before your eyes**.*

The Hebrew is *lest your brother be dishonoured before you* — **niqlah**, made light of,
held in contempt.

Two words matter in that sentence.

**Your brother.** He has just been convicted and sentenced, and the law still calls him
that.

**Before your eyes.** The concern is what watching it would do — to him, and to the people
watching.

## What the Jews did with it

Rabbinic practice reduced it to thirty-nine, so that a miscount could not push the total
over the limit.

Paul records receiving it five times: *from the Jews five times I received **forty stripes
minus one**.*

Two hundred lashes, by his own count.

## What Jesus received

Roman scourging had no limit. The **flagellum** was leather thongs weighted with bone or
metal, and the number was left to the soldiers.

The Gospels record the fact in a single word and describe nothing.

## What Isaiah says about the appearance

*His visage was **marred more than any man**, and His form more than the sons of men.* See
the note on Isaiah 52:13–15.

The Hebrew is *disfigured*.

## The Hebrew for a blow

**Makkah** — a stroke, a wound, a plague. It is the word in this passage for the beating,
and the same word runs through the curses of Deuteronomy 28 for the diseases. See the note
on Deuteronomy 28:58–68.

The word for a lash and the word for a plague are the same word.

## The verse that follows immediately

*You shall **not muzzle an ox while it treads out the grain**.*

Paul quotes it twice about paying those who preach.

The law puts a limit on beating a convicted man and a prohibition on starving a working
animal in consecutive verses.

> **What the ceiling protects.** Not the guilty man's comfort — the beating happens. The
> reason Moses gives is that past a point it stops being a sentence and starts being
> degradation, in front of witnesses.
