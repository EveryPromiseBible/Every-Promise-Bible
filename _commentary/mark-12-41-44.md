He sits down opposite the treasury and watches people give money, which is the least likely thing in
the chapter.

## Where He is

*"**Jesus** sat down across from the temple treasury and watched the people dropping their money
in."*

The Court of the Women had thirteen trumpet-shaped receptacles along the wall. Metal coins in a
metal horn made a noise, and the amount was audible.

**Mark** says He **watched** — **etheorei**, the imperfect: He kept watching. It is the verb behind
*theatre*.

## What has just happened

The whole chapter is confrontation: the vineyard tenants, the tax question, the resurrection
question, the greatest commandment, and then:

*"**Beware of the scribes**, who desire to go around in long robes, love greetings in the
marketplaces, the best seats in the synagogues… **who devour widows' houses**, and for a pretence
make long prayers."*

That is the verse immediately before this paragraph. He warns about men who consume widows' houses,
and then sits down and watches a widow put her house in the box.

**Mark** does not comment on the juxtaposition. He simply puts the two next to each other.

## The amount

*"**Two small copper coins**, together hardly worth a penny."*

**Lepta duo** — the **lepton** was the smallest coin in circulation, worth about six minutes of a
labourer's day.

She had two. She could have kept one, and nobody would ever have known.

## What He calls them over for

*"He called His disciples over to Him."*

**Proskalesamenos** — He summoned them. The same verb He uses to call the twelve in chapter 3 and to
call the crowd in chapter 7.

Whatever He is about to say, He does not want them to miss it.

## The verdict

*"**This poor widow has put in more than all the rest of them.**"*

**Pleion panton** — more than all. Not *more, proportionally*; the comparison is stated flatly and
the arithmetic is His.

## The reason

*"They all gave **out of what they had to spare**. She, **out of her poverty**, gave everything she had
— **all she had left to live on**."*

The Greek of the last phrase is **holon ton bion autes** — her whole life, her whole living.

**Bios** — the word for livelihood, the means of life. She did not give a large percentage. She gave
the category.

## Who else did that

*For **you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He
became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich.*

**Paul**'s two words are **eptocheusen** — He became **ptochos**, the beggar-poor, which is the exact
adjective **Mark** uses of the widow.

*"**Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one's life for his friends.**"*

Within the week, the man watching her would give His whole **bios** in the same city, and be
observed doing it by a crowd that thought it was worth nothing.

## What He does not do

Stop her.

He has just said the scribes devour widows' houses. He watches the system take her last two coins
and says nothing to her — and four verses later, walking out of that building, He says of it *not one
stone shall be left upon another*.

> **The word.** *Holon ton bion autes* — her whole living. And the man watching, who had just
> condemned the people running the treasury, said nothing to stop her, and inside the week gave His
> own in the same city.
