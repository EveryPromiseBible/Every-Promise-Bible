The second sabbath in Antioch of Pisidia, and the crowd is the problem.

## The turnout

*The following Sabbath **nearly the whole city gathered to hear the word of God**.*

One sermon, one week's word of mouth. See the note on Acts 13:42–43.

## The reaction

*But **when the Jews saw the crowds, they were filled with jealousy**.*

The Greek is **zelou** — zeal, jealousy. The trigger Luke names is the size of the crowd,
not the content.

They had invited him to speak the week before.

## What they did

*And they **contradicted what Paul was saying and spoke against it**.*

The Greek is **antelegon** — spoke against — and **blasphemountes**.

## The reply

*Then Paul and Barnabas **spoke out boldly**.*

The Greek is **parresiasamenoi** — from **parrhesia**, freedom of speech, the right to say
anything.

## The order

*"It was **necessary that the word of God be spoken to you first**."*

Paul states the priority as an obligation. He does it in every city — synagogue first —
and he writes it into Romans: *to the Jew first and also to the Greek.* See the note on
Romans 1:16–17.

## The judgment

*"But since you **push it away and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life**."*

The Greek for *push away* is **apotheisthe** — to thrust from oneself.

**Judge yourselves unworthy.** Paul does not pronounce them unworthy. He says the
verdict is self-issued, and the word is **krinete** — you judge.

## The turn

*"Look — **we are turning to the Gentiles**."*

## The authority

*"For this is what the Lord has commanded us: '**I have made you a light for the nations,
so that you may carry salvation to the ends of the earth**.'"*

Isaiah 49, spoken to the Servant.

Paul applies it to himself and Barnabas — *the Lord has commanded **us***. He reads the
Servant's commission as one he is inside.

Simeon had quoted the same passage over the infant in the temple: *a light to bring
revelation to the Gentiles, and the glory of Your people Israel.*

## What happened

*Now when the Gentiles heard this, **they were glad and glorified the word of the Lord**.
And **the word of the Lord was being spread throughout all the region**.*

## And then

*But the Jews stirred up the devout and prominent women and the chief men of the city,
raised up persecution against Paul and Barnabas, **and expelled them from their region**.*

*But they shook off the dust from their feet against them, and came to Iconium. **And the
disciples were filled with joy and with the Holy Spirit**.*

Luke ends the episode on the disciples' joy rather than on the expulsion.

## The pattern

It repeats through Acts — Iconium, Lystra, Thessalonica, Berea, Corinth. Paul says it
again at Corinth: *your blood be upon your own heads; I am clean. From now on I will go to
the Gentiles.*

And he still goes to the synagogue in the next city.

> **What turned them.** Not the sermon — they had asked for it and heard it out. Luke
> names the crowd. The following week nearly the whole city came, and that is the verse
> the jealousy is attached to.
