Seven miles of Scripture, a meal, and then He is gone.

## "He made as if to go further on"

He does not invite Himself in. He keeps walking, and they have to stop Him.

*"**Stay with us.** The day is nearly over."*

The Greek for *urged* is strong — they constrained Him, pressed Him.

## Who becomes the host

They invited Him into their house, and then:

*When He had sat down at the table with them, **He took the bread** and gave
thanks over it, and broke it, and handed it to them.*

The guest takes the bread. That is the host's part, and He takes it without asking.

## The four verbs

*Took… gave thanks… broke… handed it to them.*

The same four, in the same order, as the upper room. The same four as the five
thousand.

## What opened their eyes

*And **their eyes were opened and they knew Him**.*

The same passive as verse 16 — *their eyes were held* — now reversed. Something had
been holding them; something releases them.

And it is at the breaking of the bread that it happens. Not during the seven
miles of exposition, and not when He first appeared on the road.

## "And He was gone from their sight"

The instant they recognise Him He vanishes.

He does not stay for the meal He has just begun. They get one look, and then they
are alone with an opened Bible and a broken loaf.

## What they say to each other

*"**Were our hearts not burning inside us** while He talked with us on the road,
while He was opening the Scriptures to us?"*

That is the sentence, and note what they point at. Not the moment of recognition.
The road. The Scriptures being opened.

The burning had already happened, before they knew who He was, while a stranger
was walking them through Moses and the Prophets.

## What they do about it

*They rose up that very hour and returned to Jerusalem.*

Seven miles back, in the dark, immediately, after telling Him it was too late in
the day to travel.

> **What they tell the eleven.** *How He was known to them in the breaking of
> bread.* Of everything on that road — the exposition, the walk, the vanishing —
> that is the phrase they use for it.
