Two blind men follow Jesus indoors, and He asks them one question before doing
anything.

## The question

*Do you believe that I am able to do this?*

Not *do you have enough faith*, and not *how strong is your faith*. The object of
the question is His ability, not their capacity.

That is worth holding on to when the next line arrives.

## "According to your faith"

*Let it be done for you according to your faith.*

Read alone, it sounds like a measurement — as much healing as you have faith for.
Read as the answer to the question He just asked, it means something narrower:
according to what you have just said you believe about Me.

Their faith had exactly one item in it. They believed He could. That was enough
to be going on with.

## What they called Him

*Have mercy on us, Son of David.* It is the first time in Matthew anybody uses
that title, and it is a messianic one — two blind men see who He is before most
of the people watching.

Matthew, who is building a case throughout his Gospel that Jesus is the promised
son of David, gives the line to them.

> **The instruction they ignore.** *See that nobody hears about this* — and they
> went out and spread it everywhere. Matthew records the disobedience plainly and
> without approval. It is the reaction of people who have just received their
> sight, and the Gospel does not tidy it up.
