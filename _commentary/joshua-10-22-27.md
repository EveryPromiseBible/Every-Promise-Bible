Five kings are brought out of a cave, and what Joshua does with them is a lesson for the
men watching.

## What happened before

Five Amorite kings had allied against Gibeon, which had made peace with Israel. Joshua
marched all night from Gilgal, and the LORD *cast down large hailstones from heaven on
them*, and the sun stood still.

The five kings hid in a cave at Makkedah, and Joshua had great stones rolled over the
mouth of it and men set to guard it while the army finished the pursuit.

## The five

*The kings of **Jerusalem, Hebron, Jarmuth, Lachish, and Eglon**.*

The head of the alliance is Adoni-Zedek of Jerusalem — a name meaning *lord of
righteousness*, and a variant of Melchizedek, *king of righteousness*, who had blessed
Abram in the same city. See the note on Genesis 14:17–20.

## Who is called forward

*Joshua **called the men of Israel together** and told **the commanders who had gone with
him**.*

The whole nation watches; the commanders act.

## The instruction

*"Come, **put your feet on the necks of these kings**."*

The Hebrew is *place your feet upon the necks*.

It was a standard ancient gesture of conquest, carved on reliefs across the Near East.

## What is not asked of them

They did not capture these kings. The hailstones did more damage than the sword — Joshua
10 says so outright: *there were more who died from the hailstones than the children of
Israel killed with the sword.*

The commanders are put on the necks of men they did not defeat.

## The four words

*"**Don't be afraid or discouraged. Be strong and courageous**."*

The same four given to Joshua himself at the start of the book, three times in the first
chapter, and given to Joshua by Moses before that.

He passes on what he was told.

## The promise

*"**This is what the LORD will do to every enemy you fight**."*

Not *this is what you will do*. The LORD is the subject.

The gesture is a demonstration, and what it demonstrates is stated in the next sentence.

## The execution

*Then Joshua **struck the five kings down** and put them to death, and **hung their bodies
on five trees until evening**.*

*At sunset he ordered them **taken down**.*

Deuteronomy required it: *his body shall not remain overnight on the tree… for he who is
hanged is accursed of God.*

The same law the Jewish leaders invoked at the crucifixion. See the note on
John 19:31–34.

## Where they ended up

*And **thrown into the very cave where they had hidden**. Great stones were piled at the
entrance — and they are there to this day.*

The hiding place becomes the tomb, sealed with the same stones that had trapped them
alive.

## Where the picture goes

*Sit here at My right hand **until I make your enemies a footstool under your feet**.* See
the note on Psalms 110:1.

And Paul: *the God of peace will crush Satan **under your feet** shortly.*

> **What the commanders did.** Walked forward and stood on five necks, after a battle
> decided by hail and a long day. Joshua tells them what it means before they step off.
