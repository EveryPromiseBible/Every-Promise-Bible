Two captured kings describe men they killed, and their description is the striking
thing in the passage.

## The question

*"**What sort of men did you kill at Tabor?**"*

Gideon has run down Zebah and Zalmunna, the two Midianite kings, after the rout with
the three hundred. He asks about an incident at Tabor that the book has not
recorded.

## The answer

*"**Men like you**," they answered. "**Each one had the bearing of a king's son.**"*

The Hebrew is *according to the form of the sons of a king*.

Enemies, under no obligation to flatter, describing men they had killed — and the
word they reach for is royal.

## Who the men were

*"**Those were my brothers, my own mother's sons.**"*

Gideon's own family. The Midianites had killed his brothers at Tabor and he had not
known how, until now.

## What that says about Gideon

*"Men like you."*

The two kings are looking at the man who defeated them, and they say his brothers
looked like him — and that all of them looked like princes.

Set that beside where the book found him: *beating out wheat in a winepress —
hiding, really, keeping the grain out of Midian's sight.* And beside what he said of
himself: *my clan is the weakest in all Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's
house.* See the note on Judges 6:11–16.

The angel had called him *mighty man of valour* while he was crouching in a pit. Two
enemy kings, chapters later, independently say the same thing about his whole
family.

He was that all along. He was the last person to know it.

## Jether

*He turned to **Jether, his firstborn**, and said, "Get up, kill them." But **the boy
did not draw his sword; he was afraid**, for he was only a youth.*

Gideon hands the sword to his son, and the boy cannot do it.

Judges records the failure without comment, and the fear is given as the reason and
the age as the excuse.

## What the kings say to that

*"**Get up yourself and do it — a man's strength is measured by the man.**"*

The Hebrew is *as the man is, so is his strength.*

Said by men about to die, to the man who will kill them, and it is a taunt about the
son.

## The ornaments

*And took **the ornaments shaped like crescent moons** that hung on their camels'
necks.*

The crescents were Midianite religious symbols.

Gideon takes the gold from that campaign and makes an ephod out of it, and *all
Israel played the harlot with it there, and it became a snare to Gideon and to his
house.*

The chapter that shows him told who he really is ends with him making an idol out of
the proceeds.

> **The two answers to "what sort of men?"** The enemy said *like a king's son*.
> Gideon had said *the least in my father's house*. Both statements are in the same
> book about the same man, and only one of them was ever true.
