The **Daleth** stanza, and it is the lowest point in the psalm.

## The opening

*My soul **lies flat and clinging to the dust**.*

The Hebrew is **davqah le'aphar naphshi** — my soul clings to the dust.

**Dust** is where the dead go, and where the serpent was told to travel.

Whatever is happening to this man, he begins the stanza face down.

## The request

*Revive me, **breathe me back, the way You promised**.*

The Hebrew is **chayyeni** — make me live.

The verb runs through the whole psalm — it appears nine times, always as a request.

**The way You promised** — the request is grounded in something already said. He is not
asking for a new arrangement.

## The account

*I **told You all my roads, and You answered**.*

The Hebrew is *I declared my ways and You heard me*.

## The teaching

*Now **teach me, walk me through the ways You keep**. Help me to **understand the path You've
marked**.*

Three requests for instruction in two verses.

## The melting

*My soul is **melting down from sheer heaviness**; **lift me and hold me steady**, as You
said.*

The Hebrew is *my soul melts from grief*.

**As You said** — the second appeal to a promise in eight verses.

## The two roads

*Keep **the deceiving road far off from me**… I've **chosen out the faithful, truthful way**.*

The Hebrew is *the way of falsehood* against *the way of faithfulness*.

## The grip

*I **hold on tight to all Your steady words**; O LORD, **don't let me stand here put to
shame**.*

The Hebrew is *I have clung to Your testimonies* — the same verb as verse 25.

His soul clings to the dust, and he clings to the word. The stanza is built on one verb
used twice in opposite directions.

## The last verse

*I'll **run the road of Your commands wide open**, for **You have set my cramped heart free to
move**.*

The Hebrew is *for You shall enlarge my heart*.

The stanza opens with a man in the dust and closes with him running.

## The turn

Nothing in between explains the change. There is no rescue narrated, no circumstance
altered, and no answer described.

What happens between verse 25 and verse 32 is eight requests.

## The word for enlarge

**Rachab** — to widen, to make room. It is the root behind **Rehoboth**, the well Isaac
named when he finally dug one nobody fought him for: *now the LORD has made room for us*.

## Where the same movement appears

*I waited patiently for the LORD; and He inclined to me, and heard my cry. He also brought
me up out of a horrible pit, out of the miry clay, **and set my feet upon a rock**, and
established my steps.* See the note on Psalms 40:1–3.

Dust and clay at the start, and a firm footing at the end.

> **What changes across the stanza.** Not the situation. He is clinging to dust in the
> first line and running in the last, and the only thing that happens in between is that
> he asks — twice on the ground of something already promised.
