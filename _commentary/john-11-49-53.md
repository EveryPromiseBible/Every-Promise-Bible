The worst man in the room says the truest thing in the chapter, and does not know it.

## What prompted the meeting

Lazarus. *Then many of the Jews who had come to Mary, and had seen the things Jesus did,
believed in Him.* See the note on John 11:41–44.

And the council's problem, stated aloud: *what shall we do? For this Man works many signs. If
we let Him alone like this, everyone will believe in Him, and the Romans will come and take
away both our place and nation.*

They do not dispute the miracles. They are doing a political calculation.

## Who speaks

*One of them, named **Caiaphas, who was high priest that year**.*

John says *that year* three times in the passage. The high priesthood was supposed to be for
life; under Rome it had become an appointment, and the Romans moved men in and out.

Caiaphas held it for eighteen years, which meant he was good at the politics.

## The tone

*"**You know nothing at all. You are not thinking it through**."*

He opens by insulting the council.

## The proposal

*"**It is better for us that one man die for the people than that the whole nation be
destroyed**."*

Read as policy, it is the oldest argument there is: one life against many.

*Better for us.* He says it.

## What John does with it

*He **did not say this on his own**. Being high priest that year, **he was prophesying**.*

The Greek is **eprophēteusen**.

John claims the sentence was given to him. The office, not the man.

The same thing happens to Balaam — a hired diviner who opens his mouth to curse and finds
words in it he did not choose. *And the LORD put a word in Balaam's mouth.* See the note on
Numbers 23:4–6.

## What the sentence actually says

*That Jesus was going to **die for the nation**.*

The preposition is **huper** — on behalf of, in place of.

Caiaphas meant: one man's execution buys the nation's safety.

John reads it as substitution, and the theology of it is exact.

## The clause John adds

*And **not for that nation only, but so that He would gather into one the scattered children of
God**.*

The Greek is **sunagage eis hen** — gather together into one.

Caiaphas' arithmetic was one man against one nation. John extends it past the border.

The same shepherd sentence: *other sheep I have which are not of this fold; them also I must
bring, and there will be one flock and one shepherd*. See the note on John 10:16–18.

## The outcome

*So **from that day on they were making their plans to put Him to death**.*

The decision is dated to this meeting.

The raising of Lazarus is what triggers it, which is why John puts the seventh sign here.

## What Caiaphas says at the trial

*"I put You under oath by the living God: tell us if You are the Christ, the Son of God!"*

And when He answers, Caiaphas tears his clothes — which the high priest was forbidden to do.

## The other unwitting prophecy

Pilate wrote the title over the cross in three languages and refused to change it: *what I
have written, I have written*. See the note on John 19:12–16.

Two men who wanted Him dead, and both left an accurate statement on the record.

> **What the office produced.** John says the man did not speak on his own — and the sentence
> he was given, meant as a political calculation, states substitution exactly.
