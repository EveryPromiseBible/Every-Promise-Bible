One verse, and the impossibility is stated before any reason is given.

## The claim

*"**No one can serve two masters**."*

The Greek is **oudeis dunatai** — no one is able.

Not *should not*. Cannot. It is a statement about what is possible.

## The word for serve

**Douleuein** — to be a slave to, from **doulos**, bondservant.

Not employment. A slave belonged to one household, was owned rather than hired, and had no hours
of his own to sell elsewhere.

Employees can hold two jobs. Slaves cannot have two owners.

## The two outcomes

*"Either he will **hate the one and love the other**, or he will **hold on to the one and think
nothing of the other**."*

The Greek for the second pair is **anthexetai** — will cling to, hold fast — and
**kataphronesei**, will look down on.

Two ways of describing the same failure. Whichever way it goes, one of them loses.

## What is not offered

A third option in which both are served at a reduced level.

## The application

*"**You cannot serve God and money both**."*

The Greek is **mamona** — an Aramaic loanword, **mammon**, meaning wealth or property.

Its root may be the same as **amen** — that which is trusted, what you rely on. The word for
money and the word for *so be it* may share a stem meaning *firm*.

## What is being said about money

Not that it is evil. Jesus names it as a rival master, which is a claim about its capacity, not
its morality.

Money is the only thing in the Gospels He puts opposite God in this construction.

## Where the verse sits

Immediately before: *do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust
destroy… **for where your treasure is, there your heart will be also***. See the note on
Matthew 6:19–21.

And the lamp of the body, and the single eye.

Immediately after: *therefore I say to you, **do not worry about your life**, what you will eat or
what you will drink.* See the note on Matthew 6:25–34.

## The order of the three

Treasure, then eye, then master, then worry.

The passage moves from what you store, to what you look at, to what owns you, to what you are
afraid of. Jesus treats them as one subject.

## The therefore

Verse 25 begins with **dia touto** — for this reason.

The instruction not to worry is grounded in the verse about the two masters. Anxiety about
provision is treated as a symptom of divided service, not as a separate problem.

## What Paul says

*For **the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil**, for which some have strayed from the
faith in their greediness.* See the note on 1 Timothy 6:9–10.

The love of it, and *a* root of *all kinds* — Paul is more precise than the proverb usually
quoted from him.

## What is said to the rich

*Command those who are rich in this present age **not to be haughty, nor to trust in uncertain
riches** but in the living God, who gives us richly all things to enjoy.*

Not to divest. Not to trust.

## What the parable adds

*"You cannot serve God and mammon." **Now the Pharisees, who were lovers of money, also heard all
these things, and they derided Him**.*

Luke records the reaction, and then Jesus tells the story of the rich man and Lazarus.

> **The verb.** **Douleuein** — to be owned. Employees can hold two jobs; slaves cannot have two
> owners, and that is the whole argument.
