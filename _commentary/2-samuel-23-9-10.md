Everyone else fell back. He did not move, and by the end his hand would not open.

## Who he is

*Next came **Eleazar son of Dodai, the Ahohite, one of the three mighty men**.*

The second name in David's list of thirty-seven. The three are a category of their own above the
thirty.

## Where he stood

*He **stood with David** when they taunted the Philistines gathered for battle.*

The Hebrew is **becharpam** — in their defying, their reproaching. The same root as Goliath's word
in the valley: *I defy the armies of Israel*.

The provocation that made David famous is what this man stands in the middle of.

## What everyone else did

*The men of Israel **fell back**.*

The Hebrew is **vaya'alu ish Yisrael** — the men of Israel went up, i.e. withdrew.

Not a rout. A withdrawal, orderly enough, and he simply stayed where he was.

## What he did

*But **Eleazar held his ground and struck down the Philistines**.*

The Hebrew is **vayaqam** — he arose, he stood.

No manoeuvre is described. The entire account of his fighting is that he did not leave.

## The detail

*Until **his hand grew weary and froze to his sword**.*

The Hebrew is **vatidbaq yado el-hacherev** — his hand clung to the sword.

**Dabaq** — to cleave, to stick fast. The word in Genesis 2 for a man cleaving to his wife, and in
Deuteronomy for cleaving to the LORD.

## What the word means here

He could not let go. The grip had set.

Somebody had to open his fingers afterwards.

## Who won

*The LORD won a great victory that day.*

The Hebrew is **vaya'as YHWH teshu'ah gedolah** — the LORD worked a great salvation.

The man stands and swings, and the sentence credits the LORD. Both are in the text without any
attempt to divide the labour.

## The troops

*The troops came back to Eleazar — but **only to strip the fallen**.*

They return for the plunder of a battle they were not in.

## The one beside him

*Shammah… **stationed himself in the middle of the field**, defended it, and struck down the
Philistines.* See the note on 2 Samuel 23:11–12.

A field of lentils. The same verb, the same refusal to move, over a vegetable patch.

## What the three have in common

None of them advances. All of them stay.

The list of David's mighty men records, as its highest tier, three men whose distinguishing act was
holding a position.

## Where the chapter sits

Immediately after David's last words — *He has made with me an everlasting covenant, ordered in all
things and secure*. See the note on 2 Samuel 23:5.

And immediately before the census that ends the book.

> **The grip.** *His hand clung to the sword* — the same verb Genesis uses for a man cleaving to his
> wife. By the end of the day somebody else had to open his fingers.
