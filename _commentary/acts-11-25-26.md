A man goes looking for someone everybody else had lost track of, and a name gets coined.

## The search

*Then Barnabas **left for Tarsus to look for Saul**, and when he found him he brought him back to
Antioch.*

The Greek is **anazetesai** — to search out, to hunt for. The word implies the person is not
easy to find.

Saul had been sent home to Tarsus years earlier, after the Jerusalem believers realised the
Hellenists were trying to kill him. See the note on Acts 9:28–31.

He had been there somewhere between five and ten years. Nobody had come for him.

## Who Barnabas is

The man who had vouched for Saul in Jerusalem when the disciples were afraid of him — *Barnabas
took him and brought him to the apostles*.

His name means **son of encouragement**, given to him by the apostles after he sold a field and
laid the money at their feet.

Twice now he has fetched Saul into a room that would not otherwise have had him.

## Why Antioch

The gospel had gone there through unnamed men from Cyprus and Cyrene who *spoke to the
Hellenists, preaching the Lord Jesus* — the first deliberate Gentile preaching in the book, done
by people Luke does not name.

*And the hand of the Lord was with them, and a great number believed.*

Jerusalem sent Barnabas to look at it, and *when he came and had seen the grace of God, he was
glad*.

## What he saw

**Ten charin tou Theou** — the grace of God. Luke says it was visible.

## The year

*For **a whole year the two of them met with the church** and taught a great many people.*

**Eniauton holon** — a full year. Luke marks the length, which he rarely does.

A year of teaching, by two men, in a Gentile city, before the name appears.

## The name

*And it was **at Antioch that the disciples were first called Christians**.*

The Greek is **chrematisai** — a word for being publicly styled, transacting business under a
name, receiving an official designation.

**Christianoi** is a Latinate formation: the *-ianus* suffix marked a household or a partisan
group. *Herodianoi* were Herod's people. *Christianoi* were Christ's people.

## Where it appears

Three times in the New Testament, and never as a self-description.

Here. Agrippa: *you almost persuade me to become a Christian*. And Peter: *if anyone suffers as
a Christian, let him not be ashamed*. See the note on 1 Peter 4:15–16.

The believers called themselves disciples, brothers, saints, and *the Way*. The name that stuck
was given to them from outside.

## Not in Jerusalem

The name is coined in a Gentile city, by outsiders, about a congregation that a man had to be
fetched from obscurity to teach.

Antioch becomes the sending church — *now separate to Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which
I have called them*. See the note on Acts 13:1–3.

Every missionary journey in the book starts from the city where the name started.

## What the two of them do next

The chapter ends with a famine, and the Antioch believers sending relief to Judea *by the hands
of Barnabas and Saul*.

The first recorded act of the first church called Christian is sending money to the church that
had been afraid of one of them.

> **The word.** **Chrematisai** — to be publicly styled, to go by a name in the world's business.
> It was a designation given from outside, and it was given after a year of teaching, not before.
