God's first question out of the whirlwind, and it is about a construction site.

## The question

*"**Where were you** when I laid the foundations of the earth? Tell Me, if you
understand so much."*

Job has been demanding an audience for thirty chapters. He gets one, and it opens
with a question he cannot begin to answer.

## The vocabulary

*Who **marked out its measurements**? Who **stretched the line** across it and set
it true? On what were **its footings** sunk? Who laid **its cornerstone**?*

Every term is a builder's term — the measuring line, the footings, the cornerstone.

God describes creation as a job of work with a plan and a surveyor.

## Who was watching

*"…that morning when **the stars sang together and all the sons of God shouted for
joy**."*

Two groups, or one described twice in Hebrew parallel: the morning stars, and the
sons of God.

**Sons of God** — **bene ha-elohim**. In Job the phrase means angels; the same
expression opens chapter 1, where *the sons of God came to present themselves
before the LORD, and the adversary came among them too.* See the note on
Job 1:6–8.

They were present at the founding of the earth, and they were singing.

## What that dates

Angels existed before the earth did. They watched it being laid.

Which places them before Genesis 1:1 in the order of things, and puts a spectator's
gallery at the creation account.

## The verbs

**Sang** and **shouted**. The Hebrew for the second is the shout of triumph — the
noise Israel made when the walls of Jericho fell, and when the ark came into the
camp.

Nothing in the passage is quiet.

## Why it is God's opening move

Job's friends had argued about causes for thirty chapters. God does not enter that
argument.

He asks about the morning of the world, and then goes on for four chapters about
the sea's doors, the storehouses of snow, the ostrich, the wild donkey, and the
horse that laughs at fear. See the note on Job 38:1–3.

Not one of the questions is about Job's conduct.

## What the singing means for the man on the ash heap

He is being shown a world that was made deliberately, measured, footed, and
cornerstoned, and cheered while it went up.

The answer to *why is this happening to me* is never given. What is given is the
architect.

> **Where the cornerstone turns up again.** *The stone which the builders rejected
> has become the chief cornerstone.* Job hears about the cornerstone of the earth;
> the Psalms and the Gospels name one the builders threw out.
