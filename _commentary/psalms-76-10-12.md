One line, and it settles what happens to everything thrown at God's people.

## The claim

*Even **human rage** will end up **praising You**.*

Not tolerated. Not survived. It ends up as praise.

The Hebrew for *rage* is the hot word — burning anger, fury. It is what men do at
their worst, and the psalm says the finish of it is God's glory.

## The second half

*And **what rage is left, You put on like a belt**.*

The Hebrew is *You gird Yourself with the remainder of wrath*. Whatever anger
does not end up as praise is strapped on as a belt — held, restrained, and worn
by Him.

A belt is what you fasten so nothing swings loose. What is left over is not
running free; it is buckled to Him.

## Haman

The whole of Esther is this verse in narrative.

- his **rage** at one man not bowing produced the decree
- the decree produced the fast and Esther's approach to the throne
- his **rage** built a seventy-five-foot gallows, and it is what he was hanged on
- his **boast** to his wife and friends brought out the advisers' verdict against
  him
- his **pleading** for his life across a couch is what the king read as assault

Every furious thing Haman did was load-bearing in his own destruction, and the
Jews came out of it with the ring, the house and a feast that is still kept.

Not one of those steps required a miracle, and God is not named in any of them.

## What the psalm says about kings

*He **cuts down the pride of rulers**; He is a terror to the kings of the earth.*

The Hebrew for *cuts down* is to cut off, as of a vine being pruned — the word
used of a grape harvest.

Ahasuerus, who ruled from India to Cush, spent the whole book being moved by his
own temper and never knew who was moving him.

## The instruction in the middle

*Make your vows to the LORD your God and **keep them**.*

Set between the rage that turns to praise and the kings who are cut down, the
psalm's one command to the reader is small and domestic: say what you will do, and
do it.

> **How the psalm begins.** *In Judah God is known; His name is great in Israel.*
> Known, and named. The book of Esther is the same God at work in a place where He
> was neither.
