Marching orders, and the reason given for them is a gap.

## Where they start

*Early the next morning Joshua and all the Israelites **broke camp at Shittim**.*

Shittim is where the Balaam story ends badly — *the people began to commit harlotry with the
women of Moab*, and twenty-four thousand died in the plague.

The last camp before the land is the site of the last disaster.

## The waiting

*And came to the edge of the Jordan, and there they camped **before crossing**. **After three
days**.*

Three days of looking at a river they have no way over.

The chapter later notes the season: *the Jordan overflows all its banks during the whole time
of harvest*.

## The order

*"When you see **the ark of the covenant** of the LORD your God being carried by the
Levitical priests, then **leave your places and follow it**."*

The instruction is not a route. It is an object to follow.

Forty years earlier the same people had followed a pillar of cloud and fire. Here the sign is
a gold box carried on poles by men on foot.

## The reason

*"That way **you'll know which road to take, for you have never come this way before**."*

The Hebrew is *you have not passed this way before, yesterday or the day before*.

The argument is not that the ark knows the way and they do not. It is that nobody in the
column has been here.

## The distance

*"But **keep your distance — about three thousand feet** between you and the ark. **Don't
come near it**."*

The Hebrew is two thousand cubits.

That is a long way — most of a mile. The people at the back of the column can see the ark and
nothing else about the crossing.

## Why the gap

Two reasons work at once.

Two thousand cubits is the traditional Sabbath day's journey — the distance a man could
travel on the seventh day, measured from the edge of the camp.

And at that range, the ark is visible to everybody. A box carried by four men in the middle
of two million people would be invisible at close quarters.

The instruction that keeps them back is the instruction that lets them all see.

## What the ark contained

The tablets of the law, Aaron's rod that budded, and a pot of manna — under a gold lid with
two cherubim facing each other.

Hebrews lists them. The lid is the mercy seat.

## What happens next

*"Sanctify yourselves, for tomorrow the LORD will do wonders among you."*

And then the priests' feet touch the water and the river stops upstream at a town called
Adam. See the note on Joshua 3:14–17.

## The ark going first elsewhere

*And the ark of the covenant of the LORD **went before them** for three days' journey, to
search out a resting place for them.* See the note on Numbers 10:33–36.

Same arrangement, at the start of the wilderness years and at the end of them.

## The one time it goes wrong

Years later, at Perez Uzzah, the ark is put on a new cart and a man steadies it when the oxen
stumble and dies on the spot.

Poles, carried by Levites, was always the instruction.

## What the crossing was for

*"That all the peoples of the earth may know the hand of the LORD, that it is mighty, that
you may fear the LORD your God forever."* See the note on Joshua 4:19–24.

> **The reason for the gap.** Not distance for its own sake — a mile of open ground, so that
> everybody in a column of two million could see one gold box and know where the road went.
