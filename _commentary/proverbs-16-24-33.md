Ten proverbs, and most of them are about what a mouth does.

## The honeycomb

*Kind words are like a honeycomb, **sweet to the soul and healing to the bones**.*

The Hebrew is **imrei-noam** — words of pleasantness.

**Tsuph devash** — a honeycomb, the dripping comb.

## The two effects

**Matoq lannephesh** — sweet to the soul. And **marpe la'atsem** — healing to the bone.

The soul and the skeleton. The proverb claims a physical effect and does not hedge it.

**Marpe** — healing, a cure, health. The same root as **rapha**, the word in *I am the LORD who
heals you*. See the note on Exodus 15:22–27.

## Why bones

The bone is the deepest part of the body in Hebrew idiom, and the place where grief is felt: *my
bones waste away*, *my bones are troubled*.

Words reach that far, in both directions.

## The way that seems right

*There is a way that seems right to a man, **but its end is the way to death**.*

The proverb appears twice in the book, word for word, here and in chapter 14.

The Hebrew for *seems right* is **yashar lifnei-ish** — straight before a man.

Not obviously wrong. Straight-looking.

## The worker's hunger

*A worker's hunger does its work for him, for **his own empty mouth keeps driving him on**.*

The Hebrew is **nephesh amel** — the appetite of the labourer.

A wry proverb: hunger is a manager. Nobody has to motivate a man who needs to eat.

## The four about the mouth

*A worthless man **digs up evil**, and his words are like **a scorching fire**.*

The Hebrew is **koreh ra'ah** — digs a pit of evil. And **ke'esh tsarevet** — like a scorching
fire.

*A crooked man spreads strife, and **a whisperer separates close friends**.*

**Nirgan** — a whisperer, a slanderer. The proverb is repeated in chapter 18: *the words of a
talebearer are like tasty trifles, and they go down into the inmost body*.

## The violent man

*A violent man **lures his neighbour** and leads him down a road that is not good.*

**Yephatteh** — entices, persuades, seduces.

*He **winks his eyes**, plotting mischief. He **purses his lips** and evil is done.*

Two gestures, no words. The proverb notices that most of what a manipulative man communicates is
done with his face.

## The grey head

*Gray hair is a crown of glory, gained by **a life lived rightly**.*

The Hebrew is **ateret tipheret** — a crown of beauty.

The qualification matters: **bederek tsedaqah timmatse** — it is found in the way of
righteousness. Age alone is not the crown.

## The city

*Better to be **slow to anger than mighty**, and **to rule your spirit than to take a city**.*

The Hebrew is **erek appayim** — long of nostrils, the idiom for patience. It is the phrase used
of God at Sinai: *the LORD, the LORD God, merciful and gracious, **longsuffering***.

**Moshel beruchov** — ruling his own spirit.

The comparison is with military conquest, which was the ancient world's highest measure of a
man.

## The last one

*The lot is cast into the lap, **but its every decision is from the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **umeYHWH kol-mishpato** — and from the LORD is its whole judgment.

Lots were used for real decisions: dividing the land, choosing Saul, identifying Jonah, and
replacing Judas.

The chapter that has spent ten verses on human speech and self-control ends by saying the
outcome of a thrown stone belongs to God.

> **The claim.** *Healing to the bones.* **Marpe** — the same root as the divine name given at
> Marah, attached here to what somebody says to somebody else.
