The hardest saying in Mark, and the last verse tells you what it was about.

## What has been said

The scribes from Jerusalem have just announced their verdict: *He has Beelzebul, and by the
ruler of the demons He casts out demons*. See the note on Mark 3:20–22.

He has answered it — a divided kingdom cannot stand, and a strong man's house is only plundered
after the strong man is tied up.

Then this.

## The scope

*"I tell you the truth: **every sin people commit can be forgiven them, and every slander they
speak can be forgiven**."*

The Greek is **panta ta hamartemata** — all the sins. And **hai blasphemiai hosa ean
blasphemesosin** — the blasphemies, however many they blaspheme.

The sentence starts with the widest amnesty in the Gospels, and Mark's wording piles it up:
all, and however many.

Everything after this has to be read against that opening.

## The exception

*"But **whoever speaks against the Holy Spirit is never forgiven**; that guilt stays with them
for all time."*

The Greek is **enochos estin aioniou hamartematos** — guilty of an eternal sin.

## The explanation

*He said this **because they were saying, "He has an unclean spirit in Him."***

Mark supplies the reason in the next sentence, which is the most important thing in the
passage.

The saying is not general. It is attached to one specific accusation, made by one specific
group, in one specific room.

Matthew records the same explanation.

## What the accusation was

The Spirit is the one at work in the exorcisms. Jesus says so in Matthew's version: *if I cast
out demons by the Spirit of God, surely the kingdom of God has come upon you*.

They watched the Spirit set people free and said it was the devil doing it.

That is what is being named — not swearing, not a moment of doubt, not a lapse under pressure.

## Who was doing it

Men who had come from Jerusalem specifically to assess Him, who could not deny the works, and
who chose an explanation they knew was false rather than accept the obvious one.

## The pattern in the same Gospel

Peter denies Him three times with curses, and is on a beach eating fish a few weeks later.

The disciples all abandon Him and are all recommissioned.

Paul describes himself as *a blasphemer, a persecutor, and an insolent man* — and adds *but I
obtained mercy because I did it ignorantly in unbelief*.

Blasphemy is on his own list of what he did, and he is the apostle to the Gentiles.

## What the passage does not do

It does not describe a state anybody stumbles into.

The one thing the text supplies as a definition is verse 30, and it is about a settled verdict
delivered by men who knew better.

## What the chapter goes on to

*Then His brothers and His mother came… and He said, "Who is My mother, or My brothers?"*

And *whoever does the will of God is My brother and My sister and mother*.

The paragraph about the unforgivable is immediately followed by the widest definition of family
in the Gospel.

## What the same Gospel opens with

*The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God.*

And John baptising *for the remission of sins*.

> **The verse that defines it.** Mark supplies it himself: *because they were saying, He has an
> unclean spirit*. The saying is attached to one accusation, made by men who could not deny
> what they had watched.
