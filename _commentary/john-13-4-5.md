Four verbs, and John records each one as if it mattered.

## What He knew first

*Jesus, knowing that **the Father had given all things into His hands**, and that He had come
from God and was going to God.* See the note on John 13:1–3.

That is the sentence the action comes out of. Not despite knowing it — because of it.

The washing is what a man does who has just been handed everything.

## The rising

*So He **rose from the table**.*

Mid-meal. Nobody asked.

## The clothing

*And **laid His outer clothing aside**.*

The Greek is **tithesin ta himatia** — he lays down his garments.

**Tithemi** is the verb He uses of His life: *I **lay down** My life for the sheep*. And *no
one takes it from Me, but I lay it down of Myself*. See the note on John 10:16–18.

John uses the same word here, and again in verse 12 when He picks the garments back up —
**elaben**, took again, which is also the resurrection verb in John 10: *I have power to lay
it down, and I have power to take it again*.

The paragraph is shaped like the incarnation.

## The towel

*He took a towel and **tied it around His waist**.*

The Greek is **diezosen** — girded Himself.

That is slave's dress. A servant tied a cloth round the waist to work in.

## Whose job it was

The lowest servant in the house. Feet in sandals on those roads needed washing before a meal,
and a Jewish household would not require even a Hebrew slave to do it — it was reserved for
Gentile slaves, or for a wife or child.

Nobody in the room had volunteered.

Luke records what they had been arguing about that same evening: *there was also a dispute
among them, as to which of them should be considered the greatest*.

## The water

*Then He **poured water into a basin**.*

He fetches it Himself.

## The washing

*And began to **wash His disciples' feet, one man after another**.*

The Greek is a present infinitive: He kept washing.

## Whose feet

All of them.

Judas is in the room and does not leave until verse 30. John has already said *the devil
having already put it into the heart of Judas Iscariot to betray Him*.

Those feet are in the basin.

## Peter's objection

*"**You shall never wash my feet!**"*

*"If I do not wash you, **you have no part with Me**."*

*"Lord, **not my feet only, but also my hands and my head!**"*

Twice wrong in three sentences, and both times at full volume.

## The distinction

*"**He who is bathed needs only to wash his feet, but is completely clean**."*

Two different washings. The bath happens once; the feet get dirty on the way to dinner.

The same distinction the priests knew — washed entirely at consecration, and hands and feet at
the laver from then on. See the note on Leviticus 7:35–38.

## What He says afterwards

*"Do you know what I have done to you? You call Me Teacher and Lord, and you say well, for so
I am. **If I then, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one
another's feet**."*

*"**I have given you an example**, that you should do as I have done to you."*

The Greek is **hupodeigma** — a pattern, a specimen.

## The blessing

*"**If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them**."*

Knowing is assumed. The blessing is attached to the second clause.

> **Where the humility comes from.** The verse before it. He knew everything had been put into
> His hands — and the next thing He did was take His coat off and pick up a basin.
