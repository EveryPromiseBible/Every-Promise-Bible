Three days after the greatest deliverance in their history, they cannot find a drink.

## What has just happened

The Red Sea. The song of Moses, with Miriam and the timbrels: *I will sing to the LORD, for He
has triumphed gloriously*.

*The horse and its rider He has thrown into the sea.* See the note on Exodus 15:1–5.

The singing ends at verse 21. Verse 22 begins the journey.

## The three days

*For **three days they traveled through the wilderness and found no water**.*

They had just watched a sea open. Three days later there is nothing to drink.

The narrative puts the two things back to back without comment.

## Marah

*They came to Marah, but **they could not drink its water, for it was bitter** — that is why the
place is called **Marah, which means bitter**.*

Water, at last, and undrinkable.

The Hebrew **marah** is the word Naomi uses of herself: *do not call me Naomi; call me Mara, for
the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me*.

## The grumbling

*So **the people grumbled against Moses**, saying, "**What are we to drink?**"*

The Hebrew is **vayyillonu** — murmured.

It is the first of many. The word will recur through the wilderness narratives, and this is where
it starts.

## What Moses does

*Moses **cried out to the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyitzaq** — cried out, the same verb used of Israel groaning in Egypt.

He does not answer the complaint. He takes it upward.

## The wood

*And **the LORD showed him a piece of wood**. He threw it into the water, **and the water became
sweet**.*

The Hebrew is **etz** — a tree, or a piece of wood.

The LORD **showed** him — **vayyorehu**, from **yarah**, to point out, to instruct. The same root
as **torah**.

## What the wood did

The text does not say the wood had properties. It says God showed it to him.

**Etz** is the word for a living tree and for the timber cut out of one. It is the word in the
sentence Paul reaches for about the cross: *cursed is everyone who hangs on a **tree***. See
the note on Galatians 3:10–14.

Something is thrown into the bitter water, and notice what does not happen. The water is not
drained, replaced, or walked away from. It is the same spring, and they drink it.

The wood did not remove the bitterness. It went into it.

## What is given there

*There **the LORD gave them a rule and a standard**, and **there He tested them**.*

The Hebrew is **choq umishpat** — a statute and an ordinance.

Before Sinai. This is the first legislation in Israel's history and it is given at a spring.

## The condition

*"**If you listen carefully to the voice of the LORD your God**, and do what is right in His eyes,
giving heed to His commands and keeping His decrees."*

Four verbs for one thing: listen, do, give heed, keep.

## The promise

*"**Then I will not bring on you any of the diseases I brought on the Egyptians**."*

The plagues, named as diseases. They had watched them happen to somebody else.

## The name

*"**For I am the LORD, the One who heals you**."*

The Hebrew is **ani YHWH rophecha** — I am the LORD your physician.

**Rapha** — to heal, to mend, to repair. The word used of mending pottery and of healing a
wound.

It is the first of the compound divine names given in the wilderness, and it is given at the
place named *bitter*.

## Elim

*Then they came to Elim, where there were **twelve springs of water and seventy palm trees**, and
they camped there beside the water.*

Twelve springs — one for each tribe. Seventy palms — the number of the elders, and of the souls
who went down into Egypt with Jacob.

## The distance

Elim is one stage further on.

The abundance was a short walk past the bitter place, and the name was given at the bitter one.

## The condition, and where it went

The promise here is framed as an *if*: **if** you listen carefully, **then** I will not bring
these diseases on you.

Isaiah states the same promise with the grammar completely changed: *surely **He has borne**
our griefs and **carried** our sorrows… and **by His stripes we are healed***. See the note on
Isaiah 53:4–6.

And Peter puts it further back still, into the past tense, and names the tree: *who Himself
bore our sins in His own body **on the tree**… by whose stripes **you were healed***. See the
note on 1 Peter 2:24.

At Marah the healing hung on their listening. At Calvary it hangs on His bearing, and the
same word — the tree — is standing in both sentences.

## What He calls Himself here

Not *the LORD who will heal you if*. **Ani YHWH rophecha** — I am the LORD your physician.

It is a name, not a transaction. He announces who He is over the worst water they had found,
and Elim was one march further on the whole time.

> **Where the name is given.** At Marah — *the LORD your physician*, spoken over water that had
> just been undrinkable, one day's march short of twelve springs, with a piece of wood in it.
