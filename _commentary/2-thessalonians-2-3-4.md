Paul answers a panic with a sequence.

## The warning

*Do not let anyone fool you, **whatever method is used**.*

The Greek is **kata medena tropon** — by no means, in no manner.

He has just listed three: a spirit, a word, a forged letter. See the note on
2 Thessalonians 2:1–2.

## The structure

*That day does not come **until** the falling away comes **first**, and the man of sin is
uncovered.*

Two conditions, and a *first*.

Paul's answer to *has the day already come?* is: check whether these two have happened.

## The first

*The **falling away**.*

The Greek is **he apostasia** — with the definite article. A specific one, not apostasy in
general.

The word means a defection or revolt. In the Greek Old Testament it is used of political
rebellion and of turning from God.

## The second

*And **the man of sin is uncovered** — **the son of destruction**.*

The Greek is **apokaluphthe** — revealed, unveiled. Paul uses the word three times in this
passage, always of this figure.

Which implies he exists before he is revealed.

**Son of destruction** — the same phrase Jesus uses of Judas in the high-priestly prayer.

## The four descriptions

*The one who **sets himself against everything called God or given worship**.*

The Greek is **ho antikeimenos** — the one lying against, the opposer.

*And **lifts himself over all of it**.*

**Huperairomenos** — raising himself above.

*So far that he **takes his seat in God's own temple**.*

**Kathisai** — to sit down.

*And **puts himself on display as God**.*

**Apodeiknunta heauton** — exhibiting himself, demonstrating himself to be.

## Where the picture comes from

Daniel: *the king shall do according to his own will; he shall exalt and magnify himself above
every god, shall speak blasphemies against the God of gods*.

And the abomination of desolation, which Jesus names in the Olivet discourse: *when you see
the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, standing in the holy place*.

And the king of Babylon in Isaiah 14: *I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above
the stars of God… I will be like the Most High*.

The pattern in every case is self-exaltation ending in a seat.

## What John calls him

*Even now **many antichrists** have come… he is antichrist who denies the Father and the Son.*

John uses the word in the plural and the singular, and says the many are evidence the one is
coming.

## The restraint

*And now you know **what is restraining**, that he may be revealed in his own time… only He who
now restrains will do so until He is taken out of the way.*

The Greek is **to katechon** — the thing holding down. Neuter in one verse and masculine in the
next.

Paul says *you know* — they had been told in person. Nobody since has been sure.

## The end of him

*And then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord will **consume with the breath of His
mouth** and destroy with the brightness of His coming.*

The Greek is **anelei to pneumati tou stomatos autou**.

Isaiah 11: *He shall strike the earth with the rod of His mouth, and with the breath of His
lips He shall slay the wicked*.

## What Paul says immediately after

*But we are bound to give thanks to God always for you, brethren beloved by the Lord, because
**God from the beginning chose you for salvation**.*

The passage about the man of sin is followed, without a break, by a thanksgiving.

Paul answers a frightened church with a timetable and then tells them they were chosen.

> **What the answer is.** Not reassurance in general. Two named events, neither of which has
> happened — so the day they were told they had missed has not come.
