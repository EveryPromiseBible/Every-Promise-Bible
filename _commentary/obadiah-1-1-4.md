The shortest book in the Old Testament, and it is twenty-one verses about one grudge.

## Who Edom is

Esau's descendants. Jacob's brother's people, and the book will say so directly: *the violence you
did to **your brother Jacob**.* See the note on Obadiah 1:10–11.

The whole quarrel is a family one, four hundred years old.

## Where they lived

*"You live in **the clefts of the rock**, high up, and you make your home in the heights."*

The Hebrew is **bechagvei-sela** — in the clefts of Sela, which is also the name of their capital.
The Greek for **sela** is **petra**, and the ruins are still there: a city reached through a
kilometre of slot canyon, with tombs cut into red sandstone cliffs.

It was, by the standards of the time, unassailable.

## The sentence

*"**The pride of your heart has fooled you.**"*

The Hebrew is **zedon libbekha hishi'ekha** — the presumption of your heart has deceived you.

The verb is the one used of the serpent in Genesis 3: *the serpent deceived me, and I ate.*

## What pride tells them

*"You say in your heart, '**Who can bring me down to the ground?**'"*

A rhetorical question, asked of nobody, inside the heart. Obadiah records the internal monologue.

*"And I will say to my soul, 'Soul, you have many goods laid up for many years; take your ease.'"
But God said to him, "**Fool! This night your soul will be required of you.**"*

**Luke** 12. The same shape: a man talking to himself about his own security, and a voice from
outside the conversation.

## The answer

*"Though you **fly as high as the eagle**, though you **set your nest among the stars**, from there
**I will bring you down**."*

The Hebrew is **oridkha** — I will bring you down. It is answered directly against their own
question, using their own verb.

*"**How you are fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning!**… For you have said in your
heart: 'I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God'… **Yet you shall
be brought down to Sheol**."*

Isaiah 14, and it is Obadiah's paragraph in another key — the nest among the stars, the heart that
speaks, and the same verb for the descent.

## The one who went the other way

*Who, being in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal with God, but **made
Himself of no reputation**… He **humbled Himself** and became obedient to the point of death, even
the death of the cross. **Therefore God also has highly exalted Him**.*

**Philippians** 2. Edom builds high and is brought down. **Christ** comes down and is raised, and
**Paul** puts a *therefore* between the two halves.

*"For **whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted**."*

**Luke** 14, said at a dinner party about where to sit.

> **The question and the answer.** *Who can bring me down to the ground?* — asked inside a heart,
> in a city carved into a cliff. The reply uses the questioner's own verb: *from there I will bring
> you down.*
