The most generous sentence in the first chapter of Isaiah, and the chapter around
it is one of the harshest in the book.

## What comes before it

A nation described as sick from head to foot, a country burned, worship God calls
a burden He is weary of carrying, and hands full of blood.

Then: *come now, let us talk this through.*

The invitation arrives in the middle of the indictment, not after it.

## The word

The Hebrew is a legal term — to argue a case, to reason together, to settle a
dispute. It is the language of a courtroom.

God summons them to the argument, and then does not argue. He offers terms.

## The two colours

*Though your sins are scarlet, they can turn white as snow. Though they are red
as crimson, they can become soft and clean as wool.*

Both dyes named were the fastest, most permanent colours in the ancient world.
Scarlet and crimson came from an insect and soaked into the fibre — cloth dyed
that way did not fade, and could not be washed out.

That is the point of choosing them. The stain being described is the kind that
does not come out.

## What they become

Not merely clean — **snow** and **wool**. Wool is what the cloth was before it was
dyed.

The picture is not of a stain removed but of the material restored to its
original state, as though it had never been in the vat.

> **What follows immediately.** *If you are willing and obedient, you will eat the
> good of the land.* The offer has a shape, and it belongs to the covenant Isaiah
> is prosecuting. What Christians have always heard in the verse is the colour
> chemistry: God naming the most permanent stain available and saying it can go.
