Paul opens the three great chapters on the Spirit not with a display, but with a test — and the
test turns out to be a sentence anybody in the room could say.

## The word "gifts" is not there

*Now about **the gifts of the Spirit**, brothers and sisters, I do not want you to be ignorant.*

The Greek is **peri de ton pneumatikon** — *concerning the spirituals*. There is no noun for gifts
in the sentence; translators supply one because the chapter goes on to talk about **charismata**.

And **charisma** is **charis** — grace — with an ending that means *the thing grace produced*. Every
item on the list that follows is grace taking a visible shape. Nothing in the chapter is earned,
which is why Paul can hand the whole subject to a church he has spent eleven chapters correcting.

## Mute idols and a spoken Lord

*You know that when you were Gentiles you were carried away to **mute idols**, however you were led.*

Corinth knew ecstatic religion. The pagan shrines ran on frenzy, and the more a worshipper lost
control the more authentic it was reckoned to be. Paul's word **apagomenoi** — *carried away, led
off* — is the word for a prisoner being marched somewhere.

And the gods at the end of it could not speak.

So the apostle will not let intensity be the test. Something that overwhelms you is not thereby
from God; they had years of being overwhelmed by nothing at all.

## The test is a sentence, not a sensation

*No one speaking by the Spirit of God says, "Jesus is accursed," and no one is able to say, "Jesus
is Lord," except by the Holy Spirit.*

**Kyrios Iesous** — three syllables and a name. And **Kyrios** is the word the Greek Old Testament
puts wherever the Hebrew has the divine name, so the confession is not *Jesus is my master*. It is
*Jesus is YHWH*.

Paul says no one is **able** — **dynatai** — to say it. Not *allowed*. Able. The sentence is beyond
natural human reach, and anyone who has said it and meant it has already been carried there.

> **If you can say it, He is the reason you can.** People who worry whether they have the Spirit
> have usually already answered the question and not noticed. You called Jesus Lord. Paul says that
> is not something flesh and blood arrives at — the same thing Jesus told Peter on the road to
> Caesarea Philippi. The evidence you are looking for is in the sentence you were about to say
> while looking for it.
