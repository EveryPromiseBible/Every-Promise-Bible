The shortest book in the Old Testament, and this is its turn.

## What the book is

Twenty-one verses, one subject: Edom.

Edom is Esau's line. The quarrel is between two brothers, and it has been running for a
thousand years by the time Obadiah writes.

## What Edom did

*"For **violence against your brother Jacob**, shame shall cover you."*

*"In the day that you stood on the other side… **even you were as one of them**."*

The charge, laid out across eight verses: they stood by and watched.

*"You should not have gazed on the day of your brother… **nor stood at the crossroads to cut
off those among them who escaped**."*

Jerusalem fell, and the relatives on the ridge across the valley watched, and then caught the
refugees coming out.

## Where they lived

Petra, and the mountain strongholds of Seir. Rock fortresses, approached through a slot canyon.

*"The pride of your heart has deceived you, **you who dwell in the clefts of the rock**… who
say in your heart, '**Who will bring me down to the ground?**'"*

The answer: *though you ascend as high as the eagle, and though you set your nest among the
stars, from there I will bring you down*.

## The turn

*But **on Mount Zion there will be deliverance**.*

The Hebrew is **peletah** — an escape, those who escape, a remnant.

The refugees who were cut off at the crossroads, and now the word is used of a survival on the
mountain.

## The holiness

*And **it will be holy**.*

The Hebrew is *and it shall be holiness*.

## The inheritance

*And **the house of Jacob will take back its inheritance**.*

The Hebrew is **yireshu** — shall possess, take possession of, dispossess.

The same verb used all through Joshua of taking the land, and in Deuteronomy: *go in and
possess the land which the LORD swore to your fathers*.

## The three houses

*The **house of Jacob** will be a fire, and the **house of Joseph** a flame, and the **house of
Esau** stubble.*

Two burning and one burnable.

**Jacob** here is Judah, the southern kingdom. **Joseph** is the northern tribes. Obadiah puts
them together, which is a small reunification in one line.

## The image

*They will **set it ablaze and consume it**, and **no one from the house of Esau will
survive**.*

The Hebrew is *there shall be no survivor of the house of Esau*.

The same word as **peletah** in verse 17, negated. Zion has an escape; Edom has none.

## The signature

*The LORD has spoken it.*

## What happened

Edom disappears. Nabateans occupied the territory, the Edomites were pushed west into the
Negev, became the Idumeans, and were absorbed after the first century.

Herod the Great was an Idumean, which puts an Edomite on the throne at the birth of the Messiah
— and he orders the children of Bethlehem killed.

## The last line of the book

*Then **saviours shall come to Mount Zion** to judge the mountains of Esau, and **the kingdom
shall be the LORD's**.*

Twenty-one verses about a family feud, ending on the kingdom.

## Where the quarrel started

*And the LORD said to her: "Two nations are in your womb, two peoples shall be separated from
your body."*

And a bowl of stew, and a stolen blessing.

Hebrews names Esau as the warning: *lest there be any fornicator or profane person like Esau,
who for one morsel of food sold his birthright*. See the note on
Hebrews 12:12–17.

> **What the two words do.** *Peletah* — escape. Zion has one, in verse 17. Edom has none, in
> verse 18 — and the men of Edom had spent the fall of Jerusalem catching people who were
> escaping.
