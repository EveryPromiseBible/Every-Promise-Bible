The most quoted psalm in the New Testament, and these two verses are the least quoted part of
it.

## What comes first

*The LORD said to my Lord, "**Sit at My right hand, till I make Your enemies Your footstool**."*
See the note on Psalms 110:1.

The verse Jesus used to silence the Pharisees, and the one Hebrews returns to repeatedly.

## The sceptre

*The LORD will **stretch out your mighty scepter from Zion**.*

The Hebrew is **matteh uzzekha** — the rod of your strength.

Sent out from Zion, which means the seat is elsewhere and the authority travels.

## The command

*Rule **right in the midst of your enemies**.*

The Hebrew is **redeh beqerev oyvekha**.

Not after they are removed. In the middle of them, while verse 1 is still running — *until I
make Your enemies Your footstool*.

The reign is described as happening during the *until*.

## The willing

*Your people will come to you, **glad and willing**.*

The Hebrew is **nedavot** — freewill offerings.

It is the word for the voluntary sacrifice in Leviticus, brought by somebody who did not have
to. See the note on Leviticus 1:1–2.

The people are described as an offering that offers itself.

## The day

*On the **day of your power**.*

The Hebrew is **beyom cheilekha** — in the day of your force, your army.

**Chayil** again — strength, ability, an army, wealth. The same noun as the woman of valour and
the capable men. See the note on Proverbs 31:10–12.

## The splendour

*In **holy splendor**.*

The Hebrew is **behadrei qodesh** — in the beauties of holiness, or in holy array.

Some manuscripts read *on the holy mountains*. The two differ by one letter.

## The dawn

*From **the womb of the dawn**, your young men come to you **like fresh dew of the morning**.*

The Hebrew is **merechem mishchar** — from the womb of the morning. And **tal yaldutekha**, the
dew of your youth.

The line is famously difficult and every translation handles it differently.

The images are all of freshness: a birth, a sunrise, dew on the ground before the heat.

Dew appears without anybody seeing it arrive. It is on the grass in the morning and nobody
carried it there.

## What follows

*The LORD has sworn and will not relent, "**You are a priest forever according to the order of
Melchizedek**."*

The verse Hebrews builds a whole chapter on. See the note on Hebrews 7:1–2.

King in verse 2, and priest in verse 4 — the two offices Israel kept apart, in one man, and
Melchizedek is the precedent.

## How the psalm ends

*He shall judge among the nations… He shall execute the heads of many countries.*

*He shall **drink of the brook by the wayside**; therefore He shall lift up the head.*

The last verse is a soldier stopping at a stream mid-pursuit. Gideon's three hundred were
chosen by how they drank.

## How much the New Testament uses it

More than any other Old Testament chapter. Verse 1 is quoted or alluded to more than twenty
times.

Jesus uses it on the Pharisees. Peter uses it at Pentecost. Paul uses it. Hebrews uses it in
four separate chapters.

> **When the ruling happens.** Not after the enemies are dealt with. *In the midst of them* —
> while verse 1 is still saying *until*.
