---
key: Philippians 4:11–12
title: Contentment is learned
updated: 2026-08-07
---

He does not say he has it. He says he was taught it.

## The disclaimer

*I do not say this **because I am in want**.*

The Greek is **kath' husterēsin** — according to lack.

He has just thanked them for a gift and immediately clarifies that the thanks was not a hint.

## The claim

*I have **learned**, whatever my circumstances, **to be content within them**.*

The Greek is **emathon** — aorist. I learned. A point in the past, not a temperament he was born
with.

## The word

**Autarkēs** — self-sufficient, having enough in oneself.

## Where the word comes from

Stoic vocabulary. The Stoic ideal was a man whose resources were entirely internal and who therefore
needed nothing outside himself.

Paul takes the word and empties it in the next sentence: *I can do all things **through Christ who
strengthens me***.

The sufficiency is real and it is not self-generated.

## The two directions

*I know what it is to be **brought low**, and I know what it is to **have plenty**.*

The Greek is **tapeinousthai** — to be humbled, lowered. And **perisseuein** — to overflow.

## Which is harder

He names both as things requiring instruction.

The letter does not treat abundance as the easy case.

## The initiation

*In every place and in every sort of condition **I have been taught the secret**.*

The Greek is **memuēmai** — I have been initiated.

From **mueō**, the verb used of induction into a mystery religion. The root of *mystery*.

## What was he initiated into

*Of **a full stomach and of hunger**, of **having more than enough and of going without**.*

Four states, in two pairs.

The mystery he says he was let into is knowing how to be hungry.

## Where he learned it

*In stripes, in imprisonments… in hunger and thirst, in fastings often, in cold and nakedness.* See
the note on 2 Corinthians 11:23–29.

The curriculum is on the record.

## The line that follows

*I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me.* See the note on Philippians 4:13.

The Greek is **panta ischuō en tō endunamounti me** — I am strong for all things in the one
empowering me.

## What *all things* means here

The four items just listed.

The sentence has a context, and the context is a list of circumstances rather than a list of
achievements.

## What he says next

*Nevertheless **you have done well that you shared in my distress**.*

Having said he did not need it, he says it was good that they sent it.

Both are allowed to stand.

## The account

*Not that I seek the gift, but **I seek the fruit that abounds to your account**.* See the note on
Philippians 4:17.

An accounting metaphor. The benefit is booked to the giver.

## And the promise

*And **my God shall supply all your need** according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus.* See the
note on Philippians 4:19–20.

Said by a man in prison, to the people who had just supplied his.

> **The verb.** Not *I am content* — *I learned to be*. And the word for the learning is the one used
> of initiation into a mystery.
