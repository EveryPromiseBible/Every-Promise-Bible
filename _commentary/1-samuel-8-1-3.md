Three verses that set up everything that follows — the demand for a king, the
warning about what a king will do, and the whole monarchy.

## The decision

*Samuel had grown old, and so he handed the reins of Israel to his sons.*

Nothing in the book of Judges established succession by inheritance. Judges were
raised up individually; the office did not pass from father to son. Samuel makes
his sons judges anyway.

The text does not editorialise. It records the appointment and then records the
result.

## The result

*They chased after money, took bribes, and bent the truth to line their own
pockets.*

Bribery and perverted justice are exactly what the law forbids of judges, in
almost these words — Deuteronomy warns that a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise.
Samuel's sons are doing the specific thing the office exists to prevent.

## The echo nobody in the story mentions

Samuel had grown up in the house of Eli, whose own sons were corrupt, and whose
failure to restrain them was the reason his house was cut off. Samuel himself
delivered that message as a boy.

Then he appoints his own sons, and they turn out the same way. The book puts the
two households in parallel and never comments on it.

> **What it triggers.** The elders come to Samuel and ask for a king *like all
> the other nations*, and they cite his sons as the reason. Their complaint is
> legitimate. What they ask for as the remedy is the thing God says amounts to
> rejecting Him — and He tells Samuel to give it to them anyway, with a warning
> attached.
