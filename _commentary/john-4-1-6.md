Six verses of travel arrangements, and every detail in them is load-bearing.

## "His road took Him through Samaria"

The Greek is *He had to pass through Samaria* — it was necessary.

Geographically it was the direct route, and Jews going north routinely crossed the
Jordan to avoid it rather than set foot in that country. The necessity in John's
sentence is not about mileage.

## The wall

*Jews have no dealings with Samaritans*, John says a few verses later, and his
disciples *marvelled that He talked with a woman.*

Two walls in one conversation, and He goes through both.

Paul names it in Ephesians: *He has broken down the middle wall of partition.*

## Whose ground it was

*Near **the piece of ground Jacob had given to his son Joseph**, and **Jacob's
well** was there.*

John names the owner. This is Joseph's portion, and Jacob's blessing over Joseph
was:

*Joseph is a fruitful vine, **a fruitful vine beside a spring**, whose branches
**climb over a wall**.*

A spring, and branches over a wall. He is sitting at the well on the land, and the
wall He crosses is the one He is about to cross with a Samaritan woman. See the
note on Genesis 49:22–26.

## "Worn out"

*Jesus was **worn out from the journey**, and He sat down **just as He was**
beside the well.*

The Greek for *worn out* is to labour to the point of exhaustion. John, who opens
his Gospel with *the Word was God*, writes this six verses into chapter 4.

*Just as He was* — no ceremony, nothing arranged. He sat down the way a tired man
sits down.

## The sixth hour

Noon. The hottest part of the day, and the wrong time to draw water — the women
came at evening, together.

She comes alone at midday because of what her life is, and He is there, tired, at
exactly that hour.

## What He asks her for

*"Give Me a drink."*

The first thing He says to her is a request. He has no bucket and the well is
deep, and He opens the conversation from a position of need.

By the end of the chapter she has left her water jar, gone into the town, and
brought it out to meet Him. See the note on John 4:10–15.

> **What He tells the disciples when they come back.** They had gone to buy food
> and He would not eat. *My food is to do the will of Him who sent Me.* Then: *lift
> up your eyes and look at the fields, for they are already white for harvest.* He
> is looking at a Samaritan town walking toward Him.
