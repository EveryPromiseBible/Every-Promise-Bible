The seventh plague, and the only time Pharaoh says the words.

## The gesture

*"**Stretch out your hand toward the sky**, so hail will fall over all of Egypt."*

Moses lifts the staff. The plagues come through it — the Nile, the frogs, the dust, the hail, the
locusts, and eventually the sea.

## The storm

*The LORD sent **thunder and hail, with fire flashing down to the ground**.*

The Hebrew is **esh mithlaqqachath** — fire taking hold of itself, fire flashing back and forth.

In a country where it almost never rains, a hailstorm with lightning is not weather. It is
outside the category.

## The damage

*The hail struck everything in the fields throughout Egypt, both people and animals, and **it
battered every plant and every tree**.*

## The exemption

*Only in **Goshen, where the Israelites lived, was there no hail**.*

The fourth time the text names it. See the note on Exodus 8:20–24.

## The confession

*Pharaoh sent for Moses and Aaron and said, "**This time I have sinned. The LORD is in the right,
and I and my people are in the wrong**."*

The Hebrew is **chatati happa'am** — I have sinned this time.

**YHWH hatzaddiq** — the LORD is the righteous one.

## What is remarkable about it

Nothing like it appears anywhere else in the plague narrative.

He names the sin as his, states that God is right, and includes his officials in the verdict.

## The request

*"**Pray to the LORD**, for we have had enough of God's thunder and hail. I will let you go; you
need not stay any longer."*

## What Moses says

*"When I leave the city, **I will spread out my hands to the LORD**. The thunder will stop… so that
you may know **the earth belongs to the LORD**."*

He agrees to pray, and names the reason: not relief, but the proof of ownership.

## The assessment

*"But **I know that you and your officials still do not fear the LORD God**."*

Moses says it to his face, having just agreed to intercede for him.

He does not believe the confession, and he prays anyway.

## The agricultural note

*The **flax and the barley were ruined**, for the barley was in the ear and the flax in bud, but
**the wheat and spelt were not, since they ripen later**.*

An observation about the calendar, dropped into a plague narrative.

Barley heads out in January or February. Wheat is a month behind.

It tells the reader what time of year this is, and it means the locusts of chapter 10 will find
something left to eat.

## What happens

*Moses left Pharaoh, went out of the city, and spread out his hands to the LORD. **The thunder and
hail stopped**.*

He did what he said.

## The reversal

*But **when Pharaoh saw that it had ceased, he sinned again and hardened his heart**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyosef lachato** — he added to sin.

The confession lasted exactly as long as the storm.

## The pattern

Relief, and then reversal. It happens with the frogs — *when Pharaoh saw that there was relief, he
hardened his heart* — and again here.

The narrative notes both times that the change came after the pressure lifted.

## The last line

*His heart stayed hard, and he would not let the people go, **just as the LORD had said through
Moses**.*

> **The confession.** *This time I have sinned. The LORD is in the right.* It is the only one in
> the book, and Moses tells him to his face that he does not believe it — then goes out and prays.
