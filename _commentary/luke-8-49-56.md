The news arrives while He is still talking to somebody else.

## The timing

*While He was **still speaking**.*

He is finishing the conversation with the woman who touched His clothes — telling her
*daughter, your faith has made you well*.

Jairus has been standing in the street through all of it, with a dying child at home. See
the note on Mark 5:30–34.

## The message

*"**Your daughter is dead. Do not trouble the Teacher any further**."*

The Greek is **skulle** — do not bother, do not harass.

It is meant kindly. There is nothing left to ask for.

## What Jesus does with it

*But Jesus **overheard it** and said to Jairus, "**Do not be afraid. Only believe, and she
will be well**."*

The Greek is *having heard* — He was not addressed.

**Only believe** — the Greek is a single word, **pisteuson**, aorist imperative.

## Who goes in

*He would not let anyone go in with Him except **Peter and James and John**, and **the
child's father and mother**.*

Five people. The three He takes everywhere, and the parents.

Peter is in the room, and thirty years later he does the same thing in an upstairs room in
Joppa. See the note on Acts 9:36–43.

## What was happening in the house

*Everyone there was **weeping and wailing over her**.*

Professional mourners were engaged immediately in that culture — flute players and
wailing women. Matthew mentions the flutes.

The house is already full of a funeral.

## What He says

*"**Stop your crying. She is not dead. She is asleep**."*

## The reaction

*And **they laughed at Him**, because **they knew she had died**.*

The Greek is **kategelon** — laughed him to scorn.

They were right about the fact. Luke says so plainly: *knowing that she was dead*.

## What He meant

Sleep is the New Testament's usual word for a believer's death, and He uses it of Lazarus
too — where the disciples make the same mistake in the opposite direction. See the note on
John 11:11–16.

It is not a denial of the death. It is a statement about how long it lasts.

## The clearing

*Then He **put them all outside**.*

The Greek is **ekbalon** — thrust out.

The mourners go. Only the five stay.

## The words

*And **took her by the hand** and called out, "**My child, get up!**"*

Mark keeps the Aramaic: **Talitha koum**.

## What happened

*And **her spirit came back to her, and she stood up right then**.*

Luke the physician gives the sequence.

## The last instruction

*He told them to **give her something to eat**.*

Twelve years old, raised from the dead, and the instruction is dinner.

Mark notes her age at exactly this point, and the woman on the road had been bleeding for
twelve years.

## The silence

*Her parents **could hardly take it in**, and He told them **not to say a word to anyone**.*

Which was never going to hold. Matthew: *and the report of this went out into all that
land.*

> **Who was right about the facts.** The mourners. She was dead and they knew it, and they
> laughed — and He put them outside and called her the way you would call a child in the
> morning.
