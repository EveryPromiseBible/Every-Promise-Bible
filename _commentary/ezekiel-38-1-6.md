An invasion is announced, and the first thing said about it is who is bringing it.

## Where this sits

Ezekiel 37 is the valley of dry bones and the two sticks made one. See the note on
Ezekiel 37:9–10.

Ezekiel 40 begins the vision of the new temple, measured cubit by cubit.

Chapters 38 and 39 sit between the restoration and the temple.

## The address

*"Son of man, **turn your face toward Gog, of the land of Magog**, the chief prince of **Meshech and
Tubal**."*

The Hebrew is **sim panecha** — set your face. Ezekiel is told to do this repeatedly, toward
Jerusalem, toward the mountains, toward Egypt.

## The names

**Magog**, **Meshech**, **Tubal**, **Gomer**, **Beth-togarmah** — all appear in the table of nations
in Genesis 10 as sons or grandsons of Japheth.

**Persia**, **Cush** and **Put** — modern Iran, and the regions of Sudan and Libya.

The list runs to the far edges of the known world, north and south.

## Gog

The Hebrew **Gog** is not in Genesis 10. It is a person, not a nation — *the chief prince*.

**Nesi rosh** — the phrase can be read *chief prince of Meshech* or *prince of Rosh, Meshech*. The
grammar has been argued over for centuries.

## The declaration

*"Listen, Gog. **I am against you**, chief prince of Meshech and Tubal."*

The Hebrew is **hineni elecha** — behold, I am toward you.

## The turning

*"**I will turn you around. I will set hooks in your jaws and lead you out**."*

The Hebrew is **veshovavticha venatatti chachim bilchayecha**.

**Chachim** — hooks, rings. The image is of a large animal, or a captive, being led by a ring
through the jaw.

Assyrian reliefs show prisoners led exactly this way.

## What the image says

The invasion is not the invader's initiative.

He is described as being brought out, against his will, by the one who says *I am against you*.

The same image is used of Sennacherib in Isaiah: *I will put My hook in your nose and My bridle
in your lips, and I will turn you back*.

## The army

*"You and all your army, **horses and horsemen fully arrayed**, a vast company carrying **shields
and bucklers**, every hand upon a sword."*

Two sizes of shield — the large body shield and the small round buckler.

*"All with shield and helmet."*

## The scale

*"Many peoples are gathered to you."*

Seven nations named, from the far north to Africa.

## The other name

*"**Gomer marches beside you**, and all its bands. **Beth-togarmah from the far north** comes down
with all its ranks."*

The Hebrew is **miyyarketei tzaphon** — from the remote parts of the north.

## What happens to them

*"**I will call for a sword against Gog** throughout all My mountains… every man's sword will be
against his brother."*

And: *"**You shall fall upon the mountains of Israel**, you and all your troops."*

The chapter that begins with hooks in the jaw ends with the army destroyed on the ground it came
for.

## The stated purpose

*"**Thus I will magnify Myself and sanctify Myself**, and I will be known in the eyes of many
nations. **Then they shall know that I am the LORD**."*

The refrain of the whole book. Ezekiel uses it more than sixty times.

## What is on the other side

Chapters 40 to 48: a temple measured in detail, the glory returning through the east gate, a
river running from under the threshold and getting deeper, and the city's new name.

*And the name of the city from that day shall be: **THE LORD IS THERE**.* See the note on
Ezekiel 48:30–34.

## Whose hand is on it

*"I will turn you around. **I will set hooks in your jaws and lead you out**."*

The invasion is not the invader's initiative. The chapter says so in its fourth verse, before a
single soldier moves.

*"For truly **against Your holy Servant Jesus**, whom You anointed, both Herod and Pontius Pilate,
with the Gentiles and the people of Israel, were gathered together **to do whatever Your hand and
Your purpose determined before to be done**."*

**Acts** 4, and it is the same claim about a larger gathering: a king, a governor, the Gentiles and
the crowd, all assembled — and the sentence names whose hand had determined it without excusing
one of them.

*Him, being delivered by the determined purpose and foreknowledge of God, **you have taken by
lawless hands, have crucified, and put to death**.*

**Peter** holds both halves in one sentence at Pentecost, exactly as Ezekiel does.

## Disarmed

*"I will call for a sword against Gog… you shall fall upon the mountains of Israel."*

*Having **disarmed principalities and powers, He made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over
them in it**.*

**Colossians** 2, and the place **Paul** names for the triumph is a cross — an event that looked
from the outside exactly like the other side winning.

## Then they shall know

The refrain of the whole book, more than sixty times.

*That at the name of **Jesus** every knee should bow, of those in heaven, and of those on earth,
and of those under the earth, and **that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord**,
to the glory of God the Father.*

*And the name of the city from that day shall be: THE LORD IS THERE.*

*And I heard a loud voice from heaven saying, "**Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men**, and
He will dwell with them."*

**Revelation** 21 ends where Ezekiel ends, with a city whose distinguishing feature is who is in
it.

> **The hooks.** The invader is described as being led out by the jaw. The chapter opens with an
> army massing and says immediately whose hand is on it.
