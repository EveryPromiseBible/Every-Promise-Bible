The diagnosis, and God states it in the first person without softening.

## The observation

*"You **expected much, and it came to little**."*

The Hebrew is *you looked for much, and behold, it came to little*.

The same complaint as verse 6 — the planting, the eating, the wages in a torn purse. See the
note on Haggai 1:6.

## The admission

*"What you brought home, **I blew away**."*

The Hebrew is **venaphachti bo** — and I blew on it.

Not neglect, and not bad luck. God claims it.

## The question

*"**And why?** declares the LORD Almighty."*

He asks it Himself and answers it in the next clause.

## The reason

*"**Because My house lies in ruins while each of you hurries home to his own**."*

The Hebrew is **atem ratsim ish levéto** — you are running, each man to his own house.

*Running.* The verb is about speed.

Nobody in the passage is idle. They are working hard, on their own places, at pace.

The charge is not laziness. It is order of priority, and the picture is of a crowd of people
each moving quickly in a different direction.

## The drought

*"So the skies above you have **held back their dew**, and the ground has **held back its
harvest**."*

The Hebrew is **kal'u** — restrained, shut up. The same verb for both.

Sky and ground, described as withholding.

## The extent

*"I called for a **drought** on the fields and the hills, on **the grain and the new wine and the
oil**, on everything the ground brings forth, on **people and livestock**, on all the work of
your hands."*

The Hebrew for drought is **chorev** — and it is a play on **charev**, the word for *ruins* used
of the temple two verses earlier.

The house lies **charev**; the land gets **chorev**. One consonant apart.

Haggai is a careful writer and the pun is deliberate.

## The triad

Grain, new wine and oil. The standard summary of that agriculture, and the same three the third
horseman deals in. See the note on Revelation 6:5–6.

## What is not said

That they had stopped believing.

They were back from exile, they had rebuilt an altar, they had laid a foundation, and they were
still calling it *the LORD's house*. They had simply concluded the time had not come.

## What happens twenty-three days later

*Then Zerubbabel… and Joshua… **with all the remnant of the people, obeyed the voice of the LORD
their God**.*

*And the LORD stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel… and the spirit of all the remnant of the
people; and they came and worked on the house of the LORD.*

The obedience and the stirring are both recorded. Haggai does not choose between them.

## What is said in between

*"**I am with you**," says the LORD.*

Between the rebuke and the work, and before any of it is done.

## What He says a month later

To men who remembered Solomon's temple and were weeping at the size of this one:

*"Be strong… and work; for I am with you… **according to the word that I covenanted with you when
you came out of Egypt, so My Spirit remains among you. Do not fear!**"*

*"The glory of this latter temple shall be greater than the former."*

## Where the priority sentence goes

*"But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added
to you."* See the note on Matthew 6:25–34.

> **What the charge is not.** Idleness. *Each man runs to his own house* — the verb is about
> speed, and the drought lands on people who were working hard in the wrong order.
