Two verses, and every clause is a comparison with what the high priest used to do.

## What comes before

A description of the tabernacle: the lampstand, the table, the showbread, the veil, and behind
it the ark with the golden pot of manna, Aaron's rod that budded, and the tablets.

And the arrangement: *into the second part the high priest went alone once a year, not without
blood, which he offered for himself and for the people's sins committed in ignorance*.

One man, one day, once a year, and never without blood.

## The turn

*But **Christ has come**.*

## The title

*He is the **High Priest of everything good that was on its way**.*

The Greek is *high priest of good things to come*.

The good things are the substance the whole system was a shadow of: *for the law, having a
shadow of the good things to come, and not the very image of the things*. See the note on
Hebrews 10:1–2.

## The tent

*He passed through **a greater tent, a truer one, which no human hands put up** — it does not
belong to this made world at all.*

The Greek is **ou cheiropoietou** — not hand-made. And **ou tautes tes ktiseos** — not of this
creation.

Moses had been told to build his *according to the pattern shown you on the mountain*. The
tabernacle was a copy, and the writer says so: *the copies of the things in the heavens*.

## What He did not bring

*And He did **not bring the blood of goats and calves**.*

The two animals of the Day of Atonement: a bull for the priest's own sin, and goats for the
people's.

## What He did bring

*He brought **His own**.*

Three words in the English and two in the Greek — **dia tou idiou haimatos**.

The high priest carried blood in a basin. This one carried His own, which means the priest and
the sacrifice are the same person.

The letter has already said it: *who does not need daily, as those high priests, to offer up
sacrifices, first for His own sins and then for the people's, for this He did once for all
**when He offered up Himself***.

## The frequency

*He went into the holy place **one time only**.*

The Greek is **ephapax** — once for all. The letter's key word, and it uses it three times.

The old arrangement's defining feature was repetition. Every year, the same day, the same
ritual, because it had not worked.

## The result

*And He came away **having bought us back forever**.*

The Greek is **aionian lutrosin heuramenos** — having obtained eternal redemption.

**Lutrosis** — the ransom price paid to free a slave or a prisoner.

**Aionian** — eternal, and the adjective is doing the work. Not a year's covering, renewed
annually.

## The argument that follows

*For if the blood of bulls and goats… sanctifies for the purifying of the flesh, **how much
more shall the blood of Christ**, who through the eternal Spirit offered Himself without spot
to God, **cleanse your conscience from dead works to serve the living God**?*

The old system reached the flesh. The claim here is that this one reaches the conscience.

## The will

*For where there is a testament, there must also of necessity be the death of the testator. For
a testament is in force after men are dead, since it has no power at all while the testator
lives.*

A pun that works in Greek and not in Hebrew: **diatheke** means both *covenant* and *will*.

## The three appearings

*He has appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself.*

*Now to appear in the presence of God for us.*

*To those who eagerly wait for Him He will appear a second time, apart from sin, for
salvation.*

Past, present, future — in three consecutive sentences.

> **What changed between the two priests.** Not the ritual and not the building. The high
> priest carried blood in a basin; this one carried His own, which makes the priest and the
> offering the same person.
