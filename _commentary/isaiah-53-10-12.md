The last three verses of the song, and they are the only ones with a future in them.

## The hardest line

*Yet **it was the LORD's will to crush him** and cause him to suffer.*

The Hebrew is *it pleased the LORD to bruise him*.

Isaiah has spent the chapter saying the servant was despised by men, rejected by men,
oppressed and afflicted — and then names who was behind it.

## The offering

*And though the LORD makes **his life an offering for guilt**.*

The Hebrew is **asham** — the guilt offering, the one in Leviticus 5 brought when
restitution had to be made as well as atonement.

Of the five offerings, this is the one about repayment.

## The turn

Everything after this is future tense, and all of it is gain:

- *He will **see his offspring***
- *and **prolong his days***
- *and **the will of the LORD will prosper in his hand***

A man who has just been described as cut off out of the land of the living, without
descendants, sees offspring and long days.

## "Prosper"

The Hebrew is **tsalach** — to advance, to succeed, to push through. The same word used
of Joseph in Potiphar's house. See the note on Genesis 39:1–6.

## The satisfaction

*After **the anguish of his soul**, he will **see the light and be satisfied**.*

The Hebrew is *the travail of his soul*. **Satisfied** is **saba** — full, having had
enough.

Something is on the far side of it that is worth what it cost, and He looks at it and is
content.

## How the justifying works

*By **his knowledge** my righteous servant **will justify many**, and **he will bear their
iniquities**.*

The two halves in one line. He justifies, and He bears — the second is how the first
happens.

**Justify** is the legal word: to declare in the right. It is not the same as making
somebody better.

## The division

*Therefore I will **give him a portion among the great**, and he will **divide the spoils
with the strong**.*

Conqueror's language — the general dividing the plunder after the battle.

## The four reasons

The Hebrew gives four clauses, each stating why:

- *because he **poured out his life even to death***
- *and was **counted in among the lawbreakers***
- *for he **bore the sin of many***
- *and **made intercession for the transgressors***

## Where Jesus quotes it

*"I say to you that this which is written must still be accomplished in Me: **'And He was
numbered with the transgressors.'** For the things concerning Me have an end."*

He quotes verse 12 about Himself, on the night of the arrest, and the next day two
criminals are crucified either side of Him.

## The last clause

*And **made intercession for the transgressors**.*

Isaiah's final word about the servant is not the death. It is the praying.

Luke records it from the cross: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they
do.* And Hebrews puts it in the present tense: *He always lives to make intercession for
them.*

> **Where the chapter ends up.** It opens *who has believed our report?* and closes with
> spoils divided and a man satisfied. The turn happens at the guilt offering.
