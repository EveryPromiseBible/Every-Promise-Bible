The last item specified for the tabernacle, and it has no dimensions.

## The object

*Make **a bronze basin with a bronze stand** for washing.*

The Hebrew is **kiyyor nechoshet vekanno nechoshet** — a laver of bronze and its base of bronze.

## What is missing

Size, shape, capacity, ornament.

Everything else in the tabernacle is measured to the cubit — the ark, the table, the lampstand,
the altar, the court. This one is not.

## Where the bronze came from

*He made the laver of bronze and its base of bronze, **from the bronze mirrors of the serving women
who assembled at the door of the tabernacle of meeting**.*

Polished bronze mirrors, donated by the women who served at the entrance.

The basin priests looked into to see whether they were clean was made out of mirrors.

## The position

*Set it **between the Tent of Meeting and the altar**.*

Between the place of sacrifice and the place of entry.

A priest coming from the altar with blood on him passes it on the way in.

## The contents

*And **fill it with water**.*

No further instruction about how often, or how much.

## Who uses it

*Aaron and his sons will **wash their hands and feet** from it.*

Hands — what they do. Feet — where they go.

## The first occasion

*Whenever they **enter the Tent of Meeting**, they wash with water, **so they will not die**.*

## The second

*And whenever they **come up to the altar to minister**… they wash their hands and feet, **so they
will not die**.*

## The repetition

The phrase **velo yamutu** — that they die not — appears twice in four verses.

Not a suggestion. The washing is between them and death, going in and going out.

## What they had already had

The consecration washing, at ordination: *Aaron and his sons you shall bring to the door of the
tabernacle of meeting, **and you shall wash them with water***.

That happened once. This one is daily, and repeated.

## The two washings

One bath at ordination, never repeated. Hands and feet, every time they serve.

*"Anyone who has bathed does not need washing except for his feet; he is clean all over."* See the
note on John 13:6–11.

The Greek uses two verbs for the same distinction — **louo** for the bath and **nipto** for the
part.

## The duration

*This is **a lasting rule for Aaron and his descendants, generation after generation**.*

## What replaced it in Solomon's temple

A cast sea of bronze, thirty cubits round, on twelve oxen, holding two thousand baths — and ten
smaller lavers besides.

## What Paul says the washing is now

*That He might sanctify and cleanse her **with the washing of water by the word**.* See the note on
Ephesians 5:25–27.

The Greek is **to loutro tou hudatos en rhemati** — the bath of water in the word.

## What the mirrors mean

*For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man **observing his natural face
in a mirror**; for he observes himself, goes away, and immediately forgets.* See the note on
James 1:22.

> **The one item with no measurements.** Everything else in the tabernacle is given to the cubit.
> The basin has none — and it was made out of the mirrors the women donated.
