One verse about how time looks from the other side.

## The framing

*Dear friends, **do not let this one thing slip past you**.*

Peter flags it. He is about to say something he expects to be skated over.

## The claim

*To the Lord, **a single day is the same size as a thousand years, and a thousand
years is no longer than a day**.*

Both directions, and that is the point.

Read only the first half and it says God is slow. Read both and it says the
comparison does not apply — a day can hold a millennium and a millennium can pass
like a day.

He is not on the scale. That is the claim.

## Where it comes from

Psalm 90, Moses' psalm: *a thousand years in Your sight are like yesterday when it is
past, and like a watch in the night.*

The oldest psalm in the book, written by a man leading a nation through a
forty-year detour.

## What Peter is answering

The scoffers, three verses earlier: *"**Where is the promise of His coming?** For
since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of
creation."*

Their argument is from elapsed time. Nothing has happened, therefore nothing will.

Peter's reply is that they are measuring with the wrong instrument.

## And the reason for the delay

The very next verse: *the Lord is not slack concerning His promise, as some count
slackness, **but is longsuffering toward us, not willing that any should perish** but
that all should come to repentance.*

The waiting is not indifference or inability. It is named as patience with a purpose,
and the purpose is people. See the note on 2 Peter 3:9.

## What Peter has already used as evidence

*By the word of God the heavens were of old, and the earth standing out of water and
in the water, by which the world that then existed **perished, being flooded with
water**.*

The scoffers say nothing ever changes. Peter points at the flood.

## What the chapter is building to

*The day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night… **but we, according to His
promise, look for new heavens and a new earth in which righteousness dwells**.*

And his conclusion is practical: *what manner of persons ought you to be in holy
conduct… looking for and hastening the coming of the day of God.*

> **What the verse is not for.** Peter is not offering a formula for dating anything —
> he opens the chapter saying the day comes like a thief, unannounced. The verse
> removes a calculation rather than supplying one.
