John says why he wrote the paragraph, and then tells them they did not need it.

## The reason

*I have written this to you about **the people who are trying to lead you off**.*

The Greek is **planonton** — deceiving, leading astray. The root of *planet* — the
wandering star.

The chapter has been about them: *they went out from us, but they were not of us*.

## The word

*But **that anointing from Him stays right in you**.*

The Greek is **chrisma** — an anointing, the oil itself. It occurs three times in the New
Testament, all in this chapter.

**Christos** — Christ, the Anointed One — comes from the same root. The anointing and the
Anointed share the word.

## The permanence

*Stays right in you.*

The Greek is **menei** — abides, remains. It is John's favourite verb; he uses it more than
sixty times across his Gospel and letters.

## The claim

*And **nobody has to teach you**.*

Which is a strange sentence to find in the middle of a letter written to teach them.

He has just spent two chapters instructing them, and warns them about false teachers in the
verse before.

The sentence is not against teaching. It is against needing a class of people to mediate
what they already have.

## What the anointing does

*His anointing **teaches you about everything**, and **it is true, and there is no lie in
it**.*

Two claims: it teaches, and it does not lie.

The false teachers are the contrast. John has just called their message a lie: *who is a
liar but he who denies that Jesus is the Christ?*

## The instruction

*So **just as it has taught you — you will stay in Him**.*

The Greek can be read as a statement or an imperative. Either way the verb is **meno**
again — the same word as the anointing's abiding.

It abides in them; they abide in Him.

## What he said seven verses earlier

*But **you have an anointing from the Holy One, and you know all things**.* See the note on
1 John 2:20–21.

He says it twice in one chapter, which is how John signals something.

## What Jesus said about the same thing

*"But the Helper, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in My name, **He will teach you
all things**, and bring to your remembrance all things that I said to you."*

And: *"When He, the Spirit of truth, has come, **He will guide you into all truth**."* See
the note on John 16:12–15.

John was in the room when both sentences were spoken, and he is writing decades later.

## What the letter keeps doing

Testing. John's letters are full of *by this we know* — five or six times over. He does not
tell them to trust a feeling.

The tests are doctrinal and practical: what they say about Christ, and whether they love
each other.

## Who he was writing to

*My little children.* He uses it seven times.

An old man, the last of the twelve, writing to congregations he had watched people walk
out of.

> **The odd sentence.** *Nobody has to teach you* — written by a teacher, in a letter, four
> verses after warning them about teachers. What he is removing is the middleman, not the
> instruction.
