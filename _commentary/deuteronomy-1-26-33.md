Moses recalls the day the nation refused, and he quotes what they said in their tents.

## The refusal

*"But **you would not go up**. You **rebelled against the command** of the LORD your
God."*

Kadesh, in the second year. The twelve spies had come back; two said go, ten said no.

## What was said in private

*"You **grumbled in your tents** and said."*

The Hebrew is *you murmured in your tents*.

Moses reports the tent conversation, which is not in the Numbers account. Forty years on,
in his last sermon, he tells them what they said to each other when they thought nobody
was listening.

## The sentence

*"**The LORD hates us**. That's why He brought us out of Egypt — to hand us over to the
Amorites and destroy us."*

The Hebrew is **sinah** — hatred.

They had watched the plagues, the sea, the pillar, the manna. Their conclusion about the
motive was that He hated them.

## The report they believed

*"They told us the people are **bigger and taller** than we are, the **cities are huge
with walls up to the sky**, and they even saw **giants — the sons of the Anakim**."*

Every item is from the ten spies' report. Numbers has it: *we were like grasshoppers in
our own sight, and so we were in their sight.*

Nothing in it was untrue. The walls were high and the Anakim were there — Joshua and
Caleb never disputed the facts.

## What Moses said at the time

*"**Don't be terrified. Don't be afraid of them**."*

*"The LORD your God, who **goes ahead of you**, **He Himself will fight for you**."*

## The evidence he gave them

*"Just as you saw Him do **in Egypt right before your eyes**, and **in this wilderness**."*

Two things they had personally watched.

## The image

*"You saw how the LORD your God **carried you, the way a father carries his son**, along
the whole road you traveled until you reached this place."*

The Hebrew is **nasa** — to lift and bear.

A father carrying a son is not a picture of a God who hates. Moses puts it directly
against the sentence they said in their tents.

## The verdict

*"Yet even after all that, **you did not trust the LORD your God**."*

The Hebrew is *you did not believe*.

Moses names the failure, and it is not cowardice or bad intelligence.

## What He had been doing the whole time

*"The One who **went ahead of you on the journey, searching out a place for you to
camp**, in **fire by night and in a cloud by day**, to show you the way to walk."*

The Hebrew for *searching out* is **tur** — the same verb used of the twelve spies
searching out the land.

God had been scouting ahead the whole way. They sent twelve men to do the job that was
already being done, and believed the report.

## What happened

Forty years of circling Mount Seir. See the note on Deuteronomy 2:1–7.

And of that period Moses says in the same book: *these forty years the LORD your God has
been with you, and **you have lacked nothing**.*

## Who is listening

The children of the people who said it. Everyone over twenty that day is dead except
Caleb and Joshua.

> **What they got wrong.** Not the height of the walls. Moses does not correct a single
> fact in their report — he answers the sentence about who was bringing them out and why.
