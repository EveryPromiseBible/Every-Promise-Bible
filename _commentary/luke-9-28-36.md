Luke's account of the transfiguration keeps three details the others leave out.

## The reason for going up

*He took Peter and John and James with Him and went up on a mountain **to pray**.*

Luke alone gives the purpose. Matthew and Mark say He led them up; Luke says why.

Luke does this throughout — he records Jesus praying at His baptism, before choosing the
twelve, before Peter's confession, and in Gethsemane.

## When it happened

*And **while He was praying**, the look of His face was changed.*

The change occurs during the prayer, not after it.

Matthew uses **metamorphoo** — transformed. Luke avoids the word, which had associations
with pagan myth for Greek readers, and describes the appearance instead. See the note on
Matthew 17:1–8.

## The two men

*Two men appeared, talking with Him — **Moses and Elijah** — and they were **shining with
glory**.*

The lawgiver and the prophet. Moses died and was buried by God in a valley in Moab; Elijah
went up in a whirlwind. See the note on 2 Kings 2:11–12.

## What they discussed

*They were speaking with Him about **His departure**, the thing **He was going to
accomplish at Jerusalem**.*

Only Luke records the subject.

The Greek for *departure* is **exodos**.

Moses, who led the first exodus, is standing on a mountain discussing the second one.

**Accomplish** — the Greek is **pleroun**, to fulfil, to complete.

## The disciples

*Peter and the two with him were **heavy with sleep**, and **when they came fully awake**
they saw His glory.*

Luke alone says they were asleep. He notes the same thing in Gethsemane — *He found them
sleeping from sorrow*.

They nearly missed it.

## Peter's offer

*"Master, it is good for us to be here. **Let us put up three shelters** — one for You, one
for Moses, and one for Elijah."*

Three, of equal size.

Luke adds: *he **did not know what he was saying**.*

And notes when he said it: *as Moses and Elijah were leaving*. He is trying to keep them.

## The interruption

*And **while he was still speaking**, a cloud came and settled over them.*

He is cut off mid-sentence, twice over — by the cloud and then by the voice.

## The voice

*"**This is My Son, the One I love. Listen to Him.**"*

Luke's version has *the One I love*; some manuscripts read *My Chosen One*.

The command is the same in all three Gospels: **listen to Him**.

It answers the offer of three shelters. There are not three voices to attend to.

## The last line

*When the voice had finished, **Jesus was there alone**.*

## The silence

*And **they kept it to themselves**. In those days they told no one a word of what they had
seen.*

Peter broke the silence eventually, in writing: *we were eyewitnesses of His majesty… we
heard this voice which came from heaven when we were with Him on the holy mountain.*

## What was said eight days before

*"The Son of Man must suffer many things… and be killed, and be raised the third day."*

And *"if anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross
daily."*

The glory on the mountain comes eight days after the first announcement of the cross, and
the conversation on it is about the same subject.

> **What Luke keeps that nobody else does.** That He went up to pray, that the change
> happened while He prayed, that the three were asleep, and what the three of them were
> talking about.
