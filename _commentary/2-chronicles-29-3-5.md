A new king's first recorded act, and it is about a set of doors.

## The timing

*In **the first month of the first year of his reign**.*

Hezekiah is twenty-five. His father Ahaz had spent sixteen years importing Assyrian
altars, cutting up the temple furniture, and finally shutting the building.

The son does not wait. The chronicler dates it to the first month.

## The doors

*He **opened the doors of the house of the LORD and repaired them**.*

His father had *shut up the doors of the house of the LORD*, in the previous chapter, in
the same words.

The first act of the reign is the exact reversal of the last act of the previous one.

**Repaired** — the Hebrew is **chazaq**, to strengthen, to make firm. The same root as
his own name, **Chizqiyahu**: *the LORD strengthens*.

## Who he calls

*He brought in **the priests and the Levites** and gathered them **in the square on the
east**.*

The east side — the side the glory left by in Ezekiel's vision, and the side it returns
by. See the note on Ezekiel 10:18–22.

## The order of the instruction

*"Listen to me, Levites. **Consecrate yourselves now, and consecrate the house** of the
LORD."*

Themselves first, then the building. He does not send them into a defiled temple
unprepared.

The Hebrew for *consecrate* is **qadash** — to set apart, to make holy.

## The job

*"**Carry out the filth from the holy place**."*

The Hebrew is **niddah** — a strong word for uncleanness, used in Leviticus for
ceremonial defilement and in the prophets for what is repulsive.

**Carry out.** It has to be picked up and physically removed. Nothing about the
instruction is symbolic.

## What was in there

Ahaz's imports. He had seen an altar in Damascus, sent the plans to the priest, had a
copy built, and moved the bronze altar aside to make room. He cut up the bases of the
lavers, took down the sea from off the bronze oxen, and closed the doors.

Two chapters of demolition, and this is the clearing up.

## The order of the whole operation

Doors opened, staff called, staff consecrated, building consecrated, filth removed.

Then the burnt offering, then the Levites with the instruments of David, then the song,
and then the Passover in chapter 30 — kept a month late because there were not enough
priests ready in time.

## What Hezekiah says next

He tells them why the doors had been shut, and does not spare his own father. See the
note on 2 Chronicles 29:6–9.

## The verdict on him

*He did what was right in the sight of the LORD, according to all that his father David
had done.*

Not *his father Ahaz*. The chronicler skips a generation to find the comparison.

> **Where the reign starts.** Not with an army, a treaty, or a building programme. A
> young king opens a door his father closed and tells the Levites to carry the rubbish
> out.
