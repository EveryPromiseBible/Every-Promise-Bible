The disciples ask a question about method and get an answer about hearts.

## The question

*"**Why do You speak to them in stories?**"*

The Greek is **en parabolais** — in parables. A parable is something thrown alongside
something else.

The question is fair. He has just told a story about seed and soil to a crowd on a beach and
then sat down without explaining it.

## The first answer

*"Because **you have been given the secrets of the kingdom of heaven to know, and they have
not**."*

The Greek is **musteria** — mysteries. In the New Testament a mystery is not a puzzle but
something previously hidden that has now been disclosed.

And **dedotai** — it has been given. Passive.

## The principle

*"To the one who has, more will be given, and he will have **far more than enough**; but from
the one who has nothing, **even the little he has will be taken away**."*

The Greek is **perisseuthesetai** — will overflow.

Luke sharpens the second half: *even what he **thinks he has** will be taken from him*. See
the note on Luke 8:16–18.

## The reason restated

*"**That is why I speak to them in stories**."*

## The diagnosis

*"They **look and do not really see**; they **listen and do not really hear**, and **nothing
settles into their understanding**."*

The Greek is *seeing they do not see, and hearing they do not hear*.

The faculties are working. The reception is not.

## The quotation

*"In them the words of Isaiah are coming true."*

Isaiah 6, from the prophet's commission — the hardest passage in the book, given to him
immediately after *here am I, send me*.

*"You will **hear and hear, and never understand**; you will **look and look, and never
see**."*

## The order in Isaiah

*"For this people's **heart has grown heavy**. Their **ears have gone dull**, and they have
**shut their eyes tight**."*

The heart first, then the ears, then the eyes. And the last one is active — *they have shut
their eyes*, not *their eyes were shut*.

Matthew quotes the Greek version, which puts the closing in the people's own hands. The
Hebrew has the imperative form, and reads as a commission to make the heart dull.

## What the closed eyes prevent

*"Otherwise they would **see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with
their hearts, and turn back to Me, and I would heal them**."*

Five verbs in a chain, and healing is at the end of it.

The reason given for the shutting is stated from inside: this is what would happen otherwise.

## The turn

*"But **how blessed your eyes are, because they see, and your ears, because they hear**."*

The same two organs, and the same two verbs, with the negative removed.

## The longing

*"Many **prophets** and many good and righteous people **longed to see what you are seeing
and never saw it**, and to hear what you are hearing and never heard it."*

The men who wrote the book wanted to see this.

Peter says the same: *of this salvation the prophets have inquired and searched carefully…
searching what, or what manner of time, the Spirit of Christ who was in them was
indicating*.

And Abraham: *your father Abraham rejoiced to see My day, and he saw it and was glad*.

## What the parables do

They do two jobs at once, and the passage says so. They reveal, to anyone who comes and asks
what the story meant. And they conceal, from anyone content to hear a nice story about
farming and go home.

The disciples get the explanation because they came and asked. That is the whole difference
in the chapter.

## What He explains next

*"Therefore **hear the parable of the sower**."* See the note on Matthew 13:18–23.

He answers the question about method by explaining the parable to the people who asked.

> **What separates the two groups in the chapter.** Not intelligence. The disciples came and
> asked what it meant, and the explanation follows immediately.
