Two men set out, and the narrator gives the terrain in detail because the terrain is the
story.

## The proposal

*"**Come on — let's cross over to the Philistine outpost on the other side**."*

Said by Jonathan to his armour-bearer.

An armour-bearer is a young man who carries the shield and the spare weapons. He is staff, not
an equal.

## The omission

*But **he didn't tell his father**.*

The Hebrew states it plainly and the narrator does not comment.

Whether it is initiative or insubordination is left open. Saul is the king and the commander.

## Where Saul is

*Staying **on the outskirts of Gibeah under a pomegranate tree at Migron**, with about six
hundred men.*

Under a tree, at the edge, with the whole army.

The contrast with the two men on the cliff is not stated and does not need to be.

## Who is with him

*(**Ahijah** was there wearing the ephod — the son of Ahitub, **Ichabod's brother**, son of
Phinehas, son of Eli, the LORD's priest at Shiloh.)*

Four generations, in one parenthesis.

**Ichabod** — *the glory has departed*. Named by his mother as she died in childbirth on the day
the ark was captured and Eli fell backwards off his seat.

This is that family. The priesthood under judgment, still functioning, wearing the ephod, at
the king's elbow.

Saul consults him later in the chapter, and then tells him to stop mid-enquiry.

## The ignorance

*No one knew Jonathan had gone.*

## The crossing

*Now to reach the Philistine outpost, Jonathan had to **pass between two rocky crags**.*

## The names

*One was called **Bozez**, the other **Seneh**.*

The Hebrew names are usually taken as *shining* or *slippery*, and *thorny*.

The narrator gives them because somebody knew them. This is local geography, remembered.

## The orientation

*One cliff stood **to the north facing Michmash**, the other **to the south facing Geba**.*

The Wadi Suweinit runs between them — a steep gorge with cliffs on both sides.

The garrison is on top of the northern one.

## What the passage establishes

The route is a climb, up a gorge, into a defended position, by two men.

Whatever happens next cannot be attributed to numbers, to terrain, or to equipment — the
previous paragraph established that there were two swords in the whole army. See the note on
1 Samuel 13:19–23.

## What Jonathan says next

*"Maybe the LORD will act for us. **Nothing can stop the LORD from saving, whether by many or by
few**."* See the note on 1 Samuel 14:6–10.

## Who Jonathan is

The king's son and heir.

The man who later strips off his own robe, armour, sword, bow and belt and gives them to
David — the boy who will take the throne he was born to.

*And Jonathan loved him as his own soul.*

## What he says on that occasion

*"**You shall be king over Israel, and I shall be next to you**."*

He knew.

> **The clause the narrator leaves alone.** *He did not tell his father.* Saul is under a tree
> with six hundred men and a priest, and his son goes over the ridge with one servant.
