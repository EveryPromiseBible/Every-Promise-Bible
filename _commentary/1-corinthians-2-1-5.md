Paul describes his own arrival in Corinth, and every detail is a disqualification.

## What he did not bring

*I did not come with **grand speech or impressive wisdom**.*

The Greek is **huperoche logou e sophias** — superiority of word or wisdom.

Corinth was a Greek city that paid for rhetoric. A travelling speaker was judged on
delivery, and Paul's opponents there later said his *bodily presence is weak, and his
speech contemptible*.

## The decision

*For **I made up my mind to know nothing** while I was among you **except Jesus Christ, and
Him crucified**.*

The Greek is **ekrina** — I judged, I decided. It was a deliberate narrowing.

**And Him crucified** — the perfect participle: crucified and standing crucified.

He has just spent a chapter on why: *we proclaim Christ crucified — a stumbling block to
the Jews and foolishness to the Greeks.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 1:18–25.

## Where he had come from

Athens. Acts 17 has him on the Areopagus quoting Greek poets, and the response was that
some mocked and some said *we will hear you again on this matter*.

Then: *after these things Paul departed from Athens and went to Corinth.*

## The three states

*I was with you **in weakness**, and **in fear**, and **in much trembling**.*

The Greek is **astheneia**, **phobos**, and **tromos polys**.

He is not describing humility as a posture. He says he was frightened.

Acts confirms it: in Corinth the Lord spoke to him in a vision — *do not be afraid, but
speak, and do not keep silent; for I am with you, and no one will attack you to hurt
you.*

You do not say that to a man who is not afraid.

## What the preaching was not

*My speaking and my preaching were **not made of the persuasive words of man's wisdom**.*

The Greek word for *persuasive* is rare and may have been coined here.

## What it was

*But were **a showing of the Spirit and of power**.*

The Greek is **apodeixis** — a demonstration, a proof. It was a technical term in logic
for a conclusive argument.

He rejects one kind of proof and claims another.

## The reason

*So that **your faith might not rest on the wisdom of men but on the power of God**.*

The Greek for *rest on* is *be in*.

Paul names the purpose of his own weakness: a faith built on a persuasive speaker stands
on the speaker.

## What Corinth was like

The letter is written to a church with factions, a lawsuit, a case of immorality worse
than the pagans', drunkenness at the Lord's table, and an argument about which gift
ranked highest.

That congregation came out of eighteen months of preaching by a frightened man who had
decided to talk about one thing.

## What he says about the message next

*But we speak the wisdom of God in a mystery, the hidden wisdom which God ordained before
the ages for our glory, **which none of the rulers of this age knew; for had they known,
they would not have crucified the Lord of glory**.*

> **What he lists as his qualifications.** No rhetoric, no impressive wisdom, one subject,
> weakness, fear and much trembling — and he says he arranged it that way on purpose.
