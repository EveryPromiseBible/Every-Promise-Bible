Put on trial for eating with Gentiles, Peter's defence is to tell the story again.

## The charge

The verses before: *those of the circumcision contended with him, saying, "**You went in to
uncircumcised men and ate with them**!"*

The accusation is about a meal.

## The method

*So Peter **began at the beginning and laid it all out for them in order**.*

The Greek is **kathexes** — in sequence. It is the word Luke uses in his Gospel's preface
about his own method: *to write to you an orderly account*.

Peter does not argue. He narrates.

Luke tells the Cornelius story twice — chapter 10 as it happens, chapter 11 as Peter
reports it — which is unusual in a book this compressed, and it is how Luke marks what
matters.

## Where he was

*"I was in the city of Joppa, **praying**."*

The same town Jonah sailed from to avoid going to Gentiles.

## The sheet

*"Something like **a great sheet coming down out of heaven, let down by its four corners**,
and **it came right to me**."*

Four corners — the four quarters of the earth.

**It came right to me** — Peter adds that detail in the retelling. It did not appear near
him; it arrived.

## The contents

*"**Four-footed animals of the earth, and wild beasts, and creeping things, and birds of
the air**."*

Every category in Leviticus 11, clean and unclean together.

## The command

*"**Get up, Peter. Kill and eat**."*

## The refusal

*"**Never, Lord. Nothing unholy or unclean has ever passed my lips**."*

The Greek is *by no means* — the strong double negative.

He addresses Him as Lord and then refuses. He had done the same in the boat — *depart from
me, for I am a sinful man* — and at the foot-washing: *You shall never wash my feet.*

His record is clean and he says so.

## The correction

*"**What God has cleansed, you must not call unholy**."*

The Greek for *cleansed* is **ekatharisen** — purified.

The issue is not what the animals are. It is who has ruled on them.

## Three times

*"**This happened three times**, and then it was all drawn back up into heaven."*

Peter is the man who denied three times and was asked three times on the beach.

## The timing

*"**At that very moment** three men, sent to me from Caesarea, arrived at the house."*

The vision ends and there is knocking at the door. The interpretation walks up the street.

## The instruction

*"The Spirit told me to **go with them without hesitating**."*

The Greek is **meden diakrinomenon** — making no distinction, doubting nothing.

The same root as the word Peter uses at the council years later: *He made **no distinction
at all** between us and them.* See the note on Acts 15:6–9.

## The witnesses

*"And **these six brothers went with me too**."*

Peter brought corroboration to Jerusalem. Six men, standing there while he speaks.

## What Cornelius had been told

*"He will **speak words to you by which you and all your household will be saved**."*

An angel appeared to Cornelius and did not preach to him. It told him where to send for a
man.

## What happens next

*"As I began to speak, **the Holy Spirit fell on them just as He did on us at the
beginning**."* See the note on Acts 11:15–18.

And the room goes quiet.

> **How he defends himself.** By telling it in order, with six witnesses in the room, and
> letting the sequence do the arguing — a vision he refused, an instruction he could not
> answer, and three men at the door before it was over.
