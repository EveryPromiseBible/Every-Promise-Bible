Two divided kingdoms and one leader, promised by a prophet in the one that was about to be
destroyed.

## The division

The nation had split after Solomon: ten tribes in the north under Jeroboam, two in the south under
Rehoboam. Two hundred years of separate kings, separate shrines and periodic war.

Hosea is a northern prophet, and Assyria takes the north within his lifetime.

## The promise

*"Then the people of Judah and the people of Israel will be **gathered together as one**."*

The Hebrew is **veniqbetzu… yachdav** — and they shall be gathered together, as one.

*"Say to them, 'Thus says the Lord GOD: Surely I will take the children of Israel from among the
nations… **and I will make them one nation in the land**… and **one king shall be king over them
all**.'"*

Ezekiel is told to take two sticks, write Judah on one and Ephraim on the other, and hold them so
they become one in his hand.

## The head

*"They will choose **a single head** for themselves."*

The Hebrew is **rosh echad** — one head.

*And **He is the head of the body, the church**, who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead,
that in all things He may have the preeminence.*

*And **other sheep I have which are not of this fold**; them also I must bring, and they will hear
My voice; and **there will be one flock and one shepherd**.*

**John** 10, and **Jesus** makes the gathering His own work in the first person: *I must bring
them.*

*For **He Himself is our peace**, who has made both one, and has **broken down the middle wall of
separation**… so as to create in Himself **one new man from the two**, thus making peace.*

**Ephesians** 2 takes the older division — Jew and Gentile — and describes exactly Hosea's
arithmetic: two, made one, under one head.

## The rising up

*"They will **rise up out of the land**."*

The Hebrew is **ve'alu min-ha'aretz** — and they shall go up from the land. It is the exodus verb,
and it is also the word used of coming up out of a grave.

## The day of Jezreel

*"For great will be **the day of Jezreel**."*

Jezreel was the first child's name, given as a judgement — *soon I will call the house of Jehu to
account for the blood of Jezreel.*

But the name itself means **God sows**. Hosea takes a name that was a sentence and reads it as a
promise, and the chapter after this uses it that way: *I will plant her for Myself in the land.*
See the note on Hosea 2:21–23.

*"**Unless a grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it
produces much grain.**"*

## The three names

All three children are renamed in this passage and the next: Not-My-People becomes My-People,
Not-Pitied becomes Pitied, and Jezreel stops meaning bloodshed and starts meaning sowing.

*"**To him who overcomes** I will give some of the hidden manna to eat. And I will give him a white
stone, and on the stone **a new name written**."*

> **What happens to a name.** Jezreel was given to a child as a verdict about a massacre. The same
> word means *God sows*, and by chapter 2 that is what it is being used for.
