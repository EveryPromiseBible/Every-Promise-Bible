The disciples have gone. A member of the council that condemned Him comes forward.

## Who he was

*A **respected member of the council**, a man who was himself **waiting for the
kingdom of God**.*

The council is the Sanhedrin — the body that had tried Jesus that same morning.
Luke adds that he *had not consented to their decision and deed*.

Matthew says he was rich. John says he was a disciple *secretly, for fear of the
Jews*.

## "It took courage to walk in there"

Mark says it outright, and Mark is the shortest Gospel.

He goes to Pilate — a Roman governor who has just executed a man for sedition — and
asks for the body of the condemned. There is no way to do that anonymously.

Everything he had spent a career protecting goes in that request.

## Pilate's surprise

*Pilate was **surprised to hear that Jesus was already dead**.*

Crucifixion normally took days. Pilate sends for the centurion and checks.

Mark alone records this exchange, and it settles the question of whether He was
actually dead: the officer who ran the execution confirmed it to the governor
before the body was released.

## What Joseph did with his money

*Joseph **bought** a length of linen, took Him down, wrapped Him in the cloth, and
laid Him in a tomb that had been **cut out of the rock**.*

He paid for the linen. Matthew says the tomb was *his own new tomb, which he had
hewn out of the rock*.

A wealthy man's grave, bought and cut for himself, given away on a Friday
afternoon.

## What Isaiah had said

*And they made His grave with the wicked — but with the rich at His death.*

Both halves. He was executed between two criminals and buried in a rich man's
tomb, and Isaiah put the two clauses side by side seven hundred years earlier.

## John's addition

Nicodemus came with him — the other secret one, the man who had come by night in
chapter 3 — carrying *a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about a hundred pounds*.

That is a royal burial quantity, and an enormous expense.

The two members of the Sanhedrin who would not declare themselves while He was
alive are the two men handling His body.

## The women watching

*Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses were watching, and **they saw where He
was laid**.*

Mark notes it because it matters two days later. They knew which tomb.

> **What was in the tomb by Sunday.** The linen lying there, and the cloth that had
> been around His head *folded together in a place by itself.* John looked in and
> reports the arrangement of the cloth before he reports anything else.
