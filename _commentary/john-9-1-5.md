---
key: John 9:1–5
title: "Whose fault was it"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The disciples ask a question with two options, and He rejects both.

## The question

*"**Rabbi, whose sin caused this — his own, or his parents'?**"*

It is a closed question. The man is blind from birth, so the sin must be his parents', or — on a
rabbinic view that a child could sin in the womb — his own.

Nobody in the group considers a third possibility. That is the assumption the whole chapter takes
apart.

## The answer

*"**Neither this man nor his parents sinned.**"*

**Oute houtos hemarten oute hoi goneis autou.**

He does not say they were sinless. He says this is not why.

The book of Job spends thirty-eight chapters on the same argument, with three friends holding the
disciples' position, and God's verdict on them at the end is that they *have not spoken of Me what is
right*.

*"**Or those eighteen on whom the tower in Siloam fell and killed them — do you think that they were
worse sinners than all other men who dwelt in Jerusalem? I tell you, no.**"*

**Luke** 13, and **Jesus** rules out the same inference in the same words.

## What He puts instead

*"**But this happened so that the works of God might be displayed in him.**"*

The Greek has no verb in the first clause — literally *but that the works of God should be revealed
in him* — and translators supply one. It is not a statement that God caused the blindness to make a
point. It is a redirection of the question from cause to purpose.

*And we know that **all things work together for good** to those who love God.*

*But we have this treasure in **earthen vessels**, that the excellence of the power may be of God and
not of us.*

## The urgency

*"**We must work the works of Him who sent Me while it is day. The night is coming when no one can
work.**"*

**Hemas dei** — we must. He includes them in the sentence.

## The claim He repeats

*"**As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world.**"* See the note on John 8:12–14.

Said immediately before He gives sight to a man who has never had it. The chapter then runs the
metaphor to the end: the blind man sees and worships, and the Pharisees who can see are told *now
you say, "We see." Therefore your sin remains.*

## What the man ends up saying

*"**One thing I know: that though I was blind, now I see.**"*

He cannot answer any of the theological questions put to him and says so twice. What he has is a
before and an after.

*And He found him, and said to him, "Do you believe in the Son of God?"… "**Lord, I believe!**" And he
worshipped Him.*

**Jesus** goes and finds him after he has been thrown out of the synagogue for it.

> **What He rejects.** Both halves of the question. The disciples offer two candidates for blame and
> He declines both — and then turns the sentence from *why did this happen* to *what is it for*.
