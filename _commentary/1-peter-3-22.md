The last clause of a difficult paragraph, and it is where the whole argument was going.

## Where the sentence starts

*For Christ also suffered once for sins, **the just for the unjust**, that He might bring us to
God, being put to death in the flesh but made alive by the Spirit.* See the note on
1 Peter 3:18–20.

Then the spirits in prison, the days of Noah, the eight souls saved through water, and baptism as
the antitype.

The whole passage is one long sentence in Greek, and this is its end.

## The ascension

*Who **has gone into heaven**.*

The Greek is **poreutheis eis ouranon**.

The same participle used two verses earlier of going to the spirits in prison. Peter uses one
verb for both journeys.

## The position

*And is **at God's right hand**.*

The Greek is **en dexia tou Theou**.

Psalm 110: *the LORD said to my Lord, "Sit at My right hand, till I make Your enemies Your
footstool."* See the note on Psalms 110:1.

The most quoted Old Testament verse in the New Testament.

## The three groups

*With **angels and authorities and powers** all put under Him.*

The Greek is **angelon kai exousion kai dunameon**.

The vocabulary Paul uses for the ranked spiritual orders: *principalities, powers, might,
dominion*.

Peter names three and does not sort them further.

## The verb

**Hupotagenton** — having been subjected, put in order under.

Aorist passive participle. Completed, and done to them.

## What that answers

The letter is written to believers scattered across five Roman provinces, under pressure, told to
expect *the fiery trial which is to try you*.

Peter has just told slaves to submit to harsh masters, wives to submit to unbelieving husbands,
and everyone to *sanctify the Lord God in your hearts, and always be ready to give a defence*.

The last clause of the argument is that the powers ranged against them are already under
somebody's feet.

## Where else Peter argues from position

*Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who according to His abundant mercy has
begotten us again to a living hope **through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead**.* See
the note on 1 Peter 1:3–5.

*To an inheritance incorruptible and undefiled and that does not fade away, **reserved in heaven for
you**.*

## What Paul says in the same terms

*Far above all principality and power and might and dominion… **and He put all things under His
feet**.* See the note on Ephesians 1:20–23.

The same three-part list, the same verb, the same psalm.

## Hebrews' qualification

*You have put all things in subjection under his feet. For in that He put all in subjection under
him, He left nothing that is not put under him. **But now we do not yet see all things put under
him**.*

*But we see Jesus.* See the note on Hebrews 2:8–9.

Hebrews states the tension the New Testament does not resolve: it is done, and it is not yet
visible.

## What follows in Peter

*Therefore, since Christ suffered for us in the flesh, **arm yourselves also with the same mind**.*

The conclusion drawn from the enthronement is an instruction about suffering.

> **The tense.** **Hupotagenton** — aorist passive. Already done, and done to them. Written to
> people under pressure in five provinces.
