Paul names the problem in Corinth and then names what is behind it.

## Who he is describing

*For such men are **false apostles, workers who deceive, remaking themselves into apostles of
Christ**.*

The Greek is **pseudapostoloi, ergatai dolioi** — false apostles, deceitful workers.

**Metaschematizomenoi** — transforming themselves, changing their outward figure.

## What they were teaching

Not something obviously wrong.

*For if he who comes preaches **another Jesus** whom we have not preached, or if you receive **a
different spirit**… or **a different gospel** which you have not accepted — you may well put up with
it!*

The vocabulary is the same. Jesus, Spirit, gospel. A different content under the same words.

## What Paul is defending

His own standing, reluctantly.

*I hope you will bear with me in a little folly.*

He is being compared unfavourably with polished visiting teachers: *his letters are weighty and
powerful, but his bodily presence is weak, and his speech contemptible*.

## The word

**Metaschematizo** — to change the outward form while the inner reality stays what it was.

Not **metamorphoo**, which is the word for real transformation: *be transformed by the renewing of
your mind*, and *we are being transformed into the same image*.

Paul chooses the word for appearance.

## The comparison

*And that is **no wonder**, for **Satan himself remakes himself into an angel of light**.*

The Greek is **ou thauma** — no marvel.

Paul says the human case is unsurprising because of the pattern above it.

## What is not said

That the disguise fails.

The passage does not describe an obvious counterfeit. It describes one that works, which is why
Paul has to write about it.

## The conclusion

*So it is **no great thing if his servants also remake themselves into servants of righteousness**.*

The Greek is **diakonoi dikaiosunes** — ministers of righteousness.

The impersonation is not of vice. It is of righteousness.

## The end

*Their end will be **according to their works**.*

The Greek is **kata ta erga auton**.

Paul does not undertake to unmask them. He states where it goes.

## What he offers instead of credentials

*Are they ministers of Christ? — I speak as a fool — **I am more**: in labours more abundant, in
stripes above measure, in prisons more frequently, **in deaths often**.*

Five times thirty-nine lashes. Three times beaten with rods. Once stoned. Three shipwrecks. A
night and a day in the deep.

*Besides the other things, what comes upon me daily: **my deep concern for all the churches**.* See
the note on 2 Corinthians 11:24–28.

## What he boasts in

*If I must boast, **I will boast in the things which concern my infirmity**.*

And the escape from Damascus in a basket, let down through a window in the wall — which he
includes in the list.

## The test he gives elsewhere

*Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but **test the spirits, whether they are of God**; because
many false prophets have gone out into the world.* See the note on 1 John 4:1–3.

## What he fears

*But I fear, lest somehow, as the serpent deceived Eve by his craftiness, so **your minds may be
corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ**.*

The Greek is **haplotetos** — singleness, simplicity.

The danger he names is not being argued out of the faith. It is being argued into a more
complicated version of it.

> **The verb.** **Metaschematizo** — to change the outward figure, not the thing itself. And what
> is being impersonated is righteousness, not vice.
