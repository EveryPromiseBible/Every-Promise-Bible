The first thing built on the new earth is an altar, and God's answer to it holds the
world's weather in place.

## The first act

*Then Noah **built an altar to the LORD**.*

The first altar named in Scripture. Cain and Abel brought offerings; nobody had built
one before this.

He is standing on a planet with eight people on it, and this is what he does first.

## The cost

*Taking **some of every clean animal and bird**, he offered burnt offerings on it.*

Every clean species had come aboard by sevens rather than twos — the extra ones are on
this altar.

The surplus that would have looked like breeding stock on an empty earth is what he
burns.

## The aroma

*The LORD **smelled the pleasing aroma**.*

The Hebrew is *a soothing savour* — **reyach nichoach**, a restful smell. It becomes the
standard phrase for accepted sacrifice through the whole of Leviticus.

Paul reaches for it once, about Christ: *an offering and a sacrifice to God for a
sweet-smelling aroma.*

## What He says in His heart

*And said **in His heart**.*

Moses records it as an interior resolution, not an announcement. The promise is made
before Noah is told it.

## "Never again"

*"**Never again** will I curse the ground because of humankind… and **never again** will
I destroy all living creatures as I have done."*

Twice, in one breath.

## The reason attached

*"**Even though** every inclination of the human heart is bent toward evil **from
childhood**."*

That is nearly word for word what God said before the flood — *every intent of the
thoughts of his heart was only evil continually* — and there it was the reason for the
judgment.

Here the identical assessment is the thing the promise is made in spite of.

Nothing about the human heart has changed between chapter 6 and chapter 8. What has
changed is that there is an altar in front of Him.

## What is promised

*"As long as the earth endures, **seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and
winter, day and night will never cease**."*

Four pairs. Every one is a cycle, and the guarantee is that they keep turning.

Seedtime comes first and harvest second, in that order, permanently.

## What it is guaranteed by

Not by the stability of the climate and not by anything Noah does afterward — the next
chapter has him drunk in a tent.

By a sentence God said to Himself over a burning altar.

## The sign

The rainbow comes in the next chapter, given as the visible token of this. See the note
on Genesis 9:8–17.

> **What it rests on.** God smelled a sacrifice and put the seasons beyond recall. The
> rhythm of the world's food supply is fastened to an altar.
