The charge, and the verb in it is not one of the violent ones.

## The relationship

*"Because of the violence you did to **your brother Jacob**."*

The Hebrew is **achikha Ya'aqov** — your brother Jacob.

Edom is Esau. The book never lets the reader forget that this is a family matter, and Deuteronomy
had made a point of it: *you shall not abhor an Edomite, for he is your brother.*

## What they actually did

*"**On the day you stood aside**, on the day strangers carried off his wealth, when foreigners
marched through his gates and cast lots for Jerusalem, **you were there too. You were like one of
them**."*

The Hebrew for *stood aside* is **beyom amodkha minneged** — in the day of your standing opposite,
at a distance.

The charge is not that Edom sacked Jerusalem. It is that Edom watched.

## The verbs that follow

The next paragraph lists eight things they should not have done, and most of them are not actions
at all:

*You should not have **gazed** on the day of your brother… you should not have **rejoiced** over the
children of Judah… you should not have **spoken proudly** in the day of distress.*

Watching, being pleased, and talking about it.

## What standing at a distance costs

*"Now a certain priest came down that road. And when he saw him, **he passed by on the other
side**. Likewise a Levite… came and looked, and **passed by on the other side**."*

**Luke** 10. Two men whose only recorded action is not stopping, and **Jesus** builds the whole
parable on it.

*"Then He will also say to those on the left hand, 'Depart from Me'… for I was hungry and **you gave
Me no food**; I was thirsty and **you gave Me no drink**.'"*

**Matthew** 25 has a list of six charges and every one of them is an omission.

*Therefore, **to him who knows to do good and does not do it, to him it is sin**.*

## The brother word

*Whoever hates his brother is a murderer, and you know that no murderer has eternal life abiding in
him.*

*"**Cain**, who was of the wicked one and murdered his brother. And why did he murder him? Because
his works were evil and his brother's righteous."*

**John** puts the first family quarrel in Scripture next to the command to love, and Obadiah's
subject is the second one.

## What the day was

*They cast lots for Jerusalem.*

*They divided My garments among them, and **for My clothing they cast lots**.*

Psalm 22, quoted in all four gospels. The soldiers do it at the foot of a cross while, according to
**Matthew**, everybody who knew Him *stood at a distance, watching*.

*But all His acquaintances, and the women who followed Him from Galilee, **stood at a distance**,
watching these things.*

**Luke**'s phrase is **apo makrothen** — Obadiah's charge, in Greek, of the people who loved Him.

> **The charge.** Not that they did it — that they were there. *On the day you stood aside… you
> were like one of them.* Obadiah's eight accusations are almost all things Edom failed to do.
