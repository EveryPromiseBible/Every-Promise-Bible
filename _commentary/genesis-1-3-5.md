The first recorded action of God toward a dark world is that He spoke.

## What He was looking at

The verse before: *the earth was without form and void, and darkness was on the
face of the deep.*

That is the situation. Formless, empty, dark.

## What He did about it

*Then God **spoke**. "Let there be light."*

He did not repair the darkness, examine it, or comment on how dark it was. He
said what He wanted to be there.

The Hebrew is three words: *yehi or* — and it is answered by two: *vayehi or.*
Light was.

## "At once, effortlessly, simply because He said so"

There is no interval in the sentence. The saying and the being are the same
moment.

Hebrews puts it this way: *the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that the
things which are seen were not made of things which are visible.*

## Light before the sun

The sun, moon and stars are not made until the fourth day. There is light on the
first day and no light source in the sky for three more.

Whatever this light is, it does not come from a lamp.

## "God looked at the light and loved what He saw"

The first thing called **good** in the Bible.

And He *drew a line between the light and the darkness*. He separated them rather
than mixing them or managing the balance.

## What the pattern is

God is made in no one's image; man is made in His. And the first thing recorded of
Him is that He faced a dark, empty, formless situation and said what He wanted.

Not what He could see. What He wanted.

Proverbs puts the same thing about us: *death and life are in the power of the
tongue.* See the note on Proverbs 18:17–24.

## Evening, then morning

*Evening came, then morning — the first day.*

The Hebrew day runs from dark to light, not light to dark. Every one of the six
days is counted that way.

The direction is always toward the morning.

> **Where the New Testament puts this verse.** *For it is the God who commanded
> light to shine out of darkness who has shone in our hearts, to give the light of
> the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.* Paul quotes day
> one and says the same command was spoken over a person.
