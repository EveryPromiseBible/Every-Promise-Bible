The town He grew up in tries to kill Him, minutes after admiring Him.

## What they had just said

Two verses earlier: *all bore witness to Him and **marvelled at the gracious words**
which proceeded out of His mouth. And they said, "Is this not Joseph's son?"*

He had read Isaiah 61 — good news to the poor, healing for the brokenhearted,
liberty to the captives, sight to the blind — and sat down and said *today this
Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing.* See the note on Luke 4:16–21.

They were pleased.

## What turned them

He brought up two Gentiles.

*There were many widows in Israel in the days of Elijah… but to none of them was
Elijah sent except to Zarephath, in the region of Sidon, to a woman who was a widow.
And many lepers were in Israel in the time of Elisha the prophet, and none of them
was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian.*

Two famines of blessing inside Israel, and both times the prophet went outside.

That is the sentence that empties the room.

## The rage

*Everyone in the synagogue heard that, and they were **filled with rage**.*

The Greek is **thumos** — a boiling over, fury.

From *marvelled at His gracious words* to this, in the space of two sentences.

## What they tried

*They **drove Him out of the town**, and brought Him to the edge of the hill the town
was built on, **meaning to throw Him off it headlong**.*

Nazareth sits on a ridge. This is an attempted execution by mob, on the Sabbath, by
His own neighbours — the people who had watched Him grow up.

## What He did

*But **He walked right through the middle of them** and went on His way.*

No struggle is described, no miracle announced, no rebuke. He walks through the
crowd holding Him.

John records the same thing in the temple after *before Abraham was, I AM* — they
picked up stones, *but Jesus hid Himself and went out, passing right through the
middle of them.* See the note on John 8:57–59.

Nobody laid a hand on Him until the night He let them.

## What Nazareth got

Mark's account of the same visit: *He could do no mighty work there, except that He
laid His hands on a few sick people and healed them. And He marvelled because of
their unbelief.*

He had opened the scroll and read out what He came to do, and offered it to them
first. They took Him to the cliff instead, and He went elsewhere.

The offer was withdrawn from the town, not from the world.

## The two marvellings

Mark says Jesus *marvelled* at Nazareth's unbelief. Matthew says He *marvelled* at a
Roman centurion's faith, and said *I have not found faith like this anywhere in
Israel.* See the note on Matthew 8:10–13.

The only two things recorded as astonishing Him, and they are on opposite sides of
the same question.

> **What He had said just before the two examples.** *No prophet is accepted in his
> own country.* He knew what the room was going to do, and read the passage anyway.
