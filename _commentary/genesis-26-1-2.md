A famine, and a father's example, and a different instruction.

## The comparison

*A famine in the land — **different from the earlier one in Abraham's time**.*

Genesis makes the reader think of it. In that one, Abram *went down to Egypt to stay
awhile, for the famine was severe* — with no word from God anywhere in the passage,
and it ended in a lie about his wife and a rebuke from a pagan king. See the note on
Genesis 12:10–20.

Isaac is standing in the same situation his father handled badly.

## Where he went

*Isaac went to **Abimelech, king of the Philistines, in Gerar**.*

Gerar is on the way to Egypt, not in it. He has started moving in the familiar
direction.

## The word

*"**Do not go down to Egypt. Live in the land I tell you to live in.**"*

Two commands, and the second one does not name the place. He is told to stay and to
be told later.

## Why it is a hard word

Egypt had the Nile. When the rains failed in Canaan, Egypt still had a river and
granaries, and everybody in that region knew it.

He is told to stay in the country with the famine in it.

## What follows

*"Stay in this land, and **I will be with you and bless you**… I will perform the oath
which I swore to Abraham your father."* See the note on Genesis 26:3–5.

The promise is attached to the staying.

## What happened

*Isaac sowed in that land, and **reaped in the same year a hundredfold**; and the LORD
blessed him.* See the note on Genesis 26:12–14.

He plants a crop in a famine and gets a hundred times back.

The Philistines end up envying him and stopping his wells, and Abimelech asks him to
leave because *you are much mightier than we.*

## The one thing Isaac repeated

He told Abimelech that Rebekah was his sister — the same lie his father had told, in
the same town, to a king of the same name.

Genesis records the obedience and the inherited fault in the same chapter.

## What Isaac is otherwise known for

Very little. He is the quietest of the three patriarchs — no journey like Abraham's, no
wrestling like Jacob's.

He dug his father's wells again, after the Philistines had filled them in, *and he
called them by the names which his father had called them.*

## Why this son does not leave

*"**Do not go down to Egypt.** Live in the land I tell you about."*

His father had gone down in a famine and come back out in disgrace. See the note on
Genesis 12:10–20.

But there is more to it than a warning off a bad precedent. Isaac is the son who was laid on
the wood at Moriah, and from that chapter on Genesis never lets him out of the country — not
for a wife, and not for food. See the note on Genesis 24:5–6.

*By faith he dwelt in the land of promise as in a foreign country, **dwelling in tents with
Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise**.*

## What is promised in a famine

*"I will be with you and bless you… and **in your seed all the nations of the earth shall be
blessed**."*

The same sentence given to Abraham, repeated to the second generation without a single
condition added — and Paul says who the Seed in it is: *He does not say, "And to seeds," as of
many, but as of one, "And to your Seed," **who is Christ***. See the note on
Galatians 3:15–18.

> **What the chapter is really about.** Staying put. Abraham went down and came back
> up. Isaac is told not to go, sows in the famine, and is the only patriarch who never
> leaves the land.
