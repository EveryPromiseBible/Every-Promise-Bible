The last speech any of the friends make, and it is six verses long.

## The note the text makes

*Then Bildad from Shuah answered, **briefly**.*

Three rounds of speeches have run for twenty-two chapters. The first cycle is long, the second
shorter, and this is the third — and Zophar does not speak at all.

The friends are running out.

## What is true in it

*"**Dominion and dread belong to God**. **He keeps order in His high heavens**."*

The Hebrew is **hamshel vaphachad** — rule and terror.

*"**Can anyone count His armies?** On whom does His light not rise?"*

Nothing in this is false. It is standard, correct, orthodox theology about God's greatness.

## The question

*"So **how can a mortal be righteous before God?** How can anyone born of a woman be pure?"*

The Hebrew is **umah-yitzdaq enosh im-El**.

It is the right question. It is the question the whole book is circling.

## Where the question came from

Job asked it himself in chapter 9: *how can a man be righteous before God?*

And Eliphaz asked it in chapter 4, having heard it from a spirit in the night: *can a mortal be
more righteous than God?* See the note on Job 4:12–17.

Three of them have now asked it, and none has an answer.

## The argument

*"Look, **even the moon is not bright to Him**, and **the stars are not pure in His sight**."*

If the brightest things in creation are dim by comparison, then a man has no case.

## The conclusion

*"**How much less a human being, who is only a maggot, a child of dust, who is only a worm**."*

The Hebrew is **rimmah** — maggot, and **tolea** — worm.

## What is wrong with it

Nothing, doctrinally. And it is aimed at a man sitting in ashes scraping his sores with a
potsherd, whom God has already called *blameless and upright*.

The friends' theology is largely correct and their application of it is what God rebukes at the
end: *My wrath is aroused against you and your two friends, for **you have not spoken of Me what
is right, as My servant Job has**.*

## What Job says next

*"**How have you helped him who is without power?** How have you saved the arm that has no
strength? How have you counselled one who has no wisdom?"*

The reply opens with sarcasm and then turns into the longest speech in the book.

## Where Job's answer had already gone

*"**For I know that my Redeemer lives**, and He shall stand at last on the earth… **and after my
skin is destroyed, this I know, that in my flesh I shall see God**."* See the note on
Job 19:25–27.

Six chapters earlier, from the man Bildad is calling a worm.

## What Job had asked for

*"**Nor is there any mediator between us**, who may lay his hand on us both."* See the note on
Job 9:32–35.

A man to stand between, touching both parties. Bildad's question has no such answer available to
it.

## Where the answer eventually comes

*For there is one God and **one Mediator between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus**.* See the note
on 1 Timothy 2:5–6.

## The other worm

*But I am a worm, and no man; a reproach of men, and despised by the people.* — Psalm 22, the
psalm quoted from the cross.

The Hebrew there is **tolaath**, the same root Bildad uses. It is also the name of the crimson
grub from which scarlet dye was made.

> **What is wrong with the speech.** Not the theology. The audience — a man God has already called
> blameless, being told he is a maggot by a friend who came to comfort him.
