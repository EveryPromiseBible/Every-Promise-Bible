He does not begin with what happened. He begins with a reason for loving.

## The opening

*I love the LORD, **for He heard my voice**.*

The Hebrew is **ahavti ki-yishma YHWH** — I love, because the LORD hears.

The verb has no object in the Hebrew. Literally: *I love, because…*

## What is missing

The name is supplied by translators; the Hebrew simply says *I love*.

The psalm opens with the verb standing alone and the reason attached to it.

## The reason

*He heard **my cries for mercy**.*

The Hebrew is **tachanunai** — my supplications. From **chanan**, to show favour.

## The posture

*He **bent down to listen to me**.*

The Hebrew is **hittah ozno li** — He inclined His ear to me.

The image is of somebody stooping to the level of a voice too low to hear from standing.

## The consequence

*So **as long as I live I will call on Him**.*

The Hebrew is **uveyamai eqra** — and in my days I will call.

Being heard once is given as the reason for calling for the rest of a life.

## What had happened

*The **ropes of death** had wound around me, the **grip of the grave** had found me.*

The Hebrew is **chevlei-mavet** — cords of death. And **metzarei she'ol** — straits, narrow places,
of Sheol.

**Metzar** — a tight place. The same root as **Mitzrayim**, Egypt.

## The third line

*I met only **trouble and sorrow**.*

The Hebrew is **tzarah veyagon** — distress and grief.

## What he did

*Then I **called on the name of the LORD**: "**O LORD, I beg You, save my life**."*

The Hebrew is **anna YHWH malletah nafshi**.

**Anna** — a particle of entreaty. Please, I beseech.

## The length of the prayer

Four words.

The whole recorded petition of a man in the cords of death is one line.

## What the psalm asks later

*"**What shall I render to the LORD for all His benefits toward me**?"* See the note on
Psalms 116:12–14.

And the answer he gives himself: *I will take up the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of
the LORD*.

The response to being given something is to pick up another cup.

## Where the psalm is sung

One of the Hallel psalms, 113–118, sung at Passover.

*And when they had sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives.*

This is in the portion sung after the meal. He sang about the cords of death on the way to
Gethsemane.

## The cup

*I will take up **the cup of salvation**.*

The Hebrew is **kos-yeshu'ot**. **Yeshu'ot** — salvations, plural.

And in the garden a few hours later: *O My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from Me*.
See the note on Matthew 26:36–39.

## What the psalm says about the death

*Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of His saints.* See the note on Psalms 116:15–19.

The Hebrew is **yaqar** — costly, weighty, rare.

> **The stoop.** *He inclined His ear.* The picture is somebody bending down to hear a voice that
> could not be heard from standing.
