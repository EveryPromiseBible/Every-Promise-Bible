The most uncomfortable *therefore* in the Gospels, and John puts it there
deliberately.

## The message

*So the sisters sent word to Jesus: "**Lord, the one You love is sick.**"*

They do not ask for anything. No request, no instruction, no *please come*.

They name the relationship and state the situation, and leave it there. It is one
of the shortest prayers in Scripture and one of the most confident.

## The love, stated three times

- *the one **You love** is sick* — the sisters' words
- *Now **Jesus loved** Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus* — John's own note
- and later, at the tomb, the crowd: *see how **He loved** him!*

John is making sure nobody can read what follows as indifference.

## The sentence

*And when He heard that Lazarus was ill, **He stayed on where He was two more
days**.*

In the Greek, the word joining the love to the delay is *therefore*. **Because** He
loved them, He stayed.

That is not the connective anybody would choose. John chose it.

## What He said about it

*"This sickness **will not end in death**. It is for the glory of God, so that the
Son of God may be glorified through it."*

Lazarus does die. So either the statement is wrong, or *end* means something other
than the moment of death — and the rest of the chapter makes clear which.

Death happens and is not the end of the sentence.

## The arithmetic of the delay

Bethany was about two days' journey away. He waits two days, then travels two days,
and Lazarus has been in the tomb four days when He arrives.

Jewish belief of the period held that the spirit lingered near the body for three
days. Four days was past any possible ambiguity — and Martha says so at the tomb:
*Lord, by this time he stinks.*

The delay put the situation beyond every explanation except one.

## What He told the disciples

*"Lazarus is dead. **And I am glad for your sakes that I was not there**, that you
may believe."*

Glad. He says it plainly.

Had He come at once, Lazarus would have been healed and the sisters would have had
their brother back — which is what they wanted, and less than what they got.

## What the waiting was not

Not reluctance, not distraction, and not a failure to hear. He heard, He loved, and
He stayed — in that order, in one sentence.

> **What He does when He finally arrives.** Weeps. He knows exactly what He is about
> to do, He arranged the timing Himself, and He still stands at the grave and cries.
