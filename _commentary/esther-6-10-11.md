Haman describes, in loving detail, the honours he wants for himself, and then has
to carry every one of them out for the man he came to hang.

## What he had just designed

The king had asked what should be done for the man he delights to honour, and
Haman — *whom would the king delight to honour more than me?* — designed it
himself: the king's own robe, the king's own horse, the crown on its head, and a
noble to lead it through the city square shouting.

He was writing his own coronation.

## "For Mordecai the Jew"

*"Do all of this for **Mordecai the Jew**, who sits at the king's gate."*

The king names him with the two things Haman hates most: his name, and his
nation.

*Who sits at the king's gate* — the very posture that started the whole rage. See
the note on Esther 5:9–13.

## "Leave out nothing"

*"**Leave out nothing** of everything you have described."*

Every item. The king will not let him trim it.

Haman had built the list for maximum glory, and now the length of it is the
length of his own humiliation.

## What he had come to ask

He is standing in that court at daybreak for one reason: to get permission to
hang Mordecai on a gallows already built and waiting outside his house.

He never gets the sentence out. The king speaks first, and by the time Haman is
allowed to answer he is holding a robe.

## The verbs

*So Haman **took** the robe and the horse, and he **dressed** Mordecai, and he
**led** him through the city square… **calling out** before him.*

Four verbs, all his, all performed on the man he meant to kill. He dressed him
with his own hands.

*"This is what is done for the man the king delights to honour!"* — shouted, by
Haman, in the street, all afternoon.

## What is not happening

No one has intervened. No prophet has spoken, no plague has fallen, nobody has
prayed on the page.

A sleepless night, an old record, and a man's own vanity handing him the exact
opposite of what he came for.

## What he had designed

The robe the king had worn, the horse the king had ridden, the crown on its head, and a noble
prince to lead it through the square shouting.

Haman wrote the ceremony assuming he was describing himself.

*Whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and **he who humbles himself will be exalted**.*

**Jesus** says it twice in Luke, and Esther 6 is the plainest illustration of it in the Old
Testament.

## Who has to do it

*"**Hasten, take the robe and the horse, as you have suggested, and do so for Mordecai the
Jew**… Leave nothing undone of all that you have spoken."*

The man who designed the honour has to lead the horse and shout the words.

## Nobody arranged it

Nobody plans any of it. Mordecai has not asked, Esther has not intervened, and the king cannot
sleep — that is the whole mechanism. A sleepless night, a chronicle read aloud, and an
overlooked entry.

*And we know that **all things work together for good** to those who love God.*

> **The word for it.** *Whoever digs a pit will fall into it, and a stone will come
> back on the one who starts it rolling.* Haman's own gallows, his own robe, his
> own parade. Every instrument in this book turns around in the hand that made it.
