A list of names, and one of them is worth stopping on.

## What the list is

*These are the people of the province who came up out of captivity… now returning
to Jerusalem and Judah, **each one to his own town**.*

Seventy years after Nebuchadnezzar took them, Cyrus the Persian issues a decree
and they are allowed to go home.

Not everybody went. That is the fact behind the whole book of Esther. Many Jews
had built lives in Babylon and Persia across three generations and stayed.

## "Each one to his own town"

The towns had been empty for seventy years. Most of the returners had never seen
them; they are going to places they know only from their grandparents.

## The name in the middle

*They came with Zerubbabel, together with Jeshua, Nehemiah, Seraiah, Reelaiah,
**Mordecai**, Bilshan…*

Whether or not this is the man in the palace at Susa, the name is here in the
list of those who went up — and it tells you the road home was open.

The Jews of Esther's book are in Persia by choice. Susa is where they had settled
when Jerusalem was available.

## Why that matters for Esther

Esther is the story of the people who did not go back, in the season Hosea called
*not My people* — no temple, no land, no prophet, and God's name never spoken in
the whole of it.

And He is at work in every chapter of it anyway. That is the thing the book was
written to show: they were out of the land, out of the covenant blessings, out of
His visible dealings — and not out of His hands.

## The tally

*This is the tally of the men of the people of Israel, **counted by their
families**.*

The chapter that follows is fifty-eight verses of numbers, family by family,
village by village. Down to the singers, the gatekeepers, and the temple servants.

Somebody counted, and wrote it down, and it was kept.

> **What they came back to.** Rubble, and a foundation. When the older men who had
> seen the first temple saw the new foundation laid, Ezra says they wept aloud
> while everyone else shouted for joy, and nobody could tell the weeping from the
> shouting.
