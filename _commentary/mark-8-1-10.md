Four thousand people, seven loaves, and disciples who have already watched this
happen once.

## What starts it

*I have compassion for the crowd, because they have already been with Me three
days and have nothing to eat.*

He raises it, not them. And the reason given is the length of time they have
stayed — three days without provisions, some of them a long way from home.

*If I send them home hungry, they will collapse on the way.*

## The question

*Where could anyone get enough bread to feed these people out here in this
lonely place?*

They had been present at the five thousand. Mark has told that story two chapters
earlier, with twelve baskets left over.

He records the question without comment, which is more damning than an
explanation would be.

## The differences from the first feeding

Mark keeps the numbers distinct, and they are not the same story told twice:

| | five thousand | four thousand |
|---|---|---|
| loaves | five | seven |
| baskets left | twelve, *kophinoi* | seven, *spyrides* |
| location | Jewish side of the lake | Gentile region, the Decapolis |

Even the word for basket changes. The second is the large hamper — the kind Paul
was later lowered over a wall in.

## Why the Gentile setting matters

This is the region where the man freed from the legion had been telling people
what the Lord had done for him. See the note on Mark 5:18–20.

The crowd that stays three days to listen is in the country that once asked Jesus
to leave.

> **What Jesus says about it later.** In the boat He asks them: when I broke five
> loaves for five thousand, how many baskets? Twelve. And the seven for four
> thousand? Seven. *Do you still not understand?* He treats the two feedings as a
> single lesson they had failed twice.
