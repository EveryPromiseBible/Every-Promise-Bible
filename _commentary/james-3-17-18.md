James has just described a wisdom that is earthly and produces disorder. Here is
the other kind, and he gives it a checklist.

## What comes first

*The wisdom that comes down from above is **clean, first of all**.*

He says *first* deliberately — this one leads. The word is about being pure,
unmixed, without a hidden agenda.

Everything else on the list is relational, and the purity has to come first
because the rest can be counterfeited by someone who simply wants to be liked.

## Then the list

- **it makes peace**
- **it goes easy on people** — gentle, considerate, the word for not insisting on
  the letter of your rights
- **it will hear you out and give ground** — willing to be persuaded, open to
  reason
- **packed with mercy and with good things that actually grow**
- **it does not play favourites and it never puts on a face**

Read it as a test and it becomes very practical. Not *is this clever*, but: does
it make peace, does it go easy, will it listen, does it produce anything.

Wisdom that is technically correct and leaves wreckage behind it fails the test
James sets.

## The harvest line

*People who make peace plant their seed in peace, and what comes up out of that
ground is a life put right.*

The image is agricultural and the sequence matters — the peace is both the soil
and the seed. Righteousness is the crop, not the method.

> **What he is contrasting it with.** The verses just above describe wisdom that
> is earthly, unspiritual, and demonic, marked by bitter envy and selfish
> ambition — and James says where it shows: *disorder and every evil practice.*
> Both wisdoms are recognised by what grows out of them, which is the only test
> he offers for either.
