The number is given twice, and the second one is where the weight falls.

## The form

*There are **six things the LORD hates**, yes, **seven that are detestable to Him**.*

The Hebrew is **shesh hennah sane YHWH veshev to'avat nafsho**.

## The device

A numerical proverb: *x, and x plus one*.

Amos uses it — *for three transgressions, and for four*. Proverbs 30 is full of it.

The last item in the list is the one the form is built toward.

## The word

**To'evah** — abomination, something detestable. Used of idolatry and of the practices that got the
Canaanites expelled.

## The anatomy

The first six are body parts, in order from the top.

*Eyes* that look down on others. *A tongue* that speaks lies. *Hands* that shed innocent blood. *A
heart* that hatches wicked schemes. *Feet* quick to run toward evil.

Eyes, tongue, hands, heart, feet.

## The first

*Eyes that **look down on others**.*

The Hebrew is **einayim ramot** — lofty eyes.

Pride heads the list, as it does in the New Testament vice lists.

## The middle

*A heart that hatches wicked schemes.*

The Hebrew is **lev choresh machshevot aven** — a heart ploughing plans of iniquity.

**Charash** — to plough, to engrave, to fabricate.

## The sixth

*A false witness who **breathes out lies**.*

The Hebrew is **yafiach kezavim**.

The same construction as the psalm: *false witnesses rise against me, breathing out violence*. See
the note on Psalms 27:11–14.

## The seventh

*And **one who stirs up conflict among his own**.*

The Hebrew is **umeshalleach medanim bein achim** — one sending out discords among brothers.

## Where the emphasis falls

Last, in the *six, yes seven* form.

The list has murder in it at number three, and the climactic position is given to a person who sets
brothers against each other.

## What that person does

**Shalach** — to send, to let loose. The verb used of releasing an arrow or an animal.

Not a fight. Something released that goes on running afterwards.

## The chapter around it

The passage sits between a warning about surety for a neighbour and an extended warning about
adultery.

## What Proverbs says elsewhere about the same person

*A perverse man sows strife, and **a whisperer separates the best of friends**.* See the note on
Proverbs 16:24–33.

And *the words of a talebearer are like tasty trifles, and they **go down into the inmost body**.* See
the note on Proverbs 18:8.

The Hebrew is **kemitlahamim** — like dainties, morsels swallowed greedily.

## The other side

*Behold, **how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity**!* See the note on
Psalms 133:1.

And *endeavouring to **keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace***. See the note on
Ephesians 4:1–3.

The Greek is **tērein** — to guard, to keep watch over. Not to create.

> **The seventh place.** The form builds to its last item, and the list that contains bloodshed
> reserves that place for the man who sets brothers against each other.
