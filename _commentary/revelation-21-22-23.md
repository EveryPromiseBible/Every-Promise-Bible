---
key: Revelation 21:22–23
title: "No temple, and no need of a lamp"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Two things are missing from the city, and John is careful to say what replaced them.

## The building that is not there

*And **I saw no temple in it**.*

Consider who is writing. A Jew, from a nation whose entire worship was organised around one
building, whose Scriptures spend more chapters on its construction than on the creation of the
world, and whose people had watched it burn.

And the last vision of the last book reports its absence as good news.

## Why a temple existed

A temple exists to solve a problem: God is present, and people cannot come near. Everything about
the architecture said so — an outer court, an inner court, a veil, a room entered once a year by one
man carrying blood.

*Because **the Lord God who holds all power, and the Lamb, are its temple**.*

There is no fenced-off place in the city because there is nothing left to fence. That is what the
tearing of the veil started, *from top to bottom*, at the moment Jesus died.

## No sun, no moon

*The city has no use for the sun or the moon.*

Not that they have been destroyed — that they are not needed. **Ou chreian echei**: no requirement.

Isaiah promised it: *the sun shall no longer be your light by day… but the LORD will be to you an
everlasting light.*

## The lamp

*The glory of God is what lights it, and **the Lamb is its lamp**.*

**Lychnos** — a hand-lamp, the small clay light a household carried from room to room. It is the word
Jesus used of Himself as the light nobody puts under a basket, and the word used of John the Baptist,
*a burning and shining lamp*.

The glory is the light; the Lamb is what it shines through. That is the same arrangement Paul
describes now: *the light of the knowledge of the glory of God **in the face of Jesus Christ***.

Glory that would be unapproachable in itself is met, in that city as in this world, in a Person you
can look at.

> **Everything the old system was for is simply present.** No temple, because the separation is over.
> No lamp, because the Light is there. John is not describing a city that has lost its religion — he
> is describing one that has been given the thing all of it was pointing at, and the thing turns out
> to be a Lamb.
