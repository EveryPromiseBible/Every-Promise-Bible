title: He lifted his hands and blessed them
updated: 2026-08-09

The offerings are finished, and the first thing the high priest does with his hands is raise
them over the people.

## What has been offered

*Having offered **the sin offering, the burnt offering, and the peace offering**, he came down.*

The whole sequence, in order, on the eighth day — the first day the priesthood actually
functioned.

## The gesture

*Then Aaron **lifted his hands toward the people and blessed them**.*

The first recorded priestly blessing in Scripture, and it is given from hands that have been
handling blood all morning.

The words are almost certainly the ones from Numbers 6: *the LORD bless you and keep you; the
LORD make His face shine upon you and be gracious to you.*

## Where the same posture turns up

*And He led them out as far as Bethany, and **He lifted up His hands and blessed them**. Now it
came to pass, **while He blessed them, He was parted from them** and carried up into heaven.*

The last thing Luke records Jesus doing is this, and it is the posture He left in.

He did not finish. The blessing was still going when He went.

## What comes down

*And the glory of the LORD appeared to all the people. **Fire came out from the presence of the
LORD and consumed the burnt offering** and the fat on the altar.*

The fire is not lit by a priest. It comes out from the presence and takes the offering off the
altar.

That is the sign the sacrifice was accepted, and it arrives after the blessing rather than before
it.

## What happened when the fire fell again

*When the Day of Pentecost had fully come… there appeared to them divided tongues, **as of
fire**, and one sat upon each of them.*

The first time, fire falls on a sacrifice on an altar. The second time, it falls on people. In
both cases it comes down after somebody has gone in and come out again.

## The reaction

*And when all the people saw it, **they shouted for joy and fell facedown to the ground**.*

Both at once. A shout and a face in the dirt.

## Going in and coming out

*Moses and Aaron went into the tent of meeting. **When they came out**, they blessed the people.*

The people stand outside and wait, and what they are waiting for is the man to come out again.

It is the same anxiety the bells on the hem were there to answer. See the note on
Exodus 28:31–35.

*So Christ was offered once to bear the sins of many. **To those who eagerly wait for Him He will
appear a second time**, apart from sin, for salvation.*

Hebrews puts it in exactly that shape: a priest who went in, and a congregation outside waiting
for Him to come back out.

> **What He was doing when He left.** *While He blessed them, He was parted from them.* Aaron
> came down when the blessing was finished. The other one never lowered His hands.
