The second half of the sentence that begins with grace appearing.

## What comes before

*Because **the grace of God has come out into the open**, bringing rescue with it… and that
same grace **takes us in hand and teaches us**.* See the note on Titus 2:11–12.

This is the same sentence continuing. The teaching has an object in view.

## The posture

*All the while we are **watching the road ahead**, **glad about what is coming**.*

The Greek is **prosdechomenoi** — looking for, waiting to receive.

**The blessed hope** — Paul calls the waiting itself blessed.

## What is coming

*The day **the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ comes into full view**.*

The Greek is **epiphaneia** — appearing, manifestation. The same root as the verb in verse
11, where grace *appeared*.

Two appearings, one past and one future, and the sentence is held together by the word.

## The title

*Our **great God and Savior Jesus Christ**.*

The Greek construction — one article governing both nouns — is one of the clearest
statements in the New Testament that the two titles belong to one person.

Peter uses the identical construction: *our God and Saviour Jesus Christ*. See the note on
2 Peter 1:1–2.

## The self-giving

*He **gave Himself up for us**.*

The Greek is **edoken heauton** — gave Himself. Paul uses it repeatedly: *who loved me and
gave Himself for me*, and *gave Himself for the church*.

## The first purpose

*To **buy us out from under every kind of lawlessness**.*

The Greek is **lutrosetai** — to ransom, to redeem by paying a price. From **lutron**, the
ransom money.

It is the word Jesus used of Himself: *to give His life a **ransom** for many.* See the
note on Matthew 20:24–28.

**Every kind of lawlessness** — **anomia**, without law. Not one kind.

## The second purpose

*And to **wash us clean**.*

The Greek is **katharise** — to purify. The word for ritual cleansing.

## The third

*And **make us a people who belong to Him and to nobody else**.*

The Greek is **laon periousion** — a people for His own possession.

The phrase comes straight out of Exodus: *you shall be **a special treasure** to Me above
all people.* And Deuteronomy: *the LORD has chosen you to be a people for Himself, a
special treasure.*

Paul applies Israel's title to a mixed congregation on Crete.

## What they are for

*With **an appetite for doing good**.*

The Greek is **zeloten kalon ergon** — zealots for good works.

Paul, who spent his letters arguing that works do not justify, ends this sentence with
zeal for them — and puts them at the far end of a chain that begins with grace appearing.

## The order of the whole sentence

Grace appeared → it teaches → we watch the road → He gave Himself → to ransom, to cleanse,
to make a people → with an appetite for good.

Nothing in the chain is asked of the reader before the giving.

## What Paul says next

*Speak these things, exhort, and rebuke with all authority. **Let no one despise you**.*

The last line of the chapter, to a young man left on Crete.

> **Where the good works land.** At the end. Paul runs the whole sentence from an
> appearing, through a ransom and a washing, to a people who belong to somebody — and the
> appetite is the last item, not the first.
