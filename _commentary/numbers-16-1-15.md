The complaint is made in the language of a compliment to everybody else.

## Who leads it

*Korah, **a Levite**.*

Not an outsider. Korah is Kohathite — the branch of Levi that carried the ark, the
table, the lampstand and the altar on their shoulders. He had the most sacred haulage
job in Israel.

And he was Moses' first cousin.

## Who joins him

*Dathan and Abiram and On, and with them **two hundred and fifty leaders of Israel, men
of name and standing**.*

The Hebrew is *men of renown*, called of the assembly.

This is not a mob. It is the leadership.

## The accusation

*"**You take too much on yourselves**," they said.*

The Hebrew is *you have gone too far*, *enough from you*.

## The argument

*"**The whole community is holy, every one of them, and the LORD is among them all.
Why do you lift yourselves above the assembly of the LORD?**"*

Every clause of that is true. God had said it at Sinai — *you shall be to Me a kingdom
of priests and a holy nation*.

Korah takes an accurate statement about the nation and uses it to conclude that no
particular calling exists.

## Moses' first response

*When Moses heard it, **he fell facedown**.*

He does not answer. Numbers records him doing this repeatedly — at Kadesh, at Taberah,
here.

The man accused of lifting himself up goes down on the ground.

## The test he proposes

*"In the morning **the LORD will show who is His** and who is holy… **Take censers**, all
of you, and put fire and incense in them before the LORD tomorrow, and **we will see whom
He chooses**."*

He does not defend the appointment. He hands the question over and sets a time.

Incense was priest's work exclusively. Two hundred and fifty men are invited to do it,
and the offer is not a trick — it is the test they asked for.

## The question back to Korah

*"You Levites — **is it a small thing that God has drawn you close** to serve at His
dwelling? **Yet you reach for the priesthood too**."*

The Hebrew for *drawn you close* is **qarab** — brought near. It is the word for
approaching the altar.

He already had the nearness. The complaint is about the office.

## Dathan and Abiram

*He sent for Dathan and Abiram, but **they refused to come**.*

And their answer inverts the whole exodus:

*"**You brought us out of a land flowing with milk and honey** to die in this desert."*

That phrase describes Canaan, in every other place it appears in the Torah. They apply
it to Egypt — the country of the brick quotas and the river of dead babies.

*"And now you would **lord it over us**."*

## Moses' defence

*Moses was **deeply grieved** and said to the LORD, "**I have not taken so much as a
donkey from them, nor wronged a single one**."*

The only thing he answers is the charge of profiteering, and he answers it to God rather
than to them.

Samuel says almost the same words at the end of his life: *whose ox have I taken, or
whose donkey have I taken?*

## What the quarrel was really about

The priesthood. Korah already had the nearness — he carried the ark on his shoulders — and what
he wanted was the office.

*And **no man takes this honor to himself**, but he who is called by God, just as Aaron was. So
also **Christ did not glorify Himself to become High Priest**, but it was He who said to Him,
"You are My Son, today I have begotten You."*

Hebrews makes Korah's exact point and settles it the opposite way. Nobody appoints himself to
this, and the argument is closed by pointing at Someone who did not appoint Himself either.

## Where Korah's name ends up

*Woe to them! For they have gone in the way of Cain, have run greedily in the error of Balaam for
profit, and **perished in the rebellion of Korah**.*

Jude lists him with Cain and Balaam.

And yet — *nevertheless **the children of Korah did not die***. Eleven psalms carry their name,
including *as the deer pants for the water brooks*. See the note on Numbers 16:16–35.

> **What the whole quarrel is about.** Access. Korah, who carried the holy things,
> wanted to burn the incense — and Moses answers by handing out two hundred and fifty
> censers and letting God decide.
