Matthew's version of the explanation, and one word in it is not in Mark's.

## The first soil

*When anyone **hears the message of the kingdom and it does not take hold in him**, the evil
one comes and **snatches away what was sown in his heart**.*

The Greek for *does not take hold* is **me sunientos** — not understanding, not putting it
together.

Mark says only that Satan comes and takes it. Matthew names the gap the theft goes
through. See the note on Mark 4:13–20.

**Sown in his heart** — Matthew locates the seed.

## The second

*The one who **hears the word and welcomes it right away with joy**, but **it has no root in
him and does not last**.*

The Greek is **proskairos** — temporary, for a season.

*As soon as **trouble or persecution comes because of the word**, he falls away.*

**Because of the word.** The pressure is named as arriving on account of the thing sown,
not despite it.

## The third

*The one who hears the word, but **the worries of this life and the false promises of wealth
crowd in and choke it**.*

The Greek for *false promises* is **apate** — deceitfulness. Riches are not called evil;
they are called deceptive.

Two things do the choking here. Mark has three; Luke has *pleasures of life* as well.

*And **it never comes to anything**.*

The Greek is *it becomes unfruitful*. The plant is alive and produces nothing.

## The fourth

*The one who **hears the word and takes it in and understands it**.*

Three verbs where the others have one or two. Matthew's good soil is defined by
understanding — the same word that was missing from the first soil.

The explanation is framed by it: **sunientos** at the start, **sunieis** at the end.

## The yields

*A **hundred times** what was sown, or **sixty**, or **thirty**.*

Matthew runs them downward; Mark runs them up. Both keep the range.

The good soil is not uniform, and all three figures are called good.

## The verse before the explanation

*"**Therefore I speak to them in parables, because seeing they do not see, and hearing they
do not hear, nor do they understand**."*

And the quotation from Isaiah 6, given in full — *lest they should understand with their
hearts and turn, so that I should heal them*.

## What He says to the disciples

*"But **blessed are your eyes for they see, and your ears for they hear**; for assuredly I
say to you that **many prophets and righteous men desired to see what you see, and did not
see it**."*

## Where the chapter goes

Seven parables in one chapter — the sower, the wheat and tares, the mustard seed, the
leaven, the hidden treasure, the pearl, and the dragnet.

Matthew notes: *all these things Jesus spoke to the multitude in parables; and without a
parable He did not speak to them.*

## The one word Matthew adds

Understanding, at both ends of the explanation.

The first soil loses the seed because it is not understood. The fourth bears because it
is.

> **What Matthew supplies that Mark does not.** The gap the birds come through. He names
> it at the top of the explanation and again at the bottom, and it is the same word both
> times.
