Two verses describing an arrangement, and every clause in them is a restriction.

## The outer room

*The priests were **in and out of the front room every day**, doing the work of the
worship.*

Daily traffic. The lampstand to trim, the bread to change, the incense morning and
evening.

Ordinary priests, ordinary access, ordinary work.

## The inner room

*But the inner room — **only the high priest** went in there, and **only one day a
year**, and **never without blood**.*

Three limits stacked in one sentence:

- one man
- one day
- and a required condition

## Which day

The Day of Atonement, Yom Kippur. Once a year, and Leviticus 16 sets out the
procedure in detail — the linen garments, the washing, the incense to cover the mercy
seat in cloud, the blood sprinkled seven times.

Aaron is warned that he *shall not come at just any time into the Holy Place*, on
pain of death.

## For himself first

*Which he offered **for himself** and for the sins the people had blundered into.*

The high priest could not represent anybody until he had dealt with himself.

Hebrews makes the point again a few chapters later: *He does not need daily, as
those high priests, to offer up sacrifices, first for His own sins and then for the
people's.*

## "The sins the people had blundered into"

The Greek is **agnoema** — sins of ignorance, things done without knowing.

That is what the Day of Atonement covered. The system had provision for what people
did not realise they had done, and nothing at all for deliberate, high-handed sin.

## What the writer concludes from it

*The Holy Spirit indicating this, that **the way into the Holiest of All was not yet
made manifest** while the first tabernacle was still standing.*

The closed curtain was a message. As long as that room was shut, the point was being
made that the way in was not open.

## And what happened to the curtain

*Then, behold, the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom.*

Matthew records it at the moment of the death. **From top to bottom** — nobody
standing on the floor could have started it there.

## The contrast Hebrews draws

*Therefore, brethren, having boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus, by
a new and living way which He consecrated for us, through the veil, that is, His
flesh… **let us draw near**.*

One man, one day, never without blood — and then *let us draw near*, addressed to
everybody, with no day named. See the note on Hebrews 10:19–21.

> **What was in the room.** The ark, and above it the mercy seat with the cherubim.
> And after the exile, according to the historians, the room was empty — the ark was
> gone. The high priest went in once a year, with blood, into an empty room, for
> centuries.
