The fourth son, and the chapter changes register completely.

## The name

*"Judah, your brothers will **praise** you."*

**Yehudah** means praise. Leah named him: *now I will praise the LORD.*

The first three sons produced instability and violence. The fourth is called praise
and gets the throne.

## What praise does in that tribe's history

Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, faced three armies with no plan, and was told: *you
will not even need to fight this battle. Take your positions, stand still, and
watch the salvation of the LORD.*

He appointed singers to go out **in front of the army**, and *when they began to
sing and to praise, the LORD set ambushes* — and it was over before Judah reached
the battlefield. See the note on 2 Chronicles 20:16–19.

## The lion

*"Judah is a **young lion**… like a lion he crouches and lies down — who would dare
rouse him?"*

Three stages of one animal in two lines: the young lion that has come up from the
prey, the mature lion, and the lioness.

Revelation names Him from this verse: *the Lion of the tribe of Judah has
prevailed.*

## The sceptre

*"The sceptre will not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff from between his
feet, **until he to whom it belongs shall come**, and the obedience of the peoples
will be his."*

The Hebrew of *he to whom it belongs* is **Shiloh** — a word that occurs nowhere
else, and the oldest Jewish readings take it as a name of Messiah. Its root
carries the sense of rest, tranquility.

*And the obedience of the peoples* — not Israel. The peoples, plural, the nations.

## The donkey and the vine

*"He will **tie his donkey to the vine**, his colt to the choicest branch."*

Nobody ties livestock to a vine. A donkey would strip it in an hour, and a vine is
the most carefully tended plant a man owns.

It is a picture of abundance so complete that the loss does not matter.

And a colt, tied, waiting for a king — *tell the daughter of Zion, behold, your
King is coming to you, lowly, and sitting on a donkey, a colt, the foal of a
donkey.* They found it *tied* at a doorway, and He rode it into Jerusalem.

## The wine

*"He will **wash his garments in wine**, his robes in **the blood of grapes**."*

Not spilled on. Washed in.

Isaiah 63 takes the same image the other way: *why is Your apparel red, like one
who treads in the winepress? I have trodden the winepress alone.*

## The vine, again

*"I am the vine; you are the branches… **apart from Me you can do nothing**."*

He says it in an upper room, on the night of the cup, and the branch's whole job in
that passage is to stay attached. The fruit is not the branch's work. See the note
on John 15:5–8.

> **How long the sceptre stayed.** Judah held the right to try capital cases until
> Rome took it away, and the old accounts record the rabbis lamenting that the
> sceptre had departed and Shiloh had not come. He was in Nazareth at the time.
