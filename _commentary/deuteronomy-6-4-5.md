The sentence a Jewish child learns first and a dying Jew says last.

## The name

**Shema** — the first word, and the name of the prayer.

*Hear.* An imperative, and it means listen and act, both at once. Hebrew has no separate verb
for obey.

## The claim

*The LORD our God, **the LORD is one**.*

The Hebrew is four words: **YHWH Eloheinu YHWH echad**.

It can be read *the LORD our God is one LORD*, or *the LORD is our God, the LORD alone*.
Either way the point is exclusivity.

## The word for one

**Echad** — one. And it is the word used in Genesis of a man and his wife: *they shall become
**one** flesh*.

Not the word for solitary uniqueness, which is **yachid** — the word used of Isaac, Abraham's
only son.

## Where it is written

On the doorposts. *You shall write them on the doorposts of your house and on your gates* —
which is what a mezuzah is, a small case with these verses inside, on the frame of a door.

And on the arm and forehead, in the little boxes of the tefillin.

## The command

*And **love the LORD your God**.*

The first thing commanded after the declaration is an affection.

Not fear, not serve, not obey. Love — and it is the only religion in the ancient world that
commands it.

## The three

*With your **whole heart**, with your **whole soul**, with **everything you have**.*

The Hebrew is **levav**, **nephesh**, **meod**.

**Levav** — the heart, which in Hebrew is the seat of thought and will as much as feeling.

**Nephesh** — the whole self, the life.

**Meod** — an adverb meaning *very*, *much*, used here as a noun. *With all your muchness*.
The old translations render it *might*; the rabbis read it as property.

## What is left out

Nothing. That is the design of the list.

*Let nothing in you be held back. Let Him have all of you.*

## What Jesus does with it

Asked which is the first commandment of all, He recites it — including the *Hear, O Israel*
line, which nobody else in the New Testament quotes.

*"This is the first commandment. And the second, like it, is this: **You shall love your
neighbour as yourself**. There is no other commandment greater than these."*

He adds a fourth item to the list — **dianoia**, mind — when He quotes it in Greek.

## The scribe's answer

*"Well said, Teacher. You have spoken the truth… and **to love Him with all the heart, with
all the understanding, with all the soul, and with all the strength, and to love one's
neighbour as oneself, is more than all the whole burnt offerings and sacrifices**."*

And Jesus: *you are not far from the kingdom of God*.

## What follows in Deuteronomy

*"And these words which I command you today **shall be in your heart**. **You shall teach
them diligently to your children**, and shall talk of them **when you sit in your house, when
you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up**."*

Four settings, covering the whole of a day. The teaching is domestic and constant.

## What comes after that

*"When the LORD your God brings you into the land… with **great and good cities which you did
not build**, houses full of all good things which you did not fill… **then beware, lest you
forget the LORD**."* See the note on Deuteronomy 6:10–12.

The Shema is immediately followed by a warning about prosperity.

> **What is commanded first.** Not obedience. *Love* — and the list of what to love Him with
> is built so that nothing is left outside it.
