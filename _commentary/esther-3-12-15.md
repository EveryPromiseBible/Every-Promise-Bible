The genocide is not a mob. It is paperwork, properly filed, in every language of
the empire.

## The machinery

*The king's scribes were called… to each province in **its own script** and to
each people in **its own language**.*

Translated for every people so that nobody could plead they had not understood.
The empire's whole administrative apparatus is turned on a single ethnic group.

*Sealed with the king's ring.* Under Persian law that made it irrevocable — the
rule the reader was taught back in chapter 1, when it was only about a queen not
coming to dinner.

## Four verbs

*Ordering that all Jews be **destroyed, killed, and wiped out**, young and old,
little children and women, all in a single day — and their goods taken as
plunder.*

The piling-up is deliberate. And the phrase *young and old, little children and
women* is Amalek's signature: the people at the back, the ones who cannot run.

## The plunder clause

*Their goods taken as plunder.*

Haman is buying the empire's cooperation. Anyone who kills a Jew keeps the house.

Hold this clause in mind — in chapter 9, when the Jews defend themselves and win,
the writer says three separate times that *they did not lay a hand on the
plunder.* They were entitled to it under the counter-decree and they left it.

## Eleven months

The lot had fallen on the thirteenth of Adar. The letters go out in the first
month, Nisan.

Every Jew in the world now has a date on which they are legally to be killed, and
eleven months to look at it.

## The last sentence

*Then **the king and Haman sat down to drink**, while the city of Susa reeled in
confusion.*

Two men with a glass, and a city outside the window not knowing what has hit it.
The writer sets the two clauses in one line and lets them stand.

This is the low point of the book. There is no God named, no prophet, no army, no
appeal — a sealed law that cannot be revoked, and the men who wrote it are
drinking.

> **What the reader knows and Susa does not.** Nisan is the month of Passover.
> The decree ordering the destruction of the Jews was written on the day before
> the feast that commemorates their deliverance.
