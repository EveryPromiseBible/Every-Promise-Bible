title: "Prepared for you from the making of the world"

The verdict on the right hand, and two things in it are surprising.

## The invitation

*"**Come, you who are blessed by My Father. Take what is yours, the kingdom prepared for you from
the making of the world.**"*

The Greek is **hetoimasmenen hymin apo kataboles kosmou** — prepared for you from the foundation of
the world.

The kingdom is not awarded at the end of the list of works. It was made ready before the world was.

*Just as **He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world**, that we should be holy and
without blame before Him in love.*

*Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has begotten us again to a living hope…
**to an inheritance incorruptible and undefiled and that does not fade away, reserved in heaven for
you**.*

*"Do not fear, little flock, for **it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom**."*

## The six

Hungry, thirsty, a stranger, naked, sick, in prison. And six responses: fed, given a drink, taken
in, clothed, looked after, visited.

Nothing on the list is religious, and nothing on it is difficult to understand.

## What the righteous say

*"**Lord, when did we see You hungry and feed You?**"*

They do not recognise their own works.

That is the second surprise and it is the larger one. The people being commended have no
recollection of the evidence, cannot produce the list, and query the verdict.

Nothing in the scene shows anybody presenting a record. The ones who do present a record are in
**Matthew** 7 — *have we not prophesied in Your name?* — and they are the ones turned away. See the
note on Matthew 7:21–23.

*For **we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand
that we should walk in them**.*

**Ephesians** 2, one verse after *by grace you have been saved… not of works*. The works are
prepared in advance too, and by the same person who prepared the kingdom.

## The answer

*"**Whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of Mine, you did for Me.**"*

The Greek is **heni touton ton adelphon mou ton elachiston** — one of the least of these brothers of
Mine.

He does not say the poor represent Him or remind Him of Himself. He says the transaction was with
Him.

*"He who receives you receives Me, and **he who receives Me receives Him who sent Me**."*

*"**Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?**"… "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting."*

**Acts** 9. Saul had been arresting Christians and had never met **Jesus**, and the sentence on the
Damascus road puts Him at the receiving end of it — which is Matthew 25 in the negative.

## Who the King is

*"Then **the King** will say."*

The only place in the gospels **Jesus** calls Himself that in a narrative rather than accepting the
title from somebody else.

And He is describing Himself, in the same paragraph, as having been hungry, thirsty, a stranger,
naked, sick and imprisoned.

*Who, being in the form of God… **made Himself of no reputation**, taking the form of a bondservant.*

> **What the commended cannot do.** Produce the evidence. They ask when it happened, four times.
> The people in this gospel who can recite their record are the ones told *I never knew you*.
