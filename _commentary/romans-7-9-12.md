Paul tells the story of his own encounter with one commandment, and the ending is a
defence of it.

## The before

*There was a time when I was **alive apart from the law**.*

The Greek is *I was alive without the law once*.

He is not describing innocence. He is describing a period when the commandment had not
yet landed on him.

## The arrival

*Then **the commandment came**, and **sin sprang to life**, and **I died**.*

Three clauses in sequence, and the middle one is the surprise.

The Greek for *sprang to life* is **anezesen** — revived, came back to life.

Sin is described as the thing that woke up. Paul does not say the commandment created it;
he says it was there and dormant.

## Which commandment

He names it two verses earlier: *I would not have known covetousness unless the law had
said, "**You shall not covet**."*

The tenth. The only one of the ten that is entirely internal — nobody can see it, and no
court can try it.

## The reversal

*The very commandment that was **meant for life** turned out, **in my case**, to bring
death.*

The Greek is *the commandment which was unto life, this I found to be unto death*.

Leviticus had said it: *you shall therefore keep My statutes and My judgments, **which if
a man does, he shall live by them**.*

The offer was life. Paul reports the outcome in his own case.

## The mechanism

*For sin **found its opening in the commandment**, **deceived me**, and through it **killed
me**.*

The Greek for *opening* is **aphorme** — a base of operations, the place an expedition
starts from. It was a military word for a bridgehead.

Sin used the prohibition as its launching point.

**Deceived me** — **exapatesen**, thoroughly deceived. It is the word used of the serpent
in the garden: *the serpent deceived me, and I ate.*

The pattern is the same. A command, a suggestion about it, and a death.

## The verdict

*So **the law is holy**, and **the commandment is holy and right and good**.*

Paul has just said it killed him, and this is his conclusion.

Three adjectives, and the whole point of the paragraph is that none of them is being
withdrawn.

He says it again in the next verse: *has then what is good become death to me? Certainly
not! But **sin**, that it might appear sin, was producing death in me through what is
good.*

The fault is located and it is not in the statute.

## Where the chapter goes

*For what I am doing, I do not understand… the good that I will to do, I do not do.* See
the note on Romans 7:18–20.

And the cry: *O wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me?*

Then chapter 8: *there is no condemnation now — none at all.* See the note on
Romans 8:1–4.

## What Paul had been before

*Concerning the righteousness which is in the law, blameless.*

The man who says *I died* had a perfect external record.

## Who does the delivering

*Who will deliver me from this body of death? **I thank God — through Jesus Christ our Lord!***

The chapter's own resolution, and the grammar matters: **Paul** does not answer *what* will deliver
him. He answers with a name and a thanksgiving.

*For **what the law could not do in that it was weak through the flesh, God did by sending His own
Son** in the likeness of sinful flesh, on account of sin: He condemned sin in the flesh.*

## What the commandment did

*The commandment, which was to bring life, **I found to bring death**.*

*For **the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life**.*

*Who also made us sufficient as ministers of the new covenant… For if **the ministry of condemnation**
had glory, the ministry of righteousness exceeds much more in glory.*

**2 Corinthians** 3, and **Paul** calls the same law a ministry of condemnation and of death, twice,
in a paragraph that also calls it glorious.

## What he had been

*Concerning the law, **a Pharisee**… concerning the righteousness which is in the law, blameless.*

*But what things were gain to me, these **I have counted loss for Christ**… that I may gain Christ and
be found in Him, **not having my own righteousness**.*

The man writing *the commandment killed me* had the best law record available in his generation, and
says in **Philippians** what he did with it.

> **What the paragraph protects.** The law. Paul describes it doing him lethal damage and
> then spends the last verse insisting it is holy, right and good — and puts the blame on
> what used it as a bridgehead.
