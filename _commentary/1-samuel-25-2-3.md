Two people introduced in one verse, and the writer sets them against each other
word for word.

## His name

*His name was **Nabal**.*

**Nabal** means fool. Not a nickname the chapter awards him — his own wife says
it later: *as his name is, so is he. Nabal is his name, and folly is with him.*

The Hebrew **nabal** is not the word for a simpleton. It is the moral fool of
Proverbs and the Psalms — *the fool has said in his heart, there is no God.*

## What he had

*He was **very rich** — three thousand sheep and a thousand goats.*

The writer gives the figures. He is a substantial man, and shearing time was the
year's payday.

## The two descriptions

*She was a woman of **good sense and lovely to look at**, but the man was **harsh
and mean** in his dealings.*

The Hebrew for her good sense is **sekel** — insight, prudence, the wisdom that
produces good outcomes. It is the same root as the word in Joshua 1:8, where
meditating on the book produces *good success*.

Her sense is listed **before** her looks.

And his two adjectives are hard and evil in his doings. The contrast is deliberate
and total.

## What she does with it

David had protected Nabal's shepherds in the wilderness — *they were a wall to us
both by night and day*, one of the servants says. At shearing, David asks for
provision and is insulted: *who is David? Who is the son of Jesse?*

David straps on his sword and takes four hundred men to kill every male in the
household.

Abigail loads two hundred loaves, two skins of wine, five dressed sheep, roasted
grain, raisins and figs, and rides out to meet him without telling her husband.

## The speech

It is one of the longest by a woman in the Old Testament, and it works. She takes
the blame herself, feeds four hundred men, and then tells David what he is about to
throw away:

*"When the LORD has done for my lord all the good He has promised, and made you
ruler over Israel, then you will have **no cause for grief, no burden on your
conscience** for shedding blood without cause."*

She argues from his future rather than his anger. See the note on
1 Samuel 25:29–31.

## What David says

*"**Blessed is your advice**, and blessed are you, because you have kept me this
day from coming to bloodshed and from avenging myself with my own hand."*

He blesses the counsel before he blesses the woman.

## What became of them

Nabal held a feast *like the feast of a king* that same night, drunk. She told him
in the morning, *and his heart died within him, and he became like a stone*, and
ten days later he was dead.

David sent for Abigail and married her.

> **The house he was from.** *He was of the house of Caleb* — the man who at
> eighty-five asked for the mountain with the giants on it. The blood was there; the
> spirit was not. See the note on Joshua 14:6–15.
