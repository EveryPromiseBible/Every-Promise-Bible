Four verses, and everything in Paul's life afterward comes out of them.

## What he was doing

The verse before: *Saul, still **breathing threats and murder** against the disciples of
the Lord, went to the high priest and asked letters from him to the synagogues of
Damascus, so that if he found any who were of the Way, he might bring them bound to
Jerusalem.*

He is on official business with warrants in his bag. Damascus is a hundred and fifty
miles from Jerusalem — a week's walk, undertaken deliberately.

## The light

*Suddenly **a light out of heaven blazed all around him**.*

He tells it twice more in Acts, and each time adds a detail: *at midday*, and *brighter
than the sun*, and *shining around me and those who journeyed with me*.

Midday, and it outshone the sun.

## The fall

*He **fell to the ground**.*

## The name, twice

*"**Saul, Saul**, why are you persecuting Me?"*

The doubled name — *Abraham, Abraham*, *Moses, Moses*, *Samuel, Samuel*, *Martha,
Martha*, *Simon, Simon*.

Every time in Scripture it precedes something serious, and every time it is warm.

## The question

*"Why are you **persecuting Me**?"*

Not *why are you persecuting them*. Paul had never met Jesus and had been arresting
people in houses in Jerusalem.

The identification is the whole of what he is told, and his letters never stop working it
out: *you are the body of Christ*, and *if one member suffers, all the members suffer with
it*.

## The question back

*"**Who are You, Lord?**"*

The Greek **kurie** can be simply *sir*. He does not yet know.

It is one of the few genuinely open questions in Scripture, asked by a man face down in
the road.

## The answer

*"**I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting**."*

In his own account to Agrippa he adds *Jesus **of Nazareth***.

## The goads

*"It is hard for you to **kick against the goads**."*

A goad was a pointed stick used to drive oxen. An animal that kicked back drove it in
further.

The phrase was a Greek proverb — it appears in Euripides — and Paul, from Tarsus, would
have known it as a piece of ordinary speech.

## The next question

*Trembling and astonished, he said, "**Lord, what do You want me to do?**"*

The second use of *Lord* in the passage, and the man asking is the one who had letters in
his bag an hour earlier.

## What he is told

*"**Get up and go into the city, and you will be told what you must do**."*

He is not told what the assignment is. He is told to go and wait to be told.

## What followed

Three days without sight, eating and drinking nothing, in a house on Straight Street —
and then Ananias, who had to be argued into going: *Lord, I have heard from many about
this man, how much harm he has done to Your saints.*

The answer: *Go, for **he is a chosen vessel of Mine** to bear My name before Gentiles,
kings, and the children of Israel. For **I will show him how many things he must suffer**
for My name's sake.*

## How Paul describes it later

*Last of all He was seen by me too, **as by a child born at the wrong time**.* See the note
on 1 Corinthians 15:3–8.

And: *I am the least of the apostles, who am not worthy to be called an apostle, because I
persecuted the church of God.*

He never stops mentioning it.

> **What he was given on the road.** A name, a question, and an instruction to go and be
> told. The commission itself was delivered days later, through a frightened man who did
> not want to knock on the door.
