Paul's last letter, and this is what he tells a young man to do when everything else is
coming apart.

## The contrast

*But **you**.*

The Greek is emphatic — *su de*.

What comes before it: a list of what the last days will be like — *lovers of themselves,
lovers of money, boasters, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents* — eighteen items, and
then *evil men and impostors will grow worse and worse*.

Against all of it: *but you*.

## The instruction

*Hold your ground **in what you learned and came to be sure of**.*

The Greek is **mene** — remain, abide, stay put.

Not advance, and not defend. Stay.

## The first reason

*Because **you know the ones you learned it from**.*

The Greek is plural — *from whom you learned*.

Paul names them at the start of the letter: *the genuine faith that is in you, which dwelt
first in **your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice**.*

Two women, named, three generations back.

Acts adds the detail: Timothy's father was Greek. The Scripture came down the maternal
line.

## The second

*And you know that **ever since you were a small child you have been at home in the sacred
writings**.*

The Greek is **apo brephous** — from infancy. **Brephos** is the word for an unborn or
newborn child; Luke uses it of John leaping in the womb.

## What he learned

*The **sacred writings**.*

The Greek is **hiera grammata** — the standard Jewish term for the Hebrew Scriptures.

Timothy learned the Old Testament as a small boy, in a Greek town, from a Jewish mother and
grandmother, years before Paul arrived in Lystra.

## What they can do

*And they are able to **make you wise for the salvation that comes through faith in Christ
Jesus**.*

The Greek is **sophisai** — to make wise.

The clause is careful. The writings make wise *for* salvation, and the salvation comes
*through faith in Christ Jesus*.

Paul is describing what the Old Testament did for a boy who met Christ later.

## What comes next

The verse everybody knows:

*All Scripture is **given by inspiration of God**, and is profitable for doctrine, for
reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness.*

The Greek is **theopneustos** — God-breathed. One word, and it appears once in the New
Testament.

## What the letter is

Paul's last. He is in a cold cell — *bring the cloak that I left with Carpus* — most of his
companions have gone, and *only Luke is with me*.

*I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith.* See the
note on 2 Timothy 4:6–8.

## What he asks Timothy to do

*Be diligent to come to me quickly… **come before winter**.*

Whether he arrived is not recorded.

> **What Paul tells him to fall back on.** Not new arguments. What he learned, and who he
> learned it from — two women who taught him the Scriptures before he could walk.
