Paul writes a second letter because somebody had forged the first.

## The subject

*Now about **the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ**, and about **our being gathered together to
Him**.*

Two things named, and Paul treats them as one topic.

The Greek for *coming* is **parousia** — the arrival, a royal visit.

And **episunagoges** — a gathering together, the noun behind *synagogue* with an intensifier.
It occurs twice in the New Testament; the other is Hebrews' *not forsaking the assembling of
ourselves together*.

## What he had already written

The first letter, one chapter earlier in the canon: *the Lord Himself will descend from
heaven with a shout… and we who are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in
the clouds*. See the note on 1 Thessalonians 4:16–18.

That is the gathering he means. He is not introducing a new subject; he is correcting the
handling of an old one.

## The request

*We are asking you, friends, **not to let yourselves be shaken loose from your senses or
thrown into alarm**.*

The Greek is **saleuthenai** — to be shaken, as a ship torn from its moorings or a building
in an earthquake.

And **throeisthai** — to be alarmed, startled. The same word Jesus uses in the Olivet
discourse: *see that you are not troubled*.

## The three channels

*Not by any **spirit**, not by anything **said**, not by any **letter dressed up to look as
though it came from us**.*

Prophecy, preaching, and correspondence.

The third is the alarming one. Somebody had written to the Thessalonians in Paul's name.

That is why he signs off this letter the way he does: *the salutation of Paul with my own
hand, which is a sign in every epistle; **so I write***.

He had to establish a handwriting sample.

## The claim being spread

*Claiming that **the day of Christ has already arrived**.*

The Greek is **enesteken** — a perfect: has come and is present.

Not that it is near. That it has happened, and they missed it.

## Why that was frightening

They were being persecuted. The first chapter of this letter is about it: *your patience and
faith in all your persecutions and tribulations that you endure*.

If the day had come and this was it, then what they were suffering was the tribulation and
the gathering had not included them.

Paul's whole answer is aimed at that fear.

## What he says next

*Do not let anyone fool you, whatever method is used. **That day does not come until the
falling away comes first, and the man of sin is uncovered**.* See the note on
2 Thessalonians 2:3–4.

He does not argue from feelings. He gives them a checkable sequence.

## The pastoral shape of it

The correction is not *stop speculating*. It is: here is what has to happen first, and it has
not happened, so you can stop being frightened.

## What he had said in the first letter

*But concerning the times and the seasons, brethren, you have no need that I should write to
you. For you yourselves know perfectly that the day of the Lord so comes as a thief in the
night.*

And *God did not appoint us to wrath, but to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ*.

## What he closes this section with

*Therefore, brethren, **stand fast and hold the traditions which you were taught**, whether by
word or our epistle.*

And then: *now may our Lord Jesus Christ Himself, and our God and Father, who has loved us and
given us everlasting consolation and good hope by grace, **comfort your hearts and establish
you** in every good word and work.*

The Greek is **sterixai** — make firm. The opposite of **saleuthenai**, shaken loose.

> **What he is answering.** Not curiosity. A forged letter telling frightened people the day
> had already come and they had been left in it.
