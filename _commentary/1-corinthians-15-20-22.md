After sixteen verses of what would be true if Christ had not been raised, Paul turns the corner
with three words — *but now Christ is risen* — and immediately reaches for a harvest festival.

## Firstfruits

*The **firstfruits** of all those who have fallen asleep.*

**Aparche** — the first sheaf of the barley harvest. Leviticus 23 sets the day: the priest waves it
*on the day after the Sabbath* that falls in Passover week, and until that sheaf is waved not one
loaf of the new crop may be eaten anywhere in Israel.

That is the Sunday morning He walked out of the tomb.

And a firstfruits offering is not a sample. It is a legal claim on everything still standing in the
field. The sheaf goes up, and the whole harvest is thereby declared holy and gathered in due
course. *And if the firstfruit is holy, the lump is also holy.*

So Paul is not saying Christ's resurrection is encouraging evidence about ours. He is saying it has
already committed the rest of the field.

## Fallen asleep

The word for the dead here is **kekoimemenon** — those who have been put to sleep. It is where our
word cemetery comes from: **koimeterion**, a sleeping place, a dormitory.

Christians did not invent that word to be brave. They used it because of what the first sheaf
proved about the morning the rest of the crop comes in.

## Two men, and everyone behind them

*For since death came through a man, the resurrection of the dead has come through a Man as well.
For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all shall be made alive.*

The whole race hangs on what one man did — which is the fact everybody instinctively resents until
they notice it is the only mechanism by which they could be rescued. If you were never consulted
about Adam, you are not required to qualify for Christ.

And Paul insists elsewhere that the second transaction is the larger of the two: *not as the offence,
so also is the free gift… **much more** the grace of God and the gift by the grace of the one Man,
Jesus Christ, abounded to many.*

> **The day the sheaf was waved.** It was not a symbol He arranged to fulfil. The feast had been
> kept for fifteen centuries, on that morning, by people who had no idea what they were holding up.
> The barley went up in Jerusalem and the tomb was already empty outside it. Every harvest that has
> ever been promised to a believer was legally secured that morning by one sheaf.
