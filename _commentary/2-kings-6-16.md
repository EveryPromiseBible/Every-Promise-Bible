One sentence, spoken to a terrified servant who has just done the arithmetic
correctly.

## What the servant saw

The verses before: the king of Syria has sent *horses and chariots and a great army*
to Dothan by night, *and they surrounded the city.*

The servant gets up early, goes out, and there it is.

*"Alas, my master! **What shall we do?**"*

His count is right. Two men, one town, and an army round it.

## The answer

*"**Don't be afraid.** Those who are with us are **more than those who are with
them**."*

Elisha does not dispute the servant's figures. He adds a column the servant did not
know about.

## What Elisha prays

*"LORD, I pray, **open his eyes that he may see**."*

Not *remove the army*. Not *get us out*. Open his eyes.

The prophet's request is about the servant's perception, and nothing about the
situation.

## What was there the whole time

*And the LORD opened the eyes of the young man, and he saw. And behold, **the
mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha**.*

It had been there before the prayer. The praying changed the seeing.

Elisha was not asking for reinforcements. He could already see them, which is why he
was calm at dawn with an army outside.

## What happens next

Elisha prays a second time — *strike this people with blindness* — and then walks the
entire Syrian army into Samaria and asks the king of Israel to feed them.

*"Set food and water before them, that they may eat and drink and go to their
master."*

*Then he prepared a great feast for them; and after they ate and drank, he sent them
away.*

## The result

*So the bands of Syrian raiders **came no more** into the land of Israel.*

The campaign ends not with a slaughter but with a meal.

## Why the army was there at all

Elisha had been telling the king of Israel where the Syrian ambushes were, repeatedly
— *the prophet who is in Israel tells the king of Israel the words that you speak in
your bedroom.*

The army was sent to capture one man who knew too much.

> **The other prayer in the chapter.** A borrowed axe head falls into the Jordan and a
> student cries out *alas, master, it was borrowed.* Elisha cuts a stick, throws it
> in, and the iron floats. Chariots of fire and a lost tool in the same chapter, and
> the book gives both the same attention.
