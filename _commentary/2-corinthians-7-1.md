One sentence, and everything in it depends on the first clause.

## The connective

*So, dear friends, **since these are the promises we hold**.*

The Greek is **tautas oun echontes tas epangelias** — having therefore these promises.

The imperative that follows rests on something already possessed.

## Which promises

The end of chapter 6, a chain of Old Testament quotations:

*"**I will dwell in them and walk among them. I will be their God, and they shall be My people**."*

*"**I will receive you. I will be a Father to you, and you shall be My sons and daughters**, says the
LORD Almighty."*

Leviticus, Isaiah, Ezekiel and 2 Samuel, assembled into one.

## The order

Promises first, then the cleansing.

Paul does not tell them to purify themselves in order to obtain the relationship. The relationship
is the ground.

## The instruction

*Let us **wash ourselves clean of everything that stains the body or the spirit**.*

The Greek is **katharisomen heautous apo pantos molusmou** — let us cleanse ourselves from every
defilement.

**Molusmos** — a stain, a soiling. It occurs once in the New Testament.

## The two categories

*Body or spirit.*

The Greek is **sarkos kai pneumatos** — flesh and spirit.

Paul does not divide them into the visible sins and the interior ones and treat the second as
lesser. Both are named in one clause.

## The word *ourselves*

**Heautous** — reflexive.

The subject and the object are the same. Nobody else does this for them.

## The purpose

*Bringing **holiness all the way to completion**.*

The Greek is **epitelountes hagiosunen** — perfecting holiness.

**Epiteleo** — to bring to an end, to finish what was started.

## What that implies

Something already begun.

The verb assumes a process in progress and asks for it to be carried through, not initiated.

## The manner

*In **the awe of God**.*

The Greek is **en phobo Theou** — in the fear of God.

## What comes immediately before

*"**Come out from among them and be separate**, says the Lord. Do not touch what is unclean, and I
will receive you."*

Isaiah 52, quoted — a command to the exiles leaving Babylon.

## What Paul was writing about

*Do not be unequally yoked together with unbelievers. For what fellowship has righteousness with
lawlessness?*

*And what agreement has the temple of God with idols? For **you are the temple of the living God**.*

Corinth had temples on every corner and meat markets attached to them.

## What follows

*Open your hearts to us. We have wronged no one, we have corrupted no one, we have cheated no
one.*

*I have great boldness of speech toward you, I have great pride in you. **I am filled with comfort. I
am exceedingly joyful in all our tribulation**.*

The severest paragraph in the letter is followed by the warmest.

## What the letter has already said about the cleansing

*But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, **are being
transformed into the same image**.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 3:16–18.

Passive, and the only active verb is the looking.

> **The connective.** *Since these are the promises we hold.* The cleansing rests on a relationship
> already stated, and the verb for perfecting assumes something already begun.
