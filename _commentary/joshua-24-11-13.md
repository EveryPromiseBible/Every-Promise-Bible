Joshua is old, the conquest is behind them, and he stands the nation at Shechem and
tells them what happened — in the first person, as God.

## Who is speaking

The whole recital is *I brought, I gave, I sent, I destroyed*. Joshua is quoting God
speaking about the campaign Joshua himself led.

The general's own account of the war gives the credit to somebody else, all the way
through.

## The list of enemies

*The people of Jericho fought against you — as did the **Amorites, Perizzites,
Canaanites, Hittites, Girgashites, Hivites, and Jebusites** — but **I gave them all
into your hands**.*

Seven nations named, and one verb for the outcome. Not *you defeated them*. **I gave
them.**

## The hornet

*I sent **the hornet** ahead of you, and it drove out the two kings of the Amorites
before you — **not by your sword or your bow**.*

The Hebrew is **tsirah**, and it was promised back in Exodus: *I will send hornets
before you, which shall drive out the Hivite, the Canaanite and the Hittite.*

Whatever it was, the point of the clause is the last six words. Their weapons were
carried, drawn and used — and the verse says the ground was not taken by them.

## The three things given

- **A land you did not toil for**
- **Cities you did not build but now live in**
- **Vineyards and olive groves you did not plant but now eat from**

An olive tree takes fifteen years to bear properly and a vineyard four. They walked
into groves planted by men who never harvested them.

## What Deuteronomy had said in advance

*Great and good cities which you did not build, houses full of all good things which
you did not fill, hewn-out wells which you did not dig, vineyards and olive trees
which you did not plant — when you have eaten and are full, **then beware, lest you
forget the LORD**.*

The danger named is not poverty. It is amnesia.

## Why Joshua is saying it

The recital ends with the demand: *choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve…
**but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD**.*

The list of what was given is the argument. He sets out everything they received
without earning, and then asks for a decision.

## What they still had to do

They crossed, they marched, they fought at Jericho and Ai and the Valley of Aijalon.
Joshua does not pretend otherwise — the fighting is in the verse.

He puts the labour and the result in the same sentence and attributes the result to
God.

## The whole list, and their part in it

*"I gave you **a land for which you did not labour**, and **cities which you did not build**,
and you dwell in them; **you eat of the vineyards and olive groves which you did not plant**."*

Joshua recites it at the end of his life, and the point of the recital is what is missing from
it.

Paul says it of Christ's people in exactly the same shape: *by grace you have been saved
through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, **not of works**, lest anyone
should boast.*

> **What they had done.** Nothing in the list. They received a settled country with
> the wells already dug — and Joshua's whole point in reciting it is that a people
> living off what they did not build are living off a gift.
