Paul tells Timothy how to treat the men who lead, and the word he uses does two
jobs.

## The instruction

*The elders who **lead well** should be counted worthy of **a double portion of
honour**.*

The Greek for *honour* is **time** — and it means both esteem and price. It is the
same noun Peter uses in *give her honour* to husbands, and it is the ordinary word
for the cost of a thing.

Paul's proof text settles which sense he has in mind here.

## The two quotations

*"**Do not muzzle an ox while it treads out the grain**"* — Deuteronomy 25.

*"**The worker has earned his pay**"* — and this one is a saying of Jesus, from
Luke 10, when He sent out the seventy.

Paul quotes Moses and Jesus in one breath and calls both of them *the Scripture*.

The Gospel of Luke was recent, and Paul is already citing it alongside the law.

## The ox, again

He used the same verse in 1 Corinthians 9, with the same application, and there he
spelled out the logic: *is it oxen God is concerned about, or does He say it
entirely for our sake?* See the note on 1 Corinthians 9:7–10.

The picture is an animal working with food under its feet all day. The law forbids
tying its mouth.

## "Especially those who preach and teach"

*And **most of all those who wear themselves out** in preaching and teaching.*

The Greek is to labour to exhaustion — the word Paul uses of his own work.

He distinguishes within the group: elders who lead well get the double portion, and
those who wear themselves out teaching get it most.

## What surrounds this in the chapter

The verses before are about widows — who to support, who not to, at length. The
verses after are about accusations against an elder: *do not receive an accusation
against an elder except from two or three witnesses.*

So the passage is a run of practical instructions about money and reputation inside
a church, and this one sits in the middle of it.

## Paul's own practice

He took none of it. He argued the right thoroughly in Corinth and then made tents,
*that we might not be a burden to any of you.*

He legislates generosity toward others and declines it for himself, and both are in
his letters without any sense of contradiction.

> **The verse right after.** *Do not receive an accusation against an elder except
> from two or three witnesses.* Pay them properly and protect them from casual
> accusation — Paul puts the two instructions side by side, and both are about not
> making a hard job harder.
