key: 3 John 1:3–4
title: "No greater joy"
updated: 2026-08-09

An old man says what pleases him most, and the sentence has no qualifications in it.

## The report

*"It made me so glad when **some of the brothers turned up and told me the truth is in you** — that
you really are living it out."*

**Erchomenon adelphon kai martyrounton** — brothers coming and testifying.

Travelling Christians arrive and report on a man they have stayed with. The news travels by people
rather than letters, which is what the rest of the letter is about.

## The sentence

*"**There is no better news anyone could bring me than to hear that my children are living in the
truth.**"*

The Greek is **meizoteran touton ouk echo charan** — I have no greater joy than these things.

**Meizoteran** is a double comparative, which is not correct Greek and is emphatic in the way
*more better* is emphatic in English. **John** is straining the grammar.

## My children

*"**My children.**"*

**Ta ema tekna.** Gaius is not his son by birth, and the letter never says **John** converted him.

*My little children, for whom I labour in birth again until **Christ** is formed in you.*

*For though you might have ten thousand instructors in Christ, yet you do not have many fathers; for
**in Christ Jesus I have begotten you through the gospel**.*

*Yes, I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth.*

**Paul** says it about the Corinthians and **John** says it about Gaius, and neither means anything
biological.

## What the joy is not attached to

Their giving, their numbers, their soundness in a controversy, or their usefulness to him.

*Walking in the truth.* The Greek is **en aletheia peripatountas** — walking about in truth. An
ordinary verb for an ordinary day.

*"By this all will know that you are My disciples, **if you have love for one another**."*

## Whose joy this is

*"These things I have spoken to you, that **My joy may remain in you**, and that your joy may be
full."*

**John** 15, and the joy in the letter is the one described in the gospel as transferred.

*Who for **the joy that was set before Him** endured the cross, despising the shame.*

*"**Likewise, I say to you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who
repents.**"*

The old man's greatest joy turns out to be the same category as heaven's, and **Luke** 15 says what
sets it off.

## The word that holds the letter together

**Aletheia** — truth. It occurs six times in fourteen verses: *the truth that holds us together*,
*the truth is in you*, *walking in the truth*, *the truth itself*, and twice more.

> **The grammar he breaks.** *Meizoteran* — a double comparative, which is not correct Greek. He is
> reaching for something stronger than the language has, and the thing that produces it is a report
> that somebody is living an ordinary day honestly.
