They announce that they finally understand, and He tells them what they will do in a few hours.

## Their claim

*"There — **now You are speaking plainly, with no picture at all**."*

The Greek is **en parrēsia laleis kai paroimian oudemian legeis** — You speak plainly and say no
figure.

## What prompted it

*"I came forth from the Father and have come into the world. Again, I leave the world and go to the
Father."* See the note on John 16:25–28.

One sentence with no metaphor in it, after a whole discourse of them.

## Their conclusion

*"Now we can see that **You know everything**… This is why **we believe that You came from God**."*

## What is right about it

The confession is correct. He does come from God.

## The reply

*"**Do you believe now**?"*

The Greek is **arti pisteuete** — can be read as a question or a statement.

Either way it hangs.

## What He tells them

*"Look, **the hour is coming, and has already come, when you will be scattered, each of you to his
own home, and you will leave Me alone**."*

The Greek is **skorpisthēte** — scattered. The shepherd-and-sheep word.

Zechariah: *strike the Shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered*.

## The timing

*And has already come.* Within hours.

## The clause He adds

*"**Yet I am not alone, because the Father is with Me**."*

## What happens to that clause

*"My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?"* See the note on Matthew 27:45–47.

He says the Father is with Him on the way to the one hour when He will say otherwise.

## The purpose

*"**I have told you these things so that in Me you may have peace**."*

The Greek is **en emoi eirēnēn echēte**.

The peace has an address. Not *peace about your circumstances* — peace located in a person.

## The concession

*"**In the world you will have trouble**."*

The Greek is **thlipsin echete** — you have pressure. From **thlibō**, to crush, to press.

Present tense. Not a warning about the future.

## What is not offered

Removal.

He grants the pressure in the same breath as the peace, and does not offer to take it away.

## The command

*"But **take heart**."*

The Greek is **tharseite** — be of good courage.

The word He used to the paralysed man, to the woman with the haemorrhage, and to the disciples on
the water: *it is I; do not be afraid*.

## The reason

*"**I have overcome the world**."*

The Greek is **egō nenikēka ton kosmon**.

## The tense

Perfect. A completed action with a standing result.

Spoken before the arrest, before the trial, before the cross.

## The word

**Nikaō** — to conquer. The noun is **nikē**, victory.

## Where it goes

*"To him who overcomes I will grant to sit with Me on My throne, **as I also overcame** and sat down
with My Father on His throne."* See the note on Revelation 3:21.

And *this is the victory that has overcome the world — our faith*. See the note on 1 John 5:4.

## What comes next

*Jesus spoke these words, lifted up His eyes to heaven, and said: "Father, the hour has come."* See
the note on John 17:1–5.

The last recorded sentence before the prayer is a claim of victory, said the night before.

> **The tense.** *I have overcome* — perfect, completed — spoken hours before the arrest. The
> victory is announced in the past tense from inside the upper room.
