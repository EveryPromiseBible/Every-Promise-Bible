A warning delivered to one household, seven years before it is needed.

## Who she is

*The woman **whose son he had restored to life**.*

The Shunammite. A wealthy woman who had noticed Elisha passing and built him a room on the roof —
a bed, a table, a stool and a lampstand.

She had asked for nothing. When he offered to speak for her to the king or the commander of the
army, she said *I dwell among my own people*.

## What had happened

Elisha promised her a son, and she said *no, my lord… do not lie to your maidservant*.

The boy was born. Years later he collapsed in the harvest field — *my head, my head* — and died on
her knees at noon.

She laid him on the prophet's bed, saddled a donkey, rode to Carmel and would not say what was
wrong. *It is well*, she kept saying. See the note on 2 Kings 4:25–30.

## The warning

*"**Go away with your family and stay for a while wherever you can**."*

The Hebrew is **quwmi ulechi at uveitech vegurri ba'asher taguri** — arise and go, you and your
household, and sojourn wherever you can sojourn.

**Gur** — to sojourn, to live as a resident alien. Abraham's word.

## The reason

*"Because **the LORD has decreed a famine in the land that will last seven years**."*

The Hebrew is **qara YHWH lara'av** — the LORD has called for a famine.

**Called for.** As one summons a servant.

## The specificity

Seven years, named in advance.

She has time to sell nothing, pack, and leave in order.

## What it costs her

Her house and her land, left standing empty in a country under famine.

Property abandoned for seven years does not stay yours. The rest of the chapter is about getting
it back.

## What she does

*The woman **did as the man of God said**.* See the note on 2 Kings 8:2.

No recorded question.

## Where she goes

*And stayed in **the land of the Philistines** seven years.*

Israel's enemies. The only place with food.

Naomi's family did the same thing in Moab, and it went badly. Isaac was told to stay put and not
go down to Egypt. See the note on Genesis 26:1–2.

Here she is told to go, and she goes.

## What happens at the end of the seven years

She comes back and finds her property gone, and goes to the king to appeal.

And the king is at that moment asking Gehazi to *tell me all the great things Elisha has done* —
and Gehazi is in the middle of the story of the boy raised from the dead when she walks in. See
the note on 2 Kings 8:4–5.

## What the king gives her

*"**Give back everything that belonged to her, including all the produce of her land from the day she
left the country until now**."* See the note on 2 Kings 8:6.

The land, and seven years of harvests off land she was not farming.

## The word for famine

**Ra'av** — hunger, famine.

Elisha's ministry runs through famine repeatedly: the poisoned stew, the twenty loaves that fed a
hundred, the siege of Samaria and the four lepers at the gate. See the note on
2 Kings 7:3–4.

> **The verb.** *The LORD has called for a famine.* Summoned, as one calls a servant — and the
> household most likely to be ruined by it gets seven years' notice.
