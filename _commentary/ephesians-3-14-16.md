Paul stops arguing and kneels, and the prayer that follows is the high point of the letter.

## The connection

*This is why.*

The Greek is **toutou charin** — for this cause.

He started the same sentence at the beginning of the chapter — *for this reason I, Paul, the
prisoner of Christ Jesus for you Gentiles* — and then interrupted himself for thirteen verses
about the mystery, and comes back to it here.

The whole of Ephesians 3:2–13 is a parenthesis.

## The posture

*I **bend my knees** before the Father.*

Standing was the ordinary posture for prayer among Jews. Kneeling marks unusual intensity.

Solomon knelt at the dedication of the temple. Daniel knelt three times a day. Paul kneels
twice in Acts, both times at partings, and in Gethsemane He *knelt down and prayed*.

## The name

*The Father from whom **the whole family in heaven and on earth takes its name**.*

The Greek plays on two words that sound alike: **patera** and **patria** — Father and family,
or fatherhood.

The sentence says: every grouping that is called a family anywhere is named after Him. The
word for the thing comes from Him, not the other way round.

## What he asks for

*Out of the **sheer wealth of His glory**.*

The Greek is **kata to ploutos tes doxes** — according to the riches of His glory.

**Kata**, according to — the measure is not *out of* the riches, as if taking from a store,
but *in proportion to* them.

Paul uses the same construction in Philippians: *my God shall supply all your need according
to His riches in glory*. See the note on Philippians 4:19–20.

## The request

*He would **strengthen you with power by His Spirit**.*

The Greek is **krataiothenai dunamei** — to be made mighty with power.

Passive. He asks that it be done to them.

## Where

*Deep in **the hidden person you are inside**.*

The Greek is **eis ton eso anthropon** — into the inner man.

Not the circumstances, and not the visible life. Paul asks for power in the part nobody sees.

He uses the phrase elsewhere: *even though our outward man is perishing, yet **the inward man
is being renewed day by day***. See the note on 2 Corinthians 4:16–18.

## What the strength is for

The next clause: *that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith* — and then the roots,
the foundation, the four dimensions, and the fullness. See the note on
Ephesians 3:17–19.

Everything in the prayer is one sentence in Greek. The strengthening in verse 16 is what makes
the comprehending in verse 18 possible.

## What he is not asking for

Nothing about circumstances. Paul is in prison as he writes and does not mention it, except
to say *I ask that you do not lose heart at my tribulations for you, which is your glory*.

## What comes at the end

*Now to Him who is able to do **exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think**,
according to the power that works in us, to Him be glory in the church by Christ Jesus to all
generations, forever and ever. Amen.*

The same **kata** again — according to the power already working.

And it is the last word of the letter's first half. Chapter 4 starts *I therefore, the
prisoner of the Lord, beseech you to walk worthy*.

Three chapters of position, then three of conduct, with a prayer on the hinge.

> **Where he asks for the power.** Not in the circumstances. *Into the inner man* — the part
> of a person nobody else can see.
