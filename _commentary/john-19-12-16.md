The trial ends with the chief priests saying something they cannot have meant.

## Pilate's position

*From then on Pilate **kept trying to release Him**.*

The Greek is an imperfect — he went on seeking.

He has already said *I find no fault in Him* three times, sent Him to Herod, offered
Barabbas, and had Him flogged in the hope that it would be enough.

## The lever

*"**If you let this man go, you are no friend of Caesar**."*

**Friend of Caesar** — **amicus Caesaris** — was a formal honour, an actual title granted to
favoured officials.

Pilate's patron in Rome, Sejanus, had been executed for treason a few years earlier, which
left every man he had appointed exposed.

The sentence is a threat about his career and he understands it immediately.

## The charge restated

*"Anyone who **makes himself a king** is setting himself against Caesar."*

They have moved the charge. It started as blasphemy, which Rome did not care about, and has
been converted into sedition, which Rome executed people for.

## The bench

*He brought Jesus outside and **took his seat on the judgment bench**.*

The Greek is **bema** — the raised platform a Roman magistrate pronounced sentence from.

## The place

*At the place called **the Stone Pavement — in Hebrew, Gabbatha**.*

**Lithostrotos** in Greek — a paved area. **Gabbatha** in Aramaic, probably from a root
meaning height or ridge.

John gives both names, as he does with Golgotha and Siloam. He is writing for people who
could walk to these places.

## The hour

*It was **the day of preparation for the Passover**, about the **sixth hour**.*

Noon, on the day the lambs were killed.

Mark says He was crucified at the third hour, and the difference between the two counts has
been argued over for centuries — Roman reckoning from midnight against Jewish from sunrise.

What John is doing is fixing the time by the temple. At this hour the priests began killing
lambs.

## The presentation

*And Pilate said, "**Here is your King**."*

The Greek is **ide ho basileus humon**.

He had said *behold the Man* an hour earlier. Now *behold your King*, and he means it as an
insult to them.

## The shout

*"**Away with Him! Away with Him! Crucify Him!**"*

## The question

*"**Am I to crucify your King?**"*

Pilate asks it, and it is the last thing he says before giving in.

## The answer

*"**We have no king but Caesar**."*

Said by the chief priests. The men whose entire office existed on the claim that God was
Israel's king.

The Passover liturgy itself has the line *besides You we have no king, redeemer or saviour*.

They said it at noon on the day of preparation.

## What Samuel had said

When Israel first asked for a king: *they have not rejected you, but **they have rejected Me,
that I should not reign over them***.

## What the sign said

Pilate wrote it: **Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews**, in Hebrew, Greek and Latin.

The chief priests asked him to change it to *He said, I am King of the Jews*.

*Pilate answered, "**What I have written, I have written**."*

He gives them the crucifixion and refuses them the wording.

> **What the sentence cost.** *We have no king but Caesar* — said by the priesthood, in the
> temple city, at the hour the Passover lambs were being killed.
