Six towns are set aside before the land is even entered, for people who have not yet done
anything.

## The timing

*"**When you cross into Canaan**, choose cities of refuge."*

Israel is still on the plains of Moab. The land has not been taken, the tribes have not
been allotted, and the provision is already made.

Somebody is going to need this, and God legislates for them in advance.

## Who they are for

*"So that **a person who kills someone by accident, without meaning to**, may flee
there."*

The Hebrew is *who kills any person unintentionally* — **bishgagah**, in error, by
mistake.

Deuteronomy gives the example: two men chopping wood, the axe head flies off the handle,
and the neighbour dies.

## What they are

*"These will be **a shelter from the avenger**."*

The **goel haddam** — the avenger of blood, the nearest kinsman, whose duty it was to
pursue.

The same word **goel** is the kinsman-redeemer: Boaz in Ruth, and the *my Redeemer lives*
of Job.

The nearest relative both redeems and avenges, depending on which side of the death you
are on.

## What the refuge does not do

*"So that the one who caused a death **is not struck down before he can stand trial
before the community**."*

It is not an acquittal. It buys a trial.

The runner is protected long enough to be heard, and the congregation decides which
category he is in. See the note on Numbers 35:16–21.

## The number and the layout

*"Set apart **six such cities — three on the east side of the Jordan and three in
Canaan**."*

Joshua names them: Kedesh, Shechem and Hebron in the west; Bezer, Ramoth and Golan in the
east.

They are spread deliberately, so that no part of the country is far from one. All six
were Levitical cities.

Later Jewish tradition held that the roads to them were kept in repair and signposted.

## Who could use them

*"They will be a refuge for **the Israelite, for the foreigner, and for anyone living
among you**."*

Three categories, and two of them are not Israelites.

The law is unusual in extending identically to the resident alien.

## What the runner had to do

Get there, and stay. The city held him until the death of the high priest, and then he
went home free.

One man's death released everybody in all six cities at once.

## Where the priests come in

The cities of refuge were Levite towns, so a man fleeing a death ran into a place full of
priests and lived off the priests' portion. See the note on Numbers 18:8–20.

## The provision, described

*Here is God's tenderness, **built right into the map**: a door left open, a place of
safety for the frightened and the innocent.*

## Where the language turns up

*We, **who ran for shelter** and grabbed hold of the hope held out in front of us.*

The Greek in Hebrews 6 is the vocabulary of fleeing for refuge. See the note on
Hebrews 6:18.

> **What the law provides for.** Not the guilty and not the safe. The man who did it,
> did not mean to, and is being chased — and the arrangement was made before anyone
> crossed the river.
