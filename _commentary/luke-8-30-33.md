The largest concentration of the enemy named anywhere in the Gospels, and it takes
one word.

## Where He was

The eastern shore of the lake, the country of the Gerasenes — the old territory of
Gad, out beyond the Jordan.

Gad's blessing was one line: *Gad will be attacked by raiders, but he will turn
and strike at their heels.* The Hebrew hammers the word for *troop* five times
over. See the note on Genesis 49:19.

He crossed the water into the tribe of the troop, and the first thing that met Him
on the beach was a legion.

## The name

*"What is your name?" "**Legion**," he answered, because a great many demons had
gone into him.*

A Roman legion was some thousands of soldiers at full strength. The man does not
give a name — he gives a headcount.

Mark adds *for we are many*, and the count of the pigs: about two thousand.

## Who is asking whom

*And they **begged** Him not to order them away into the abyss… the demons
**begged** Him to let them go into the pigs instead.*

Twice in three verses. Whatever they are, they cannot move without permission, and
the whole exchange is them asking and Him deciding.

**The abyss** is the bottomless place, the same word John uses in Revelation. It
is what they are afraid of, and they name it themselves.

## What it cost Him

Nothing. No struggle, no formula, no repetition. *He gave them permission*, and
they came out.

Luke has already told us what the man was before: no clothes, no house, living
among the tombs, breaking chains and shackles, driven into the wilderness.

And after: *sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed and in his right mind.*

## What the town wanted

Not more of it. *They asked Him to depart from them, for they were seized with
great fear.*

He got into the boat and went.

## The one who wanted to come

The man begged to go with Him — a third begging in the chapter — and this is the
one request Jesus refuses.

*"Return to your own house, and tell what great things God has done for you." And
he went his way and proclaimed throughout the whole city.*

The man who could not be bound is sent home to talk about it, and he is the first
person commissioned to preach in Gentile territory.

> **Gad's line, and where it lands.** *He will turn and strike at their heels.* The
> heel is the word from Genesis 3 — *He shall bruise your head, and you shall
> bruise His heel.* Jacob used it over the son whose name means troop, and a legion
> went into the lake on that tribe's ground.
