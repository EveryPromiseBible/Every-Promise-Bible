A prophet pays money for his own wife.

## The purchase

*"So **I bought her back for myself**."*

The Hebrew is **va'ekreha li** — and I purchased her for myself. The verb is used of buying, and
of acquiring by payment.

She is his wife. He has to pay to get her.

## The price

*"**Fifteen pieces of silver and a homer and a half of barley**."*

Fifteen shekels is half of thirty. Thirty shekels is what the law sets as compensation for a slave
gored by an ox — *he shall give to their master thirty shekels of silver.*

The barley is reckoned by some to make up the difference in kind. Either way, the total is roughly
the price of a slave, and barley is the cheapest grain — the offering of the poor and the flour
brought in the jealousy ritual.

He does not pay a bride price. He pays what a slave costs, in coin and animal feed.

## What thirty became

*Then one of the twelve, called Judas Iscariot, went to the chief priests and said, "What are you
willing to give me if I deliver Him to you?" And **they counted out to him thirty pieces of
silver**.*

*"And I said to them, 'If it is agreeable to you, give me my wages'… **So they weighed out for my
wages thirty pieces of silver**. And the LORD said to me, "Throw it to the potter" — that princely
price they set on Me.*

Zechariah 11, quoted at the betrayal. The slave's price appears twice in Scripture as a valuation
of a person, and the second time the person is **Christ**.

## Who else was bought

*Or do you not know that… you are not your own? For **you were bought at a price**.*

*Knowing that **you were not redeemed with corruptible things, like silver or gold**… but with **the
precious blood of Christ**.*

**Peter** makes the contrast explicit, and Hosea 3 is the passage it answers. Hosea paid silver and
barley. The price named in **1 Peter** is deliberately not that.

## The terms

*"You will live with me many days. You will not wander, you will not give yourself to another, and
**I will be true to you**."*

Three clauses about her and one about him, and his is unconditional.

The Hebrew of the last is *and also I toward you* — the sentence is elliptical, and it is the only
promise in the verse.

## What it is a picture of

*"For the people of Israel will live **many days without king or prince, without sacrifice or
sacred stone**."*

The next verse, and it maps the terms onto the nation: a long period with nothing, and then
*afterward the children of Israel shall return and seek the LORD their God and David their king,
and shall fear the LORD and His goodness in the latter days.*

## The word for goodness

*They shall fear the LORD **and His goodness**.*

*Or do you despise the riches of His goodness, forbearance and longsuffering, not knowing that
**the goodness of God leads you to repentance**?*

> **The sum.** Fifteen shekels and a homer and a half of barley — about the price of a slave, half
> of it paid in the cheapest grain there was. A prophet buying his own wife out of another man's
> house, and told to do it because that is what God is doing.
