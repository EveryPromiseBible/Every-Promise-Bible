The first meeting with the eleven, and He does not knock.

## The day

*That same day, in the evening of **the first day of the week**.*

Sunday. The tomb was found empty that morning.

Mary Magdalene has already told them — *I have seen the Lord* — and the two from Emmaus have
just arrived with the same news. See the note on Luke 24:33–35.

They are still behind a locked door.

## The reason

*The disciples were together **behind shut doors, because they were afraid of the Jewish
leaders**.*

John states it plainly. Not waiting, not praying — afraid.

The men who arrested Him three days ago know all their faces.

## The arrival

*And **Jesus came and stood there in the middle of them**.*

John does not explain the doors. He mentions them, mentions that they were shut, and then puts
Him inside.

He does it twice — verse 19, and again in verse 26 when Thomas is there: *the doors being shut,
Jesus came and stood in the midst*.

## The greeting

*"**Peace be with you**."*

**Shalom aleichem** — the ordinary greeting.

Said to men who had all promised not to leave Him and had all left. Nothing in the sentence
refers to it.

He says it three times in this chapter.

## The evidence

*When He had said it, **He showed them His hands and His side**.*

Hands and side. Luke's version has hands and feet — Luke was interested in the nail wounds;
John was standing at the cross and saw the spear go in.

*One of the soldiers pierced His side with a spear, and immediately blood and water came out.*

The risen body carries the marks. They are the identification.

## The reaction

*And the disciples were **overjoyed to see the Lord**.*

The Greek is **echaresan** — they rejoiced.

Luke's account of the same evening has them frightened first, and then *they still could not
believe it, for sheer joy*. See the note on Luke 24:36–43.

## What He had promised about this

*"You now have sorrow; but **I will see you again and your heart will rejoice**, and your joy no
one will take from you."*

Said in the upper room on the Thursday.

## What comes next

*"As the Father has sent Me, so I am sending you."* And then the breath. See the note on
John 20:21–23.

## Who was missing

*Now Thomas, called the Twin, one of the twelve, **was not with them when Jesus came**.*

Eight days later He comes again, doors shut again, and says *reach your finger here… do not be
unbelieving, but believing*.

And Thomas: *my Lord and my God!*

## What John says the chapter is for

*And truly Jesus did many other signs in the presence of His disciples, which are not written
in this book; but **these are written that you may believe** that Jesus is the Christ, the Son
of God, and that believing you may have life in His name.* See the note on
John 20:30–31.

## The state of the room

Frightened men, a locked door, and no expectation.

Nothing they did produced the visit.

> **What John mentions and does not explain.** The doors were shut. He says it here and again
> eight days later, both times in the same clause as the arrival.
