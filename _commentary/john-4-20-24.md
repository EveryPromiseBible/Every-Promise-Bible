She changes the subject to theology and He follows her there.

## The move

*"Our fathers worshiped **on this mountain**, and you people say that **Jerusalem** is the
place."*

He has just told her about her five husbands. This is the next thing she says.

It reads as a diversion, and it is the oldest live argument between their two peoples.

## The mountain

Gerizim, visible from Jacob's well where they are standing. The Samaritans had built a temple
on it, and a Jewish king had destroyed it about a hundred and fifty years earlier.

They had scriptural grounds: Deuteronomy names Gerizim as the mountain of blessing.

## The answer to the question

*"**Believe Me, woman, the hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this
mountain nor in Jerusalem**."*

Neither. He does not settle the argument; he retires it.

## The correction

*"**You worship what you do not know; we worship what we do know, for salvation comes from
the Jews**."*

He does not pretend the two positions are equivalent. The Samaritans held only the five books
of Moses and had cut themselves off from the prophets.

*Salvation is of the Jews* — said by a Jew to a Samaritan, and it is a statement about where
the Messiah comes from, not about who gets in.

## The hour

*"But **the hour is coming, and it is here right now**."*

John's double formula, and he uses it twice in this Gospel. The other is about the dead
hearing His voice.

Coming, and already here. Something is being inaugurated in the conversation.

## The worshippers

*"**True worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and in truth**."*

The Greek is **en pneumati kai aletheia** — one preposition governing both nouns, which ties
them together as one thing rather than two.

Not sincerity plus correctness. Something single.

## The astonishing clause

*"**Those are the worshipers the Father is looking for**."*

The Greek is **zetei** — seeks, searches for.

The Father is described as looking for worshippers. Nowhere else in Scripture is it put that
way round.

## The ground

*"**God is Spirit**, and the ones who worship Him **must worship in spirit and in truth**."*

Three statements in the Bible take this form: God is spirit, God is light, God is love. The
other two are John's as well.

The **must** follows from the nature. Worship has to match what He is.

## Why the location question dissolves

If God is spirit, a mountain is not the issue. Solomon knew it at the dedication of the
temple: *behold, heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain You. How much less this
temple which I have built!*

And Stephen, on trial for allegedly speaking against the temple, quotes Isaiah at them: *the
Most High does not dwell in temples made with hands*. See the note on Acts 6:8–11.

## Who is being told

A Samaritan woman at a well at noon, with five husbands behind her.

The fullest statement about the nature of worship in the New Testament is made to her, in a
one-to-one conversation, in the wrong country.

## What she says next

*"I know that Messiah is coming."* And the answer: *I am He, the very one speaking to you
now*. See the note on John 4:25–26.

> **The clause that reverses the direction.** *Those are the worshippers the Father is looking
> for.* The seeking, in that sentence, is His.
