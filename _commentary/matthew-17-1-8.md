Six days after telling them He is going to die, He takes three of them up a hill.

## The count

*Six days later.*

Matthew and Mark both date it. Luke says *about eight days*.

Six days after what: Peter's confession, the first announcement of the cross, and Peter
being told *get behind Me, Satan*.

The men who go up the mountain include the one who had just been rebuked.

## The three

*Peter, James, and John the brother of James.*

The same three at Jairus's house and in Gethsemane. Matthew names them the same way each
time.

## The word

*And there **He was transformed in front of them**.*

The Greek is **metamorphoo** — the root of *metamorphosis*. Changed in form.

Paul uses the same verb of the reader: *be **transformed** by the renewing of your
mind*, and *we are being **transformed** into the same image from glory to glory*.

## What they saw

*His face **shone like the sun**, and His clothes turned **as white as the light**.*

Moses' face shone after forty days on Sinai, and he had to veil it — but that was
reflected. Mark says of the clothes *such as no launderer on earth can whiten them.*

## Who arrives

*Then **Moses and Elijah** appeared to them, **talking with Him**.*

The lawgiver and the prophet. One died and God buried him; the other never died at all.
See the note on 2 Kings 2:11–12.

Luke says what they were discussing: *they spoke of **His decease** which He was about to
accomplish at Jerusalem* — the Greek is **exodos**.

## Peter's offer

*"Lord, **it is good for us to be here**. If You want, **I will put up three shelters** —
one for You, one for Moses, and one for Elijah."*

Three, equally sized. Mark adds the note: *he did not know what to say, for they were
greatly afraid.*

## The interruption

*While he was still speaking, **a bright cloud covered them**.*

He is cut off mid-sentence.

The cloud is the **shekinah** — the one that filled the tabernacle and the temple, and
that led Israel through the wilderness.

## What the voice says

*"**This is My Son, whom I love. My delight is in Him. Listen to Him.**"*

The first two sentences are word for word what was said at the Jordan. See the note on
Matthew 3:16–17.

The third is new, and it is the only command in the passage: **listen to Him**.

It answers the offer of three shelters.

## The reaction

*When the disciples heard it, they **fell facedown, terrified**.*

Not at the shining face. At the voice.

## The touch

*But **Jesus came over and touched them**. "**Get up**," He said. "**Do not be afraid**."*

He crosses to them. The Greek is *having come and having touched*.

## The last line

*And when they lifted their eyes they **saw no one but Jesus Himself**.*

Moses gone, Elijah gone, the cloud gone. The Greek is *Jesus only*.

The command was to listen to Him, and when they look up He is the only one there.

> **What was said on the way down.** *Tell the vision to no one until the Son of Man is
> risen from the dead.* Peter kept it for decades, and then wrote it down: *we were
> eyewitnesses of His majesty… we heard this voice which came from heaven when we were
> with Him on the holy mountain.*
