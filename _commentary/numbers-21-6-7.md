They ask for the snakes to be removed, and they are given something else instead.

## What came

*So the LORD **sent fiery serpents** among them.*

The Hebrew is **nechashim seraphim** — burning serpents. **Saraph** means to burn, and it is
the same word behind the **seraphim** of Isaiah 6.

Burning, probably from the effect of the bite.

## What they say

*"**We have sinned**, for we spoke against the LORD and against you."*

A complete confession, and it names both parties they had spoken against, in the order Numbers
recorded the offence. See the note on Numbers 21:5.

This is one of the few places in the wilderness accounts where the people say it plainly and
without excuse.

## What they ask for

*"**Pray to the LORD, that He would take the serpents away from us.**"*

A reasonable request, and the obvious one. Remove the problem.

## What Moses does

*So **Moses prayed for the people**.*

Four words. He does not lecture them first, does not point out that he had told them so, and
does not negotiate.

They had spoken against him personally — *against the LORD and against you* — and he prays.

## What he is not given

The removal of the serpents.

Read the next verse. *Make a fiery serpent, and set it on a pole; and it shall be that everyone
who is bitten, when he looks at it, shall live.* See the note on Numbers 21:8–9.

The snakes stay. What is provided is a way to live through being bitten.

## Why that matters

The prayer was answered, and it was answered better than it was asked.

Removing the serpents would have helped the people alive at that moment. What was given
instead was a remedy that worked for anybody bitten from then on, and it worked by looking.

## The one He chose

*And **as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness**, even so must the Son of Man be
lifted up, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have eternal life.* See the note
on John 3:14–18.

Of every image available in the wilderness — the manna, the rock, the water, the cloud — this
is the one Jesus took for Himself, in a night conversation with a Pharisee.

*For He made Him who knew no sin **to be sin for us**, that we might become the righteousness
of God in Him.*

The thing that was killing them, lifted up, and the cure is a look.

> **What they asked for and what they got.** They asked Him to take the snakes away. He left
> the snakes and gave them something to look at.
