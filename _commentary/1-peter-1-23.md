Peter explains a second birth by naming what it grew from.

## The verb

*You have been **born a second time**.*

The Greek is **anagegennemenoi** — a perfect passive participle. Having been begotten again,
and standing in that condition.

Passive. Nobody in the New Testament is described as having done this to himself.

Peter uses the word twice, and both are in this chapter: *He has begotten us again to a
living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead*.

## The two seeds

*Not out of a **seed that rots**, but out of one that **never will**.*

The Greek is **phthartes** and **aphthartou** — corruptible and incorruptible.

The same pair Paul uses of the resurrection body: *it is sown in corruption, it is raised in
incorruption*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:53–55.

**Spora** — seed, sowing.

## What the seed is

*The **word of God, alive, and still standing** when everything else has gone.*

The Greek is **zontos kai menontos** — living and remaining.

Two participles, and neither is about power. One says it is alive; the other says it stays.

## What comes next

Peter quotes Isaiah:

*"All flesh is as grass, and all the glory of man as the flower of the grass. **The grass
withers, and its flower falls away, but the word of the LORD endures forever**."*

Isaiah 40, and the line before it in Isaiah is *the grass withers, the flower fades, because
the breath of the LORD blows upon it*.

## The connection Peter makes

*Now **this is the word which by the gospel was preached to you**.*

The everlasting word of Isaiah 40 and the sermon they heard are identified as the same thing.

The seed that produced the second birth is what somebody said to them.

## What has just been said

*Since you have purified your souls in obeying the truth… **love one another fervently with a
pure heart**.*

The instruction comes first and this verse is its reason. *Since* — because you were born
from that kind of seed.

## The verses before that

*You were **not redeemed with corruptible things**, like silver or gold, from your aimless
conduct received by tradition from your fathers, but with the precious blood of Christ.* See
the note on 1 Peter 1:14–16.

The same adjective — **phthartois**, corruptible — of silver and gold.

Money rots, in Peter's vocabulary. Twice in ten verses he sets the perishable against the
imperishable, and puts precious metal on the perishable side.

## The other seed passages

*The seed is the word of God.* Luke's explanation of the sower. See the note on
Matthew 13:18–23.

*A man scatters seed on the ground… and he has no idea how.* See the note on Mark 4:26–29.

And James: *of His own will He brought us forth by the word of truth, that we might be a kind
of firstfruits of His creatures.*

Four writers, one image, and in every case the word is the seed rather than the soil or the
farmer.

## What the chapter opens with

*Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who according to His abundant mercy
has begotten us again to a living hope… to **an inheritance incorruptible and undefiled and
that does not fade away, reserved in heaven for you***.

Incorruptible again — the third use in one chapter. The seed, the inheritance, and by
implication the person.

> **What the birth came out of.** Not a decision described as a seed, and not an experience.
> *The word of God* — living, and still standing when the grass has gone.
