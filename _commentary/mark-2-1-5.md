Four men make a hole in somebody's house, and the first thing said to the man they
lower through it is not about his legs.

## Where

*He came back to **Capernaum**, and word got around that **He was home**.*

Mark says *at home* — probably Peter's house, where He had healed Peter's
mother-in-law and where the whole town had gathered at the door after sunset.

## The crowd

*So many people packed in that **there was no room left, not even out by the doorway**.*

The door is blocked from the inside and the outside.

## What He was doing

*And **He was speaking the word to them**.*

Mark puts it in there so the reader knows what gets interrupted.

## The four

*Four men came carrying a paralyzed man **on a mat**.*

Mark alone gives the number. One at each corner.

## The obstacle

*When they **could not get anywhere near Jesus** because of the crush of people.*

They try the ordinary route first and it fails.

## What they did

*They went up on top of the house, **broke open the roof right above Him, dug their way
through**, and lowered the mat down.*

A Galilean roof was beams laid across, packed with branches, reeds and clay, rolled
flat. An outside stair went up to it — it was usable space.

**Dug** is the right word. They are removing packed earth, and it is falling into the
room the whole time, onto the crowd and onto Jesus.

## What it cost

Somebody's roof. Nobody in the account asks permission, and Mark records no complaint
from the householder.

## What Jesus saw

*Jesus **saw their faith**.*

**Their** — plural. The four, and the man on the mat.

Faith is described as visible. What He saw was a hole in a ceiling and a stretcher coming
down on ropes.

## What He said

*"**Son, your sins are forgiven**."*

Nobody asked for that. Five men went to enormous trouble about a pair of legs, and the
first sentence addresses something else.

**Son** — the Greek is **teknon**, child. An affectionate word.

## What Mark leaves out

Any explanation. The paralysis is not said to be caused by sin, and the man is not
questioned or told to confess.

## The order

Forgiveness first, walking second. The healing comes seven verses later, and Jesus says
outright that it happens *so that you will know the Son of Man has authority here on
earth to forgive sins.*

The legs are the evidence for the sentence about the sins. See the note on Mark 2:6–12.

## What the scribes make of it

They are in the room, sitting, and they conclude it is blasphemy — *nobody can forgive
sins except God alone.*

Their logic is sound. It is the premise about who is speaking that they have wrong.

> **What the four did.** Carried him, failed at the door, climbed, dug through a roof,
> and lowered him into a room full of people. Mark calls the whole sequence *their
> faith*.
