The first Christian sermon opens by dealing with a heckle.

## Who stands up

*Then **Peter stood up with the eleven**.*

Eleven, so Matthias has been added. Luke has just recorded the casting of lots.

**With the eleven** — Peter speaks, and the other eleven stand with him. It is presented
as the group's statement.

## Who this is

The man who denied three times in a courtyard fifty days earlier, in the same city,
addressing the crowd that includes people who were there.

He had been told it would happen and told what to do afterward: *when you have turned
back, strengthen your brothers.* See the note on Luke 22:31–34.

## The voice

*And he **raised his voice**.*

Luke notes it. Three thousand respond by the end, so the crowd is large.

## The address

*"Men of Judea, and **all of you staying here in Jerusalem**."*

The visitors from fifteen countries listed a few verses earlier. See the note on
Acts 2:9–11.

## The offer

*"**Let me tell you what this is**. Listen carefully to my words."*

The Greek is *let this be known to you*.

He begins by taking responsibility for explaining an event nobody in the street can
account for.

## The heckle

Some in the crowd had said *"They are full of new wine."*

## The answer

*"**These men are not drunk, as you suppose — it is only nine in the morning**."*

The Greek is *the third hour of the day*.

Devout Jews fasted until the morning sacrifice on a feast day. Nobody had eaten, let
alone been drinking.

It is the plainest possible reply, and it is the entire rebuttal. He does not argue
about the nature of the experience.

## The pivot

*"**No, this is what was spoken of through the prophet Joel**."*

He does not explain the phenomenon. He identifies it.

## What he quotes

Joel 2, at length: *"I will pour out of My Spirit on all flesh; your sons and your
daughters shall prophesy… **and whoever calls on the name of the LORD shall be saved**."*

He quotes the whole passage, including the wonders in heaven and the blood and fire and
smoke, and ends on the last line.

## Where the sermon goes

Three Old Testament passages, in order: Joel, then Psalm 16 (*You will not leave my soul
in Hades*), then Psalm 110 (*The LORD said to my Lord, "Sit at My right hand"*). See the
note on Psalms 110:1.

Almost the whole sermon is quotation.

## The charge

*"**Him, being delivered by the determined purpose and foreknowledge of God, you have
taken by lawless hands, have crucified, and put to death**; whom God raised up."*

Both halves in one sentence — God's purpose and their hands, neither cancelling the
other.

## The response

*Now when they heard this, **they were cut to the heart**, and said to Peter and the rest
of the apostles, "**Men and brethren, what shall we do?**"*

And that day about three thousand were added.

> **How the first sermon begins.** Not with a text and not with an appeal. With a man
> pointing at a clock.
