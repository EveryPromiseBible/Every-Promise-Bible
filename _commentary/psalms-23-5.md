Four lines, and the shepherd has become a host.

## Where the psalm has got to

Verses 1 to 4 are pasture, water, paths and a valley — shepherd's country.

Here the imagery changes without warning. There is a table, oil, and a cup. This is
a house, and the reader is a guest.

## The table

*You **set a table before me** in the presence of my enemies.*

The Hebrew is to arrange, to lay out in order. It is unhurried.

## "In the presence of my enemies"

They are still there. Not defeated, not removed, not converted — present, and
watching.

The psalm does not describe a rescue from the room. It describes a meal served in
it.

## The oil

*You **anoint my head with oil**.*

Two things this could be, and both are in the Old Testament.

A host poured oil on a guest's head at a feast — ordinary hospitality, and a mark of
honour. Jesus notes its absence at Simon the Pharisee's table: *you did not anoint My
head with oil.*

And a shepherd poured oil on a sheep's head against flies and infection.

The psalm has been about a shepherd for four verses and is about a host in this one.
Both readings sit inside the same line.

## The cup

*My cup **overflows**.*

The Hebrew is *my cup is saturation* — running over the top.

A host who fills a cup to the brim has been generous. A host who keeps pouring after
it is full is making a point.

## What the enemies get

Nothing. They are not fed, not addressed, and not asked to leave.

They are made to watch, and the watching is part of what the verse is describing.

## Where the psalm goes next

*Surely **goodness and mercy will pursue me** all the days of my life, and I will
dwell in the house of the LORD forever.*

The Hebrew for *pursue* is a hunter's word — the verb used of Pharaoh's chariots
chasing Israel. See the note on Psalms 23:6.

The table is in a house, and the last line says the guest is moving in.

## The shape of the whole psalm

Pasture, water, valley, table, house. It moves outdoors to indoors, and from *He* to
*You* at the valley and never goes back.

> **What the enemies were in David's life.** Saul with three thousand men, the
> Philistines, and eventually his own son. The man who wrote about a table set in
> front of enemies had eaten a good many meals looking over his shoulder.
