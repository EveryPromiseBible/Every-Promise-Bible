Three verses immediately after the warning about a full house.

## What comes before

*When the LORD your God brings you into the land… with **great and good cities which you did not
build**, houses full of all good things which you did not fill, wells you did not dig,
vineyards and olive trees you did not plant — when you have eaten and are full, **then beware,
lest you forget the LORD**.* See the note on Deuteronomy 6:10–12.

Then this.

## The three

*Hold the LORD your God **in awe**. **Serve** Him. **Take your oaths in His name** and no
other.*

**Yare** — fear, revere. **Avad** — serve, work for. And swearing by the name.

An oath in the ancient world invoked a god as witness and guarantor. Swearing by a name was a
declaration of which court you expected to answer to.

## What Jesus does with the line

*"**Away with you, Satan! For it is written, 'You shall worship the LORD your God, and Him only
you shall serve.'**"* See the note on Matthew 4:8–11.

The third temptation, answered from this verse.

He quotes *serve*, and the Greek Old Testament has *worship* where the Hebrew has *fear* — which
is why the citation reads as it does.

All three of His answers in the wilderness come from Deuteronomy 6 and 8.

## The prohibition

*Do not go running after other gods, **the gods of the peoples all around you**.*

Not distant religions. The neighbours'.

Joshua names the same two threats at Shechem: the gods your fathers served beyond the River, and
the gods of the Amorites in whose land you now live. See the note on
Joshua 24:14–15.

Behind them or around them, and those are the only two options he offers.

## The reason

*For the LORD your God, **right there in your midst**, is a God who **will not share your
heart**.*

The Hebrew is **el qanna** — a jealous God.

**Qanna** is used only of God, five times, and always in this connection.

The English word carries the wrong flavour. This is not envy of somebody else's property. It is
the jealousy of a spouse, and the whole prophetic tradition takes it that way — Hosea marries
an unfaithful woman to make the point.

Indifference would be the alternative, and it would be worse.

## The clause in the middle

*Right there in your midst.*

The Hebrew is **beqirbekha** — in your inward parts, among you.

The jealousy is stated as a consequence of nearness. He is not a distant deity with territorial
claims; He is described as living among them.

## The warning

*Do not stir up His anger against you and be **swept off the face of the land**.*

The land they are about to be given, in the same paragraph as the giving.

## What follows immediately

*You shall not tempt the LORD your God as you tempted Him in **Massah**.*

Massah — *testing*. Where they demanded water and asked *is the LORD among us or not?*

That is the verse Jesus quotes at the second temptation, on the pinnacle. See the note on
Matthew 4:5–7.

Two of the three answers come out of this one chapter.

## What the chapter opened with

*Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one. **And you shall love the LORD your God with
all your heart**.* See the note on Deuteronomy 6:4–5.

The command to love, and then a paragraph about a God who will not share.

They are the same subject.

> **Why the jealousy is mentioned.** Not distance. The reason given in the sentence is that He
> is *in your midst* — the word is the one for a marriage, and the alternative to it is
> indifference.
