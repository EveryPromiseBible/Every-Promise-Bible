The one place He could not work, and Mark says why in a single word.

## Where He goes

*Jesus left that place and came to **His hometown**.*

Nazareth. He has just raised Jairus' daughter and healed the woman in the crowd on the way
there.

*And His disciples came with Him.*

## The teaching

*On the Sabbath He began to teach in the synagogue, and the people who heard Him **could
hardly take it in**.*

The Greek is **exeplessonto** — struck out of themselves, astonished.

The astonishment is real and it does not help.

## The questions

*"**Where did this man get all this?**"*

*"What kind of **wisdom** is this that has been given to Him, and these **mighty works done
by His hands**?"*

They concede both. The wisdom is real and the works are real. What they cannot place is the
source.

## The list

*"Is this not **the carpenter, the son of Mary**, the brother of **James and Joses and Judas
and Simon**? And are **His sisters** not living right here among us?"*

The Greek is **tekton** — a builder or craftsman, working in wood or stone. Only Mark applies
it to Jesus rather than to Joseph.

**Son of Mary** — a man was named for his father. Naming him by his mother, in that culture,
is not a compliment.

Four brothers named. Two of them wrote letters in the New Testament, and none of them
believed Him at this point: *for even His brothers did not believe in Him*.

## The verdict

*And they **took offense at Him**.*

The Greek is **eskandalizonto** — they were scandalised, they stumbled over Him.

They had the evidence and the familiarity, and the familiarity won.

## His comment

*"A prophet is **respected everywhere except in his own hometown, among his own relatives,
and in his own house**."*

Three circles, tightening. Town, relatives, house.

## The sentence

*And **He was not able to do many mighty works there**.*

The Greek is **ouk edunato** — He could not.

Matthew softens it to *He did not do many mighty works there*. Mark writes the harder
version.

## The exception

*He laid His hands on **a few who were sick and healed them**, and that was all.*

Even the sentence about what He could not do contains healings.

## What surprised Him

*Their **refusal to believe astonished Him**.*

The Greek is **ethaumazen** — He marvelled.

Two things in the Gospels are said to have astonished Jesus. This, and the faith of a Roman
centurion: *I have not found such great faith, not even in Israel*.

Unbelief at home, and belief in a Gentile officer.

## What He does about it

*And **He went out to teach in the villages all around**.*

No argument, no second attempt. He leaves and works elsewhere, and the next thing He does is
send the twelve out in pairs.

## What Luke records of the same visit

The sermon on Isaiah 61 — *the Spirit of the LORD is upon Me* — and *today this Scripture is
fulfilled in your hearing*.

And then the crowd tries to throw Him off a cliff.

## What changed later

His brother James, who did not believe here, is running the Jerusalem church by Acts 15 and
opens his letter *James, a bondservant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ*.

Paul lists an appearance to him: *after that He was seen by James*.

> **The word Mark uses.** *He could not.* Matthew writes *did not*, and Mark leaves the harder
> sentence standing — followed immediately by a note that He healed a few of them anyway.
