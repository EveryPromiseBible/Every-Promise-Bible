A father checks the time and finds it matches.

## What happened

A royal official from Capernaum came to Cana — about twenty miles — because his son was dying.

*"Sir, come down before my child dies!"*

*Jesus said to him, "**Go your way; your son lives**." So the man believed the word that Jesus spoke
to him, and he went his way.* See the note on John 4:46–50.

He had asked Him to come. He is sent home alone.

## The journey

*And as **he was now going down**, his servants met him.*

Twenty miles, downhill from Cana to Capernaum. He does not run.

He had come up urgently and goes back at walking pace, which is its own comment.

## The news

*And told him, saying, "**Your son lives!**"*

The Greek is **ho pais sou ze** — the same three words Jesus used.

The servants report it in the exact phrase, without knowing they are quoting.

## The question

*Then he **inquired of them the hour** when he got better.*

The Greek is **eputheto** — he asked, he enquired.

He does not celebrate first. He wants the time.

## The answer

*And they said to him, "**Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever left him**."*

The seventh hour — about one in the afternoon.

## The arithmetic

*So the father knew that it was **at the same hour** in which Jesus said to him, "Your son lives."*

The Greek is **en ekeine te hora** — in that hour.

## Why he asked

Because a boy can recover on his own.

The father is doing what anyone honest would do: checking whether the recovery had anything to do
with the word.

The Gospel records the verification.

## The distance

Twenty miles, and no contact.

The healings in the Gospels are almost all by touch or by presence. This one and the centurion's
servant are the exceptions, and both are at a distance.

## The household

*And **he himself believed, and his whole household**.*

The Greek is **episteusen autos kai he oikia autou hole**.

## The two believings

Verse 50: *the man believed the word that Jesus spoke to him*.

Verse 53: *he himself believed*.

The first is trusting a sentence enough to go home. The second is something larger, and John
distinguishes them.

## The count

*This again is **the second sign** Jesus did when He had come out of Judea into Galilee.*

The Greek is **deuteron semeion**.

John numbers the first two and then stops counting. The first was water into wine at Cana. See
the note on John 2:6–8.

Both in Cana, and the second one is performed on somebody twenty miles away.

## What John says at the end of the Gospel

*And truly Jesus did many other signs in the presence of His disciples, which are not written in
this book; **but these are written that you may believe**.*

Seven signs are selected, and this is one of them.

## What He had said to the man

*"**Unless you people see signs and wonders, you will by no means believe**."*

Said before the healing, to a father whose son was dying. And the man did not argue: *Sir, come
down before my child dies*.

> **What the father does first.** Asks the servants what time the fever broke. The Gospel records
> him checking, and records the answer.
