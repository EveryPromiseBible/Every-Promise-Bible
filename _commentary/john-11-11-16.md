He tells them plainly, and then has to tell them plainly again.

## The word

*"**Our friend Lazarus** has fallen asleep."*

**Our** friend. He includes them.

The Greek is **kekoimetai** — has fallen asleep. It is the standard New Testament word for
a believer's death, and the root of *cemetery* — a sleeping-place.

## The purpose

*"**I am going there to wake him up**."*

## The misunderstanding

*"Lord, **if he is only sleeping, he will get better**."*

The Greek is *he will be saved* — in the medical sense, he will recover.

It is a reasonable inference. Sleep in a sick man is a good sign.

## What John says about it

*Jesus had been speaking of his death, but **they thought He meant the rest of ordinary
sleep**.*

John explains it for the reader, which he does throughout the Gospel — the temple of His
body, the water the woman asked for, the food the disciples did not know about. See the
note on John 4:31–34.

## The plain version

*So then He told them **plainly**, "**Lazarus is dead**."*

The Greek is **parresia** — openly, without figure.

## The strangest clause

*"And **for your sake I am glad I was not there**, because **now you will believe**."*

He says He is glad about the timing of a death.

The reason given is about the eleven men in front of Him.

He had said it when the message first came: *this sickness is not unto death, but for the
glory of God, that the Son of God may be glorified through it.*

And then: *so when He heard that he was sick, **He stayed two more days** in the place where
He was.*

The delay was deliberate and John says so.

## The instruction

*"**But let us go to him**."*

## Why that is dangerous

The verses before this: they had just left Judea because *the Jews sought again to stone
Him*. The disciples had said *Rabbi, lately the Jews sought to stone You, and are You going
there again?*

Bethany is two miles from Jerusalem.

## Thomas

*Then Thomas, the one called the Twin, said to the other disciples, "**Let us go as well, so
that we may die with Him**."*

It is the first thing Thomas says in the Gospel, and it is usually read as pessimism.

It is also a decision to go.

He is remembered for the sentence he says at the end — *unless I see in His hands the print
of the nails* — and for the one after that: *my Lord and my God*.

## What happens at Bethany

Four days in the tomb. Martha meets Him on the road; Mary stays in the house until she is
called.

*"I am the resurrection and the life."*

And the shortest verse in the Bible: *Jesus wept*.

He knew what He was about to do and He wept anyway.

## The order at the tomb

*"Take away the stone."* Then the prayer. Then *"Lazarus, come forth!"*

And: *"**Loose him, and let him go**."*

The raising is His and the unwrapping is theirs.

> **What He is glad about.** Not the death. The timing — and the reason He gives is that
> the men standing in front of Him are about to believe something they could not have
> otherwise.
