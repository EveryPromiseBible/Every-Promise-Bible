A child dies at noon and his mother says one sentence to everyone she meets.

## The field

**4:18** *The boy grew. One day he **went out to his father among the harvesters**.*

The son she had been promised and had not asked for. See the note on
2 Kings 4:8–10.

## The collapse

**4:19** *Suddenly he cried, "**My head! My head!**"*

Two words, repeated. Sunstroke is the usual reading; the text does not say.

## The father

**4:19** again: *"**Carry him to his mother**," the father told a servant.*

He does not go himself. The harvest is on and he sends the boy back with a servant.

Nothing in the account suggests he understands how serious it is.

## The lap

**4:20** *The boy **sat on her lap until noon — and then he died**.*

The Hebrew gives the hour.

## What she did with the body

**4:21** *She **carried him up and laid him on the bed of the man of God**, **shut the door on him**,
and **went out**.*

Three actions, and none of them is what a mother would ordinarily do.

She does not call the household, does not wail, and does not send for the burial party.

She puts him on the prophet's bed — in the room she built, on the furniture she chose —
and closes the door.

## What she says to her husband

**4:22** *"**Send me one of the servants and a donkey**, so I can hurry to the man of God and come
back."*

She does not tell him the boy is dead.

## His objection

**4:23** *"**Why go to him today?** It's **not the New Moon or the Sabbath**."*

Which tells you the local custom: you visited a prophet on holy days.

He is puzzled by the schedule, not alarmed.

## Her answer

**4:23** again: *"**It's all right**," she said.*

The Hebrew is **shalom** — peace, it is well.

She says it three times in the chapter. Here to her husband, then to Gehazi sent to meet
her on the road — **4:26** *is it well with you? Is it well with your husband? Is it well with the
child?* and she answers **shalom** — and only when she reaches Elisha does anything else
come out.

She will not say it to anyone but him.

## The ride

**4:24** *She **saddled the donkey** and told her servant, "**Lead on. Don't slow down for me unless
I tell you**."*

The Hebrew is *do not slacken the pace for me*.

Shunem to Carmel is about fifteen miles.

## What happens at the mountain

Elisha sees her coming and sends Gehazi. She goes past him, comes to Elisha and **4:27** *caught him
by the feet*.

Gehazi tries to push her away, and Elisha says: **4:27** again: *let her alone, for **her soul is in deep
distress**, and **the LORD has hidden it from me, and has not told me**.*

He did not know. The prophet says so.

## What she says at last

**4:28** *"**Did I ask a son of my lord? Did I not say, Do not deceive me?**"*

The only complaint she makes in the chapter, and it is the sentence she had said when the
promise was given.

## What follows

Gehazi is sent ahead with the staff and nothing happens. Elisha comes himself, shuts the
door on the two of them, prays, and stretches himself on the child. See the note on
2 Kings 4:32–37.

**4:35** *Then the child sneezed seven times, and the child opened his eyes.*

## The word she keeps saying

**Shalom.**

To her husband, when he asks why she is going: **4:23** again: *it is well*. To Gehazi, when he runs out to
meet her and asks after the child: **4:26** again: *it is well*.

Her son is dead on the prophet's bed upstairs and she says *shalom* to everybody she passes.

## What she does not do

Bury him. Tell the neighbours. Send for her husband.

**4:21** again: *She went up and **laid him on the bed of the man of God, shut the door upon him**, and went
out.*

She puts the dead boy in the room she built, closes the door, and saddles a donkey.

## Where she says the true thing

**4:27–28** again: *And when she came to the man of God at the hill, **she caught him by the feet**… "Did I ask a
son of my lord? Did I not say, 'Do not deceive me'?"*

Not to her husband, and not to the servant. She holds it until she is at the feet of the only
person who can do anything about it, and then she says all of it.

**1 Peter 5:7** *Casting all your care upon Him, **for He cares for you**.*

**John 11:32** *Then Mary, when she came where Jesus was, **fell down at His feet**, saying to Him, "Lord, if
You had been here, my brother would not have died."*

## What Gehazi tries to do

**4:27** again: *And Gehazi came near to push her away.*

And Elisha: **4:27** again: *"**Let her alone, for her soul is in deep distress**, and the LORD has hidden it
from me."*

**Matthew 19:14** *"**Let the little children come to Me, and do not forbid them.**"*

> **What she says to everyone on the road.** **4:23** again: *Shalom.* A dead boy is on a bed behind a
> shut door and she tells her husband and the prophet's servant that it is well — and
> says nothing else until she has hold of Elisha's feet.
