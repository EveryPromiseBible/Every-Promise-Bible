One sentence, and it holds two tenses that most people would not put together.

## The sentence

*By **that one offering** He has **brought to completion, forever**, the people **He is
making holy**.*

## The first half

*Brought to completion, forever.*

The Greek is **teteleioken eis to dienekes** — a perfect tense, and a phrase meaning
*continuously, for all time*.

Perfect tense: done, and standing done. Nothing is pending.

**Teleioo** is the word the writer uses throughout — the same root as *it is finished*.

## The second half

*The people **He is making holy**.*

The Greek is **tous hagiazomenous** — a present passive participle. Being sanctified,
ongoing, in progress.

## The two together

Completed forever, and being made holy — in one sentence, about the same people, by the
same offering.

The writer sets them side by side and does not reconcile them. One describes standing and
one describes process, and both are attributed to the single offering.

## What it answers

The chapter's argument about the old arrangement: sacrifices *year after year* that *could
never bring the people who came to worship all the way to completion*. See the note on
Hebrews 10:1–2.

Same verb, negated. What the repetition could not do, one offering did.

## "One offering"

The chapter says it four ways in a few verses:

*By that will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ
**once for all***.

*But this Man, after He had offered **one sacrifice for sins forever**, sat down at the
right hand of God.* See the note on Hebrews 10:11–13.

**Ephapax** — once, finally, never repeated.

## The verse that follows

*But the Holy Spirit also witnesses to us; for after He had said before, "**This is the
covenant that I will make with them**…"* — and then Jeremiah, and *their sins and their
lawless deeds I will remember no more*. See the note on Hebrews 10:15–17.

## And then

*Now **where there is remission of these, there is no longer an offering for sin**.* See the
note on Hebrews 10:18.

The argument closes.

## Where the writer goes from here

*Therefore, brethren, having boldness to enter the Holiest… **let us draw near**.*

Everything after verse 18 is invitation.

## The word for the people

*The people He is making holy.*

The Greek is the same root as **hagios**, holy, saint. The New Testament's usual word for
believers is the participle of this verb.

They are called the sanctified — a name for something being done to them.

> **The two tenses.** Finished and continuing. The writer puts a perfect and a present in
> one sentence and attaches both to a single offering — one is what they are, and the
> other is what is happening.
