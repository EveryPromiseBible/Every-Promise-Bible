title: He took him from the sheepfolds

Seventy-two verses of Israel failing, and the psalm ends on a shepherd.

## Where he was taken from

*He chose David His servant, and **took him from the sheepfolds**; **from following the ewes
that had young** He brought him.*

The Hebrew is precise: **me'achar alot** — from behind the nursing ewes. Not from the flock
generally. From the pregnant and feeding animals, the ones that cannot be driven and have to be
led slowly.

*"He will feed His flock like a shepherd; He will gather the lambs with His arm… **and gently
lead those who are with young**."*

Isaiah uses the same category of animal for what God is like.

## What he was made

*To shepherd Jacob His people.*

The word is not king. The psalm describes the throne with the vocabulary of the job he already
had.

*"**I am the good shepherd.** The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep."*

*Now may the God of peace… **that great Shepherd of the sheep**.*

## The two things said about how he did it

*So he shepherded them **with an upright heart**, and guided them **with a skillful hand**.*

Integrity and competence. The psalm names both, and it names the heart first.

## What the seventy-two verses before it are

A catalogue of the nation's failures — the wilderness, the quail, the forgetting, the idols at
Shiloh, the ark captured. Verse after verse of *they did not keep the covenant of God*.

And then the last three verses are about a choice God makes anyway.

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that **while we were still sinners, Christ
died for us**.*

## Who the psalm is really pointing at

*"You are My Son, today I have begotten You."* See the note on Psalms 2:7–9.

David is the last name in Psalm 78 and not the last word. The line runs from the sheepfolds to
a stable, and **Luke** records that the first people told were shepherds in a field.

> **Where he was standing.** Behind the nursing ewes — the animals that cannot be hurried. That
> is the training the psalm says God took him out of, for the job of leading a nation.
