Two verses, and the second one changes the tone of the first completely.

## What God calls Himself here

*Your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel* — and then two working descriptions:

- **who teaches you what is best for you** — the Hebrew is the verb for profiting
  or benefiting. Teaching aimed at the pupil's advantage.
- **who leads you in the way you should go**

Both are stated as ongoing character, not as offers. This is what He is like.

## Then the sigh

*If only you had listened to My commands, your peace would have flowed like a
river, your righteousness like the waves of the sea.*

That is not a threat. It is regret — the kind of sentence a parent says looking
at a situation that did not have to happen. And it is spoken to a people already
in exile, already living with the consequences.

The images are worth noticing: a river, and sea waves. Both are pictures of
something that keeps coming. Not a reservoir that could run out — a supply with a
source behind it.

## The same sigh, in a human voice

*If only you had listened… your peace would have flowed like a river.*

*"**O Jerusalem, Jerusalem**, the one who kills the prophets and stones those who are sent to her!
**How often I wanted to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her chicks under her
wings, but you were not willing!**"*

**Matthew** 23. The identical sentence — regret rather than threat, spoken over the same city, by
somebody standing in it. And **Luke** adds that He was weeping when He said it.

## The teacher

*Who teaches you what is best for you.* The Hebrew verb is about profiting the pupil.

*"**Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart**, and you will
find rest for your souls."*

**Jesus** describes His own teaching by naming His temperament first. Isaiah says the teaching is
aimed at the learner's advantage; **Matthew** 11 says what the teacher is like in the room.

## Peace like a river

*Therefore, having been justified by faith, **we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus
Christ**.*

*"Peace I leave with you, My peace I give to you… **Let not your heart be troubled**."*

Isaiah's regret is that the river never started. **Romans** says where the source was finally
opened, and it was not on the listening side of the sentence.

> **Read on.** The very next verses do not end in the sigh. God goes straight to
> bringing them out, telling them to leave Babylon with a shout, and reminding
> them He led people through the desert without letting them go thirsty. The
> regret is real and it does not get the last word.
