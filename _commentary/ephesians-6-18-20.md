The armour passage ends, and there is no seventh piece — there is a posture.

## What comes before

The belt, the breastplate, the shoes, the shield, the helmet and the sword. See the note on
Ephesians 6:14–17.

Six items, and then this, joined to them by a participle rather than a new sentence.

Prayer is not listed as another item of equipment. It is how the whole thing is worn.

## The four *alls*

The Greek piles them up:

*Praying at **every moment**.*

*With **every kind of prayer and request**.*

*Staying wide awake to this very thing, with **all perseverance**.*

*And pleading for **all God's people**.*

Four occurrences of **pas** in one sentence. Every time, every kind, all perseverance, all the
saints.

## In the Spirit

*In the Spirit.*

The Greek is **en pneumati**.

Jude uses the same phrase: *praying in the Holy Spirit*. See the note on Jude 1:20–21.

## The watching

*Staying **wide awake** to this very thing.*

The Greek is **agrupnountes** — sleepless, keeping watch. From roots meaning *to chase sleep*.

The word for a sentry's night duty.

## Who he asks them to pray for

*And **pray for me**.*

Paul, who has just described a fully armed believer, asks a congregation to pray for him.

## What he asks for

*That **words may be given me**, that I may **open my mouth boldly**.*

Not release. Not comfort. Not safety.

The Greek is **hina moi dothe logos** — that speech may be given to me.

## The subject

*To make known **the mystery of the good news**.*

The mystery he has already defined in chapter 3: *that the Gentiles should be fellow heirs, of
the same body, and partakers of His promise in Christ through the gospel*.

## The description

*For which I am **an ambassador in chains**.*

The Greek is **presbeuo en halusei** — I serve as an ambassador in a chain.

An ambassador is the one person a state guarantees safe passage to. Paul is one, wearing a
chain, and says both in the same breath without complaint.

He uses the same word in 2 Corinthians: *we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were
pleading through us*. See the note on 2 Corinthians 5:20–21.

## The repetition

*That in it **I may speak boldly, as I ought to speak**.*

He asks for boldness twice in two verses. It is the only thing he requests.

The Greek is **parresiasomai** — to speak freely, openly. The word for a citizen's right to say
what he thinks in the assembly.

Written by a man who had nothing of the kind.

## What he had prayed for them

*That He would grant you, according to the riches of His glory, to be strengthened with might
through His Spirit in the inner man.* See the note on Ephesians 3:14–16.

The letter has one prayer going each way.

## How the letter ends

*Peace to the brethren, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
**Grace be with all those who love our Lord Jesus Christ in sincerity**.*

> **What he asks a congregation to pray for.** Not release from the chain. Words, and the nerve
> to use them — twice, in two verses.
