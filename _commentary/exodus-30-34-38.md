A recipe with four ingredients, a rule about proportions, and a prohibition on making it
for yourself.

## The four

*"Take fragrant spices — **gum resin, onycha, and galbanum** — along with **pure
frankincense**."*

- **Gum resin** — the Hebrew is **nataph**, *a drop*. It is the sap that bleeds out of a
  cut in the bark and hardens. The word for dropping is used of speech in the Song of
  Solomon and of the prophets' oracles.
- **Onycha** — **shecheleth**. Taken from a shellfish, and it gives off its scent only
  when crushed and burned.
- **Galbanum** — **chelbenah**, from **cheleb**, fat — the word used for the choicest
  portion, the part of the sacrifice that belonged to God. On its own galbanum is
  bitter and unpleasant. In the blend it fixes the other scents so they last.
- **Frankincense** — **lebonah**, from the root for *white*. The only one specified as
  **pure**, and the one that is already fragrant without being burned.

Three of the four have to be wounded, crushed or burned to give anything off.

## The proportions

*All **in equal amounts**.*

The Hebrew is *part for part shall it be*. No ingredient dominates, including the bitter
one.

## The maker

*Blend them into a fragrant incense, **the work of a skilled perfumer**.*

The Hebrew is *rokeach* — a compounder of ointments, a professional.

## The salt

*Seasoned with **salt**, pure and holy.*

Salt goes into it, as it goes into every grain offering. See the note on
Leviticus 2:11–13.

## Where it went

*Grind some of it into **fine powder** and set it **in front of the Testimony in the Tent
of Meeting, where I'll meet with you**.*

The Testimony is the ark with the tablets in it. The incense stands directly in front of
the place God said He would meet them.

The golden altar of incense stood immediately outside the veil, and the smoke went over
it into the Most Holy Place.

## "Most holy"

*Treat it as **most holy**.*

The Hebrew is *holy of holies* — the same phrase as the innermost room. Of the things
made for the tabernacle, only a few carry it.

## The prohibition

*"**Never make incense with this formula for your own use**… Anyone who makes any like it
**just to enjoy the scent** is to be **cut off from his people**."*

The Hebrew for *to enjoy the scent* is *to smell it*.

It could not be duplicated, sold, worn, or kept at home. There was one blend and one
place to burn it.

## Who burned it

The priest, morning and evening, at the golden altar. Uzziah the king tried it and came
out of the temple a leper. Two of Aaron's sons offered fire that was not from the altar
and died.

And two hundred and fifty men stood at the tent door with censers, and fire came out.
See the note on Numbers 16:16–35.

The same substance, in the wrong hand, kills.

## What it becomes

*The four creatures and the twenty-four elders… every one of them had a harp, and **a
gold bowl full of burning incense — and the incense is the prayers of God's people**.*

See the note on Revelation 5:7–8, and on Psalms 141:1–2, where David asks for exactly
this.

> **What the recipe is.** Four things that mostly stink or do nothing until they are
> crushed and set on fire, in equal parts, blended by an expert — and the result is
> what stands in front of the place God said He would meet them.
