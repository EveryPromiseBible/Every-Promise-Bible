The end of a chapter about a nation with nothing to offer, and God supplies the whole
answer Himself.

## What the chapter has said

*Your iniquities have separated you from your God… your hands are defiled with blood… we
grope for the wall like the blind… we look for justice, but there is none.*

Then, at verse 16: *He saw that there was no man, and **wondered that there was no
intercessor**; therefore **His own arm brought salvation** for Him.*

Nobody was found, so He did it Himself. That is the setting for these verses.

## The repayment

*He will **repay according to what has been done**, wrath to His foes and just return to
His enemies.*

## The fear

*From **the west**, people will fear the name of the LORD, and from **the east**, His
glory.*

The Hebrew is *from the going down of the sun* and *from the rising*. Both directions,
which is the Hebrew way of saying everywhere.

## The famous line

*When **the enemy comes rushing in like a flood**, **the Spirit of the LORD will lift up a
banner against him**.*

The Hebrew here is genuinely difficult, and translations divide over the punctuation.
Some read it as the enemy coming in like a flood; others as *the Spirit of the LORD will
lift up a standard against him* when *He shall come like a rushing stream*.

Either way the two elements are a flood and a raised banner.

The Hebrew for *banner* is **nes** — a standard, a signal pole. It is the word Moses used
after Amalek: *the LORD is my Banner* — **Jehovah Nissi**. See the note on
Exodus 17:8–13.

A standard was raised so scattered troops could see where to rally.

## The Redeemer

*"**The Redeemer will come to Zion**," says the LORD, "**to those in Jacob who turn away
from their sins**."*

The Hebrew is **goel** — the kinsman-redeemer, the relative obliged to buy back what a
family member had lost. Boaz's role in Ruth, and Job's word: *I know that my Redeemer
lives.*

## Where Paul quotes it

Romans 11, at the end of three chapters about Israel: *"**The Deliverer will come out of
Zion, and He will turn away ungodliness from Jacob**."*

Paul quotes the Greek version, which has the Redeemer doing the turning rather than the
people. See the note on Romans 11:5–6.

## What follows in Isaiah

*"As for Me," says the LORD, "**this is My covenant with them: My Spirit who is upon you,
and My words which I have put in your mouth, shall not depart** from your mouth… from
this time and forevermore."*

And then chapter 60 opens: *Arise, shine, for your light has come.* See the note on
Isaiah 60:1–3.

## The armour underneath

Verse 17, between the *no man* and this passage: *for **He put on righteousness as a
breastplate, and a helmet of salvation on His head**.*

Paul takes the same pieces and puts them on the reader in Ephesians 6. See the note on
Ephesians 6:14–17.

In Isaiah, God wears it because nobody else was found.

> **How the chapter is built.** A list of what the people cannot do, a note that no
> intercessor could be found, and then everything from that point on is God's own arm,
> God's own armour, and a Redeemer coming to Zion.
