Jesus tells the disciples that something is about to change in how they pray, and
He is precise about what.

## What changes

*On that day you will not ask Me anything.*

For three years they had asked Him directly — He was standing there. After the
cross and the ascension the arrangement alters: *whatever you ask the Father in
My name, He will give you.*

Not a downgrade. The request now goes to the Father, and it carries the Son's
name on it.

## "In My name"

Not a formula appended to the end of a prayer. A name in that world meant
authority to act on someone's behalf — the way a servant buying at market in his
master's name was doing the master's business with the master's credit.

So praying in His name is asking for what He would ask for, backed by who He is
rather than by who we are.

## The sentence that stings a little

*Until now you have not asked for anything in My name.*

They had been with Him the whole time and had never once done this. It was not
possible yet — the door He is describing opens at the resurrection.

He says it without reproach. It is a statement about timing, and the next words
are an invitation.

## What it is for

*Ask, and you will receive, **so that your joy may be full**.*

The stated purpose of answered prayer here is not efficiency or provision. It is
joy — filled up, brought to the brim.

> **Where it sits.** This is in the middle of the last conversation before
> Gethsemane, in a passage about sorrow turning to joy like a woman after
> childbirth. He is preparing men for the worst night of their lives by telling
> them how they will pray afterwards.
