The last paragraph about Moses, and it records three things nobody else in the
Bible gets.

## He was buried by God

*He buried him in the valley in Moab… but to this day no one knows where his
grave is.*

The subject of that verb is the LORD. Moses climbs a mountain alone, dies alone,
and is buried by God — no funeral party, no family plot, no marker.

The reason is not given. Jude preserves a strange fragment about a dispute over
the body, and the plainest guess is the simplest: a known grave of Moses would
have become a shrine within a generation.

## The physical detail

*Moses was a hundred and twenty years old when he died, yet his eyes were not
weak and his strength had not left him.*

The writer goes out of his way to say Moses was not failing. He did not die of
old age in the ordinary sense; he died because his part was finished and the
river was somebody else's to cross.

That detail sits in scripture alongside Psalm 90 — Moses' own psalm, with its
seventy years, eighty if we are strong. The man who wrote that line lived to 120
undimmed. The Bible keeps both on the page.

> **Thirty days.** Israel wept for Moses for a month, the same span they later
> gave Aaron. Then the book ends by saying no prophet has risen since like him,
> whom the LORD knew face to face — and the next book opens with God telling
> Joshua that Moses is dead and it is time to cross. Both the grief and the
> moving on are given their room.
