Two trees, chosen for two different qualities, and a promise about the far end of
a life.

## The palm and the cedar

*The righteous will flourish like the palm tree, they will grow tall like a cedar
of Lebanon.*

- **the palm** — grows in the desert, fruits for decades, and its trunk bends in
  a storm without snapping. It also bears its best dates late in life.
- **the cedar** — the great timber tree of the ancient world, slow, enormous, and
  used to build the temple itself.

One thrives where nothing should; the other lasts and towers. Between them they
cover fruitfulness and endurance.

## Where they are planted

*Planted in the house of the LORD, they will bloom in the courts of our God.*

The Hebrew is *transplanted* — moved from somewhere else and put here
deliberately. Neither tree grew where it happened to land.

And the location is the point of the psalm: not planted near the house, or
visiting it. In it.

## The promise about age

*Even in old age they still bear fruit; they stay fresh and green.*

The two words are *fat* and *fresh* — full of sap, still green. It is a
horticultural picture, and it is set against everything people expect of the
last stretch of a life: drying out, thinning, stopping.

## What the fruit is

*Singing out that the LORD is upright. He is my Rock, and there is no wrong in
Him.*

The psalm names its own harvest. The old man still bearing fruit is bearing
testimony — still able to say what God is like, out of a long life of finding
out.

## What is promised to the old

*They shall still bear fruit in old age; **they shall be fresh and flourishing**.*

The Hebrew is *fat and green* — the vocabulary of a tree in full sap, applied to people past
their strength.

*Therefore we do not lose heart. Even though our outward man is perishing, yet **the inward man
is being renewed day by day**.*

## Who did the planting

*Those who are **planted in the house of the LORD** shall flourish in the courts of our God.*

**Shatul** — transplanted, put there by somebody else. The same verb as the tree of Psalm 1.
See the note on Psalms 1:1–3.

Nothing in the picture grew where it landed.

## What the fruit is for

*To declare that **the LORD is upright**; He is my rock, and there is no unrighteousness in
Him.*

The fruit in old age is testimony. The psalm says what the flourishing is evidence of, and it
is not the person flourishing.

**Jesus**: *"by this My Father is glorified, that you **bear much fruit**; so you will be My
disciples."*

## The oil

*I have been **anointed with fresh oil**.*

**Balloti beshemen ra'anan** — soaked in green oil. The word for **Messiah** is the participle
of that verb.

> **The psalm's title.** *A song for the Sabbath day.* This is the rest-day
> psalm, and it opens with how good it is to give thanks in the morning and at
> night. The flourishing it describes is the fruit of a life lived at rest, not
> of a life spent striving.
