The scroll is taken, and the room starts singing something new.

## What has happened

A sealed scroll in the right hand of the One on the throne, an angel asking who is worthy, and
nobody found in heaven, on earth or under it. See the note on Revelation 5:1–2.

John weeps.

Then an elder: *behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah has prevailed*. And he looks and sees a
Lamb, standing, with its throat cut.

## The new song

*And they sang **a song nobody had ever sung before**.*

The Greek is **oden kainen** — a new song.

**Kainos** — new in kind, not merely new in time.

The phrase runs through the psalms: *sing to the LORD a new song*, six or seven times, always
after something has happened.

Nothing in the previous repertoire covers this.

## The worthiness

*You are the One who **deserves to take the scroll and break the seals** on it.*

The angel's question is answered.

## The reason

*You were killed.*

The Greek is **esphages** — slaughtered, butchered. The sacrificial word.

That is the credential. Not power, not rank, not victory in the ordinary sense.

## The purchase

*And **with Your own blood You bought us back for God**.*

The Greek is **egorasas** — bought at market. The slave-market word.

## The four

*Out of **every family line, every language, every people, every nation**.*

Four categories, and Revelation uses this formula seven times with the order shuffled each time.

**Ek pases phules** — out of every tribe.

The preposition matters. Not *every tribe*, but *out of* every tribe. Some from each.

## The status

*You have made us **kings and priests belonging to our God**.*

The Greek is **basileian kai hiereis** — a kingdom and priests.

It is the sentence from Sinai: *you shall be to Me **a kingdom of priests** and a holy nation*.

Peter uses it too: *you are a chosen generation, **a royal priesthood**, a holy nation*. See the
note on 1 Peter 2:9.

The offer made to Israel at the mountain, applied here to people out of every nation.

## The location

*And **it is on the earth that we will reign**.*

The Greek is **epi tes ges**.

The song ends on the ground rather than in the sky.

Which is what the last two chapters describe: a city coming down, and *the tabernacle of God is
with men, and He will dwell with them*.

## What happens next

The numbers.

*Ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands*, and their song is shorter and
simpler: *worthy is the Lamb who was slain to receive power and riches and wisdom and strength
and honour and glory and blessing*.

And then the outermost ring: *every creature which is in heaven and on the earth and under the
earth and such as are in the sea, and all that are in them*.

The three realms that had nobody worthy in them, in chapter 5 verse 3, are all singing by
verse 13.

## What the elders are holding

*Each having a harp, and golden bowls full of incense, **which are the prayers of the saints**.*

The prayers are in the room when the scroll is opened.

> **The credential named in the song.** *You were killed.* The room searched three realms for
> somebody worthy, and the qualification turns out to be the slaughter.
