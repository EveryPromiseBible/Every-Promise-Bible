title: "He would not lift his eyes"

Seven words of prayer, and the man who says them goes home right with God.

## Where he stands

*"The tax collector stood **a long way back**."*

**Makrothen** — at a distance. He is in the temple and not near the front of it.

*But now in **Christ Jesus** you who once were far off **have been brought near by the blood of
Christ**.*

## What he will not do

*"**He would not so much as lift his eyes toward heaven.**"*

**Luke** puts it in the imperfect: he kept not lifting them.

*And Nebuchadnezzar **lifted his eyes to heaven, and his understanding returned**.* See the note on
Daniel 4:34–35.

Two men, and the postures are opposite; what they have in common is that neither is defending
himself any more.

## What he does

*"**He struck his chest with his fist.**"*

**Etypten to stethos** — an imperfect again, and a gesture of grief. **Luke** records it once more,
at the cross: *and the whole crowd… beat their breasts and returned.*

## The seven words

*"**God, be merciful to me. I am a sinner.**"*

The Greek is not the ordinary word for mercy. **Hilastheti** — *be propitiated toward me* — a passive
imperative from the root behind **hilasterion**, the mercy seat.

He is not asking to be excused. He is asking that what stands between them be dealt with, and the
verb assumes a sacrifice.

*Whom God set forth as **a propitiation by His blood**, through faith… **that He might be just and the
justifier** of the one who has faith in **Jesus**.*

He asks for it standing in the building where blood was sprinkled once a year on a lid. **Romans** 3
says where it was actually provided.

## And the article

*"I am **the** sinner."*

The Greek is **to hamartolo** — with the definite article. Not *a sinner among others*; he prays as
though he were the only one.

*This is a faithful saying and worthy of all acceptance, that **Christ Jesus came into the world to
save sinners, of whom I am chief**.*

## The verdict

*"**That man went down to his house right with God, and the other did not.**"*

**Dedikaiomenos** — a perfect passive participle. Justified, by somebody else, and it stays done.

It is the verb **Paul** builds two letters on, and **Luke** has **Jesus** using it of a man who has
said seven words and hit himself in the chest.

> **The verb he uses.** Not *have mercy* — **hilastheti**, *be propitiated toward me*. He is standing
> in the building where that was done once a year, asking for it in the passive, and Romans 3 names
> where it finally was.
