Peter tells his readers what they will hear, and then gives the argument in the
mockers' own words.

## The prediction

*In the last days scoffers will come, scoffing and following their own evil
desires.*

He does not treat the objection as a surprise to be defended against. He treats
its arrival as evidence — the mocking is itself one of the things that was said
would happen.

## Their case

*Where is the promise of His coming? Ever since our ancestors died, everything
goes on exactly as it has since the beginning of creation.*

It is a decent argument, and it is still made. Two parts:

- **the delay** — the people who first heard the promise have died
- **the uniformity** — nothing ever changes; the world runs on as it always has

Nobody is disputing the facts. Peter's answer is that the second point is false
as history.

## How he answers it

He points at the flood. The world *did* change once, catastrophically, and the
people who assumed continuity were wrong then too.

Then the two answers that follow: God's reckoning of time is not ours, and the
delay is deliberate patience. See the note on 2 Peter 3:9.

## What he says they are doing

*They deliberately forget.* The Greek is about it escaping their notice on
purpose — a chosen blindness rather than an oversight.

That is Peter's diagnosis of the argument. Not that the scoffers lack
information. That the information is available and being stepped around.

> **The scale he sets it in.** The chapter runs from creation, through the flood,
> to the elements melting and a new heavens and new earth. The mockers' claim that
> everything continues unchanged is answered by a history in which it has already
> not, and a future in which it will not again.
