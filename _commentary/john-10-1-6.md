Two men get into the same enclosure, and the difference is the door.

## What has just happened

A blind man was healed, questioned twice by the Pharisees, and thrown out of the synagogue. See the
note on John 9:33–38.

*Jesus heard that they had cast him out, and when He had found him, He said to him, "Do you believe
in the Son of God?"*

This chapter continues without a break. The shepherds who threw a sheep out are standing there.

## The first man

*"The man who **does not come into the sheepfold through the gate, but climbs in some other way**, is
**a thief and a robber**."*

The Greek is **kleptēs kai lēstēs** — a thief and a bandit.

**Kleptēs** works by stealth. **Lēstēs** works by force. Barabbas is called a **lēstēs**.

## The second

*"The one who comes in **through the gate** is the shepherd of the sheep."*

The same enclosure, entered legitimately.

## The gatekeeper

*"**The gatekeeper opens to him**."*

A communal fold held several flocks overnight, with one watchman at the door.

## The morning problem

Several flocks are mixed in one pen. Nobody sorts them out by hand.

## How it is solved

*"And the sheep listen for his voice, and **he calls his own sheep by name and leads them out**."*

The Greek is **phōnei ta idia probata kat' onoma**.

Each shepherd calls, and his own animals separate themselves out of the mass.

## The names

Not a head count. Names.

Palestinian shepherds named their sheep by feature or habit, and travellers have reported the
practice into modern times.

## The direction

*"When he has brought out all his own, **he walks on ahead of them**, and the sheep follow him."*

The Greek is **emprosthen autōn poreuetai** — he goes before them.

Eastern shepherding leads. Driving from behind is a Western practice.

## The reason they follow

*"**Because they know his voice**."*

Not because they can see where they are going.

## The stranger

*"A stranger they will **never follow**. They will **run from him**, because **a stranger's voice means
nothing to them**."*

The Greek is **ou mē akolouthēsōsin** — the emphatic double negative.

**Pheuxontai** — they will flee.

## What the sheep are credited with

Not intelligence. Recognition.

The passage does not say the sheep evaluate the stranger's argument. It says the voice does not
register.

## The response

*Jesus **set this picture before them**, but **they did not understand** what He was telling them.*

The Greek is **paroimian** — a figure, a proverb. John's word; the Synoptics use **parabolē**.

## What He says next

*"**I am the door of the sheep**… If anyone enters by Me, he will be saved."* See the note on
John 10:7–10.

And *"the thief does not come except to steal, and to kill, and to destroy. **I have come that they
may have life, and that they may have it more abundantly**."*

## And after that

*"**I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep**."* See the note on
John 10:11–13.

The Greek is **ho poimēn ho kalos** — the beautiful, the noble shepherd.

## The hireling

*"He who is a hireling… sees the wolf coming and **leaves the sheep and flees**."*

The verb the sheep used of the stranger, now used of the hired man.

## Where it lands

*"**My sheep hear My voice, and I know them, and they follow Me**… and no one is able to snatch them
out of My Father's hand."* See the note on John 10:27–30.

> **The sorting.** Several flocks in one pen, and no one separates them by hand. Each shepherd calls,
> and his own come out of the crowd to the voice.
