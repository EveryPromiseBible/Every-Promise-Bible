Two verses, and the second one is harder to believe than the first.

## What has just happened

A man crippled from birth had stood up at Paul's word, the crowd had decided Paul and Barnabas
were Hermes and Zeus, and the priest of Zeus had brought oxen and garlands to the gates. See
the note on Acts 14:8–10.

Paul had torn his clothes and run in among them shouting *we also are men with the same nature
as you*.

## The turn

*Then **Jews came from Antioch and Iconium** and won the crowds over.*

They travelled to do it. Antioch of Pisidia is about a hundred miles away.

The Greek is **peisantes tous ochlous** — having persuaded the crowds.

The same crowd. That morning they wanted to sacrifice to him.

## The stoning

*And they **stoned Paul and dragged him outside the city**, thinking he was dead.*

Stoning was the Jewish method of execution, and it was thorough — the intention is to kill.

*Thinking he was dead* — the Greek is **nomizontes auton tethnekenai**.

Luke does not say Paul was dead, and does not say he was not. He reports what the crowd
concluded.

## Where they left him

Outside the city, which is where bodies went.

## The disciples

*But when **the disciples gathered around him**.*

There were disciples in Lystra already. Timothy was from there, and he may well be standing in
this circle.

Nothing is recorded of what they did. They stood round him.

## The sentence

*He **got up and went back into the city**.*

The Greek is **anastas eiselthen** — having risen, he entered.

Back through the same gate, past the same people.

## The next morning

*And **the next day he left with Barnabas for Derbe**.*

A day's walk, sixty miles, the day after being stoned.

## What he does after Derbe

Comes back. Through Lystra, Iconium and Antioch — the three towns that had just tried to kill
him — *strengthening the souls of the disciples, exhorting them to continue in the faith, and
saying, "**We must through many tribulations enter the kingdom of God**."*

He walks back into all three.

## What he wrote about it

*From the Jews five times I received forty stripes minus one. Three times I was beaten with
rods, **once I was stoned**, three times I was shipwrecked; a night and a day I have been in
the deep.*

One line in a list.

And to Timothy, at the end: *persecutions, afflictions, which happened to me at Antioch, at
Iconium, at Lystra — what persecutions I endured. **And out of them all the Lord delivered
me**.*

Those are the three towns, named in order, in a letter to a man from the third one.

## The other passage sometimes attached

*I know a man in Christ who fourteen years ago… was caught up to the third heaven… and heard
inaccessible words.*

Some have connected it to this day, and Paul does not say.

## What Luke does not do

Call it a miracle.

He records what happened, in the order it happened, and moves on to the next town.

> **The sentence Luke writes flat.** *He got up and went back into the city.* Not out of the
> region, not to recover — back through the gate he had been dragged out of.
