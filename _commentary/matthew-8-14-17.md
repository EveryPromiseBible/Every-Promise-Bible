An evening of healing, and then Matthew stops the narrative to tell you what he
thinks was happening.

## The quotation

*This was to fill out what the prophet Isaiah had said: "He took up our
weaknesses and carried our diseases away."*

The line is from Isaiah 53 — the Servant chapter, the one about being pierced for
our transgressions and crushed for our iniquities.

Matthew, writing under inspiration and knowing exactly which chapter he is
reaching into, applies it to **an evening of physical healings**. Not to the
forgiveness of sins, which is what the chapter is usually preached as. To fevers
and demons and bodies.

## The Hebrew he is drawing on

Isaiah 53:4 in Hebrew reads *surely he has borne our sicknesses and carried our
pains*. The words are the ordinary ones for illness and physical suffering; they
are used elsewhere in the Old Testament of disease.

So Matthew is not stretching the verse. He is quoting it at its plain sense, and
attaching it to what he had just watched.

## "Healed everyone who was sick"

The scope is worth reading slowly. Not many. Not those with sufficient faith.
*Everyone.* Matthew says the same in several other places — great crowds, and all
of them healed.

Nobody in these scenes is turned away, and no one is told they did not qualify.

> **The fever "let go of her."** Matthew's verb is the one used of releasing a
> prisoner or forgiving a debt. He touches her hand and the fever lets go — and
> the next thing she does is get up and serve. Restored, and immediately useful.
