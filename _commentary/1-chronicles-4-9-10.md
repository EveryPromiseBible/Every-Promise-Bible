Nine chapters of genealogy, and the record stops for two verses over one man.

## Where it sits

*Tucked into the long roll of names.*

1 Chronicles opens with Adam and runs through nine chapters of lists. Names, fathers,
sons, clans, with almost no story attached to any of them.

Then Jabez, and then straight back to the list. Nothing before or after connects to
him.

## What is said about him first

*Jabez was **more honored than his brothers**.*

The Hebrew is **nikbad** — from **kabed**, heavy, weighty. The same root as glory.

The verdict is given before the prayer. The chronicler states it and then explains it.

## The name

*His mother had named him Jabez — **which sounds like "pain"** — because, she said, **"I
gave birth to him in pain."**"*

The Hebrew is **Yabets**, and **otseb** is pain, sorrow, toil. The letters are
rearranged, which Hebrew naming does often.

She named him after the worst day of her life, and he carried it every time anyone
called him.

## The refusal

*But Jabez **would not let his name have the last word**.*

Whatever else was in his family, his name was a sentence pronounced over him at birth
by somebody else.

## The prayer

*He lifted his voice to the God of Israel and prayed, **bold and plain**.*

Four requests, and he does not soften any of them:

- *"Oh, that You would **bless me — really bless me!**"* — the Hebrew doubles the verb,
  *blessing You would bless*, the intensive form.
- *"**Enlarge my borders**, widen the space of my life."* — territory. He asks for more
  ground.
- *"Let **Your hand be on me**, near me, holding me."*
- *"And **keep me from harm**, so that I will be **free from pain**."*

The last word of his prayer is the word in his name.

## Where he asks the enlarging from

He does not go and take it. He asks the God of Israel, out loud, for more.

## The answer

*And God… **granted him exactly what he asked. Every word of it**.*

The Hebrew is *God brought about what he requested*. No negotiation, no partial answer,
no rebuke for asking about land and safety.

## Why it is recorded

The chronicler was writing for people who had come back from exile to a ruined city,
rereading their family lists to work out who they were.

Into that he puts a man whose name meant pain, who asked for enlargement, and got it.

## The word for honoured

The same root turns up over Abram coming out of Egypt — *very heavy in livestock, silver
and gold*. See the note on Genesis 13:1–4.

Weight, in Hebrew, is what honour is made of.

> **What the two verses do not contain.** Any condition. He is not told to qualify, and
> nothing is required of him first. He asked, and the next clause is that God gave it.
