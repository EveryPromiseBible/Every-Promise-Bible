Elihu's first claim is that the silence Job has been complaining about is not
silence.

## What Job had said

*Behold, I go forward, but He is not there; and backward, but I cannot perceive
Him.* And: *I cry out to You, but You do not answer me.*

Thirty chapters of a man saying God will not speak to him.

## Elihu's answer

*"The truth is, **God does speak**. He speaks **one way, then another**, and people
simply **miss it**."*

Not that God has spoken once and stopped. Repeatedly, in different modes — and the
failure named is on the hearing end.

The Hebrew is that man does not perceive it.

## When

*"He speaks **in a dream, in a vision of the night**, when **deep sleep settles over
everyone** and they are drowsing on their beds."*

The Hebrew for *deep sleep* is **tardemah** — the same word used of Adam when the
woman was taken from his side, and of Abram when the covenant was cut and only the
fire passed between the pieces.

Both of those are occasions when God did something to a man who could not
participate.

## What He does then

*"That is when **He opens their ears** and presses His warning home."*

The Hebrew is *He uncovers the ear* — the idiom for disclosing something privately,
the way you lift a man's hair to speak to him quietly.

The ear is described as needing opening. It is not that the speech was too faint.

## What it is for

Three purposes, all protective:

- *to **turn a person back from some reckless plan***
- *to **keep pride from swallowing him whole***
- *to **hold a soul back from the pit**, to keep a life from crossing over by the
  sword*

Nothing here is punitive. Every clause is about stopping somebody before something.

## The alternative Elihu offers

This is the first of two ways he says God speaks. The second is the sickbed:

*"Or He speaks through pain, laying a person low on his bed with an ache that never
leaves his bones."* See the note on Job 33:19–22.

And then the messenger, and the ransom found. See the note on Job 33:23–24.

## Who else was spoken to in the night

Jacob, on a stone, with a stairway and the covenant. Solomon at Gibeon, given
wisdom in a dream. Joseph, told four times in dreams what to do about a child.
Paul, told *do not be afraid, but speak* and *this night the angel of God stood by
me.*

Scripture is full of it, and Elihu says it is a standing arrangement rather than a
set of exceptions.

> **What Elihu is doing that the three friends did not.** He tells Job that God is
> already talking. The others explained Job's suffering. He says the silence is not
> what it appears to be, and then names a mediator.
