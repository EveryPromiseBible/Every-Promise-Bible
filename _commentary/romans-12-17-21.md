Five verses of instruction about people who have wronged you, and one realistic clause in
the middle.

## The rule

*Do not **pay anyone back evil for evil**.*

The Greek is **apodidontes** — repaying, giving back what is owed.

Paul treats revenge as a debt being settled. The point of the passage is who is entitled to
collect it.

## The visible part

*Give thought to **what is honorable in the sight of everyone**.*

The Greek is **pronooumenoi** — thinking ahead, taking forethought.

Not just doing right, but doing it where it can be seen. Paul says the same about the
collection: *providing honourable things, not only in the sight of the Lord but also in the
sight of men*. See the note on 2 Corinthians 8:16–17.

## The two qualifiers

*If it can be done*, and *as far as it rests with you*, **be at peace with everyone**.

The Greek is **ei dunaton, to ex humon** — if possible, the part that is from you.

Paul writes the command and then fences it twice. Peace is not always achievable, and the
other person's half is not the reader's responsibility.

The instruction is to have nothing left owing on your side.

## The prohibition

*Beloved, **never take revenge into your own hands**, but **step back and make room for the
wrath of God**.*

The Greek is **dote topon** — give place, leave room.

The picture is of stepping aside so someone else can get through.

Paul does not say the wrong will go unanswered. He says it is not yours to answer.

## The quotation

*"**Vengeance is Mine; I will repay**, says the Lord."*

Deuteronomy 32, from the Song of Moses.

Same verb as verse 17 — repay, **antapodoso**. The debt Paul told them not to settle is
one God says He will settle.

## The reversal

*So then, **if your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he is thirsty, give him something to
drink**.*

Proverbs 25, quoted almost exactly.

Not neutrality. Food and water, handed over by you, to him.

Elisha does it literally: he catches a Syrian army blind, and instead of killing them,
*he prepared a great feast for them; and after they ate and drank, he sent them away*. And
*the bands of Syria came no more into the land of Israel*.

## The coals

*In doing this you will **heap burning coals on his head**.*

The most argued-over line in the chapter.

The oldest reading takes it from an Egyptian rite of repentance, where a man carried a pan
of coals on his head to show he was sorry. Another reads it as the neighbourly act of
carrying live embers to relight a fire that had gone out.

Either way, Proverbs adds a clause Paul leaves off: *and the LORD will reward you*.

## The summary

*Do not let evil defeat you; **defeat evil with good**.*

The Greek is **nika en to agatho to kakon** — conquer the evil in the good.

Both halves use the same verb. The choice is not between fighting and not fighting; it is
about which side wins the exchange.

## What comes immediately after

Chapter 13: *let every soul be subject to the governing authorities… for he is God's
minister, an avenger to execute wrath on him who practises evil.*

Paul removes vengeance from the individual in one chapter and assigns it to the magistrate
in the next. Nothing here says wrong goes unpunished.

## What the chapter opened with

*I beseech you therefore, brethren, **by the mercies of God**, that you present your bodies
a living sacrifice.* See the note on Romans 12:1–2.

Eleven chapters of doctrine come before the first instruction, and the instruction is
introduced by the mercies.

## What the whole section is built on

*I beseech you therefore, brethren, **by the mercies of God**, that you present your bodies a living
sacrifice.*

**Romans** 12:1, and it is the hinge of the letter. Eleven chapters of mercy come first, and every
instruction after it — including this paragraph — is a response rather than a transaction.

*Oiktirmon tou theou* — the compassions of God. **Paul** does not appeal to duty.

## Who did it

*Do not repay anyone evil for evil… **overcome evil with good**.*

*Who, when He was reviled, **did not revile in return**; when He suffered, He did not threaten, but
committed Himself to Him who judges righteously.*

*Then **Jesus** said, "**Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do.**"*

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, **Christ died for
us**… For if when we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son.*

**Romans** 5, in the same letter. The instruction to treat an enemy well is not an ideal held over
the reader; **Paul** has already described it being done to them.

## Leaving room

*"**Vengeance is Mine, I will repay,**" says the Lord.*

The instruction is not that the wrong does not matter. It is that the case is being handled
elsewhere, and the reader is not the court.

*Who shall bring a charge against God's elect? **It is God who justifies.**"*

> **The clause that makes it liveable.** *If it can be done, and as far as it rests with
> you.* Paul asks for peace and then admits, in writing, that half of it is out of your
> hands.
