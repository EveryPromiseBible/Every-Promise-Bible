The last of six contrasts in the chapter, and it ends on a word that has been mistranslated into a
threat.

## The quotation that is not one

*"You have heard that it was said, '**Love your neighbour and hate your enemy**.'"*

The first half is Leviticus 19:18. The second half is in no verse of the Old Testament.

He is quoting the received teaching, not the text — an inference somebody drew from the command and
then attached to it.

## The four imperatives

*"**Love** your enemies. **Bless** the ones who curse you. **Do good** to the ones who hate you.
**Pray** for the ones who insult you and hunt you down."*

Escalating. Feeling, speech, action, and then intercession, which is the hardest because it has to
be sincere and nobody is watching.

*Then **Jesus** said, "**Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do.**"*

**Luke** 23, from the cross, and it is the fourth imperative carried out on the people carrying it
out.

*Then he knelt down and cried out with a loud voice, "**Lord, do not charge them with this sin.**"
And when he had said this, he fell asleep.*

**Acts** 7, and Stephen is looking at Saul of Tarsus while he says it.

## The reason

*"Because **He makes His sun rise on the evil and on the good**, and He sends His rain on those who
do right and on those who do not."*

The argument is not that enemies deserve it. It is that God's ordinary provision is already
indiscriminate and always has been, and anybody who has eaten this year has been on the receiving
end of it.

## Who the enemies turned out to be

*For if **when we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son**, much more,
having been reconciled, we shall be saved by His life.*

**Romans** 5, and **Paul**'s word is **echthroi** — the same noun **Jesus** uses here. The command
is not an ideal held over the hearers; it is a description of what was done to them.

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that **while we were still sinners, Christ died for
us**.*

*And you, who once were alienated and **enemies in your mind by wicked works**, yet now He has
reconciled.*

## The last word

*"So **be whole and complete, the way your Father in heaven is whole and complete**."*

The Greek is **teleioi** — brought to its end, finished, entire. It is not the Latin *perfectus*
that English inherited, and it is not a moral scorecard.

The parallel in **Luke** has *be merciful, just as your Father also is merciful* — which is what the
paragraph has been about since the first imperative.

*For by one offering **He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified**.*

**Hebrews** 10, and the verb is **teteleioken** — from the same root. The completeness **Jesus**
names as the standard is stated elsewhere as something already done to people who are still in
progress.

> **The half He quotes that is not in the Bible.** *Hate your enemy* appears in no verse of the Old
> Testament. And the command that replaces it turns out, in Romans 5, to be a description of what
> had already been done to everybody hearing it.
