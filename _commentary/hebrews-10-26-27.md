The second great warning of the letter, and it uses the same argument as the good news
eight verses earlier.

## Who is being written to

Hebrew believers under pressure to return to the temple. The whole letter is written
against that one move, and the temple was still standing when it was sent.

## The sin

*If we **choose to keep on sinning after we have received the truth and come to know
it**.*

The Greek is *if we sin **wilfully*** — **hekousios**, deliberately, of one's own choice.
It is a rare word, and it appears here and once in Peter, of shepherding willingly.

*After we have received the knowledge of the truth* — **epignosis**, full knowledge. Not
ignorance.

## The consequence

*No **sacrifice for sins is left**.*

The Greek is *there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins*.

## The identical clause, eight verses earlier

*And where those have been forgiven, **there is no offering for sin left to make**.* See
the note on Hebrews 10:18.

The same statement, in nearly the same words, and there it is the conclusion of the good
news.

The letter has argued that there is one sacrifice, offered once, and no other. That is a
comfort to a man resting on it and a terror to a man walking away from it — and the fact
is the same fact either way.

## What is described instead

*Only **a terror of waiting for judgment**, and **a fire, burning with His anger, that will
swallow up everyone who sets himself against God**.*

The Greek is *a certain fearful expectation of judgment, and fiery indignation*.

The phrase *swallow up the adversaries* comes from Isaiah 26.

## What the sin in view is

The next verse defines it, and it is not an ordinary lapse: *walked all over the Son of
God*, *treated the blood of the covenant as something common*, *insulted the Spirit of
grace*. See the note on Hebrews 10:28–29.

Three descriptions, and all three are about repudiating the sacrifice rather than falling
short of a standard.

## What comes immediately before

*Do not drop out of gathering together, the way some have gotten into the habit of
doing.* See the note on Hebrews 10:23–25.

The writer sets the warning right after a description of people quietly ceasing to turn
up. He is tracing where a road goes.

## What comes after

*But **recall the former days** in which, after you were illuminated, you endured a great
struggle with sufferings… **you had compassion on me in my chains**.*

*Therefore **do not cast away your confidence**, which has great reward.*

And then: *"**But we are not of those who draw back to perdition, but of those who
believe** to the saving of the soul."*

The writer includes himself and them in the second category, five verses after the
warning.

## The pattern in the letter

He does this every time. Chapter 6's warning is followed immediately by *but beloved, we
are convinced of better things concerning you.* See the note on Hebrews 6:9–10.

Warning, then reassurance, in that order, twice.

> **The sentence that does both jobs.** *There is no sacrifice left.* The letter says it
> twice — once as the reason nothing more is needed, and once as the reason there is
> nowhere else to go.
