John's disciples bring him a complaint, and his answer is the last thing recorded of him
in this Gospel.

## The complaint

*"Rabbi, the man who was with you on the other side of the Jordan, **the one you testified
about** — He is baptizing now, and **everyone is going to Him**."*

They do not use the name. *The man who was with you.*

They are indignant on their teacher's behalf, and the phrase they use — *the one you
testified about* — makes the grievance worse in their telling: he vouched for the man
taking his crowd.

## The premise

*"**No one can receive a single thing unless it is given to him from heaven**."*

He answers the crowd question with a statement about supply.

It applies both ways. Whatever Jesus is receiving was given; whatever John had was given
too.

## The reminder

*"You yourselves can testify that I said, '**I am not the Messiah; I was sent on ahead of
Him**.'"*

He had said it plainly to the delegation from Jerusalem: *I am not the Christ*, and *I am
not* to Elijah, and *no* to the Prophet.

*I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness.*

## The picture

*"**The one who has the bride is the bridegroom**."*

The bride belongs to the groom. Nobody in a wedding is confused about this.

## The friend

*"**The bridegroom's friend stands nearby, listening for him**, and he is **flooded with joy
the moment he hears the bridegroom's voice**."*

The **shoshbin** — the groom's friend in Jewish custom, who arranged the wedding, managed
the details, and had one job on the night: to stand at the door and wait.

When he heard the groom's voice he handed everything over and his work was finished.

His joy is stated as coming from a sound.

## The claim

*"**That is my joy, and it is complete**."*

The Greek is **pepleromai** — filled full, perfect tense.

He does not say he is coping with it.

## The sentence

*"**He must become greater and greater; I must become less and less**."*

The Greek is **auxanein** and **elattousthai** — to increase and to be diminished.

**Must** — the Greek is **dei**, it is necessary. He states it as the way things are
arranged, not as something he is bracing for.

## What happened next

He was arrested by Herod for saying the marriage to Herodias was unlawful, and beheaded on
a birthday.

From prison he sent two disciples to ask *are You the Coming One, or do we look for
another?* — which is the question of a man in the dark.

Jesus answered with a list of what was happening, and then turned to the crowd and said
*among those born of women there has not risen one greater than John the Baptist*. See the
note on Matthew 11:12–15.

## What Jesus said about his joy

*"He was the burning and shining lamp, and **you were willing for a time to rejoice in his
light**."*

## The last words in the paragraph

The verses that follow — *the One who comes from above is above everything* — read as either
John continuing or the Evangelist taking over. The Greek gives no quotation marks. See the
note on John 3:31–36.

> **What the friend is for.** To stand at the door and listen. John's answer to losing his
> crowd is that his joy was never in having one — it was in hearing a voice, and he says
> it is now full.
