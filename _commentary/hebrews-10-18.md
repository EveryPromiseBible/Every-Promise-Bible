One sentence, and it is the conclusion of ten chapters.

## The sentence

*And **where those have been forgiven**, there is **no offering for sin left to make**.*

The Greek is *now where there is remission of these, there is no longer an offering for
sin*.

## What "those" refers to

The quotation immediately before it: *"and their sins and their lawless deeds **I will
remember no more**."* See the note on Hebrews 10:15–17.

The writer quotes Jeremiah and then draws one inference from it.

## The logic

If the sins are not being remembered, there is nothing for another offering to be about.

An offering addresses something. Remove the something, and the offering has no subject.

The writer does not argue that further sacrifice is forbidden, ineffective, or
disrespectful. He says there is nothing left for it to do.

## The word

**Remission** — the Greek is **aphesis**, a sending away, a release. It is the word used
of prisoners being let go and of debts being cancelled.

It is also the word in the Greek Old Testament for the Year of Jubilee.

## What the chapter has established

- The law was a shadow, and the repetition proved it never finished the job. See the
  note on Hebrews 10:1–2.
- The sacrifices reopened the account annually rather than closing it. See the note on
  Hebrews 10:3–4.
- A body was prepared instead. See the note on Hebrews 10:5–7.
- The first arrangement was cancelled to establish the second. See the note on
  Hebrews 10:8–9.
- Every priest stands daily; this one sat down. See the note on Hebrews 10:11–13.
- The Spirit testifies: *I will remember no more.*

Then this verse, and the argument is closed.

## What follows it

The word **therefore**, and the whole tone of the letter changes:

*Therefore, brethren, having **boldness to enter the Holiest** by the blood of Jesus, by a
new and living way which He consecrated for us, through the veil… **let us draw near**.*
See the note on Hebrews 10:19–21.

Everything after this verse is invitation. Everything before it is argument.

## The three "let us"

*Let us draw near… let us hold fast the confession of our hope without wavering… and let
us consider one another.* See the note on Hebrews 10:23–25.

Three, in a row, and all three rest on this sentence.

## Why it mattered to the readers

The temple was standing. The offerings were being made daily, a few days' walk away, and
the people reading this letter were under pressure to go back to them.

The writer's answer is not that the temple is unpleasant or that they will be
disappointed. It is that there is nothing there to make an offering about.

> **The shortest verse in the argument.** Ten chapters of priesthood, covenant, oath and
> sacrifice, and it comes down to one clause: if the sins are not remembered, nothing
> remains to be offered.
