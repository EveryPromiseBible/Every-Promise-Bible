An old man adds up his situation and reaches a conclusion that is wrong in every
particular.

## What he says

*"**You are robbing me of my children. Joseph is gone, Simeon is gone, and now you
want to take Benjamin. Everything is against me.**"*

Three losses counted, and then the summary.

## Item by item

**Joseph is gone.** Joseph is alive, second in Egypt, and is the man they have just
been dealing with. He is the reason there is grain.

**Simeon is gone.** Simeon is being held by his own brother, in comfort, as surety.
He is not in danger.

**Now you want to take Benjamin.** Benjamin is the one condition on which the whole
family is about to be reunited and fed through five more years of famine.

Every fact in the list is true, and every conclusion drawn from it is false.

## The silver

*As they emptied their sacks, **there was each man's bundle of silver** in his sack.
When they and their father saw the silver, **they were afraid**.*

The money was put back by Joseph, as a gift. Grain paid for and the payment
returned.

They receive a kindness and are frightened by it, because they cannot imagine a
reason for it that is not a trap.

## "Everything is against me"

The Hebrew is *all these things are against me*.

It is one of the most quoted sentences in Genesis, and it is spoken by a man who is
mistaken about all three of the things he has just listed.

Paul's line in Romans is the mirror of it: *we know that **all things work together
for good** to those who love God.* See the note on Romans 8:28–30.

Same word about the same kind of situation, from the other side of it.

## What Jacob could see

Nothing that was actually happening. His son was ruler of the greatest empire on
earth and had wept in the next room; the silver was a present; the demand for
Benjamin was the last step before the reveal.

From inside the tent it looked like a family being dismantled.

## Reuben's offer

*"**You may put both my sons to death** if I don't bring him back."*

It is a serious offer and a terrible one. Jacob does not even answer it.

Judah's version, one chapter later, is the one that works: *I myself will be surety
for him; from my hand you shall require him.* He offers himself rather than his
children, and Jacob agrees.

## What he says last

*"His brother is dead, and **he alone is left**."*

Two sons of Rachel, and he speaks as though one is gone and one is all he has.

Both are alive, and within a chapter or two he will have them both.

> **What he says when he finds out.** *"It is enough. **Joseph my son is still
> alive.** I will go and see him before I die."* And Genesis records that *the spirit
> of Jacob their father revived.*
