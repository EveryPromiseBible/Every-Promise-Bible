They leave in a hurry, and the text stops to say the date was exact.

## The march

*The Israelites set out from **Rameses toward Succoth** — about **six hundred thousand men on foot**,
besides women and children.*

The Hebrew is **shesh-me'ot eleph ragli haggevarim** — six hundred thousand men on foot.

**Rameses** — one of the store cities they had built. **Succoth** means *booths*.

## The mixed multitude

*A **mixed multitude** went up with them, along with great flocks and herds.*

The Hebrew is **erev rav** — a great mixture.

Egyptians, and others. People who had watched ten plagues and drawn a conclusion.

They are not Israelites and they leave with Israel. Later they cause trouble — *the mixed
multitude who were among them yielded to intense craving* — and Numbers records it without
retracting this verse.

## The bread

*From the dough they had brought **they baked flat loaves of unleavened bread, for it had not
risen**.*

## The reason

*They had been **thrust out of Egypt with no time to prepare food** for themselves.*

The Hebrew is **gorshu** — driven out, expelled.

The unleavened bread is not primarily a symbol here. It is a description of what happens to dough
when you are being pushed out of the country.

The feast that commemorates it keeps the fact in the diet.

## The number

*The Israelites had lived in Egypt **four hundred and thirty years**.*

## The precision

*And at the end of those four hundred and thirty years, **on that very day**, all the LORD's
companies marched out of Egypt.*

The Hebrew is **be'etzem hayyom hazzeh** — in the bone of this day. The idiom for *on that exact
day*.

## What had been said

*"Know certainly that your descendants will be strangers in a land that is not theirs, and will
serve them, and they will afflict them **four hundred years**."*

*"But in the fourth generation they shall return here."* See the note on
Genesis 15:12–16.

Said to Abram in a deep sleep, with a smoking oven and a burning torch passing between the pieces.

## The military word

*All **the LORD's companies** marched out.*

The Hebrew is **tzivot YHWH** — the armies, the hosts of the LORD.

The same noun in the title **YHWH Tzeva'ot**, the LORD of hosts, and the word Haggai uses fourteen
times in two chapters. See the note on Haggai 1:2.

A slave population walks out of a superpower and the narrator calls them armies.

## The vigil

*It was **a night of keeping watch, the LORD's own vigil** to bring them out.*

The Hebrew is **leil shimmurim** — a night of watchings.

## Whose watch it was

The LORD's.

The people had been indoors behind blood-marked doorposts, told *none of you shall go out of the
door of his house until morning*.

The watching was being done on the other side of the door.

## The reciprocal

*And so **this same night belongs to the LORD, kept as a watch by all Israel through all their
generations**.*

He kept watch that night; they keep watch on that night, every year, since.

The Passover seder is still held, and still on that date.

## What was carried out

Silver, gold and clothing, asked for and given: *thus they plundered the Egyptians*. See the note
on Exodus 12:33–36.

And Joseph's bones, as he had made them swear: *God will surely visit you, and you shall carry up
my bones from here*.

Four hundred years in a coffin in Egypt, and somebody remembered.

> **The two watches.** The LORD's vigil that night, on the outside of the doors — and the vigil
> Israel keeps on the same night, every year since, on the inside.
