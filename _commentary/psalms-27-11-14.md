The confident psalm ends with an instruction the writer appears to be giving himself.

## The request

*Teach me Your way, O LORD. **Lead me on a level path**.*

The Hebrew is **be'orach mishor** — in a path of levelness, of evenness.

**Mishor** — a plain, flat ground. The opposite of the broken terrain a hunted man crosses.

## The reason

*Because of **those who watch to trip me**.*

The Hebrew is **shorerai** — my watchers, those lying in wait.

## The plea

*Do not hand me over to the desire of my foes.*

The Hebrew is **benefesh tzarai** — to the appetite, the soul, of my adversaries.

## The charge against them

*For **false witnesses rise against me, breathing out violence**.*

The Hebrew is **edei-sheqer viyfeach chamas**.

**Yafeach** — one who breathes out, puffs out.

## The same phrase in Proverbs

*A false witness **who breathes out lies**, and one who stirs up conflict among his own.* See the note
on Proverbs 6:16–19.

The last two of the seven things God hates.

## The turn

*Yet **I am sure of this**.*

The Hebrew is **lulei he'emanti** — a broken construction. Literally: *unless I had believed…*

The sentence has no ending. The Masoretes marked it with dots above the word — one of a handful of
such marks in the Hebrew Bible.

## What the break implies

*Unless I had believed I would see the goodness of the LORD…* — and then nothing.

The unspoken half is left for the reader. Most translations supply the confident reading.

## The confidence

*I will see **the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living**.*

The Hebrew is **tuv-YHWH be'eretz chayyim**.

## The location

Not afterwards. Here.

**Eretz chayyim** — the land of the living, in contrast to Sheol.

## The instruction

*Wait for the LORD. **Be strong, and let your heart take courage**. Wait for the LORD.*

The Hebrew is **qavveh el-YHWH**, twice.

**Qavah** — to wait, to hope. The root is a cord, something twisted and stretched taut.

## The doubling

The line opens and closes the verse. Whatever is between is bracketed by the same command.

## Who is speaking to whom

There is no addressee named.

The most natural reading is that the psalmist is instructing himself — the same move as *why are
you cast down, O my soul?*

## The word for strength

**Chazaq ve'ya'ametz libbeka** — be strong, and He will strengthen your heart.

The same pair Joshua was given three times: *be strong and of good courage*. See the note on
Joshua 1:6–9.

## What the psalm asked for at the start

*One thing I have desired… **to behold the beauty of the LORD**.* See the note on Psalms 27:4–6.

And the confidence that opens it: *the LORD is my light and my rescue; whom shall I fear?* See the
note on Psalms 27:1–3.

## Where the waiting goes

*But **those who wait on the LORD shall renew their strength**; they shall mount up with wings like
eagles.* See the note on Isaiah 40:29–31.

The same verb, **qoveh**.

> **The unfinished sentence.** *Unless I had believed I would see the goodness of the LORD…* — and
> the Hebrew stops. The scribes marked it. The other half was never written down.
