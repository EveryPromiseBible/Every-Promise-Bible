God asks Job about specific star groups by name, and asks whether he can steer
them.

## The names

*"Can you bind the beautiful cords of **the Pleiades**, or loosen **the belt of
Orion**? Can you lead out **the constellations** in their seasons, or guide **the
Bear with her cubs** across the sky?"*

Four named groups, in a book generally reckoned the oldest in the Bible.

Whoever wrote Job knew the night sky well enough to name its features and expect the
reader to recognise them.

## The Pleiades

*Bind the beautiful cords.*

The Hebrew is **Kimah**, and the word rendered *cords* is a word for sweet
influences or chains — a bond.

The Pleiades are a genuine cluster. The stars in it are gravitationally associated
and move together through space, which is not true of most constellations, where the
stars are unrelated and only appear near each other from here.

The question asks about binding something that is, in fact, bound.

## Orion

*Loosen **the belt** of Orion.*

The Hebrew is **Kesil**, and the word is *bands* — the cords that tie him.

Orion's belt is the most recognisable line of stars in the sky, and the three are
drifting apart. In tens of thousands of years the belt will not be a belt.

Bound, and loosening. The two questions are exact opposites and both are put to Job.

## The constellations

*"Can you **lead out the constellations in their seasons**?"*

The Hebrew is **Mazzaroth** — a word that appears once in Scripture, here.

It is generally taken as the twelve signs, the zodiac band the sun travels through
across a year, and *in their seasons* is what they do: each one rises at its own time
and gives way to the next.

They come out in order, and Job is asked whether he brings them.

## The Bear

*"Or **guide the Bear with her cubs**?"*

**Ayish** and her sons — the great bear, and the stars around her.

The Hebrew verb is to lead, to guide, the word used of a shepherd with a flock.

## The laws

*"Do you know **the laws that govern the heavens**? Could you **set their rule over
the earth**?"*

The Hebrew for *laws* is **chuqqot** — statutes, fixed decrees. The same word used of
the law given at Sinai.

The heavens are described as running on statutes, and having a governing effect on
the earth below — which, in the plainest sense, they do: seasons, tides, the length
of a day.

## Where this sits

Chapter 38, out of the whirlwind, in the middle of four chapters of questions about
the sea's doors, the storehouses of snow, the ostrich, the wild donkey and the horse.

Not one of them is about Job's conduct. See the note on Job 38:1–3.

## What the sky is doing, according to Scripture

*The heavens are telling the glory of God… there is no speech, there are no words…
yet their sound goes out through all the earth.* See the note on Psalms 19:1–4.

Speech with no language, going out to everybody.

> **What God says the stars did at the beginning.** *When the morning stars sang
> together and all the sons of God shouted for joy* — five verses earlier in this same
> speech. He asks Job about their orbits, and mentions that they sang. See the note
> on Job 38:4–7.
