He takes the most impressive religion in the region apart by describing how it is made.

## The address

*Hear the word the LORD speaks to you, **house of Israel**.*

Babylon is coming, and Babylonian religion with it — astrology, omens, processions with images
carried through the streets.

Jeremiah's audience is about to be surrounded by it.

## The first instruction

*"**Do not learn the ways of the nations**."*

The Hebrew is **al-derek haggoyim al-tilmadu**.

**Lamad** — to learn, to be trained in. The root behind *talmud*.

Not *do not worship their gods*. Do not be schooled in their habits.

## The second

*"**Do not be terrified by signs in the sky**, though the nations are terrified by them."*

The Hebrew is **me'otot hashamayim** — signs of the heavens.

Babylonian religion was astronomical. Eclipses, comets, planetary conjunctions and the movement
of stars were read as messages about the fate of kings and harvests.

## What is conceded

*Though the nations are terrified by them.*

The fear is real and widespread. Jeremiah does not deny that everyone around them is frightened.

He tells one people not to join in.

## The verdict

*"**The customs of the peoples are empty**."*

The Hebrew is **hevel** — vapour, breath. The word Ecclesiastes uses twenty-nine times. See the
note on Ecclesiastes 1:1–3.

## The manufacture

*"**Someone cuts a tree from the forest**. A craftsman shapes it with his tools."*

The argument is a production sequence. Jeremiah walks the reader backwards from the finished
object to the woodpile.

## The decoration

*"He decorates it with **silver and gold**."*

The precious metal goes on last, over the wood.

## The nails

*"And **fastens it with hammer and nails so it will not topple over**."*

The Hebrew is **bemasmerot uvmaqqavot yechazzequm velo yaphiq**.

The god has to be nailed down so it does not fall over.

That single detail is the whole argument, and Isaiah makes the same one: *he seeks for himself a
skilful workman to prepare a carved image that will not totter*.

## The image

*"Their idols are like **a scarecrow standing in a melon patch**."*

The Hebrew is **ketomer miqshah** — like a pillar, or a palm-trunk figure, in a cucumber field.

Something shaped like a person, standing in a field, that frightens birds and nothing else.

## The three disabilities

*"**They cannot speak. They have to be carried, because they cannot walk**."*

Silent and immobile.

The psalm makes the same list at length: *they have mouths, but they do not speak; eyes, but they
do not see… **those who make them are like them***.

## The conclusion

*"**Do not be afraid of them. They can do you no harm, and they cannot do you any good either**."*

The Hebrew is **ki-lo yare'u vegam-heytev eyn otam**.

Both halves matter. The gods that cannot hurt you also cannot help you.

## What is set against it

*"Inasmuch as **there is none like You, O LORD**; You are great, and Your name is great in
might."*

*But **the LORD is the true God; He is the living God and the everlasting King**. At His wrath the
earth will tremble.* See the note on Jeremiah 10:6–10.

A god that has to be nailed upright, against one at whose anger the ground moves.

## The one that has to be carried

*"They have to be carried."*

Isaiah answers it directly: *even to your old age, I am He; and even to grey hairs I will carry
you. **I have made, and I will bear**; even I will carry, and will deliver you*.

One kind of god is carried. The other carries.

> **The detail.** It has to be nailed down so it will not fall over. Jeremiah builds the whole
> argument out of the manufacturing process.
