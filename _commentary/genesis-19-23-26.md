The destruction takes one verse. The sentence before it and the sentence after it are about
two people.

## The timing

*The sun had risen over the land **when Lot reached Zoar**.*

The angel had said it: *hurry, escape there, for I cannot do anything until you arrive there*.
See the note on Genesis 19:15–17.

The account keeps the two clocks together. He arrives, and the sun is up, and then it happens.

## What fell

*Then the LORD **rained down burning sulfur** on Sodom and Gomorrah — **out of the heavens from
the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **gophrith va'esh** — brimstone and fire.

The doubled phrasing — *from the LORD, out of the heavens* — is unusual and the rabbis and the
commentators have argued over it for centuries.

## The extent

*He overthrew **those cities and the entire plain, all who lived in the cities, and everything
that grew in the land**.*

The Hebrew for *overthrew* is **vayyahaphok** — turned upside down.

The word becomes the standard term for it. Isaiah, Jeremiah, Amos and Peter all use *the
overthrow*.

And the vegetation is named. The plain Lot chose because it was *well watered, like the garden
of the LORD* is included in the list of what stopped growing. See the note on
Genesis 13:5–13.

## The wife

*But **Lot's wife looked back**, and she **became a pillar of salt**.*

The Hebrew is **vattabbet** — she looked, she gazed. Not a glance over the shoulder; the verb
suggests regarding something with attention.

And *from behind him* — she had fallen behind Lot, which means she had slowed.

## What the instruction had been

*"Do not look behind you, nor stay anywhere in the plain."*

Given to all four of them, at the edge of the city, by the same messenger.

## What she is not given

A name.

Genesis records her twice: once as one of four wrists taken by an angel, and once here.

## Where the salt is

The southern end of the Dead Sea is a region of salt formations, and Josephus reports being
shown the pillar in the first century.

The lake itself is roughly ten times as salty as the ocean, and nothing lives in it.

## The three words

*"**Remember Lot's wife**."*

Jesus, in the middle of the Olivet discourse in Luke, and that is the entire quotation.

The sentence He attaches to it: *whoever seeks to save his life will lose it, and whoever loses
his life will preserve it*.

## What she was looking at

Genesis does not say, and the surrounding sentences suggest it.

A house, a city, two daughters left behind with the men they were pledged to, and the whole of
an adult life.

The passage does not present her as wicked. It presents her as somebody who turned round.

## What was in front

Zoar, which was not the destination. The instruction had been *flee to the hills*, and Lot had
argued for the small town instead.

They end up in the hills anyway a few verses later — *for he was afraid to dwell in Zoar* — and
the chapter finishes in a cave.

## The verse that follows

*And Abraham went early in the morning to the place where he had stood before the LORD.* See
the note on Genesis 19:27–29.

Genesis cuts from the pillar of salt to a man walking back to where he had prayed.

> **The verb.** Not a glance. She *gazed* — and Genesis notes she was behind him, which means
> she had already slowed down.
