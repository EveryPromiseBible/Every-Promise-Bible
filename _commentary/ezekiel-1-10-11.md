The strangest opening of any book in the Bible, and Ezekiel is straining to describe it.

## Where he is

*By the River Chebar, in the land of the Chaldeans.*

A canal in Babylon, among the exiles taken with Jehoiachin. Ezekiel is thirty years old and a
priest who will never serve in a temple.

*The heavens were opened and I saw visions of God.*

## How he describes it

Everything is hedged.

*The **likeness** of four living creatures. **As for the likeness** of their faces. **Something
like** a firmament. **The appearance of the likeness** of the glory of the LORD.*

He is a careful writer refusing to overclaim. Four layers of qualification before he will say
what he saw.

## The four

*Each of the four had the face of **a man** in front, the face of **a lion** on the right, the
face of **an ox** on the left, and the face of **an eagle** behind.*

Four faces on each creature, and four creatures — sixteen faces, facing every way at once.

*They did not turn when they went.*

## What the four are

The traditional reading takes them as the highest of four orders: man among creatures, lion
among wild beasts, ox among domestic animals, eagle among birds.

## Where they turn up again

Revelation, round the throne, one face each rather than four: *the first like a lion, the second
like a calf, the third with a face like a man, the fourth like a flying eagle*. See the note on
Revelation 4:5–6.

And in Revelation each of the four calls out one of the first four seals.

## The wings

*Their wings were **spread upward**; two wings of each **touched the wings of the creature
beside it**, and **two covered their bodies**.*

Four wings each. Two up and joined to the next creature, two covering.

Isaiah's seraphim had six: *with two he covered his face, with two he covered his feet, and with
two he flew*.

## The wheels

The next paragraph: *a wheel in the middle of a wheel*, rims full of eyes, moving without
turning, *for the spirit of the living creatures was in the wheels*.

## What is above them

*And above the firmament over their heads was the likeness of a throne, in appearance like a
sapphire stone; and on the likeness of the throne was **a likeness with the appearance of a
man**.*

The vision's destination is a seat, and somebody on it.

## The last line of the chapter

*This was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD. **So when I saw it, I fell on
my face**.*

Four chapters of preamble and one sentence of reaction.

## What he is told next

*"Son of man, stand on your feet, and I will speak to you."*

And the scroll he is made to eat: *and it was in my mouth like honey in sweetness*. And then:
*go, get to the house of Israel and speak with My words to them*.

## Why the vision came where it did

Babylon.

The glory the priests had assumed was fixed to a building in Jerusalem turns up beside an
irrigation canal in a foreign country, with wheels on it.

Ezekiel later watches it leave the temple — *then the glory of the LORD departed from the
threshold of the temple* — and at the end of the book he watches it come back.

> **How he writes it.** *The likeness of the appearance.* Four layers of qualification before
> he will commit to a description — and the last sentence of the chapter is that he fell over.
