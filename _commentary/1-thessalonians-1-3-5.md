Paul's opening thanksgiving, and each of the three is given a verb.

## The three with their nouns

*How your **faith goes to work**, how your **love keeps laboring on**, how your **hope in
our Lord Jesus Christ holds steady and patient** through everything.*

The Greek is three genitive pairs:

- **Ergou tes pisteos** — the work of faith
- **Kopou tes agapes** — the labour of love
- **Hupomones tes elpidos** — the endurance of hope

Paul's triad appears throughout his letters — *faith, hope and love, these three* — and
here each is attached to something it produces.

## The three words

- **Ergon** — work, what is done
- **Kopos** — labour to weariness, toil that costs
- **Hupomone** — remaining under, holding position

The second is stronger than the first, and the third is not activity at all.

## Which goes with which

Faith works. Love is the one that wears you out. Hope is what stays under the weight.

Paul does not shuffle them; the pairings are the same when he repeats the triad in the
same letter: *putting on the breastplate of faith and love, and as a helmet the hope of
salvation.*

## Where it happens

*And **all of it lived out in the sight of our God and Father**.*

## The address

*Dear friends, **loved by God**.*

The Greek is a perfect passive participle — having been loved and standing loved.

## The claim

*We know that **He chose you**.*

The Greek is **eklogen** — election.

Paul says he knows it, and then gives his evidence — which is the striking part.

## The evidence

*Because **when we brought you the good news it did not arrive as words and nothing
more**.*

The Greek is *not in word only*.

He does not argue from their sincerity, their decision, or their conduct. He argues from
what the preaching was like when it landed.

## The three things it came with

*It came **with power**, and **with the Holy Spirit**, and **with a deep and settled
certainty**.*

The last is **plerophoria polle** — much full assurance. Full-carrying, complete
conviction.

## The second half of the evidence

*And **you saw for yourselves the kind of men we were while we were with you**, and **it was
for your sake that we were that way**.*

The conduct of the messengers is offered as part of it, and the reason given for the
conduct is the readers.

## How long he was there

Acts 17: three sabbaths in the synagogue, then a mob, then Jason arrested and made to post
bond, then Paul and Silas sent away by night.

Weeks. And what he says about the church that came out of it is in the next verses: *the
word of the Lord rang out from you… your trust in God has gone out everywhere.* See the
note on 1 Thessalonians 1:8–10.

## The rest of the sentence

*And you became followers of us and of the Lord, **having received the word in much
affliction, with joy of the Holy Spirit**.*

Affliction and joy in the same clause.

> **What Paul offers as proof of election.** Not the converts' performance. The manner in
> which the message arrived — power, the Spirit, and full assurance — and the fact that
> they saw what the messengers were like.
