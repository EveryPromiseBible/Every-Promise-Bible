---
key: Luke 12:49–53
title: "A baptism I have to be baptised with"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Three sentences in which **Jesus** says what He came to do, and none of them is comfortable.

## The fire

*"**I came to throw fire on the earth, and how I wish it were already blazing.**"*

**Ti thelo ei ede anephthe** — the Greek is difficult and reads like an unfinished exclamation.

Fire in Scripture is both judgement and purification, and **Luke** is the evangelist who reports it
falling: *there appeared to them divided tongues, as of fire, and one sat upon each of them.*

*"**He will baptise you with the Holy Spirit and fire.**"*

John the Baptist says it in chapter 3 of the same gospel, and puts it in the same sentence as a
baptism.

## The baptism

*"**But there is a baptism I have to be baptised with, and I am hemmed in with longing until it is
finished.**"*

He has already been baptised, in chapter 3. This is a second one, and He is not talking about water.

*"**Are you able to drink the cup that I drink, and be baptised with the baptism that I am baptised
with?**"*

**Mark** 10, said to James and John when they asked for the two seats. In both places the baptism is
the death.

*Or do you not know that as many of us as were **baptised into Christ Jesus were baptised into His
death**?*

## Hemmed in

**Synechomai** — I am held together, constrained, pressed on both sides.

*For **the love of Christ compels us**, because we judge thus: that if One died for all, then all
died.*

**Paul** uses the same verb in **2 Corinthians** 5, of himself, and names what is doing the pressing.

## The division

*"**Do you imagine that I came to hand out peace on the earth? No, I tell you, but division.**"*

And then the households: father against son, mother against daughter, five in one house divided
three against two.

*"For **I have come to set a man against his father**… and a man's enemies will be those of his own
household."*

**Matthew** 10, quoting Micah 7:6.

## And the peace He does give

*"**Peace I leave with you, My peace I give to you; not as the world gives do I give to you.**"*

*Therefore, having been justified by faith, **we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus
Christ**.*

*For **He Himself is our peace**.*

The peace He denies is the kind that leaves everybody as they were. The peace He gives is between God
and a person, and **Luke** 12 is honest that it costs something at the dinner table.

> **The sentence in the middle.** *There is a baptism I have to be baptised with, and I am hemmed in
> until it is finished.* He had been baptised nine chapters earlier. This one is the cross, and the
> verb for what it was doing to Him is the one Paul uses of being under pressure from love.
