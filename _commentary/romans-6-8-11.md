Paul makes his case about the believer by first making it about Christ.

## What is true of Him

*Christ, having been raised from the dead, **dies no more**; **death no longer has
any mastery over Him**.*

Two negatives, both permanent. Whatever death had a claim on, that claim is
finished.

## The death He died

*The death He died was **a death to sin, died once for all and never again**.*

The Greek is **ephapax** — once, once and for all, decisively. Hebrews uses the same
word repeatedly: *once for all He offered up Himself*, *by one offering He has
perfected forever*.

He did not die to sin repeatedly, and there is no arrangement by which He does it
again.

## The life He lives

*And **the life He lives is a life lived to God**.*

Present tense. That is what He is doing now.

## "In the same way"

*In the same way, **count yourselves** dead indeed to sin, but alive to God through
Jesus Christ our Lord.*

Everything said about Him in the three verses before is what the reader is told to
reckon about himself. The pattern is not aspirational — it is the same set of
facts, applied.

## "Count"

The Greek is **logizomai** — the bookkeeping word. To reckon, to enter into the
account, to regard as so.

It does not mean to pretend, and it does not manufacture the fact. An accountant
reckons what is already in the ledger. Paul has spent seven verses putting the
figures there before asking anyone to add them up.

## Where the reckoning sits in the chapter

Paul's order through Romans 6 is fixed and never reverses:

1. *we died to sin* — stated
2. *buried with Him… raised* — stated
3. *our old man was crucified with Him* — stated
4. *Christ dies no more* — stated
5. **then**: *count yourselves dead indeed to sin*
6. **then**: *do not let sin reign in your mortal body*

The command in verse 12 is the first instruction in the chapter, and it arrives
eleven verses after the facts it rests on.

## "Through Jesus Christ our Lord"

The last phrase locates it. Not dead to sin in yourself, examined on your own
terms — dead to sin *in Him*, which is the position the whole chapter has been
describing.

> **What "dead to sin" does not mean.** Not that sin has stopped existing or
> knocking. Paul says *do not let it reign* in the very next verse, which would be
> a strange thing to say to someone it could no longer approach. Dead to sin is
> about jurisdiction — who has a claim — not about whether the old master still
> calls round.
