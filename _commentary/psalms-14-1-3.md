A survey conducted from heaven, and the result is quoted by Paul at the centre of Romans.

## The sentence

*The fool says **in his heart**, "There is no God."*

The Hebrew is **amar naval belibbo eyn Elohim**.

**Naval** — the fool. Not a stupid man. The word describes a moral condition: the person who has
no regard for anything, and whose name Abigail's husband bore.

## Where he says it

**Belibbo** — in his heart.

Not a philosophical position advanced in public. Something a person says to himself.

## What follows from it

*They are **rotten**, their deeds are **vile**; not one of them does good.*

The Hebrew is **hishchitu hit'ivu alilah** — they have corrupted, they have done abominably.

The psalm does not treat the denial as an abstract error. It names conduct.

## The survey

*The LORD **leans down from heaven to look** over all the children of dust.*

The Hebrew is **hishqiph** — to lean over and gaze down, as from a window.

The same verb used of God looking down on Sodom, and on the Egyptians at the sea.

## What He is looking for

*Searching for **one who understands**, one who **reaches out for God**.*

The Hebrew is **maskil doresh et-Elohim** — one acting wisely, seeking God.

Two things: intelligence about the situation, and a move toward Him.

## The result

*But they have **all turned away, all gone sour together**; not one does good, **not even one**.*

The Hebrew is **hakkol sar yachdav ne'elachu** — all turned aside, together they became putrid.

**Alach** — to spoil, to turn rancid. The word for milk gone off.

## The count

*Not even one.*

The Hebrew is **eyn gam-echad**.

The psalm surveys the whole species and returns a nil result.

## What Paul does with it

*As it is written: "**There is none righteous, no, not one; there is none who understands; there is
none who seeks after God**."*

The opening of a chain of quotations — this psalm, then Psalm 5, Psalm 140, Psalm 10, Isaiah 59,
Psalm 36.

*Now we know that whatever the law says, it says to those who are under the law, **that every mouth
may be stopped**.* See the note on Romans 3:19–20.

## What the chain is for

To close the case before the offer.

*But now the righteousness of God apart from the law is revealed… **for all have sinned and fall
short of the glory of God, being justified freely by His grace**.* See the note on
Romans 3:23–24.

## What the psalm says next

*There they are **in great fear**, for **God is with the generation of the righteous**.*

Which sits oddly against *not one does good*, and the psalm does not reconcile it.

## The last verse

*Oh, that the salvation of Israel would come out of Zion! When the LORD brings back the captivity
of His people, **let Jacob rejoice and Israel be glad**.*

The psalm that finds nobody good ends by asking for salvation to come out of Zion.

## The duplicate

Psalm 53 is the same psalm, with minor variations and **Elohim** where this one has **YHWH**.

The Psalter preserves both.

> **The verdict.** A survey of the whole species conducted from heaven, and the count returned is
> *not even one* — which is the sentence Paul builds the case in Romans 3 on.
