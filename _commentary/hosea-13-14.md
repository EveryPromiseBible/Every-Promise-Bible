One verse in the middle of the harshest chapter in the book, and **Paul** takes it to a funeral.

## Where it sits

Hosea 13 is unrelieved. A lion, a leopard, a bear robbed of her cubs, and *I will destroy you, O
Israel.* The verse before this one has Ephraim as a child who will not come to the birth.

And then, without transition, two lines of pure promise.

## The two verbs

*"**I will ransom them** from the power of the grave. **I will redeem them** from death."*

**Padah** and **ga'al**.

**Padah** — to ransom, to buy out by paying a price. **Ga'al** — the kinsman-redeemer's verb, the
obligation of the nearest relative to buy back what a family member has lost. It is Boaz's word in
Ruth.

Both are commercial and both are family. Neither is a rescue by force.

## Sheol

*"From the power of **the grave**."*

The Hebrew is **miyyad she'ol** — from the hand of Sheol.

## The taunt

*"**Where, O death, are your plagues? Where, O grave, is your destruction?**"*

Two questions thrown at death, in a chapter about a nation being destroyed.

## Who quotes it

*So when this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality, then
shall be brought to pass the saying that is written: "Death is swallowed up in victory."

**"O Death, where is your sting? O Hades, where is your victory?"** The sting of death is sin, and
the strength of sin is the law. **But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord
Jesus Christ.***

**1 Corinthians** 15, at the end of the New Testament's longest argument about resurrection.
**Paul** puts Hosea 13:14 beside Isaiah 25:8 and lets the two taunt death together.

He is quoting a verse from the bleakest page in Hosea and using it as the closing line of the
gospel's account of the last enemy.

## What he adds

*The sting of death is sin, and the strength of sin is the law.*

**Paul** explains why the questions can be asked. Death's power was never mortality; it was the
charge sheet.

*Having wiped out the handwriting of requirements that was against us… **having nailed it to the
cross**.*

## And who holds the keys

*"**I am He who lives, and was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore.** Amen. And **I have the
keys of Hades and of Death**."*

**Revelation** 1, and the two nouns are Hosea's two, in the same order.

*Inasmuch then as the children have partaken of flesh and blood, He Himself likewise shared in the
same, **that through death He might destroy him who had the power of death**.*

**Hebrews** 2, and the qualification it names is the kinsman-redeemer's: He had to be a relative
first. **Ga'al** requires it.

## The last clause

*"Compassion will be hidden from My eyes."*

The verse ends darkly and the chapter goes on getting worse. Hosea puts the promise in and does
not soften what surrounds it.

*"How can I give you up, Ephraim?… **all My compassion is stirred and burning**."* See the note on
Hosea 11:8.

Two chapters earlier the compassion is burning. Here it is hidden. The book holds both, and
**1 Corinthians** 15 decides which one had the last word.

> **Where Paul found it.** In the harshest chapter of Hosea, between a lion and a bear, two lines
> of promise — and he made them the closing taunt of the resurrection chapter.
