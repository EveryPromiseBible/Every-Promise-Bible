A pilgrim song, and the hardest stretch of the road is named.

## Who wrote it

*To the Chief Musician. On an instrument of Gath. A Psalm of the sons of Korah.*

Korah led the rebellion in Numbers 16 and the ground opened. *Nevertheless the children of Korah
did not die.*

His descendants became temple singers, and eleven psalms carry their name.

## What has come before

*How lovely is Your tabernacle, O LORD of hosts!*

*Even the sparrow has found a home, and the swallow a nest for herself, where she may lay her
young — even Your altars, O LORD of hosts.* See the note on Psalms 84:1–4.

Birds nesting in the temple rafters, which the psalmist envies.

## The blessing

*Blessed is the one **whose strength is in You**.*

The Hebrew is **ashrei adam oz-lo vach** — happy is the man whose strength is in You.

## The heart

*In whose heart are **the highways to Zion**.*

The Hebrew is **mesillot bilvavam** — highways in their heart.

**Mesillah** — a raised road, a built-up causeway.

The road is inside them. They are not on it yet.

## What the psalm is about

The three annual pilgrim feasts, when Israel travelled to Jerusalem.

Many walked for days across hard country to get there.

## The valley

*As they pass through **the Valley of Weeping**.*

The Hebrew is **emeq habbacha**.

**Bacha** — either weeping, or the balsam tree, which grows in dry ground and weeps sap when cut.

The Hebrew allows both, and the poem probably wants both: a dry valley named for a tree that
weeps.

## What they do to it

*They **make it a place of springs**.*

The Hebrew is **ma'yan yeshituhu** — they make it a spring.

The pilgrims are the subject. The valley does not change on its own.

## What that means

Travellers dug cisterns and cleared wells as they went, for themselves and for those behind them.

The dry stretch is left wetter than they found it.

## The rain

*The **early rain covers it with pools**.*

The Hebrew is **moreh** — the early rain, the October rains that break the summer.

**Berakhot** — pools, and the word is one letter from **berakhot**, blessings. The Hebrew ear
catches both.

## The line

*They go **from strength to strength**.*

The Hebrew is **yelchu mechayil el-chayil**.

Which is not how walking works. Distance takes strength out of a traveller.

The psalm says the opposite, and the valley is between the two strengths.

## The arrival

*Each one **appears before God in Zion**.*

The Hebrew is **yera'eh el-Elohim betziyyon** — is seen before God in Zion.

Every one of them arrives. The psalm does not lose anybody in the valley.

## What follows

*For a day in Your courts is better than a thousand. **I would rather be a doorkeeper in the house
of my God** than dwell in the tents of wickedness.*

*For the LORD God is a sun and shield; **the LORD will give grace and glory; no good thing will He
withhold from those who walk uprightly**.* See the note on Psalms 84:10–12.

## What Paul says about the same direction

*Therefore we do not lose heart. Even though our outward man is perishing, **yet the inward man is
being renewed day by day**.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 4:16–18.

> **The line.** *From strength to strength* — which is not how walking works. And the valley the
> pilgrims leave wetter than they found it is between the two.
