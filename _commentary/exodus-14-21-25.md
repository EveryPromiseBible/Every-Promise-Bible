The crossing takes all night, and the text says what did the work.

## The position

Pharaoh's army behind, the sea in front, and Moses saying *stand still, and see the salvation of
the LORD*.

And God's answer: *why do you cry to Me? **Tell the children of Israel to go forward***. See the
note on Exodus 14:13–16.

## The gesture

*Then Moses **stretched out his hand over the sea**.*

The staff, held out. Nothing else is asked of him.

## The mechanism

*And **all that night the LORD drove the sea back with a strong east wind**, turning it into dry
land.*

The Hebrew is **beruach qadim azzah** — with a strong east wind.

The text names a wind and a direction, and says it blew all night.

**Ruach** — wind, breath, spirit. The same noun that moved over the waters in Genesis 1.

## The duration

All night.

Not instantaneous. The people stood on the shore in the dark while a wind blew, and the ground
appeared gradually.

## The result

*The waters were divided, and **the Israelites walked through the sea on dry ground**, a wall of
water on their right and on their left.*

The Hebrew is **bayabbashah** — on dry ground, not mud.

**Chomah** — a wall.

## The pursuit

*The Egyptians pursued them — **all Pharaoh's horses, chariots, and horsemen** followed them into
the sea.*

They go in. The whole army follows a slave column onto a seabed.

## The morning watch

*But **in the morning watch**, the LORD looked down on the Egyptian army **from the pillar of fire
and cloud** and threw it into confusion.*

The Hebrew is **be'ashmoret habboqer** — the last watch of the night, roughly two to six.

**Vayyaqoshem** — He looked down. The verb is used of a hostile gaze.

## The pillar

It had moved during the night: *the Angel of God who went before the camp of Israel moved and went
behind them*, and *it came between the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel*.

*So it was a cloud and darkness to the one, and it gave light by night to the other.*

The same object, doing opposite things to the two armies.

## The wheels

*He **jammed their chariot wheels so they could barely move**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyasar et ophan markevotav** — He removed, or turned aside, their chariot
wheels.

The Samaritan text and the Septuagint read *bound*, or *clogged*.

## What the Egyptians say

*"**Let us flee from Israel — the LORD is fighting for them against Egypt**."*

The Hebrew is **YHWH nilcham lahem** — the LORD fights for them.

The covenant name, spoken by the Egyptian army, in the middle of the sea.

They have worked it out. It is too late.

## What Moses had said

*"**The LORD will fight for you, and you shall hold your peace**."*

The same verb, said before the crossing. The Egyptians repeat his sentence back in the last
minutes of their lives.

## What happens next

*So the waters returned and covered the chariots, the horsemen, and all the army of Pharaoh…
**not so much as one of them remained**.*

*Thus Israel saw the great work which the LORD had done in Egypt; so the people feared the LORD,
and believed the LORD and His servant Moses.*

## The song

*I will sing to the LORD, for He has triumphed gloriously! **The horse and its rider He has thrown
into the sea**.* See the note on Exodus 15:1–5.

Miriam and the timbrels, and then three days later they cannot find water. See the note on
Exodus 15:22–27.

## What the psalm says about the path

*Your way was in the sea, Your path in the great waters, **and Your footsteps were not known**.*

> **What the text names.** A strong east wind, blowing all night. The people waited in the dark
> while the ground came up.
