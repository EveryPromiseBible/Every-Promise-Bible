A prayer said out loud, and it is the most striking thing Jesus says about Himself outside
John.

## When

*At that time.*

Immediately after the woes on the cities where most of His miracles had been done —
Chorazin, Bethsaida, Capernaum. *If the mighty works which were done in you had been done in
Sodom, it would have remained until this day.*

The prayer follows a paragraph about rejection.

## The opening

*"**I thank You, Father, Lord of heaven and earth**."*

The Greek is **exomologoumai** — I acknowledge fully, I confess, I praise.

Two titles in one breath: Father, and Lord of heaven and earth. The intimate one and the
cosmic one.

## The content

*"That You have **hidden these things from the clever and the well-schooled** and have **shown
them to little children**."*

The Greek is **sophon kai suneton** — the wise and the intelligent. And **nepiois** —
infants, the very young.

He thanks God for a concealment.

## What is being hidden

*These things* — the identity of the Son, which is what the next two verses are about.

## The reason

*"Yes, Father, **because this is what pleased You**."*

The Greek is **eudokia** — good pleasure, what seemed good.

No further explanation is offered. Jesus does not argue that the clever deserved it or that
the children earned it.

## What Paul does with the same idea

*Where is the wise? Where is the scribe? Where is the disputer of this age? Has not God made
foolish the wisdom of this world?… **God has chosen the foolish things of the world to put to
shame the wise**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 1:18–25.

And *not many wise according to the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, are called*.

## The handover

*"**Everything has been handed over to Me by My Father**."*

The Greek is **panta moi paredothe** — all things were delivered to Me.

The same verb used of Judas handing Him over, and of the Father delivering Him up for us all.

## The knowing

*"**No one really knows the Son except the Father, and no one really knows the Father except
the Son**."*

The Greek is **epiginoskei** — knows fully, thoroughly.

A closed circle, stated in both directions. It is the kind of sentence John's Gospel is full
of and the other three are not.

## The opening in it

*"And **anyone the Son chooses to show Him to**."*

The Greek is **boletai apokalupsai** — wills to reveal.

The circle has one door and the Son holds it.

## What follows immediately

*"**Come to Me, all you who labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest**. Take My
yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest
for your souls. For My yoke is easy and My burden is light."* See the note on
Matthew 11:28–30.

The invitation comes directly after the sentence about the closed circle. The revealing He
mentions in verse 27 is issued as an invitation in verse 28.

And the terms are not intellectual. *All you who labour and are heavy laden.*

## Who the infants are

Not literal children, though He uses them as an object lesson elsewhere: *unless you are
converted and become as little children, you will by no means enter the kingdom of heaven*.

The word describes people with no standing to bring — which is the qualification the
invitation asks for.

> **What He thanks God for.** The hiding. And then, two sentences later, He opens it to
> anyone who is tired.
