Two verses, and they are the search party talking.

## The change of voice

Verses 3 to 5 were David's vow, in the first person singular. See the note on
Psalms 132:3–5.

Now it is *we*. A group is speaking, and they are recounting a hunt.

## The hearing

*We **heard of it in Ephrathah**.*

**Ephrathah** is Bethlehem — *but you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, though you are little among the
thousands of Judah, yet out of you shall come forth to Me the One to be Ruler in Israel*.

David's home town. That is where the party starts from, or where the report reached them.

## The finding

*We **found it in the fields of Jaar**.*

**Jaar** — forest, wood. Short for **Kirjath Jearim**, the city of forests.

That is where the ark had been sitting, in Abinadab's house on the hill, for twenty years and
then through the whole of Saul's reign. See the note on 1 Samuel 7:2–4.

## What the two place names do

Bethlehem and the fields of the wood.

The psalm compresses the whole story into one line: they heard about it where David came from,
and found it out in the countryside.

*In the fields* is the point. It was not in a sanctuary. It was on a farm, in a house, up a
hill, where nobody had been to see it.

## Whose fault that was

Nobody's, specifically, and everybody's.

The Chronicler puts it plainly when David finally moves: *let us bring the ark of our God back
to us, **for we did not enquire at it in the days of Saul***.

Forty years of a king who never sent.

## The response

*Let us **go into His dwelling**. Let us **bow low at His footstool**.*

The Hebrew is **mishkenotav** — His dwellings, plural. The tabernacle word.

And **hadom raglav** — the footstool of His feet.

The ark is called God's footstool elsewhere: *worship at His footstool*, and David to the
assembly: *I had it in my heart to build a house of rest for the ark of the covenant of the
LORD, and for **the footstool of our God**.*

A footstool implies a throne, and the throne is not in the room.

## What is above it

The mercy seat, and the two cherubim facing each other with wings overshadowing.

*And there I will meet with you, and I will speak with you from above the mercy seat, from
between the two cherubim.*

Paul's word for what Christ was publicly set forth as. See the note on
Romans 3:25–26.

## What follows in the psalm

*Rise up, LORD, to Your resting place, **You and the ark of Your strength**.* See the note on
Psalms 132:8–10.

That sentence is Moses' — *rise up, O LORD* — said whenever the ark set out in the wilderness.
See the note on Numbers 10:33–36.

The psalm is quoting a marching order at the end of the march.

## Where it was sung

Solomon quotes the next verses at the dedication of the temple, when the ark is finally carried
in and set under the wings of the cherubim in the Holy of Holies.

*Now therefore, arise, O LORD God, to Your resting place, You and the ark of Your strength.*

The search that started *in the fields of the wood* ends in a room.

> **Where they found it.** Not in a sanctuary. In the fields — a house on a hill that a whole
> generation had not visited.
