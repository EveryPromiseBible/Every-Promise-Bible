Paul answers his critics by proving he has every right they say he has forfeited — and then, in the
next breath, refuses to use any of them.

## Apologia

*This is my **defense** to those who examine me.*

**Apologia**, a formal legal defence, and **anakrinousin**, the same courtroom verb he used five
chapters earlier when he called their examination *a very small thing*.

So he is not defending himself. He said already that he does not need the verdict. What he is
defending is **the right**, because if the Corinthians can talk themselves into believing an
apostle has no claim on their support, the next man through the door will starve.

He argues the case, wins it comprehensively — and then declines the award.

## Rights he has and does not take

*Do we not have a **right** to eat and to drink? … a right to take along a believing wife, as the
rest of the apostles do, and the brothers of the Lord, and Cephas?*

The word is **exousia** — authority. Not a favour he is angling for; a legitimate claim, and he
stacks up the precedent: the other apostles, the Lord's own family, Peter by name.

Then the whole chapter turns: *we did not use this right, but endure all things lest we hinder the
gospel of Christ.*

That is the shape of the mind Paul tells the Philippians to have — *who, being in the form of God,
did not consider it robbery to be equal with God, but made Himself of no reputation.* The rights
were real. That is what made laying them down worth anything.

*For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He
became poor.*

## The brothers of the Lord

An easy phrase to read past. These are the men who did not believe in Him — *for even His brothers
did not believe in Him* — and who once went out to take charge of Him because they thought He was
out of His mind.

By the time Paul writes, they are travelling workers whose names carry weight in the churches, and
one of them has written a letter that is in the Bible.

Nobody argued them into it. Something happened in a garden tomb, and one of the resurrection
appearances Paul lists in this very letter is *then He was seen by James*.

> **And Barnabas.** *Or is it only Barnabas and I who have no right to leave off working for a
> living?* Of all the names Paul could have set beside his own, he picks the man who vouched for
> him when the church in Jerusalem was still frightened of him. Two men who worked with their hands
> rather than press a claim — and one of them had already spent his credibility on the other.
