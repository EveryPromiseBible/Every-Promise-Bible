The answer to the vow, and it uses the same words back.

## The choosing

*For the LORD has **chosen Zion**.*

The Hebrew is **bachar** — chose, selected.

The psalm's first half was David swearing he would not sleep until he found a place. See the
note on Psalms 132:3–5.

The second half is God saying He had already picked one.

## The desiring

*He has **desired it for His home**.*

The Hebrew is **ivvah** — longed for, craved. A strong verb.

It is the same word used of appetite — *the mixed multitude who were among them **yielded to
intense craving***.

God is described as wanting an address.

## The declaration

*This is **My resting place forever**.*

**Menuchati** — My rest, My resting place. The same noun David used in his vow and the psalm
used in the summons: *arise, LORD, to Your resting place*. See the note on
Psalms 132:8–10.

The request and the answer use one word.

## The dwelling

*Here **I will dwell**.*

The Hebrew is **eshev** — I will sit, remain, be enthroned.

## The reason, repeated

*For **I have desired it**.*

The same verb as verse 13, said twice in two verses.

No other reason is offered. Not Zion's merit, not its position, not its history — He wanted it.

## What Zion was

A hill with a Jebusite fortress on it, which David took by sending men up a water shaft.

Nothing about it recommended itself. It was not the highest ground in the region; Gibeon had the
tabernacle and Bethel had the history.

Deuteronomy had promised only *the place which the LORD your God chooses*, without naming it,
about twenty times.

This is the naming.

## The exchange

David: *I will find a place for the LORD, a dwelling for the Mighty One of Jacob*.

God: *this is My resting place forever; here I will dwell, for I have desired it*.

The man swears to find one and is told one was chosen.

## What the temple ends up being

*Yet regard the prayer of Your servant… that Your eyes may be open toward this temple night and
day, toward the place of which You said, "**My name shall be there**."*

Solomon, at the dedication, and in the same prayer: *but will God indeed dwell on the earth?
Behold, heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain You. How much less this temple which I
have built!*

He says both.

## Where the resting place goes

*The Most High does not dwell in temples made with hands.* Stephen, quoting Isaiah, on trial.
See the note on Acts 6:8–11.

*Do you not know that **you are the temple of God** and that the Spirit of God dwells in you?*

And *in whom you also are being built together for a dwelling place of God in the Spirit*.

## The last word of the psalm

*"There I will make the horn of David grow; **I have prepared a lamp for My Anointed**. His
enemies I will clothe with shame, but upon Himself His crown shall flourish."*

A lamp, prepared, for the Messiah.

The promise that runs through Kings whenever a bad king reigns: *the LORD would not destroy
Judah, for the sake of His servant David, **as He promised him to give a lamp to him and his
sons forever**.*

> **The reason given, twice.** *I have desired it.* Nothing about the hill, and nothing about
> the man who asked — He says He wanted it, and says it in both verses.
