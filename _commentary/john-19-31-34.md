John records a piece of Roman procedure and one thing that did not go according to it.

## Why they were in a hurry

*It was **the day of preparation**, and the Jewish leaders did not want the bodies left on
the crosses through the Sabbath, **since that Sabbath was a high holy day**.*

Passover Sabbath. Deuteronomy required a hanged body to be taken down the same day —
*his body shall not remain overnight on the tree… for he who is hanged is accursed of
God.*

The men who arranged the execution are concerned about ceremonial defilement.

## The procedure

*So they asked Pilate to have **the legs of the men broken** and the bodies taken away.*

The **crurifragium** — the legs smashed with an iron mallet. It killed by removing the
ability to push up to breathe, and it was the standard way of finishing a crucifixion
quickly.

## The two

*The soldiers came and did this to **the first man, and to the other one** crucified with
Him.*

Both robbers. The soldiers work along the row.

## The exception

*But when they came to Jesus and **saw that He was already dead, they did not break His
legs**.*

They stop because the procedure is unnecessary, not out of any regard.

## Why John records it

Two verses on, he says: *for these things were done that the Scripture should be
fulfilled, "**Not one of His bones shall be broken**."*

That is the Passover law from Exodus 12 — of the lamb: *nor shall you break one of its
bones*.

And Psalm 34: *He guards all his bones; not one of them is broken.*

## The spear

*Instead, **one of the soldiers pierced His side with a spear**.*

The Greek is **enuxen** — jabbed, pierced. A professional's check.

## What came out

*And **at once blood and water came out**.*

John gives it as an observation, and in the next verse insists on his own presence: *and
he who has seen has testified, and his testimony is true; and he knows that he is telling
the truth, so that you may believe.*

He stops the narrative to swear to it.

## The second Scripture

*And again another Scripture says, "**They shall look on Him whom they pierced**."*

Zechariah 12, and Revelation quotes it too.

## The rock that was struck

*Strike the rock, and water will pour out for the people to drink*, and God's own clause:
*I myself will stand there before you, on the rock.* See the note on Exodus 17:4–7.

Paul's identification: *that Rock was Christ.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 10:1–5.

## What John writes later

*This is He who came **by water and blood** — Jesus Christ; not only by water, but by
water and blood.*

He returns to the pair in his letter, decades on.

## What happens next in the chapter

Joseph of Arimathea, a secret disciple, asks Pilate for the body. Nicodemus comes with a
hundred pounds of myrrh and aloes — the man who first came at night.

Both of them come out into the open once He is dead.

> **What the soldiers decided.** Not to break the legs, because there was no point — and
> John says a Passover regulation about a lamb's bones was kept by a Roman working party
> who had never heard of it.
