Paul picks the veil back up, and this time it is over somebody else.

## Where it comes from

Chapter 3 ended with Moses' veil, the hardened minds, and the covering that is still there
when the old covenant is read aloud. See the note on 2 Corinthians 3:12–15.

Then: *therefore, since we have this ministry… **we have renounced the hidden things of
shame**, not walking in craftiness nor handling the word of God deceitfully.*

He has just said his own preaching hides nothing. This paragraph answers the obvious
objection — then why does anybody miss it?

## The concession

*And **if the good news we bring is veiled at all**.*

He allows the possibility rather than denying it.

## Where the veil is

*It is veiled **among those who are perishing**.*

Not over the message. Over an audience.

The Greek is a present participle — *those who are perishing*, in progress. Paul used the
same pair in the first letter: *the message of the cross is foolishness to **those who are
perishing**, but to us who **are being saved** it is the power of God.* See the note on
1 Corinthians 1:18–25.

## Who did it

*Whose minds **the god of this present age has blinded**.*

The Greek is **etuphlosen** — blinded, from the word for blind.

**The god of this age** — Paul's phrase, used once. John's Gospel has *the ruler of this
world*, three times.

The blinding is described as an act by an agent, and the organ is the mind.

## What is being blocked

*So that **the light streaming from the good news of the glory of Christ** will not dawn on
them.*

The Greek is **augasai** — to shine upon, to dawn.

What is prevented is not information arriving. It is light dawning.

## Who Christ is called

*Christ, **who is the image of God**.*

The Greek is **eikon** — image, likeness, exact representation.

Paul uses it in Colossians: *He is the image of the invisible God*. And Hebrews: *the
express image of His person*.

## What Paul says he does about it

The next verse: *for **we do not preach ourselves, but Christ Jesus the Lord**, and
ourselves your bondservants for Jesus' sake.*

## The answer to the blindness

*For it is the God who commanded light to shine out of darkness who has **shone in our
hearts** to give **the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus
Christ**.*

Paul reaches for Genesis 1 — *let there be light* — and says the same command was issued
into a heart.

The remedy for a blinding is a creation act, and it is described in exactly those terms.

## The vessel

*But **we have this treasure in earthen vessels**, that the excellence of the power may be
of God and not of us.*

Two verses later, and it is one of the sharpest sentences in his letters.

## What the chapter is like

*Hard-pressed on every side, yet not crushed; perplexed, but not in despair; persecuted,
but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed.*

Four pairs, and the whole chapter ends *the things which are seen are temporary, but the
things which are not seen are eternal.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 4:16–18.

> **Where Paul locates the difficulty.** Not in the message and not in the preaching — he
> has just said he hides nothing. In an audience, and in something done to their minds
> — and the remedy he names is the sentence God spoke over the dark on the first day.
