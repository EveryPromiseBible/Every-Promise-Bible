Four things that keep moving and never arrive anywhere.

## The generations

*One generation walks off the stage, and another walks on. **But the earth stays where it is,
unmoved, always**.*

The Hebrew is **le'olam omadet** — stands forever.

People are the transient element. The ground they walk on is not.

Qoheleth begins his survey by inverting what we assume.

## The sun

*The sun rises, **tired and faithful**. The sun sets, and **hurries back to the place it will rise
from again**.*

The Hebrew is **sho'ef** — panting, gasping. The word for hard breathing.

It runs all day, and then runs back overnight to the starting line to do it again.

## The wind

*Round and round it goes, **turning and returning, never finished, never still**.*

The Hebrew piles the same root up: **sovev sovev**, going around and going around.

South, north, around, back. Motion in every direction, and no progress in any.

## The rivers

*Every river runs to the sea, **yet the sea is never full**.*

*And the water goes back to the place the rivers began, so the running can start all over again.*

An observation about the water cycle, made in Hebrew poetry, in a book about futility.

## The verdict on all four

*Everything is weary. **More weary than anyone can say**.*

The Hebrew is **yege'im** — wearied, exhausted. Applied to things.

## The senses

*The eye looks and looks and **is never satisfied with seeing**. The ear listens and listens and
**is never filled with hearing**.*

Two organs designed to take things in, and neither reaches capacity.

The sea will not fill, and neither will the eye. Qoheleth puts them in the same paragraph.

## The famous line

*Whatever has been is what will be. Whatever has been done is what will be done. **There is
nothing new under the sun**.*

The Hebrew is **eyn kol chadash tachat hashamesh**.

*Under the sun* again — the frame of the whole investigation. See the note on
Ecclesiastes 1:1–3.

## The challenge

*Is there anything of which someone says, "**Look at this, it is new**"? **It was already here,
long before us**, in the ages that came and went.*

He anticipates the objection and answers it: you only think it is new because you were not there
before.

## The last cut

*No one remembers the people who lived before. And **the people yet to come will not be remembered
either** by those who come after them.*

Not just that achievement fades. That the achievers do.

This is the sharpest sentence in the section, and it is aimed at the one consolation the ancient
world had — being remembered.

## What is new

Everything Qoheleth says is true of what is *under the sun*, which is the boundary he set
himself.

*Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, **he is a new creation**; old things have passed away.* See
the note on 2 Corinthians 5:16–17.

*Behold, **I make all things new**.* See the note on Revelation 21:5–8.

The word Qoheleth searched the world for and could not find, spoken from the throne.

## What is remembered

*The righteous will be **in everlasting remembrance***.

*I will write on him **the name of My God**… and **My new name***. See the note on
Revelation 3:12–13.

Qoheleth's last complaint is that nobody is remembered, and the answer offered is a name written
down.

## What he does with the observation

Not despair. Three chapters later: *there is a time for everything*, and *He has made everything
beautiful in its time*. See the note on Ecclesiastes 3:9–15.

The circling is not denied. It is put under someone's hand.

> **The sun's day.** **Sho'ef** — panting. It runs all day, and then runs back overnight to the
> line it started from, to do it again tomorrow.
