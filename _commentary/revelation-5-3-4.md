The only weeping in heaven recorded in Scripture, and it lasts two verses.

## What is in the hand

*And I saw in the right hand of Him who sat on the throne **a scroll written inside and on the back,
sealed with seven seals**.*

The Greek is **biblion gegrammenon esothen kai opisthen** — a book written within and on the back.

A Roman will was sealed with seven seals, and opened only in the presence of the heirs.

## The question

*Then I saw a strong angel proclaiming with a loud voice, "**Who is worthy to open the scroll and to
loose its seals?**"*

The Greek is **tis axios** — who is worthy.

Not who is able. Who has the right.

## The search

*And **nobody could**. Not in heaven, not on the earth, not under the earth.*

Three realms. The whole of creation, searched.

## The double negative

*There was **no one able to open it, no one even able to look inside it**.*

The Greek is **oudeis edunato… oude blepein auto**.

Not only could nobody open it. Nobody could even look.

## The weeping

*So **I cried**. I stood there and **cried hard**.*

The Greek is **eklaion polu** — I was weeping much. Imperfect: he kept crying.

**Klaio** — the strong word for weeping aloud. The verb used of Peter after the denial and of
Jesus at Lazarus's tomb.

## Why he weeps

*Because **no one had been found who deserved to open the scroll**, or read it, or so much as look at
it.*

## What the scroll is

The book does not say directly.

What is clear is what happens when it opens: the seals are broken one at a time and history moves.
It is the document by which everything else in the book proceeds.

If it stays shut, nothing happens.

## What John understands

That an unopened scroll means an unfinished story.

He is on Patmos, the churches are under pressure, and the sealed document in the throne room is
the only thing that would resolve any of it.

## The interruption

*But **one of the elders said to me, "Do not weep. Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root
of David, has prevailed to open the scroll**."*

The Greek is **enikesen** — has conquered. Aorist.

## What he sees when he looks

*And I looked, and behold, in the midst of the throne… **stood a Lamb as though it had been slain**.*
See the note on Revelation 5:5–6.

He is told to look at a Lion and sees a Lamb, standing, with the marks of slaughter on it.

## How the conquering was done

Not by the Lion's method.

*"You are worthy… for **You were slain, and have redeemed us to God by Your blood** out of every
tribe and tongue and people and nation."*

## What the room does

*And they sang a new song… **and the number of them was ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands
of thousands**.*

*"Worthy is the Lamb who was slain."*

## Where the weeping ends up

*And **God will wipe away every tear from their eyes**; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow, nor
crying.* See the note on Revelation 21:3–4.

> **The two verses.** The only recorded weeping in heaven, and it is stopped by an elder pointing
> at a Lion — and what John turns and sees is a slaughtered Lamb standing up.
