The one recorded occasion **Jesus** tries to take His disciples on holiday, and it does not work.

## What they had just done

*"The apostles came back to **Jesus** and told Him everything — **all they had done and all they had
taught**."*

They have been out two by two with authority over unclean spirits, and this is the debrief. It is
also the only place in **Mark** the twelve are called *apostles* — **apostoloi**, the sent ones,
which is the point at that moment.

## The invitation

*"**Come away with Me, just you and Me, off to a quiet place, and get some rest.**"*

The Greek is **deute hymeis autoi kat' idian eis eremon topon kai anapauesthe**.

Three things in it. **Come away with Me** — the destination is company before it is a place.
**By yourselves** — privately. And **anapauesthe**, rest, the present imperative: keep resting.

*"Come to Me, all you who labour and are heavy laden, and **I will give you rest**."*

**Matthew** 11's word is **anapauso** — the same verb, in the future, with Him as the subject.

## The reason given

*"So many people were coming and going that **they had not even had a chance to eat**."*

Not that they had failed, and not that they had sinned. They were busy, and it was good work, and
He calls a halt to it.

*And on the seventh day God ended His work which He had done, and **He rested** on the seventh day.*

*There remains therefore **a rest for the people of God**.*

## What happens

*"But the crowds saw them go… and they came running on foot from all the towns and **got there ahead
of them**."*

The retreat is discovered and outrun. They arrive at the lonely place to find it full.

## What He does about it

*"When Jesus stepped ashore and saw that great crowd waiting for Him, **something moved in the
deepest part of Him**."*

The Greek is **esplanchnisthe** — from **splanchna**, the inward organs. It is a physical word and
it is used of **Jesus** about a dozen times in the gospels and almost nowhere else.

It is the verb in the parable of the prodigal — *his father saw him and had compassion* — and in the
Samaritan, and in the king who cancels the debt. Three parables, and in each one it is the hinge.

## What He saw

*"They looked to Him like **sheep with no shepherd anywhere**."*

The phrase is Moses', asking God for a successor: *let the LORD set a man over the congregation…
that the congregation of the LORD may not be like sheep which have no shepherd.*

And Ezekiel's: *My sheep wandered through all the mountains… and no one was seeking or searching for
them.*

*"**I am the good shepherd.** The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep."*

## What He does with His day off

*"So He began to teach them, and **He taught them many things**."*

And then, at the end of the same afternoon, He feeds all of them. See the note on
Mark 6:35–41.

The rest He offered is not cancelled by this — **Mark** has Him withdrawing to pray repeatedly, and
the next paragraph has Him going up a mountain alone. But on this occasion the crowd got there
first, and what He felt when He saw them decided the day.

> **The invitation.** *Come away with Me* — the destination named first is a Person, and the reason
> given is that they had not eaten. Then the crowd outran the boat, and the verb Mark uses for what
> happened in Him is the one from the prodigal's father.
