Two verses between a murder and a miracle.

## What He had heard

*When Jesus **heard it**.*

The verses before: Herod's birthday, the dancing girl, the request put up to her by her
mother, and John the Baptist beheaded in prison.

*And his disciples came and took away the body and buried it, **and went and told Jesus**.*

That is what He heard.

## Who John was to Him

His cousin. The man who baptised Him and said *behold the Lamb of God*.

He had said of him: *among those born of women there has not risen one greater than John
the Baptist.* See the note on Matthew 11:12–15.

## What He did

*He **withdrew from that place by boat to a quiet spot, to be alone**.*

The Greek is **anechoresen** — withdrew, retired. And **kat' idian**, privately.

He goes looking for a deserted place.

## What happened instead

*But **the crowds heard where He was going and followed Him on foot from the towns**.*

They walk round the lake and get there first. Mark says *they ran there on foot from all
the cities, arrived before them, and came together to Him*.

He lands and the place is full.

## What He saw

*And when He came ashore and **saw the great crowd waiting**.*

The Greek is *coming out, He saw a great multitude*.

## What moved

*His **heart went out to them**.*

The Greek is **esplanchnisthe** — from **splanchna**, the inward parts. The gut-word.

The same verb Matthew uses of Him looking at the crowds in chapter 9. See the note on
Matthew 9:35–38.

## What He did about it

*And **He healed their sick**.*

## What is not in the passage

Any note that this was difficult, that He was owed the afternoon, or that the crowd was
inconsiderate.

Matthew records the news, the withdrawal, the crowd arriving first, and the compassion, in
that order, and offers no comment on the sequence.

## What follows

*When it was evening, His disciples came to Him, saying, "This is a deserted place, and the
hour is already late. **Send the multitudes away**."*

*But Jesus said to them, "**They do not need to go away. You give them something to
eat**."*

Five loaves, two fish, five thousand men besides women and children, twelve baskets over.

It is the only miracle in all four Gospels.

## And after that

*Immediately Jesus made His disciples get into the boat and go before Him to the other
side, while He sent the multitudes away.*

*And when He had sent the multitudes away, **He went up on the mountain by Himself to
pray**.*

He gets the solitude at the end of the day, after everyone has been fed — and then walks
out on the water to the boat.

> **The order of the paragraph.** He hears his cousin has been executed, gets in a boat to
> be alone, finds the shore crowded when he lands, and the word Matthew uses for what
> happened next is the one for being moved in the gut.
