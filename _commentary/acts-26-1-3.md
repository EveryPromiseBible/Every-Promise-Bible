Paul's last recorded defence, and he opens by saying he is glad to be there.

## Where he is

Caesarea, in the audience hall, two years into custody.

Festus has just taken over from Felix and inherited a case he cannot make sense of. He says so
to Agrippa: *I have nothing certain to write to my lord concerning him… **for it seems to me
unreasonable to send a prisoner and not to specify the charges against him***.

## Who is listening

*Agrippa* — Herod Agrippa II, the last of the Herods. Great-grandson of the Herod who killed the
children at Bethlehem, son of the Herod who executed James and was eaten by worms.

His sister Bernice is with him. And Festus, the governor, and *the commanders and the prominent
men of the city*.

Luke had noted the entrance: *with great pomp*.

## The permission

*"**You have permission to speak for yourself**."*

## The gesture

*Then Paul **stretched out his hand** and began his defense.*

The Greek is **ekteinas ten cheira** — the orator's posture.

He is in chains. Luke mentions them at the end of the chapter and Paul refers to them himself.

## The opening

*"King Agrippa, **I count myself fortunate** that it is before you I am to make my defense
today."*

The Greek is **hegemai emauton makarion** — I consider myself blessed, happy.

**Makarios** is the beatitude word. *Blessed are the poor in spirit.*

A prisoner, two years without trial, in chains, opening with *I count myself blessed*.

## The reason he gives

*"Above all because **you are so well acquainted with all the customs and questions that occupy
the Jews**."*

Agrippa had the right to appoint the high priest and oversaw the temple treasury. He knew the
material.

Paul is glad of an audience who will follow the argument. That is the whole reason.

## The request

*"So I ask you to **hear me patiently**."*

The Greek is **makrothumos** — with longsuffering.

## What he does with the hearing

Tells his own story. Brought up in Jerusalem, a Pharisee, *the strictest sect of our religion*.

*"I myself thought I must do many things contrary to the name of Jesus of Nazareth… **and being
exceedingly enraged against them, I persecuted them even to foreign cities**."*

And then the road, the light, the voice, and the Aramaic sentence: *Saul, Saul, why are you
persecuting Me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads*.

## The commission he reports

*"To open their eyes, in order to **turn them from darkness to light**, and from the power of
Satan to God, that they may receive forgiveness of sins and an inheritance among those who are
sanctified by faith in Me."*

## The interruption

*"**Paul, you are beside yourself! Much learning is driving you mad!**"*

Festus, shouting.

And Paul's answer: *I am not mad, most noble Festus, but speak the words of truth and reason*.

## Where it ends

*"You **almost persuade me** to become a Christian."* See the note on
Acts 26:27–29.

## What the verdict was

*"This man is doing nothing deserving of death or chains."*

*And Agrippa said to Festus, "**This man might have been set free if he had not appealed to
Caesar**."*

Innocent, and going to Rome anyway — which is where he had been told he was going.

> **How he opens.** *I count myself blessed.* Two years in custody, in chains, before a Herod —
> and the reason he gives is that this listener will understand the argument.
