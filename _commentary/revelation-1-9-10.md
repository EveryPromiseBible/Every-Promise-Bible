John introduces himself, and every word in the introduction is levelling.

## The name

*I, John.*

No title. Not apostle, not elder, not the disciple whom Jesus loved.

The book that contains the most extraordinary material in Scripture is signed by a man who
declines every credential he has.

## The relation

*Your **brother and companion**.*

The Greek is **adelphos kai sunkoinonos** — brother and fellow-sharer.

**Sunkoinonos** — a partner, somebody in on the same thing. From **koinonia**.

## What they share

*In **the tribulation and kingdom and patient endurance** which are in Jesus Christ.*

Three nouns under one article in the Greek, which binds them into a single condition.

Tribulation, kingdom, and endurance — held together rather than sequenced. Not *first the
tribulation, later the kingdom*. All three, now.

**Hupomone** — remaining under.

## The place

*Was on the island that is called **Patmos**.*

A rocky island in the Aegean, about ten miles by six, used by Rome as a place of banishment.

## The reason

*For **the word of God and for the testimony of Jesus Christ**.*

The same phrase he uses of the souls under the altar in chapter 6.

He does not say he was arrested unjustly, and he does not complain.

## The day

*I was **in the Spirit on the Lord's Day**.*

The Greek is **en te kuriake hemera**.

The earliest surviving use of the phrase for Sunday. Within a generation it is standard —
Ignatius and the Didache both use it.

## The state

*In the Spirit.*

The Greek is **en pneumati**.

John uses it four times in the book, and each marks a major shift: here, at the throne room in
chapter 4, in the wilderness in chapter 17, and on the great high mountain in chapter 21.

## The sound

*And I heard behind me **a loud voice, as of a trumpet**.*

Behind him. He has to turn to see who is speaking.

## What he turns to see

*Then I turned to see the voice that spoke with me. And having turned I saw **seven golden
lampstands**, and in the midst of the seven lampstands **One like the Son of Man**.* See the
note on Revelation 1:12–13.

## What he does

*And when I saw Him, **I fell at His feet as dead**.*

The man who had leaned on His chest at supper. See the note on John 13:21–26.

*But He laid His right hand on me, saying to me, "**Do not be afraid; I am the First and the
Last. I am He who lives, and was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore**."*

## The instruction

*"Write the things which you have seen, and the things which are, and the things which will take
place after this."*

Three tenses, and the book divides along them.

## Who is writing

An old man on a prison island, describing himself as a fellow-sufferer before he describes
anything else.

Everything in the next twenty-one chapters — the throne, the seals, the trumpets, the dragon,
the city — is written by somebody in exile who introduces himself as *your brother*.

> **What he leaves out.** Every title he has. The book with the most authority claimed in it is
> signed *your brother and companion in the tribulation*.
