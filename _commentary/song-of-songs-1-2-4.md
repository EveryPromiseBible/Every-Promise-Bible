The book opens in the middle of a sentence, and the first voice is hers.

## The opening

*Kiss me, **kiss me with the kisses of your mouth**.*

The Hebrew is **yishaqeni minneshiqot pihu** — let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth.

The Hebrew begins in the third person — *let him* — and shifts to the second by the end of the
verse. The book does this repeatedly.

## Who speaks first

She does.

The Song is unusual in ancient literature for how much of it is in a woman's voice. She speaks
more lines than he does.

## The comparison

*For **your love is sweeter than wine**.*

The Hebrew is **tovim dodeicha miyyayin** — better are your loves than wine.

Wine is the standard of pleasure. The comparison recurs in the book, and he repeats it back to
her later: *how much better than wine*. See the note on Song of Songs 4:9–11.

## The scent

*The scent you carry is **a gift poured out**.*

The Hebrew is **lereach shemaneicha tovim** — for the fragrance of your good oils.

## The name

*Your very name **a fragrance spilling wide**.*

The Hebrew is **shemen turaq shemecha** — your name is oil poured out.

**Shemen** — oil. **Shem** — name. A pun the English cannot carry.

Poured-out oil fills a room. A stoppered jar does not.

## What it produces

*And **this is why the young women love you**.*

**Alamot** — young women. The same root as **almah**, the word in Isaiah's sign. See the note on
Matthew 1:22–23.

Her love is not exclusive of others'. She notes that everyone else feels the same and does not
treat it as competition.

## The invitation

*Take my hand and **let us run**.*

The Hebrew is **mashcheni acharecha narutzah** — draw me after you, let us run.

Two verbs, one singular and one plural. He draws; they run.

The drawing is his. The running is joint.

## The room

*Bring me into your rooms, my king.*

The Hebrew is **chadarav** — his chambers.

**Cheder** — the innermost room. The same noun in Proverbs, of what the lamp of the LORD
searches. See the note on Proverbs 20:23–30.

## The title

**Hammelek** — the king.

The book is attributed to Solomon in the first verse, and he is called king throughout.

## What she says next

*I am dark, but lovely… **do not look upon me, because I am dark, because the sun has tanned me***.

*My mother's sons were angry with me; **they made me the keeper of the vineyards, but my own
vineyard I have not kept***.

The first thing she says about herself is an apology for her appearance, and the reason is that
she was made to work outdoors.

## What he says about that

*I have compared you, my love, to my filly among Pharaoh's chariots.*

*Behold, you are fair, my love! Behold, you are fair!*

He does not agree with her assessment and does not argue with it. He states his own.

## What the book is

Poetry in two voices, with a chorus, and no narrator to explain.

The word **love** appears throughout; the word **God** does not appear once in the Hebrew text.

## The drawing

*"**No one can come to Me unless the Father who sent Me draws him**."*

*"And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, **will draw all peoples to Myself**."* See the note on
John 12:32–33.

> **The pun.** **Shemen** and **shem** — oil and name. A name like oil poured out, which is a way
> of saying it does not stay in the jar.
