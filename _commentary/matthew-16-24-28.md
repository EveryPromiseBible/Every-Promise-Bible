Said immediately after Peter is called Satan, and the two are connected.

## What has just happened

Caesarea Philippi. *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God* — and *blessed are you,
Simon Bar-Jonah, for flesh and blood has not revealed this to you*.

And then the first passion prediction, and Peter taking Him aside: *far be it from You, Lord;
this shall not happen to You!*

*"**Get behind Me, Satan!** You are an offence to Me, for you are not mindful of the things of
God but the things of men."*

The same man, in the same conversation, gets the highest commendation and the sharpest rebuke
in the Gospel.

## The condition

*"**If anyone wants to come after Me**."*

The Greek is **ei tis thelei** — if anyone wills.

Nothing compulsory. It is stated as a choice, and the terms are given before the choosing.

## The first

*"He must **let go of himself**."*

The Greek is **aparnesastho heauton** — deny himself, disown himself.

It is the same verb used of Peter in the courtyard a few chapters later: *he began to curse and
swear, "I do not know the man"*.

Peter denied Christ. The instruction is to do that to yourself instead.

## The second

*"**Take up his cross**."*

Not a hardship or an illness. In that world a man carrying a crossbeam through the streets was
going one place, and everybody watching knew it.

Rome had crucified thousands in Galilee within living memory. The image had no metaphorical
softness in it.

## The third

*"And **follow Me**."*

Present imperative — keep following.

## The paradox

*"For **whoever wants to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for My sake will
find it**."*

The Greek word is **psuche** — life, soul, self. The same word in both halves.

Jesus says a version of this more often than almost anything else. It appears in all four
Gospels, and Matthew records it twice.

## The question

*"**What good is it to a man if he gains the whole world and loses his own soul?**"*

The Greek is **zemiothe** — be fined, suffer loss, forfeit.

A commercial word. The whole verse is an accountant's question about a trade.

*"Or **what could a man ever give in exchange for his soul?**"*

**Antallagma** — an exchange, the price paid.

The second question answers the first. If you have already traded it away, there is nothing
left to buy it back with.

## What the world had just been offered for

*"All of this I will give You, if You will fall down and worship me."* See the note on
Matthew 4:8–11.

The whole world, for one act. He had refused it, and now He asks what it would be worth.

## The return

*"For the Son of Man is going to **come in the glory of His Father with His angels**, and then
He will repay each person according to what he has done."*

## The last verse

*"There are some standing here who **will not taste death before they see the Son of Man coming
in His kingdom**."*

The hardest sentence in the chapter, and Matthew places what follows six days later:

*Jesus took Peter, James, and John his brother, led them up on a high mountain by themselves,
**and He was transfigured before them**.*

The chapter break is a printer's decision. Peter himself makes the connection in his letter:
*we were eyewitnesses of His majesty… when we were with Him on the holy mountain*.

> **The verb.** Deny — the same one used of Peter in the courtyard. He denied Christ; the
> instruction here is to do that to yourself.
