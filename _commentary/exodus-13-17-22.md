The first navigational decision of the exodus, and the reason given is about what they
could take.

## The road not taken

*God **did not lead them along the road through Philistine country**, though it was **the
shorter way**.*

The coast road — the Via Maris — ran from Egypt up through Gaza into Canaan. It was the
trade route and the army route, and it was about ten days' walk.

Moses notes that it was shorter. The text does not pretend the long way was more
efficient.

## The reason

*For God said, "**If they face war, they may lose heart and turn back to Egypt**."*

The Hebrew is *lest the people change their minds when they see war*.

The short road was garrisoned by Egyptian forts and then by Philistine cities. They would
have hit an army in the first week.

## What the reason is about

Not the danger. Their readiness for it.

The decision is made about people who have been slaves for four hundred years and have
never fought anything.

## The route

*So God led the people **the roundabout way, through the wilderness toward the Red Sea**.*

The Hebrew is a verb for turning aside, going about.

The long way ends at a sea with an army behind them — which is where the deliverance
happens, and where Exodus 14 says *the Egyptians whom you see today, you shall see again no
more forever*.

## Their condition

*The Israelites went up out of Egypt **ready for battle**.*

The Hebrew is *in orderly ranks*, or *armed*. They were not defenceless.

They were equipped and God routed them around the fight anyway.

## The bones

*Moses **carried the bones of Joseph** with him.*

*For Joseph had made the Israelites swear an oath: "**God will surely come to you — carry my
bones up with you from here**."*

Four hundred years earlier, the second man in Egypt gave instructions about a coffin. See
the note on Genesis 50:15–21.

Somebody kept it, moved it, and knew where it was on the night they left. Joshua buries it
at Shechem at the end of the conquest.

## The guide

*And here is the wonder of it: **the LORD Himself went ahead of them**.*

*By day in **a pillar of cloud** to lead the way, and by night in **a pillar of fire** to give
light — **so they could travel by day or by night**.*

Two forms, one presence.

The cloud is shade in a desert and the fire is light in the dark. Each is what the hour
needs.

## The constancy

*Neither the pillar of cloud by day nor the pillar of fire by night **ever left its place in
front of the people**.*

Moses says it as a summary, and it holds for forty years — Nehemiah still mentions it
centuries later: *You did not forsake them… the pillar of cloud did not depart from them by
day.*

## What Deuteronomy says about the forty years

*He went ahead of you on the journey, **searching out a place for you to camp**, in fire by
night and in a cloud by day, **to show you the way to walk**.* See the note on
Deuteronomy 1:26–33.

## What the passage is for

The shortest route was available, known, and rejected — and the sentence Moses records is
about the people's hearts rather than the road's condition.

> **Why the long way.** Not because the short one was blocked. Moses says God looked at
> what was on it and at who was walking, and turned them.
