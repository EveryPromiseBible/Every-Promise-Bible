The examination at the end of a three-year training programme, and four foreigners come top.

## What has happened

Four young men from Judah's nobility, taken to Babylon, renamed, and enrolled in a three-year
course in *the language and literature of the Chaldeans*.

They asked to be excused the king's food and wine, and were tested on vegetables and water for ten
days.

*And at the end of ten days their features appeared better and fatter in flesh than all the young
men who ate the portion of the king's delicacies.* See the note on Daniel 1:12–16.

## The gift

*As for these four young men, **God gave them knowledge and skill in all literature and wisdom**; and
Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams.* See the note on Daniel 1:17.

The narrator says where the ability came from before the examination happens.

## The presentation

*When the training days were over, **the chief official brought them all in before Nebuchadnezzar**.*

Ashpenaz, the man who had been afraid to grant the diet request — *I fear my lord the king… why
should he see your faces looking worse?*

## The interview

*The king **talked with them**.*

The Hebrew is **vaydabber ittam hammelek** — and the king spoke with them.

Nebuchadnezzar conducts it himself.

## The result

*And among all of them **not one was found like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah**.*

Their Hebrew names, which the narrator uses even after recording the Babylonian ones —
Belteshazzar, Shadrach, Meshach, Abed-Nego.

The book alternates, and here it uses the ones their parents gave them.

## What the names mean

**Daniel** — God is my judge. **Hananiah** — the LORD is gracious. **Mishael** — who is what God is.
**Azariah** — the LORD has helped.

Every one of them carries the divine name. The Babylonian replacements carry Bel and Nebo.

## The appointment

*So they **took their place, standing before the king**.*

The Hebrew is **vayya'amdu liphnei hammelek** — and they stood before the king.

The idiom for entering royal service.

## The margin

*And in every matter of wisdom and understanding the king asked them about, he found them **ten
times better than all the magicians and enchanters in his whole kingdom**.*

The Hebrew is **eser yadot** — ten hands, ten times.

## Who they beat

**Chartummim** — magicians, the priestly scribes. And **ashshaphim** — enchanters, conjurers.

The professional class of Babylonian learning, in the world's leading centre of it.

## Where that matters later

Chapter 2. Nebuchadnezzar dreams and refuses to tell anybody what he dreamed.

*"The thing that the king requires is difficult, and **there is no other who can tell it to the king
except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh**."*

The whole class is sentenced to death, and Daniel asks for time.

*"But **there is a God in heaven who reveals secrets**."* See the note on Daniel 2:27–30.

## What is happening in the background

Chapter 1 is a school report. Chapters 2 to 6 are what the graduates do.

The furnace, the writing on the wall, the lions' den — all of it runs from four teenagers who
asked about the menu.

## The last verse of the chapter

*Thus Daniel continued **until the first year of King Cyrus**.*

Sixty-odd years. He outlasts the empire that deported him.

> **The order.** The narrator states in verse 17 that God gave them the ability, and only then
> reports the examination result. The margin is ten times, in Babylon's own subject.
