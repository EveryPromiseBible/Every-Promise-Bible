The promise Peter reached for on the day it happened.

## "And afterward"

The Hebrew is *and it shall come to pass afterward*.

After what: the chapter before this is the locust plague and the call to return —
*rend your heart and not your garments*.

And then the restoration: *I will restore to you the years that the swarming locust has
eaten*, and *you shall eat in plenty and be satisfied*.

The pouring out comes after the restoring.

## The verb

*I will **pour out** My Spirit.*

The Hebrew is **shaphak** — to pour, to spill, the word used of blood being poured out at
the altar.

Not a portion measured out. A vessel emptied.

## Who it lands on

*On **all people**.*

The Hebrew is *all flesh* — **kol basar**.

Under the old arrangement the Spirit came on individuals for tasks: Bezalel for the
tabernacle, the seventy elders, judges, kings, prophets. Named men, for jobs.

Moses had wished for this: *oh, that all the LORD's people were prophets, that the LORD
would put His Spirit upon them!*

## The four groups

- *Your **sons and your daughters** will prophesy*
- *Your **old men** will dream dreams*
- *Your **young men** will see visions*
- *Even on **the servants, both men and women***

Male and female, old and young, free and slave. Every division that mattered in that
society appears and is crossed.

## The servants

*Even on the servants, **both men and women**.*

The Hebrew is **avadim** and **shephachot** — male and female slaves.

Joel names them last and separately, which puts the emphasis on them.

## What is not in the list

Priests. Levites. The temple. Any qualification at all.

## Where Peter uses it

Standing in front of a crowd in Jerusalem at nine in the morning, dealing with a
heckle: *no, **this is what was spoken of through the prophet Joel**.* See the notes on
Acts 2:14–16 and Acts 2:17–18.

He quotes the whole passage, including the wonders and the blood and fire and smoke, and
ends on the last line: *whoever calls on the name of the LORD shall be saved.*

## The change Peter makes

Joel has *afterward*. Acts has *in the last days*. Peter says which afterward it was.

## What Paul says about the divisions

*There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is neither male
nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus.*

The same three pairs Joel crosses.

> **What is unusual about the promise.** Not the Spirit — Israel knew about that. That
> the list of recipients has no qualification in it, and that Joel names the slaves
> twice.
