The phrase everybody knows, in a chapter about shepherds who fed themselves
instead of the sheep.

## What the chapter is

Ezekiel 34 is God's charge against Israel's leaders: you eat the fat, you clothe
yourselves with the wool, you slaughter the fattened animals, and you do not feed
the flock. The weak were not strengthened, the sick not healed, the lost not
sought.

Then: *I Myself will search for My sheep.* God takes the job over personally, and
these verses describe the country afterwards.

## The covenant of peace

*I will make with them a covenant of peace and rid the land of wild beasts, so
that they may live safely in the wilderness and sleep in the forests.*

Safety in the two places nobody was safe. The wilderness and the forest were
where you were most exposed — and the promise is that they will sleep there.

## The showers

*I will send down showers in their season. There will be showers of blessing.*

The phrase is literally *showers of blessing*, and Ezekiel keeps the two rains
framework of Deuteronomy — *in their season*.

Note where the blessing is aimed: *I will make them and the places around My hill
a blessing.* The people are made into one before the rain is mentioned. The
showers land on ground that has already been described as a blessing to its
neighbours.

## The purpose stated at the end

*And they will know that I am the LORD, when I break the bars of their yoke and
rescue them from the hands of those who enslaved them.*

Not primarily so the crops come in. So that they know who did it.

> **Who the shepherd turns out to be.** The chapter goes on: *I will place over
> them one shepherd, My servant David, and he will tend them.* Written centuries
> after David died. Jesus picks the whole chapter up in John 10 — the good
> shepherd, the sheep who were scattered, the hirelings who ran. See the note on
> John 10:7–10.
