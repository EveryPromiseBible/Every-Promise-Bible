Paul dates his calling to before he had done anything at all.

## When it happened

*God, who had **set me apart from my mother's womb** and called me **through His
grace**.*

Not from the Damascus road. From the womb.

The Greek for *set apart* is **aphorizo** — to mark off, to separate for a purpose.
It is the same root as *Pharisee*, which is what he had been, and he uses it here
of what God had done long before.

## What is excluded by the dating

Everything he had listed a few verses earlier: *I advanced in Judaism beyond many
of my contemporaries, being more exceedingly zealous for the traditions of my
fathers.*

And everything on the other side of it — *I persecuted the church of God beyond
measure and tried to destroy it.*

Neither the achievements nor the atrocities are the reason. He was set apart before
either existed, and the mechanism named is **grace**.

## Who else was told this

Jeremiah: *before I formed you in the womb I knew you; before you were born I
sanctified you; I ordained you a prophet to the nations.*

Isaiah: *the LORD has called Me from the womb; from the body of My mother He has
named Me.*

## "Was pleased"

*Was **pleased** to reveal His Son in me.*

The Greek is **eudokeo** — to take good pleasure in, to think it good. It is the
word the Father uses at the baptism: *in whom I am well pleased.*

The revealing was not a rescue operation forced on Him. It pleased Him.

## "In me"

Not *to me*. Paul says the Son was revealed **in** him.

## What it was for

*So that I might **announce Him among the nations**.*

The calling and the assignment arrive together. He is not given a revelation and
then left to find a use for it.

## What he did not do

*And the moment that happened, **I did not sit down and talk it over with flesh and
blood**.*

He did not go up to Jerusalem to the apostles. He went to Arabia, and came back to
Damascus, and it was three years before he saw Peter.

The whole point of the passage is where his gospel came from: *I neither received
it from man, nor was I taught it, but it came through the revelation of Jesus
Christ.*

> **What he had been on the way to do.** Carrying letters to Damascus to arrest
> people. The commission he was actually given that morning was *to open their eyes,
> to turn them from darkness to light* — and he had been set apart for it before he
> was born.
