---
key: Exodus 32:15–20
title: The tablets never reached the camp
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Moses comes down carrying something written by God, and it does not survive the walk.

## What he was carrying

*The two tablets of the covenant in his hands — **written on both sides, front and back**. They
were **the work of God**, and the writing was **God's own writing**, engraved on the stone.*

Said three times over in one verse. The stone was His work, the writing was His writing, and it
was cut into both faces so nothing could be added.

Nothing else in Scripture is described as written by God in His own hand. See the note on
Exodus 31:18.

## What Joshua heard

*"That sounds like **the roar of battle** in the camp."*

Joshua has been waiting somewhere on the mountain the whole forty days. He is a soldier, and he
reads the noise as fighting.

## What Moses heard

*"It's **not the sound of victory, and not the sound of defeat**. What I hear is **the sound of
singing**."*

Three kinds of shouting, and he can tell them apart. The camp is not under attack and is not
celebrating a win.

They are having a party.

## What he does

*As Moses came near the camp and **saw the calf and the dancing**, his anger blazed. He **threw
the tablets down and shattered them at the foot of the mountain**.*

Not in the camp. **At the foot of the mountain**, before he reached the people.

The law, in the only copy God ever wrote Himself, broke on the way down — and Israel never once
saw it whole.

## What that means

There was never a moment when the tablets God wrote stood intact in front of the people they
were written for.

By the time the ten commandments arrive in the camp at all, they are a second set, cut out of
stone Moses had to quarry himself: *cut two tablets like the first*. See the note on
Exodus 34:1–4.

## Where the second set went

Into a box.

*The ark of the covenant… in which were the golden pot that had the manna, Aaron's rod that
budded, and **the tablets of the covenant**.*

And over the box, a lid: *the mercy seat*, with the blood put on it once a year. See the note
on Exodus 25:17–22.

The commandments end up inside a chest with a blood-sprinkled cover between them and the God
who wrote them. That is where the law was kept, and that is the only arrangement under which
Israel survived having it.

## What he made them drink

*He took the calf, **burned it in the fire, ground it to powder, scattered it on the water, and
made the Israelites drink it**.*

Four verbs, and the destruction is total. Then he makes them swallow it.

Whatever they had made, they had to take back inside themselves.

## What the day cost

*And about **three thousand men** of the people fell that day.* See the note on
Exodus 32:25–29.

Three thousand, on the day the law came down.

*Then those who gladly received his word were baptized; and that day about **three thousand
souls were added** to them.*

The same figure, on the day the Spirit came down. Luke gives the number, and the two days run
in opposite directions.

> **Where they broke.** At the foot of the mountain, before he got to the camp. The only copy
> the finger of God ever wrote never reached the people it was addressed to.
