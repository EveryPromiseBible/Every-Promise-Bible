key: Psalms 40:1–3
title: He put a new song in my mouth
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Three verses of rescue, and the psalm they open is quoted in Hebrews about Christ.

## The waiting

*I **waited patiently** for the LORD.*

The Hebrew doubles the verb — **qavvoh qivvithi**, waiting I waited. It is not a description of
composure. It is a description of how long.

## What He did

*He turned to me and heard my cry. He lifted me out of **the pit of ruin**, out of **the miry
clay**. He set my feet **upon a rock** and made my steps firm.*

Four verbs and none of them is the psalmist's. Heard, lifted, set, established.

The Hebrew for *miry clay* is **tit hayyaven** — mud you sink in. There is nothing to push
against, which is the point of the image: a man in that pit cannot get purchase to climb.

*For **when we were still without strength**, in due time **Christ** died for the ungodly.*

## The new song

*He **put a new song in my mouth**, a song of praise to our God.*

Given, not composed. The psalm does not say he found words; it says they were put there.

*And they sang **a new song**, saying: "You are worthy… for You were slain, and have redeemed us
to God by Your blood."*

**Revelation** records what the new song turns out to be about, and it is not the rescue of the
singer.

## What it does to onlookers

*Many will see it and stand in awe **and put their trust in the LORD**.*

The rescue is public and the song is the evidence. Nobody is preached at in the verse; they
watch a man who was in a pit singing.

## Where the psalm goes

*Sacrifice and offering You did not desire; **My ears You have opened**… Then I said, "Behold, I
come; in the scroll of the book it is written of Me. **I delight to do Your will, O My God**."*

**Hebrews** quotes those verses of **Christ**, using the Greek Old Testament's rendering of the
opened ear — *a body You have prepared for Me*. See the note on Deuteronomy 15:12–18.

Psalm 40 begins with a man lifted out of a pit and ends with the words of the One who went into
it.

> **What was put there.** The song. He waited, was lifted, was set on a rock — and then the
> words were supplied too.
