The one parable Jesus explains in full, and He says it is the key to the rest.

## The rebuke

*"**You do not understand this story? Then how will you understand any of them?**"*

Mark alone keeps it.

This parable is treated as foundational. If the mechanism here is missed, none of the
others will work.

## What is being sown

*"**The farmer is sowing the word**."*

Not a message about morality or effort. The seed is the word, and everything that follows
describes what happens to it.

## The first soil

*"Some people are like **the seed along the path**: the word is sown, and **Satan comes right
away and snatches it out of them**."*

The Greek is **euthus** — immediately.

Path soil is not bad soil. It is soil that has been walked on until it is hard, and the
seed sits on top of it where the birds can see it.

## The second

*"Some are like **the seed on rocky ground**: **the moment they hear the word they welcome it
with joy**, but **it has no root in them and does not last**."*

The joy is real and Mark says so.

The problem is depth. Rocky ground in that country is a shelf of limestone under a thin
layer of soil — the seed germinates fast because the shelf holds the heat, and dies fast
because the root has nowhere to go.

*"So when **trouble or persecution comes because of the word**, they fall away at once."*

**Because of the word.** The pressure is named as arriving on account of the thing sown.

## The third

*"Others are like **the seed sown among thorns**: **they hear the word**, but…"*

Three things crowd in:

- *"**the worries of this life**"*
- *"**the pull of money**"* — the Greek is *the deceitfulness of riches*
- *"**the craving for other things**"*

Luke's version says *pleasures of life*; Matthew has the first two.

None of the three is a sin in itself. Worry, money, and wanting other things.

*"All crowd in and **choke the word**, and **it never comes to anything**."*

The Greek is **sumpnigousin** — choke, throttle, crowd out.

The plant is alive and produces nothing.

## The fourth

*"And some are like **the seed sown on good soil**: they **hear the word and welcome it**, and
**it bears fruit in them — thirty times what was sown, or sixty, or a hundred**."*

Two verbs: hear and welcome. The Greek is **paradechontai** — receive, accept.

## The yields

Three different figures, and all three are called good.

The good soil is not uniform. Mark keeps the range.

Normal yields in that agriculture ran to seven or eight times. Thirty is exceptional and a
hundred is extraordinary — the number Isaac reaped in a famine year. See the note on
Genesis 26:12–14.

## What all four have in common

The same seed and the same sower. Nothing in the parable varies except the ground.

## What the chapter says next

*"Is a lamp brought to be put under a basket or under a bed? Is it not to be set on a
lampstand?"*

And: *"**Take heed what you hear**. With the same measure you use, it will be measured to
you."*

Luke has *take heed **how** you hear*. Between them, both the content and the manner.

> **What varies in the story.** Not the seed. One sower, one word, four grounds — and the
> third one is choked by three things none of which are wrong in themselves.
