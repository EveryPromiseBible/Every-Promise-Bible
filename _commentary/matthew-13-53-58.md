The end of the parable chapter, and He goes home.

## The transition

*When Jesus had **finished these stories**, He moved on from there.*

Matthew's formula. He uses it five times, at the end of each of the five great discourses —
the Sermon on the Mount, the mission charge, the parables, the church discourse, and the
Olivet discourse.

Seven parables have just been told: the sower, the tares, the mustard seed, the leaven, the
treasure, the pearl, the dragnet.

## The place

*He came to **His hometown** and began teaching the people in their synagogue.*

Nazareth.

## The reaction

*And they **could hardly take it in**.*

The Greek is **ekplessesthai** — struck out of themselves.

Astonishment is the right response and it does not get them anywhere.

## The questions

*"**Where did this man get such wisdom, and these mighty works?**"*

They concede both. Neither the teaching nor the miracles is disputed.

What they cannot place is the source, and they have a theory: he came from here, so it cannot
be from anywhere.

## The family

*"Is He not **the carpenter's son**? Is His mother not named Mary, and are His brothers not
**James and Joses and Simon and Judas**? And are **all His sisters not here among us**?"*

Matthew says *the carpenter's son*. Mark says *the carpenter*. See the note on Mark 6:1–6.

Four brothers by name, and sisters, plural, unnamed. A large family, all of them local, and
every one of them known.

Two of the brothers wrote letters that are in the New Testament. Neither believed Him at this
point.

## The verdict

*And they **took offense at Him**.*

The Greek is **eskandalizonto** — they stumbled over Him. The root of *scandal*.

## The proverb

*"A prophet is **honored everywhere except in his own hometown and in his own house**."*

The Greek is **atimos** — without honour.

The last three words are the sharpest. Not just the town — the house.

John says it plainly: *for even His brothers did not believe in Him*.

## The sentence

*And **He did not do many miracles there, because they would not believe**.*

Matthew's phrasing. Mark's is harder: *He was not able to do many mighty works there*.

Matthew softens *could not* to *did not*, and keeps the reason.

## What the chapter has just said

The parable of the sower and its explanation. Four soils, and the seed is the same in all
four. See the note on Matthew 13:18–23.

Nazareth is the chapter's illustration of itself. Matthew puts the wayside soil at the end of
the chapter about soils, and does not comment.

## What He said in the Nazareth synagogue

Luke's account of what is probably the same visit: He reads Isaiah 61, hands back the scroll,
sits down, and says *today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing*. See the note on
Luke 4:16–21.

*So all bore witness to Him and marvelled at the gracious words which proceeded out of His
mouth. And they said, "Is this not Joseph's son?"*

The same question, and then the crowd tries to throw Him off a cliff.

## What He does after

Sends the twelve out, two by two, with instructions about which houses to stay in and what to
do when a town will not receive them.

> **What they got right.** The wisdom, the miracles, the family and the trade. Everything on
> their list is accurate, and the conclusion they draw from it is that there is nothing to
> see.
