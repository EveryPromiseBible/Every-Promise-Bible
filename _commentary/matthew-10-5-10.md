The first sending, and it has a boundary on it that will not survive the resurrection.

## The sending

*These twelve Jesus sent out, and He gave them their instructions.*

The Greek is **apesteilen** — sent, from which **apostolos**, one sent.

The chapter opens with the twelve named, and then this.

## The restriction

*"**Do not take the road to the Gentiles**, and **do not enter any Samaritan town**. **Go instead to
the lost sheep of the house of Israel**."*

Two prohibitions and one direction.

## Why

He says it of Himself later, to the Canaanite woman: *I was not sent except to the lost sheep of
the house of Israel*. See the note on Matthew 15:21–28.

Paul states the order: *to the Jew first and also to the Greek*.

The restriction is about sequence, and the sequence is stated as temporary by everything that
follows the resurrection.

## What is lifted later

*"**Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations**."* See the note on
Matthew 28:16–20.

**Panta ta ethne** — all the nations. The exact word that was forbidden in chapter 10.

And in Acts: *you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and **Samaria**, and to
the end of the earth*.

Both of the two forbidden categories are named in the commission.

## The message

*"As you go, say this: '**The kingdom of heaven has come near**.'"*

The Greek is **engiken** — has drawn near and stands near. Perfect tense.

The same sentence John the Baptist preached, and the same one Jesus preached.

## The four instructions

*"**Heal the sick, raise the dead, make lepers clean, drive out demons**."*

Four imperatives, and the second is not a metaphor.

They are given what He has been doing, in the same order of difficulty.

## The line

*"**You received it as a gift; give it as a gift**."*

The Greek is **dorean elabete, dorean dote** — freely you received, freely give.

**Dorean** — as a gift, without payment, gratis. It is the word Paul uses of justification:
*being justified **freely** by His grace*.

Two words in Greek, repeated. The construction is deliberately identical on both sides.

## The packing list

*"**Do not pack gold or silver or copper in your belts**, no travel bag, no second shirt, no spare
sandals, no walking stick."*

Three metals, descending in value — they are not to carry the cheap coins either.

**Pera** — a travel bag, the sort a beggar or a wandering philosopher carried for collections.

## What the list rules out

Self-sufficiency, and also the appearance of it.

The travelling teachers of the period carried a bag and a staff. The twelve are sent without the
kit that identified the profession.

## The reason

*"**The worker deserves to be fed**."*

The Greek is **axios gar ho ergates tes trophes autou** — worthy is the worker of his food.

Luke's version has *his wages*, and Paul quotes it as Scripture alongside Deuteronomy. See the
note on 1 Timothy 5:17–18.

## The arrangement

They give without charge and are fed by those they go to.

Not payment for the message — the next verses set out how they are to find a worthy household and
stay there.

## What He tells them to expect

*"**Behold, I send you out as sheep in the midst of wolves**. Therefore be wise as serpents and
harmless as doves."*

*"You will be hated by all for My name's sake."* See the note on Matthew 10:16–20.

The commission and the warning are in the same paragraph.

> **The two words.** **Dorean… dorean** — as a gift, as a gift. The same term on both sides of the
> sentence, which is why the packing list follows it.
