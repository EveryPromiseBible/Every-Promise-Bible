Paul is building a case, and this paragraph is the part where nobody is exempt.

## Where the argument is

Chapter 1 described the Gentile world and ended *who, knowing the righteous judgment of God…
not only do the same but also approve of those who practise them*.

Chapter 2 opens by turning on the reader: *therefore you are inexcusable, O man, whoever you are
who judge, for **in whatever you judge another you condemn yourself**, for you who judge practise
the same things*.

## The principle

*He will **repay each person according to what that person has done**.*

The Greek is **apodosei hekasto kata ta erga autou**.

It is Psalm 62 and Proverbs 24, quoted almost word for word.

## Why Paul says it

Because the argument he is building requires it. If God judges by deeds, without partiality, then
the case against everybody is complete — and that is what chapter 3 concludes.

*There is none righteous, no, not one.*

## The first group

*To those who **by patient perseverance in doing good** seek glory and honor and immortality, He
will give eternal life.*

The Greek is **kath' hupomonen ergou agathou** — according to patient endurance of good work.

## What that describes

A standard. Paul is not saying anybody meets it.

By chapter 3 he has said that no one does: *for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of
God*. See the note on Romans 3:23–24.

## The second group

*But for those who are **self-seeking, who refuse to obey the truth** and give themselves to
wrongdoing instead, there will be **anger and fury**.*

The Greek is **ex eritheias** — out of selfish ambition. The same noun James uses. See the note on
James 3:13–16.

## The two lists

*Trouble and distress will come upon **every human being who does evil, the Jew first and also the
Gentile**.*

*But glory and honor and peace will come to **everyone who does good, the Jew first and also the
Gentile**.*

## The phrase

**Ioudaiou te proton kai Hellenos** — to the Jew first and also to the Greek.

Paul uses it in chapter 1 of the gospel: *it is the power of God to salvation for everyone who
believes, for the Jew first and also for the Greek*.

Here he uses it twice, of judgment and of reward.

Priority in the gospel means priority in the accounting.

## The conclusion

*For **with God there is no favoritism**.*

The Greek is **ou gar estin prosopolempsia para to Theo** — there is no face-receiving with God.

## The idiom

**Prosopolempsia** — from **prosopon** (face) and **lambano** (to receive). A literal rendering of
the Hebrew **nasa panim** — to lift the face.

In court, the judge lifting a defendant's face to see who it was. Partiality.

The law forbids it of judges: *you shall not show partiality in judgment*.

## Where else it appears

*And you, masters, do the same things to them, giving up threatening, knowing that **your own Master
also is in heaven, and there is no partiality with Him**.* — Paul, to slave-owners.

*My brethren, do not hold the believing of our Lord Jesus Christ… **with partiality**.* — James, about
seating a rich man well and a poor man badly.

Peter, in Cornelius's house: *in truth I perceive that God shows no partiality*.

## Where the argument arrives

*Therefore **by the deeds of the law no flesh will be justified** in His sight.*

*But now the righteousness of God apart from the law is revealed… **even the righteousness of God,
through believing in Jesus Christ, to all and on all who believe. For there is no difference**.* See
the note on Romans 3:21–22.

The same phrase — no difference — that closed the judgment closes the offer.

> **The idiom.** **Prosopolempsia** — face-receiving. A judge lifting a defendant's face to see
> whose it was. Paul uses the impartiality to close off every exemption, and then to open the offer.
