The corruption is described in procedural detail, which is what makes it damning.

## The verdict first

*Now Eli's sons were **scoundrels**. They had **no regard for the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **bene beliyaal** — sons of worthlessness. The phrase is used of the worst
men in the Old Testament.

**No regard for the LORD** — the Hebrew is *they did not know the LORD*.

They are the priests at Shiloh, where the tabernacle stood. Their father is the high
priest and the judge of Israel.

## The custom

*Here was **their custom** with the people.*

The Hebrew is *the priests' custom with the people*. It had become the practice, not an
occasional abuse.

## The fork

*The priest's servant would show up **while the meat was still boiling**, holding **a
three-pronged fork**. He'd **jab it into the pot or kettle or pan**, and **whatever the fork
brought up, the priest took**.*

Four vessels named. The detail is procedural, and that is the point — somebody set this
up.

Leviticus assigned the priests specific portions: the breast and the right thigh, waved and
heaved. This is a fork in a pot, taking whatever comes out.

## Who it was done to

*That's how they treated **all the Israelites who came to Shiloh**.*

Every pilgrim, every time.

## The worse half

*Worse — **even before the fat was burned to the LORD**.*

The fat belonged to God. Leviticus is explicit and repeats it: *all the fat is the LORD's…
you shall eat neither fat nor blood.*

They were taking their portion before God's portion went on the fire.

## The demand

*"Hand over some meat for the priest to roast. **He won't take boiled meat from you — only
raw**."*

They want it uncooked so they can roast it their own way. It is a preference.

## The threat

*And if the man said, "**Let the fat be burned first**, then take what you want," the servant
would answer, "**No. Give it now, or I'll take it by force**."*

The worshipper is the one arguing for the law.

## The verdict

*So **the sin of these young men was very great in the LORD's eyes**, because they were
**treating the LORD's offering with contempt**.*

The Hebrew is *they despised the offering of the LORD*.

The charge is not theft. It is contempt for what was being brought.

## The consequence

*And **men abhorred the offering of the LORD**.*

The next clause of the passage, and it is the real damage. People stopped wanting to come.

## The refrain

The chapter alternates. After every account of the sons: *but Samuel ministered before the
LORD, even as a child, wearing a linen ephod.*

And after the warning Eli gives them: *and the boy Samuel kept growing, both in size and in
favour — with the LORD and with people.* See the note on 1 Samuel 2:22–26.

Luke uses almost the same sentence of Jesus at twelve. See the note on Luke 2:51–52.

## What happened

Both sons died on the same day at Aphek, the ark was captured, and Eli fell backward off
his seat and broke his neck at the news.

His daughter-in-law, dying in labour, named the child **Ichabod** — *the glory has
departed*.

> **What made it great in God's eyes.** Not the amount taken. The fork went into the pot
> before the fat went on the fire — and the men bringing the sacrifices were the ones
> objecting.
