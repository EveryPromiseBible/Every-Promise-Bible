A command shouted at architecture.

## Who is being addressed

*Lift up your heads, **O gates**! Be lifted up, **you ancient doors**.*

Not the people. The gates.

The psalm tells the doorways to make themselves higher, because what is coming
through will not fit under them as they stand.

## "Ancient doors"

The Hebrew is *doors of old*, everlasting doors. Whatever gates these are, they
are older than the city.

## The question and the answer

*Who is this King of glory?*

The psalm asks it, and answers it, and then asks again a few lines later and
answers again. It is written to be sung antiphonally — one voice at the gate, one
voice inside.

*The LORD, **strong and mighty**, the LORD, **mighty in battle**.*

The answer is military. He is identified by a fight He has been in.

## Which battle

The psalm before it is the shepherd. The psalm before that is the cross —
*forsaken*, *pierced*, and *He has done it*.

By the time the gates are told to lift their heads, the battle referred to is
over. He is not going out to it; He is coming back from it.

## The three psalms as one sequence

- **22** — the Saviour dying, forsaken, the work finished
- **23** — the Shepherd, present, walking the days and the valley with His own
- **24** — the King of glory coming in through the gates

Past, present, and what is still ahead. They stand in that order in the book and
they have always been read that way. See the notes on Psalms 22:1–5 and
Psalms 23:6.

## How the psalm opens

*The earth is the LORD's, and everything in it, the world and all who live in
it.*

The claim is made before the King arrives at the gate. He owns the city He is
being asked to be let into.

## Who goes up

The middle of the psalm asks who may ascend the hill of the LORD, and answers:
*he who has clean hands and a pure heart.*

Then it names the generation that seeks Him, and the last line of that section is
*they will receive blessing from the LORD, and righteousness from the God of
their salvation.*

Righteousness is listed as something received from Him, in the same breath as the
blessing.

> **The last line of the psalm.** *The LORD of hosts — He is the King of glory.*
> The title is Yahweh Sabaoth, the LORD of armies. The gates are opened for the
> commander, coming home.
