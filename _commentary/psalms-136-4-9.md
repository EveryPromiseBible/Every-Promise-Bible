Every line has an answer, and the answer never changes.

## The form

Twenty-six verses, and every one of them ends the same way:

*His steadfast love endures forever.*

The Hebrew is **ki le'olam chasdo** — for His **chesed** is forever.

It is antiphonal. One voice sings the line; the congregation sings the response.

## The refrain's word

**Chesed** — the covenant word. Loyal love, kindness that is owed because a relationship exists,
translated variously as mercy, lovingkindness, steadfast love.

Twenty-six times in twenty-six verses.

## Where the psalm is used

Called *the Great Hallel* in Jewish tradition, and sung at Passover.

The Chronicler records it at the dedication of the temple: *when the priests came out of the holy
place… **the trumpeters and singers were as one**, to make one sound to be heard in praising and
thanking the LORD*, and the words were *for He is good, for His mercy endures forever*.

The house filled with cloud at that moment.

## The opening of this section

*To Him **who alone does great wonders**.*

The Hebrew is **niphla'ot gedolot levaddo** — great wonders, by Himself alone.

The word for wonders is the one used of the plagues and of the crossing.

## The heavens

*To Him **who made the heavens with wisdom**.*

The Hebrew is **bitvunah** — with understanding.

Proverbs 3: *the LORD by wisdom founded the earth; by understanding He established the heavens*.
See the note on Proverbs 3:19–20.

## The earth

*To Him who **spread the earth over the waters**.*

The Hebrew is **roqa** — beat out, hammered flat, like metal.

The same root as **raqia**, the firmament of Genesis 1.

## The lights

*To Him who **made the great lights**.*

Genesis 1:16: *God made two great lights*.

The psalm follows the creation account in order — heavens, earth, waters, lights — and attaches
the refrain to each step.

## The sun

*The sun **to rule the day**.*

The Hebrew is **lememshelet bayom** — for dominion in the day.

Genesis uses the same verb, and both texts avoid naming the sun and moon. Every neighbouring
culture worshipped them; Genesis and this psalm call them *the greater light* and *the lesser*.

## The moon

*The moon and stars **to rule the night**.*

Stars added almost as an afterthought, exactly as in Genesis: *He made the stars also*.

## What the refrain does to creation

Every act of making gets the covenant word attached to it.

The sun is not simply an astronomical fact. It rises because of **chesed**.

## Where the psalm goes next

*To Him who **struck down the firstborn of Egypt**, His steadfast love endures forever.* See the
note on Psalms 136:10–15.

The same response, on a judgment.

## Where it ends

*Who remembered us in our lowly state… and rescued us from our enemies… **who gives food to all
flesh**.*

The Hebrew is **noten lechem lechol-basar** — gives bread to all flesh.

Twenty-five verses of cosmology and national history, and the last act named is feeding
everything that lives.

## The last verse

*Oh, give thanks to the God of heaven! For His mercy endures forever.*

> **The form.** Twenty-six lines, twenty-six identical answers. The sun rises, the earth is
> spread, the stars are set — and each one is followed by the covenant word.
