The Corinthians had split into fan clubs — some for Paul, some for Apollos. Paul
answers with farming.

## The tenses

*I planted, Apollos watered, but God is the One who made it grow.*

In Greek the first two verbs are aorist — single, finished actions. Somebody
turned up, did a job, and left. The third is imperfect: **kept on giving the
growth**, continuous, still going.

The whole argument is in the grammar. Two men each did a discrete piece of work.
One party has been doing the thing that actually matters, the whole time.

## "Neither one is anything"

That is a hard sentence about himself, and Paul means it as a corrective to
people building identities out of which preacher they preferred.

But he does not leave them worthless: *each will receive his own reward according
to his own labour*. Not according to results. According to labour — which is the
only part a planter or waterer controls.

> **The last line.** *We are fellow workers who belong to God; you are God's
> field, God's building.* Paul flips the metaphor mid-sentence, from farm to
> construction, and he will run with the building for the next several verses.
> Both images have the same point: the workers are staff, and the property is
> somebody else's.
