One verse of autobiography in the plural, placed immediately before the letter's statement of the
gospel.

## The turn

*"**We were no different ourselves once.**"*

The Greek is **emen gar pote kai hemeis** — for we ourselves also were once.

The paragraph before this has been instructions about submitting to authorities, speaking evil of no
one, and showing gentleness *to all men*. And then **Paul** gives the reason, and the reason is a
list of what he and Titus used to be.

## The seven

*"We had **no sense**; we **would not listen**; we were **taken in and led off**; we were **slaves to
every kind of craving and pleasure**; we lived in **malice and envy**; we were **hateful**, and we
**hated each other**."*

**Anoetoi, apeitheis, planomenoi, douleuontes, diagontes en kakia kai phthono, stygetoi, misountes
allelous.**

Seven items, and the last is reciprocal: hated, and hating.

**Paul** does not distinguish himself from the Cretans he has just described in chapter 1 as *liars,
savage animals, lazy bellies*. He puts himself in the same sentence.

## Why it is here

Because of what it is the ground of.

The instruction is *show gentleness to all men*, and the argument for it is not that people deserve
gentleness. It is that the person being asked was in the list.

*"**Should you not also have had compassion on your fellow servant, just as I had pity on you?**"*

*And such were some of you. **But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified**
in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of our God.*

**1 Corinthians** 6, and it is the same move: an unsoftened list, and then a turn.

## The slavery

*"**Slaves to every kind of craving and pleasure.**"*

**Douleuontes** — serving as slaves. **Paul** uses it of the past tense repeatedly.

*For when you were slaves of sin, you were free in regard to righteousness… **But now having been
set free from sin, and having become slaves of God**, you have your fruit to holiness.*

## The next word

*"**But then** the kindness of God our Saviour came out into the open."*

**Hote de** — but when. See the note on Titus 3:4–7.

The verse exists to be the dark half of a sentence whose second half is the appearing of kindness.
It is not a diagnosis left standing; it is a setup, and the paragraph does not pause between them.

*But God, who is rich in mercy, because of His great love with which He loved us, **even when we were
dead in trespasses, made us alive together with Christ**.*

> **Who is in the list.** Paul, writing it. He has just called the Cretans liars and savage animals,
> and the next paragraph opens *we were no different ourselves* — and the whole point of it is the
> instruction two verses earlier to be gentle with everybody.
