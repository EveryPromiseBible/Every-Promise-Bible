The statue is enormous, blazing and terrifying, and what destroys it is a rock.

## "Not by human hands"

*This is the stone you saw, **cut from the mountain but not by human hands**.*

The one detail Daniel repeats. It is what distinguishes the stone from everything
else in the dream — every empire in the statue was quarried, shaped and raised by
men.

Nobody made this one.

## Where it hits

The stone strikes the **feet** — the iron and clay, the weakest joint, the part
everything else is standing on. And the whole statue comes down at once.

*Broke to pieces the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver, and the gold.*

Note the order Daniel lists them in: from the feet up to the head. It collapses
from the bottom.

## What happens to the pieces

The verses before this describe it: the fragments become *like chaff on a summer
threshing floor*, and the wind carries them off so that no trace of them is found.

Not defeated and occupied. Gone.

## The kingdom

*In the days of those kings, **the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that will
never be destroyed**. It will not be left to another people… and it will stand
forever.*

Three things said about it:

- **never destroyed** — unlike every kingdom in the statue
- **not left to another people** — no succession, no handover; the whole statue
  is a chain of handovers
- **stand forever**

## And the stone becomes a mountain

*The stone that struck the statue became a great mountain and filled the whole
earth.*

It arrives small enough to be called a stone and ends up filling everything.

## Who this was said to

A Babylonian king, by a captive, about the end of Babylon and everything that
would follow it — and Nebuchadnezzar's response is to fall on his face.

*"Truly your God is the God of gods and the Lord of kings."*

## The last line

*The great God has made known to the king **what is yet to come**. The dream is
true, and its meaning is sure.*

Daniel signs off on it. Two clauses, both about reliability.

> **What the stone is called elsewhere.** *The stone the builders rejected has
> become the chief cornerstone* — Psalm 118, and quoted by Jesus of Himself the
> week He died, and by Peter in Acts 4. A stone the builders looked at and
> discarded; a stone no builder cut.
