Two verses about hearing, tucked between the parables.

## The instruction

*"**Be careful how you listen.**"*

Not *what* you listen to — Luke's version has *how you hear*, and Mark's is *what you
hear*, and between them they cover both.

The point is that hearing is something that can be done well or badly, and that the
manner of it has consequences.

## The measure

*"**The measure you use will be the measure used for you**, and more besides."*

The same saying appears in Luke 6 about judging, condemning, forgiving and giving.
See the note on Luke 6:37–38.

Here it is applied to listening. However much attention you bring is the amount you
get back — *and more besides.*

## The hard sentence

*"**The one who has will be given more**, and from the one who has nothing, even the
little he has will be taken away."*

It reads as unfair until you notice what is being measured.

The context is the parable of the sower, which Mark has just finished — four soils,
the same seed, and the difference is entirely in the ground.

Nothing about the sowing varies. What a hearer ends up with depends on what he does
with what he heard, and the man who does nothing with it loses even the memory of
it.

## What surrounds this

Immediately before: *is a lamp brought to be put under a basket or under a bed? Is
it not to be set on a lampstand?*

Immediately after: the seed growing secretly, and the mustard seed.

So the sequence is — the lamp is meant to be seen, so pay attention to how you hear,
because the seed grows on its own once it is in the ground.

## The lamp and the hearing

Mark puts a lamp and an instruction about listening in consecutive breaths.

The lampstand in the tabernacle was the only light in a windowless room, and the
priest's daily job at it was trimming the wicks so it kept burning. See the note on
Exodus 25:31–40.

## The unavoidable disclosure

*"For there is nothing hidden which will not be revealed, nor has anything been kept
secret but that it should come to light."*

That is the verse in between. Whatever is put under the basket does not stay there.

> **The line Mark repeats.** *If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear.* He uses it
> twice in this one chapter — before the explanation of the sower and again here.
> Every occurrence of it in the Gospels is attached to something that had just gone
> over somebody's head.
