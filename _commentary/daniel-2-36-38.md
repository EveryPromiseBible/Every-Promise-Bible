He tells the king he is the head of gold, and in the same breath tells him who put him there.

## The plural

*"This was the dream. Now **we will tell the king its meaning**."*

The Aramaic is **nemar** — we will say.

Daniel has just come from a night of prayer with three friends. The pronoun keeps them in it.

## The title

*"You, O king, are **a king of kings**."*

The Aramaic is **melek malkaya**.

Ezekiel uses the same phrase of Nebuchadnezzar. Revelation uses it of Christ.

## The clause that follows

*"**The God of heaven has given you** the kingdom, the power, the strength, and the glory."*

The Aramaic is **yehav lak** — has given to you.

Four things listed, and all four are on the receiving end of one verb.

## Where the empire came from

Not from Babylon's armies.

Daniel stands in front of the man who sacked Jerusalem and tells him his conquest was a grant.

## The extent

*"**Wherever people live**, or the beasts of the field, or the birds of the air, **He has placed them
in your hand** and made you ruler over them all."*

The wording reaches past the human population to the animals and the birds — the language of
Genesis 1, dominion over fish, birds and every living thing.

## The sentence

*"**You are that head of gold**."*

The Aramaic is **antah hu resha di dahava**.

## What the head is attached to

Silver chest and arms. Bronze belly and thighs. Iron legs. Feet of iron mixed with clay.

The metals get less valuable and harder as they descend. Gold is the top and the softest.

## The stone

*A stone was cut out **without hands**, which struck the image on its feet… and **became a great
mountain and filled the whole earth**.* See the note on Daniel 2:34–35.

*The God of heaven will set up a kingdom which shall never be destroyed.* See the note on
Daniel 2:44–45.

The head of gold is told, to his face, that the whole statue ends as chaff on a threshing floor.

## What the king does with it

*Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face, prostrate before Daniel.*

And then builds an image of gold, sixty cubits high, and commands everyone to bow to it. See the
note on Daniel 3:1–7.

He was told he was the head of gold. He builds the whole statue in gold, and gives it no other
metals.

## What it takes to teach him

*"**The kingdom has departed from you**… you will eat grass like an ox, and seven times will pass
over you, **until you know that the Most High rules in the kingdom of men**."* See the note on
Daniel 4:31–32.

The lesson of the chapter, repeated with a seven-year demonstration two chapters later.

## Where he lands

*"I, Nebuchadnezzar, **lifted my eyes to heaven, and my understanding returned to me**."* See the note
on Daniel 4:34–35.

> **The verb.** Four things — kingdom, power, strength, glory — and one verb governing all of them:
> *has given you*. The greatest empire on earth described as a hand-over.
