---
key: James 2:17
title: Does James contradict Paul?
updated: 2026-08-07
---

This is the verse people set against Paul. Paul says a person is put right with God through faith and not by works. James says faith without works is dead. Here are seven reasons they are not fighting each other.

## 1. They are answering two different questions

Paul is answering: how does a guilty person get right with God? James is answering: how can you tell a living faith from a dead one? Same words, different question. Neither of them has to lose.

## 2. They are arguing with two different people

Paul's opponent thinks doing good earns you standing with God. James's opponent thinks saying you believe is enough and nothing needs to follow. Neither man is attacking what the other one taught.

## 3. James tells you exactly which faith he is rejecting

Three verses back, the man SAYS he has faith. It is a claim, not a life. And two verses on, the belief being tested is simply believing God exists — which, as James points out, the demons manage too, and it makes them shake.

He is not attacking trust in God. He is attacking a counterfeit that he carefully describes first.

## 4. The little word "that" — the strongest point of all

In Greek there is a small word, the, which James attaches to faith every single time he points at the counterfeit: 2:14, this verse, 2:20, 2:22 and 2:26. Your text shows it — that faith, this faith. He means the one he has just described.

And he drops that little word in exactly one place: 2:24, where he stops describing the fake and states the general rule.

So every time he means the counterfeit, the word is there. The one time he states the principle, it is gone. That is grammar, not opinion — it sits in the text whether anyone likes it or not.

## 5. Abraham's dates settle the order

Abraham believed God and was counted righteous in Genesis 15. He put Isaac on the altar in Genesis 22 — chapters later, years later. James quotes the earlier moment and says the altar filled it full. The standing came first; the deed filled it out. There is more on this at 2:21.

## 6. Chapter 1 had already settled it

Before this argument even begins James has said four things in order: God acted first and nobody talked Him into it (1:18); the message is already planted in you, and it is the message that saves you (1:21); now carry it out (1:22); and you are blessed IN the doing, not paid for it (1:25).

The gift comes before the work — twenty verses before anyone mentions works.

## 7. The whole passage is about showing, not paying

Show me. I will show you. Do you need showing. Look at what happened. You see it now. Over and over. James is asking for evidence that the faith is alive, not for payment to make it count.

> One thing not to claim: do not say Paul and James use different words. They use the same ones — faith, works, justified, righteousness, the same Abraham, and the same Old Testament verse about him. That is precisely why this passage has been argued over for centuries. The claim that holds up is not that they used different words, but that they used the same words to answer different questions.
