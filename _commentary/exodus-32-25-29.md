The day the tablets came down the mountain, and what happened at the bottom of
it.

## The situation

*Moses saw that the people were running wild — Aaron had let them get out of
control.*

They had been at Sinai for weeks. Moses was on the mountain receiving the law;
the camp had made a calf and was holding a festival for it.

The Hebrew for *running wild* suggests being let loose, unrestrained — and the
text puts the responsibility on Aaron for permitting it.

## The line drawn

*Whoever is for the LORD, come to me.*

A call to choose sides, in the open, in front of everyone. The Levites come.

What follows is grim and the text does not soften it: swords through the camp,
gate to gate, and about three thousand dead.

## The number

Three thousand, on the day the law was given.

That figure appears once more in Scripture, and the setting is deliberate:
Pentecost — by tradition the anniversary of Sinai — when Peter preaches and *about
three thousand people were added* to them.

Same festival, same number, opposite direction. Luke was writing for readers who
knew Exodus.

## What Moses says afterwards

*Today you have been set apart to the LORD… And so a blessing has come to you
this day.*

The tribe that answered the call becomes the priestly tribe. It is the origin of
Levi's role in Israel, and the passage records both the cost and the appointment
in the same sentence.

> **How to hold this passage.** It is a hard chapter and it should not be
> smoothed over. What can be said is what the New Testament says: the law came
> through Moses, and grace and truth came through Jesus Christ — and the two
> three-thousands are the plainest illustration in Scripture of what each of them
> produced on its opening day.
