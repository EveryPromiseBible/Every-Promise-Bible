Paul has just asked for prayer because *not all have faith*, and then turns the word
around.

## The contrast

*"**But the Lord is faithful.**"*

The verse before: *pray… that we may be delivered from unreasonable and wicked men,
**for not all have faith**.*

Paul names a shortage of faith in people and answers it with the faithfulness of the
Lord. Same root, opposite subject.

He does the same in 2 Timothy: *if we are faithless, **He remains faithful**; He
cannot deny Himself.*

## The two things He does

*"He is the one who will **make you steady on your feet**, and He is the one who will
**stand guard over you and keep you clear of evil**."*

The Greek for the first is **sterizo** — to establish, to set firmly, to prop up.

The second is to guard, the word for a garrison keeping watch.

Both verbs are His and neither is asked of the readers.

## What Paul is confident about

*"And where you are concerned, **we are confident in the Lord**: you are doing what we
told you, and you will go on doing it."*

Note where the confidence is placed. Not *we are confident in you* — **in the Lord**,
about you.

Paul's expectation of their perseverance rests on somebody other than them.

## The prayer

*"May the Lord **steer your hearts straight into the love of God**, and straight into
**the patience that waits for Christ**."*

The Greek is to make straight, to direct — the same verb used of guiding a ship.

Two destinations: God's love, and patience.

## "The patience that waits for Christ"

The Greek is *the patience of Christ*, and it has been read both ways — the endurance
Christ Himself had, and the endurance that waits for Him.

Either reading is at home in this letter. The Thessalonians had got themselves in a
state about the Lord's return; some had stopped working over it, which is what the
rest of the chapter is about.

## What the letter is dealing with

Persecution, and confusion about the second coming, and people who had quit their
jobs to wait for it.

Paul's answer to the last of those is the plainest sentence in the letter: *if anyone
will not work, neither shall he eat.* See the note on
2 Thessalonians 3:10–12.

He writes about being guarded and steadied, and then tells them to go back to work.

> **How Paul signs it.** *The salutation of Paul with my own hand, which is a sign in
> every epistle; so I write.* Somebody had been sending the Thessalonians forged
> letters about the day of the Lord, and Paul started signing his own.
