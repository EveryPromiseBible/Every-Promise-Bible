Three wells, two arguments, and a man who keeps moving rather than fighting.

## The sequence

The first well: the herdsmen of Gerar quarrelled over it. He named it **Esek** — contention.

The second: they quarrelled over that too. He named it **Sitnah** — hostility, opposition.

## What he does each time

Moves.

*He moved on from there and dug another well.*

He has the numbers to fight — the chapter has just said the Philistines envied him because he had
become *very prosperous*, with great flocks and a large household.

## The third

*And **no one quarreled over it**.*

## The name

*So he named it **Rehoboth, "room"**.*

The Hebrew is **rechovot** — broad places, wide spaces. From **rachav**, to be wide.

## What he says

*"**Now the LORD has given us room**, and we will flourish in the land."*

The Hebrew is **hirchiv YHWH lanu** — the LORD has made wide for us.

## Who he credits

Not his own patience, and not the Philistines' fatigue.

The room is stated to be a gift, after two rounds of giving ground.

## The root elsewhere

*You have **set my feet in a wide place**.* And *He brought me out into a broad place; He delivered
me, because He delighted in me*. See the note on Psalms 18:16–24.

The same word. Deliverance in the psalms is often described as space.

## What he had been doing before

*Then Isaac **sowed in that land, and reaped in the same year a hundredfold**.* See the note on
Genesis 26:12–14.

A famine year. He sowed in it, because he had been told not to go down to Egypt.

## The wells he was re-digging

*And Isaac dug again the wells of water which they had dug **in the days of Abraham his father**, for
the Philistines had **stopped them up** after the death of Abraham.*

*And he called them by the names which his father had called them.*

He is not prospecting. He is reopening his father's wells and restoring their names.

## Who arrives next

*Then Abimelech came to him from Gerar… "**We have certainly seen that the LORD is with you**."* See
the note on Genesis 26:26–31.

The men who drove him off come to make a treaty, and give as their reason the thing they had been
contesting.

## What happens the same day

*It came to pass the same day that Isaac's servants came and told him about the well… and they
called it **Shebah** — oath. Therefore the name of the city is **Beersheba**.*

## The kind of man Isaac is

The quietest of the three patriarchs.

Abraham argues with God over Sodom; Jacob wrestles an angel. Isaac digs wells, gives way twice, and
gets the land.

## Where the pattern is stated

*Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth.* See the note on Matthew 5:3–12.

The Greek is **praeis** — the Septuagint's word in Psalm 37: *the meek shall inherit the earth, and
shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace*.

## The chapter's promise

*"I am the God of your father Abraham; **do not fear, for I am with you**. I will bless you and
multiply your descendants."* See the note on Genesis 26:23–25.

Spoken the night he arrived at Beersheba, after the third well.

> **The two names he left behind.** Contention and Hostility, both abandoned. He kept the third one:
> Room — and said God gave it, not that he had won it.
