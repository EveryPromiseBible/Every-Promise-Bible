This verse is the hinge of Peter's letter. Everything before it is what God has
done; everything after it is how to live. And the joint between the two is a
single instruction that most readings skip over to get to the commands that
follow.

## Three pictures, and only one of them is the command

- **get your mind stripped down and ready to move** — the Greek is one word,
  literally *gird up the loins of your mind*. A man in a long robe pulled it
  through his belt to run or work. Nobody had to explain it.
- **keep a clear head** — the word for not being drunk, used of staying alert.
- **pin your hope … on the kindness that arrives the day Jesus is revealed**

The first two are participles. The third is the actual imperative. In Greek that
distinction is unmistakable: the sentence is *hope*, and the other two describe
the posture of someone doing it.

Which means the command in this verse is not "try harder" or "stay sober." It is
**hope**, and hope aimed at one specific thing.

## What he tells them to hope in

Not in an improved version of themselves. Not in surviving the pressure they
were under — and they were under real pressure; that is why the letter exists.

He tells them to put their hope on *the kindness that arrives* — grace, being
brought to them, at the revealing of Jesus Christ. It is spoken of as something
in transit, on its way, arriving.

---

## "All of it, nothing held back"

There is an adverb in the sentence — *teleios*, completely, without reserve. It
can attach to either the clear head or the hoping, and translators split over
it. Either way the force is the same: nothing partial. Peter is not asking for
hope with a fallback plan behind it.

That is a hard instruction for people being pressed, which is exactly who he is
writing to. The alternative he is ruling out is not despair. It is hedging —
hoping in grace while quietly keeping a second account open in your own effort.

## Why it comes exactly here

Look at what precedes it: an inheritance kept in heaven, a salvation ready to be
revealed, prophets and angels leaning in to look at it. Peter spends twelve
verses on what has been done and is coming. *So* — get your mind ready, and put
your hope there.

The order is not decorative. The commands in the rest of the letter are
addressed to people who have already been told where to look.

> **On the holiness that follows.** Three verses later comes *be holy, for I am
> holy*, and it is often quoted on its own as the letter's demand. In Peter's
> sentence it stands downstream of this one: hope fixed on grace first, and
> holiness described as what children of that Father look like.
