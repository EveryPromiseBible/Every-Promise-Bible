The argument turns on a single adverb, and the writer spells out what the alternative would cost.

## The comparison

*He did not go in to **offer Himself over and over**, the way the high priest walks into the holy
place **year after year with blood that is not his own**.*

The Greek is **en haimati allotrio** — with blood belonging to another.

The high priest went in on the Day of Atonement with the blood of a bull and a goat. Not his.

## What Hebrews has already said about that

*But into the second part the high priest went alone once a year, not without blood, which he
offered for himself and for the people's sins committed in ignorance.* See the note on
Hebrews 9:6–7.

*Which cannot make him who performed the service perfect in regard to the conscience.*

## The consequence

*If that were so, **He would have had to suffer again and again since the world was founded**.*

The Greek is **apo kataboles kosmou** — from the foundation of the world.

## What the argument does

Takes the repetition seriously and runs it backwards.

If His offering worked the way theirs did, it would need repeating; and since sin runs back to the
beginning, He would have had to die repeatedly since the world began.

The absurdity is the proof.

## The turn

*But as it is, **He has appeared one time**.*

The Greek is **hapax** — once, once for all.

The word runs through the letter: *once for all when He offered up Himself*, *by His own blood He
entered the Most Holy Place **once for all***, *we have been sanctified through the offering of the
body of Jesus Christ **once for all***.

## The timing

*At **the close of the ages**.*

The Greek is **epi sunteleia ton aionon** — at the completion of the ages.

Not at the start of history and not near the end of it. At the point everything before was
running toward.

## The effect

*And by sacrificing Himself **He has canceled sin**.*

The Greek is **eis athetesin tes hamartias** — for the putting away of sin.

**Athetesis** — annulment. A legal word for setting a document aside.

Hebrews uses it once more, of the law: *there is an **annulling** of the former commandment because
of its weakness*.

## What follows

*And as it is appointed for men to die once, but after this the judgment, so Christ was offered
once to bear the sins of many.*

*To those who eagerly wait for Him **He will appear a second time, apart from sin, for salvation**.*
See the note on Hebrews 9:27–28.

## The three appearings

Hebrews names them in these verses.

He **has appeared** — at the close of the ages, to put away sin.

He **now appears** — *in the presence of God for us*, verse 24.

He **will appear** — a second time, apart from sin.

Past, present, future, in one paragraph.

## What the word *apart from sin* means

Not that He was sinless — that has been said. That the second appearing has nothing to do with
sin, because the sin business is finished.

## What the next chapter says

*For by one offering **He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified**.* See the note on
Hebrews 10:14.

*Now where there is remission of these, **there is no longer an offering for sin**.* See the note on
Hebrews 10:18.

> **The argument.** Take the repetition seriously and run it back: if His offering needed
> repeating, He would have had to die over and over since the world began. The absurdity is the
> proof.
