The psalm puts two things side by side that do not obviously belong together.

## The three actions

*The LORD **rebuilds Jerusalem**. He **gathers the scattered** of Israel. He **heals
the brokenhearted** and **binds up their wounds**.*

A city, a nation, and a person — and they are in one breath.

The scale drops from a skyline to an individual without any change of tone.

## "Binds up"

The Hebrew is **chabash** — to bandage, to wrap. It is what you do to a wound with
cloth, and it is what a physician does.

The word for *wounds* is the word for pain, hurt, a sore place.

Nothing here is metaphorical in the Hebrew. It is field-dressing vocabulary.

## What comes immediately after

*He **counts the number of the stars**; He **calls them all by name**.*

That is verse 4, and it is the line the passage is best known for pairing with
verse 3.

The same God binding up one person's wounds is counting a sky nobody has finished
counting, and naming every one of them.

Then verse 5: *Great is our Lord, and mighty in power; His understanding is
infinite.*

The stars are there to establish the size of the One doing the bandaging.

## The stars, and Abraham

The verb behind counting the stars is **saphar** — to number, and to recount, to
tell.

It is the same word God used when He took Abram outside: *count the stars, if you
can even number them.* See the note on Genesis 15:4–5.

## Who the psalm is for

*He rebuilds Jerusalem. He gathers the scattered.*

Written for people coming home from exile to a ruined city. Not an untroubled
congregation — a returning one.

## What the psalm says God is not impressed by

Further down: *He does not delight in the strength of the horse; He takes no
pleasure in the legs of a man.*

The two fastest things in that world, and both are dismissed in one verse.

*The LORD takes pleasure in those who fear Him, in those who hope in His mercy.*

## How it opens

*Hallelujah! How **good** it is to sing to our God, how **pleasant**, how
**fitting** the praise.*

Three words for it. The psalm argues that praise is good, that it is enjoyable, and
that it is appropriate — and then gives four verses of reasons.

> **What the psalm ends on.** *He declares His word to Jacob, His statutes and His
> judgements to Israel. He has not dealt thus with any nation.* The final boast of
> the psalm is not the stars or the city. It is that He talked to them.
