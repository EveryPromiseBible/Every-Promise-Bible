The last paragraph of John 3, and it is hard to tell where the Baptist stops speaking and
the writer starts.

## What comes before

John's disciples had come to him complaining that everyone was going to Jesus instead.

His answer: *a man can receive nothing unless it has been given to him from heaven… **He
must increase, but I must decrease**.*

Then this, and no quotation marks in the Greek to say who is talking.

## The origin

*"The One who **comes from above is above everything and everyone**."*

Said twice in three lines. John's Gospel builds on the contrast: *you are from beneath; I
am from above*.

## The two speakers

*"Whoever **belongs to the earth is of the earth, and speaks as one who belongs to the
earth**."*

Not an insult. A statement about range. A man can only report from where he has been.

## The testimony

*"He **testifies to what He has seen and heard**, and **no one accepts His testimony**."*

The Greek is the legal word — **marturia**.

**No one** — and then, immediately, the exception.

## What accepting it means

*"Whoever does accept it has **put his own name to this: that God tells the truth**."*

The Greek is *has set his seal that God is true*.

A seal was a signature — pressed into wax to certify a document.

Believing is described as countersigning a statement about God's character.

John says the reverse in his letter: *he who does not believe God has made Him a liar*.

## The measure

*"Since **God does not hand Him the Spirit in measured amounts**."*

The Greek is *not by measure* — **ou ek metrou**.

The rabbis spoke of the Spirit being given to the prophets in measures. This clause
removes the measuring cup.

## What the Father has done

*"**The Father loves the Son and has put everything into His hands**."*

Both clauses in one line, and John repeats the pair through the Gospel: *the Father loves
the Son and shows Him all things*, and at the supper *knowing that the Father had given
all things into His hands*. See the note on John 13:1–3.

The love and the handing-over are always together.

## The two outcomes

*"Whoever **believes in the Son has life that never ends**."*

Present tense. Has.

*"Whoever **refuses to believe the Son will not see life**; **the anger of God remains on
him**."*

The Greek for *refuses to believe* is **apeithon** — disobeying, refusing to be
persuaded. It is a word about the will rather than the intellect.

**Remains** — **menei**, the word John uses everywhere for abiding. Not *will come upon*.
It is already there and stays.

## Where this sits in the chapter

Twenty verses after *God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son*.

The same chapter contains both sentences, and John does not soften either.

## Who said it

The Baptist's last recorded words in this Gospel. The next time John mentions him he is
in prison, and after that he is dead.

*He was the burning and shining lamp, and you were willing for a time to rejoice in his
light.*

> **The clause about the Spirit.** *Not by measure.* Everything else in the paragraph is
> about where a speaker has come from and what he is therefore able to report — and this
> is why the reporting is unlimited.
