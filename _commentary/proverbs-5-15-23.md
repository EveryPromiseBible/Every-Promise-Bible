A father's warning to a son, and the middle of it turns into something warmer than a
warning.

## The image

*Drink water from **your own cistern**, fresh water from **your own well**.*

Water rights were the most valuable thing a household owned in that country. A cistern was
cut into rock at real expense and belonged to the family who dug it.

The whole passage runs on the metaphor without ever dropping it.

## The question

*Should **your springs overflow in the streets**, your streams of water **in the public
squares**?*

Water spilled into a street is water lost. The image is of something valuable running away
where anybody can use it.

## The instruction

*Let them be **yours alone**, never to be shared with strangers.*

## The blessing

*May **your fountain be blessed**, and may you **rejoice in the wife of your youth**.*

The Hebrew is *let your fountain be blessed*.

**The wife of your youth** — the phrase turns up in Malachi as well, in the passage about
God hating divorce: *she is your companion and your wife by covenant*.

## The animals

*A **loving doe**, a **graceful deer**.*

The Hebrew is *a loving hind, a graceful doe*.

Proverbs is not usually tender. This is the exception, and it sits in the middle of a
chapter about avoiding an adulteress.

## The verb

*Let her tenderness **fill you always**, be **forever captivated by her love**.*

The Hebrew is **shagah** — to be intoxicated, to reel, to go astray in the sense of being
carried away.

It is the same word used two verses later of the man who is captivated by somebody else's
wife. Proverbs uses one verb for both and lets the direction decide.

## The turn

*Why be **captivated, my son, by another woman**? Why **embrace the arms of a stranger**?*

Same verb. The question is not whether a man will be carried away — the passage assumes he
will — but by whom.

## The witness

*For **a man's ways are in full view of the LORD**, and **He watches every path he takes**.*

The Hebrew is *He ponders all his goings* — **palas**, to weigh, to make level.

Nothing in the chapter is framed as a secret that might be discovered.

## The trap

*The wicked are **trapped by their own sins**, held fast **in the cords of their own
guilt**.*

Nobody else does the tying. The image is of a man caught in rope he made.

Job's friend Elihu uses the same picture of cords. See the note on Job 36:8–12.

## The ending

*They will **die for want of discipline**, led astray **by their own great folly**.*

The Hebrew for *led astray* is **shagah** again — the third use in the chapter.

Carried away by the wife, carried away by the stranger, and carried away to death. One
verb, three times, and the whole chapter turns on where the man lets it take him.

## What the chapter opened with

*For the lips of an immoral woman drip honey, and her mouth is smoother than oil; **but in
the end she is bitter as wormwood**.*

## Where the psalm puts the same picture

*Your wife within your house will be like a fruitful vine.* See the note on
Psalms 128:3–4.

> **The word the chapter is built on.** *Shagah* — to be carried away. Proverbs uses it of
> the wife, the stranger, and the ruin, and never suggests the son will avoid being
> carried away by something.
