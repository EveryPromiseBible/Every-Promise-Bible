The psalm rules out three directions and leaves one.

## The three that are excluded

*For no one is lifted up **from the east or the west, or from the wilderness**.*

East, west, and the desert to the south. The one direction the psalm does not
name is up.

Whatever is left after those three is the answer, and the psalmist leaves the
reader to say it.

## The verb

The Hebrew is to lift up, to raise, to exalt — and it is the word used for
promotion, for a head being raised, for a man being set high.

Not one of the compass points can do it.

## What is left

*It is **God who judges**. He brings one down and lifts another up.*

Both halves are His. The bringing down and the lifting up are the same hand doing
two things.

The Hebrew for *judges* is not primarily about a courtroom. It is the word for
governing, deciding, ruling a matter — the verb the book of Judges is named
after.

## Two examples in one sermon's reach

**Uzziah** — helped until he was strong, and struck at the altar the day his heart
got proud. Fifty-two years, and the last of them in a separate house.

**Haman** — promoted above every official for no stated reason, and hanged on his
own gallows within the day. And **Mordecai**, sitting at a gate, given the ring
that had been on Haman's hand.

Neither man's rise came from the east or the west. Neither man's fall did either.

## The cup

*There is a **cup in the hand of the LORD**, foaming with wine, well mixed, and He
pours it out.*

The image is of a cup passed round — the prophets use it repeatedly, and it is the
cup Jesus names in Gethsemane.

*Down to the very dregs the wicked of the earth will drink it dry.* The dregs are
the sediment at the bottom, the last and worst of it.

## Who is holding it

The whole passage is about whose hand things are in. The cup is in His hand; the
lifting is His; the bringing down is His.

Nobody in the psalm arranges their own position.

> **How the psalm opens.** *We give thanks to You, O God, we give thanks, for Your
> name is near.* The name is *near* — which is what the book of Esther demonstrates
> without once writing it down.
