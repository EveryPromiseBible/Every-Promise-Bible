Paul is on his knees, and this is what he asks for.

## The posture

*For this reason **I bow my knees** to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.*

Standing was the ordinary posture for prayer. Paul kneels twice in Acts, both times at
partings.

## The first request

*That **Christ would settle down and live in your hearts** as you trust Him.*

The Greek is **katoikesai** — to take up permanent residence, as opposed to **paroikeo**, to
stay as a visitor.

Written to people who already have Him. What is asked for is not arrival but settling.

## The roots and the foundation

*With your **roots sunk deep in love** and your **whole weight resting on love**.*

Two metaphors in one clause, and they do not mix: a plant and a building.

The Greek is *rooted and grounded* — **errizomenoi** and **tethemeliomenoi**, both perfect
participles.

Love is the soil in the first and the bedrock in the second. Neither image makes love the
thing produced.

## The strength

*Would **have the strength to take in**.*

The Greek is **exischusete** — to be fully able, to have the force for it.

Comprehension is treated as something requiring power. Paul is asking that they be *made
strong enough to understand*.

## Who does it

*Together with **all God's holy people**.*

The Greek is **sun pasin tois hagiois** — with all the saints.

Not alone. The dimensions are too large for one person's measuring.

## The four

*The **width** and the **length** and the **depth** and the **height** of it.*

Paul never says of what. The sentence has four dimensions and no noun.

The nearest one is *the love of Christ*, in the next line.

Zophar uses the same four of God Himself — *higher than heaven… deeper than Sheol… longer
than the earth and broader than the sea*. See the note on Job 11:5–6.

## The impossible clause

*And **to know the love of Christ, which is more than knowing can hold**.*

The Greek is *to know the love of Christ which surpasses knowledge* — **gnonai** and
**huperballousan tes gnoseos**.

He asks them to know the thing he says exceeds knowing, in the same breath, without
flinching.

**Huperballo** — to throw beyond, to overshoot the mark.

## The result

*That you would be **filled, right up to the brim, with all the fullness of God**.*

The Greek is **eis pan to pleroma tou theou** — unto all the fullness.

And the measure of the filling is God's own fullness. Paul has already used the word of
Christ in Colossians: *in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily*.

## The order

Knowing the love comes first, in verse 19. The filling is the *that* clause hanging off it.

Paul does not ask for the fullness and then the love.

## The doxology

*Now to Him who is able to **do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think**,
according to the power that works in us.*

The Greek is **huper ekperissou** — Paul stacking prefixes because no single word is large
enough. The same *huper* as in *surpasses knowledge*.

And *according to the power that works in us* — the measure is not what we ask for but what
is already operating.

## What comes before it

*To me, **the least of all the saints**, this grace was given, that I should preach among
the Gentiles **the unsearchable riches of Christ**.*

The Greek for *unsearchable* is **anexichniaston** — untraceable, whose footprints cannot be
followed.

> **The prayer's shape.** Four dimensions with nothing named after them, a knowing that runs
> past what knowing holds, and then — the point of all of it — being filled to the brim.
