title: Those who make them become like them
updated: 2026-08-09

Six senses listed and denied, and then the sentence that turns the whole thing around.

## The inventory

*Mouths but cannot speak, eyes but cannot see, ears but cannot hear, noses but cannot smell.
**Their hands cannot feel, their feet cannot walk**, no sound comes from their throats.*

Seven organs, and every one is dead. The psalm builds an anatomy of something that has the
shape of a person and none of the functions.

## The verdict

*Those who make them **become just like them**, and so does everyone who trusts them.*

That is the argument of the psalm, and it is not that idols are useless. It is that people take
the shape of what they worship.

## Where the same mechanism runs the other way

*But we all, with unveiled face, **beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being
transformed into the same image** from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord.*

**Paul** describes the identical process with the opposite object. Look at something dead and
you go deaf and blind; look at Him and you are changed into the same image.

The Greek is **metamorphoumetha** — the word used of the Transfiguration.

## Who has all seven

*The eyes of the LORD are on the righteous, and **His ears are open to their cry**.*

*"He has **anointed Me** to preach the gospel to the poor… to open the eyes of the blind."*

Everything the idol cannot do, the psalm's God is described doing, and the gospels record
**Jesus** doing in person — speaking, seeing, hearing, touching, walking.

*That which was from the beginning, **which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes,
which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled**, concerning the Word of life.*

**John** opens his first letter with a list of senses, and every one of them is engaged.

## What the psalm says instead

*But **our God is in heaven; He does whatever He pleases**.*

And three times after the verdict: *trust in the LORD; He is their help and their shield.*

> **The sentence that turns it.** Not that the idols are powerless — that the people who make
> them end up like them. Worship is transformative either way; the only question in the psalm
> is what is being looked at.
