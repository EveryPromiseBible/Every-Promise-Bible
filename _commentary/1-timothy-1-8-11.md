---
key: 1 Timothy 1:8–11
title: "Who the law was written for"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul concedes everything his opponents want to say about the law, and then tells them who it is
addressed to.

## Lawfully

*The law is a good thing… **provided a man handles it the way law was meant to be handled**.*

**Ean tis auto nomimos chretai** — if anyone uses it lawfully. There is a play on words in the
Greek: it is possible to use the law unlawfully, and the men in Ephesus *wanting to be teachers of
the law* were doing exactly that.

Using it lawfully means using it for the purpose it was issued for. A tool is not honoured by being
used for a job it was not made for.

## Not drawn up with the good man in mind

*Law is **not drawn up with the good man in mind**.*

**Dikaio nomos ou keitai** — law is not laid down for a righteous person.

That is the sentence, and it is startling enough that most readers soften it. Paul does not say the
righteous keep the law easily. He says the legislation is not addressed to them at all — the way
traffic law is written for the road and not aimed at a man asleep in his bed.

And in Paul's vocabulary *righteous* is never a compliment about behaviour. It is a verdict: *being
justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus*. The righteous person
here is the one God has declared righteous.

## The list is the Ten Commandments

Read what follows in order and the shape appears. Men who kill their fathers and mothers — the
fifth. Murderers — the sixth. Those who sleep with anyone they please — the seventh. Those who
steal human beings and sell them — the eighth, in its most extreme form, since kidnapping a person
is the largest theft available. Liars and men who will swear to anything — the ninth.

Paul is walking down the second table of the Decalogue and naming, at each stop, the kind of person
that commandment exists to restrain.

Not one of them is a Christian.

## What healthy teaching is measured against

*Teaching measured against **the shining good news of the blessed God**.*

**Kata to euangelion tes doxes tou makariou theou** — according to the gospel of the glory of the
blessed God.

The direction of measurement is the whole argument. The gospel is not checked against the law. Sound
doctrine is checked against the gospel — and anything that has to be defended by pushing believers
back under the law has failed the test before the discussion starts.

*For sin shall not have dominion over you, **for you are not under law but under grace**.*

> **The law is good, and it is not addressed to you.** Paul never runs the law down; he calls it
> good, holy and just. He simply refuses to point it at people Christ has already made righteous.
> *The law was our tutor to bring us to Christ… but after faith has come, we are no longer under a
> tutor.* The tutor did his job. He walked you to the door and handed you over.
