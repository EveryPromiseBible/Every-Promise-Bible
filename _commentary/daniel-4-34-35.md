The order matters: he looks up first, and then his mind comes back.

## The timing

*And **when those days had passed**.*

Seven times, however long that was. The text gives no account of them from the inside.

## The first act

*I, Nebuchadnezzar, **lifted my eyes to heaven, and my understanding returned to me**.*

The Aramaic is **nitlet** — I lifted. And **mand'i alai yetuv** — my knowledge returned upon me.

## The sequence

The lifting is reported before the returning.

The chapter does not say his sanity came back and then he prayed. It says he looked up, and then
his mind was his again.

## What an ox cannot do

An ox's eyes do not go to the sky. The posture of the animal is downward.

The first human thing he does is raise his head.

## What he does next

*I **blessed the Most High**, and I **praised and honoured Him who lives forever**.*

Three verbs. The first use of his restored speech is a doxology.

## The title

**Illa'ah** — the Most High. The name used through the chapter, and the one in the sentence he was
given: *until you know that the Most High rules*.

He now uses it himself.

## The two clauses about the kingdom

*His dominion is **an everlasting dominion**, and His kingdom goes on **from one generation to the
next**.*

A man who has just lost a kingdom describing one that cannot be lost.

## The comparison

*All the peoples of the earth are **counted as nothing** before Him.*

The Aramaic is **kelah chashivin** — reckoned as nothing.

Isaiah: *the nations are as a drop in a bucket*.

## The reach

*He does as He pleases **with the armies of heaven** and **with those who live on the earth**.*

Both realms in one sentence.

## The last line

*No one can **stop His hand** or say to Him, "**What are You doing?**"*

The Aramaic is **lo yimcheh bideh** — none can strike His hand.

## Who else asks the question

*Woe to him who strives with his Maker… shall the clay say to him who forms it, "What are you
making?"* See the note on Isaiah 45:9–10.

And Job, who is asked *where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?* and answers *I
lay my hand over my mouth*.

## What is restored

*My reason returned to me, and **my honour and splendour returned to me**… **and excellent majesty was
added to me**.*

More than he had. The restoration overshoots.

## The closing sentence

*Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise and extol and honour the King of heaven, all of whose works are
truth, and His ways justice. **And those who walk in pride He is able to put down**.*

The last words of the chapter, by the man they were about.

## The son who did not read it

*"And you, Belshazzar, his son, **you have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this**."* See
the note on Daniel 5:22–23.

The document was published. The next king had it, and the sentence Daniel uses against him is that
he knew.

> **The order.** He lifts his eyes, and then his understanding returns. The chapter puts the looking
> up first.
