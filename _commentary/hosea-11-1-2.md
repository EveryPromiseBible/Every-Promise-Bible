A backward look at the exodus, and **Matthew** puts it in a story about a baby.

## The tense

*"**When Israel was a child, I loved him.**"*

The Hebrew is **ki na'ar Yisrael va'ohavehu** — when Israel was a youth, and I loved him.

The chapter that will spend twelve verses on Ephraim's idolatry opens by saying the affection came
first and came early.

## The title

*"**Out of Egypt I called My son.**"*

**Beni** — My son. Singular, and it is the nation.

The claim goes back to the first message Moses was given for Pharaoh: *thus says the LORD: Israel
is My son, My firstborn. So I say to you, let My son go that he may serve Me.*

## Who quotes it

*When he arose, he took the young Child and His mother by night and departed for Egypt, and was
there until the death of Herod, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the Lord through
the prophet, saying, "**Out of Egypt I called My Son**."*

**Matthew** 2, and the quotation has puzzled readers for centuries because Hosea 11:1 is plainly a
backward glance at the exodus, not a prediction.

**Matthew** is not misreading it. He is saying the Son who came up out of Egypt the first time was
a nation that then *kept sacrificing to the Baals* — and there is a second Son who goes down and
comes up and does not.

Where Israel failed in the wilderness, **Matthew** has Him tested forty days in one, answering each
temptation with a sentence out of Deuteronomy.

## What follows in Hosea

*"**But the more I called, the further they wandered from Me.**"*

The Hebrew is difficult and the sense is that the calling and the going were simultaneous.

*"**How often I wanted to gather your children together**, as a hen gathers her chicks under her
wings, **but you were not willing**!"*

**Matthew** 23, in the same gospel, and it is Hosea 11:2 in **Jesus**' voice over the same city.

## The verses after

*"**It was I who taught Ephraim to walk, lifting him up in My arms.** But they never understood
that I healed them. I drew them with cords of a man, with bands of love."*

A parent teaching a toddler to walk. It is the tenderest image of God in the prophets, and it is
in a chapter about a nation that would not stop worshipping idols.

*"**How can I give you up, Ephraim?**"* See the note on Hosea 11:8.

## The two sons

*"For God so loved the world that He gave **His only begotten Son**."*

*"This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased."*

Hosea's son is called out of Egypt and goes wrong. **Matthew**'s is called out of Egypt at two
years old, and the voice over the Jordan says what could not be said about the first.

> **What Matthew is doing.** Not mistaking a backward glance for a prediction. He is putting a
> second Son on the same road — down into Egypt, up out of it, into a wilderness — and reporting
> that this time it held.
