A father's instruction, and it ends by comparing two roads by how much you can see on
them.

## Who is speaking

*Listen, **my son**.*

Proverbs 4 opens *hear, my children, the instruction of a father*, and then does something
unusual — Solomon quotes his own father teaching him:

*When I was my father's son, tender and the only one in the sight of my mother, he also
taught me, and said to me, "**Let your heart retain my words**."*

Three generations in one chapter.

## The promise

*Take my words to heart, and **the years of your life will be many**.*

## The two verbs

*When you **walk**, nothing will hold you back; when you **run**, you will not stumble.*

The Hebrew for *hold back* is about a stride being cramped — the picture is of a path wide
enough to move on.

Walking and running are separated deliberately: the first is about obstruction, the second
about speed.

## The grip

*Hold on to my instruction, **do not let it go**, guard it well, **for it is your life**.*

Four imperatives in one line, and the last clause gives the reason: not *it will improve
your life*. It **is** your life.

## The four ways of avoiding

*Do not **set foot** on the path of the wicked, do not **walk** in the way of evil. **Avoid**
it, do not travel on it, **turn away** from it and pass on by.*

Five clauses saying one thing. Proverbs rarely repeats itself this hard.

The counsel is not about resisting on the road. It is about not being on it.

## What the wicked cannot do

*For they **cannot sleep unless they do wrong**, they **lose their rest unless they trip
someone up**.*

The Hebrew is *their sleep is taken away unless they cause someone to fall*.

An appetite that has to be fed before the day can end.

## Their diet

*They **feed on the bread of wickedness**, they **drink the wine of violence**.*

Staple food and drink. It is not an occasional thing; it is what they live on.

## The first road

*But **the path of the righteous is like the dawn, growing brighter and brighter until full
day**.*

The Hebrew is *the light of dawn that shines ever brighter unto the perfect day*.

Not a floodlight switched on. First light, and then more of it, on a curve.

The measure of the road is how much can be seen from it, and it increases.

## The second road

*The way of the wicked is **like deep darkness**; they **cannot even see what makes them
fall**.*

The Hebrew is **aphelah** — thick darkness, gloom.

The second clause is the sting. Not that they fall — that they never find out what over.

## The two comparisons

Both roads are described by visibility rather than by comfort or outcome. One gets
brighter; on the other the cause of the fall stays invisible.

## What the chapter says next

*Keep your heart **with all diligence**, for out of it spring the issues of life.*

And then a run of instructions about the parts of a body: the mouth, the eyes, the
eyelids, the feet.

*Ponder the path of your feet, and let all your ways be established.*

The Hebrew for *established* is **kun** — fixed, set firm.

## Where the dawn image goes

*Until the day dawns and **the morning star rises in your hearts**.* — Peter, of the
prophetic word.

And Malachi: *the Sun of Righteousness shall arise with healing in His wings.*

> **How the two roads are told apart.** Not by where they lead. Proverbs measures them by
> the light on them — one brightening toward noon, and one where a man cannot see what
> tripped him.
