Matthew's account of the sixth to the ninth hour, and it is three verses long.

## The hour

*From **the sixth hour, at midday**.*

Noon. The sun at its highest.

## The dark

*Darkness came over **the whole land**, and it **stayed until the ninth hour**.*

Three hours.

Passover falls at full moon, when a solar eclipse is astronomically impossible. Whatever this
was, it was not that.

## What is happening at the ninth hour

The evening sacrifice, and the Passover lambs being killed at the temple a short walk away.

## The cry

*And at about the ninth hour Jesus **cried out with a loud voice**.*

The Greek is **aneboesen phone megale**.

A man dying of crucifixion suffocates slowly. Volume is the last thing he has.

## The words

*"**Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani?**"*

Matthew keeps the Hebrew form **Eli**; Mark has the Aramaic **Eloi**. See the note on
Mark 15:33–36.

The difference matters for the next verse.

## The translation

*"**My God, My God, why have You abandoned Me?**"*

Psalm 22:1, and the psalm goes on to describe a great deal of what is happening on that hill:
*they pierced My hands and My feet*, *they divide My garments among them*, and *all who see Me
laugh Me to scorn*. See the note on Psalms 22:16–18.

Matthew has already recorded two of those in the previous paragraph.

## What the psalm ends with

*You have answered Me… I will declare Your name to My brethren; in the midst of the assembly I
will praise You.*

*All the ends of the world shall remember and turn to the LORD.*

And the final words in Hebrew: **ki asah** — He has done it.

The psalm that opens with abandonment closes with a finished work and the nations turning.

## The misunderstanding

*Some of those standing there heard it and said, "**He is calling for Elijah**."*

**Eli** and **Eliyahu**. Close, at a distance, in a crowd, from a man who had been scourged.

Elijah was expected before the day of the LORD — Malachi's closing sentence — and there was a
popular belief that he came to help the righteous in extremity.

## What they do about it

*Immediately one of them ran and took a sponge, filled it with sour wine and put it on a reed,
and offered it to Him to drink.*

*The rest said, "Let Him alone; **let us see if Elijah will come to save Him**."*

The last recorded human speech before the death is a proposal to watch.

## What happens next in Matthew

*And Jesus cried out again with a loud voice, and yielded up His spirit.*

*Then, behold, **the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom**; and the earth
quaked, and the rocks were split.*

Matthew alone adds the graves opened and many bodies of the saints raised.

And the centurion: *truly this was the Son of God!*

## The word

*Abandoned.*

The Greek is **egkatelipes** — left behind, forsaken.

It is the only recorded sentence from the cross in which He does not address God as *Father*.

## What the darkness had meant before

The ninth plague — three days of it, *darkness which may even be felt*, over Egypt only, and
*all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings*. See the note on
Exodus 10:21–29.

The plague immediately before the first Passover.

And Amos: *I will make the sun go down at noon, and I will darken the earth in broad
daylight*.

> **What He quoted.** Not a cry He composed. The first line of a psalm whose last two words in
> Hebrew are *He has done it*.
