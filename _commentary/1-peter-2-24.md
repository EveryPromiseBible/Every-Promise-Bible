Peter quotes Isaiah 53 and changes the tense, and the change is the whole point.

## The verb

*He hauled our sins up onto the wood Himself, in His own body.*

The Greek is **anaphero** — to carry up, to bear upward. It is the word used in
the Greek Old Testament of a priest carrying a sacrifice **up** onto the altar.

Peter, writing about a man nailed to a raised beam, chooses the altar word.

## "In His own body"

He is precise about the location of the transaction. Not in principle, not
representatively — in a body, on a piece of wood.

The word he uses for the cross is *xylon*, timber, the same word the Greek Old
Testament uses in the line about anyone hung on a tree being under a curse. Paul
quotes that verse in Galatians for the same reason.

## Isaiah, in the past tense

*You were healed by his wounds.*

Isaiah wrote *we are healed* — a prophet describing something ahead of him. Peter
writes **were**, aorist: done.

That is the difference the cross makes to the sentence. Isaiah is looking
forward; Peter is looking back at an accomplished thing.

## What it is for

*So that we would be dead to sin and alive for what is right.*

The purpose clause is worth keeping attached. Peter does not present the bearing
of sins as an end in itself but as the ground of a life lived differently — and
the two verbs are both about a state, not an effort.

> **Who he was writing to.** The paragraph is addressed to household slaves being
> treated unjustly, and the verses just before hold up Christ's response to
> suffering as the pattern: He did not retaliate, but *entrusted Himself to Him
> who judges justly.* The healing sentence arrives in a passage about people with
> no power, and Peter tells them what was carried for them.
