Four names for one person, and the second one is the problem.

## Where it comes

*Nevertheless the gloom will not be upon her who is distressed… **The people who walked in darkness
have seen a great light**.*

The northern territories — Zebulun and Naphtali — overrun by Assyria first.

Matthew quotes it of Galilee: *the people who sat in darkness have seen a great light*.

## The two verbs

*For to us **a child is born**, to us **a son is given**.*

The Hebrew is **yeled yullad-lanu ben nittan-lanu**.

Born, and given. One is human arrival, and the other is a gift from elsewhere.

The pair is deliberate.

## The government

*And **the government will rest upon his shoulders**.*

The Hebrew is **hammisrah al-shikmo** — the rule upon his shoulder.

**Misrah** — dominion. A rare noun, used twice, both in these verses.

## The first name

*Wonderful Counselor.*

The Hebrew is **pele yo'etz**.

**Pele** — a wonder, a miracle. The noun used of God's acts at the sea. It can stand alone —
*Wonder* — or govern the next word.

## The second

*Mighty God.*

The Hebrew is **El Gibbor**.

**El** — God. Isaiah uses **El Gibbor** of the LORD Himself one chapter later: *the remnant will
return to the Mighty God*.

The same two words, of a child.

## The third

*Everlasting Father.*

The Hebrew is **Avi-ad** — father of eternity, or father of perpetuity.

Not a claim about the Trinity's internal relations. A Semitic idiom: the father of something is
its source or its characteristic possessor.

## The fourth

*Prince of Peace.*

The Hebrew is **Sar Shalom**.

**Shalom** — wholeness, completeness, not merely the absence of war.

## The increase

*Of the increase of his government and of peace **there will be no end**.*

The Hebrew has an oddity: the word for *increase* is written with a closed final letter in the
middle of the word, the only such case in the Hebrew Bible.

The rabbis noticed it and argued about it for centuries.

## The throne

*He will **reign on the throne of David** and over his kingdom, to establish it and uphold it **with
justice and righteousness**.*

The promise to David: *your throne shall be established forever*. See the note on
2 Samuel 7:12–17.

## The duration

*From now and forever.*

The Hebrew is **me'attah ve'ad-olam**.

## The guarantee

*The **zeal of the LORD of hosts** will accomplish this.*

The Hebrew is **qin'at YHWH Tzeva'ot**.

**Qin'ah** — zeal, jealousy. Passionate insistence.

Nothing in the passage depends on the child, the throne, or the nation. The last line names who
will see to it.

## What Gabriel says

*"And the Lord God will give Him **the throne of His father David**. And He will reign over the house
of Jacob forever, and **of His kingdom there will be no end**."* See the note on
Luke 1:30–33.

Two phrases from this passage, quoted to a girl in Nazareth.

## Where the shoulder turns up

*And He, bearing His cross, went out.*

> **The two verbs.** Born, and given. One is a human arrival and the other is a gift from
> somewhere else, and the sentence holds both.
