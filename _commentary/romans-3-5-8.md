Paul quotes what people were saying about his preaching, and the quote is unflattering.

## The objection

*If our wrongdoing **only makes God's rightness stand out more clearly**, what do we
conclude? That **God is unfair to bring His anger down on us**?*

The argument: if my failure displays His faithfulness, punishing me looks unreasonable.

## The apology for raising it

*I am **putting it the way people put it**.*

The Greek is **kata anthropon lego** — I speak as a man.

Paul flags that he is voicing somebody else's argument and is uncomfortable holding it even
for a sentence.

## The answer

*No, of course not.*

The Greek is **me genoito** — the strongest negative available. It appears ten times in
Romans, and every one is a reply to a bad inference from grace.

*If God were unfair, **how could He ever judge the world at all**?*

The counter is short: a God who cannot judge fairly cannot judge, and everybody in the
argument already assumes He will.

## The objection pressed harder

*If my **falsehood only makes God's truth shine brighter and adds to His glory**, why am I
**still being judged as a sinner**?*

Same argument, one step further. Now the speaker is claiming his lie is a contribution.

## The reductio

*And why not go all the way and say, "**Let us do evil so that good can come of it**"?*

Paul does not answer this one with logic. He answers it with attribution.

## The slander

*That is exactly what **some people slander us with, insisting we teach it**.*

The Greek is **blasphemoumetha** — we are defamed.

Somebody was going round saying Paul taught *do evil that good may come*. He is not
constructing a hypothetical opponent; he is reporting an accusation in circulation.

## The verdict

*Anyone who actually argues that way is **rightly condemned**.*

One clause, and he moves on. He does not refute it.

## Why the accusation existed

Because of what he actually taught, which is in the next chapter: *to him who does not work
but believes on Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is accounted for righteousness*.
See the note on Romans 4:4–5.

And *where sin abounded, grace abounded much more*. See the note on Romans 5:20–21.

Nobody misrepresents a message about trying harder. The charge is evidence of what he was
preaching.

## Where he answers it properly

Three chapters later, and at length: *what shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that
grace may abound? Certainly not! **How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it?***
See the note on Romans 6:1–2.

The answer, when he gives it, is not a warning. It is a question about what happened to the
person.

## What he says just before this

*What advantage then has the Jew? **Much in every way!** Chiefly because to them were
committed the oracles of God.*

*For what if some did not believe? Will their unbelief make the faithfulness of God without
effect? Certainly not! Indeed, **let God be true but every man a liar**.*

That last line is quoted from Psalm 116, and it is the sentence the objection in verse 7 is
trying to exploit.

## Where the chapter goes

*There is none righteous, no, not one* — a chain of six Old Testament quotations — and then
*but now the righteousness of God apart from the law is revealed*.

The slander is answered in verse 8 and the actual doctrine arrives in verse 21.

> **What the accusation proves.** Nobody accused Paul of making obedience too easy. They
> accused him of teaching *let us do evil that good may come* — which is only a plausible
> misreading of one kind of message.
