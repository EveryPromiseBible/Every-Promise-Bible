The manna has one rule and one exception, and the exception is the Sabbath.

## The rule up to this point

*"Let no one leave any of it till morning."*

Some had ignored it: *but some of them left part of it until morning, and it bred worms and
stank*.

Gather it every day, and only what you need. An omer each.

And the note the chapter adds: *he who gathered much had nothing left over, and he who
gathered little had no lack*.

Paul quotes that line about the collection for Jerusalem.

## The sixth day

*On the sixth day they gathered **twice as much — two omers each**.*

They did it before being told to. Something in the ground provided double and they picked it
up.

*And the leaders of the community came and told Moses.*

The leaders report an anomaly. Nobody had explained the arrangement yet.

## The explanation

*"This is what the LORD meant: **Tomorrow is a day of rest, a holy Sabbath set apart for the
LORD**."*

The Hebrew is **shabbaton shabbat qodesh**.

This is the first appearance of the Sabbath as a command in Scripture. It arrives here, over
food, before Sinai and before the ten commandments.

## The instruction

*"So **bake what you want to bake, boil what you want to boil**, and **whatever is left over,
set it aside and keep it until morning**."*

The cooking is done the day before. The keeping is explicitly permitted — the one day the
standing rule is reversed.

## The exception

*They kept it as Moses commanded, and **this time it did not stink, and there were no maggots
in it**.*

The same substance, the same overnight period, and a different outcome.

Nothing about the food changed. What changed is which day it was.

## The confirmation

*"Eat it today, for today is a Sabbath to the LORD; **today you will not find any out in the
field**."*

*"Six days you gather it, but the seventh day is the Sabbath — **on that day there will be
none**."*

Said twice, and some went out looking anyway: *and it happened that some of the people went
out on the seventh day to gather, but they found none*.

## What the double portion means

Nobody had to work on the seventh day because the sixth had already supplied it.

The rest was not a gap in provision. It was made possible by a provision that arrived early.

## How long it went on

*And the children of Israel ate manna forty years, until they came to an inhabited land.*

Six days on, one day off, for forty years. Roughly two thousand consecutive weeks of the same
pattern, including the double day.

## When it stopped

*Then the manna ceased on the day after they had eaten the produce of the land; and the
children of Israel had manna no longer.*

The day after the Passover at Gilgal, on the other side of the Jordan. See the note on
Joshua 4:19–24.

## What it was called

*And the house of Israel called its name **Manna**.*

The Hebrew is **man hu** — *what is it?* They named it after their own question.

## Where a jar of it ended up

*"Fill an omer with it, to be kept for your generations."*

Inside the ark, with the tablets and Aaron's rod.

## What He says about it

*"Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and are dead… **I am the living bread which
came down from heaven**."*

And in the prayer: *give us this day our daily bread*. See the note on Matthew 6:9–13.

The Greek there is **epiousion** — a word that appears nowhere else in Greek literature.
Most read it as *for the coming day*.

> **What made the difference on the sixth day.** Nothing about the food. The same manna, kept
> the same length of time, and on that one night it did not rot.
