Peter is explaining a phenomenon, and his explanation runs backwards from the
noise in the street to a throne nobody present can see.

## The chain

- *This Jesus God has raised up, and we are all witnesses of it*
- *lifted up to the right hand of God*
- *having received from the Father the promised Holy Spirit*
- *He has poured out what you are seeing and hearing*

Four links: resurrection, ascension, receiving, pouring. Peter builds the whole
argument to explain a sound and a sight.

## What the crowd can check

*What you are seeing and hearing.* They are not being asked to accept an
invisible claim. Something audible and visible is happening in front of them, and
Peter says: here is what it proves.

The evidence for the ascension, in his argument, is the Spirit's arrival. Nobody
watched Jesus sit down at the right hand of God — but this is what it looks like
from below.

## "Having received… He has poured out"

The Son receives from the Father and gives to the people. Peter puts all three in
one sentence without explanation, and the church spent centuries working out what
he had said in passing.

## Why it mattered that day

The crowd's first suggestion was that these people were drunk at nine in the
morning. Peter's answer is not a defence of behaviour. It is a claim about
location: the man you crucified is at God's right hand, and this is Him acting
from there.

> **What the hearers do next.** *They were cut to the heart* and asked what to do
> — and Peter's answer includes *the promise is for you and your children and all
> who are far off.* The pouring out described here is presented as available to
> the crowd, not just to the hundred and twenty in the room.
