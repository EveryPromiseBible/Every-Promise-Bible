An instruction to be strong, and it names what does the strengthening.

## The command

*So then, my son, **let the grace that is in Christ Jesus be the thing that makes you strong**.*

The Greek is **endunamou** — be strengthened, and it is passive.

Not *strengthen yourself*. Be made strong, by something.

## Where the strength comes from

*The grace that is in Christ Jesus.*

**En te chariti** — in the grace. The strengthening happens inside it, not by effort applied to
it.

Paul uses the same passive of himself: *I thank Christ Jesus our Lord who has enabled me* —
**endunamosanti me** — and *I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me*. See the note
on Philippians 4:11–13.

## Why Timothy needs it

He is young, in charge of a difficult church, and Paul is in prison and not coming back.

*Do not be ashamed of the testimony of our Lord, nor of me His prisoner.*

The previous chapter has just named two men who deserted — *all those in Asia have turned away
from me* — and one who did not: *Onesiphorus often refreshed me, and was not ashamed of my
chain*. See the note on 2 Timothy 1:15.

## The second instruction

*And everything you heard me say **with plenty of witnesses standing there**.*

The Greek is **dia pollon marturon** — through many witnesses.

Nothing private. The content Timothy is to pass on was said in public and can be checked.

## What to do with it

*Put it into the hands of **people who can be trusted**, people who **will be able to teach others
in their turn**.*

The Greek is **parathou** — deposit it, entrust it. It is a banking word, used of goods left with
someone for safekeeping.

Paul uses the same root of God: *He is able to keep what I have committed to Him until that
Day*.

## The two qualifications

**Pistoi** — trustworthy. And **hikanoi** — competent, up to it.

Reliable and able. Paul does not ask for eloquence or seniority.

## The chain

Paul → Timothy → trustworthy people → others.

Four links, and the letter that lays it out is written by a man whose own link is about to be
cut.

*The time of my departure is at hand.* See the note on 2 Timothy 4:6–8.

## What comes immediately after

Three pictures, in a row: the soldier who does not entangle himself with civilian affairs, the
athlete who is not crowned unless he competes by the rules, and the farmer who works before he
eats.

*The hard-working farmer must be first to partake of the crops.* See the note on
2 Timothy 2:3–7.

Strength that comes from grace, and then three images of effort — and Paul puts them in that
order.

## What the grace already did

*Who has saved us and called us with a holy calling, **not according to our works, but according
to His own purpose and grace** which was given to us in Christ Jesus **before time began**.* See
the note on 2 Timothy 1:8–10.

Given before there was anybody to give it to. That is the grace Timothy is told to be
strengthened in.

> **The verb.** **Endunamou** is passive — *be made strong* — and the agent is named in the same
> sentence: the grace that is in Christ Jesus.
