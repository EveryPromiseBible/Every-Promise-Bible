A servant bows in the dirt beside a well because a girl mentioned her grandfather.

## The errand

Abraham had made him swear: *you shall go to my country and to my family, and take a wife for my
son Isaac*.

He is unnamed in the chapter. Genesis calls him *the servant*, *the man*, and *the oldest servant
of his house* — and gives him the longest chapter in the book.

## What he had prayed

*"**O LORD God of my master Abraham, please give me success this day**… Let it be that the young
woman to whom I say, 'Please let down your pitcher that I may drink,' and she says, 'Drink, and
I will also give your camels a drink' — let her be the one."* See the note on
Genesis 24:12–14.

The Hebrew for *give me success* is **haqreh-na lephanai** — cause it to happen before me.

**Qarah** — to meet, to happen, to occur by chance. He asks for a chance encounter, from the one
who arranges them.

## What happened

*And it happened, **before he had finished speaking**, that behold, Rebekah… came out with her
pitcher on her shoulder.*

She gave him a drink, and then *I will draw water for your camels also, until they have finished
drinking*.

Ten camels, after a journey. A thirsty camel drinks twenty-five gallons. She kept going back to
the well.

## The question

He gives her a gold nose ring and two bracelets, and asks *whose daughter are you?*

*"I am the daughter of Bethuel, **Milcah's son, whom she bore to Nahor**."*

Nahor is Abraham's brother. She is inside the family, at the well, at the hour he prayed.

## The response

*At that, the man **bowed his head and worshiped the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **vayiqqod… vayishtachu** — he bowed low and prostrated himself.

By a well, in front of a girl he has just met, in a foreign town.

## The blessing

*"**Blessed be the LORD, the God of my master Abraham**," he said, "**who has not abandoned His
kindness and faithfulness to my master**."*

The Hebrew is **chasdo va'amitto** — His **chesed** and His **emet**.

The same pair Jacob uses two chapters later — *I am not worthy of the least of all the kindness
and faithfulness* — and the pair that becomes *grace and truth* in John's prologue. See the note
on Genesis 32:9–12.

## What he says about himself

*"**As for me, the LORD has led me on the very road** to the house of my master's relatives."*

The Hebrew is **baderek nachani YHWH** — in the way the LORD led me.

**Anochi** — *as for me* — is emphatic. He puts himself into the sentence.

## The tense

*Has led.* Past.

He is standing at the well. He has not yet met the family, been welcomed, made the request, or
been refused or accepted. Nothing is settled.

He calls it done at the point where the sign came in.

## Where the leading was felt

Not at the start. He set out with ten camels and no address, and only prayed for the sign when
he arrived.

The guidance is identified retrospectively, which is how the chapter presents it.

## What he does next

Refuses to eat until he has said his errand: *I will not eat until I have told about my
business*.

And then tells the whole story again, at length — the oath, the prayer, the well, the girl — so
that Genesis records it twice.

## The family's answer

*"**The thing comes from the LORD; we cannot speak to you either bad or good**."*

## Rebekah's

*"**Will you go with this man?**" And she said, "**I will go**."*

The same two words, in Hebrew, that Abraham's own call required of him.

> **The tense.** *The LORD has led me.* Said at the well, before the family had been met, the
> request made, or a single thing agreed.
