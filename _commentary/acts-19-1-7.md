Paul arrives in a city and finds a dozen men who are half-informed.

## The setting

*While **Apollos was at Corinth**, Paul traveled through the inland regions and came to
**Ephesus**.*

Apollos has just been introduced at the end of chapter 18: *an eloquent man and mighty in the
Scriptures… **though he knew only the baptism of John**.*

Aquila and Priscilla had taken him aside and *explained to him the way of God more
accurately*.

Luke puts the two stories side by side. Same gap, two different remedies.

## The question

*"**Have you received the Holy Spirit since you came to believe?**"*

Paul asks it on arrival. Something about them prompted the question.

## The answer

*"**We have not so much as heard whether there is a Holy Spirit**."*

The Greek can be read as *we have not heard whether the Holy Spirit has been given* — which
fits better, since John the Baptist had preached about the Spirit and any Jew knew the Spirit
from the Scriptures.

Either way: they had not heard that the promise had arrived.

## The second question

*"**Into what, then, were you baptized?**"*

Paul reasons backwards from the gap to its cause.

*"Into John's baptism."*

## What John's baptism was

*"John baptized with a baptism that went with **a change of mind**, telling the people to
**believe in the One coming after him**."*

The Greek is **metanoias** — repentance, a changed mind.

John's own account of it: *I indeed baptize you with water… but He who is coming after me is
mightier than I. **He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire**.*

John's baptism was a pointer. It was working exactly as designed, and it was not the
destination.

These men had stopped at the signpost.

## What Paul adds

*"That is, **in Jesus the Messiah**."*

One name, and it closes the gap.

## What happened

*When they heard this, they were **baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus**.*

The only recorded rebaptism in the New Testament.

## The laying on of hands

*And when Paul **laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came on them**, and they **spoke in
tongues and prophesied**.*

The same pattern as Samaria in chapter 8, and the same signs as Cornelius' house in chapter
10 — where nobody laid hands on anybody and the Spirit fell mid-sermon. See the note on
Acts 10:44–48.

Luke records the variations without systematising them. Jerusalem, Samaria, Caesarea and
Ephesus all happen differently.

## The number

*There were **about twelve men** in all.*

Luke counts them. A small group, in a large city, and Ephesus becomes the base for two years:
*so that all who dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks*.

## What Paul does next

Three months in the synagogue, then the school of Tyrannus daily for two years.

And then the riot: *great is Diana of the Ephesians!* — two hours of shouting in a theatre,
because the silversmiths' trade in shrines was collapsing.

## What he later writes to them

*In Him you also trusted, after you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation; in
whom also, having believed, **you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise**, who is the
guarantee of our inheritance.*

And: *be filled with the Spirit*. See the note on Ephesians 5:18–21.

> **What Paul reasoned from.** A gap, back to its cause. He asked what they had received,
> then what they had been baptized into, and found men who had stopped at John's signpost.
