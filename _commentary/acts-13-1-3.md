The most consequential meeting in Acts, and Luke starts by listing who was in the room.

## The place

*In the church at **Antioch**.*

Not Jerusalem. The third city of the empire, in Syria, where the gospel had gone after the
persecution scattered everybody.

*And the disciples were first called Christians in Antioch.*

The centre of gravity moves here, and everything from chapter 13 to the end of the book goes out
from it.

## The five

*Barnabas.*

A Levite from Cyprus who sold a field and laid the money at the apostles' feet, and who vouched
for Saul when Jerusalem would not have him.

*Simeon who was called **Niger**.*

Latin for *black*. The name suggests an African.

*Lucius of **Cyrene**.*

North Africa, in modern Libya.

*Manaen, who had been **brought up alongside Herod the tetrarch**.*

The Greek is **suntrophos** — a foster-brother, or a boy raised with a royal child as a
companion.

Herod Antipas killed John the Baptist and mocked Jesus at His trial. The man who grew up beside
him is teaching in Antioch.

*And Saul.*

Last on the list. He has been in Tarsus for years, and Barnabas had to go and fetch him.

## What the list contains

A Cypriot Levite, an African, a Libyan, a foster-brother of a king, and a former persecutor.

Luke did not have to name them, and he does.

## What they were doing

*While they were **worshiping the Lord and fasting**.*

The Greek is **leitourgounton** — from which *liturgy*. It is the Septuagint word for priests
serving at the altar.

Applied here to five men in a room, and Luke uses no other word for what they were doing.

## The interruption

*The Holy Spirit said, "**Set apart for Me Barnabas and Saul, for the work I have called them
to**."*

The Greek is **aphorisate** — separate, mark off.

The same root Paul uses of himself in Romans: *called to be an apostle, **separated** to the
gospel of God*.

*For the work I have called them to* — the calling had already happened. This is the release of
it.

## Who is sent

The two best men they had.

Barnabas is the reason the Antioch church exists — Jerusalem had sent him to look into it. Saul
is the one Barnabas fetched.

The church gives away its leadership.

## What they did

*So after they had **fasted and prayed and laid their hands on them, they sent them off**.*

Three actions, and then the release.

The Greek is **apelusan** — dismissed, let go.

## Who actually sent them

The next verse: *so, **being sent out by the Holy Spirit**, they went down to Seleucia*.

Luke says it twice, two ways. The church laid hands on them, and the Spirit sent them. See the
note on Acts 13:4–8.

## What comes out of it

Three missionary journeys. Cyprus, Galatia, Macedonia, Achaia, Asia. The letters to the
Galatians, Thessalonians, Corinthians, Romans, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians.

Everything after this in the New Testament, more or less, traces back to five men fasting in
Antioch.

## Where they report back

*Now when they had come and gathered the church together, they **reported all that God had done
with them**, and that He had opened the door of faith to the Gentiles.*

They come home and tell them.

> **Who was in the room.** A Levite, an African, a Libyan, a king's foster-brother, and the man
> who had been arresting Christians. Luke names all five before anything happens.
