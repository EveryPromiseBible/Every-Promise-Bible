One sentence in this passage is doing quiet work, and it is easy to read past.

## What every other woman did

The chapter has already explained the arrangement: each young woman went in to
the king once, and could take with her whatever she asked for from the harem.
One night, one chance, and an open request.

You can imagine what most of them took.

## What Esther took

*She asked for nothing except what Hegai, the king's eunuch and keeper of the
women, suggested.*

Hegai had been running that household for years. He knew the king. Esther, with
one opportunity and permission to ask for anything, asked the man who actually
knew what he was talking about.

It is not passivity — she is a woman in an impossible position playing the one
card available with unusual coolness. It is judgement.

## What she did not take

*She **requested nothing but what Hegai the king's eunuch advised**.*

Every other woman took whatever she wanted from the harem to the king's chamber. Esther takes
the advice of the man who knew the king, and nothing else.

## The word behind the whole book

**Chen** — favour. *Esther obtained favour in the sight of all who saw her.* And again: *she
obtained grace and favour in his sight more than all the virgins.*

It is the word said over Noah at the bottom of the worst page in Genesis, and over Mary at the
annunciation — *you have found favour with God*. See the note on Genesis 6:5–8.

The Greek Old Testament renders **chen** with **charis**, which is the New Testament's word for
grace.

**Paul**: *by the grace of God I am what I am, and His grace toward me was not in vain.*

## What she brought

Nothing of her own. She arrives with borrowed advice and receives favour, and the book records
both without connecting them to any quality in her.

*For **by grace you have been saved through faith**, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift
of God.*

> **A book that never mentions God.** Esther is the only book in the Bible in
> which God's name does not appear, and moments like this are why readers have
> always felt Him in it anyway: nothing miraculous happens, and yet the details
> keep landing right. The story is told entirely at ground level — a favour here,
> a sleepless night there — and the shape only becomes visible from the far end
> of it.

## The word the chapter keeps using

*Esther **obtained favour** in the sight of all who looked at her.* The Hebrew is
**chen** — grace, unearned favour, the same word used of Noah finding grace in the
eyes of the LORD.

The chapter uses it about her again and again. She wins the position without
qualifications: no family, no wealth, no nation to speak for her, an orphan and a
captive. What she has is favour, and the writer says so every time she advances.

It is what carries her in chapter 2 and it is what saves her life in chapter 5,
when a golden sceptre is held out to a woman who came in unsummoned. See the note
on Esther 5:1–2.
