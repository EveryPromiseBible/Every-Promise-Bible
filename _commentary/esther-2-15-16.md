One sentence in this passage is doing quiet work, and it is easy to read past.

## What every other woman did

The chapter has already explained the arrangement: each young woman went in to
the king once, and could take with her whatever she asked for from the harem.
One night, one chance, and an open request.

You can imagine what most of them took.

## What Esther took

*She asked for nothing except what Hegai, the king's eunuch and keeper of the
women, suggested.*

Hegai had been running that household for years. He knew the king. Esther, with
one opportunity and permission to ask for anything, asked the man who actually
knew what he was talking about.

It is not passivity — she is a woman in an impossible position playing the one
card available with unusual coolness. It is judgement.

> **A book that never mentions God.** Esther is the only book in the Bible in
> which God's name does not appear, and moments like this are why readers have
> always felt Him in it anyway: nothing miraculous happens, and yet the details
> keep landing right. The story is told entirely at ground level — a favour here,
> a sleepless night there — and the shape only becomes visible from the far end
> of it.
