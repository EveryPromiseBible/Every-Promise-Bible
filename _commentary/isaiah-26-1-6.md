A song about a city, with the best-known line in Isaiah in the middle of it.

## The walls

*We have **a strong city**. **He makes salvation its walls** and its rampart.*

The defences are not masonry. Salvation is the wall.

Isaiah is writing to people whose actual city walls were about to be tested by
Assyria.

## Who comes in

*Open the gates, that **the righteous nation may enter, the nation that keeps
faith**.*

The Hebrew is *keeps truth* — **emunim**, faithfulness, the root of *amen*.

## The line

*You keep him in **perfect peace** whose **mind is stayed on You**, because **he trusts
in You**.*

The Hebrew for *perfect peace* is **shalom shalom** — peace, peace. The word twice.

Hebrew has no superlative form; doubling is how it says *complete*. Holy, holy, holy
works the same way.

## "Stayed"

**Samak** — to lean, to prop, to rest one's weight on. It is the verb used of a man
laying his hand on the head of a sacrifice and pressing down. See the note on
Leviticus 1:10–13.

Not *thinks about*. Leans on.

## The order

Trust first, then the leaning, then the peace. Isaiah gives the causal chain in the
sentence: *because he trusts in You.*

## The reason

*Trust in the LORD forever, for **the LORD, the LORD Himself, is an everlasting
Rock**.*

The Hebrew is *the Rock of Ages* — **tsur olamim**, and the hymn takes its title from
here.

## What He does to the other city

*He has **brought low those who lived on high**, the lofty city He lays low. He lays it
low to the ground. He casts it into the dust.*

Three verbs of bringing down, one after another.

## Who tramples it

*The foot tramples it down, **the feet of the poor, the steps of the needy**.*

The people with nothing walk over what the high city becomes.

## What the chapter is

*In that day this song will be sung* — a song written for a day that has not arrived,
given to people to learn in advance.

Isaiah 24 to 27 is often called the little apocalypse: the earth broken open, the
feast on the mountain, death swallowed up forever, and *the LORD will wipe away tears
from all faces.*

This song sits inside it.

## What the leaning verb is doing

**Samak** — to press one's weight down. The verb used of a hand laid on the head of a sacrifice.

That is not a metaphor for concentration. It is the gesture of transferring what is yours onto
something that will carry it away.

*Who Himself **bore our sins in His own body on the tree**.*

*And **the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds
through Christ Jesus**.*

**Paul**'s verb is **phroureo** — to garrison. Isaiah has salvation for a wall and the mind
leaning; **Philippians** has peace as the sentry on it.

## Peace, peace

**Shalom shalom** — the doubling that makes a Hebrew superlative.

*Therefore, having been **justified by faith, we have peace with God** through our Lord **Jesus
Christ**.*

*"**Peace I leave with you, My peace I give to you**; not as the world gives do I give to you."*

Two peaces in the New Testament as well: one made at the cross and one handed over in an upper
room.

*And **He Himself is our peace**.*

## The Rock of Ages

*Tsur olamim* — the everlasting Rock.

*And all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed
them, and **that Rock was Christ**.*

> **What the chapter says a few verses later.** *With my soul I have desired You in the
> night; yes, by my spirit within me I will seek You early.* The man singing about
> perfect peace says he was awake in the dark looking for God.
