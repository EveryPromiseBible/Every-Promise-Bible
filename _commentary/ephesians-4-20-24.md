Three infinitives, and they are all about clothing.

## What comes before

A description of the Gentile life they came out of: *the futility of their mind, having
their understanding darkened… past feeling, they have given themselves over to
lewdness*.

The Greek for *past feeling* is **apelgekotes** — having stopped feeling pain. Callused.

## The turn

*But **that is not how you came to know Christ**.*

The Greek is *you have not so learned Christ* — and the object of the verb is a person, not
a body of teaching.

Nobody else in Greek literature writes *learn Christ*. Paul makes the phrase up.

## The parenthesis

*Assuming you have really **heard Him and been taught in Him**, and **the truth is in
Jesus**.*

Heard *Him* — not about Him. Taught *in* Him.

## The first infinitive

*That you **take off, like a worn-out garment, the person you used to be**.*

The Greek is **apothesthai** — put off, lay aside, as clothes at the end of a day.

The same verb is used of the witnesses laying their coats at the feet of Saul at Stephen's
stoning.

## What the old self is doing

*The old self **that is rotting away under desires that keep lying to you**.*

The Greek is **phtheiromenon** — being corrupted, present tense: the process is ongoing.

And the desires are **tes apates** — of deceit. The lying is what they are made of.

The old self is not dangerous because it is strong. It is described as decaying, and its
grip is a false promise.

## The middle infinitive

*That you **be made new in the spirit of your mind**.*

The Greek is **ananeousthai** — a present passive. Be being renewed, continuously, by
somebody else.

The two clothing verbs are aorist — done, at a point. The renewing between them is present
and ongoing.

## The third infinitive

*And that you **put on the new self**.*

**Endusasthai** — the dressing verb. The same one Paul uses of the resurrection body: *this
mortal must put on immortality*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:53–55.

## What the new self is

*The one **created to be like God**, in the **righteousness and holiness that come of the
truth**.*

The Greek is **ktisthenta** — created. An aorist passive participle: it was made, and God
made it.

*According to God* — **kata theon**, on the pattern of God.

Genesis is behind it: *let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness*. Paul's
version of the new man is a return to the original specification.

Colossians has the same pair: *put off the old man with his deeds, and have put on the new
man who is **renewed in knowledge according to the image of Him who created him***.

## The order

Off, renewed, on. And the new garment is not manufactured by the wearer — it is described in
the passive, already created.

## What follows immediately

*Therefore, **putting away lying**, let each one speak truth with his neighbour.*

The same verb, **apothemenoi**, laying aside.

And then the list runs on: anger that does not carry into the next day, no more stealing but
working with the hands, no corrupt word, no bitterness or wrath or clamour.

The whole practical section of Ephesians hangs off the three infinitives.

## The last one in the list

*And **be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, just as God in Christ
forgave you**.*

The standard is not an amount. It is a precedent.

> **What the old coat is doing while you wear it.** Rotting — Paul says so in the present
> tense, and the desires holding it on are described as liars.
