The Father speaks aloud twice in Matthew's Gospel. This is the first, and the
timing is the whole point.

## When it is said

Jesus has not preached a sermon, healed anybody, called a disciple or told a
parable. His public ministry begins in the next chapter, after forty days in the
desert.

The words come first.

## What is said

*This is My Son, the One I love. My delight is in Him.*

Three statements: identity, love, and pleasure. Nothing is conditional and
nothing refers to output.

The Greek behind *my delight is in Him* is a verb about being well pleased,
taking pleasure in something — and it is in a tense that reads as a settled
state rather than a reaction to a recent event.

## Two texts underneath it

The sentence is stitched from two places every listener would have known:

- *You are my Son* — Psalm 2, spoken of the anointed king
- *in whom my soul delights* — Isaiah 42, the opening of the first Servant Song

King and servant, in one line. Matthew's readers would have heard both, and the
second one leads to the servant who is pierced for transgressions.

> **Where else the words turn up.** On the mountain at the transfiguration, the
> same voice says nearly the same thing and adds three words: *listen to Him.* The
> Father's public statements about the Son are remarkably consistent, and neither
> of them is about performance.
