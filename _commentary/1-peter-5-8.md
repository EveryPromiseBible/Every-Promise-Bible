Two short commands and then a description.

## The commands

*Keep your head clear. **Stay awake**.*

The Greek is **nepho** — to be sober, unintoxicated, clear-minded — and **gregoreo**, to
be awake, to keep watch.

Both are single words in the Greek and both are imperatives. Nothing about fighting yet.

## Who is writing it

The man who was told *watch and pray* in Gethsemane and fell asleep three times, and who
was told at the supper table that Satan had asked for him. See the note on
Luke 22:31–34.

He uses the same verb here that Jesus used on him in the garden.

## The enemy named

*You have an enemy — **the devil**.*

The Greek is **antidikos**, adversary — a legal word, the opposing party in a lawsuit.
And **diabolos**, the slanderer, the one who throws accusations across.

Both words come out of a courtroom rather than a battlefield.

## What he is doing

*He is out **walking around**.*

The Greek is **peripateo** — going about. It is what the accuser says he has been doing
in Job: *from going to and fro on the earth, and from walking back and forth on it.*
See the note on Job 1:6–12.

## The roar

*With **a lion's roar in his throat**.*

The Greek is *as a roaring lion*.

A lion that roars is a lion announcing itself. Roaring is what a lion does when it is
not currently in a position to bite — the sound travels five miles, and it is meant to
be heard.

The comparison is *as*. Peter says he is **like** one.

## What he is after

*Hunting for **somebody to eat**.*

The Greek is *seeking whom he may devour*. **Seeking** — he is looking, which means he
has not found.

## Who else is called a lion

*The **Lion of the tribe of Judah**, the Root of David, has prevailed.*

And Revelation's answer to the announcement: John turns to look at the Lion and sees *a
Lamb as though it had been slain.*

## What Peter says to do about it

Not run and not argue. The next verse: *stand up to him. **Plant your feet in the
faith**.* See the note on 1 Peter 5:9.

Resist, in the Greek, is **anthistemi** — to set yourself against, to hold ground.

## What comes immediately before

*Cast **all your care** upon Him, for He cares for you.*

Peter puts the anxiety verse and the lion verse back to back, in that order. The
watchfulness he asks for is not the same thing as worry, and he removes the worry first.

> **What is being described.** A prowler, making noise, looking for somebody who is not
> paying attention. Peter's instruction is to be sober and awake — and then, in the next
> breath, to stand still.
