Three verses that appear to contradict themselves, and the tension is the point.

## The proverb

*"**Jesus Himself had said that a prophet gets no honour in his own country.**"*

And then, immediately: *yet when He arrived in Galilee, the Galileans welcomed Him.*

**John** states the rule and then reports the exception in the next sentence, without smoothing it.

## Why the welcome

*"**Because they had seen everything He did in Jerusalem at the festival.**"*

That is the reason **John** gives, and it is not a compliment. The welcome is on the strength of the
signs, and the same evangelist has already told us what **Jesus** thinks of that:

*Now when He was in Jerusalem at the Passover… many believed in His name when they saw the signs
which He did. **But Jesus did not commit Himself to them**, because He knew all men.*

**John** 2, and the verb is the same one: they *believed* in Him and He did not *believe* in them.

## The next thing that happens

The official from Capernaum whose son is dying, and **Jesus**' first words to him:

*"**Unless you people see signs and wonders, you will by no means believe.**"*

And then the man is given nothing to see. He is told *go your way; your son lives*, and **John**
notes: *so the man believed the word that Jesus spoke to him, and he went his way.*

He walks twenty miles on a sentence.

## What John is setting up

Faith that runs on what has been watched, against faith that runs on what has been said.

*Then **Jesus** said to him, "Thomas, because you have seen Me, you have believed. **Blessed are those
who have not seen and yet have believed**."*

*So then **faith comes by hearing**, and hearing by the word of God.*

*For **we walk by faith, not by sight**.*

## His own country

Which country **John** means has been argued for centuries — Judea, Nazareth, or Galilee at large.

The line lands either way. **Luke** 4 has the Nazareth synagogue taking Him to a cliff, and **John** 7
records that *even His brothers did not believe in Him*.

> **The reason for the welcome.** *Because they had seen everything He did.* John states it flatly,
> and the next paragraph has Jesus refusing to give a dying boy's father anything to see.
