One verse, and it contains the thing Jonah was afraid of.

## What was preached

*"**Forty more days, and Nineveh will be overthrown.**"*

Five words in Hebrew — **od arba'im yom veNineveh nehpakhet**. It is the shortest recorded sermon
in Scripture, and it contains no offer, no condition, no mention of God's name and no instruction.

*"Yet forty days."* There is not an *unless* in it.

## What happened

*"And the people of Nineveh **believed God**."*

The Hebrew is **vayya'aminu anshei Nineveh be'Elohim** — the same construction used of Abraham in
Genesis 15: *he believed in the LORD, and He accounted it to him for righteousness.*

*And **Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness**.*

**Paul** builds two chapters on that verb in Genesis. The narrator of Jonah uses it of Assyrians.

## The king

*"Let everyone call urgently on God. Let them **turn from their evil ways**… **Who knows? God may
yet relent** and turn from His fierce anger, so that we will not perish."*

**Mi yodea** — who knows. He has been given no promise and he says so. The whole city fasts on a
maybe.

## The verse

*"When God saw what they did, **how they turned from their evil ways**, He relented. He did not bring
on them the destruction He had threatened, and **He spared them**."*

The Hebrew is **vayyinnachem** — and God relented, was moved to compassion, changed His course.

And **nehpakhet**, the verb in Jonah's sermon — *overthrown* — is the word used of Sodom. It is also
the word for being turned around. The city was overthrown; the direction of the overturning was not
what anybody expected.

## Why Jonah ran

*"Isn't this what I said while I was still at home? That is why I fled… for **I knew that You are a
gracious and merciful God, slow to anger and abundant in lovingkindness, One who relents from doing
harm**."*

Chapter 4, and it is a quotation — the self-description God gave Moses in the cleft of the rock.
See the note on Exodus 34:5–7.

Jonah's complaint is that the creed is true. He can recite it, and he resents it, and the whole
book is his objection to the character of God.

## What Jesus does with the Ninevites

*"**The men of Nineveh will rise up in the judgment with this generation and condemn it, because they
repented at the preaching of Jonah; and indeed a greater than Jonah is here.**"*

**Matthew** 12. He puts Assyrians in the witness box against Israel, and the point of comparison is
the quality of the preacher.

Nineveh turned on five words from a man who did not want to be there and never mentioned mercy.

*The Lord is not slack concerning His promise, as some count slackness, but is longsuffering toward
us, **not willing that any should perish** but that all should come to repentance.*

*"Likewise, I say to you, **there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who
repents**."*

## Who did not rejoice

*But to Jonah this seemed terribly wrong, and **he burned with anger**.*

**Luke** 15 has an elder brother standing outside a party for the same reason.

> **What the sermon contained.** No condition and no offer — *yet forty days*. The king's response
> is *who knows?*, and the whole city fasts on it. And the one person in the book who knew for
> certain that God relents is the one who ran.
