A boy hears his name in the dark and gets out of bed, and he is right about everything except who is calling.

## The setting

The verse before: *the word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no widespread
revelation*.

Eli is old and nearly blind, his sons are corrupt priests, and the lamp of God has not yet gone
out. See the note on 1 Samuel 3:1–3.

## The first call

*Then the LORD called, "**Samuel!**" And Samuel answered, "**Here I am**."*

The Hebrew is **hineni** — the word Abraham used on the mountain and Moses at the bush.

He answers correctly. He simply attributes it to the wrong person.

## What he does

*He ran to Eli and said, "Here I am — you called me."*

**He ran.** A boy who is not resentful about being woken and not slow about it.

*"I didn't call," Eli said. "Go back and lie down."*

## The second

*Again the LORD called, "Samuel!" And Samuel got up and went to Eli.*

*"I didn't call, **my son**," Eli said.*

The address softens. Eli has two sons of his own, and he calls this one *my son*.

## The narrator's explanation

*Now **Samuel did not yet know the LORD**; the word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him.*

Which is placed here deliberately. Samuel has been serving in the tabernacle since he was
weaned, wearing a linen ephod, ministering before the LORD.

He has the job, the clothes, and the location, and the narrator says he does not yet know Him.

## The third

*A third time the LORD called, "Samuel!"*

Three times, and each time the boy gets up and goes to a man.

The voice does not change tactics, raise itself, or explain. It says the name again.

## What Eli works out

*Then **Eli understood: it was the LORD calling the boy**.*

The Hebrew is **vayaven Eli** — Eli discerned.

His own house is under judgment. A man of God has already come and told him what is coming. He
has every reason not to want another word from the LORD in that building.

He tells the boy how to answer it anyway.

## The instruction

*"Go and lie down, and if He calls you, say, '**Speak, LORD, for Your servant is listening**.'"*

The Hebrew is **dabber YHWH ki shomea avdecha**.

Eli, who cannot hear, teaches the boy who can.

## What the boy actually says

When it comes, Samuel drops the divine name: *speak, for Your servant hears*.

The narrator records the difference and does not comment on it.

## What the message turns out to be

*Behold, I will do something in Israel at which both ears of everyone who hears it will tingle…
**I will judge his house forever for the iniquity which he knows about**.*

The first word Samuel ever receives is against the man who taught him how to receive it.

*So Samuel lay down until morning… **and Samuel was afraid to tell Eli the vision***. See the note
on 1 Samuel 3:15–18.

And Eli's answer: *it is the LORD. Let Him do what seems good to Him.*

## How it ends

*So Samuel grew, and the LORD was with him and **let none of his words fall to the ground***.

The Hebrew image is of an arrow or a word dropping short.

*And all Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew that Samuel had been established as a prophet of the
LORD.*

## What the three trips were

Not wasted. A boy learning that the voice was worth getting up for before he knew whose it was.

> **What the narrator says at the second call.** *Samuel did not yet know the LORD.* Said of a boy
> who had lived in the tabernacle since he was weaned and wore the linen ephod.
