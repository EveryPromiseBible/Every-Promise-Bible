An old man who had kept a king alive is offered a place at his table, and lists
reasons why not.

## What he had done

*It was he who had **kept the king supplied** while David stayed at Mahanaim.*

David was in flight from Absalom, with an army and no supply line. Chapter 17 gives
the inventory Barzillai brought out: beds, basins, wheat, barley, flour, parched
grain, beans, lentils, honey, curds, sheep and cheese.

*For they said, "The people are hungry and weary and thirsty in the wilderness."*

He backed the true king when the throne was in doubt and it was dangerous to do so.

## The invitation

*"**Cross over with me. Come to Jerusalem, and I will care for you.**"*

Open-ended, from a restored king, to the man who had fed him. There is no condition
on it.

## The answer

*"How many years do I have left…? **I am eighty now.**"*

And then a run of questions, all in the negative:

- *"Can I still **tell good from bad**?"*
- *"Can I **taste** what I eat and drink?"*
- *"Can I still **hear** the voices of singers, men and women?"*
- *"Why should your servant be **a burden** to my lord the king?"*

Judgement, taste, hearing — he inventories what he has lost, and concludes from the
list that the invitation is not for him.

## Where he wants to go instead

*"**Let me go back home, and let me die in my own town, near the graves of my
father and mother.**"*

He asks to go back and be buried where his parents are. That is his stated
ambition.

## What he sends instead of himself

*"But here is **your servant Kimham** — let him cross over with my lord the king."*

Most readers take Kimham for his son. The place at the table gets filled; Barzillai
is not in it.

He crosses *a little way over the Jordan*, takes the king's kiss and blessing, and
goes home.

## Set beside Caleb

Caleb, at eighty-five, is five years older than Barzillai here, and this is what he
says:

*"I am as strong this day as on the day Moses sent me; just as my strength was
then, so now is my strength for war… **give me this mountain**" — the one with the
giants and the fortified cities on it.*

Two old men, five years apart. One counts what he can no longer taste; the other
asks for the hill country. See the note on Joshua 14:6–15.

## What David does with it

He does not argue. *The king kissed Barzillai and blessed him, and Barzillai went
home.*

Nothing is taken away from him, and nothing is forced on him. The offer stood and
he declined it, and the record is kind to him throughout — David remembers him on
his deathbed and tells Solomon to show kindness to his sons.

> **What he does not say.** He never doubts David or the kingdom. His reasons are
> all about himself — his age, his senses, his usefulness. The invitation was not
> conditional on any of them.
