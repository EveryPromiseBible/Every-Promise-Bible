A building project that had been stopped for sixteen years gets finished in four,
and Ezra names why.

## What had happened

The foundation was laid, and then the local officials wrote to the Persian court and
the work was halted by decree. It stayed halted through the rest of Cyrus's reign
and into Darius's.

Then Tattenai the governor asked the question that reopened it — *who authorised
this?* — and the search of the archives at Ecbatana turned up Cyrus's original
decree.

## What the officials did

*Then Tattenai and Shethar-bozenai and their companions **did exactly what King
Darius had sent, and they did it carefully**.*

The men who tried to stop it are now enforcing it. Darius's reply had ordered them
not only to leave the Jews alone but to fund the work out of the provincial taxes,
and to supply the sacrifices daily.

The opposition became the budget.

## The two reasons given

*The work went well in their hands, **carried along by the words of Haggai the
prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo**.*

The Aramaic is that they prospered *through the prophesying* of these two men.

Ezra names the preaching as the cause. Not the decree — that came later, and the
prophets started in the second year of Darius, before the ruling.

Haggai's message was *consider your ways* and *I am with you, says the LORD.*
Zechariah's, to Zerubbabel, was *not by might nor by power, but by My Spirit* and
*grace, grace to it.* See the note on Zechariah 4:6–10.

They preached, and men who had given up picked up their tools.

## The double authorship

*Just as **the God of Israel had commanded**, and as **Cyrus and Darius and
Artaxerxes** king of Persia had decreed.*

Ezra puts God's command and three pagan emperors' decrees in one sentence, as though
they were one instruction.

## The date

*The house was finished on **the third day of the month Adar, in the sixth year of
the reign of King Darius**.*

Ezra dates it precisely. Roughly twenty years after the foundation was laid, four
years after the preaching started.

## What it was not

The old men who had seen Solomon's temple wept when the foundation went down, because
this one was so much smaller.

Haggai had addressed that directly: *who is left among you who saw this temple in
its former glory? And how do you see it now? Is it not in your eyes as nothing?* —
and then: *the glory of this latter temple shall be greater than the former.*

## What happens next in the chapter

Dedication, a hundred bulls, two hundred rams, four hundred lambs, and twelve male
goats *according to the number of the tribes of Israel* — twelve, though only two
tribes had come home.

And then they kept the Passover.

> **Which building it became.** This is the temple Herod later enlarged, and the one
> Jesus walked into and cleared. *The glory of this latter temple shall be greater
> than the former* — the greater glory turned out to be a Person standing in it.
