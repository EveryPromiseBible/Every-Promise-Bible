Joel has spent a chapter and a half describing a locust invasion that stripped
the country bare. This is the turn.

## Who is told not to be afraid

Three times, and the first two are addressed to things that cannot hear:

- *Do not be afraid, **land***
- *Do not be afraid, **you wild animals**, for the pastures of the wilderness are
  turning green again*
- *Be glad, **you children of Zion***

The ground and the animals are addressed before the people. The devastation was
total, so the reversal is announced to everything it touched.

## The rain

*He gives you the early rain in right measure, and He sends the showers down for
you, the early rain and the late, **just as before**.*

Both rains, in their seasons, restored to what they used to be. The Hebrew for
*in right measure* carries the sense of according to what is right — the proper
amount, neither withheld nor destructive.

Not more than before. **As** before — which, to a country that had watched
everything eaten, is the whole promise.

## The overflow

*The threshing floors will be full of grain, and the vats will overflow with new
wine and oil.*

The three crops of Deuteronomy 11, named again: grain, wine, oil. The promise is
being restated in the vocabulary of the original covenant blessing.

## The famous line just after

*I will repay you for the years the locusts have eaten.* It comes two verses on,
and this passage is the run-up to it.

Note that Joel does not say the years were not lost. He says they will be repaid
— which assumes the loss was real.

> **Where the chapter ends.** *And afterward, I will pour out My Spirit on all
> people.* The same verb — pour — that has been used of rain the whole way
> through. Peter quotes it at Pentecost, and by then the harvest language has
> become something else. See the note on Acts 2:1–4.
