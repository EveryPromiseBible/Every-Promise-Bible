Paul uses a piece of civic vocabulary that Philippi in particular would have felt.

## The word

*Our **citizenship** is registered in heaven.*

The Greek is **politeuma** — a colony, a commonwealth, the body of citizens whose
register you are on.

Philippi was a Roman colony. Its people held Roman citizenship though most of them
had never seen Rome, lived under Roman law, and were governed as though the city were
a piece of Italy transplanted into Macedonia.

Paul is writing to people who already knew what it meant to be enrolled somewhere
you do not live.

## What follows from it

*And it is **from there** that we are **watching eagerly** for the Saviour.*

The Greek is **apekdechomai** — to await with the head craned forward.

A colony's business was to look toward the capital. Paul does the same with the
direction of expectation.

## The change

*Who will **change these lowly bodies** of ours so that they are **formed like His own
glorious body**.*

The Greek for *change* is **metaschematizo** — to alter the outward form; and *formed
like* is **summorphos**, sharing the same inner form.

Both words, in one sentence, about the same event.

**Lowly** is *the body of our humiliation* — Paul does not flatter it. He is writing
from prison, in a body that had been beaten five times with thirty-nine lashes and
three times with rods.

## By what power

*By the working of that power of His **which can bring all things under Himself**.*

The same energy that will subdue everything is what is applied to a body.

## What Paul had just said about himself

The verses before: *not that I have already attained, or am already perfected; but I
press on… **forgetting those things which are behind**, and reaching forward to those
which are ahead.*

And: *brethren, join in following my example.*

He had also just listed what he had thrown away — circumcised the eighth day, of the
tribe of Benjamin, a Pharisee, blameless concerning the righteousness in the law —
and called all of it loss.

## The contrast in the paragraph

Immediately before this: *many walk… **whose god is their belly**, and whose glory is in
their shame — **who set their mind on earthly things**.*

Two citizenships, two horizons. See the note on Philippians 3:17–19.

## What the letter is like

Written from a Roman cell, with a capital case pending, to a church that had been
sending him money since a riverside prayer meeting.

*Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice.*

> **The next sentence.** *Therefore, my beloved and longed-for brethren, my joy and
> crown, **so stand fast in the Lord**.* The citizenship is stated, and the instruction
> attached to it is to stand still.
