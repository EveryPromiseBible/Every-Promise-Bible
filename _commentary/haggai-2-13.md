The second question, and the answer is the opposite of the first.

## The question

*"If someone **made unclean by a dead body** touches any of these things, **does it become
unclean?**"*

The Hebrew is **teme-nephesh** — unclean by a soul, the standard idiom for corpse defilement.

## Why the corpse

Of all the sources of uncleanness in the law, contact with the dead was the most severe.

*He who touches the dead body of anyone shall be unclean seven days.* Seven days, not one, and it
required the water of purification made with the ashes of a red heifer.

## The answer

*And the priests answered, "**Yes. It becomes unclean**."*

Correct again. Numbers 19 says so explicitly: *whatever the unclean person touches shall be
unclean*.

## The two answers together

Holy meat, at second hand: no.

Corpse defilement, at second hand: yes.

The same priests, the same day, and the two rulings do not match — because the law itself does
not treat them symmetrically. See the note on Haggai 2:12.

## What the asymmetry means

Under that arrangement, holiness was fragile and defilement was not.

A clean man could not make an unclean thing clean by touching it. An unclean man could make a
clean thing unclean by touching it.

## The application

*"**So is this people, and so is this nation before Me**," says the LORD, "and so is every work of
their hands; **and what they offer there is unclean**."*

The offerings on the altar were not sanctifying the sixteen years of neglected building. The
neglect was contaminating the offerings.

## What the date says

*"**From this day forward** I will bless you."

The same oracle. The ruling about defilement is followed immediately by a blessing dated to the
day it was spoken.

Nothing had been completed. The temple would not be finished for another four years.

## The word after the ruling

*"**Consider now from this day forward**, from the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month… **is the
seed still in the barn?** As yet the vine, the fig tree, the pomegranate, and the olive tree have
not yielded fruit."*

Nothing has come up yet. And: *from this day I will bless you*.

The promise is made in December, before anything is planted, about a harvest nobody can see.

## Where the traffic reverses

*Then He put out His hand and touched him, saying, "I am willing; be cleansed."*

*And He came and touched the open coffin.*

*Then He took the child by the hand and said to her, "Talitha, cumi."* See the note on
Mark 5:38–43.

Leprosy, a corpse, and a dead child. Under Haggai's ruling every one of those contacts defiles
the toucher.

## The woman in the crowd

Twelve years unclean, reaching through a crowd for the hem of a garment — which under the law
made every person she pressed past unclean too.

*And immediately Jesus, knowing in Himself that power had gone out of Him.* See the note on
Mark 5:30–34.

Something went out. Nothing came in.

## What Hebrews says about the ashes

*For if the blood of bulls and goats and **the ashes of a heifer**, sprinkling the unclean,
sanctifies for the purifying of the flesh, **how much more shall the blood of Christ**… cleanse
your conscience.* See the note on Hebrews 9:11–14.

The remedy for corpse defilement is the one Hebrews argues from.

> **The pair.** Holy at second hand: no. Unclean at second hand: yes. The priests answered their
> own law correctly both times, and the two answers do not match.
