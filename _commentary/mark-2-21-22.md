Two household illustrations, and both of them end with something ruined.

## The question that produced them

*Then the disciples of John and of the Pharisees were fasting. And they came and said to Him, "**Why
do the disciples of John and of the Pharisees fast, but Your disciples do not fast?**"*

A fair question. Both religious movements of the day fasted, and this one did not.

## The first answer

*"**Can the friends of the bridegroom fast while the bridegroom is with them?**"*

*"But the days will come when the bridegroom will be taken away from them, and then they will fast
in those days."*

The Greek is **aparthe** — be taken away, removed by force.

The first hint in Mark of what is coming.

## The cloth

*"Nobody stitches a piece of **new, unshrunk cloth** onto an old coat."*

The Greek is **rhakous agnaphou** — a patch of unfulled cloth.

**Agnaphos** — not yet processed. New cloth had not been shrunk.

## What happens

*"If they do, **the new patch pulls away from the old cloth and the tear ends up worse than it was**."*

The new material shrinks when washed and takes the old fabric with it.

Trying to save the coat destroys it.

## The wineskins

*"And nobody pours **new wine into old wineskins**."*

Wine was stored in whole animal skins. New wine ferments, and fermentation produces gas.

A fresh skin has enough give to expand with it. An old one has already stretched once and gone
brittle.

## The double loss

*"The wine **bursts the skins**, and **the wine is lost and the skins are ruined**."*

Both. Not one or the other.

The attempt to keep the old container costs the new contents as well.

## The instruction

*"**New wine goes into new wineskins**."*

The Greek is **oinon neon eis askous kainous**.

Two different words for *new*: **neos** — recent in time, and **kainos** — new in kind.

## What He is saying

Not that the old was bad.

Both illustrations concern things that were serviceable. The coat worked; the skin had held wine
once. The problem is that neither can take what is arriving.

## The context

Chapter 2 is a run of collisions: forgiving a paralytic's sins, eating with tax collectors, not
fasting, and picking grain on the Sabbath.

*"The Sabbath was made for man, and not man for the Sabbath."*

And then a withered hand healed in the synagogue, and *the Pharisees went out and immediately
plotted with the Herodians against Him, how they might destroy Him*.

## Luke's addition

*"And no one, having drunk old wine, immediately desires new; for he says, '**The old is better**.'"*

Luke alone records it, and it is an observation about taste rather than an argument.

People prefer what they are used to.

## What Paul says with the same distinction

*The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 3:4–6.

*By calling this one new, **He has made the first one old**.* See the note on
Hebrews 8:13.

## Why the old will not hold it

*"**No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth on an old garment**… and no one puts new wine into old
wineskins."*

The two pictures make one point and it is not that the old was bad. The garment was serviceable and
the skins had held wine.

They are simply not built to take what is arriving.

*In that He says, "A new covenant," **He has made the first obsolete**. Now what is becoming obsolete
and growing old is ready to vanish away.*

**Hebrews** 8, at the end of the longest quotation of the Old Testament in the New — Jeremiah's new
covenant, cited in full.

*For **the law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came through Jesus Christ**.*

## What prompted it

The question was about fasting: *why do John's disciples and the disciples of the Pharisees fast,
but Your disciples do not fast?*

And the answer before these two pictures: *"**Can the friends of the bridegroom fast while the
bridegroom is with them?**"*

The reason given for the new arrangement is not a doctrine. It is a wedding, and who is standing in
the room.

*"**He who has the bride is the bridegroom.**"*

## The new garment

*For as many of you as were baptised into Christ **have put on Christ**.*

*I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for **He has clothed me with
the garments of salvation, He has covered me with the robe of righteousness**.*

The patch is refused and a whole garment is given instead.

## And the new container

*Therefore, if anyone is in **Christ**, he is **a new creation**; old things have passed away; behold,
all things have become new.*

*"**I will give you a new heart** and put a new spirit within you; I will take the heart of stone out
of your flesh and give you a heart of flesh."*

Ezekiel 36 is the wineskin promised. What **Jesus** describes as a storage problem, Ezekiel had
already answered by promising a new container rather than a repaired one.

*"Do not marvel that I said to you, '**You must be born again**.'"*

> **The double loss.** Not the skins or the wine — both. Trying to keep the old container costs the
> new contents as well.
