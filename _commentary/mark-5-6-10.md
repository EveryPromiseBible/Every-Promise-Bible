The one place in the gospels somebody is asked their name, and the answer is a number.

## Who runs

*"But when he saw **Jesus** far off, **he ran and threw himself down in front of Him**."*

The man has been living among the tombs, cutting himself with stones, and nobody could bind him any
more, *not even with chains*.

He is the most dangerous person in the district and the first thing he does is run toward the boat.

## What he says

*"**What do You want with me, Jesus, Son of the Most High God?**"*

The Greek is **ti emoi kai soi** — what to me and to you, a Semitic idiom meaning *what business do
we have with each other?*

And the title is exact. The demons in **Mark** consistently identify Him correctly, before the
disciples do — *I know who You are, the Holy One of God* — and He consistently silences them.

*You believe that there is one God. You do well. **Even the demons believe — and tremble!***

**James** 2, and it is the same observation: right doctrine, held with terror, and it saves nobody.

## The tense

*"He said it because **Jesus had already spoken**: 'Come out of this man, you unclean spirit.'"*

**Mark** puts the command first in time and second in the telling. The screaming is a response to an
order already given.

## The question

*"**What is your name?**"*

He asks. Nothing in the story requires it — the command has already been issued — and it is the only
time in the gospels the question is put to somebody in this condition.

The man has not been addressed as a person by anybody for a long time.

## The answer

*"**My name is Legion, because there are many of us.**"*

A Roman legion was six thousand men. The word is not a Hebrew or Aramaic term; it is the Latin the
occupying army used.

The answer is a military unit rather than a name, and the pronouns collapse in the middle of the
sentence — *my* name, *there are many of us*.

## What is left of him

*And they came to Jesus, and saw the one who had been demon-possessed and had the legion, **sitting
and clothed and in his right mind**.*

Three participles, and each one reverses something from the opening description: he could not be
restrained, and he is sitting; he was naked, and he is clothed; he was screaming, and he is
sane.

*Therefore, if anyone is in **Christ**, he is **a new creation**.*

## What he asks for, and what he is given

*And when He got into the boat, he who had been demon-possessed begged Him that he might be with
Him. However, **Jesus** did not permit him, but said to him, "**Go home to your friends, and tell them
what great things the Lord has done for you**."*

He asks to join the twelve and is refused, and sent instead to the Decapolis — ten Gentile cities.

*And he departed and began to proclaim in Decapolis all that Jesus had done for him; **and all
marvelled**.*

The first missionary to the Gentiles in **Mark** is a man who had been living in a graveyard that
morning, and his entire message is his own biography.

> **The question He asks.** *What is your name?* Nothing in the exorcism needs it. It is put to a
> man nobody had spoken to as a person in years, and the answer that comes back is the name of an
> occupying army.
