A boy with a sling makes a speech before he makes a shot, and the speech is longer
than the fight.

## The two inventories

*"You come at me with **sword and spear and javelin**, but I come at you **in the
name of the LORD Almighty**."*

Goliath's equipment has been listed in detail earlier in the chapter — a bronze
helmet, a coat of mail weighing five thousand shekels, bronze greaves, a bronze
javelin, a spear whose shaft was like a weaver's beam, and a shield-bearer walking
ahead of him.

David's list has one item on it, and it is a name.

## What he calls God

*The LORD Almighty, **the God of the armies of Israel** — the very One you have
defied.*

**LORD of hosts** — Yahweh Sabaoth, the God of armies. The title is chosen for the
occasion.

And the offence is relocated. Forty days of Goliath shouting at Israel, and David
identifies who was actually being insulted.

## What he says before it happens

*"**This very day** the LORD will hand you over to me. **I'll strike you down and
cut off your head.**"*

Past-tense certainty about a future event, spoken in the open, to the man's face,
before a stone has left the pouch.

He goes further — *the bodies of the whole Philistine army I'll give to the birds*
— announcing not just a duel won but an army routed.

## Why

*"**So the whole earth will know there is a God in Israel.**"*

The stated purpose is not David's survival or Israel's border. It is what everybody
watching will conclude.

## The sentence in the middle

*"**The LORD does not save with sword or spear.**"*

Said by a man who is about to use a sling and a stone — which are also not nothing.

He is not despising the weapon. He is naming what the weapon is not.

## And the last clause

*"**For the battle is the LORD's**, and He will give all of you into our hands."*

The Hebrew is *the battle belongs to the LORD*. It is a statement of ownership.

Jehoshaphat is told the same thing in almost the same words centuries later: *the
battle is not yours, but God's* — and he sends singers out in front of the army.
See the note on 2 Chronicles 20:16–19.

## What he had already told Saul

The lion and the bear. *Your servant has killed both lion and bear, and this
uncircumcised Philistine will be like one of them.*

He had a record nobody had seen, built in a field with nobody watching, and it is
what he was standing on.

> **What David ran toward.** *When the Philistine arose and came and drew near to
> meet David, **David hurried and ran toward the army** to meet the Philistine.* He
> did not wait to be approached, and the text notes that he ran.
