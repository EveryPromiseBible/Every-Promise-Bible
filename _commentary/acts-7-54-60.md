Luke reports a detail here that he does not report anywhere else, and the whole
scene turns on it.

## Sitting, everywhere but here

The New Testament describes the risen Christ at the right hand of God more than a
dozen times, and almost always **seated** — Hebrews makes a point of it, because
a seated priest is one whose work is finished.

Stephen, with the court screaming at him, looks up and sees Jesus **standing**.

Luke says it twice in two verses, once as narration and once in Stephen's own
words. He is not being careless with a posture.

## What Stephen says as he dies

Two sentences, and both of them are Jesus' own words from the cross with one word
changed:

- *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit* — Jesus had said *Father, into Your hands I
  commit my spirit*
- *Lord, do not hold this sin against them* — Jesus had said *Father, forgive
  them*

Stephen prays to Jesus what Jesus prayed to the Father. For the first Christian
to die, that is a considerable thing to have on record, and Luke simply lets it
stand.

## "He fell asleep"

Luke has just described a stoning in unflinching detail, and he ends it with the
gentlest verb available. It became the ordinary Christian word for a believer's
death — the same one Paul uses in Thessalonians — and it did not mean the death
was mild. It meant it was not final.

> **The coats at the young man's feet.** Luke names him: Saul. He will not
> mention it again for chapters, but it is here, in the first paragraph about a
> Christian being killed, that Paul enters the story — holding the coats.
