The Preacher's sign-off, and the two images he chooses for words are both about pressure.

## Where the book has been

Twelve chapters of a man testing everything.

Wisdom, pleasure, building, planting, silver, gold, singers, work — *and indeed all was vanity
and grasping for the wind*.

**Hevel** — vapour, breath. The word appears nearly forty times.

## What has just come

The most beautiful passage about aging in the language: *remember now your Creator in the days
of your youth*, and then the failing house — the keepers of the house trembling, the grinders
ceasing, the doors shut in the street, the almond tree blossoming, the grasshopper a burden.

*Before the silver cord is loosed, or the golden bowl is broken.*

## The first image

*The words of the wise are **like goads**.*

The Hebrew is **darvonot** — ox goads.

The same tool Shamgar killed six hundred men with. See the note on Judges 3:31.

A goad is not decoration. It is a pointed pole for moving an animal that has stopped, and its
Hebrew name is *a teacher of oxen*.

Words that get you moving.

## The second

*And the sayings of the masters are **like nails firmly driven in**.*

The Hebrew is **masmerot netuim** — planted nails.

**Baalei asuppot** — masters of collections, which probably means the compilers of anthologies
like Proverbs.

A goad moves you. A nail holds something in place.

Two functions: disturbance and fixity. Between them they cover what teaching is for.

## The source

*Given by **one Shepherd**.*

The Hebrew is **mero'eh echad**.

A single sentence, and it is the theological anchor of a book that has spent twelve chapters
being sceptical about everything.

Whatever wisdom the collections contain, the Preacher attributes it to one source.

## The warning

*Beyond these, my child, **take care**.*

The Hebrew is *be warned*.

## The famous line

*Of the making of many books there is **no end**, and **much study wearies the body**.*

The Hebrew is **yegiat basar** — weariness of the flesh.

Written on a scroll by a man who had written several, and it has been quoted by exhausted
students ever since.

The point is not that books are bad. It is that the list of them does not close, and a person
does.

## The conclusion

*Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: **Fear God and keep His commandments, for this
is man's all**.*

The Hebrew is *this is the whole of man*.

*For God will bring every work into judgment, including every secret thing, whether good or
evil.*

Twelve chapters of vapour, and the last two verses land on something solid.

## What the book is doing

Ecclesiastes is the honest book. It says out loud that the same thing happens to the wise man
and the fool, that the race is not to the swift, and that nobody knows what comes after.

And then it ends by naming one Shepherd and one duty.

## Where the goad turns up again

*"Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me? **It is hard for you to kick against the goads**."*

Said on the Damascus road.

An ox that kicks back at the goad only drives it in further.

> **The two images.** A goad moves what has stopped; a nail holds what would move. And the
> Preacher attributes both to one Shepherd, in a book that doubts nearly everything else.
