Two verbs about the love of God, and the second one is what makes the verse
usable.

## Known, and trusted

*We have come to know the love God has for us, and we have put our weight on it.*

Both are perfect tense — arrived at, and standing.

The first is knowledge: having come to see it. The second is the ordinary word
for believing or trusting, and the Illumination renders it physically, which is
what trust means when it is real. Not *we agree with it*. We have leaned on it.

A person can know something is load-bearing and still stand somewhere else.

## "Love is what God is"

John says it twice in this chapter, and it is the only sentence of its kind in
Scripture — no other quality is described this way. Not *God is loving*, which
would be an attribute among others.

Everything else John says about God in this letter — light, truth, righteousness
— is held together by it rather than competing with it.

## Staying

*Whoever stays in love stays in God, and God stays in him.*

The verb is **meno**, John's great word: abide, remain, stay put. It runs
through his Gospel — abide in Me, and I in you — and all through this letter.

It does not describe an achievement. It describes not leaving.

> **What it is for.** The next verse says the purpose: *so that we can stand bold
> on the day of judgment* — and then, *there is no fear in love; perfect love
> throws fear outside.* The knowing and the leaning are what put the fear out. See
> the note on 1 John 4:17.
