The verse an observant Jewish man says every weekday morning while winding on his phylacteries.

## The verb

*"**I will betroth you** to Myself forever."*

The Hebrew is **ve'erastikh** — the legal word for betrothal, the binding stage before the wedding
that could only be ended by divorce.

It is said three times in two verses. *I will betroth… I will betroth… I will betroth.*

## Who is being betrothed

An adulteress. The chapter's first half is a divorce proceeding — *she is no wife to Me now, and I
am no husband to her* — and the verses before this one strip her, hedge her way and take back the
grain.

Nothing in the chapter records her repenting. The betrothal is announced over a woman who is still
described as having gone after her lovers.

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that **while we were still sinners, Christ died
for us**.*

## The five things it is done in

*"In **righteousness** and **justice**, in **steadfast love** and in **tender mercy**. I will betroth
you in **faithfulness**."*

**Tzedeq**, **mishpat**, **chesed**, **rachamim**, **emunah**.

Every one of the five is a quality of the one doing the betrothing, and not a condition placed on
the one being betrothed. She brings nothing to the list.

*Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but **according to His mercy He saved us**.*

## Forever

*"**Forever.**" **Le'olam.***

The one thing a marriage contract in that world could not promise, because divorce existed and the
chapter has just been about one.

*"For I, the LORD, do not change."*

*If we are faithless, **He remains faithful; He cannot deny Himself**.*

## And you will know Me

*"**And you will know Me. You will know the LORD.**"*

The Hebrew **yada** is the verb used of a husband and wife. The betrothal's stated outcome is not
obedience or gratitude. It is knowledge, in the marital sense of the word.

*"And this is eternal life, **that they may know You**, the only true God, and **Jesus Christ** whom
You have sent."*

## Where the betrothal goes

*For I am jealous for you with godly jealousy. For **I have betrothed you to one husband, that I
may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ**.*

**Paul**'s verb is **hermosamen**, and **2 Corinthians** 11 is the only place in the New Testament
the word appears. He says it to a congregation whose faults occupy two letters.

*Husbands, love your wives, just as **Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her**… that
He might present her to Himself **a glorious church, not having spot or wrinkle**.*

*"Let us be glad and rejoice and give Him glory, for **the marriage of the Lamb has come**."*

## What is said daily

In Jewish practice these two verses are recited every weekday morning as the strap of the
phylactery is wound three times around the finger — once for each *I will betroth you*.

> **What she brings.** Nothing. Righteousness, justice, steadfast love, tender mercy and
> faithfulness are all listed as what He betroths her *in*, and all five belong to Him.
