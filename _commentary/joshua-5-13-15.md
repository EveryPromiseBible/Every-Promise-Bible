He asks which side the man is on, and the answer is that the question is wrong.

## Where it happens

*One day, while Joshua was **near Jericho**.*

The Hebrew is **biricho** — in Jericho, or by it.

A commander doing reconnaissance on a walled city he has been told to take.

## What he sees

*He **looked up and saw a man standing before him, a drawn sword in his hand**.*

The Hebrew is **ish** — a man. That is all the text gives at first.

**Chereb shelufah** — a sword drawn out.

## What he does

*Joshua **walked up to him**.*

Toward the drawn sword, alone, at night, outside an enemy city.

## The question

*"**Are you for us, or for our enemies**?"*

The only two categories available to a general.

## The answer

*"**Neither**," the man answered.*

The Hebrew is **lo** — no.

## What the word does

It refuses the question rather than answering it.

Joshua asked whose side He was on. The reply declines to be placed on a side at all.

## The correction

*"**I have come now as the commander of the army of the LORD**."*

The Hebrew is **sar-tzeva-YHWH** — prince of the host of the LORD.

The point is not which army He supports. It is that He has one of His own, and has arrived to lead
it.

## Who commands whom

Joshua has been commander since chapter 1.

The man with the drawn sword announces that the position is taken.

## The response

*And Joshua **fell facedown to the ground in worship** and said, "**What does my Lord command His
servant**?"*

The Hebrew is **vayishtachu** — he prostrated himself.

An angel would refuse this. Twice in Revelation: *see that you do not do that! I am your fellow
servant*.

## What he is told

*"**Take off your sandals from your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy**."*

Almost word for word what Moses was told at the bush. See the note on Exodus 3:1–3.

The Hebrew there is **shal-ne'aleicha me'al ragleicha**. Here it is nearly identical.

## The last three words

*And Joshua did.*

No argument, no question about the battle plan.

## What comes next

The instructions for Jericho: march round it once a day for six days, seven times on the seventh,
and shout. See the note on Joshua 6:1–5.

A plan no commander would produce, given to a man who has just handed over command.

## The chapter before it

Circumcision at Gilgal, then the first Passover in the land, then the manna stopping.

*Then the manna ceased on the day after they had eaten the produce of the land.* See the note on
Joshua 5:10–12.

The forty-year supply stops the day the fields start. He does not withdraw provision; He changes
its form.

## The sword

*Then the LORD will go forth and fight against those nations, as He fights in the day of battle.*

And the rider on the white horse, *out of whose mouth goes a sharp sword*. See the note on
Revelation 19:11.

> **The refusal.** *Are You for us or for our enemies?* — *Neither.* He does not take sides in
> Joshua's war. He arrives with an army of His own and takes the command.
