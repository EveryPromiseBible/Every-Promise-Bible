A man with a title lies down in the road, and the clock is already running.

## The return

*When Jesus came back across, **a crowd welcomed Him, because they had all been waiting and
watching for Him**.*

The Greek is **apedexato** — received gladly. And **prosdokontes** — expecting, on the lookout.

He has just come from the other side of the lake, where the Gadarenes had *asked Him to depart
from them, for they were seized with great fear*. See the note on Mark 5:1–5.

One shore begs Him to leave. The other has been watching the water for Him.

## The man

*And a man named **Jairus** came, **a leader of the synagogue**.*

The Greek is **archon tes sunagoges** — ruler of the synagogue.

The office in charge of the building, the services, and who was invited to read and speak.

His name is the Greek form of **Ya'ir** — *he will enlighten*, or *he will awaken*.

## What his position means

The synagogue leadership is the group already in conflict with Jesus over the Sabbath healings.

He is the most establishment figure in the town.

## What he does

*And **fell down at Jesus' feet**.*

The Greek is **peson para tous podas** — falling at the feet.

In public, in front of a crowd, in the street.

## The request

*Begging Him to come to his house.*

The Greek is **parekalei** — was imploring. Imperfect: he kept asking.

## The reason

*Because **his only daughter, a girl of about twelve, was dying**.*

The Greek is **thugater monogenes** — an only-begotten daughter.

**Monogenes** — the word John uses of the Son.

Luke notes the same detail at Nain — *the only son of his mother, and she was a widow* — and of
the boy with the spirit: *he is my only child*.

Luke keeps counting.

## The age

Twelve.

The woman in the next paragraph has been bleeding for twelve years. Luke sets the numbers next
to each other and says nothing about them.

## The delay

*So Jesus went with him, and **the crowds pressed in so tightly that they were nearly crushing
Him**.*

The Greek is **sunepnigon** — were choking, suffocating. The same verb Luke uses of thorns
choking seed.

## What happens on the way

A woman who has spent everything she had on physicians touches the edge of His garment, and He
stops.

*"Who touched Me?"* And Peter: *Master, the multitudes throng and press You, and You say, "Who
touched Me?"* See the note on Luke 8:43–48.

## What the stop costs

While He is speaking to her, a messenger arrives: *your daughter is dead. Do not trouble the
Teacher*.

The delay and the death are in consecutive verses.

## What He says

*"**Do not be afraid; only believe, and she will be made well**."* See the note on
Luke 8:49–56.

And at the house, *she is not dead, but sleeping*, and they laughed at Him.

*Then He took her by the hand and called, saying, "Little girl, arise."*

*And He commanded that she be given something to eat.*

## What the two stories share

Twelve years and twelve years old. A woman nobody could name and a girl whose father everybody
knew.

One reaches out and is stopped and questioned. One is dead before He arrives.

Luke braids them deliberately, and the interruption that costs the girl her life is what the
woman gets.

> **The clock.** He is on His way to a dying child and stops to find out who touched Him. Luke
> puts the delay and the death in consecutive verses.
