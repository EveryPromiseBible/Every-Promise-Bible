An inventory of the inner room, and then the writer stops himself.

## The curtain

*Behind **a second curtain** was another room.*

The Greek is **meta to deuteron katapetasma**.

Two curtains: one at the entrance to the holy place, and one before the innermost room.

## The name

*And this one they called **the holiest place there was**.*

The Greek is **Hagia Hagion** — Holy of Holies. A Hebrew superlative rendered into Greek.

The high priest entered once a year, on the Day of Atonement, with blood.

## What was in it

*The **gold vessel they burned incense in**.*

**Thumiaterion** — a censer, or the golden altar of incense.

Exodus places the incense altar outside the veil. Hebrews associates it with the inner room, which
matches its function: it stood immediately before the curtain and its smoke went through.

## The chest

*And **the covenant chest, gold over every inch of it**.*

The ark. Acacia wood overlaid with gold inside and out.

## The three contents

*Inside the chest **a gold jar of the bread from heaven**, and **the staff of Aaron that had budded**,
and **the stone tablets the covenant was written on**.*

## What the three are

**The manna** — the food that would not keep, kept. One omer laid up *before the Testimony*. See
the note on Exodus 16:19–21.

**Aaron's rod** — a dead stick that *sprouted and put forth buds, produced blossoms and yielded
ripe almonds* overnight, when the priesthood was disputed.

**The tablets** — the second set, since Moses broke the first at the foot of the mountain.

## What they have in common

Each one is a memorial of a rebellion.

The manna, from the complaining about food. The rod, from Korah's challenge to the priesthood.
The tablets, from the golden calf. See the note on Exodus 32:1–4.

Three records of failure, sealed in a gold box under the mercy seat.

## What is above

*Above the chest were **the shining winged figures**, their wings spread **over the place where mercy
was given**.*

The Greek is **Cheroubim doxes** — cherubim of glory. And **hilasterion** — the place of
propitiation, the mercy seat.

The Septuagint uses **hilasterion** for the Hebrew **kapporet** — the lid of the ark.

## Where else the word appears

*Whom God set forth as **a propitiation** by His blood, through faith.* — of Christ, in Romans 3.

The same noun.

## What happened there

*And there I will meet with you, and I will speak with you **from above the mercy seat, from between
the two cherubim**.*

Blood was sprinkled on it once a year, over the record of the broken covenant inside.

## The interruption

*There is **far more that could be said about all of this, but not now**.*

The Greek is **peri hon ouk estin nun legein kata meros** — concerning which it is not now to speak
in detail.

The writer stops. He has a different argument to make, and the furniture is not it.

## What he goes on to say

*But Christ came as High Priest… **with His own blood He entered the Most Holy Place once for all**,
having obtained eternal redemption.* See the note on Hebrews 9:11–12.

*How much more will the blood of Christ… **scour your conscience clean of all that dead work**.* See
the note on Hebrews 9:13–14.

## What happened to the curtain

*Then, behold, **the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom**.*

Matthew notes the direction.

*Therefore, brethren, having boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus, by a new and
living way which He consecrated for us, **through the veil, that is, His flesh**.* See the note on
Hebrews 10:19–21.

> **The three objects.** Manna, a budded rod, and stone tablets — memorials of three rebellions,
> sealed in a gold box, with the mercy seat as its lid.
