The verdict is delivered before the remedy, and Paul makes sure nobody is left standing outside the
dock.

## The question

*So what is the verdict? **Are we any better off than anyone else**?*

The Greek is **ti oun? proechometha?**

The verb is disputed — it can be middle or passive. Either *do we hold an advantage* or *are we
excelled*.

## The answer

*Not in the least.*

The Greek is **ou pantōs**.

## What has come before

Two and a half chapters of prosecution. The Gentile world in chapter 1, then the moralist who
judges it, then the Jew who has the law. See the notes on Romans 1:18–23 and Romans 2:1–5.

Each section closes the door on one class of person. This is where the doors meet.

## The charge

*Every one of us is **under the power of sin**.*

The Greek is **huph' hamartian einai** — to be under sin.

The preposition is spatial. Sin as a regime, with everyone inside its jurisdiction.

## The evidence

*The Scriptures say it plainly.*

What follows is a chain of quotations — from Psalms and Isaiah, six or seven passages stitched
together. The rabbinic method of **charaz**, stringing pearls.

Paul is not making the argument himself. He is letting Israel's own texts make it.

## The first

*"**There is no one who is right with God, not even one**."*

The Greek is **ouk estin dikaios oude heis**.

Psalm 14, with Paul's word **dikaios** in place of the psalm's *doing good*.

## The two faculties

*"There is **no one who understands**. There is **no one who searches for God**."*

Mind and will. The intellect and the appetite.

## The movement

*"**All of them have wandered off**."*

The Greek is **exeklinan** — they turned aside. A road word.

## The condition

*"Together they have **gone rotten**."*

The Greek is **ēchreiōthēsan** — they became useless, spoiled. Used of milk that has turned.

## The repetition

*"Not one of them does what is good — **not a single one**."*

The refrain returns. **Oude heis** twice in four lines.

Paul is not describing a majority. He is closing an exception.

## Why it has to be total

If one man qualified, there would be a second way to be right with God, and the chapter's
conclusion would fail.

## Where it lands

*Now we know that whatever the law says, it says to those who are under the law, that **every mouth
may be stopped**, and all the world may become guilty before God.* See the note on Romans 3:19–20.

The Greek is **phrachthē** — to be fenced, blocked, muzzled.

The purpose of the indictment is a silence.

## What is said into the silence

*But now **the righteousness of God apart from the law is revealed**… **to all and on all who believe.
For there is no difference**.* See the note on Romans 3:21–22.

The same phrase that condemned — *no difference* — is the phrase that opens the door.

## The next line

*For **all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God**, being **justified freely by His grace**.*
See the note on Romans 3:23–24.

The Greek is **dōrean** — as a gift, without cause, for nothing.

> **The refrain.** *Not even one*, twice in four lines. If a single exception existed there would be
> two ways to be right with God, and the chapter's conclusion would not hold.
