Peter, whose name means a stone, writes about stones.

## "Keep coming to Him"

The Greek is a present participle — continuous. Not *come once*; keep coming.

It is the same posture the whole passage assumes: something already true, being
returned to.

## The stone that is alive

*He is a stone, and **He is alive**.*

The two words do not belong together. A stone is the standard image for what does
not move, does not grow, and is not alive.

Peter puts them side by side without explaining, and then does it again to his
readers: *and you are stones too, **alive the way He is**.*

## Rejected and chosen

*People took one look and wanted nothing to do with Him — but God chose Him, and
to God He is worth everything.*

Two verdicts on the same stone, and Peter reports both. The builders' assessment
was not a mistake about the stone's quality; it was simply theirs.

Peter is quoting Psalm 118 — *the stone the builders rejected has become the
cornerstone* — the psalm sung at the close of Passover, on the night of the
bread and the cup.

## What God is doing with the stones

*God is **stacking you up** into a house He lives in.*

Passive. The stones are not building anything; they are being built.

And the house is not a place people visit. It is where He lives, and it is made
out of them.

## Priests carrying the Spirit's own sacrifices

*You are **the priests in it**, set apart, and the sacrifices you carry in are the
Spirit's own — and through Jesus Christ God is glad to take them.*

Two things worth slowing down on.

**Priests.** Not a class within the house. The stones and the priests are the
same people. Under the old covenant one tribe served and everybody else waited
outside a curtain.

**The Spirit's own.** The offerings are called *spiritual sacrifices* — they
originate on the other side. The priest carries in what he was given to carry.

*Through Jesus Christ God is glad to take them.* The acceptability is located in
the phrase *through Jesus Christ*, and not in the quality of the offering.

> **The building materials.** No stone in this house was quarried square. Peter's
> readers were *scattered strangers*, refugees in five provinces, and he tells them
> they are load-bearing.
