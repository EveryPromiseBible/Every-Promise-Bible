The city falls, and the exception is named out loud in the middle of the
instruction to destroy it.

## Where the exception is placed

*The city and everything in it are set apart to the LORD for destruction. Only
Rahab and everyone in her house will live.*

Not an afterthought once the dust settled. Joshua announces it as part of the
command, before the shout, before the wall goes down. The rescue was arranged in
advance and spoken publicly.

## Her house was in the wall

Chapter 2 mentions it almost in passing: *her house was built into the city wall,
so that she lived within the wall itself.*

Which means when the wall came down, the one structure that had to stand was the
one built into the thing that fell. The text does not comment on it. It simply
records the wall collapsing and Rahab surviving, and leaves the reader to put the
two facts side by side.

## The reason given

*Because she hid the messengers we sent.* Not because she was Israelite — she was
not. Not because she had reformed — the narrative never says so, and both New
Testament writers who mention her still call her by her trade.

She had made a covenant with the people of God, marked her house, and gathered
her family inside it. That is the whole of her qualification.

> **What follows immediately.** The next paragraph is Achan taking what was
> devoted to destruction, and the trouble it brings on the whole camp. The
> chapter puts them together on purpose: an outsider who is spared because she
> was inside the terms, and an insider who is ruined because he ignored them.
