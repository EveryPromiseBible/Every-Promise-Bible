Four instructions in a letter that is otherwise entirely about danger.

## What the letter is

Twenty-five verses, and most of them are a warning.

Jude says he changed his subject: *beloved, while I was very diligent to write to you
concerning our common salvation, **I found it necessary to write to you exhorting you to
contend earnestly for the faith***.

He had a different letter planned.

## Who wrote it

*Jude, a bondservant of Jesus Christ, and **brother of James**.*

Which makes him Jesus' brother too, and he does not say so. Neither does James.

Both introduce themselves as servants.

## The turn

*But you, **dear friends**.*

The Greek is **agapetoi** — beloved. He uses it three times in the closing paragraphs, each
time to mark a break from the warning.

## The first

*Build your lives up on **the faith that is holiest of all**.*

The Greek is **epoikodomountes** — building upon. A present participle: keep building.

**Te hagiotate humon pistei** — your most holy faith. A superlative, and it is the foundation
rather than the building.

## The second

*And **pray in the Holy Spirit**.*

The same phrase Paul uses at the end of the armour passage. See the note on
Ephesians 6:18–20.

## The third

*Stay right where **the love of God is**.*

The Greek is **heautous en agape theou teresate** — keep yourselves in the love of God.

Not *keep God loving you*. Stay in it, as a place.

The same construction Jesus uses: *abide in My love*. See the note on
John 15:9–11.

## The fourth

*And keep **watching the road for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ**.*

The Greek is **prosdechomenoi** — waiting for, expecting. The word used of Simeon *waiting for
the Consolation of Israel*. See the note on Luke 2:25–28.

## Which brings you where

*Which brings you into **life that never ends**.*

## The shape of the four

Two are about the present — build, pray. One is a location — stay. One looks forward — watch.

And only the third is imperative in Greek. The other three are participles hanging off it:
building, praying, and watching are how the keeping is done.

## Who does the keeping

Jude ends the letter with the answer:

*Now to Him **who is able to keep you from stumbling**, and to present you faultless before the
presence of His glory with exceeding joy.*

The Greek is **phulaxai** — a different verb from verse 21, and God is the subject.

He tells them to keep themselves and then closes by saying who is able to.

## The doxology

*To God our Saviour, who alone is wise, be glory and majesty, dominion and power, both now and
forever. Amen.*

The last two verses of Jude are read aloud at the end of services more often than the other
twenty-three are read at all.

## The word he uses for the faultless

**Amomous** — without blemish. The sacrificial word, from the Hebrew **mum**. See the note on
Song of Songs 4:6–7.

The same word Paul uses of the church presented *without spot or wrinkle*.

## What comes immediately before

*These are sensual persons, who cause divisions, not having the Spirit.*

And then *but you, beloved*. The contrast is the point of the paragraph.

> **The one imperative.** *Keep yourselves in the love of God* — and the letter's last sentence
> is about the One who is able to keep them from stumbling.
