The question a priest asks an angel, and what it costs him.

## The question

*"**How can I be sure of this?** I am an old man, and my wife is well along in years."*

The Greek is *by what shall I know this*.

He asks for a sign, which is not in itself an unreasonable request. Gideon asked for one
twice and got both.

## Who he is talking to

*"**I am Gabriel. I stand in the presence of God**, and I was sent to speak with you and
**hand you this good news**."*

The angel gives his own name and his position, which is rare — angels in Scripture usually
decline to.

Gabriel appears four times in the Bible: twice to Daniel, and twice in this chapter. The
same messenger who explained the seventy weeks turns up to announce their fulfilment. See
the note on Daniel 9:20–21.

**Hand you this good news** — the Greek is **euangelisasthai**, to evangelise.

## The consequence

*"And now, **because you did not believe what I told you**, you will be **silent — unable to
say a word — until the day all of this happens**."*

The Greek is **siopon** — silent — and the later verses show he was deaf as well: they
*made signs to his father*.

Nine months without speech.

## Why it lands as it does

The reply is not *you asked for a sign*. It is *you did not believe*.

Luke sets up the comparison deliberately. Mary asks a question too, in the same chapter,
and is not silenced. See the note on Luke 1:34–38.

Her question is *how can this happen?* — a request for the mechanism.

His is *how can I be sure?* — a request for grounds.

## The guarantee

*"**And it will happen. Every word of it will come true right on time**."*

The Greek is *which shall be fulfilled in their season*.

The unbelief does not cancel anything. The promise is restated in the same breath as the
sentence.

## What the silence is

It is a sign. He asked for one and got one, and it is the thing he asked with.

## What the crowd saw

*But when he came out, **he could not speak to them**; and they perceived that he had seen a
vision in the temple, for **he beckoned to them and remained speechless**.*

He should have come out and pronounced the priestly blessing — *the LORD bless you and keep
you* — over the people who had been praying outside.

He could not.

## How it ends

Nine months later, at the circumcision, they hand him a writing tablet and he writes *his
name is John*.

*Right then **his mouth was opened and his tongue was set loose**, and he began to speak —
and the first thing that came out of him was praise to God.* See the note on
Luke 1:59–67.

He gets his voice back the moment he agrees with what he was told.

## What he says with it

The Benedictus — one of the three songs in Luke's opening chapters:

*Blessed is the Lord God of Israel, for **He has visited and redeemed His people**.*

A man who spent nine months unable to say anything opens with that.

> **What the sentence was.** Not a punishment for asking. He asked how he could be sure,
> and was given nine months in which he could do nothing but wait and watch it happen.
