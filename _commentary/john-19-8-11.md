The governor asks two questions and gets silence for one and a correction for the other.

## Why he is afraid

*When Pilate heard that, he was **more afraid than ever**.*

What he had just heard: *we have a law, and according to our law **He ought to die,
because He made Himself the Son of God**.*

**More afraid than ever** — the Greek is *he was the more afraid*, which means he was
already.

His wife had sent word that morning: *have nothing to do with that just Man, for I have
suffered many things today in a dream because of Him.*

## The first question

*He went back inside and said to Jesus, "**Where do You come from?**"*

Not *who are You*. Where from.

Roman religion had categories for men descended from gods, and Pilate is asking inside
them.

## The silence

*But **Jesus gave him no answer**.*

The Greek is *gave him no answer*.

He had answered Pilate's earlier question about the kingdom at length. This one He does
not take.

Isaiah: *He was oppressed and He was afflicted, **yet He opened not His mouth**.*

## The second question

*"**You will not speak to me?** Do You not know that **I have the authority to release You
and the authority to crucify You**?"*

Pilate states his position accurately. He did have both.

He had already tried to use the first half — three times he says *I find no fault in Him*
— and had scourged Him hoping it would be enough.

## The answer

*"**You would have no authority over Me at all if it had not been given to you from
above**."*

The Greek is **exousia** — authority, the right to act.

Jesus does not deny the authority. He locates its source, and the sentence puts Pilate
inside an arrangement he did not set up.

Paul says the general form of it: *there is no authority except from God, and the
authorities that exist are appointed by God.*

## The comparison

*"That is why **the one who handed Me over to you carries the greater guilt**."*

The Greek is **meizona hamartian** — greater sin.

Not *no guilt* for Pilate. **Greater** for the other party, which means both are real and
one is larger.

Who handed Him over: Caiaphas and the council, in John's account — *the chief priests and
officers delivered Him to Pilate*.

## What Pilate does next

*From then on **Pilate sought to release Him**.*

He tries again, and the crowd produces the sentence that ends it: *if you let this Man
go, **you are not Caesar's friend***.

Tiberius's reign was not a good time to have that said about you. Pilate sits down on the
judgment seat and hands Him over.

## What he wrote

*And Pilate wrote a title and put it on the cross. And the writing was: **JESUS OF
NAZARETH, THE KING OF THE JEWS**.*

In three languages, and when the chief priests objected: *what I have written, I have
written.* See the note on Luke 23:32–38.

The one thing he would not revise.

> **What is conceded and what is corrected.** Jesus does not tell Pilate he lacks the
> power. He tells him where it came from — and then says the greater weight is on
> somebody else in the room.
