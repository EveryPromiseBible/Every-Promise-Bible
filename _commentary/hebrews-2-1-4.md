The first of the letter's warnings, and the danger it names is drifting rather than rebelling.

## What comes before

Chapter 1, entirely about the Son: the brightness of God's glory, the express image of His
person, upholding all things by the word of His power. See the note on
Hebrews 1:1–3.

Seven Old Testament quotations, all showing Him higher than the angels.

*Therefore* — and the warning follows from the person, not from a rule.

## The instruction

*We owe what we have heard **a far closer hearing than we have been giving it**.*

The Greek is **perissoteros prosechein** — to give more abundant heed.

## The danger

*Or at some point **we will be swept right past it**.*

The Greek is **pararuomen** — to flow past, to drift by. A nautical word: a ship carried past
its harbour by a current.

The picture is not a man walking away. It is a man standing still in moving water.

Nothing has to be decided for this to happen. That is the point of choosing the word.

## The argument from the lesser

*The message that came **through angels** held firm, and **every violation of it, every refusal
to obey, was paid back exactly what it was owed**.*

The Greek is **endikon misthapodosian** — a just recompense.

*Through angels* is how the New Testament describes the giving of the law. Stephen says it —
*you who have received the law by the direction of angels* — and Paul in Galatians: *it was
appointed through angels by the hand of a mediator*.

Sinai, and the fence, and the penalty for touching the edge. See the note on
Exodus 19:10–13.

## The question

*Then **how will we escape, if we treat a rescue this enormous as nothing?**"*

The Greek is **telikautes** — so great.

And **amelesantes** — having neglected, disregarded. Not rejected. Neglected.

It is the same word used of the men in the parable who were invited to the wedding and *made
light of it, and went their ways, one to his own farm, another to his business*.

Nobody in that parable was hostile. They had other things on.

## The three witnesses

*The Lord was the first to speak of it.*

*The people who heard Him **made it certain to us**.*

*And **God signed His name to it alongside them**.*

Three tiers: the Lord, the hearers, and God's own confirmation.

The writer places himself in the second generation — *made it certain to us* — which is one of
the reasons Hebrews is not thought to be by an eyewitness.

## The signature

*With **signs**, with **wonders**, with **powers of every kind at work**, and with **gifts of
the Holy Spirit handed out however He wanted**.*

The Greek is **sunepimarturountos** — bearing witness alongside. One word.

Four kinds of corroboration listed, and the last is *according to His own will*.

## What the letter's warnings all have in common

There are five of them, spread through the book, and each is embedded in an argument about who
Christ is rather than a list of rules.

The next one: *beware, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief in
departing from the living God*. See the note on Hebrews 3:12–15.

## What is being drifted past

*So great a salvation.*

The Greek word order puts the size first. The thing at risk is not standing; it is attention.

> **The verb chosen.** Not walking away — being carried past, like a ship missing its harbour.
> It describes something that happens to a person who does nothing at all.
