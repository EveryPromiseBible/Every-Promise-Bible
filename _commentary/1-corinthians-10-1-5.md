Paul lists five things every one of them had, and then says most of them died in the
desert.

## The word he repeats

***All*** — five times in four verses.

*All our fathers were **under the cloud**, and **all** of them passed through the sea, and
**all** of them were baptized into Moses, and **all** of them ate the same spiritual food,
and **all** of them drank the same spiritual drink.*

Nobody was left out of anything on the list.

## Whose fathers

*Our fathers.*

Paul is writing to a mostly Gentile church in Corinth and calls Israel's wilderness
generation **our** fathers.

## The cloud and the sea

*Baptized into Moses **in the cloud and in the sea**.*

The pillar over them and the water either side. Paul reads the crossing as a baptism —
they went in one thing and came out another, following a leader.

## The food

*The same **spiritual food**.*

The manna, six mornings a week for forty years.

## The drink

*The same **spiritual drink** — for they drank from **a spiritual Rock that went with
them**.*

The rock at Rephidim and the rock at Kadesh, thirty-eight years apart. See the notes on
Exodus 17:4–7 and Numbers 20:10–11.

Paul says the Rock **went with them** — the Greek is *the accompanying Rock*.

## The identification

*And **that Rock was Christ**.*

Paul states it flatly, in the past tense, about an object in the Sinai desert.

It is the strongest identification of an Old Testament thing with Christ that Paul makes
anywhere.

## The turn

*And yet **with most of them God was not well pleased**.*

The Greek is *with the majority of them*.

Of the adults who came out of Egypt, two entered Canaan: Caleb and Joshua.

## The ending

*For they were **scattered and left across the wilderness**.*

The Greek is *they were strewn about* — **katestrothesan**, laid low, spread over the
ground.

## Why Paul is saying it

The next verse: *now these things became **our examples**, to the intent that we should
not lust after evil things as they also lusted.*

The Greek for *examples* is **typoi** — types, patterns, the impression left by a stamp.

And then: *let him who thinks he stands **take heed lest he fall**.*

Corinth was a confident church. Paul had spent the previous chapter on his own
discipline — *lest, when I have preached to others, I myself should become disqualified.*

## What is being argued

Not that the provision was inadequate. Paul says twice that it was **the same** for all
of them, and calls it spiritual both times.

## What follows immediately

*No temptation has overtaken you except such as is common to man; but **God is faithful,
who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able**, but with the temptation
will also make the way of escape.*

The most quoted verse in the chapter comes directly after the wilderness graves.

> **The one clause everything hangs on.** *That Rock was Christ.* The people who drank
> from it are the same people Paul says were scattered across the desert, and he puts
> both facts in one paragraph.
