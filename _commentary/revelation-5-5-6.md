John is told to look at a Lion and sees a Lamb, and the whole book turns on the
substitution.

## Why he was crying

The verses before: a scroll sealed with seven seals, and *no one in heaven or on
the earth or under the earth was able to open it*, and John *wept much*.

He is not weeping over a book. Whatever the scroll is, its unopened state is what
undoes him.

## The announcement

*"**Stop crying. Look — the Lion out of Judah's tribe, the Root of David, has
won.**"*

Two titles, and both are old promises.

**The Lion of Judah** is Jacob's blessing in Genesis 49: *Judah is a young lion…
who would dare rouse him?* See the note on Genesis 49:8–12.

**The Root of David** is Isaiah 11: *there shall come forth a Rod from the stem of
Jesse, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots.*

## "Has won"

The Greek is **enikesen** — He conquered, aorist tense. A completed victory,
already achieved, reported as news.

## What John actually sees

*So I looked, and there in the middle of it all… **stood a Lamb**.*

He is told Lion. He sees Lamb.

Not a correction — the same Person. But the conquering was done in the other form.

## "He had been killed, and He was standing"

The Greek is a lamb *as though slain* — bearing the marks of slaughter, and on its
feet.

Both facts held together in one clause: the wounds are visible and He is standing.
It is the only description of the risen Christ in Scripture that keeps the killing
in the picture.

## The seven horns and seven eyes

**Horns** are the Old Testament image for power, **eyes** for seeing and knowing,
and **seven** is completeness.

*And the eyes are the seven Spirits of God, sent out into every part of the earth.*

## Where the story started

Genesis 3:15, in the garden, to the serpent: *He shall bruise your head, and you
shall bruise His heel.*

A heel bruised and a head crushed — a wound taken in the winning. That is the
Lamb standing with the marks on Him, and it is the first promise in the Bible
arriving at its end. See the note on Genesis 3:14–15.

The woman's seed at the beginning, the Lion of Judah at the end.

## What happens next

They sing a new song: *You are worthy to take the scroll, for **You were slain**,
and have redeemed us to God by Your blood out of every tribe and tongue and people
and nation.*

The reason given for His worthiness is not the Lion. It is the slaying.

> **The scroll.** Nobody in the vision could open it, and John's grief was
> proportionate to that. Whatever it holds — the title deed of the earth, the
> unrolling of what is to come — the point of the chapter is that the seals came off
> in the hands of somebody who had been killed.
