The light has risen in the verses before this. Now Isaiah tells the city to look
up and see who is walking toward it.

## The children first

*Your sons come from far away, and your daughters are carried close and safe.*

Before any mention of wealth, the returning crowd is family. Isaiah is writing to
a people who had watched their children marched off into exile, and the first
thing he describes coming back is them — the daughters carried, which is the
detail a parent notices.

## Then the wealth

*The wealth of the sea will be brought to you, and the riches of the nations will
come streaming in.*

The Hebrew for wealth is a broad word — strength, force, resources, army. It can
mean a nation's substance in every sense. And *the sea* means overseas trade,
which for Judah meant the whole Mediterranean world.

The picture is of a city that had nothing becoming the destination everything
arrives at.

## The reaction

*You will see it, and your face will glow. Your heart will race and swell with
joy.*

The Hebrew is physical: the heart *trembles and grows wide*. It is the body's
response to news too large to take in — the same verb used elsewhere of fear,
here turned to joy.

> **On reading this as a promise of transfer.** The passage has a long history of
> being applied to personal finances. What it actually describes is a restored
> city at the centre of a world that had scattered it, and Revelation picks it up
> that way — the kings of the earth bringing their glory into the new Jerusalem,
> with the gates never shut. It is worth reading first as the thing it says
> before it is applied to anything smaller.
