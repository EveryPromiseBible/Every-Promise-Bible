Moses describes the country ahead in the concrete terms of somebody who has been
living on manna for forty years.

## The list

*Streams and springs, water welling up from valleys and hills; wheat and barley,
vines and fig trees and pomegranates; olive oil and honey; bread without going
without; stones of iron, and hills you can dig copper from.*

Seven crops — the same seven the rabbis later called the species of the land —
plus water, bread, and metal.

It is not a poetic abundance. It is an inventory: food, drink, and the raw
material for tools and weapons.

## The two lines that frame it

Before: *keep the commands of the LORD your God — walk in His ways and hold Him
in awe.*

After, a few verses on: *when you have eaten and are satisfied, praise the LORD
your God for the good land He has given you.*

The abundance sits between an instruction and a thanksgiving. Moses is not
worried about their poverty. He is worried about what full barns do to memory —
the very next warning is *be careful that you do not forget.*

## The danger he names

*You may say to yourself, "My power and the strength of my hands have produced
this wealth for me."*

That is the risk this chapter exists to address, and it comes with the remedy:
*remember the LORD your God, for it is He who gives you the ability to produce
wealth.*

Not that the ability is unreal. That its source is elsewhere.

> **Why the manna comes first.** The chapter opens with forty years of being
> humbled, hungry, and fed with something they had never seen — *to teach you
> that man does not live on bread alone.* The lesson in the desert is what makes
> the land safe to enter. Jesus quotes that line in His own wilderness. See the
> note on Matthew 4:1–4.
