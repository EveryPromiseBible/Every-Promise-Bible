He is offered dinner and will not eat until he has said his piece, and this is how it opens.

## Where he is

In Bethuel's house in Mesopotamia, having followed Rebekah home from the well.

The family have brought water for his feet, fed his camels and set food in front of him, and he
has said *I will not eat until I have told about my errand*.

## The first sentence

*"**I am Abraham's servant**," he began.*

The Hebrew is **eved Avraham anochi**.

He has been given ten camels, gold ornaments, and authority to negotiate a marriage. He begins by
naming whose he is.

The chapter never gives his own name.

## The subject

*"**The LORD has blessed my master greatly, and he has become wealthy**."*

The Hebrew is **berak et-adoni me'od vayyigdal** — blessed my master greatly, and he became
great.

The verb comes first. The blessing is the cause and the wealth is the result, in the order he
states it.

## The inventory

*"God has given him **flocks and herds, silver and gold, servants and maids, camels and
donkeys**."*

Four pairs. Livestock, metal, people, transport.

The servant lists his master's estate to a family he wants to give a daughter to, which is what
anybody in that position would do — and every item is prefaced *God has given him*.

## What is not mentioned

That Abraham left home at seventy-five with a promise and no map.

That he had waited twenty-five years for the son named in the next sentence.

## The son

*"And **Sarah my master's wife bore him a son in his old age**."*

The Hebrew notes both the wife and the age.

Isaac was born when Abraham was a hundred, after Sarah laughed at the announcement in the tent
door. See the note on Genesis 18:9–15.

## The inheritance

*"And **to that son my master has given all that he owns**."*

The Hebrew is **kol-asher-lo** — all that is his.

Everything in the inventory belongs to the son.

## Why he says it

He is about to ask for a wife for that son. The family need to know what she is marrying into.

But the shape of the speech is: God blessed, the blessing produced this, and all of it is now the
son's.

## What he leaves out

Ishmael, who was sent away with Hagar.

And that Abraham had already had to bury Sarah — chapter 23 is the field at Machpelah, and this
chapter opens *Abraham was old, well advanced in age*.

## What comes next in the speech

The oath, the prayer at the well, and the sign — *let it be that the young woman to whom I say,
"Please let down your pitcher"…* See the note on Genesis 24:12–14.

He tells the whole story again, in full, so Genesis records it twice.

## The point he lands on

*"**The LORD has led me on the very road** to the house of my master's relatives."* See the note on
Genesis 24:26–27.

## The family's verdict

*"**The thing comes from the LORD**; we cannot speak to you either bad or good."*

## The heir

Everything the servant has just listed belongs to one son, and the servant's whole errand is to
find that son a wife.

He does not enrich himself, negotiate for himself, or take credit anywhere in the chapter. The
longest chapter in Genesis is given to a man who introduces himself as somebody's servant and is
never named.

## What he is actually doing

Read the speech as a job description.

He never gives his own name. He talks about the master, then about the master's son, then
about everything the master owns — and ends by saying that all of it now belongs to the son.
Then he asks a woman to come.

*"**He will not speak of Himself**… He will glorify Me, for **He will take of what is Mine
and declare it to you**. **All things that the Father has are Mine**; therefore I said that
He will take of Mine and declare it to you."* See the note on John 16:12–15.

That is this chapter, sentence for sentence. An unnamed servant, sent by a father, telling a
bride what belongs to the son — and the son's estate is described as *all that the father
has*.

## What the inventory is for

Flocks and herds, silver and gold, servants and maids, camels and donkeys — and then *to that
son my master has given all that he owns*.

He is not boasting about the family's money. He is telling her what she is marrying into,
which is the only reason any of it matters to her.

*And if children, then heirs — heirs of God and **joint heirs with Christ**.*

> **The word order.** *The LORD has blessed my master, and he has become wealthy.* Cause first,
> then result — and everything on the inventory is now the son's.
