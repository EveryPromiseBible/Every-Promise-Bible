A night at the threshing floor is over, and the older woman gives one instruction.

## What happened

Naomi had sent her: *wash yourself and anoint yourself, put on your best garment and go down to the
threshing floor*.

*And it shall be, when he lies down… go in, uncover his feet, and lie down; and he will tell you
what you should do.* See the note on Ruth 3:1–5.

## What Ruth said there

*"**Take your maidservant under your wing, for you are a close relative**."*

The Hebrew is **uphrasta khnaphekha** — spread your wing over your maidservant.

**Kanaph** — wing, and also the corner of a garment.

Boaz had prayed that she would find refuge *under whose wings you have come*. She asks him to
spread his own. See the note on Ruth 2:11–12.

## What he said

*"**Blessed are you of the LORD, my daughter!** For you have shown more kindness at the end than at
the beginning, in that **you did not go after young men, whether poor or rich**."*

*"And now, my daughter, do not fear. I will do for you all that you request, for **all the people of
my town know that you are a virtuous woman**."*

The Hebrew is **eshet chayil** — a woman of valour. The phrase that opens Proverbs 31.

## The complication

*"Now it is true that I am a close relative; **however, there is a relative closer than I**."*

There is a man with a prior claim, and Boaz names him rather than acting first.

## The morning

*When Ruth came to her mother-in-law, Naomi asked, "**How did it go, my daughter?**"*

The Hebrew is **mi-at bitti** — literally *who are you, my daughter?*

An idiom, and an odd one. It may mean *how did you fare*, or it may be asking what her standing is
now.

## The report

*So Ruth **told her everything Boaz had done**.*

## The barley

*"And **he gave me these six measures of barley**," she said. "He told me, '**Don't go back to your
mother-in-law empty-handed**.'"*

Six measures — the unit is not named, and the weight would have been substantial.

## What the gift says

Boaz sends the grain to Naomi.

The message is not for Ruth. He is telling the older woman that the matter is in hand, and he does
it in the one language a widow in a famine understands.

## The word *empty*

Naomi had come back from Moab saying *I went out full, and the LORD has brought me home again
empty*.

**Reqam** — empty. The same word Boaz uses.

He may or may not know he is answering her. The narrator puts the two sentences in the same book.

## The instruction

*Then Naomi said, "**Wait, my daughter, until you see how it all turns out**."*

The Hebrew is **shevi biti** — sit, my daughter.

**Yashav** — to sit, to remain, to dwell.

She has just walked home before dawn from a threshing floor with a gamble on it, and the
instruction is to sit down.

## The reason

*"That man **will not rest — he'll settle the matter before this day is done**."*

The Hebrew is **lo yishqot ha'ish ki-im-killah** — the man will not be quiet until he has finished
it.

## The two verbs

She sits. He does not rest.

The contrast is the point of the sentence, and it is what the next chapter is about.

## What he does

Goes to the gate, sits down, waits for the nearer kinsman to pass, gathers ten elders, and puts
the case.

*"Buy it back yourself. I cannot redeem it."*

*So Boaz took Ruth and she became his wife.* See the note on Ruth 4:9–12.

Same day, as Naomi said.

> **The two verbs.** *Sit, my daughter* — and *the man will not rest until he has finished it*.
> Naomi's whole instruction is built on the contrast.
