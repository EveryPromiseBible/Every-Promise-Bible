Seven proverbs, and five of them name the LORD as the one who actually decides.

## The plans

*The **plans of the heart belong to us**, but **the answer of the tongue comes from the
LORD**.*

Two halves, and the division is between what a person arranges inwardly and what comes
out.

Proverbs says this repeatedly — *a man's heart plans his way, but the LORD directs his
steps*, eight verses later.

## The eyes

*A man's ways **all seem clean in his own eyes**, but **the LORD weighs the spirit**.*

The Hebrew for *weighs* is **takan** — to measure, to gauge, to test the weight of.

The proverb does not say the man is lying. He genuinely thinks so, and the assessment is
being made elsewhere on a different instrument.

## The commitment

*Commit your work to the LORD, and **your plans will find firm ground**.*

The Hebrew for *commit* is **galal** — to roll. Roll your works onto the LORD.

**Find firm ground** is **kun** — established, fixed, made to stand.

The verse before said the plans are ours. This one says what to do with them.

## The purpose

*The LORD has **made everything for its purpose**, even the wicked for the day of
trouble.*

The Hebrew is *for its own answer* — everything has a matching end.

## Pride

*The LORD **hates every proud heart**. Be sure of this: **it will not go unpunished**.*

The Hebrew is *an abomination to the LORD is everyone proud of heart*, and then *hand to
hand* — a gesture that meant a thing was as good as settled.

Proverbs returns to it two chapters on: *pride goes before destruction.* See the note on
Proverbs 18:9–16.

## The covering

*By **love and faithfulness sin is covered over**, and by **the fear of the LORD one turns
from evil**.*

The Hebrew is **chesed ve'emet** — covenant loyalty and truth. The same pairing Jacob
asks Joseph for on his deathbed, and the pairing John puts in his prologue: *full of
grace and truth*. See the note on Genesis 47:27–31.

**Covered over** is **kaphar** — the atonement word. The verb behind **kapporeth**, the
mercy seat, and behind Yom Kippur.

Sin is not said to be excused or overlooked. It is atoned for, and the agents named are
love and faithfulness.

## The two halves of that verse

Atonement in the first clause, and turning from evil in the second — in that order.

## The enemies

*When **a man's ways please the LORD**, even **his enemies are made to be at peace with
him**.*

The Hebrew is *He makes even his enemies to be at peace with him* — God is the one doing
it.

The peace is not negotiated by the man and is not conditional on the enemies changing
their minds. It is made.

> **Where the chapter puts the initiative.** The heart plans, the eyes approve, the man
> commits — and in every one of the seven verses the outcome is attached to the LORD:
> the answer, the weighing, the establishing, the purpose, the covering, and the peace.
