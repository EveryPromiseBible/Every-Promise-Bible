God takes a childless old man outside at night and gives him something to look
at.

## The correction

*"**No. That man will not be your heir.**"*

Abram had proposed Eliezer of Damascus, his steward. It was the sensible legal
arrangement of that world for a man with no son.

God refuses the workaround. *A son from your own body — he will be your heir.*

## Outside

*And God **led him outside into the night**.*

He is taken out of the tent. Whatever conversation was happening indoors, it
continues under the sky.

## The word

*"Look up. **Count the stars**, if you can even number them."*

The Hebrew verb is **saphar** — and it carries both senses at once. To count, and
to recount: to tell, to declare, to narrate.

It is the root behind **sepher**, a book or scroll, and **sopher**, a scribe.

So the instruction has two readings sitting in one word: number them, and read what
they tell.

## What the heavens are said to be doing

Psalm 19 puts it plainly: *the heavens **declare** the glory of God, and the
firmament **shows His handiwork**. Day unto day utters speech, and night unto night
**reveals knowledge**.*

And *there is no speech nor language where their voice is not heard. Their line has
gone out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world.*

The sky is described as speech going out to every nation, with no language barrier.

## The story told there

The account runs from the virgin to the lion — from **Virgo**, the woman with the
seed, to **Leo**, the lion.

Genesis 3:15, spoken in the garden: *He shall bruise your head, and you shall
bruise His heel* — the seed of the woman.

Revelation 5:5: *the **Lion** out of Judah's tribe, the Root of David, has won.*

The first promise and the final victory, and the whole of it written overhead where
Abram was told to look. See the notes on Genesis 3:14–15 and Revelation 5:5–6.

## What he did with it

*And he believed the LORD, and He counted it to him as righteousness.*

That is the very next verse — the sentence Romans and Galatians are built on. It
follows a night sky and nothing else. See the note on Genesis 15:6.

## The sand, later

In Genesis 22, after Isaac on the mountain, God adds the second image: *as the
stars of the heaven and as the sand which is on the seashore.*

Two innumerable things, and both given as a picture before there was one child.

> **How long he had to hold it.** Isaac is born in chapter 21. Between this night
> and that morning is roughly a quarter of a century, and a stretch of it he spent
> being called *father of a multitude* with no son by his wife. See the note on
> Genesis 17:3–8.
