Paul takes one detail out of Exodus 34 and builds a whole argument on it.

## The hope

*Since we have **a hope like that**, we speak with **complete openness, nothing held back
and nothing veiled**.*

The Greek is **parrhesia** — freedom of speech, the right to say anything in a place
where you belong. It is the same word Hebrews uses about entering the Holiest. See the
note on Hebrews 10:19–21.

The hope in question is the one just described: a glory that stays, against a glory that
was going out. See the note on 2 Corinthians 3:7–11.

## The contrast

*We are **not like Moses**, who would **put a veil over his face**.*

Exodus 34 records the veil. It does not say why.

## Paul's reading of it

*So that Israel **could not keep looking at the end of what was passing away**.*

The Greek is *the end of that which was being abolished*.

Paul's account is that the veil was not there because the glory was too bright. It was
there so nobody would watch it fade.

The shining came from forty days on the mountain, and it wore off — and Moses put the
veil on afterward, between the times he went back in.

## The transfer

*But **their minds had gone hard**.*

The Greek is **eporothe** — petrified, calloused, made stony.

The veil moves from Moses' face to their understanding.

## Where it is now

*And **to this day, when the old covenant is read aloud, that same veil is still lying
there, unlifted**.*

Paul is describing a synagogue service he had sat in a thousand times.

The scroll is read, correctly, in Hebrew, by people who honour it — and he says something
is over it.

## Where it is removed

*Because **it is in Christ that it is done away with**.*

The Greek is the same verb as the fading — **katargeitai**, abolished.

The veil and the glory it hid are removed by the same word.

## The restatement

*Yes, **to this day, whenever Moses is read, a veil lies over their heart**.*

He says it twice in three verses, and the second time it moves from the mind to the
heart.

## What Paul is not saying

Nothing about the text being defective. He has just called the ministry of death
**glorious**, and quotes the Old Testament constantly.

The problem in his description is at the reader's end.

## What lifts it

The next verse: *nevertheless **when one turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away**.* See
the note on 2 Corinthians 3:16–18.

The Greek is a middle voice — *when it turns*. And the turning is toward a person.

## Where the chapter ends

*But we all, **with unveiled face**, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are
being transformed into the same image **from glory to glory**.*

Moses' face faded and was covered. Paul says the readers' faces are uncovered and going
the other way.

## Where the veil in the temple went

*The veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom.* See the note on
Matthew 27:51–53.

Two veils, both removed, and Paul's argument here is about the one nobody could see.

> **The detail everything rests on.** A cloth over a face in Exodus 34, and Paul's reading
> of why it was there — not to protect the onlookers from the brightness, but to keep
> them from seeing it go out.
