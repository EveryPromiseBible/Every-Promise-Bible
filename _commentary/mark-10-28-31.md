Peter states the obvious out loud and gets an answer with an unwelcome word in it.

## What has just happened

The rich young ruler. *One thing you lack: go your way, sell whatever you have and give to the
poor… and come, take up the cross, and follow Me.*

*But he was sad at this word, and went away sorrowful, for he had great possessions.*

And Mark's note about the look on Jesus' face beforehand: *then Jesus, looking at him, loved
him*.

Then the camel and the needle's eye, and the disciples *greatly astonished*.

## The observation

*"Look at us — **we have left everything behind and followed You**."*

Peter, and it is true. Boats, nets, a business, a father, and a house in Capernaum.

He does not ask a question. Matthew's version adds one: *therefore what shall we have?*

## The promise

*"There is **no one who has left home or brothers or sisters or mother or father or wife or
children or fields** for My sake and for the sake of the good news."*

Seven things listed, and the list is deliberately domestic.

## The return

*"**Who will not be given a hundred times as much in this present life**."*

The Greek is **hekatontaplasiona** — a hundredfold.

**In this present life** — the Greek is *now in this time*. Not deferred.

## What is returned

*"Homes and brothers and sisters and mothers and children and fields."*

The same list, minus one.

**Father** is on the leaving list and not on the receiving list.

Whether that is deliberate has been argued over for centuries. The obvious reading is that
there is only one of those: *do not call anyone on earth your father, for One is your Father,
He who is in heaven*.

## The clause nobody quotes

*"And **persecutions right along with them**."*

The Greek is **meta diogmon**.

It is in the middle of the promise, between the fields and the eternal life, and Matthew and
Luke both leave it out.

Mark keeps it, and Mark is writing for a church under Nero.

## The other half

*"**And in the age to come, eternal life**."*

Two timeframes: now, with persecutions, and then.

## What the hundredfold looks like

Not property. The disciples did not end up with a hundred farms.

They ended up in a movement where houses were opened to them everywhere they went — Lydia's,
the jailer's, Priscilla and Aquila's, Philip's, Mnason's.

*And all who believed were together, and had all things in common… and they broke bread from
house to house.*

The list Jesus gives back is a list of relationships, and Acts is the account of them arriving.

## The reversal

*"But **many who are first will be last, and the last will be first**."*

He says it here, and again at the end of the vineyard parable in Matthew, and again after the
narrow door in Luke.

Placed immediately after a rich man walks away and a fisherman is promised a hundredfold.

## What comes next

The third passion prediction, and then James and John asking for the seats on His right and
left.

*"You do not know what you ask. **Are you able to drink the cup that I drink?**"*

Peter's *we have left everything* and the brothers' request for the best seats are eight verses
apart.

> **The word in the middle of the promise.** *And persecutions.* Mark leaves it in; Matthew and
> Luke do not — and Mark was writing for people it was happening to.
