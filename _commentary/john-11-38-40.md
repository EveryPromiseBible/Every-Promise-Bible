The one instruction He gives before the miracle is one people can carry out.

## The state He is in

*Jesus, **moved deeply again within Himself**, came to the tomb.*

The Greek is **embrimomenos** — a strong word. It is used of snorting, of deep indignation,
of being stirred with anger.

Not sorrow. John chose a harsh word twice in this passage, and the middle of it is the
shortest verse in the Bible: *Jesus wept*.

The Greek there is **edakrusen** — He shed tears. Different from the loud wailing of the
mourners around Him, which is **klaio**.

He is at a funeral, and He is angry and crying at the same time, and He knows what He is
about to do.

## The tomb

*It was **a cave, with a stone laid across the mouth** of it.*

A rock-cut chamber with a stone across the entrance, which is what His own tomb looks like a
few weeks later.

## The instruction

*"**Take the stone away**."*

The Greek is **arate ton lithon**.

He is about to call a man out of death by speaking. He asks the bystanders to move a rock.

The one thing delegated is the only part they could do.

## The objection

*Martha, the sister of the dead man, said to Him, "**Lord, by now there will be a smell. He
has been in there four days**."*

The Greek is **ede ozei** — he already stinks.

The old versions keep it blunt. She is being practical and she is right about the chemistry.

This is the woman who said, twelve verses earlier, *I believe that You are the Christ, the
Son of God*. See the note on John 11:17–22.

## The reply

*"**Did I not tell you that if you would believe, you would see the glory of God?**"*

He had said it to the disciples, not to her — *this sickness is not unto death, but for the
glory of God*. Word must have got back.

## The order in the sentence

Believe, then see.

The usual human order is the reverse, and Martha is standing in front of a sealed cave being
asked to run it the other way.

## What was moved and what was not

The stone was in the way of nothing. He raises the dead by speaking, and a rock is no
obstacle to a voice, or to a man who walks through doors after the resurrection.

The stone was in the way of the people watching.

## What He does next

Lifts His eyes and thanks the Father — *I said it for the sake of the people standing
here* — and then shouts. See the note on John 11:41–44.

## The other stone

*"**Who is going to roll the stone back** from the entrance for us?" Then they looked up, and
**the stone had already been rolled aside**.* See the note on Mark 16:1–4.

At Lazarus' tomb He tells people to move it. At His own, nobody is asked, and it is done
before they arrive.

## What the raising set in motion

*Then, from that day on, they plotted to put Him to death.*

And *the chief priests plotted to put Lazarus to death also, because on account of him many
of the Jews went away and believed in Jesus*.

A man is raised, and the response is a plan to kill him again.

> **What He asked for.** Not faith at the graveside first — He asked for the stone, which was
> the one part of it human arms could manage.
