Paul disqualifies himself twice before saying what he is qualified for.

## What he denies

*Not that we are **equal to anything in ourselves**, as though we could claim any
of it as coming from us.*

The Greek is **hikanos** — sufficient, adequate, up to the job.

He is writing to a church that has been comparing him unfavourably with more
impressive teachers, and his answer is to concede the point entirely.

## And what he affirms

*Whatever we are equal to **comes from God**, who has made us equal to the work of
serving a new covenant.*

Same word, three times in two verses. Not sufficient — sufficiency is from God —
made sufficient.

## The two covenants

*Not of the letter but of the Spirit. For **the letter kills, and the Spirit gives
life**.*

*The letter* is **gramma** — the written character, the thing engraved.

What follows in the chapter identifies exactly what he means: *the ministry of
death, **written and engraved on stones**.*

Only one thing in Scripture was written and engraved on stones. Ten commandments,
by the finger of God.

## Two ministries, compared

Paul does the comparison himself over the next few verses, and he is not shy about
it:

- *the ministry of **death**… was glorious* — glorious, and he calls it that
- *the ministry of the **Spirit** is more glorious*
- *the ministry of **condemnation** had glory*
- *the ministry of **righteousness** exceeds much more in glory*

He never says the first was bad. He says it had glory — Moses' face shone from it.
And then he says the glory was *passing away*, and that Moses veiled his face so
nobody would watch it fade. See the note on 2 Corinthians 3:7–11.

The two Greek words he sets against each other are **katargoumenon**, being abolished,
and **menon**, remaining. Temporary and permanent — and the temporary one is the one
that came with a face too bright to look at.

## The day it was given

Moses came down the mountain with the tablets and three thousand men died at the
hands of the Levites. See the note on Exodus 32:25–29.

And at Pentecost, when the Spirit came, Peter preached and *about three thousand
souls were added to them.* See the note on Acts 2:40–41.

Two mountains, two givings, and the same number counted on each day.

## What it does not permit

Paul is not arguing that behaviour stops mattering. He is arguing about what
produces it, and Galatians answers the question directly: *walk in the Spirit, and
you shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh.*

The fruit list ends with *against such there is no law* — nothing on it needs
legislating. See the note on Galatians 5:22–24.

> **What is engraved now.** *You are our epistle, written in our hearts, known and
> read by all men… written not with ink but by the Spirit of the living God, not on
> tablets of stone but on tablets of flesh, that is, of the heart.* Paul says it two
> verses before this one. Same finger, different surface.
