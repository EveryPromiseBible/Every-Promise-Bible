The invited guests decline, and the food is already cooked.

## The anger

*The master of the house **was angry**.*

Luke says it plainly. Three men had accepted and then sent excuses when the meal was
served.

## The first sweep

*"Go out **quickly**, into **the streets and the back lanes of the town**, and bring
in **the poor and the crippled and the lame and the blind**."*

The four categories Jesus had listed a few verses earlier when telling the guests who
to invite to their own feasts — *and you will be blessed, because they cannot repay
you.*

The master does what Jesus had just recommended.

And these are people who could not have come to a normal dinner. Nobody invited the
lame to a banquet; they had nothing to give back.

## The report

*"Sir, it is done — **and there is still room**."*

The best line in the parable.

Everybody the servant could find in the town has been brought in, and the house is
not full.

## The second sweep

*"Then go out on **the country roads and along the hedgerows**, and **press them to
come in**, whoever you find."*

Outside the town now — travellers, and people sleeping rough under hedges.

The Greek for *press them* is to compel, to constrain. It is urgency rather than
force; a beggar in rags would need convincing that the invitation was real.

## The reason

*"**I want my house full.**"*

That is the master's stated motive for the whole exercise.

Not to punish the first list, and not to fill the seats out of pride. He wants the
house full.

## The verdict

*"Not one of those men on the first list will ever taste this dinner of mine."*

They are not excluded. They excused themselves — and Luke's Greek in the previous
paragraph has all three of them saying so politely. See the note on
Luke 14:15–20.

## The shape of it

Invited, declined. Town, brought in. Countryside, brought in. Room, still.

Paul's account of the same movement: *it was necessary that the word of God should be
spoken to you first; but since you reject it… we turn to the Gentiles.*

## Where He was sitting

At a Pharisee's table on the Sabbath, as a guest, having watched the other guests
choosing the best places.

He tells the room a story in which the people on the guest list miss dinner and the
street gets fed.

> **What the servant did not do.** Argue with anybody, or lower the standard of the
> meal, or ask the master to reduce it. He went out twice and came back both times,
> and the second time the house filled.
