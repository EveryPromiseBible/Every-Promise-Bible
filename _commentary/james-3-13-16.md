A test for wisdom that does not involve saying anything.

## The question

*Somebody among you **figures he's wise**, that he's got some understanding?*

The Greek is **sophos kai epistemon** — wise and understanding.

**Epistemon** is the rarer word: expert, skilled, knowledgeable. It appears once in the New
Testament, here.

## Where the question comes from

James has just spent twelve verses on the tongue — the bit in the horse's mouth, the rudder, the
spark that sets a forest going, the spring that cannot give both sweet water and bitter.

*With it we bless our God and Father, and with it we curse men.* See the note on
James 3:8–12.

The chapter opens *let not many of you become teachers*.

Having established what talking is worth, he asks who is wise.

## The test

*Then **let his life do the talking** — the things he actually does, **done gently**, because that
is what wisdom does to a man.*

The Greek is **deixato ek tes kales anastrophes ta erga autou** — let him show, out of his good
conduct, his works.

Not one thing done well. A manner of life.

## The word

**En prauteti sophias** — in the meekness of wisdom.

**Prautes** — meekness, gentleness. In Greek usage it described a strong animal under control: a
horse broken to the bridle. It is strength that has been taught restraint, not weakness.

The same word Jesus uses of Himself: *I am gentle and lowly in heart*. See the note on
Matthew 11:28–30.

## The alternative

*But if what is really sitting in your chest is **a sour ache over what other people have**, and
**a hunger to win**.*

The Greek is **zelon pikron** — bitter zeal, and **eritheian** — selfish ambition, party spirit.

**Eritheia** originally described working for hire; it came to mean canvassing for office, the
kind of ambition that builds a faction.

## The location

*Sitting in your chest.* — **en te kardia humon**, in your heart.

Both things are internal. Neither has to be said out loud to be there.

## The warning

*Do not stand there **congratulating yourself. You are lying**, and the truth is what you are
lying about.*

The Greek is **me katakauchasthe kai pseudesthe kata tes aletheias** — do not boast against and
lie against the truth.

## The origin

*That kind of wisdom **did not come down from anywhere**. It comes **up off the ground, out of the
appetites, out of hell**.*

Three adjectives in Greek: **epigeios** — earthly. **Psuchikos** — of the soul, natural,
unspiritual. **Daimoniodes** — demonic.

A descending scale, and James does not soften the last one.

## What the false wisdom produces

*Because wherever people are eaten with envy and out to win, **things fall apart**, and **every
rotten thing there is finds somewhere to live**.*

The Greek is **akatastasia** — disorder, instability, anarchy. And **pan phaulon pragma** — every
worthless deed.

The image in the second clause is of vacancy: disorder makes room, and things move in.

## What the true wisdom produces

*But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then **peaceable, gentle, willing to yield**,
full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality and without hypocrisy.* See the note on
James 3:17–18.

Eight qualities, and the first six are about how a person is with other people.

## What comes immediately after

*Where do wars and fights come from among you? **Do they not come from your desires for pleasure
that war in your members?***

Chapter 4 opens by tracing conflict to the same interior place.

> **The test.** Not what a man says about wisdom — what his life is like, and specifically whether
> the strength in it has been broken to the bridle.
