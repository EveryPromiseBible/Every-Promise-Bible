The accounting at the end of the meal, and the numbers do not work.

## The satisfaction

*Every one of them **ate until they had eaten all they wanted**.*

The Greek is **echortasthesan** — were filled, satisfied. From **chortos**, fodder. It is the word
for feeding animals to fullness rather than politely.

Nobody was rationed.

## What was there to start with

Five loaves and two fish, brought by the disciples after Jesus said *you give them something to
eat*, and they had asked whether they were expected to spend two hundred denarii. See the note on
Mark 6:35–41.

## The collection

*And when the disciples **gathered up the broken pieces that were left over**, along with the
fish, **they filled twelve baskets**.*

The Greek is **kophinoi** — the stiff wicker basket a Jewish traveller carried, large enough for
a day's provisions.

John gives the reason for the collection: *gather up the fragments that remain, **so that nothing
is lost***.

## The number

Twelve.

One for each of the men who had said *shall we go and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread?*

Mark does not comment. He states the number and moves on.

## The count

*Five thousand men had eaten that meal.*

The Greek is **andres** — males. Matthew adds *besides women and children*.

## The arithmetic

They started with five loaves and ended with twelve baskets, having fed a crowd in between.

More was left than was brought.

## What is not described

The multiplication.

Mark says He looked up to heaven, blessed, broke, and *gave them to His disciples to set before
them*. The verb is imperfect — He kept giving. See the note on Luke 9:10–17.

At no point does any Gospel say the bread grew.

## The word for fragments

**Klasmata** — broken pieces, from the verb He used on the loaves.

The leftovers are pieces of what He broke, not crusts the crowd discarded.

## The other feeding

Mark records a second one, four thousand fed from seven loaves, with **seven baskets** left —
and a different word for basket: **spurides**, the large hamper, the kind Paul was let down the
Damascus wall in.

Mark distinguishes the two vocabularies, which is how we know he means two separate events.

## What Jesus does with the numbers later

*"When I broke the five loaves for the five thousand, **how many baskets full of fragments did you
take up?**" They said to Him, "Twelve."*

*"Also, when I broke the seven for the four thousand, how many large baskets full of fragments did
you take up?" And they said, "Seven."*

*"**How is it you do not understand?**"* See the note on Mark 8:17–21.

He quizzes them on the leftovers, not the meal. The baskets were the lesson.

## What happens next in the chapter

*Immediately He made His disciples get into the boat.* And then the storm, and Him walking on the
water.

*For **they had not understood about the loaves**, because their heart was hardened.*

Mark connects the two scenes explicitly. The fear on the water is traced back to the baskets.

> **The arithmetic.** Five loaves in, twelve baskets out, five thousand fed in between — and it
> was the leftovers He examined them on afterwards, not the meal.
