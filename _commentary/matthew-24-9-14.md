Part of the answer to a question the disciples asked on the Mount of Olives.

## The question

They had asked three things: *when will these things be? And what will be the sign of Your
coming, and of the end of the age?*

The whole chapter is the answer, and it moves between the fall of Jerusalem and something
further out.

## The handing over

*"Then they will **hand you over to be persecuted and put to death**, and you will be
**hated by all nations because of My name**."*

The Greek for *hand over* is **paradosousin** — the same verb used of Judas, and of Christ
being delivered up.

**Because of My name** — the reason given is not conduct.

## The falling away

*"At that time **many will fall away**, and they will **betray one another and hate one
another**."*

The Greek is **skandalisthesontai** — tripped up, made to stumble. From **skandalon**, the
trigger of a trap.

The betraying is internal — *one another*, twice.

## The false prophets

*"Many false prophets will appear and **lead many astray**."*

Jesus says it three times in the chapter: at the start, here, and again at verse 24 — *false
christs and false prophets will rise and show great signs and wonders*.

## The mechanism

*"**Because lawlessness will spread**, the love of most people will grow cold."*

The Greek for *lawlessness* is **anomia** — from *a-nomos*, without law.

**Will spread** — the verb is to multiply.

Jesus gives a cause and an effect, and the effect is about temperature.

## The cooling

*"**The love of most people will grow cold**."*

The Greek is **psugesetai** — to be chilled, to cool by blowing on. It occurs once in the
New Testament.

The word for love is **agape**, the highest of the four Greek words for it.

Not that love is replaced by hatred. That it drops in temperature.

**Most people** — the Greek is *the many*.

## The one who holds on

*"But **the one who holds on to the end will be saved**."*

The Greek is **hupomeinas** — from **hupomeno**, to remain under. The endurance word,
the same one James says testing produces. See the note on James 1:3.

## The proclamation

*"And **this good news of the kingdom will be proclaimed in the whole world as a testimony
to all nations**, and then the end will come."*

The Greek for *inhabited world* is **oikoumene** — the whole populated earth.

**As a testimony** — the Greek is **marturion**, evidence. The preaching is described as
the depositing of testimony rather than as a result achieved.

## The order of the paragraph

Persecution, falling away, betrayal, false prophets, lawlessness multiplying, love
cooling — and then, in the middle of all of it, the message going everywhere.

Jesus does not present the two as alternatives. Both run at once.

## Where He is sitting

*Now as He sat on the Mount of Olives, the disciples came to Him privately.*

Two days before the arrest, opposite the temple He had just walked out of, having said
*not one stone shall be left here upon another*.

> **The one sentence about the reader.** Everything else in the paragraph is about what
> conditions will do. *The one who holds on to the end* is the only clause with a person
> in it, and the verb means to stay under the weight.
