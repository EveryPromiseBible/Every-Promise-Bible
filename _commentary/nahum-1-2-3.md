The book's opening statement about God, and the middle of it is a quotation.

## The first half

*"The LORD is a God who guards what is His. **He will not share His love with rivals**, and He will
not let cruelty run forever."*

The Hebrew is **El qanno** — a jealous God. The adjective is used of God only, and only in the
context of covenant loyalty.

It is not the jealousy of insecurity. It is the jealousy of a husband, and the Old Testament says
so at length.

## The sentence the verse turns on

*"Yet hear this carefully, for it is **the heart of everything**: the LORD is **slow to anger, and His
power is immense**."*

**Erekh appayim ugdol-koach** — long of nostrils, and great of power.

**Erekh appayim** is a quotation. It is the phrase God used of Himself to Moses in the cleft of the
rock, after the golden calf. See the note on Exodus 34:5–7.

Nahum puts it at the front of a book about the annihilation of a city.

## Why it is here

Because Nineveh had already had a hundred and fifty years of it.

Jonah went there, they repented, the destruction was called off, and six generations later the
empire is at its worst. The *slow* in *slow to anger* is not an abstraction in this book. It has a
measurable length, and Nahum's readers had been living inside it.

*Or do you despise **the riches of His goodness, forbearance and longsuffering**, not knowing that
the goodness of God leads you to repentance?*

**Romans** 2, and **Paul**'s word is **makrothymias** — the Greek Old Testament's rendering of
Nahum's phrase.

*"The Lord is not slack concerning His promise, as some count slackness, but is **longsuffering**
toward us, not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance."*

**Peter** answers people who were asking why nothing had happened yet, and his answer is the length
of the delay is the mercy.

## The clause after it

*"He does not lose His temper, and **He does not let the guilty simply walk away**."*

The Hebrew is **venaqqeh lo yenaqqeh** — and clearing, He will not clear.

That is also part of the Exodus 34 quotation. The self-revelation at Sinai has both halves —
*merciful and gracious, longsuffering, abounding in goodness and truth… **by no means clearing the
guilty***.

Nahum quotes the whole sentence. He does not select the frightening half.

## How both can be true

Exodus 34 states the two clauses and does not explain them. So does Nahum.

*Whom God set forth as a propitiation by His blood, through faith… to demonstrate His
righteousness, because in His forbearance God had passed over the sins that were previously
committed, **that He might be just and the justifier** of the one who has faith in **Jesus**.*

**Romans** 3, and it is the missing paragraph. **Paul** names the one place the guilty are not
cleared and are cleared, and says that until it happened the passing-over needed accounting for.

## The road He travels

*"The clouds are the dust beneath His feet, and the whirlwind and the storm are the road He
travels."*

## And the next verse but four

*"**But the LORD is good.**"* See the note on Nahum 1:7.

> **What Nahum quotes.** *Slow to anger* — the sentence God gave Moses after the golden calf — put
> at the head of a book about a city's destruction, by a prophet writing to people who had endured
> the slowness for six generations.
