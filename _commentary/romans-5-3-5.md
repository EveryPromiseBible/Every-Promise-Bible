Four links, and the last one is the point of the sentence.

## Where it starts

*Our joy holds **even in our sufferings**.*

The Greek for *sufferings* is **thlipsis** — pressure, being squeezed. It is a word
about weight from outside.

Paul does not say the pressure is good, or sent, or enjoyable. He says the joy holds
inside it.

## The chain

*Suffering works **endurance** into us, and endurance works **a character proved in
the testing**, and a character proved that way works **hope**.*

- **endurance** — *hupomone*, remaining under, staying put under weight
- **proven character** — *dokime*, the word for metal that has been through the
  assay and passed. Tested and found genuine.
- **hope**

The chain runs one way and each link produces the next.

## "Because we know"

*Because **we know** that suffering works endurance into us.*

The Greek is a settled knowledge. What makes the joy possible is not a feeling about
the pressure but information about where the pressure leads.

## The hope that does not embarrass

*And that hope **does not put us to shame**.*

The Greek is that it does not disappoint, does not humiliate. Hope that turns out to
be misplaced makes a fool of the person who held it — Paul says this one will not.

## The reason given

*Because **the love of God has been poured out and has flooded our hearts**, through
the Holy Spirit who was given to us.*

Note what secures the hope. Not the character built in the previous verse.

The chain of endurance and proven character is real, and it is not the guarantee.
The guarantee is love already poured out — the Greek is a flooding, and the tense is
completed and standing.

## What the word "hope" means here

**Elpis** — not wishing. It is confident expectation of something good, and it is
the New Testament's normal word for what a Christian has about the future.

Paul defines the object of it in the verse before: *our joy overflows in **the hope of
the glory of God**.*

## Where the paragraph is anchored

Verse 1: *since we have been put right with God through faith, **we are at peace with
God**.*

Verse 2: *through Him we have also been brought in, by faith, **to this grace in which
we now stand**.*

The suffering paragraph sits on top of a settled peace and a standing position, and
Paul establishes both before he mentions pressure.

## And what comes three verses later

*While we were still sinners, Christ died for us.* See the note on Romans 5:6–8.

The proof of the love that floods the heart is dated to the cross, not to how the
suffering turns out.

> **What Paul had been through by the time he wrote this.** Stoned and left for
> dead at Lystra, run out of three cities, beaten with rods at Philippi. The chain in
> these verses is not theory he arrived at from a desk.
