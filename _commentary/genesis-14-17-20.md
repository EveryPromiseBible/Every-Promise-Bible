A priest-king appears in three verses, blesses Abram, and vanishes from the
narrative entirely.

## Two kings come out

*The **king of Sodom** came out to meet him… And **Melchizedek king of Salem**
brought out bread and wine.*

Both arrive at the same moment, and Abram deals with both. One offers him goods;
one offers him bread and wine and a blessing.

He accepts from one and refuses the other. See the note on Genesis 14:21–24.

## The names

**Melchizedek** — *melek tsedeq*, king of righteousness.

**Salem** — peace. Hebrews makes both points: *first being translated king of
righteousness, and then also king of Salem, meaning king of peace.*

Salem is the older name of Jerusalem.

## What he brought

*Bread and wine.*

Not a sacrifice, though he is called *priest of God Most High*. There is no altar
and no animal in the passage.

Bread and wine, carried out to a man coming back from a battle.

## Who he was priest of

***El Elyon***, God Most High — and he uses the title twice more in his blessing:
*Creator of heaven and earth*.

He is a Canaanite king in the middle of a chapter full of pagan kings, and he knows
God by name. Abram accepts the blessing without hesitation.

## The blessing

*"**Blessed be Abram** by God Most High… and **blessed be God Most High**, who
delivered your enemies into your hand."*

He blesses the man and then blesses God, and the second half tells Abram who won
the battle he had just fought.

## The tenth

*Then Abram gave him **a tenth of everything**.*

The first tithe in Scripture, and everything about the setting matters:

- Four hundred and thirty years before the law
- No commandment existed, no tabernacle, no priesthood to fund
- Abram is not an Israelite; there is no Israel
- Nobody asked him for it

He had just been blessed, and he gave a tenth of the spoils to the man who blessed
him.

## The word

The Hebrew for tithe is **maaser**, and inside it sits **asar** — the root behind
**osher**, wealth, and **ashir**, rich.

The tenth and the riches are built on the same three letters.

## The order in the passage

Bread and wine first. Then the blessing. Then the tenth.

Nothing is asked for and nothing is negotiated. Melchizedek arrives carrying something,
says two blessings, and Abram responds.

## What Hebrews does with it

*Now think how big a figure this must be. **Abraham himself, the father of the whole
family**, handed him a tenth of what he had taken.*

The argument runs on rank: the lesser is blessed by the greater. Melchizedek blessed
Abraham and received from him, so Melchizedek is greater — and Levi, still in
Abraham's body, paid through him. See the notes on Hebrews 7:4–5 and Hebrews 7:9–10.

> **What the record leaves out.** No father, no mother, no genealogy, no birth, no
> death. Hebrews notices all four omissions and says he is *made like the Son of
> God, remaining a priest continually.* Three verses in Genesis, one line in Psalm
> 110, and two chapters of Hebrews built on them.
