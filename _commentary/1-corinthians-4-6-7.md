key: 1 Corinthians 4:6–7
title: "Three questions, and none of them can be answered"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul has been talking about himself and Apollos, and now he says why: he was using two names
everyone respected to make a point he could not have made with theirs.

## Not beyond what is written

*So that through us you might learn **not to think of anyone beyond what is written**.*

**Me hyper ha gegraptai** — not above the things standing written. The perfect tense is the one used
for a document still in force.

Every party spirit in the church runs on estimates of men that Scripture does not supply. Paul's
cure is not to lower the estimate but to change where it comes from: take the measure from the
book, and there is no ladder left to arrange people on.

## And the swelling again

*And that none of you would **swell with pride**, siding with one man against another.*

**Physiousthe** — the bellows word from chapter eight. Notice the mechanism Paul names: the
inflation happens *on behalf of one against another*. Party pride is comparative by construction.
It cannot be produced alone in a room; it needs somebody to be better than.

## The three questions

*For **who is it that makes you different** from anyone else? And **what do you have that you did
not receive**? And **if you did receive it, why do you boast as though you had not**?*

The first verb is **diakrinei** — who distinguishes you, who draws the line between you and the next
man. Paul asks it and does not answer, because there is no answer available anywhere inside the
man.

The second is arithmetic rather than rebuke. Not *you should not boast*, but *count what you are
holding and tell me where it came from*. Faith, understanding, the church you were born into, the
capacity to grasp any of it — everything on the inventory arrived.

*By the grace of God I am what I am*, he writes eleven chapters later, about the hardest-working
apostle of the lot, *yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me.*

The third question needs no argument at all. If it was received, boasting is not wrong so much as
factually confused.

## Why this is rest and not scolding

A man who has nothing he did not receive has nothing he can lose by comparison, nothing to defend,
and nothing to keep up. *For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of
yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, lest anyone should boast.*

Paul closes the door on boasting in the same breath as he throws the door open on the gift. The two
are the same act.

> **The question, once, quietly.** *What do you have that you did not receive?* Ask it about the
> thing you are currently most tempted to be pleased with, or most afraid of losing. Everything on
> the list came from Jesus Christ, and He gave it to a church that was suing each other at the time
> He gave it.
