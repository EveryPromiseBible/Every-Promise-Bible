Paul takes one word out of the Abraham verse and runs an accounting argument on it.

## The word

*It is **credited**.*

The Greek is **logizetai** — an accounting term. To reckon, to put to somebody's account.

It appears eleven times in this chapter.

## The first case

*When a person works, **the pay is not credited as a gift; it is credited as a debt owed**.*

The Greek is **ou logizetai kata charin alla kata opheilēma**.

**Opheilēma** — a debt. The noun in the Lord's Prayer: *forgive us our debts*.

## What that establishes

An employer who pays wages is not being generous. He is settling an account.

Nobody thanks a man for a wage. The transaction has a different name.

## The second case

*But when a person **does not work** and instead **simply trusts** the God who makes the ungodly right.*

The Greek is **tō de mē ergazomenō, pisteuonti de**.

Two participles, set against each other. Not-working, and believing.

## The phrase

*The God who **makes the ungodly right**.*

The Greek is **ton dikaiounta ton asebē** — the one justifying the ungodly.

## Why it is shocking

The Old Testament says the opposite in so many words.

*He who justifies the wicked, and he who condemns the just, both of them alike are an abomination
to the LORD.*

And in Exodus: *I will not justify the wicked*.

## What Paul has done

He has taken the phrase for a corrupt judge and made it a description of God.

## What makes it not corruption

*Being justified freely by His grace **through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus**, whom God set
forth as a propitiation.* See the note on Romans 3:23–24.

The account was settled. Not waived.

*That He might be **just and the justifier** of the one who has faith in Jesus.*

## The result

*That trust is **credited to him as rightness**.*

Back to the accounting verb.

## The verse being argued from

*And he believed in the LORD, and He **accounted it to him for righteousness**.* See the note on
Genesis 15:6.

The Hebrew is **vayachsheveha lo tzedaqah**.

## The timing argument

Paul makes it in the same chapter: Abraham was counted righteous in Genesis 15, and circumcised in
Genesis 17.

*How then was it accounted? While he was circumcised, or uncircumcised? **Not while circumcised,
but while uncircumcised**.* See the note on Romans 4:9–12.

The order of the chapters is the argument.

## The second witness

*Just as David also describes the blessedness of the man to whom **God imputes righteousness apart
from works**: "Blessed are those whose lawless deeds are forgiven."* See the note on Romans 4:6–8.

The same verb again, in a quotation from Psalm 32.

## Where it ends

*Now it was not written for his sake alone… but **also for us**. It shall be imputed to us who
believe.* See the note on Romans 4:23–25.

The verb, one last time, with the readers as the account.

> **The two words.** A wage is credited as debt; this is credited as gift. Paul's whole case rests
> on which of the two columns the entry goes in.
