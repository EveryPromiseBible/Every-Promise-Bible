Paul starts a sentence, breaks off for thirteen verses, and comes back to it.

## The opening

*This is why I am what I am: **Paul, the prisoner of Christ Jesus**.*

The Greek is **ho desmios tou Christou Iesou** — the prisoner of Christ Jesus.

Not *of Nero*, or *of Rome*. He names whose he is.

## Where he is

In custody, chained to a guard, awaiting a hearing.

*For which I am an ambassador in chains*, he says at the end of the same letter.

## On whose behalf

*On behalf of **you who come from the nations**.*

The Greek is **huper humon ton ethnon** — for you Gentiles.

## Why that is the reason

The riot that put him in custody was about Gentiles.

*"Men of Israel, help! This is the man who teaches all men everywhere against our people, the law,
and this place; and furthermore **he also brought Greeks into the temple**."*

He was arrested for the doctrine chapter 2 has just set out: *He has broken down the middle wall
of separation*.

## The broken sentence

The Greek of verse 1 has no main verb.

Paul begins *for this reason I, Paul…* and then interrupts himself. He picks the sentence up again
at verse 14: *for this reason I bow my knees to the Father*.

Thirteen verses of parenthesis, and he never finishes the first thought.

## The trust

*Surely you have heard how **the grace of God was placed in my hands as a trust to be spent on
you**.*

The Greek is **ten oikonomian tes charitos tou Theou** — the stewardship of the grace of God.

**Oikonomia** — household management. The word for what a steward does with somebody else's
property.

Grace, handed over to be administered.

## The disclosure

*How **the secret was opened to me by God Himself uncovering it**.*

The Greek is **kata apokalupsin egnoristhe moi to musterion** — by revelation the mystery was made
known to me.

**Musterion** — not something unknowable, but something previously hidden and now disclosed. See
the note on Colossians 1:26–27.

## The cross-reference

*Exactly as **I wrote to you a little earlier in a few words**.*

Pointing back to chapters 1 and 2 of this letter.

## The invitation

*When you read that, **you will be able to see for yourselves what I have understood about the
secret of Christ**.*

The Greek is **dunasthe anaginoskontes noesai** — you are able, reading, to perceive.

Paul does not claim private access. He says: read it and you will see what I saw.

## What the secret is

*That **the Gentiles should be fellow heirs, of the same body, and partakers of His promise in
Christ through the gospel**.* See the note on Ephesians 3:5–7.

Three compounds in Greek, each beginning **sun-** — together with.

Co-heirs, co-bodied, co-sharers.

## Why it is a secret

Not that Gentiles would be blessed. The Old Testament says so repeatedly — *in your seed all the
nations of the earth shall be blessed*.

The hidden part is the terms: one body, on equal footing, without becoming Jews first.

## What he calls himself next

*To me, **who am less than the least of all the saints**, this grace was given.* See the note on
Ephesians 3:8–9.

The Greek is **elachistotero** — a comparative built on a superlative. *Leaster.* A word Paul
appears to have invented.
