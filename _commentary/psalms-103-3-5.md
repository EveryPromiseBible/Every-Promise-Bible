Five things listed in three verses, and David tells himself not to forget any of
them.

## What the psalm is doing

The verse before: *Bless the LORD, O my soul, and **forget not all His
benefits**.*

He is talking to himself, and the list that follows is what he is refusing to
forget.

## The five

- *He **forgives** all your wrongdoing*
- *He **heals** all your sicknesses*
- *He **redeems** your life from the pit*
- *He **crowns** you with steadfast love and mercy*
- *He **fills** your life with good things*

**All**, twice. All your wrongdoing and all your sicknesses, and the two sit in
consecutive lines as a matched pair — the same *all* over both.

## Forgiveness and healing together

Hebrew poetry pairs lines deliberately. These two are not a list of unrelated
items; they are set side by side because they belong together.

The same pairing turns up when the paralysed man is let down through the roof:
*your sins are forgiven* first, and then *rise, take up your bed.* And in Isaiah
53, *wounded for our transgressions* and *by His stripes we are healed* are two
clauses of one sentence.

## "Redeems your life from the pit"

The Hebrew is **ga'al** — the kinsman-redeemer's word, the relative whose duty was
to buy back what a family member had lost.

## "Crowns you"

The verb is to encircle, to surround. **Steadfast love** is **chesed**, the covenant
word.

Crowned with it — worn on the head, visible.

## The renewal

*He **fills your life with good things**, so **your youth is renewed like the
eagle's**.*

The Hebrew for *fills* is to satisfy, and the older rendering is *satisfies your
mouth with good things*.

**Youth** is **na'ar** — the word for a young man, a lad. The season of natural
strength.

The verb *renewed* is **chadash**, to make new, to restore. It is the same root as
the new moon, and the new covenant.

## The eagle

Isaiah uses the same bird for the same idea: *those who wait on the LORD shall
renew their strength; they shall **mount up with wings like eagles**.* See the note
on Isaiah 40:29–31.

An eagle is long-lived and, in the ancient picture, moults and comes back with new
feathers.

## What the psalm says a few lines on

*As far as the east is from the west, so far has He removed our transgressions from
us. As a father pities his children, so the LORD pities those who fear Him. For He
knows our frame; **He remembers that we are dust**.*

The same psalm that promises renewed youth also says He has not forgotten what we
are made of.

> **What David says about the source.** *Who satisfies…* — every verb in the list
> has the same subject, and it is never the reader. Five things, one actor.
