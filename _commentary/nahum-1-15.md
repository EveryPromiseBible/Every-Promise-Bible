The verse that turns the book toward Judah, and **Paul** quotes it in Romans.

## The sightline

*"**Look, there on the mountains, the feet of one who brings good news, who proclaims peace!**"*

The Hebrew is **hinneh al-heharim raglei mevasser** — behold, upon the mountains, the feet of one
bringing news.

**Mevasser** — the herald. The Greek Old Testament uses the verb **euangelizomenou**, which is the
verb behind **gospel**.

## The same verse elsewhere

*How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news, who proclaims peace.*
See the note on Isaiah 52:7–10.

Isaiah and Nahum have it almost word for word. They are contemporaries.

*And how shall they preach unless they are sent? As it is written: "**How beautiful are the feet of
those who preach the gospel of peace, who bring glad tidings of good things!**"*

**Romans** 10, and **Paul** builds the case for sending missionaries on this sentence.

## What the news is

*"**The wicked one will never march through you again. He is cut off entirely. He is gone.**"*

**Beliyya'al** — worthlessness, and by extension the worthless one. The New Testament transliterates
it: *what accord has **Christ** with **Belial**?*

The news is not that Judah has won. It is that the one who kept marching through will not be coming
again.

*Inasmuch then as the children have partaken of flesh and blood, He Himself likewise shared in the
same, **that through death He might destroy him who had the power of death**, that is, the devil,
and release those who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage.*

## The instruction

*"**Keep your feasts, Judah. Follow through on your promises to God.**"*

The Hebrew is **chogi Yehudah chaggayikh** — celebrate, Judah, your festivals.

An occupied people had let the pilgrim feasts lapse; the road to Jerusalem was not safe and the
tribute was crushing. The first command after the announcement is to resume the parties.

*Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven… but with the unleavened bread of sincerity
and truth. **For indeed Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us.***

**Paul** in **1 Corinthians** 5 tells a church to keep the feast, and names what it is now about.

## Where the verse is numbered

In the Hebrew Bible this is Nahum 2:1 — the first verse of the second chapter rather than the last
of the first. Either way it is the hinge: everything before it is about God's character, and
everything after it is the fall of the city.

The good news is placed at the join.

## And whose feet

The image is a runner appearing on a ridge above a besieged town, before he is close enough to be
heard, and the watchers can already tell from the running what the news is.

*"Nor is there salvation in any other, for there is no other name under heaven given among men by
which we must be saved."*

> **What the herald says.** Not that the army won — that the one who kept marching through is not
> coming back. And the first instruction issued to the town is to start holding the festivals
> again.
