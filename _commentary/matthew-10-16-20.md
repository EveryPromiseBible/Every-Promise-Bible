Two animals, chosen for one quality each, and neither is chosen for the obvious
reason.

## The pairing

*Be as wise as snakes and as harmless as doves.*

- **the snake** — not for cunning in the sinister sense. The word is *phronimos*,
  shrewd, practically intelligent, the same word Jesus uses of the wise man who
  built on rock and the wise servant left in charge.
- **the dove** — the word is *akeraios*, literally *unmixed*. Pure in the sense
  of nothing added, no hidden second motive.

So the instruction is: think clearly about what you are walking into, and have
nothing in you that needs concealing.

Either quality without the other goes wrong quickly. Shrewd without unmixed is
just cunning; unmixed without shrewd is walking into a wolf pack blindfolded —
which is what He has just said He is sending them into.

## The sending

*Look at what I am doing: I am sending you out like sheep into the middle of
wolves.*

He names the danger before He gives the instruction, and He does not pretend the
odds are good. Sheep among wolves is not a fair fight, and nothing in the sentence
suggests otherwise.

## What He promises instead of safety

*Do not fret about how to speak or what to say. What you are to say will be given
to you at that hour, because it is not you speaking; it is the Spirit of your
Father speaking through you.*

The provision is not protection from the councils and the floggings — He says
plainly those are coming. It is words, supplied at the hour they are needed, by
Someone else.

> **The turn in the middle.** *That will be your chance to testify.* Being dragged
> in front of governors and kings is described as an opportunity rather than a
> disaster. The whole passage assumes the trouble and then repurposes it.
