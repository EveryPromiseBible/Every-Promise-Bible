A prophet's complaint, and in the middle of it one of the best lines in the book
about Scripture.

## The opening

*O LORD, **You know**. Remember me. Come for me.*

Three short imperatives. He is not composing; he is asking.

## The line

*When Your words came, **I ate them**. Your words became **my joy, the delight of my
heart**.*

The Hebrew is that he found them and consumed them.

Ezekiel is handed a scroll and told *eat this scroll*, and reports *it was in my
mouth like honey in sweetness*. John does the same in Revelation 10 — sweet in the
mouth and bitter in the stomach.

Eating is the standard biblical picture for taking in what is written, and Jeremiah
uses it about a word that cost him everything.

## Why he says it

*For **I bear Your name**, O LORD God of hosts.*

The eating and the bearing of the name are given as one thing.

## What it cost him

*I **never sat in the circle** of those who laugh and make merry. Because Your hand
was on me, **I sat alone**.*

He is the prophet who was forbidden to marry, forbidden to go to funerals, and
forbidden to go to feasts — see the note on Jeremiah 16:5–7.

The isolation was instructed, and he lists it here as a grievance.

## The complaint

*Why is **my pain unending**, my wound grievous and **refusing to heal**?*

He is not asking rhetorically. He wants an answer.

## The accusation

*Will You be to me **like a stream that fails**, like **waters that are not sure**?*

A wadi — a desert streambed that runs in the rains and is dry when you need it.

He is asking God whether He is a seasonal brook.

It is the sharpest thing any prophet says to God's face, and it is preserved in the
book without softening. Two chapters earlier God had called Himself *the fountain of
living waters*; Jeremiah asks whether that is true.

## The answer he gets

*If you return, then I will bring you back; you shall stand before Me. If you take
out the precious from the vile, you shall be as My mouth.*

And: *I will make you to this people a fortified bronze wall… **for I am with you, to
save you and deliver you**.*

No explanation of the pain. A restoration, a commission, and a promise of company.

## What he does next

Keeps going for another thirty-seven chapters, through a cistern, stocks, a burned
scroll he dictates again, and the fall of the city.

> **The other thing he says in this book.** *His word was in my heart **like a burning
> fire shut up in my bones**; I was weary of holding it back, and I could not.* Said
> right after *I will not make mention of Him, nor speak any more in His name.* He
> resigned, and could not do it.
