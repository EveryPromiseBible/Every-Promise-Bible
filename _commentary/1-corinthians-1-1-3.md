The address on the envelope, and it is remarkable given what is in the letter.

## The sender

*From Paul, **called by the will of God to be an apostle** of Jesus Christ.*

The Greek is **kletos apostolos** — a called apostle.

He states his commission in the first line because the Corinthians were questioning it. He
defends it at length in chapter 9 and again in the second letter.

## The co-sender

*And from **Sosthenes our brother**.*

Almost certainly the synagogue ruler in Acts 18 who was beaten in front of Gallio's judgment
seat when the case against Paul was thrown out.

If so, the man who led the prosecution is co-signing the letter.

## The address

*To the **church of God that is in Corinth**.*

**Of God**, and **in Corinth**. Both, in the same phrase.

## What was in that church

Four factions each with a favourite teacher. A man living with his father's wife. Members
suing each other in pagan courts. Prostitution being defended with a slogan. Drunkenness at
the Lord's table so bad that some were dividing the meal by class. Chaos in the meetings.
And people denying the resurrection.

All of it is in the letter. Paul knows every item on the list as he writes the greeting.

## What he calls them anyway

*To those who have been **set apart in Christ Jesus**.*

The Greek is **hegiasmenois** — a perfect passive participle. Having been sanctified, and
standing sanctified.

Done to them, already, and holding.

## The second title

*Called to be **His holy people**.*

The Greek is **kletois hagiois** — called saints.

The word *saints* in the New Testament is never a category of exceptional Christians. It is
the ordinary word for the members, and here it is used of the messiest congregation Paul
wrote to.

## The widening

*Together with **everyone everywhere who calls on the name of Jesus Christ our Lord**.*

He addresses the letter beyond the city before he starts.

## The last clause

*Who is **their Lord and ours alike**.*

Written to a church split four ways over which teacher they belonged to.

The correction is in the greeting before the argument begins.

## The greeting

*Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.*

**Charis** and **eirene** — Paul's standard pair, in every letter he wrote.

Grace first, then peace, in that order, without exception.

The Greek greeting was **chairein**, rejoice. Paul bends it into **charis** and adds the
Hebrew **shalom** behind it.

## What he says next

*I **thank my God always concerning you** for the grace of God which was given to you by
Christ Jesus.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 1:4–9.

Six verses of thanksgiving before a word of correction, and the thanksgiving ends with *God
is faithful*.

## Where the first rebuke comes

Verse 10. *Now I plead with you, brethren… that there be no divisions among you.*

Nine verses of who they are, and then the problems.

> **The order of the letter.** Sanctified, called saints, enriched, lacking no gift,
> confirmed to the end — and then six chapters of what was going wrong. He puts the
> description first and never revises it.
