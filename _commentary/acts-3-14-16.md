Peter's second sermon, preached to a crowd that has just watched a man walk.

## Where he is

Solomon's colonnade, in the temple, with a healed man holding on to him.

*Now as the lame man who was healed held on to Peter and John, all the people ran together to
them in the porch which is called Solomon's, greatly amazed.*

The sermon starts because a crowd formed around a man they all knew.

## Who the man was

*A certain man **lame from his mother's womb** was carried, whom they **laid daily** at the
gate of the temple which is called Beautiful.*

Over forty years old, the next chapter says. Everybody entering the temple had walked past him
for decades.

Peter's line to him: *silver and gold I do not have, but what I do have I give you: in the name
of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk*.

## The disowning

*"You **disowned the Holy and Righteous One**."*

The Greek is **ernesasthe** — denied.

It is the verb used of Peter in the courtyard. He picks the word for what he himself had done,
and uses it of the crowd.

## The exchange

*"And **asked for a murderer to be given to you instead**."*

Barabbas. Peter does not soften it.

## The title

*"And you killed **the Author of life**."*

The Greek is **archegon tes zoes** — the originator, the founder, the pioneer, the one who
leads the way.

Hebrews uses the same word twice of Christ: *the captain of their salvation*, and *the author
and finisher of our faith*. See the note on Hebrews 12:1–4.

The sentence is a deliberate collision. They killed the one life comes from.

## The correction

*"Whom **God raised from the dead**, and **we are witnesses of it**."*

The pattern of every sermon in Acts: you killed Him, God raised Him, we saw it.

## The mechanism

*"And it is **His name, through faith in His name**, that has made this man strong."*

The Greek is **stereosen** — made firm, solid.

**This man you see and know.** Peter appeals to the crowd's own knowledge — they had been
stepping over him for years.

## The second clause

*"**The faith that comes through Jesus** has given him this **complete health** in front of all
of you."*

The Greek is **holokleria** — wholeness in every part, nothing missing. The word occurs once
in the New Testament.

Paul uses the adjective of the whole person: *may your whole spirit, soul, and body be
preserved*. See the note on 1 Thessalonians 5:23–24.

## Whose faith

Peter says it twice, and both times the faith is routed through the name rather than through
the man's conviction.

Nothing in Acts 3 records the lame man believing anything. He was asking for money.

## What Peter says next

*"Yet now, brethren, **I know that you did it in ignorance, as did also your rulers**."*

The same reading Paul gives: *had they known, they would not have crucified the Lord of
glory*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 2:6–9.

## The offer

*"**Repent therefore and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out**, so that times of
refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord."*

The Greek for *blotted out* is **exaleiphthenai** — wiped away, erased.

## How the day ends

The priests and the Sadducees arrive, *greatly disturbed that they taught the people and
preached in Jesus the resurrection from the dead*, and Peter and John spend the night in
custody.

*However, many of those who heard the word believed; and the number of the men came to about
five thousand.*

> **The title in the middle of the accusation.** *You killed the Author of life.* Peter puts
> the two halves in one clause and does not explain it.
