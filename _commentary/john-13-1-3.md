John spends three verses telling you what Jesus knew, and then tells you what He
did with a towel.

## The hour

*Jesus knew that **His hour had come**.*

John has been counting toward this since chapter 2 — *My hour has not yet come*,
said four times through the Gospel. Here it arrives.

## "All the way to the end"

*He had loved His own who were in the world, and **He loved them all the way to the
end**.*

The Greek is **eis telos** — to the end, to the uttermost, completely.

It is the same root as the word He speaks from the cross: **tetelestai**, it is
finished. The loving and the finishing share a root.

## Judas, in the middle of it

*The devil had already put it into the heart of **Judas Iscariot** to hand Him over.*

John places this between *He loved them to the end* and *knowing the Father had given
all things into His hands.*

Judas is in the room. His feet are among the ones about to be washed.

## The three things He knew

*And Jesus knew that **the Father had placed everything into His hands**, and that
**He had come from God**, and **was going back to God**.*

Total authority, settled origin, certain destination.

That is the fullest statement in the Gospels of what Jesus was conscious of about
Himself, and John puts it here on purpose.

## What He does with that knowledge

The next verse: *He rose from supper and **laid aside His garments**, took a towel and
girded Himself. After that, He poured water into a basin and began to wash the
disciples' feet.*

Foot-washing was the job of the lowest slave in a household — a Gentile slave, in
some accounts, because a Jewish servant could not be required to do it.

The man who knew everything was in His hands took off His outer clothing and knelt
on the floor.

## Which way round it runs

John does not write *although* He knew all this. He writes *knowing* — the security
is the cause, not the obstacle.

Nobody uncertain of their position takes the lowest job. It is the man who cannot be
diminished who can afford to kneel.

## What Peter says

*"You shall never wash my feet!"* And the answer: *"If I do not wash you, you have no
part with Me."*

Then Peter asks for a bath, and is told *he who is bathed needs only to wash his
feet, and is completely clean.*

## What He says afterwards

*"You call Me Teacher and Lord, and you say well, for so I am. **If I then, your Lord
and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet.**"*

He does not lower the titles. He confirms them, and then points at the basin.

> **What Paul does with the same shape.** *Let this mind be in you which was also in
> Christ Jesus, who, being in the form of God… **made Himself of no reputation**,
> taking the form of a bondservant.* Philippians 2 argues it exactly this way: the
> descent starts from a secure height, and Paul says think like that.
