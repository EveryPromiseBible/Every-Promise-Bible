Two verses on what a teacher should stop doing, and then what he should do instead.

## What has come before

*Be strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus. And the things that you have heard from me…
**commit these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also**.* See the note on
2 Timothy 2:1–2.

Then the soldier, the athlete and the farmer.

## The instruction

*Keep putting all this in front of them, and **charge them in the sight of the Lord to stop
fighting over words**.*

The Greek is **logomachein** — to war about words. A compound Paul appears to have coined; the
noun form appears once more, in the first letter.

**Enopion tou Theou** — in God's presence. The charge is given under oath.

## What word-fighting achieves

*It **settles nothing at all**, and it does **real damage to the people who have to listen**.*

The Greek is **ep' ouden chresimon** — useful for nothing.

And **epi katastrophe ton akouonton** — to the ruin of the hearers. **Katastrophe** — the word
English took directly.

## Who gets hurt

Not the two arguing. The people listening.

That is Paul's whole reason for raising it, and it is a pastoral observation rather than a
theological one.

## What Ephesus had

*Some who teach otherwise and do not consent to wholesome words… **obsessed with disputes and
arguments over words, from which come envy, strife, reviling, evil suspicions***.

The letters to Timothy keep coming back to it: *foolish and ignorant disputes, knowing that they
generate strife*.

## The alternative

*Give everything you have to **standing in front of God as someone tested and found good**.*

The Greek is **spoudason** — be diligent, make every effort, hurry to.

**Dokimon** — approved after testing. The word used of metal that has been assayed, of coins
checked for weight.

## Whose approval

*Standing in front of God.*

Not the congregation. The argument in verse 14 was conducted in front of listeners; the work in
verse 15 is done in front of God.

## The workman

*A **worker with nothing to be ashamed of**.*

The Greek is **ergaten anepaischunton** — an unashamed workman.

A manual word. Paul's own trade was tent-making, and the metaphor is of a craftsman whose finished
job can be inspected.

## The verb

*Cutting **a clean straight road through the word of truth**.*

The Greek is **orthotomounta** — from **orthos**, straight, and **temno**, to cut.

The word occurs twice in the Greek Old Testament, both times in Proverbs, of making a path
straight: *in all your ways acknowledge Him, and **He shall direct your paths***.

## What the image is

A road-maker driving a straight line through country, or a mason cutting stone so it sits square.

Not dissecting or dividing up. Cutting straight.

## Why it matters here

The contrast is with the word-fighting of verse 14, which wanders.

One man makes a road through the text. The other argues about terms and takes his listeners
nowhere.

## What follows

*But **shun profane and idle babblings**, for they will increase to more ungodliness. And their
message will spread like cancer.*

The Greek is **gangraina** — gangrene. Paul names two men who have done it.

*Nevertheless **the solid foundation of God stands**, having this seal: "The Lord knows those who
are His."* See the note on 2 Timothy 2:19–21.

## The last word on how to handle opponents

*And **a servant of the Lord must not quarrel** but be gentle to all, able to teach, patient, in
humility correcting those who are in opposition.*

> **The verb.** **Orthotomeo** — to cut straight. A road-maker's word, set against the
> word-fighting of the verse before, which goes in circles and damages the people listening.
