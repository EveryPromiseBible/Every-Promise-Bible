title: "Who is a God like You"

The last paragraph of the book, and the first three words are the prophet's own name.

## The name

*"**Who is a God like You?**"*

The Hebrew is **mi-El kamokha**.

**Micah** is **Mikayahu** — *who is like the LORD?* The book ends by asking the question the
prophet's parents put in his name, and the whole of the last three verses is the answer.

## The verb

*"Who **pardons sin** and **passes over the wrongdoing** of the remnant of His own people?"*

**Nose avon** — lifting up, carrying away iniquity. It is the verb Isaiah 53 uses of the Servant:
*He bore the sin of many.*

And **over al-pesha** — passing over transgression. The Passover verb.

*"And when I see the blood, **I will pass over you**."*

## The reason

*"You do not hold on to Your anger forever, **because You delight to show mercy**."*

The Hebrew is **ki-chaphetz chesed hu** — for He delights in **chesed**.

**Chaphetz** — to take pleasure in, to want. The reason given for the pardon is not the remnant's
condition. It is what God enjoys.

*"For **I desire mercy and not sacrifice**, and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings."*

*"But go and learn what this means: '**I desire mercy and not sacrifice**.' For I did not come to
call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance."*

**Matthew** 9, at a table full of tax collectors.

## The turning

*"**You will turn back to us again. You will have compassion on us.**"*

**Yashuv yerachamenu** — He will return, He will have compassion. **Racham**, the womb word.

The whole book has been about Israel being told to turn. The last paragraph has God turning.

## Underfoot

*"You will **tread our sins beneath Your feet**."*

The Hebrew is **yikbosh avonoteinu** — He will subdue our iniquities, as an enemy is subdued.

The sins are treated as a hostile force to be defeated rather than a debt to be waived.

*And the God of peace will **crush Satan under your feet** shortly.*

*Having disarmed principalities and powers, **He made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over
them in it**.*

## The sea

*"And **hurl all our wrongs into the depths of the sea**."*

**Vetashlikh bimtzulot yam** — and You will cast into the depths of the sea.

**Tashlikh** is the name of a Jewish new-year custom: going to running water and emptying the
pockets into it, from this verse.

The sea in Hebrew imagery is where things go that do not come back — chaos, depth, the place with
no floor.

*"As far as the east is from the west, so far has He removed our transgressions from us."*

*"For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and **their sins and their lawless deeds I will
remember no more**."*

## The last verse

*"You will keep faith with Jacob and show Your steadfast love to Abraham, **just as You promised on
oath to our ancestors in the days of long ago**."*

The book closes on an oath sworn centuries earlier, and the ground of the pardon is not the
remnant's repentance. It is a promise to two dead men.

*For **all the promises of God in Him are Yes**, and in Him Amen.*

> **The pun.** *Mi-El kamokha* — who is a God like You? The prophet's own name asks it, and the
> answer his book gives is a God who treads sins underfoot and throws them where the water has no
> bottom, because He enjoys doing it.
