Instructions given to the harvesters about a woman who is not in earshot.

## When

*And when she rose up to glean, **Boaz commanded his young men**.*

She has just eaten with the reapers and gone back to work. See the note on
Ruth 2:14.

He waits until she has left the table.

## The first instruction

*"**Let her glean even among the sheaves**, and do not reproach her."*

The Hebrew is **gam bein ha'omarim telaqqet** — even between the sheaves let her glean.

## Why that is unusual

The law gave gleaners the corners of the field and whatever the reapers dropped.

*You shall not wholly reap the corners of your field, nor shall you gather the gleanings of your
harvest. **You shall leave them for the poor and the stranger**.*

The sheaves are the harvested crop, already bound. Nobody gleans there.

## The second

*"And **do not reproach her**."*

The Hebrew is **velo tachlimuha** — do not humiliate, do not shame her.

He anticipates that the young men will object to a Moabitess in among the sheaves, and forbids
it in advance.

## The third

*"Also **let grain from the bundles fall purposely for her**."*

The Hebrew is **shol tasholu lah min-hatzevatim** — pulling out you shall pull out for her from
the bundles.

The infinitive absolute doubles the verb: *deliberately, definitely pull out*.

## What that is

Not permission to glean. Manufactured gleanings.

The workers are told to pull handfuls out of the bound sheaves and drop them where she will
find them.

## The fourth

*"**And leave it that she may glean, and do not rebuke her**."*

The Hebrew is **velo tig'aru-bah** — do not rebuke her.

Twice in two verses he forbids them to say anything to her about it.

## What she does not know

That the field is being seeded for her.

She is working, and the yield is being arranged. Neither the law nor her own effort accounts for
what she takes home.

## The result

*So she gleaned in the field until evening, and beat out what she had gleaned, and it was **about an
ephah of barley**.* See the note on Ruth 2:17–18.

An ephah is roughly thirty pounds. A gleaner in an ordinary day might get a few.

## What Naomi asks

*"**Where have you gleaned today? And where did you work?**"*

The quantity is the question. Naomi can tell from the weight that something happened.

*"**Blessed be the one who took notice of you**."*

## What Boaz already knew

*"**It has been fully reported to me, all that you have done for your mother-in-law** since the death
of your husband, and how you have left your father and your mother and the land of your birth,
and have come to a people whom you did not know before."*

He had heard about her before she walked into the field.

## The blessing he gave

*"**The LORD repay your work, and a full reward be given you by the LORD God of Israel, under whose
wings you have come for refuge**."* See the note on Ruth 2:11–12.

He prays that God will repay her, and then quietly arranges the repayment out of his own crop.

## The word for wings

**Kenaphav** — his wings.

She later uses the same word to him at the threshing floor: *take your maidservant under your
wing*, which is also the word for the corner of a garment.

The man who prayed she would find refuge under God's wings is asked to spread his own.

## What he tells them to do

*"Let her glean even among the sheaves, and do not rebuke her. Also **let grain from the
bundles fall purposely for her**; leave it that she may glean, and **do not reprimand her**."*

The Hebrew is *pull out for her from the bundles* — deliberately, and twice he says: do not
let her be spoken to about it.

She is meant to think she is finding it.

## Who had already decided about her

Provision arranged in advance, dropped where she would come across it, by a man who had made
up his mind about her before she arrived in his field that morning.

Paul uses the same shape of Christ: *in Him we have redemption through His blood, the
forgiveness of sins, **according to the riches of His grace which He made to abound toward
us***.

The Greek is **eperisseusen** — He made it overflow.

## Whose field it was

*And she happened to come to the part of the field belonging to Boaz.*

The narrator says *happened*, and plainly does not think it was. It is the same word the
servant used at the well in Genesis 24 when he asked God for an occurrence. See the note on
Genesis 24:12–14.

> **The instruction.** Not permission to glean — *pull grain out of the bundles and drop it for
> her*. And twice: do not let her hear you say anything about it.
