Four verses, and He agrees with her twice.

## Where the conversation is

She has asked for the water that means she will not have to keep coming back. See the note on
John 4:7–9.

*"Sir, give me this water, that I may not thirst, nor come here to draw."*

## The instruction

*"**Go, call your husband, and come back here**."*

It arrives without warning, and it changes the subject completely.

## Her answer

*"**I have no husband**."*

Two words in Greek — **ouk echo andra** — and it is true as far as it goes.

The shortest answer she gives in the whole conversation. She has been talkative up to this
point.

## The first agreement

*"**You are right to say you have no husband**."*

The Greek is **kalos eipas** — you have spoken well, beautifully, rightly.

He does not correct her. He affirms the sentence.

## The record

*"You have had **five husbands**, and **the man you have now is not your husband**."*

Five. In that society a woman did not divorce; she was divorced, five times, or widowed, or
some combination.

The passage does not say which, and it does not assign blame. It states the count.

## The second agreement

*"**What you have said is true**."*

He says it twice, either side of the disclosure.

Her four words were accurate, and He wraps the facts inside two affirmations of her honesty.

## What He does not do

Rebuke her. Ask her to explain. Tell her to fix it before He continues.

The conversation goes straight on to worship, and then to His own identity.

## Her response

*"Sir, **I can see that You are a prophet**."*

The Greek is **theoro** — I perceive, I observe.

Nicodemus opened with *we know You are a teacher come from God*. See the note on
John 3:1–3.

She reaches a similar verdict, and John puts the two conversations in consecutive chapters.

## What she does next

Raises the mountain question — *our fathers worshipped on this mountain* — which reads as a
diversion and produces the fullest teaching on worship in the New Testament. See the note on
John 4:20–24.

And then: *I know that Messiah is coming*, and the answer, *I am He*. See the note on
John 4:25–26.

## What she tells the town

*"Come, see a Man who **told me all things that I ever did**. Could this be the Christ?"*

She takes the most exposing part of the conversation and makes it the headline.

Whatever the five husbands had cost her socially, she uses it as the reason to come and look.

## What the town says afterwards

*"Now we believe, **not because of what you said**, for we ourselves have heard Him."* See the
note on John 4:39–42.

## Where the exposure sits

Between a request for water and a statement about worship.

He raises it, agrees with her about it twice, and moves on.

> **What He affirms.** Her sentence. *You have said it well*, and then *what you have said is
> true* — with five husbands named in between.
