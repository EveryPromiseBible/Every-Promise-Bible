The longest Old Testament passage quoted in the New Testament, and it is four verses long.

## Where it comes

Jeremiah's ministry is forty years of announcing a siege nobody wanted to hear about.

Chapters 30 to 33 are the exception — the Book of Consolation, written to people about to lose
everything.

## The announcement

*Look, **the days are coming**, says the LORD, when I will make **a new covenant** with the house of
Israel and the house of Judah.*

The Hebrew is **berit chadashah** — a new covenant.

The phrase occurs once in the Old Testament. Here.

## Who it is with

Both houses. The northern kingdom had been gone for a century.

## What it is not like

*It will **not be like the covenant I made with their fathers** on the day I took them by the hand to
lead them out of Egypt.*

The Hebrew is **behazziqi veyadam** — when I took hold of their hand.

The image is a parent leading a child.

## What went wrong

*My covenant that **they broke**, though **I was a husband to them**.*

The Hebrew is **ve'anochi ba'alti vam** — and I was a master, or husband, to them.

The verb is disputed. The Septuagint reads it as *and I disregarded them*, and Hebrews follows the
Septuagint.

## The first clause

*I will **put My law within them**, and I will **write it on their hearts**.*

The Hebrew is **beqirbam** — in their inward parts.

The old covenant was on stone, outside, read to them. The new one is placed inside.

## The second

*I will be their God, and **they will be My people**.*

The covenant formula. It runs from Exodus to Revelation.

## The third

*No longer will anyone need to teach his neighbour… **for they will all know Me, from the least of
them to the greatest**.*

The Hebrew is **kullam yed'u oti** — all of them shall know Me.

**Yada** — knowing by relationship.

## What that does not abolish

Teaching.

Paul appoints teachers, writes letters, and tells Timothy to commit the message to faithful men.
The clause is about direct access, not the end of instruction.

## The fourth

*For **I will forgive their wickedness, and I will remember their sins no more**.*

The Hebrew is **eslach** — I will pardon. And **lo ezkor-od** — I will not remember again.

## The word *for*

**Ki** — for, because.

The last clause is the ground of the other three. The law written inside, the relationship, and the
universal knowing all rest on the sins not being remembered.

## What Hebrews does with it

Quotes the whole passage twice — in chapter 8 and again in chapter 10.

*In that He says, "**A new covenant**," **He has made the first obsolete**.* See the note on
Hebrews 8:13.

And: *"**Their sins and their lawless deeds I will remember no more**." Now where there is remission
of these, **there is no longer an offering for sin**.* See the note on
Hebrews 10:18.

Hebrews builds its central argument on the last clause.

## Where the words are said again

*"This cup is **the new covenant in My blood**, which is shed for you."*

At a table, on the night before He died.

## What follows in Jeremiah

*Thus says the LORD, who gives the sun for a light by day, the ordinances of the moon and the
stars… **If those ordinances depart from before Me, then the seed of Israel shall also cease from
being a nation before Me forever**.*

The durability of the covenant is tied to the durability of the solar system.

> and the knowing all rest on the sins not being remembered.
