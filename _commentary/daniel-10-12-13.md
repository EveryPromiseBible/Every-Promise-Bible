The messenger explains why he is late.

## What Daniel had been doing

The chapter opens: *in those days I, Daniel, was mourning three full weeks. I ate no
pleasant food, no meat or wine came into my mouth, nor did I anoint myself at all, till
three whole weeks were fulfilled.*

Twenty-one days of fasting, by an old man in Persia.

## The greeting

*"**Do not be afraid, Daniel**."*

He has just been told *O Daniel, man greatly beloved* — the same description Gabriel gave
him in chapter 9. See the note on Daniel 9:20–21.

## The timing

*"**From the first day** you set your heart to understand, and to humble yourself before
your God, **your words were heard**."*

Day one. The prayer registered immediately.

## Why he came

*"**I have come because of your words**."*

The visit is caused by the praying. Not permitted by it — caused.

## The delay

*"But **the prince of the kingdom of Persia stood against me for twenty-one days**."*

The Hebrew is *withstood me*.

The prayer was heard on day one and the answer arrived on day twenty-one. Daniel had
spent the whole of it fasting, with no indication anything was happening.

Nothing in the chapter suggests his praying was deficient, and the messenger does not
suggest it either.

## The prince of Persia

Not Cyrus. The messenger goes on to say *I will return to fight with the prince of
Persia; and when I have gone out, indeed the prince of Greece will come*.

Kings do not follow kings that way, and empires do. The chapter treats these as
authorities standing behind nations.

## The help

*"Then **Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help me**."*

Michael appears three times in Daniel and twice in the New Testament — in Jude, disputing
over the body of Moses, and in Revelation 12, at war.

Daniel 12 calls him *the great prince who stands watch over the sons of your people*.

## What Daniel could not see

Any of it. He fasted three weeks and got nothing, and then was told what had been going
on the whole time.

He never asks about it and is never told to pray differently.

## What he was in the middle of

Chapter 10 opens the last vision of the book — the long account of the kings of the north
and south that runs to the end.

The messenger says: *now I have come to make you understand what will happen to your
people in the latter days.*

## The state Daniel was in

*I retained no strength… my comeliness was turned in me into corruption, and I retained
no strength.*

He is touched three times in the chapter and told twice not to be afraid. *A hand touched
me, which made me tremble on my knees.*

## What Paul says about the categories

*We do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against **principalities, against powers,
against the rulers of the darkness of this age**.*

And immediately after listing the armour: *praying always with all prayer and
supplication in the Spirit.* See the note on Ephesians 6:14–17.

> **What the messenger says about the prayer.** That it was heard on the first day, and
> that he was dispatched because of it. The twenty-one days were not the wait for an
> answer — they were the journey.
