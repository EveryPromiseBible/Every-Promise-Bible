Three verses, and the price is quoted from a prophet nobody in the room was thinking of.

## What comes immediately before

The alabaster jar at Bethany. A woman pours very costly ointment on His head and the
disciples are indignant: *why this waste? For this fragrant oil might have been sold for
much and given to the poor.*

John names the objector: Judas, *not that he cared for the poor, but because he was a thief,
and had the money box*.

*Then* — Matthew's next word — Judas goes to the chief priests.

## The introduction

*Then **one of the Twelve, the one called Judas Iscariot**, went to the chief priests.*

*One of the Twelve.* Matthew, Mark and Luke all use the phrase, and all three use it here.

He had been sent out with the others to preach and heal. He had been given authority over
unclean spirits with the rest of them.

## The offer

*"**What will you pay me**," he asked, "**if I hand Him over to you?**"*

He names no figure. He asks what they will give.

The Greek is **paradoso** — hand over, deliver up. The same verb used of what the Father does
with the Son in Romans: *He who did not spare His own Son, but **delivered Him up** for us
all*.

## The amount

*They **counted out thirty pieces of silver** into his hands.*

Exodus 21 fixes the value: *if the ox gores a manservant or a maidservant, he shall give to
their master **thirty shekels of silver**.*

The compensation for a slave gored by a bull.

## Where the figure comes from

Zechariah 11, the shepherd who is dismissed and asks for his wages:

*So they weighed out for my wages thirty pieces of silver. And the LORD said to me,
"**Throw it to the potter** — that princely price they set on me." So I took the thirty pieces
of silver and threw them into the house of the LORD for the potter.*

The sarcasm is in the Hebrew. *That princely price.*

## What Judas does with it

*Then Judas, His betrayer, seeing that He had been condemned, was remorseful and brought back
the thirty pieces of silver to the chief priests… saying, "I have sinned by betraying
innocent blood."*

*And he **threw down the pieces of silver in the temple** and departed, and went and hanged
himself.*

Thrown into the house of the LORD, as Zechariah wrote.

## What the priests do with it

*"It is not lawful to put them into the treasury, because they are the price of blood."*

*And they consulted together and **bought with them the potter's field**, to bury strangers
in.*

To the potter.

They were scrupulous about the treasury and not about the transaction.

## What he was watching for

*From that moment on he was **watching for the right opening**.*

The Greek is **eukairian** — a good opportunity, a favourable moment.

He needed Jesus away from the crowds, and he knew where the garden was: *Judas, who betrayed
Him, also knew the place, for Jesus often met there with His disciples*.

## What He said at the table

*"**The Son of Man indeed goes just as it is written of Him, but woe to that man by whom the
Son of Man is betrayed**."*

Both halves at once, and Matthew does not soften either.

And Judas: *Rabbi, is it I?* — the only one of the twelve who says *Rabbi* rather than
*Lord*.

> **What the sum was.** Not an arbitrary bribe. Thirty shekels is the figure Exodus sets for a
> slave killed by somebody's animal, and Zechariah had already called it a princely price.
