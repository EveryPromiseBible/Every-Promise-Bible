The one scribe in the Gospels who asks a question in good faith.

## What has been happening

A run of hostile questions in the temple, in the last week. The chief priests on authority.
The Pharisees and Herodians on the tax. The Sadducees on the woman with seven husbands.

Each one is a trap, and each one fails.

## What he noticed

*One of the teachers of the law had been **listening to them argue**, and he **saw how well
Jesus answered**.*

The Greek is **kalos** — well, finely.

He is impressed by an opponent, which is where the whole exchange starts.

## The question

*"**Of all the commandments, which is the most important?**"*

The rabbis counted 613 in the law and debated which were weighty and which light. It is a
standard question, and Matthew's version says it was asked *testing Him*.

Mark's does not.

## The answer

*"The first is this: '**Hear, O Israel — the Lord our God, the Lord is one**.'"*

He includes the Shema's opening line, which nobody else in the New Testament quotes. See the
note on Deuteronomy 6:4–5.

The question was about a commandment. He starts with a statement of fact.

## The four

*"**With all your heart** and with **all your soul** and with **all your mind** and with **all
your strength**."*

Deuteronomy has three — heart, soul, might. The Greek adds **dianoia**, mind.

## The second

*"And the second is this: '**Love your neighbor as yourself**.'"*

Leviticus 19, a single clause in the middle of a chapter about not gleaning your field to the
edge and not defrauding a hired hand.

He was asked for one and gives two, and joins them.

## The verdict

*"**There is no commandment greater than these two**."*

## The scribe's reply

*"**Well said, Teacher. You have spoken the truth**."*

He agrees with the answer, and then goes further than it.

## What he adds

*"He is one, and there is no other besides Him. And to love Him with all your heart… and to
love your neighbor as yourself — **that is worth more than all the burnt offerings and
sacrifices put together**."*

The Greek is **perissoteron** — more, in excess of.

He said that in the temple, standing among the sacrifices, as a professional handler of the
law.

He is quoting the prophets: *to obey is better than sacrifice*, and *I desire mercy and not
sacrifice*, and Micah's *what does the LORD require of you but to do justly, to love mercy,
and to walk humbly with your God*.

## The response

*When Jesus heard **how thoughtfully he had answered**.*

The Greek is **nounechos** — sensibly, with understanding. It occurs once in the New
Testament.

*He said to him, "**You are not far from the kingdom of God**."*

## What that sentence is

Not a welcome, and not a rejection. A measurement.

The man has said something true and is being told he is close. Mark does not record what he
did next.

## The silence

*And after that **no one dared to ask Him anything more**.*

The run of questions ends here — not with a trap sprung, but with a man agreeing.

## What Jesus asks next

*"How is it that the scribes say that the Christ is the Son of David?"* — and He quotes
Psalm 110: *the LORD said to my Lord, "Sit at My right hand."* See the note on Psalms 110:1.

*"David himself calls Him 'Lord'; how is He then his Son?"*

And the note that follows: *and the common people heard Him gladly*.

> **What the scribe did.** Agreed, and then said that loving God and neighbour outweighed the
> whole sacrificial system — standing in the temple, as a lawyer. And was told he was close.
