The last book of the Old Testament announces a messenger, and then asks a
question nobody wants to answer.

## The two figures

*I am sending My messenger, and he will prepare the way before Me. Then suddenly
the Lord you are seeking will come to His temple.*

The Gospels take the first as John the Baptist — all four quote or allude to this
verse — and the second as Christ. *Suddenly* is the word Malachi chooses, and
four hundred silent years followed before it happened.

## The question

*Who can endure the day of His coming? Who can stand when He appears?*

The people of Malachi's day had been complaining that God was absent and that
serving Him was pointless. The prophet's answer is to ask whether they have
thought through what His arrival would mean.

It is the same reversal the prophets keep performing — Amos asking why anyone
would long for the day of the LORD, since it is darkness and not light.

## The two images

Both are about cleaning, and both hurt:

- **a refiner's fire** — the silversmith holds the metal in the heat until the
  impurities rise and can be lifted off
- **a launderer's soap** — a harsh alkaline lye, the strongest cleaning agent of
  the ancient world

The point of both is stated: *until they bring offerings to the LORD in
righteousness*. The refining has an object, and it is worship that is actually
worth something.

> **Who gets refined.** *He will purify the Levites* — the priests, the
> professionals, the people running the temple. The chapter that goes on to
> charge a nation with robbing God opens by putting the clergy in the fire first.
