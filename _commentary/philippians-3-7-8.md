---
key: Philippians 3:7–8
title: "What he moved into the loss column"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul the accountant, keeping two columns, and the shock is what he puts in the second one.

## Profit and loss

*Every item on that list that once stood in my **profit** column I have moved over to the **loss**
column.*

**Kerde** — gains, plural. **Zemian** — loss, singular. A whole list of assets collapsed into one
entry on the other side of the page.

And the tenses matter. *I have counted* is perfect — a decision made and still standing. Then he
shifts: *I **go on counting** everything as loss* — present tense, the accounting redone every day
since.

## What was on the list

Look back at what he had just enumerated: circumcised the eighth day, of the stock of Israel, the
tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews, as to the law a Pharisee, as to zeal persecuting the
church, *as to the righteousness which is in the law, **blameless***.

Not one item is a sin. Every single one is a religious credential, and the last one is a claim of
blamelessness.

That is what makes this the hardest paragraph in Paul for religious people. He is not describing a
sinner giving up his sins. He is describing a good man giving up his goodness, because he had found
out what it was worth beside the Person he had met on a road.

## The word he chose

*I count it all as **garbage hauled to the heap**.*

**Skybala** — refuse, scraps thrown out to the dogs, dung. Paul had every literary option available
and picked the crudest one in the language.

Not *less valuable*. Not *outweighed*. Refuse, and there is nothing you do with refuse but get it
out of the house.

## The one thing on the other side

*Next to the one thing that **outweighs it all** — knowing **Christ Jesus my Lord**.*

**To hyperechon** — the surpassing thing. And then the possessive: *my* Lord. The whole transaction
is personal; he did not exchange a religion for a better religion.

The next verse says what he is standing in now: *not having my own righteousness, which is from the
law, but that which is **through faith in Christ**, the righteousness which is from God by faith.*

Two righteousnesses exist. One he built over a lifetime and could recite; the other was given to
him. He cannot stand in both, and he says which one he wants to be found in.

> **The trade nobody expects.** It is not sin for holiness. It is my righteousness for His — and
> only one side of that exchange was ever worth anything. Paul made it once on a road to Damascus
> and says he has been re-checking the arithmetic ever since, and it keeps coming out the same way.
