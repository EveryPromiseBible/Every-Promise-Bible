The end of the harshest chapter in the Gospels, and it finishes in tears.

## The name, twice

*"**Jerusalem, Jerusalem**."*

The doubled name is how you address someone you love. *Abraham, Abraham.*
*Moses, Moses.* *Simon, Simon.* *Saul, Saul.*

Seven woes have just been pronounced over the scribes and Pharisees. Then this.

## "How often"

*"**How often** I have longed to gather your children together."*

Matthew's Gospel records perhaps three visits to Jerusalem. *How often* is the
speech of somebody who has been reaching for that city for a great deal longer
than three years.

The One saying it is the One who sent the prophets they killed.

## The hen

*"The way a **hen** gathers her chicks under her wings."*

The image is not lion, eagle, or shepherd. A hen — an ordinary farmyard bird with
no weapons at all, whose one method of protection is to open her wings and let
her chicks run under her, and to stay there and take whatever comes.

The wings language is the Psalms' — *under the shadow of Your wings* — and He puts
Himself in it.

## "And you were not willing"

The refusal is theirs and He says so plainly. He does not say they could not.

The Greek is *you did not want to*.

## "Your house is left to you empty"

*"Look, **your house** is left to you empty."*

*Your* house. Two chapters earlier He had called it *My house*, when He turned
over the tables — *My house shall be called a house of prayer.*

The possessive has changed, and the word *empty* is *desolate*, abandoned.

He then walks out of the temple, and the next verse of chapter 24 has the
disciples pointing at the buildings and Him saying not one stone will be left on
another.

## The condition

*"For I tell you, **you will not see Me again until** you say, 'Blessed is He who
comes in the name of the Lord.'"*

*Until.* It is not a closing of the door; it is a term.

The words He names are from Psalm 118, the crowd's shout at the triumphal entry
five days earlier. He is saying the city will say them again, and that when it
does, they will see Him.

The last word of the harshest chapter in Matthew is a word about a reunion.

> **The season this describes.** Hosea's *not My people* — the face hidden, the
> house empty, the prophets gone. And even then it is *until*, with the words of
> the return already written out for them. See the notes on Hosea 1:8–9 and
> Romans 9:25–26.
