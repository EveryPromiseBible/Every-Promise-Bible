The king offers her half his kingdom and she asks for another dinner.

## The offer

*"**What is your petition? It shall be granted. What is your request? Even to half the
kingdom, it shall be done.**"*

He says it three times across two chapters. It is court formula, but it is a blank cheque from
a man who has just spared her life.

## What she asks for

*"**Let the king and Haman come to the banquet I will prepare for them tomorrow.**"*

Another meal. That is the whole request.

## What the delay costs and buys

She has risked her life to get into the room and then does not use it. Everything hangs on one
more day.

And what happens in that day: Haman goes home and builds a gallows, his wife tells him to, the
king cannot sleep, the chronicles are read aloud, the overlooked entry is found, and Haman
walks into the palace at dawn to ask for a hanging and is handed a robe and a horse instead.
See the note on Esther 6:10–11.

None of it could have happened if she had asked on the first night.

## What the book will not say

Why she waited. No reason is given — not nerves, not strategy, not a word from God.

Esther never names Him and never explains itself. What it does is lay the timing out so
exactly that the reader cannot miss it.

*But **when the fullness of the time had come**, God sent forth His Son.*

**Paul**'s phrase for a delay nobody could see the shape of from inside.

## What the waiting is not

Passivity. She has already fasted three days, already broken the law of the inner court,
already been given the sceptre. See the note on Esther 5:1–2.

*Rest in the LORD, and **wait patiently for Him**… do not fret because of him who prospers in
his way, because of the man who brings wicked schemes to pass.*

> **What one night was worth.** She had the king's word and did not spend it. Between the
> first banquet and the second, a sleepless night turned the whole book over.
