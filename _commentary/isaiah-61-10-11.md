Two pictures of righteousness, one verse apart: a garment put on you, and a seed that comes up out
of the ground.

## The opening

*I **delight greatly in the LORD**. My soul rejoices in my God.*

The Hebrew is **sos asis** — an infinitive doubled with the verb. Rejoicing I will rejoice.

## The reason

*For He has **clothed me with garments of salvation** and **wrapped me in a robe of righteousness**.*

The Hebrew is **hilbishani** — He has dressed me. And **ye'atani** — He has wrapped, enveloped me.

Both verbs are His. Nobody in the sentence dresses himself.

## The two words

**Bigdei-yesha** — garments of salvation. **Me'il tzedaqah** — a robe of righteousness.

**Me'il** is the outer robe, the visible one. Samuel's mother made him one every year. Jonathan
took his off and gave it to David.

## The comparison

*Like **a bridegroom crowned with a splendid headdress**, like **a bride adorned with her jewels**.*

The Hebrew is **kechatan yechahen pe'er** — like a bridegroom acting the priest with a turban.

**Pe'er** — the priestly headpiece. The verb is from **kohen**, priest.

The bridegroom is described in priestly clothing on the day.

## What the chapter opened with

*The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon Me, because the LORD has anointed Me to **preach good tidings to
the poor**.* See the note on Isaiah 61:1–3.

And in that passage: *the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness*, and *beauty for ashes* —
**pe'er** for **epher**, a pun on the two words.

The headdress here is the same noun.

## The one who read it aloud

*He was handed the book of the prophet Isaiah… **"Today this Scripture is fulfilled in your
hearing."*** See the note on Luke 4:16–21.

He stopped mid-verse, before the day of vengeance, and sat down.

## The second image

*For as **the soil makes the young plant come up**, and **a garden makes the seed spring forth**.*

The Hebrew is **tatzmiach** — causes to sprout. From **tzemach**, a shoot, a branch.

## The word

**Tzemach** is a messianic title in the prophets: *behold, the days are coming, that I will raise
to David a **Branch** of righteousness*.

The verb here is the same root.

## What grows

*So the Lord GOD will make **righteousness and praise spring up before all the nations**.*

Not privately. **Neged kol-hagoyim** — in front of all the nations.

## The two pictures together

A robe is put on from outside. A seed comes up from underneath.

Isaiah gives both in consecutive verses and does not choose between them.

## What Paul does with the first

*For He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might **become the righteousness of God**
in Him.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 5:20–21.

And *put on the Lord Jesus Christ*.

## Where the robe turns up

*"**Bring out the best robe and put it on him**."*

The Greek is **stolēn tēn prōtēn** — the first robe. Spoken over a son who arrived smelling of
pigs and had a speech prepared. See the note on Luke 15:20–24.

He never finishes the speech.

## And at the end

*It was granted to her to be arrayed in fine linen, clean and bright, **for the fine linen is the
righteous acts of the saints**.* See the note on Revelation 19:7–8.

The Greek is **edothē autē** — it was given to her.

> **The two images.** A robe goes on from the outside; a seed comes up from underneath. Isaiah puts
> both in the same breath and lets them stand together.
