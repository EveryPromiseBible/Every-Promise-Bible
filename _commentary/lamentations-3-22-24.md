The turn of the book, written by a man standing in the ash of his own city.

## What is around it

Twenty-one verses of unrelieved darkness — *He built a wall around me… He set me down in the deep
places*. See the note on Lamentations 3:1–6.

And then: *this I recall to my mind, therefore I have hope.*

Nothing has changed outside. What changes is what he calls to mind.

## Why they are not swept away

*Because of **the LORD's great love** we are not swept away.*

The Hebrew is **chasdei YHWH** — the covenant loyalties of the LORD, plural. **Chesed**.

The reason given for their survival is entirely on God's side of the sentence. Not their
repentance, not a remnant's faithfulness, not anything the five poems could point at — and the
poems are honest that there was nothing to point at.

## The mercies

*For **His tender mercies never run dry**.*

**Rachamav** — from the womb word again. **Lo khalu** — they are not finished, not used up.

## The repetition

*Every morning they are new. **Every morning**.*

The Hebrew is **chadashim labbeqarim** — new for the mornings.

He is writing after a siege in which mothers ate their children, and the sentence he arrives at is
about breakfast — that the supply resets daily and had reset that day.

*"Give us this day **our daily bread**."*

**Epiousion** — a word that occurs almost nowhere else in Greek, usually taken as *for the coming
day*. The prayer asks for one day's worth, which is the ration Lamentations describes.

## Great is Your faithfulness

*Great, so great, is Your faithfulness.* **Rabbah emunatekha**.

*If we are faithless, **He remains faithful; He cannot deny Himself**.*

**2 Timothy** 2, and it is the strongest form of the claim in Scripture: the faithfulness is not
contingent, because it is not a policy. It is what He is.

*God is faithful, by whom you were called into the fellowship of His Son, **Jesus Christ** our
Lord.*

*Jesus Christ is **the same yesterday, today, and forever**.*

## The portion

*The LORD is all I have, my soul says, **so in Him I will wait, and hope**.*

The Hebrew is **chelqi** — my portion, my allotted share. It is the word for the parcel of land a
family received when the country was divided — and the tribe of Levi was given none, because *the
LORD is their inheritance*.

The man saying it has just lost everything that could be called a portion.

*The LORD is **the portion of my inheritance and my cup**; You maintain my lot.*

Psalm 16, and **Peter** quotes that psalm at Pentecost and says David *spoke concerning the
resurrection of the Christ*.

> **The line he arrives at.** Not that the city will be rebuilt — he does not know that. That the
> mercies are new this morning, and that the LORD is his portion, said by a man with nothing left
> to be a portion of.
