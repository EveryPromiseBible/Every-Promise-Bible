The last of the ten, and it is the only one no court on earth could ever hear a case about.

## What it forbids

*Do not covet your neighbor's house. Do not **set your heart** on your neighbor's spouse, or
servant, or ox, or donkey, or anything that belongs to them.*

The Hebrew is **chamad** — to desire, to take pleasure in, to want.

## Why it is different from the other nine

Every other commandment describes an act. Murder, adultery, theft, false testimony — each one
leaves evidence, has a witness, and can be brought before elders at the gate.

This one happens entirely inside a person, and nothing shows.

You can keep the other nine your whole life in front of witnesses. There is no way to keep
this one in front of anybody, because there is nobody to keep it in front of.

## The man it stopped

*I would not have known sin except through the law. For **I would not have known covetousness
unless the law had said, "You shall not covet."***

Paul picks this commandment out of the ten by name. Of everything on the tablets, the tenth is
the one he says found him out.

And then what it did: *but sin, **taking opportunity by the commandment**, produced in me all
manner of evil desire. For apart from the law sin was dead. **I was alive once without the
law, but when the commandment came, sin revived and I died**.*

The law did not create the wanting. It named it, and the naming woke it up.

## What he concludes about the law itself

*Therefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good.*

He does not blame it. He says it is good, and that it killed him — and both are in the same
paragraph.

*For sin, taking occasion by the commandment, deceived me, and by it killed me.*

## What the Illumination adds

*Be at rest with what you have; let longing for another's life not gnaw at your peace.*

That is a description of the cure rather than a restatement of the command, and it points at
where the New Testament puts contentment.

## Where contentment is grounded

*Let your conduct be without covetousness; **be content with such things as you have**. For
**He Himself has said**, "I will never leave you nor forsake you."*

Notice the reasoning. Not *be content because wanting is wrong*. Be content **because He has
said** — the contentment is hung on a promise about His presence, not on an effort to stop
wanting.

*Not that I speak in regard to need, for **I have learned** in whatever state I am, to be
content… **I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me**.*

## Where the ten were kept

Broken at the foot of the mountain, and then cut again — and the second set went into a box
with a blood-sprinkled lid over them. See the note on Exodus 32:15–20.

The commandment that found Paul out is inside the ark, under the mercy seat, with the blood on
top of it.

> **The one that cannot be enforced.** Nine of the ten leave evidence. This one happens where
> nobody can see, which is exactly why Paul says it was the one that found him.
