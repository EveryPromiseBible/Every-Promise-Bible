The answer arrives while the request is still being made.

## The prayer

*"O LORD God of my master Abraham, **please give me success this day**… Let it be that the young
woman to whom I say, 'Please let down your pitcher that I may drink,' and she says, 'Drink, and I
will also give your camels a drink' — **let her be the one You have appointed**."* See the note on
Genesis 24:12–14.

The Hebrew for *give me success* is **haqreh-na** — cause it to happen before me.

**Qarah** — to meet, to encounter, to happen. The word for chance.

He asks the God who arranges things for something that will look like coincidence.

## The timing

*Before he had even **finished praying**, there was Rebekah.*

The Hebrew is **terem killah ledabber** — before he had finished speaking.

She was already walking to the well while he was still forming the request.

## The camels

Ten of them, and they have crossed five hundred miles of desert.

A thirsty camel drinks twenty to thirty gallons. The sign the servant chose requires somebody to
draw perhaps two hundred gallons by hand, up out of a well, one jar at a time.

He picked a sign that costs the woman hours.

## Her arrival

*Coming out with **her jar on her shoulder**.*

## Her family

*She was the daughter of Bethuel son of Milcah, the wife of **Abraham's brother Nahor**.*

The narrator gives the genealogy before the servant knows any of it.

She is inside the family, which is the entire point of the errand — *you shall go to my country
and to my family*.

## The description

*The girl was **very beautiful**, a virgin; no man had known her.*

The Hebrew is **tovat mareh me'od** — good of appearance, exceedingly.

**Me'od** — very, exceedingly. The word used in Genesis 1: *and God saw everything that He had
made, and indeed it was **very** good*.

## The routine

*She went down to the spring, filled her jar, and came back up.*

Three verbs. The most ordinary sentence in the chapter.

She is doing what she does every evening. Nothing about her day has changed.

## What the servant does not know yet

That she is Nahor's granddaughter. That she will say the words. That she will go.

He is standing by a well in a foreign country with ten camels and a prayer half-finished.

## What she does

*"Drink, my lord," she said, and quickly lowered the jar into her hands and gave him a drink.*

*"I'll draw water for your camels too, **until they've had enough**."* See the note on
Genesis 24:17–20.

She offers the second half without being asked, which was the whole sign.

## What he does

*The man **watched her in silence**, wondering whether the LORD had made his journey succeed.* See
the note on Genesis 24:21–23.

He says nothing while she works.

## What Isaiah says about the timing

*It shall come to pass that **before they call, I will answer; and while they are still speaking, I
will hear**.* See the note on Isaiah 65:24–25.

The construction of this scene, stated as a promise.

## What he says at the end

*"**As for me, the LORD has led me on the very road** to the house of my master's relatives."* See
the note on Genesis 24:26–27.

Past tense, spoken at the well before anything is settled.

## Who moved first

She was on the path with the jar on her shoulder before the request was finished. Whatever
brought her out that evening, it was not the prayer — the prayer had not landed yet.

Jesus said it to men who had left their nets for Him: *you did not choose Me, but **I chose
you**.*

The bride in this chapter is sought, found, asked and brought home, and every move in the
sequence is made by somebody else first. She is drawing water at the usual hour on an
ordinary evening. See the note on Genesis 24:1–4.
