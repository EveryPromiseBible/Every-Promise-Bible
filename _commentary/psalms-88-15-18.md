The one psalm that does not turn.

## What is unusual about it

Every other lament in the Psalter breaks somewhere — *but I will trust*, *yet I will praise Him*,
*hope in God*.

This one does not. It ends where it has been the whole way.

## Who wrote it

*A Contemplation of **Heman the Ezrahite**.*

Named in Kings among the wise men Solomon surpassed. See the note on
1 Kings 4:26–34.

A temple singer, and this is his only psalm.

## How it opens

*O LORD, God of my salvation, I have cried out day and night before You.*

The one positive line, and it is in the first verse.

## The duration

*From my youth I have been **afflicted, near death**.*

The Hebrew is **ani ani vegove minno'ar** — I am afflicted and expiring from youth.

Not a crisis. A life.

## The load

*I have **carried Your terrors**, and **I am worn down**.*

The Hebrew is **nasati emecha aphunah** — I bore Your terrors, I am distracted.

**Emim** — terrors, dread. And the possessive: *Your* terrors.

He attributes them to God and says so to His face.

## The anger

*Your **burning anger has swept over me**. Your dread **has cut me apart**.*

The Hebrew is **charoneicha** — Your burnings.

**Tzimmetutuni** — have cut me off, silenced me.

## The image

*They surround me all day **like a flood**. Together **they close in on me**.*

The Hebrew is **sabbuni kammayim** — they surround me like waters.

## The last verse

*You have **taken lover and friend far from me**. **The dark is the only companion I have left**.*

The Hebrew is **meyudda'ai machshach** — my acquaintances are darkness.

The last word of the psalm is **machshach** — darkness.

## What that means for the collection

The Psalter contains a psalm that does not resolve, and the editors left it in.

Whatever else the book is doing, it is not editing out the voices that never came through.

## Why that matters

Somebody praying it is not praying badly.

The prayer book of Israel makes room for a man who has been in the dark since he was young and can
report nothing else.

## What comes immediately after it

*I will sing of the mercies of the LORD forever; with my mouth **I will make known Your
faithfulness** to all generations.*

Psalm 89, by Ethan the Ezrahite — Heman's colleague.

The editors put the two side by side.

## Where Psalm 89 ends up

*But You have cast off and abhorred… **how long, LORD? Will You hide Yourself forever?***

*Remember how short my time is.*

The psalm that opens with mercies forever ends asking how long.

## What follows that

Psalm 90 — *Lord, You have been our dwelling place in all generations*, the prayer of Moses, and
the start of Book Four. See the note on Psalms 90:13–17.

## Where the darkness turns up

*Now from the sixth hour until the ninth hour **there was darkness over all the land**.*

And *My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?* — which is the opening line of another psalm.

## The only psalm that ends in the dark

Eighty-eight has no turn. Every other lament in the Psalter reaches a *but* — *but I will trust
in Your mercy*, *but You, O LORD, are a shield for me*. This one does not.

*Loved one and friend You have put far from me, and **my acquaintances into darkness**.*

That is the last line. The psalm stops there.

## Why it is in the Bible

Because it is true, and because somebody needed it to be there. Scripture includes a prayer
with no resolution in it and does not apologise for it.

## Who prayed it

*And about the ninth hour Jesus cried out with a loud voice, saying, "**Eli, Eli, lama
sabachthani?**" — that is, "**My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?**"*

The one place in Scripture where the darkness was not answered from inside it.

*Now from the sixth hour until the ninth hour **there was darkness over all the land**.*

*For **He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us**.*

## What that does to the psalm

Nobody praying Psalm 88 is praying it alone, and nobody praying it is outside the range of
somebody who has been further in.

*For we do not have a High Priest who cannot sympathise with our weaknesses.*

> **What the Psalter keeps.** A prayer that does not turn, ending on the word *darkness*, left in
> the book by editors who could have cut it.
