Seven proverbs, and the last one makes a claim about the body.

## The crown

*The crown of the wise is their riches. **The folly of fools is only more folly**.*

The Hebrew for the second line is **ivvelet kesilim ivvelet** — the folly of fools is folly.

The same word twice. Wisdom compounds into something; folly compounds into more of itself.

## The witness

*A **truthful witness saves lives**. A liar **breathes out betrayal**.*

The Hebrew is **matzil nephashot** — a deliverer of souls.

Court testimony was often the only evidence, and a false witness in a capital case was a
murderer. The ninth commandment sits behind it.

## The confidence

*In **the fear of the LORD is strong confidence**, and **a refuge for His children**.*

The Hebrew is **mivtach-oz** — strong trust. And **machseh** — a shelter, the noun from the verb
in Psalm 118. See the note on Psalms 118:8–9.

Fear producing confidence is the paradox the book is built on.

## The spring

*The fear of the LORD is **a spring of life**, turning your feet from **the snares of death**.*

The Hebrew is **meqor chayyim** — a fountain of life.

The same phrase used of a man's mouth, of understanding, and of the teaching of the wise.

## The king

*A crowd of people is a king's glory. **Without them a ruler comes to ruin**.*

The Hebrew is **be'ephes le'om mechittat razon** — in the absence of people is the ruin of a
prince.

A king without subjects is not a king. The proverb is aimed at rulers who forget where their
standing comes from.

## The temper

*Slow to anger, and **rich in understanding**. Quick to temper, and **quick to look the fool**.*

The Hebrew is **erek appayim** — long of nostrils. The idiom for patience, used of God at Sinai:
*the LORD, the LORD God, merciful and gracious, **longsuffering***.

**Qetzar-ruach** — short of spirit. The opposite idiom.

## The last one

*A **tranquil heart gives life to the body**. **Envy is rot in the bones**.*

The Hebrew is **lev marpe chayyei besarim** — a heart of healing is life to the flesh.

**Marpe** — healing, cure. The word in *kind words are… healing to the bones*. See the note on
Proverbs 16:24–33.

## The bones

**Uraqav atzamot qin'ah** — and rottenness of bones is envy.

**Raqav** — decay, caries. The word for wood rot and for what happens to a corpse.

## What the proverb claims

Two interior states with physical consequences, stated flatly.

A settled heart keeps the flesh alive. Envy decays the skeleton.

The Hebrew does not hedge either half.

## Why bones

The deepest part of the body in Hebrew idiom, and where grief is felt: *my bones waste away*, *my
bones are troubled*.

Envy reaches the same depth as sorrow, and the proverb puts it there.

## What Job says

*"Oh, if only the old days would come back… **when the friendship of God rested warm over my
tent**."* See the note on Job 29:1–6.

## Where the chapter goes next

*He who oppresses the poor reproaches his Maker, but he who honours Him has mercy on the needy.*

*Righteousness exalts a nation, but sin is a reproach to any people.* See the note on
Proverbs 14:31–35.

> **The last proverb.** A settled heart is *life to the flesh*; envy is *rot in the bones*. Two
> interior states named as having physical outcomes, without qualification.
