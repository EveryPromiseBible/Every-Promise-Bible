They stop halfway through the looting because of something they say to each other.

## What they were doing

Four lepers had walked to the Aramean camp expecting to be killed and found it abandoned. See the
note on 2 Kings 7:3–4.

*They went into one tent and ate and drank, and carried from it silver and gold and clothing, and
went and hid them.* And then a second tent, and hid that too.

Nobody would blame them. They had been starving outside a gate that would not let them in.

## The interruption

*Then they said to each other, "**What we're doing is not right**."*

The Hebrew is **lo-chen anachnu osim** — not right, not so, are we doing.

No prophet appears. No message comes. Four men stop in the middle of a second tent and say it to
one another.

## The reason

*"**This is a day of good news, and we're keeping it to ourselves**."*

The Hebrew is **yom besorah hu** — a day of good news.

**Besorah** is the noun the Septuagint renders **euangelia** — the word behind *gospel*.

## Who is behind them

A city eating its own children, whose gate they are not permitted through.

The people who had excluded them are the people they go back for.

## What they are afraid of

*"If we wait until daylight, **punishment will overtake us**."*

The Hebrew is **avon** — iniquity, and the guilt that follows it.

Not fear of being caught with the silver. The word is about culpability.

## The decision

*"**Let's go at once and report this to the royal palace**."*

They go in the dark, which means they abandon the rest of the camp to anyone else who might
wander out.

## What they had to leave

Tents full of silver, gold, horses and clothing, unguarded, in order to reach a gatekeeper
before morning.

## What the gatekeeper does

*So they went and called to the gatekeepers of the city, and told them… **and they called out to
the gatekeepers, and they told it to the king's household inside***.

The message goes up the chain from men who could not enter the city, through the gate, to the
palace.

## The king's response

He does not believe it. *I will now tell you what the Arameans have done to us. They know that we
are hungry; therefore they have gone out of the camp to hide in the field.*

An ambush, he says. It takes a servant proposing to send five horses to check.

## What was true

*And indeed all the road was full of garments and weapons which the Arameans had thrown away in
their haste.*

The evidence had been lying on the road the whole time.

## What happened at the gate

*So the people went out and plundered the tents of the Arameans. **Then a seah of fine flour was
sold for a shekel, and two seahs of barley for a shekel, according to the word of the LORD**.*

Exactly what Elisha had said the day before.

## Who delivered it

Four men with a disease that put them outside the wall, who found the food, ate first, buried
treasure, and then stopped and said *this is not right*.

The narrative does not clean up the sequence.

## What Isaiah does with the same word

*How beautiful upon the mountains are **the feet of him who brings good news**, who proclaims
peace.* See the note on Isaiah 52:7–10.

The same root, **mevasser**, and the same picture: someone running toward a city with news of a
siege lifted.

> **What stops them.** Not a prophet and not a command. Four men in a second tent, saying it to
> each other: *this is a day of good news, and we are keeping it to ourselves.*
