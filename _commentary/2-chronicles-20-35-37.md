The last thing recorded about a good king, and it is a fleet at the bottom of a harbour.

## What had just happened

The chapter before this is Jehoshaphat's finest hour.

Three armies coming at him, and his prayer: *we have no power against this great multitude,
nor do we know what to do, but **our eyes are upon You***.

The answer: *you will not need to fight in this battle. Position yourselves, stand still, and
see the salvation of the LORD.*

He put the singers in front of the army, and the enemy destroyed each other. It took three
days to collect the plunder.

## The word that follows it

*Later, Jehoshaphat king of Judah **made an alliance with Ahaziah king of Israel, who acted
wickedly**.*

The Hebrew is **chabar** — to join, to couple, to be tied to.

Ahaziah is Ahab's son. Two years on the throne, and the verdict on him in Kings is *he served
Baal and worshipped him, and provoked the LORD God of Israel to anger*.

## What they built

*He joined with him to **build ships to sail to Tarshish**, and they built the ships at
**Ezion-geber**.*

Ezion-geber is on the Gulf of Aqaba, at the top of the Red Sea. Solomon's port.

Tarshish ships were the largest class of merchant vessel — the ocean-going kind. Solomon's
had brought back gold, silver, ivory, apes and peacocks.

This is a trading venture, not a war.

## The prophecy

*Then **Eliezer son of Dodavahu of Mareshah** prophesied against Jehoshaphat, saying,
"**Because you have made an alliance with Ahaziah, the LORD has broken apart what you have
made**."*

The Hebrew is **paratz** — to break through, burst, breach.

The prophet is named once in Scripture and this is the sentence.

## The outcome

*And **the ships were wrecked and could not sail to Tarshish**.*

Never left port.

## The reason given

Not the trade. Not the destination. The partner.

The prophet's sentence names one cause: *because you have joined yourself with Ahaziah*.

## The pattern in his reign

This is the third time.

He married his son to Ahab's daughter, and then went to Ramoth Gilead with Ahab — *I am as
you are, and my people as your people* — and nearly died in the chariot when the Syrians
mistook him for the king. See the note on 2 Chronicles 18:28–34.

The prophet Jehu met him coming home: *should you help the wicked and love those who hate
the LORD? Therefore the wrath of the LORD is upon you.*

And then he did it again.

## What the alliance cost his house

Athaliah, Ahab's daughter, married into the family here. When her son died she murdered
every royal heir she could find and ruled for six years.

The line of David survived because one baby was hidden in a bedroom in the temple.

## What Kings says

The parallel account has Ahaziah asking to join *after* the wreck — *let my servants go with
your servants in the ships* — and Jehoshaphat refusing.

Two accounts of a partnership that ended badly, and they disagree about the order of events.

## Where else the word turns up

*Ephraim is **joined to idols**; let him alone.* Hosea, and the verb is **chabur**, the same
root.

And Paul: *do not be unequally yoked together with unbelievers. For what fellowship has
righteousness with lawlessness?*

## The summary of his life

*And Jehoshaphat rested with his fathers… **the rest of the acts of Jehoshaphat, first and
last, indeed they are written** in the book of Jehu.*

The verdict elsewhere is good: *his heart took delight in the ways of the LORD*, and he sent
teachers round the towns with the book of the law.

The chronicler records all of it, in order, and ends the reign with the ships.

> **What the prophet named as the cause.** Not the venture. The partner — and the sentence
> is one line long: *because you have joined yourself with Ahaziah.*
