Two words turn the letter around.

## Where they come

After three chapters of indictment, six quoted verses about throats and tongues and
mouths, and a courtroom where every mouth is stopped and the whole world stands
accountable. See the note on Romans 3:19–20.

Then: *but now*.

## The two words

The Greek is **nuni de** — but now, at this present moment.

It is a change of era as much as a change of subject. Paul uses the same phrase at the
turns of his arguments elsewhere.

## What is apart from what

*Apart from **the law altogether**.*

The Greek is **choris nomou** — separately from law, without reference to it.

Not law softened, satisfied by degrees, or graded on a curve. Set aside as the mechanism
entirely.

## What has appeared

*A **rightness from God** has been **brought out into the open**.*

The Greek is **dikaiosune theou** — the righteousness of God. It is the theme Paul
announced in chapter 1: *in it the righteousness of God is revealed from believing to
believing.*

**Brought out into the open** — the verb is perfect passive, made manifest and standing
manifest.

## The witnesses

*And **the law and the prophets have been testifying to it all along**.*

Having said *apart from the law*, Paul immediately says the law testifies to it.

The law cannot produce it and has been pointing at it the whole time. He proves the
point in the next chapter with Abraham and David, both from the Old Testament. See the
note on Romans 4:6–8.

## The channel

*It is a rightness from God that comes **through believing in Jesus Christ**.*

Not through effort, descent, or ritual.

## The two prepositions

*And it comes **upon all** and **to all** who believe.*

Paul says it twice with different prepositions, which is not an accident of translation —
the Greek has both **eis** and **epi**.

## The levelling

*There is **no distinction between one person and another**.*

The Greek is **ou gar estin diastole** — there is no difference.

He says it here about who can receive it, and he has just said it in verse 22–23 about
who needs it: *for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.*

The same phrase does both jobs. No difference in the need, no difference in the offer.

## What comes next

*Being **justified freely by His grace** through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.*
See the note on Romans 3:23–24.

**Freely** is **dorean** — as a gift, gratuitously, for nothing. It is the word used of
being hated *without a cause*.

## Where the chapter ends

*Where is boasting then? **It is excluded**. By what law? Of works? No, but **by the law
of believing**.*

> **What has changed at "but now."** Not the verdict on anybody — Paul restates it in the
> next verse. What has appeared is a righteousness that was never on offer through the
> mechanism he has just closed.
