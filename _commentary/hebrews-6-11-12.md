The remedy for going soft is not effort. It is imitation.

## The desire

*What we want is for **every one of you** to keep that same eagerness **right through to the end**.*

The Greek is **hekaston humōn** — each one of you. And **achri telous** — until the end.

**Spoudē** — earnestness, haste, diligence.

## Where the eagerness came from

The verse before: *God is not unjust to forget your work and **the love which you have shown toward
His name**, in that you have ministered to the saints, and do minister*. See the note on
Hebrews 6:9–10.

They had already done it. The instruction is to keep going at the pace they set.

## The goal

*Until **what you hope for is a settled certainty**.*

The Greek is **pros tēn plērophorian tēs elpidos** — to the full assurance of hope.

**Plērophoria** — from **plērēs**, full, and **pherō**, to carry. Being carried to the brim.

## The word

The letter uses it three times: full assurance of hope here, and *let us draw near with a true heart
in **full assurance of faith***. See the note on Hebrews 10:22.

## The warning

*We do not want you **going soft**.*

The Greek is **nōthroi** — sluggish, dull, slow-moving.

## Where the word appeared before

*Seeing you have become **dull of hearing**.* See the note on Hebrews 5:12–14.

Same adjective. They went dull in the ears first; the danger now is going dull all over.

## The instruction

*Copy the people who **trusted God, held on, and came into what He promised**.*

The Greek is **mimētai** — imitators. The root of *mimic*.

## The three things

**Pisteōs kai makrothumias** — faith and long-suffering. And **klēronomountōn** — inheriting.

**Makrothumia** — long-temperedness. Not endurance under pressure, which is **hupomonē**, but the
refusal to snap at delay.

## What they are told to copy

Not principles. People.

The whole eleventh chapter is the roll of them: *by faith Abel… by faith Noah… by faith Abraham*.
See the note on Hebrews 11:1–3.

## The first example given

*For when God made a promise to Abraham… **"Surely blessing I will bless you"**… and so, **after he
had patiently endured**, he obtained the promise.* See the note on Hebrews 6:13–14.

The Greek is **makrothumēsas** — the same word.

Twenty-five years between the promise and Isaac.

## The two immovable things

*That by **two immutable things**, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we might have strong
consolation.* See the note on Hebrews 6:18.

A promise, and an oath sworn on Himself because there was nothing higher to swear by.

## Where it lands

*That hope is **our anchor**. It holds; **it does not slip**.* See the note on Hebrews 6:19–20.

## What comes just before this paragraph

The severest warning in the letter — *it is impossible… if they fall away, to renew them again to
repentance*. See the note on Hebrews 6:4–6.

And then, immediately: *but, beloved, **we are confident of better things concerning you***.

The writer sets the warning down and picks the readers up in the next sentence.

> **The remedy.** Not *try harder* — *copy the people who held on*. The letter answers sluggishness
> by pointing at a list of names.
