Paul, thirty years in, states what he is still after.

## What comes immediately before

*Not having my own righteousness, which is from the law, but that which is through faith in
Christ, **the righteousness which is from God by faith**.* See the note on Philippians 3:9.

The verse under discussion is the continuation of that sentence. There is no full stop
between them.

## The aim

*That **I may know Him**.*

The Greek is **tou gnonai auton** — an aorist infinitive. To come to know.

**Ginosko**, the knowing that comes by acquaintance, not the knowing of facts about.

Written by the man who had been knocked off a horse by Him on a road, planted churches across
three provinces, and written most of the New Testament.

## The list

Four things, in an order that is not the obvious one:

*Him.*

*The **power of His rising**.*

*The **sharing of His sufferings**.*

*Being **shaped to the very likeness of His death**.*

Resurrection first, then suffering, then death. Backwards from the way it happened.

## The power

The Greek is **dunamin tes anastaseos** — the power of His resurrection.

Paul has already prayed the same thing for the Ephesians: *the exceeding greatness of His
power toward us who believe, according to the working of His mighty power which He worked in
Christ when He raised Him from the dead*. See the note on Ephesians 1:20–23.

The measure of the power is the empty tomb.

## The sharing

The Greek is **koinonian pathematon autou** — fellowship of His sufferings.

**Koinonia** is the word for partnership, having in common. Paul uses it of the collection,
of the Lord's table, and of the church at Philippi's support of him.

Here it is of suffering, and he lists it as something to be gained.

## The conforming

The Greek is **summorphizomenos** — being made of the same form, present passive.

**Morphe** is the inner form, the essential shape — the same root as *He being in the form of
God*, two chapters earlier.

Not imitation. Being made the same shape, by somebody else, continuously.

## The clause

*If somehow, by whatever way, **I may arrive at the resurrection from among the dead**.*

The Greek is **ei pos katanteso** — if somehow I may attain.

It reads like doubt and is not. Paul has already said *our citizenship is in heaven, from
which we also eagerly wait for the Saviour*. See the note on Philippians 3:20–21.

The uncertainty is about the route, not the destination — the *how*, not the *whether*. He
did not know at the time of writing whether he would be executed or released.

## What he says next

*Not that I have already attained, or am already perfected; but I **press on**, that I may
lay hold of that for which Christ Jesus has also laid hold of me.*

The Greek is **dioko** — pursue, chase, and it is the word used of persecuting. The verb Paul
used to describe his own former career.

## The image

*Forgetting those things which are behind and **reaching forward** to those things which are
ahead, **I press toward the goal for the prize** of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus.*

The Greek is **epekteinomenos** — stretching out toward, the runner's body leaning past the
feet.

## What he had just discarded

Circumcised the eighth day, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of the Hebrews, a Pharisee,
blameless concerning the law.

*But what things were gain to me, these I have counted loss for Christ… I count them as
rubbish.*

The Greek is **skubala** — refuse, what is thrown out.

## The order in the passage

Righteousness received in verse 9. Knowing Him pursued in verse 10.

The pursuit comes after the possession, not before it, and the whole sentence is one
grammatical unit.

> **The order of the four.** Him, the resurrection power, the fellowship of sufferings, and
> then being shaped to His death. Paul lists them in reverse of the way they happened.
