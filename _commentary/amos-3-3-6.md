Seven rhetorical questions in a row, and they are all making one point.

## Who is asking

Amos, a herdsman from Tekoa and a tender of sycamore figs, sent north to Israel in a
prosperous decade to say the prosperity is over.

*I was no prophet, nor was I a son of a prophet, but I was a sheepbreeder and a tender of
sycamore fruit. **Then the LORD took me as I followed the flock**.*

## The form

Every one is a question with an obvious answer, and every answer is no.

The technique is cumulative. By the seventh, the reader has said no six times and is
committed.

## The first

*Can two people **walk together unless they have agreed to meet**?*

The Hebrew is *unless they be agreed* — **no'adu**, from the root for an appointed meeting.

Two people on the same road at the same time did not get there by chance. Something was
arranged.

## The lion

*Does a lion **roar in the thicket when he has caught nothing**?*

*Does a young lion **growl in his den unless he has seized his prey**?*

Two different verbs and two different stages. A lion roars when the kill is made, not while
stalking — and the second one growls over the carcass at home.

The roar is evidence that something has already happened.

## The bird

*Does a bird **fall into a trap on the ground where no snare was set**?*

*Does a trap **spring up from the earth when it has caught nothing** at all?*

The trap does not go off by itself. Something is in it.

## The trumpet

*When **the ram's horn sounds the alarm in a city, do the people not tremble**?*

The **shofar** on the wall meant an army was visible.

Nobody in a walled town heard that sound and stayed calm.

## The last one

*When **trouble comes to a city, has the LORD not sent it**?*

The Hebrew is *shall there be calamity in a city, and the LORD has not done it?*

The hardest sentence in the passage, and it is the one the other six were built to deliver.

## What the chain is arguing

Cause and effect. Nothing in the list happens without a reason behind it.

And then the conclusion, in the next verse:

*Surely the Lord GOD does nothing, unless **He reveals His secret to His servants the
prophets**.*

## What the whole thing is for

Amos is defending his right to speak.

*A lion has roared! Who will not fear? The Lord GOD has spoken! **Who can but prophesy?***

He is not choosing to prophesy. The roar has already happened and he is the sound of it
arriving.

## What he was sent to say

*"You only have I known of all the families of the earth; **therefore I will punish you for
all your iniquities**."*

Privilege as the ground of accountability, not exemption.

And the famous lines: *let justice run down like water, and righteousness like a mighty
stream*, and *I hate, I despise your feast days*.

## What the book ends with

*"Behold, the days are coming," says the LORD, "**when the plowman shall overtake the
reaper**… I will bring back the captives of My people Israel; they shall build the waste
cities and inhabit them."*

Harvest so heavy that the ploughman for the next season catches up with the men still
bringing in this one.

James quotes the same closing passage at the Jerusalem council to settle the question of
Gentile believers.

> **What the seven questions establish.** That nothing in the list is accidental — so that
> when the seventh one lands, the reader has already agreed to the logic six times.
