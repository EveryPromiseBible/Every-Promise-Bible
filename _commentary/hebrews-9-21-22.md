Two verses summarising an entire system, and the qualifier in the middle is deliberate.

## What comes before

Moses at Sinai, after reading the book of the covenant aloud: *he took the blood of calves and
goats, with water, scarlet wool, and hyssop, and **sprinkled both the book itself and all the
people**, saying, "This is the blood of the covenant which God has commanded you."*

The book was sprinkled. The document itself.

## The tent

*He sprinkled blood on **the tent as well, and on everything used in the worship**.*

Exodus records the anointing oil on the tabernacle and its furniture; Josephus and the rabbinic
tradition preserve the blood.

Everything in the building — the table, the lampstand, the altar, the basins, the shovels.

## The rule

*Under the law **nearly everything is cleansed with blood**.*

The Greek is **schedon** — nearly, almost.

The qualifier is exact. There were exceptions: a very poor man could bring flour for a sin
offering — *a tenth of an ephah of fine flour… he shall put no oil on it, nor shall he put
frankincense on it, for it is a sin offering*.

And some purifications used water or fire.

The writer knows the code and does not overstate it.

## The principle

*And **where no blood is poured out, there is no forgiveness**.*

The Greek is **haimatekchusias** — a blood-pouring, and the word occurs once in the New
Testament. The writer may have coined it.

**Aphesis** — release, dismissal, remission. The word for cancelling a debt or freeing a
prisoner.

## Where the rule comes from

Leviticus states it: *for the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it to you upon
the altar to make atonement for your souls; **for it is the blood that makes atonement for the
soul**.* See the note on Leviticus 17:10–12.

That is the reason blood was reserved and never eaten.

## What the argument is for

Not to establish that blood is required — the readers knew that.

To establish what follows: *therefore it was necessary that the copies of the things in the
heavens should be purified with these, but **the heavenly things themselves with better
sacrifices than these**.*

## What comes next

*For Christ has not entered the holy places made with hands, which are copies of the true, but
**into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us**.*

*Not that He should offer Himself often… **He then would have had to suffer often** since the
foundation of the world; but now, once at the end of the ages, He has appeared to put away sin
by the sacrifice of Himself.*

The argument turns on frequency. If the offering needed repeating, so would the dying.

## The three appearings

*He has appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself.*

*Now to appear in the presence of God for us.*

*To those who eagerly wait for Him He will appear a second time, apart from sin, for
salvation.*

Past, present and future, in consecutive sentences.

## What He brought

*Not the blood of goats and calves. **He brought His own**.* See the note on
Hebrews 9:11–12.

Which makes the priest and the sacrifice the same person.

## The other side of the rule

*In whom we have redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of sins.*

And *the blood of Jesus Christ His Son washes us clean of every sin*. See the note on
1 John 1:7.

> **The word that keeps it honest.** *Nearly.* The writer states the rule about blood and adds
> a qualifier — because he knows the exceptions and is not willing to overstate the case.
