The fourth oracle, and it is the best writing in the book of Numbers.

## Who is speaking

*"The word of Balaam son of Beor, the word of **the man whose eye is opened**."*

A hired diviner, paid to curse, on his fourth failed attempt. See the note on
Numbers 24:1–2.

## The three descriptions

*"The word of one who **hears the words of God**."*

*"Who **sees the vision of the Almighty**."*

*"**Falling down, but with his eyes uncovered**."*

The Hebrew for *Almighty* is **Shaddai** — the patriarchs' name for God. See the note on
Genesis 17:1–2.

## The opening line

*"**How lovely are your tents, Jacob**, your dwellings, Israel!"*

The Hebrew is **mah tovu** — how good.

It opens the Jewish morning service and has done for centuries. The first words a
worshipper says on entering the synagogue are a pagan's.

## What he is looking at

A refugee camp in a desert. Two million people in tents, forty years out of Egypt, with
nothing but livestock and a portable tent in the middle.

He calls it lovely.

## The four images

*"Like **valleys spreading out**."*

*"Like **gardens beside a river**."*

*"Like **aloes the LORD has planted**."*

*"Like **cedars beside the waters**."*

Every one is about water, and he is standing in a wilderness.

**Aloes the LORD has planted** — the Hebrew singles them out as God's own planting. The
same idea as Jeremiah's transplanted tree. See the note on Jeremiah 17:7–8.

## The water

*"**Water will flow from their buckets**, and their seed will have **abundant water**."*

Said over people who had been complaining about thirst for four decades.

## The king

*"**Their king will be higher than Agag**, and their kingdom will be lifted up."*

Israel has no king. Saul is centuries away, and the Agag he fails to kill is the reason
Samuel rejects him.

## The wild ox

*"God brought them out of Egypt; they have **the strength of a wild ox**."*

The Hebrew is **reem** — the untameable one. Balaam used the same image in the third
oracle. See the note on Numbers 23:21–24.

## The lion

*"Like a lion they crouch and lie down; like a lioness — **who dares to rouse them?**"*

Almost word for word Jacob's blessing over Judah in Genesis 49: *he bows down, he lies down
as a lion; who shall rouse him?*

## The last line

*"**Blessed is everyone who blesses you, and cursed is everyone who curses you**."*

The promise to Abraham, quoted back — *I will bless those who bless you, and I will curse
him who curses you*.

Balak had hired him precisely to curse them, and the oracle ends by cursing whoever does.

## Balak's reaction

*Then Balak's anger was aroused against Balaam, and he **struck his hands together**… "I
called you to curse my enemies, and look, **you have kept on blessing them these three
times!** Now therefore, **flee to your place**."*

He sends him home unpaid: *I said I would greatly honour you, but in fact the LORD has kept
you back from honour.*

## What Balaam says next

Before leaving, unpaid and dismissed, he delivers a fifth oracle anyway:

*"**I see Him, but not now; I behold Him, but not near. A Star shall come out of Jacob; a
Scepter shall rise out of Israel**."*

> **Where the words are still said.** *How lovely are your tents.* A man on a hill,
> employed to curse, describing a desert camp as gardens beside a river — and it is the
> sentence the synagogue opens with.
