Three verses of self-description, and every clause is about a divided man.

## The rule

*"**Whenever I mean to do good, evil is standing right there beside me.**"*

**Paremoi** — lies close at hand, is present with me. Not *in the past*; alongside, now.

## The delight

*"**In my inmost self I delight in the law of God.**"*

**Synedomai** — I rejoice together with. He is not describing somebody who resents the commandment.
The whole difficulty is that he agrees with it.

*Therefore **the law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good**.*

## The other law

*"**But I see a different law at work in the parts of my body, waging war against the law of my
mind.**"*

**Antistrateuomenon** — campaigning against. A military word, and the verse has four uses of
**nomos** in different senses: God's law, the law of the mind, another law in the members, and the
law of sin.

## What he does not do

Resolve it by effort.

*"**O wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death? I thank God — through
Jesus Christ our Lord!**"*

The question is **who**. He does not ask what technique will work, and the answer is a person and a
thanksgiving.

*For **what the law could not do in that it was weak through the flesh, God did by sending His own
Son**.*

*There is therefore now **no condemnation** to those who are in **Christ Jesus**.*

## Which Paul is this

Readers have argued for centuries whether the chapter describes **Paul** before conversion or after.
The tenses are present throughout, and the delight in God's law in the inner man is hard to read of
an unconverted Pharisee.

Either way, the chapter ends where the argument is settled, and it is not settled inside the man.

> **The verb.** *Paremoi* — evil lies close at hand. Present tense, alongside, and the escape when
> it comes is not a method. It is a name, and Paul thanks God for it before finishing the sentence.
