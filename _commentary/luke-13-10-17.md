Eighteen years bent double, and the argument afterwards is about whether the
timing was appropriate.

## She did not ask

*When Jesus saw her, He called her over.*

Every detail is His initiative. He sees her, calls her, speaks, and lays hands on
her. Nothing in the account records her asking for anything — she had come to the
synagogue, which is where she presumably went every week.

## What He says

*Woman, you are set free from what has been crippling you.*

The verb is perfect passive — you **have been** released, and you stand released.
He announces it as done before He touches her.

## The two words for tying

The whole argument turns on one image, and Jesus builds it deliberately:

- *Is there one of you who does not **untie** his ox or his donkey from the stall
  on the Sabbath and lead it out to water?*
- *And this woman… whom Satan has kept **tied up** for eighteen years — should she
  not be **untied** on the Sabbath day?*

Same verb, three times. If a knot can be undone for an animal that has been tied
overnight, what about a woman tied for eighteen years?

## The country He was in

Jesus based Himself in Capernaum, *in the regions of Zebulun and **Naphtali***, and
most of His work was done along that shore.

Jacob's blessing over Naphtali is one line: *Naphtali is **a doe set free**.*

A doe let loose, and a woman untied after eighteen years, in the same territory.
See the note on Genesis 49:21.

## What He calls her

*A daughter of Abraham.* Not a phrase used elsewhere in the Gospels.

He gives her the standing of an heir of the covenant, in a synagogue, in front of
the man complaining about her — and He does it in the same sentence that names
what had been holding her.

## The two reactions

*Everyone who had been opposing Him was put to shame, and the whole crowd was
overjoyed.*

Luke reports both, and the contrast is the point of the paragraph: the same event
humiliated the officials and delighted everybody else.

> **What she did first.** *Right away she stood up straight, and she began to
> praise God.* Luke gives the standing and the praise in one clause. After
> eighteen years of looking at the ground, the first thing recorded is her voice.
