Jeremiah is forbidden three ordinary human things, and each prohibition is a
sermon he has to live rather than preach.

## The instruction

*"**Do not enter a house of mourning.** Do not go to grieve or to comfort them."*

In that culture, staying away from a funeral was unthinkable. Mourning with the
bereaved was a duty everybody owed everybody.

He is told not to go, and his absence at every graveside in Jerusalem is the
message.

## The other two

The chapter forbids him three things in a row: do not marry or have children here,
do not go to a funeral, do not go to a feast.

No wife, no mourning, no celebration. He is cut off from the beginning, the end and
the middle of ordinary life, and the isolation is deliberate — it is what the
nation is about to experience.

## What is being withdrawn

*"**I have taken My peace away from this people**, says the LORD, My steadfast
love, My mercy."*

Three nouns: *shalom*, **chesed** — the covenant love — and mercy.

It is the same season Hosea named *Lo-Ammi*, not My people. See the note on
Hosea 1:8–9.

## The cup of consolation

*"No one will **lift the cup of consolation** for a father or a mother."*

There was a customary cup handed to mourners after a burial. The chapter names it
and says it will not be offered.

Nobody will break bread with the grieving. Nobody will cut himself or shave his
head. Every rite that made a death bearable is stripped out.

## What is on the other side of the chapter

Keep reading. Nine verses later:

*"Behold, the days are coming, says the LORD, that it shall no more be said, 'The
LORD lives who brought up the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt,' but
'The LORD lives **who brought up the children of Israel from the land of the north
and from all the lands where He had driven them**.' For I will bring them back into
their land which I gave to their fathers."*

He says the return will be a bigger event than the exodus — so much bigger that the
nation will change the way it swears its oaths.

That was written around 600 BC, to people about to be deported, and the scattering
that eventually followed lasted nineteen centuries.

> **The word for the sending.** *I will send for many fishermen, and they shall fish
> them… and afterward I will send for many hunters, and they shall hunt them from
> every mountain and every hill and out of the holes of the rocks.* Fishermen first,
> then hunters. Jeremiah says the gathering will reach the ones who do not come when
> they are called.
