Three armies are coming and Judah is told the battle is not theirs.

## The situation

Moab, Ammon and Mount Seir, marching on Jehoshaphat together. His own prayer sums
it up: *we have no power against this great multitude that is coming against us,
nor do we know what to do — but our eyes are on You.*

He admits all three: no strength, no plan, no idea.

## What they are told

*"**You will not even need to fight this battle.** Take your positions, stand
still, and watch the salvation of the LORD on your behalf."*

The same instruction Moses gave at the Red Sea, word for word in places: *stand
still and see the salvation of the LORD.* See the note on Exodus 14:10–14.

Three commands, and all three are about stopping.

## And yet they go out

*"Tomorrow, **go out to face them**, and the LORD will be with you."*

They are not told to stay home. They have to march to the battlefield of a battle
they will not fight.

## What they did first

*Jehoshaphat bowed with his face to the ground, and all Judah and the people of
Jerusalem fell down before the LORD to worship Him. Then the Levites… **stood up to
praise the LORD with a very loud voice**.*

The whole nation on the floor, and then a choir at full volume.

## The order of march

The next morning Jehoshaphat did something no commander does. *He appointed those
who should sing to the LORD, and who should praise the beauty of holiness, **as
they went out before the army**.*

Singers in front. Unarmed men, out ahead of the soldiers, in the tribe whose name
means praise. See the note on Genesis 49:8–12.

## What they sang

*Praise the LORD, for His mercy endures forever.*

Six words. It is the refrain of Psalm 136, repeated twenty-six times in that psalm,
and it is what Judah took into a valley with three armies in it.

Nothing about the enemy. Nothing about the danger. One line about His mercy.

## What happened

*And **when they began to sing and to praise**, the LORD set ambushes against the
people of Ammon, Moab and Mount Seir, and they were defeated.*

When they began. The timing is in the sentence.

By the time Judah reached the lookout point there was nothing standing. It took
them three days to carry away what was left.

> **What the valley was renamed.** *Beracah* — blessing. They assembled there on
> the fourth day to bless the LORD, and the name stuck. A place named for what
> happened in it, by people who had not raised a weapon.
