Mark's account of the first four, and it takes five verses.

## What they were doing

*He saw Simon and his brother Andrew **casting a net into the water**, because **fishing was
their living**.*

Mark adds the clause. They are not out for the afternoon; it is the family trade.

The Greek is a circular casting-net, thrown by hand from the shallows.

## The call

*"**Come with Me**," Jesus said to them, "and **I will make you fishers of people**."*

The Greek is *come after Me*.

## The promise

*I will make you.*

The verb is His. He does not tell them to become anything; He says what He will do to
them.

He takes the thing they already are and says the object will change.

## The response

*They **dropped their nets right there** and went with Him.*

The Greek is **euthus** — immediately, at once. Mark uses it more than forty times in
sixteen chapters; it is the pulse of his Gospel.

## The second pair

*A little farther on He saw **James son of Zebedee and his brother John, sitting in their
boat putting their nets back in order**.*

The Greek for *putting in order* is **katartizontas** — mending, repairing, setting right.

It is the same word Paul uses for restoring a fallen brother, and Peter uses of what God
does after suffering: *will put you back together*. See the note on 1 Peter 5:10.

## What they left

*And right away they **left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired men** and walked
away after Him.*

Mark mentions the hired men, which tells you the business was large enough to employ
staff. This is not destitution being walked away from.

And it mentions the father, sitting in the boat.

## What John's Gospel adds

John 1 has an earlier meeting — Andrew hears the Baptist say *behold the Lamb of God*,
follows, spends a day with Jesus, and goes to find his brother: *we have found the
Messiah.*

So this is not the first time they had met. It is the day the trade ended.

## What they went back to

After the resurrection, in John 21: *Simon Peter said to them, "I am going fishing." They
said to him, "We are going with you also."*

They fish all night and catch nothing, and a man on the shore tells them which side to
cast on.

Then a charcoal fire, breakfast, and three questions for Peter.

## Where the two pairs end up

Peter, James and John become the three taken into Jairus's house, up the mountain, and
into Gethsemane.

James is the first of the twelve to be killed, in Acts 12, by Herod's sword. John is
traditionally the last to die.

## What Mark records next

The same day: the synagogue at Capernaum, the unclean spirit, Peter's mother-in-law, and
the whole town at the door after sunset.

Mark 1 does not slow down.

> **What He says He will do.** *I will make you.* They are asked to come, and the making
> is stated as His work — and Mark says the nets went down immediately.
