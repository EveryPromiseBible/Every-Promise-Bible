One sentence, and it locates the evidence outside the reader.

## The claim

*God's love for us **came out into the open** like this.*

The Greek is **ephanerothe he agape tou Theou en hemin** — the love of God was manifested among
us, or in us.

**Phaneroo** — to make visible, to bring into the light.

## What has just been said

*Beloved, let us love one another, for love is of God… **He who does not love does not know God, for
God is love**.*

The Greek is **ho Theos agape estin** — God is love.

Not *God is loving*. A noun, not an adjective.

## What the verse does with it

Having made the largest possible statement, John immediately points at a specific event.

The abstraction is not left as an abstraction.

## The demonstration

*He had **one Son**, and He **sent Him into the world**.*

The Greek is **ton huion autou ton monogene** — His only-begotten Son.

**Monogenes** — only, unique. The word John uses four times of Jesus and once of Isaac in
Hebrews.

## The purpose

*So that **we would get our life through Him**.*

The Greek is **hina zesomen di' autou** — that we might live through Him.

## The next verse

*In this is love, **not that we loved God, but that He loved us** and sent His Son to be the
propitiation for our sins.* See the note on 1 John 4:10.

The Greek is **hilasmos** — propitiation, the covering.

John states the direction twice: not ours toward Him.

## The conclusion drawn

*Beloved, **if God so loved us, we also ought to love one another**.* See the note on
1 John 4:11.

The Greek is **opheilomen** — we owe it.

The obligation is derived, not commanded on its own footing.

## What follows that

*No one has seen God at any time. **If we love one another, God abides in us**, and His love has been
perfected in us.*

The invisible God, and the visible thing John offers as evidence, is a congregation getting on.

## Where the confidence comes from

*And we have known and believed the love that God has for us.* See the note on
1 John 4:16.

*Love has been perfected among us in this: that **we may have boldness in the day of judgment**;
because as He is, so are we in this world.* See the note on 1 John 4:17.

*There is no fear in love; but **perfect love casts out fear**.*

## The last line of the paragraph

*We love Him because **He first loved us**.* See the note on 1 John 4:19.

The Greek is **hoti autos protos egapesen hemas** — because He first loved us.

**Protos** — first. The word carries the whole argument: the sequence, not the sentiment.

## Who is writing

The disciple who described himself as *the one leaning on Jesus' bosom*, and who is the only
evangelist to record that phrase.

Sixty years later, he writes a letter with **agape** in it more than fifty times.

## What his Gospel says in the same construction

*For **God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son**.* See the note on
John 3:14–18.

The same three elements: love, the only Son, and the giving.

> **The move.** Having said *God is love* — a noun, not an adjective — John immediately points at
> a dated event, and the abstraction is not left standing on its own.
