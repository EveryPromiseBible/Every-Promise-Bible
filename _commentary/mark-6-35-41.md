The only miracle in all four Gospels, and Mark keeps the details a witness would
remember.

## The disciples' proposal

*"**Send them off** so they can go to the farms and villages around here and buy
themselves something to eat."*

Reasonable. It is late, the place is deserted, and five thousand men plus women and
children have nothing.

Their solution is dispersal.

## The answer

*"**You give them something to eat.**"*

The pronoun is emphatic in the Greek — *you*, yourselves.

He hands the problem back.

## The costing

*"Are we supposed to go and spend **two hundred days' wages** on bread?"*

The Greek is two hundred denarii. A denarius was a day's pay for a labourer.

Eight months' wages, and they have done the arithmetic on the spot. Their objection is
not lazy; it is accurate.

## The audit

*"**How many loaves do you have? Go and see.**"*

He does not answer the objection. He asks what is in hand and sends them to count.

*"**Five — and two fish.**"*

John adds that it was a boy's lunch.

## The grass

*Sit down in groups **on the green grass**.*

Mark is the only one who mentions the colour, which places it in spring, after the
rains — and Passover was near, as John notes.

It is the kind of detail somebody remembers because they were sitting on it.

## The arrangement

*The people **settled in, group after group, some of a hundred and some of fifty**.*

The Greek for the groups is a word used of garden beds laid out in rows.

Nobody is standing. Before anything is distributed, five thousand men are seated in
ordered blocks on the ground.

## The four verbs

*He **took** the five loaves and the two fish, looked up to heaven, **gave thanks**, and
**broke** the loaves apart. Then He **kept handing them** to His disciples.*

Took, gave thanks, broke, gave. The same four, in the same order, as the upper room.

## The imperfect

*He **kept handing them** to His disciples.*

The Greek tense is continuous. The multiplication is not described as a flash; it
happens in His hands, over and over, as fast as twelve men could carry it away.

## What the disciples ended up doing

Exactly what He told them to. *You give them something to eat* — and they did, all
evening, from a supply that did not stop.

## What was left

Twelve baskets. One each.

## What He tells them to do first

*"**You give them something to eat.**"*

The Greek puts the pronoun first for emphasis — **dote autois hymeis phagein**.

They have just come back from a preaching tour and He sends them at an impossible instruction
before He does anything Himself.

*"Where shall we buy bread, that these may eat?" But this He said to test him, **for He Himself knew
what He would do**.*

**John** adds the aside. The impossibility is deliberate and the answer was decided before the
question.

## What they had

*"**How many loaves do you have? Go and see.**"*

Five and two fish, and **John** says they came from a boy's lunch.

The miracle begins with an inventory of what is actually in the hands of the people who said it
could not be done.

## The word He does not use

*And **looking up to heaven, He blessed and broke the loaves**.*

**Eulogesen** — He blessed, gave thanks. It is the ordinary Jewish blessing over bread: *blessed are
You, Lord our God, King of the universe, who brings forth bread from the earth.*

The same four verbs appear in the upper room: *He took bread, blessed and broke it, and gave it to
them.* And on the road to Emmaus: *He took bread, blessed and broke it, and gave it to them. Then
their eyes were opened and they knew Him.*

Took, blessed, broke, gave — three times in **Mark** and **Luke**, and in the third the breaking is
how He is recognised.

## What the multiplying is never described

**Mark** does not say the bread multiplied. He says He gave it to the disciples *to set before them*,
and there was enough, and twelve baskets were left.

The increase happens somewhere between His hands and theirs and is not narrated.

## The sentence He drew from it

*"**I am the bread of life. He who comes to Me shall never hunger**, and he who believes in Me shall
never thirst."*

**John** 6, the day after this, to the same crowd — who had followed Him round the lake looking for
another meal.

*"**Most assuredly, I say to you, you seek Me, not because you saw the signs, but because you ate of
the loaves and were filled.**"*

## And what moved Him

*When Jesus stepped ashore and saw that great crowd waiting for Him, **something moved in the deepest
part of Him**. They looked to Him like sheep with no shepherd.* See the note on Mark 6:30–34.

The feeding is the second thing He does. The first is teach them, and the reason **Mark** gives for
both is a verb about His insides.

> **What Mark says about it a page later.** *They had not yet grasped what the loaves
