Paul describes what Abraham did with the facts, and it is not what the facts
suggested.

## The phrase

*With **nothing left to hope for**, Abraham went on **hoping and believing**.*

The Greek is *against hope, in hope he believed* — **par' elpida ep' elpidi**. Two
kinds of hope in one clause, and they are set against each other.

There was no hope available from the situation. He held a different one.

## What he refused to do

*He **refused to dwell on the facts stacked against him**.*

The Greek verb is **katanoeo** — to fix the mind on, to consider closely, to study.
It is the same word used in Hebrews 3 for *think hard about Christ Jesus*.

So it is not that Abraham was unaware. Paul is precise about what he did with the
information: he would not stare at it.

## The facts he would not stare at

Paul lists them, and he does not soften them either:

- *his own body **as good as dead**, since he was about a hundred years old*
- *and **Sarah's womb dead as well***

The Greek for the first is **nekroo** — to put to death, to make a corpse of.
Paul's own vocabulary is blunter than most translations.

Two dead things, named. That is the raw material.

## What he looked at instead

The verse before tells you: *the God who gives life to the dead and calls things
that do not exist as though they already do.* See the note on Romans 4:16–17.

He had two dead things in his hand and a promise about them, and he attended to
the One who made it.

## "So he became the father of many nations"

*Exactly as he had been told: "**That is how many your descendants will be.**"*

Paul quotes Genesis 15 — the night God took him outside and showed him the stars.

And the name he had been given, *father of a multitude*, was already true in the
telling before it was true in the household. See the note on Genesis 17:3–8.

## What is not claimed here

Paul never says Abraham felt confident, or that his circumstances improved, or that
he stopped being a hundred years old.

His body stayed as it was until it did not. What changed first was where he was
looking.

> **What Hebrews says Sarah did.** *Sarah herself was given the strength to
> conceive… **because she counted the One who promised as good for it**.* The Greek
> is a reckoning word — she assessed His credit and found it sound. She had laughed
> at the door of the tent, and the New Testament puts her in the roll of faith
> anyway. See the note on Hebrews 11:8–12.
