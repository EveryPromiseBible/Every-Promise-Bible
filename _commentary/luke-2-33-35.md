Simeon blesses the parents and then says something to the mother alone.

## The reaction

*Joseph and the child's mother stood there **marveling at the things being said** about Him.*

The Greek is **thaumazontes** — wondering.

They had already had an angel each, shepherds with a story, and a name given before
conception. And they are still astonished.

Luke has already noted her habit: *but Mary kept all these things and pondered them in her
heart*.

## The blessing

*And Simeon **blessed them**.*

Them — the parents. Not the child. Luke is careful about it.

He has just held the boy and sung. See the note on Luke 2:29–32.

## Who the next words are for

*And then said **to Mary, His mother**.*

Joseph is not addressed. What follows is said to her.

## The appointment

*"This child is **appointed for the falling and the rising again of many in Israel**."*

The Greek is **keitai** — is set, is laid.

Falling first. The order in the sentence is not the comfortable one.

Isaiah has both: *He will be as a sanctuary, but a stone of stumbling and a rock of offence
to both the houses of Israel*.

Paul quotes that at the end of Romans 9, and Peter in his first letter.

## The sign

*"And He will be **a sign that people argue about and speak against**."*

The Greek is **semeion antilegomenon** — a sign spoken against, contradicted.

A sign is meant to point. This one will be argued with.

Paul says the same of the message: *we preach Christ crucified, to the Jews a stumbling block
and to the Greeks foolishness*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 1:18–25.

## The purpose

*"So that **what has been hidden in many hearts will finally come out into the open**."*

The Greek is **dialogismoi** — reasonings, the thoughts running in a person.

The same word Jesus uses of the disciples' doubts after the resurrection: *why do these doubts
arise in your hearts?* See the note on Luke 24:36–43.

He functions as a revealer. What people do with Him shows what was already there.

## The sword

*"And **a sword will pass through your own soul too**."*

The Greek is **rhomphaia** — not the short **machaira** but the large Thracian broadsword. The
word used in Revelation of what comes out of His mouth.

Simeon does not explain it. He says it to a teenage mother holding a six-week-old baby and
stops.

## Where she is standing thirty-three years later

*Now there stood by the cross of Jesus **His mother**.*

And: *when Jesus therefore saw His mother, and the disciple whom He loved standing by, He
said to His mother, "Woman, behold your son!" Then He said to the disciple, "Behold your
mother!" And from that hour that disciple took her to his own home.*

## Where she is at the beginning of Acts

*These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women and **Mary the
mother of Jesus**, and with His brothers.*

In the upper room, and His brothers with her — the same brothers who had not believed Him.

## What they had brought that day

Two turtledoves. The offering Leviticus allows *if she cannot afford a lamb*.

The family could not afford the lamb, and had brought one anyway.

> **Who the second half is addressed to.** He blesses both parents, and then turns and speaks
> to her — falling before rising, a sign that gets argued with, and a broadsword.
