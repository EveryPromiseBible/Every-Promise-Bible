A man is reading the right passage and cannot get into it, and somebody arrives
running.

## The question

*"**Do you understand what you are reading?**"*

Philip does not open with an announcement. He asks.

The Greek has a play on it — *ginoskeis ha anaginoskeis*, do you know what you are
reading — and it survives in the English *do you get what you have got*.

## The answer

*"**How can I, unless someone guides me?**"*

No defensiveness. A treasury official, in his own chariot, on his own road, says he
cannot do it alone.

And he *invited Philip to come up and sit with him.*

## What he was reading

Isaiah 53:7–8, quoted in full:

*He was led like a sheep to the slaughter, and like a lamb silent before the one who
shears it, **so He does not open His mouth**. In His humiliation His justice was taken
away. And who can tell of His generation? For **His life is taken from the earth**.*

Of the whole scroll, he is at that page.

## The question underneath the question

*"Tell me, please, **who is the prophet speaking about? Himself, or someone else?**"*

It is the right question, and it is the one Jewish readers had argued about for
centuries.

He has no idea who the answer is, and he knows the passage is about somebody.

## What Philip does

*Then Philip opened his mouth, and **beginning from that very Scripture** he told him
the good news about Jesus.*

He starts where the man already was. He does not go back to Genesis or forward to a
different text.

The Greek is *beginning from this Scripture* — that verse, that scroll, that
sentence.

## Two men, one road

Philip left a revival in Samaria for an empty desert road, because he was told to.
See the note on Acts 8:26–29.

The Ethiopian had travelled fifteen hundred miles to worship at a temple that would
not let him past the outer court, and was going home with a scroll he could not
understand.

Both journeys converge on one chariot at one moment, and neither man arranged it.

## What comes next

*"Look, here is water. **What is there to keep me from being baptized?**"*

In a desert. See the note on Acts 8:36–38.

> **Which verse he had not reached.** Isaiah 53 goes on: *He was **wounded for our
> transgressions**, He was **bruised for our iniquities**… and by His stripes we are
> healed.* Whatever Philip said, that was a few lines further down the same column.
> See the note on Isaiah 53:4–6.
