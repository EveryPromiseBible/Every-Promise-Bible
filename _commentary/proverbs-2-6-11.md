Wisdom in this chapter is not a skill acquired. It is something given, and the
passage says where from.

## The source

*It is the LORD who gives wisdom; from His mouth come knowledge and
understanding.*

The image is spoken — out of His mouth. Which puts the search for wisdom in the
same place as the search for what He has said.

The verses just before this describe looking for it like silver and digging for
it like hidden treasure. Then the reason that search works: the thing being dug
for is being handed over.

## What it does once you have it

Read the run of verbs — every one of them is protective:

- He is a **shield** to those who walk with honest hearts
- He **guards** the paths of the just
- He **watches over** the way of those who love Him
- good sense will **keep watch** over you
- understanding will **guard** your steps

Five in six verses. In this chapter wisdom is not primarily about being clever.
It is about being kept.

## Where it settles

*Wisdom will settle into your heart, and knowledge will be a delight to your
soul.*

The Hebrew is *come into* your heart — it takes up residence rather than being
consulted. And the result is described as pleasure, not duty: knowledge
becoming something a person enjoys.

## Who the wisdom turned out to be

*But of Him you are in **Christ Jesus**, who became for us **wisdom from God** — and
righteousness and sanctification and redemption.*

**Paul** does not say Christ gives wisdom. He says Christ *became* it, for us.

*In whom are hidden **all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge**.*

The chapter opens by telling a son to dig for wisdom *like silver* and search for it *like
hidden treasure*, and **Colossians** names the seam.

## And how it is asked for

*If any of you lacks wisdom, **let him ask of God, who gives to all liberally and without
reproach**, and it will be given to him.*

**James** keeps Proverbs' terms — it is given, from His mouth — and adds two clauses the
Old Testament could not: *liberally*, and *without reproach*. Nobody is told off for
needing it.

> **What follows immediately.** *Then you will understand what is right and fair,
> every good path will open before you.* The understanding of what is right comes
> after the wisdom is given, not before. In Proverbs the discernment is a
> consequence of the gift, which is why the chapter opens by telling you to go
> looking for it.
