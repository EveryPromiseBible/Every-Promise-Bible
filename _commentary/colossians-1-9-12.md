One of Paul's two great prayers, and it runs as a chain — each link producing the
next.

## The links

- **filled with the knowledge of what He wants**, with wisdom and spiritual
  understanding
- *so that* you **walk in a way worthy of the Lord**, pleasing Him through and
  through
- **bearing fruit in every good work** and **growing in the knowledge of God**
- **strengthened with every strength** His glorious power supplies
- ready for **endurance and patience** — *and all of it with joy*
- **giving thanks to the Father**

It begins with knowing and ends with thanking. Everything in between follows from
the first link.

## What he asks for first

Not strength. **Knowledge of what God wants** — and the word for knowledge here
is the intensified form, full or thorough knowing.

Paul's prayers consistently start here. He treats seeing rightly as the thing
that produces walking rightly, rather than the other way round.

## The strength, and what it is for

*Strengthened with every strength that His own glorious power supplies, ready for
endurance of every kind and for patience that does not run out.*

Enormous language — glorious power, every strength — and then the purpose lands
somewhere unexpected. Not for miracles or conquest. For **endurance and
patience**.

And the last three words change the tone completely: *and all of it with joy.*
Not grim survival. Staying power that is glad.

## The last link

*Giving thanks to the Father, who has made us fit to take our share of what
belongs to His holy people in the light.*

The verb is about qualifying someone, making them sufficient. It is past tense
and God is the subject: the fitness was conferred, not developed.

> **Where the prayer goes next.** Straight into one of the highest passages about
> Christ in the New Testament — the image of the invisible God, in whom all
> things hold together. Paul prays for knowledge and then immediately supplies
> some.
