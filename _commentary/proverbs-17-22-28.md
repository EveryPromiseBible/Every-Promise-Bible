Seven proverbs, and the last three are all about keeping quiet.

## The medicine

*A **cheerful heart is good medicine**, but a **crushed spirit dries up the bones**.*

The Hebrew for *medicine* is **gehah** — a cure, a healing. The word occurs once in the
whole Bible, here.

And *dries up* is **yabesh** — the verb used of the ground in a drought and of Jeroboam's
withered hand.

## Where the marrow is

Bones are where blood is made. The proverb puts the drying at the deepest point in the body
it can name.

The same pairing runs through the collection: *a sound heart is life to the body, but envy
is rottenness to the bones*, and *a good report makes the bones healthy*.

## The bribe

*The wicked take a bribe **from the fold of a robe** to bend the ways of justice.*

The Hebrew is *a bribe out of the bosom* — from inside the garment, where a man carried
things.

The picture is precise: it does not come out in the open. A hand goes into a robe.

## The eyes

*Wisdom is **right before the face** of the discerning, but the eyes of a fool **wander to
the ends of the earth**.*

The Hebrew is *wisdom is before him who has understanding* — in front of his face, close
enough to look at.

The fool is not short of effort. He is looking further away than the thing is.

## The son

*A foolish son is **grief to his father** and **bitterness to the mother who bore him**.*

Both parents named, and the mother identified by the bearing.

Proverbs says it four or five ways across the book, and always with the same two people in
the room.

## The injustice

*To **punish the righteous is not good**, nor to **flog the noble for their honesty**.*

The Hebrew is *to strike princes for uprightness*.

Beating a man for being right — Proverbs names it as a category of wrong, and the whole
book of Job is an argument about it.

## The first silence

*Whoever **holds his words back has knowledge**, and the **even-tempered one has
understanding**.*

The Hebrew for *even-tempered* is *cool of spirit* — **qar-ruach**.

Two things are being described: a man who knows and does not say, and a man whose
temperature does not rise.

## The second silence

*Even **a fool who keeps quiet is thought wise**, and sensible when he holds his tongue.*

The proverb is nearly a joke, and it is entirely serious. Silence is so reliable an
indicator that it works even when it is not true.

## What the collection says elsewhere

*In the multitude of words sin is not lacking, but he who restrains his lips is wise.*

*He who guards his mouth preserves his life, but he who opens wide his lips shall have
destruction.*

*A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.* See the note on
Proverbs 15:8–15.

## Where the two halves of this passage meet

It opens with what a crushed spirit does to the bones and closes with a man who is cool of
spirit.

The medicine in verse 22 and the temperature in verse 27 are describing the same interior.

> **The word that occurs once.** *Gehah* — a cure. Proverbs uses it of a cheerful heart, and
> nowhere else in Scripture does the word appear at all.
