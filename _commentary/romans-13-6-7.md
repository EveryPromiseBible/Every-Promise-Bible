Two verses of the most ordinary instruction in the letter, and the noun **Paul** uses for tax
collectors is a surprise.

## The word

*"**For the authorities are God's ministers, giving their full attention to this very thing.**"*

**Leitourgoi theou** — God's public servants. It is the word the Greek Old Testament uses of priests
and Levites serving in the temple, and the root of English **liturgy**.

**Paul** applies it to the Roman civil service, under Nero, while collecting a tax that funded the
army occupying Judea.

He uses the same noun of himself — *a minister of Jesus Christ to the Gentiles* — and of the risen
**Christ** in **Hebrews** 8.

## The four

*"**Taxes to whom taxes are due, revenue to whom revenue, respect to whom respect, honour to whom
honour.**"*

**Phoros** — direct tax, tribute paid by a subject people. **Telos** — indirect duty, customs.
**Phobos** and **time** — fear and honour.

Two are money and two are attitude, and **Paul** lists them in the same sentence without a change of
register.

## What Jesus said about the same question

*"**Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are
God's.**"*

Asked with a coin in His hand, by men trying to trap Him. **Paul**'s **apodote** in verse 7 is the same
verb **Matthew** uses: *render*, give back what is owed.

*"Then the sons are free… **But so that we give them no offence**, go down to the sea and throw out a
hook."* See the note on Matthew 17:24–27.

## The debt that is not discharged

*"**Owe no one anything except to love one another.**"*

The next verse. **Paul** finishes the list of payable debts and names the one that stays open.

*He who loves another **has fulfilled the law**.*

*Love does no harm to a neighbour; therefore **love is the fulfilment of the law**.*

## Where the chapter ends

*But **put on the Lord Jesus Christ**, and make no provision for the flesh.*

> **The noun.** *Leitourgoi* — the word for priests serving at an altar, applied by Paul to Roman
> tax officials under Nero, in the sentence telling Christians to pay.
