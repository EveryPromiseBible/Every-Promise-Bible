Nehemiah comes back from a trip to Persia and finds the temple system quietly
collapsed. What he notices first is where people are.

## The detail that tells the story

*The Levites and singers who did the work had gone back, each to his own field.*

Nobody rebelled. Nobody made a decision. The support stopped arriving, and men
with families went home to grow food, because that is what a person does.

Nehemiah's question is aimed at the officials, not at the Levites: *why is the
house of God neglected?* He puts the failure on the people responsible for the
arrangement, not on the men who left.

## What he actually fixes

The interesting part is the administration. He does not merely restart the
collection — he appoints four named men over the storerooms, *for they were
counted faithful*, whose job is to distribute fairly among their kinsmen.

The previous arrangement had been compromised: a few verses earlier, a priest had
given a storeroom over to an official's private use. The problem was not only
supply. It was that the system had stopped being trustworthy.

Nehemiah's answer to a giving problem is accountable handling of what is given.

## What the detail tells you

*I also realised that **the portions for the Levites had not been given them**; for each of the
Levites and the singers who did the work **had gone back to his field**.*

Nobody rebelled and nobody resigned. The supply stopped, so the men who led the worship went
home to grow food, which is what men do when they are not being fed.

The temple was still standing and the singing had stopped.

## What he repairs

Not the music. The storerooms.

*Then I confronted the rulers and said, "**Why is the house of God forsaken?**" And I gathered
them together and set them in their place. Then all Judah brought the tithe of the grain and
the new wine and the oil to the storehouse.*

And he appoints treasurers over it — *for they were considered faithful, and their task was to
distribute to their brethren*.

## Who is owed

*Even so **the Lord has commanded that those who preach the gospel should live from the
gospel**.*

**Paul** argues it at length from the law about the ox and the threshing floor, and he does it
in a letter where he also says he took nothing himself.

## The prayer

*"**Remember me, O my God, concerning this, and do not wipe out my good deeds** that I have
done for the house of my God, and for its services!"*

He says it four times in the chapter. It is the last sentence of the book.

## What is said back to that kind of prayer

*For **God is not unjust to forget your work and labour of love** which you have shown toward
His name, in that you have ministered to the saints, and do minister.*

**Hebrews** answers Nehemiah's request directly, and the ground it gives is not the quality of
the work but the justice of God.

> **The prayer at the end.** *Remember me for this, O my God, and do not wipe
> away the good I have done.* Nehemiah prays this four or five times in his book,
> always about specific administrative work — walls, gates, storerooms, rosters.
> It is the closing note of a man who did unglamorous jobs and wanted them
> noticed by the only person whose notice he cared about.
