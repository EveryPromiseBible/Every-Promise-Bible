title: "Once useless, now useful"

The name of the man the whole letter is about arrives in the middle of a sentence, and it is a pun.

## The delay

*"And here is what I am asking, and who I am asking for: **my son. Onesimus.**"*

**Paul** has spent nine verses on thanks and courtesy and has not said what he wants. The Greek word
order holds the name back to the end of the clause — *I appeal to you for my child, whom I have
begotten in my chains, Onesimus.*

The relationship is established before the name is spoken.

## The name

**Onesimos** — *useful*, *profitable*. It was a common slave name, the way *Fortunatus* was: an
owner's description of what the property was for.

## The pun

*"**Time was, he was no use to you.**"*

The Greek is **ton pote soi achreston** — the once-useless one. **Achrestos**, against
**euchrestos** in the next clause — *now useful both to you and to me*.

**Paul** makes a joke on the man's name in a letter asking for his life. It is not decoration: he is
saying the name was never accurate until now.

And **achrestos** and **Christos** sound almost identical in Greek. The wordplay is one letter deep,
and the man became useful in a prison cell with **Christ** in it.

## What made the change

*"**I became his father while I was in these chains.**"*

**Egennesa** — I fathered, I begot.

*Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is **a new creation**; old things have passed away; behold,
all things have become new.*

*"Do not marvel that I said to you, '**You must be born again**.'"*

Nothing in the letter suggests Onesimus improved. **Paul** says he was fathered, in prison, by a man
in chains.

## What Paul does not say

That the theft was not real, that the running away was excusable, or that Philemon has no case. The
next paragraph concedes the debt in full and offers to pay it. See the note on
Philemon 1:18–19.

*Or do you not know that the unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom of God?… **And such were some
of you. But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified** in the name of the
Lord Jesus.*

**1 Corinthians** 6, and it is the same structure: the list is not softened, and the sentence turns
on *and such were some of you*.

## Where he turns up again

*With Onesimus, **a faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you**.*

**Colossians** 4, written at the same time and carried by the same two men. Onesimus goes back to
Colossae as a courier of Scripture.

> **The joke.** He makes a pun on a slave's name in a letter asking for his life — *the once-useless
> one*. Onesimus means *useful*, and Paul's point is that the name has only just started being
> true.
