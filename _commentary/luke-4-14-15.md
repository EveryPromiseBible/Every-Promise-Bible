Two verses of summary, and the middle word is the one **Luke** keeps using.

## The return

*"**Jesus came back into Galilee in the power of the Spirit.**"*

**En te dynamei tou Pneumatos.**

He went into the wilderness *full of the Holy Spirit* and comes out *in the power of the Spirit*.
**Luke** notes the Spirit at the baptism, the leading, the return, and then in the synagogue reading:
*the Spirit of the LORD is upon Me.*

Four times in one chapter, and this evangelist writes the sequel in which the same Spirit is poured
out on everybody else.

*"But you shall **receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you**; and you shall be witnesses
to Me."*

*How **God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power**, who went about doing
good.*

**Acts** 10, and **Peter** describes the whole ministry with the phrase from this verse.

## The good report

*"**Everyone had nothing but good to say about Him.**"*

**Doxazomenos hypo panton** — being glorified by all.

It lasts one paragraph. The next scene is Nazareth, where the same crowd takes Him to the edge of a
cliff. See the note on Luke 4:25–27.

**Luke** puts the universal approval and the attempted lynching four verses apart, on purpose. The
approval was for the reading; the trouble began with the two examples.

## What the power was for

Nothing in the summary is about display. The chapter's next paragraph has Him reading Isaiah 61 and
naming the work: good news to the poor, liberty to the captives, sight to the blind, the acceptable
year.

*"**The Son of Man has come to seek and to save that which was lost.**"*

> **The phrase Peter borrows.** *In the power of the Spirit.* Luke writes it as a travel note in
> chapter 4, and Acts 10 has Peter using it as the summary of everything Jesus ever did.
