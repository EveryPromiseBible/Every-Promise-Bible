A comparison between rain and speech, and the comparison does more work than it
first appears.

## What rain does

*Rain and snow come down from the sky and do not return there until they have
soaked the earth, making it bud and bloom, giving seed to the sower and bread to
the eater.*

Isaiah traces the whole cycle: down, into the ground, up as a plant, out as seed
and as bread. Nothing about it is instant, and nothing about it is wasted.

Rain does not look like bread on the day it falls.

## What the word does

*So it is with the word that goes out from My mouth. It will not come back to Me
empty. It will do what I intend and finish the work I sent it to do.*

Two claims: it will not return empty, and it will complete its errand.

The Hebrew for *empty* means without result, having achieved nothing — the same
word used of coming back from somewhere with nothing in your hands.

## The part the image supplies

Rain that soaks into ground and disappears has not failed. It is at the beginning
of its work.

That is what the comparison is for. A word that has been spoken and appears to
have vanished is described here as being in the middle of doing something, on a
timescale the passage has just spelled out.

## Where the chapter is going

The verses before this are the famous ones — *my thoughts are not your thoughts,
neither are your ways my ways.* And the invitation at the top of the chapter:
*come, all who are thirsty… without money and without cost.*

So the passage about a word that finishes what it was sent for sits inside an
offer of something free.

> **The chapter's last image.** *You will go out in joy and be led forth in
> peace; the mountains and hills will burst into song, and all the trees of the
> field will clap their hands.* Isaiah keeps the agriculture running to the end —
> what the rain produces, in his telling, is a harvest that celebrates.
