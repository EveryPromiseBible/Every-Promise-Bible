Ten men are sent to show a priest something that is not there yet.

## Where He is

*On His way to Jerusalem Jesus was traveling along **the border country between Samaria and
Galilee**.*

The frontier. The strip where the two populations met and neither was at home.

Luke has already recorded a Samaritan village refusing Him passage, and the disciples asking
whether to call down fire.

## The ten

*Ten men with leprosy met Him.*

The Greek is **leproi** — a category covering several skin conditions, all of which carried the
same legal consequence.

The border is exactly where a mixed group of Jews and Samaritans could exist. Their disease had
already put them outside both communities.

## The distance

*They **stood off at a distance** and raised their voices.*

The law required it: *he shall dwell alone; his dwelling shall be outside the camp*, and the
leper was to cry *unclean, unclean*.

They shout because they are not permitted to come near.

## What they shout

*"**Jesus! Master! Have mercy on us!**"*

**Epistata** — the word Luke uses for a superior, a commander. It is the word the disciples use
of Him, and no one else in the Gospel uses it except Luke's own characters on the inside.

Men outside the camp address Him the way the twelve do.

## What He does not do

Touch them. Speak a word of healing. Say anything about their faith.

Elsewhere He touches a leper — *He put out His hand and touched him, saying, "I am willing; be
cleansed"*. See the note on Luke 5:12–16.

Here He does not.

## The instruction

*"**Go and show yourselves to the priests**."*

Leviticus 14 sets out the procedure: a healed leper presents himself to the priest, who inspects
him and pronounces him clean, and then the ritual of the two birds, the cedar, the scarlet, and
the hyssop follows.

The priest is the certifying authority. He does not heal. He confirms.

## The problem with the instruction

They are still lepers when He gives it.

Being sent to a priest for inspection while visibly diseased is being sent to be turned away.

## The obedience

*And **as they went along the road, they were made clean**.*

The Greek is **en to hupagein autous** — in their going.

The healing happens in the interval between the instruction and the destination. Not at the
word, and not at the priest.

## Where else this order shows up

Naaman told to dip seven times in the Jordan, and the leprosy gone at the seventh.

The priests at the Jordan, whose feet had to be in the water before the river cut off. See the
note on Joshua 3:9–13.

The water pots at Cana, filled to the brim before anything happened. See the note on
John 2:6–8.

## The count

All ten went. All ten were cleansed.

The obedience is not distinguished at this stage, and neither is the result. What separates them
happens on the way back.

## What one of them does

*One of them, when he saw that he had been healed, **turned around and came back**… and the man
was a Samaritan.* See the note on Luke 17:15–19.

Nine keep going to the priest, which is what they were told to do.

> **Where the healing happened.** *As they went along the road.* Not at the word, and not at the
> priest — in the walking between the two.
