He is at a Pharisee's table, and He turns His back on the host to address him.

## The posture

*Then **He turned toward the woman** and said **to Simon**.*

He faces her and speaks to him. Luke records the direction of the body, which he did
not need to.

## The question

*"**Do you see this woman?**"*

Simon has seen her. His whole objection was about seeing her — *if this man were a
prophet, He would know **who and what sort of woman** this is.*

The question is not whether she is in the room.

## The three comparisons

Each one is the same shape: what Simon did not do, and what she did.

- *"You gave Me **no water for My feet** — but she has **washed My feet with her
  tears** and dried them with her hair."*
- *"You gave Me **no kiss of welcome** — but from the moment I walked in **she has not
  stopped kissing My feet**."*
- *"You did **not pour oil on My head** — but **she has poured perfume on My feet**."*

Water, a kiss, oil. All three were ordinary courtesies owed to a guest, and Simon had
skipped every one.

She supplied all three, and in each case at the feet rather than the head.

## Her hair

A woman's hair was bound up in public. She unbound it in a room full of men to dry
His feet, having already made a scene by crying on them.

## The verdict

*"So I tell you this: **her many sins have been forgiven, and that is why she has loved
so much**."*

The forgiveness is stated first and the love is the consequence. Luke's Greek puts the
forgiving in the perfect tense — already done, standing done — before she ever entered
the house.

## The other half

*"But **the one who has been forgiven little loves little**."*

Said to a man who had just been told a parable about two debtors, one owing fifty
denarii and one five hundred, and had answered correctly which would love more.

He gave the right answer about the story and missed himself in it.

## What He says to her

*Then He said to her, "**Your sins are forgiven**."*

She is told directly, out loud, in front of the table.

## The reaction

*"**Who is this man, that He even forgives sins?**"*

The same question the scribes asked when the paralytic's sins were forgiven. It is the
right question, asked by people who will not follow it anywhere.

## The dismissal

*"**Your faith has saved you. Go on your way in peace**."*

Not *your tears*, and not *your perfume*. Her faith.

The Greek for *go in peace* is the standard Hebrew farewell — *lech leshalom*, go
toward peace.

> **What the perfume was.** An alabaster flask, which had to be broken to be used. She
> came carrying it, so she had decided what she was going to do before she reached the
> door.
