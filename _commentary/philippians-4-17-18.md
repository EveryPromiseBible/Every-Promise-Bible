Paul is thanking them for money, and he stops to say what he is actually pleased about.

## The disclaimer

*"**It is not the gift itself that I am after**."*

He has just told them he has learned to be content in any state — *I know how to be
abased, and I know how to abound.*

He does not want them thinking the thank-you is a hint.

## What he is after

*"What I am after is **the fruit that keeps mounting up to your account**."*

The Greek is commercial: **karpos** (fruit, yield) and **logos** (account, ledger
entry). *Mounting up* is **pleonazo** — to increase, to abound.

Paul is using the language of a bank ledger, and the account in question is theirs.

The benefit he names is not his.

## What he had received

*"**I have everything I need, and more besides. I am amply supplied**."*

Three statements of sufficiency in a row. The Greek for *I have everything* is
**apecho** — the word used on receipts: *paid in full*.

He is writing from a Roman prison.

## Who brought it

*"Now that **Epaphroditus has put your gift into my hands**."*

Philippi sent a man as well as money. Epaphroditus nearly died on the errand — *he was
sick almost unto death; but God had mercy on him* — and Paul sends him home with the
letter.

## The three things he calls it

*"**A fragrance of sweetness, a sacrifice that is welcomed, a delight to God**."*

Every one of them is temple vocabulary, and none of them is about Paul.

- **A fragrance of sweetness** — the phrase from Leviticus for accepted sacrifice, the
  same one used over Noah's altar. See the note on Genesis 8:20–22.
- **A sacrifice that is welcomed** — **dekten**, acceptable, received.
- **A delight to God** — well-pleasing.

Money handed to a prisoner is described as smoke going up off an altar.

## Where it went

To an inmate's food and expenses in Rome. Paul says God took it as an offering.

## What Philippi had done before

*"**Even in Thessalonica you sent aid once and again** for my necessities… **no church
shared with me concerning giving and receiving but you only**."*

They had been supporting him since the beginning — since a riverside prayer meeting, a
seller of purple, and a night in the town jail.

## The sentence after this one

*"**And my God shall supply all your need according to His riches in glory by Christ
Jesus**."*

It comes immediately after the receipt. See the note on Philippians 4:19–20.

> **Whose ledger.** He says twice that he is not writing about his own supply. The
> subject of the paragraph is what is accumulating on the other side of the
> transaction.
