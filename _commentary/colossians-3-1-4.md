The whole ethical half of Colossians hangs off the word *if*.

## The condition

*If, then, **you were raised to life with Christ**.*

The Greek **ei** here is a first-class condition — *if, and it is the case*. Not a doubt.
The sense is *since*.

Paul has already stated it: *buried with Him in baptism, in which you also were raised with
Him through faith in the working of God*.

## The instruction

*Keep **seeking what is above**.*

The Greek is **zeteite** — a present imperative: go on seeking.

## The location

*Where Christ is, **seated at the right hand of God**.*

Psalm 110's line, quoted more often in the New Testament than any other Old Testament verse:
*sit at My right hand, till I make Your enemies Your footstool*. See the note on
Psalms 110:1.

*Above* is not vague. Paul gives it an address and a person.

## The second instruction

*Give your mind to what is above, **not to the things lying here on the earth**.*

The Greek is **phroneite** — set your mind, be minded. The same verb as *let this mind be in
you which was also in Christ Jesus*.

Seeking, and then thinking. Paul asks for both in two verses.

## The reason

*For **you died**.*

Flat, past tense, three words. He does not argue for it.

The end of chapter 2 has said it already: *if you died with Christ from the basic principles
of the world, why, as though living in the world, do you subject yourselves to
regulations — do not touch, do not taste, do not handle?*

Everything he is arguing against is religious rule-keeping, and the argument against it is
that the person the rules were for is dead.

## Where the life is

*And the life you now have is **hidden away with Christ in God**.*

The Greek is **kekruptai** — a perfect passive: has been hidden and stays hidden.

Two layers: with Christ, and in God. Nothing gets at it without going through both.

## What is not visible

The life is real and out of sight, and Paul says so without apology.

The Colossians were being told by somebody that they needed visible extras — festivals, new
moons, angel worship, ascetic practice. Paul's answer is that the thing they are being
offered a supplement to is not even on display.

## The appearing

*And when Christ, **who is our very life**, is **shown openly**, then **you also will be
shown together with Him, in glory**.*

The Greek is **phanerothe** — manifested, brought into the open. Twice, of the same event.

His appearing and theirs are one occurrence.

John writes it identically: *what we are going to be has not come out into the open yet…
when He does come out into the open, we will be like Him*. See the note on 1 John 3:2–3.

## The phrase in the middle

*Christ, **who is our life**.*

Not who gives life. Paul has used this construction all through the letter — *Christ in you,
the hope of glory*, and *in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily*.

## What follows immediately

*Therefore **put to death** your members which are on the earth.*

The list of behaviours starts in verse 5, and it starts with *therefore*.

Paul never puts the instructions first. Four verses of position, and then the conduct that
follows from it.

## The parallel in Ephesians

*And **raised us up together, and made us sit together in the heavenly places** in Christ
Jesus.* See the note on Ephesians 2:4–7.

Past tense there too, and the seating is already done.

> **The order of the letter.** Died, raised, hidden, and appearing — and only then a single
> word, *therefore*, before anything is asked of them.
