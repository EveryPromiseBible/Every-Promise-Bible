---
key: Acts 5:27–32
title: "We ought to obey God rather than men"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The apostles are back in front of the council they were released from, and the exchange is short.

## The charge

*"**Did we not strictly command you not to teach in this name?**"*

**Epi to onomati touto** — in this name. The high priest will not say it.

*"**And look, you have filled Jerusalem with your doctrine, and intend to bring this Man's blood on
us!**"*

*And all the people answered and said, "**His blood be on us and on our children.**"*

**Matthew** records the crowd saying it seven weeks earlier. The council is now anxious about the
thing that was volunteered.

## The answer

*"**We ought to obey God rather than men.**"*

**Peitharchein dei theo mallon e anthropois.**

Not defiance for its own sake — **dei**, it is necessary. It is the same verb **Luke** uses of the
Son of Man's suffering: *the Son of Man **must** suffer many things.*

## The sermon inside the reply

*"**The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom you murdered by hanging on a tree. Him God has
exalted to His right hand to be Prince and Saviour, to give repentance to Israel and forgiveness of
sins.**"*

Four verbs, and three of them are God's: raised, exalted, to give.

**Peter** puts the accusation and the offer in one sentence, and the offer is addressed to the men who
did it.

## What is given

*"**To give repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins.**"*

**Dounai metanoian** — to give repentance.

Both nouns are objects of the same verb. The turning is not the price of the forgiveness; both are
described as issued by the exalted **Christ**.

*Then God has also **granted to the Gentiles repentance to life**.*

*Or do you despise the riches of His goodness, forbearance and longsuffering, not knowing that **the
goodness of God leads you to repentance**?*

## Prince and Saviour

**Archegon kai Sotera** — leader, originator, and rescuer.

**Archegos** is used of **Jesus** four times in the New Testament and nowhere else: the *Prince of
life* in chapter 3, here, and twice in **Hebrews** — *the captain of their salvation*, and *the author
and finisher of our faith*.

## Who else is a witness

*"**And we are His witnesses to these things, and so also is the Holy Spirit whom God has given to
those who obey Him.**"*

Two witnesses, which is what the law required to establish a matter. **Peter** produces the second
one in front of a court.

> **The sentence in the middle of the defence.** *Whom you murdered by hanging on a tree… to give
> repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins.* The charge and the offer are one sentence, and the
> offer is made to the bench.
