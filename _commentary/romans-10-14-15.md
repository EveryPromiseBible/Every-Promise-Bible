Four questions, each one taking a step back from the last.

## The chain

*How are they to **call** on the One they have not **believed** in?*
*How are they to **believe** in the One they have never **heard** of?*
*How are they to **hear** without someone **preaching**?*
*How is anyone to **preach** unless they are **sent**?*

Call ← believe ← hear ← preach ← sent.

Paul is working backwards from the promise he has just quoted — *whoever calls on the
name of the LORD shall be saved* — to find out what has to happen first.

The chain ends at a sending, and the sending is God's.

## Why he is arguing this

The chapter is about Israel: *my heart's desire and prayer to God for Israel is that
they may be saved.*

Paul is establishing that the message went out and was heard, so that the failure to
believe was not for want of hearing. Three verses later: *have they not heard? Yes
indeed — their sound has gone out to all the earth.*

He then quotes Psalm 19, about the sky. See the note on Psalms 19:1–4.

## The quotation

*"**How beautiful are the feet** of those who carry the good news of peace, who carry
the good news of good things!"*

Isaiah 52:7, and the setting there is a runner coming over the hills toward a
besieged city with news that the siege is lifted.

*Feet* is the odd part of the compliment. It is not what anybody normally praises.

But if you are waiting for news, the feet are what you see first — coming over the
ridge, before you can make out the face or hear the shout.

## The verse before it in Isaiah

*"Your God reigns!"* — that is the message the beautiful feet are carrying.

And the verses after are the beginning of Isaiah 53. The runner with the good news is
the paragraph immediately preceding *He was wounded for our transgressions.*

## What Romans 10 says next

*So then **believing comes by hearing**, and hearing by the word of God.* See the note on
Romans 10:16–17.

The Greek is **rhema** — the spoken word.

## And what it said just before

*The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart… **if you confess with your
mouth** the Lord Jesus and **believe in your heart**… you will be saved.* See the note
on Romans 10:5–8.

The whole chapter runs on speech — mouth, heart, hearing, preaching, and a sound
going out to the ends of the world.

> **Who Paul is quoting the chain against.** Himself, twenty years earlier. He was the
> man with the letters to Damascus, and what stopped him was a voice.
