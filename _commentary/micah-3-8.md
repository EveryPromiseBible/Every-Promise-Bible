---
key: Micah 3:8
title: "But as for me"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
One verse of self-description, and it is set against three verses about prophets who preach for
money.

## What comes before

*"This is what the LORD says about the prophets who lead My people astray. **When they have
something to chew on, they cry peace**; but whoever puts nothing in their mouths, against him they
declare war."*

The charge is precise: their message tracks their fee.

*"Her leaders judge for a bribe, her **priests teach for a price**, her **prophets tell fortunes for
money** — yet they lean on the LORD and say, 'Is not the LORD among us? No harm can come upon us.'"*

Three professions, three price lists, and a doctrine of immunity to go with it.

## The turn

*"**But as for me**, I am filled with power by the Spirit of the LORD."*

The Hebrew is **ve'ulam anokhi** — but truly I.

Micah does not claim better training or a purer motive. He names a source.

*"But you shall **receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you**; and you shall be witnesses
to Me."*

**Acts** 1, and the Greek is **dynamin** — the word Micah's Greek Old Testament uses here.

## The three things he is full of

*"Filled with **power**… filled with **justice** and with **strength**."*

**Koach**, **mishpat**, **gevurah**. And the middle one is the odd one out: two words for capacity
and one for what is right.

A man can be filled with power and be a hireling. The **mishpat** is what makes the power safe.

## What it is for

*"**To declare to Jacob his rebellion and to Israel his sin.**"*

Not to comfort, and not to predict. To name the thing.

The false prophets in the paragraph above cry *peace* when there is a fee in it, and Micah's Spirit-
given task is to say the unpopular sentence.

*"**And when He has come, He will convict the world of sin**, and of righteousness, and of
judgment."*

**John** 16, and the Spirit's first named work in **Jesus**' description is Micah 3:8's.

*Now when they heard this, **they were cut to the heart**, and said to Peter and the rest of the
apostles, "Men and brethren, what shall we do?"*

## What he was not full of

*Not by might nor by power, but **by My Spirit**, says the LORD of hosts.*

Zechariah, a century later, to a man laying a foundation.

*And **my speech and my preaching were not with persuasive words of human wisdom, but in
demonstration of the Spirit and of power**, that your faith should not be in the wisdom of men but
in the power of God.*

**Paul** in **1 Corinthians** 2, describing his own arrival in a city, and making the same
distinction Micah makes.

## And what it cost him

The next verse begins *hear this, you leaders of the house of Jacob*, and four verses later:
*Zion will be plowed like a field.*

A century afterwards, when Jeremiah is on trial for his life for saying the same thing, the elders
defend him by quoting this passage of Micah — and it is the one recorded case in the Old Testament
of a prophet's words saving another prophet.

> **The turn.** *But as for me.* Three professions in the verses above are priced, and Micah's
> answer is not that he is more honest. He names what he is full of, and the middle item on the
> list is justice.
