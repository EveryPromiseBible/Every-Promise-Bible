title: "Copy what is good"

A single instruction near the end, and the noun it hangs on is imitation.

## The verb

*"**Do not copy what is bad. Copy what is good.**"*

The Greek is **me mimou to kakon alla to agathon** — do not imitate the evil but the good.

**Mimeomai** — to mimic, to model oneself on. It is the word behind *mime*, and the New Testament
uses it sparingly and always about a person.

## Who it names

The verse sits between Diotrephes, who loves being first, and Demetrius, of whom *everyone speaks
well, and so does the truth itself*. See the note on 3 John 1:9.

**John** has just given his readers a bad example and a good one, in the same paragraph, and the
instruction between them is to copy.

## What the New Testament tells people to imitate

*Therefore be **imitators of God** as dear children. And walk in love, **as Christ also has loved us
and given Himself for us**.*

**Ephesians** 5, and the Greek is **mimetai tou Theou** — the only place in Scripture anybody is told
to imitate God, and the sentence immediately supplies the model in a human life and a death.

*Imitate me, just as **I also imitate Christ**.*

**Paul** never says *imitate me* without the second clause.

*For you, brethren, became imitators of the churches of God which are in Judea in Christ Jesus.*

## The test

*"**Whoever does good belongs to God; whoever does harm has never once laid eyes on Him.**"*

**Ho agathopoion ek tou Theou estin** — the one doing good is of God.

**John** puts it as origin rather than achievement. Not *whoever does good will belong to God* —
**ek**, out of, from. The doing is evidence of where somebody came from.

*"**A good tree cannot bear bad fruit, and a rotten tree cannot bear good fruit.**"* See the note on
Matthew 7:15–20.

*For **we are His workmanship**, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared
beforehand that we should walk in them.*

## Has never once laid eyes on Him

*Ouch heoraken ton Theon* — has not seen God.

**John** uses the verb the same way in his gospel: *no one has seen God at any time. The only
begotten Son… He has declared Him.*

And in the first letter: *no one has seen God at any time. **If we love one another, God abides in
us**, and His love has been perfected in us.*

Both times the unseen God is made visible by something — in the gospel by the Son, in the letter by
people loving each other.

*"**He who has seen Me has seen the Father.**"*

> **How the test is framed.** Not that doing good earns a standing — *whoever does good is **of**
> God*. John states it as origin, and puts the instruction between a man who loved being first and
> a man the truth itself spoke well of.
