Luke marks the exact syllable where a crowd turns.

## The setting

Paul is standing on the steps of the Antonia fortress, having just been dragged out of
the temple and beaten. He asked the commander for permission to speak, motioned with his
hand, and addressed the mob in Hebrew — at which *they kept all the more silent.*

He then told them his whole story: Tarsus, Gamaliel, the persecution, the road, the
light, Ananias.

## The turn

*They had **listened to him up to that word**.*

Luke says it precisely. The whole testimony was heard.

The word was the last one of the previous verse: *"Depart, for I will send you far from
here **to the Gentiles**."*

## What they shouted

*"**Get this man off the earth** — he should not be allowed to live!"*

The Greek is *away with such a fellow from the earth*.

It is what a crowd in the same city had shouted about somebody else, in front of Pilate,
a little over twenty years earlier.

## What they did

*Shouting and **throwing off their coats** and **flinging dust into the air**.*

Both gestures are Middle Eastern expressions of outrage. Dust thrown upward is what you
do when you cannot reach the person you want to stone.

And the coats: at a stoning the witnesses laid their garments down. Paul had held them
once, at Stephen's — and he had said so in this very speech, four verses earlier.

He is watching the coats come off again, and he described the scene from memory to the
people now doing it.

## The commander's response

*The commander ordered Paul **brought into the barracks** and said he should be
**examined under the lash**.*

Claudius Lysias does not speak Hebrew. He has heard a man address a mob in a language he
does not know and watched the mob try to kill him for it.

## The lash

The Greek is **mastix** — the **flagellum**, the scourge with weighted leather thongs. It
was an interrogation instrument, not a punishment: the theory was that a man under it
would say anything true.

## The reason given

*So that he could **find out why they were shouting** against him like this.*

He wants information, and torture is the standard method for getting it out of a
non-citizen.

## What Paul says next

*"Is it lawful for you to flog a Roman citizen who has not even been condemned?"*

He waits until they are tying him up, and then asks a question. See the note on
Acts 22:25–26.

## What Roman law said

The Lex Porcia and the Lex Julia forbade scourging a citizen, and forbade binding one
without trial. Claiming citizenship falsely was a capital offence, so a claim was
generally believed.

Paul had let this happen to him before without objecting — at Philippi he was beaten
with rods and only mentioned his citizenship the next morning.

## The word that ended the hearing

The crowd listens to the whole testimony — the road, the light, the blindness, Ananias — and turns on
one sentence:

*"**Then He said to me, 'Depart, for I will send you far from here to the Gentiles.'**"*

That is the word. **Luke** puts the riot immediately after it.

*For **He Himself is our peace**, who has made both one, and **has broken down the middle wall of
separation**.*

**Paul** wrote that from prison, and the wall he names is the balustrade in the temple court that
non-Jews could not pass — the very place the accusation against him was that he had brought a Greek
inside.

## What he had been

*"I persecuted this Way to the death, binding and delivering into prisons both men and women."*

He tells the crowd he was one of them, and says so in Hebrew.

*This is a faithful saying and worthy of all acceptance, that **Christ Jesus came into the world to
save sinners, of whom I am chief**. However, for this reason I obtained mercy, that in me first
**Jesus Christ** might show all longsuffering, as a pattern.*

## What he was told on the road

*"**Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?**"… "**I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you are
persecuting.**"*

The sentence that turned him is the identification of the church with **Christ** — and it is the same
logic as **Matthew** 25: *you did it to Me*.

> **How far the speech got.** Everything — the arrest warrants, the light, the voice, the
> blindness, Ananias — was heard in silence. The sentence that broke it was about who
> the message was going to.
