A prophet is told to make a crown and put it on the wrong head.

## Where the metal comes from

*Take silver and gold from **the exiles Heldai, Tobijah, and Jedaiah, who have arrived
from Babylon**.*

Three men just off the road from exile, carrying what they had. The crown is made
out of what the returning poor brought back.

## Whose head it goes on

*Set it on the head of **Joshua son of Jozadak, the high priest**.*

Not on Zerubbabel, who was the governor and of David's line. On the priest.

Under the law, that is not allowed. Crowns belonged to Judah's kings; the priesthood
belonged to Levi. The two lines never met, and Uzziah was struck with leprosy for
crossing between them. See the note on 2 Chronicles 26:19–21.

Zechariah is instructed to do it anyway, and told what it means.

## What he is to say

*"Here is the man whose name is **the Branch**, and he will branch out from his place
and **build the temple of the LORD**."*

**The Branch** — **tsemach**, a sprout, a shoot. It is a title the prophets use of
Messiah: Jeremiah's *Branch of righteousness* raised up to David, and Isaiah's shoot
out of the stump of Jesse. See the note on Isaiah 11:1–3.

Joshua the high priest is wearing a crown as a stand-in for somebody else, and
Zechariah says so out loud while doing it.

## The sentence

*"And **he will be a priest on his throne**, and **there will be harmony between the
two**."*

The Hebrew is *the counsel of peace shall be between them both* — between the two
offices.

Crown and censer, throne and altar, in one Person, at peace with each other rather
than in tension.

Psalm 110 had already said it: *the LORD has sworn and will not relent, "You are a
priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek"* — addressed to somebody
David calls *my Lord*, sitting at God's right hand. See the note on
Matthew 22:41–46.

## Who builds it

*"**Yes, he will build the temple of the LORD**."*

Said twice in two verses, for emphasis, to people standing in front of a
half-finished building site.

And: *"**Those who are far away will come and help build** the temple of the LORD."*

Distant people, brought in to build. It is what Paul says happened: *you who once
were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ… in whom the whole
building, being fitted together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord.*

## The crown afterwards

*"The crown will be **a memorial in the temple** of the LORD."*

It comes off Joshua's head and is hung up as a reminder. He wore it for an
afternoon, as a sign.

The One it was made for wears it permanently.

## The other Joshua

The high priest here is the same man Zechariah saw in chapter 3, standing in filthy
clothes with the accuser at his right hand, and heard *take off his filthy clothes…
I have taken away your sin.* See the note on Zechariah 3:1–5.

Re-clothed in chapter 3, crowned in chapter 6.

> **What Peter calls his readers.** *A royal priesthood* — the combination the law
> kept apart, and the thing this crown was made to picture. See the note on
> 1 Peter 2:9.
