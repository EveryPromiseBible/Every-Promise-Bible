title: "A plumb line"

The third of five visions, and it is the one where Amos stops arguing.

## What happened in the first two

*This is what the Lord GOD showed me. He was forming **a swarm of locusts**… Then I said, "**Lord
GOD, forgive! Oh, that Jacob may stand, for he is small!**" So the LORD relented.*

*He was summoning **judgment by fire**… Then I said, "Lord GOD, cease! Oh, that Jacob may stand."
So the LORD relented.*

Twice the prophet intercedes, and twice the disaster is called off. The ground he offers both
times is not that Israel deserves better. It is *he is small*.

*I sought for a man among them who would **make a wall, and stand in the gap** before Me on behalf
of the land, that I should not destroy it, but **I found no one**.*

Ezekiel names the office. Amos holds it twice.

## The third vision

*"**The LORD was standing beside a wall that had been built true to plumb, with a plumb line in
His hand.**"*

The Hebrew is **anakh** — a word that occurs four times in Scripture, all of them in this
paragraph, and probably means tin or lead: the weight on a builder's line.

## The question

*"Amos, what do you see?" "**A plumb line**," I said.*

The vision is not frightening and there is nothing to intercede against. A wall, and a tool for
measuring whether a wall is straight.

## What it is for

*"**Look, I am setting a plumb line among My people Israel. I will pass them by no longer.**"*

A plumb line does not push a wall over. It reports.

And that is why Amos says nothing this time. There is no argument available against a measurement.

*Therefore by the deeds of the law no flesh will be justified in His sight, for **by the law is the
knowledge of sin**.*

*What shall we say then? Is the law sin? Certainly not! On the contrary, **I would not have known
sin except through the law**.*

**Paul** describes the law doing exactly what the **anakh** does: not building anything, not
knocking anything down, and showing where the wall is.

## What the wall was

*A wall **that had been built true to plumb**.*

The Hebrew has the wall itself made by a plumb line. It was built straight and it is no longer
straight, which is why the line is worth hanging against it.

## What is measured next

*The high places of Isaac… the holy sites of Israel… the house of Jeroboam.*

The shrines and the throne. Not the poor.

## And the standard that is a Person

*Till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect
man, **to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ**.*

**Paul**'s measure is not a rule. It is a height, and it belongs to somebody.

*For **He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us**, that we might become the righteousness of
God in Him.*

The wall does not get straightened by being measured. **2 Corinthians** 5 names the only thing that
ever moved it.

## What Amos does after

Nothing — and Amaziah the priest reports him to the king for sedition. See the note on
Amos 7:14–17.

> **Why the prophet goes quiet.** He argued down a plague of locusts and a fire. Against a plumb
> line there is nothing to say: it does not attack the wall, it reports where the wall is.
