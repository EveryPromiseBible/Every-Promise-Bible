He sets up a comparison and then does not finish it the way anyone expects.

## The comparison

*For just as **the body is one and has many parts**, and all the parts of that one body, many as
they are, **are one body**.*

A commonplace. Roman writers used the body-and-members image for the state, and everyone in
Corinth would have heard it before.

## The turn

*So also **is Christ**.*

The sentence should end *so also is the church*.

It says Christ.

Paul does not compare the church to a body and then say Christ is the head. He says the many-parted
body **is** Christ.

## Where he learned it

*"Saul, Saul, **why are you persecuting Me?**"*

He had been arresting Christians, and the voice on the Damascus road named the injury as its own.
See the note on Acts 9:3–6.

## The making of it

*For **by one Spirit we were all baptized into one body**.*

The Greek is **en heni Pneumati** — in, or by, one Spirit.

**Ebaptisthemen** — aorist passive. It happened to them; they did not do it.

## The two pairs

*Whether **Jews or Greeks**, whether **slaves or free**.*

The two deepest divisions in that city — the racial and the legal.

Corinth was a Roman colony with a large slave population. The two categories named are the ones
that governed where a person could sit, eat and marry.

Both are stated as crossed.

## The drink

*And **we were all given the one Spirit to drink**.*

The Greek is **epotisthemen** — we were made to drink, given to drink. Passive again.

Two images in one verse: immersed in, and taken in.

## The obvious sentence

*For **the body is not one part, but many**.*

Which sounds like a truism, and is aimed at a specific problem.

## What the problem was

Corinth had gifts in abundance — *you come short in no gift* — and was ranking them.

Tongues, in particular, had become a marker of status.

## What follows

*If the foot should say, "Because I am not a hand, I am not of the body," **is it therefore not of
the body?***

*And if the ear should say, "Because I am not an eye, I am not of the body"…*

Two members talking themselves out, and then two talking others out: *the eye cannot say to the
hand, "I have no need of you"*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 12:15–20.

Paul addresses the inferiority first and the superiority second.

## The unpresentable parts

*Those members of the body which we think to be less honourable, **on these we bestow greater
honour**.*

*But God composed the body, having given greater honour to that part which lacks it, that there
should be **no schism in the body**.*

## The consequence

*And if one member suffers, **all the members suffer with it**; or if one member is honoured, **all
the members rejoice with it**.*

Stated as physiology rather than as an instruction.

## What comes after the chapter

*And yet I show you a more excellent way.*

*Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become sounding
brass.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 13:1–3.

Chapter 13 is not an interruption. It is the answer to the ranking.

> **The sentence that does not end where it should.** *So also is Christ* — not *so also is the
> church*. Paul learned the identification from a voice on a road.
