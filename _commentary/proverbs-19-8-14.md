Seven couplets, and the last one draws a line between two kinds of provision.

## The two sources

*House and wealth are **handed down from parents**, but **a sensible wife comes from
the LORD**.*

That is the whole point of the verse, and it is a contrast, not a comparison.

Property comes down a family line. It can be inherited, arranged, negotiated,
insured. The other cannot.

## The word

The Hebrew is **maskeleth** — from **sakal**, to be prudent, to have insight, to act
with understanding that produces good outcomes.

It is the same root in Joshua 1:8, where meditating on the book day and night makes
a man's way prosperous and gives him *good success*.

And it is the word used of Abigail: *a woman of good sense* — **sekel** — listed
before her looks. She talked a future king out of a massacre and saved a household
in an afternoon. See the note on 1 Samuel 25:2–3.

## Where the same chapter says the opposite runs

*A foolish child is his father's ruin, and **a quarrelsome wife is a leaking roof
that never stops dripping**.*

Proverbs uses that image three times. It is not a storm. It is the drip that will
not stop — and the book's complaint about it is always its relentlessness rather
than its force.

## The glory of overlooking

*Good sense makes a person **slow to anger**, and **his glory is to overlook an
offence**.*

The Hebrew for *overlook* is to pass over, to let go by.

And it is called his **glory** — his beauty, his adornment. Not his loss, and not
his weakness.

## The king's two moods

*A king's rage is like the roar of a lion, but **his favour is like dew on the
grass**.*

Both images are natural and neither is negotiable — you cannot argue with a lion or
manufacture dew. The verse simply reports what proximity to power is like.

## What opens the passage

*Whoever gains sense **loves his own soul**; whoever keeps understanding **will find
good**.*

Getting wisdom is described as an act of self-love, which is not the usual framing.

## And the one about luxury

*Luxury does not suit a fool.*

The Hebrew is that delicate living is not fitting for him — it does not sit right,
like good clothes on the wrong man.

## Where the overlooking was done

*And above all things **have fervent love for one another, for love will cover a multitude of
sins**.*

**Peter**'s verb is **kalyptei** — covers, puts a lid over. Not excuses, and not fails to
notice.

*"**Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do.**"*

The proverb calls overlooking an offence a man's glory. **Luke** records the largest offence
ever committed and the sentence spoken over it while it was still being committed.

## Where the bride comes from

*House and wealth are handed down from parents, but **a sensible wife comes from the LORD**.*

*"**All that the Father gives Me will come to Me**, and the one who comes to Me I will by no
means cast out."*

*Husbands, love your wives, just as **Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her**…
that He might **present her to Himself a glorious church**.*

Proverbs' one thing that cannot be inherited, arranged or negotiated is given by the Father —
and **Ephesians** describes a Bridegroom receiving exactly that, from exactly that source.

## The dew

*His favour is like **dew on the grass**.*

**Ratzon** — favour, good pleasure, acceptance. The word used of a sacrifice being accepted.

*"This is My beloved Son, **in whom I am well pleased**."*

> **What Proverbs says about finding one.** *Whoever finds a wife finds a good thing,
> and obtains favour from the LORD.* The verb is to find, which is what you do with
> something you were not able to manufacture — and the favour is credited to the
> same source as the wife.
