The last warning in Romans, and the last promise, in four verses.

## Who to watch for

*Mark out those who **cause divisions and lay down stumbling blocks** contrary to the
teaching you have learned, and **stay away from them**.*

Two things they do: split, and trip.

The Greek for *stumbling blocks* is **skandalon** — the trigger stick of a trap.

## How to recognise them

*They are **serving their own appetites**, and with **smooth words and flattering
speech** they deceive the hearts of the unsuspecting.*

Two marks: what they are actually serving, and how they talk.

The Greek for *smooth words* is **chrestologia** — literally *good-speech*, plausible
talk. It occurs once in the New Testament.

And they deceive *the unsuspecting* — the Greek is the innocent, those who think no
evil of anybody.

## The two kinds of knowing

*Be **wise about what is good**, and **innocent about what is evil**.*

The Greek for *innocent* is **akeraios** — unmixed, unadulterated. It is used of
undiluted wine and unalloyed metal.

Jesus says the same thing with different animals: *be wise as serpents and harmless
as doves* — the same word for harmless.

Expertise in one direction and not the other.

## The promise

*And **the God of peace will be quick to crush Satan under your feet**.*

The Hebrew original is in Genesis 3:15 — *He shall bruise your head* — and Paul is
plainly reaching for it. See the note on Genesis 3:14–15.

But note what he does with the pronouns.

The head is crushed **under your feet**, and the one doing the crushing is **God**.

Not you, and not without you. Both are in the sentence.

## "The God of peace"

The title chosen for a verse about crushing.

Paul uses it six times, and it is the name he attaches to this.

## "Quick"

The Greek is **en tachei** — shortly, speedily.

Paul was writing to a church in the capital of an empire that would kill many of
them, and the Roman church waited a long time for anything that looked like this.

## What follows immediately

*The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. Amen.*

A warning about divisive men, a promise about Satan underfoot, and then grace.

## Where the chapter is otherwise

Romans 16 is mostly a list of names — twenty-six of them greeted personally, ten of
them women, including Phoebe who probably carried the letter, and Priscilla and
Aquila who *risked their own necks* for Paul.

The warning sits in the middle of the warmest chapter he wrote.

> **Whose feet.** *The God of peace will crush Satan **under your feet**.* Paul could
> have written *under His feet* and been entirely orthodox. He wrote the other one.
