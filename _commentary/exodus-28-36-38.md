The last item in the high priest's clothing, and it exists to solve a problem
nobody usually thinks about.

## What it said

*Make a plate of pure gold and engrave on it, as on a seal: HOLY TO THE LORD.*

Worn on the forehead, tied with a blue cord to the front of the turban. The
first thing anyone saw when the high priest faced them.

## The problem it addresses

*That way Aaron carries the weight of any guilt tied to the sacred gifts the
Israelites set apart, whatever their holy offerings may be.*

Read that again. There was guilt attached to **the holy offerings themselves** —
the gifts people brought, the worship they gave. Even the best thing Israel
brought God needed something to cover it.

That is a startling admission built into the system. Not only their sins, but
their sacrifices, required a bearer.

## Who carried it

Aaron. On his forehead. Continuously — *keep it always on his forehead*.

The high priest wore the guilt of the nation's worship and the words HOLY TO THE
LORD in the same place, at the same time.

## The purpose

*So the people and their gifts are welcomed before the LORD.*

The word is about acceptance — being received with favour. The plate was not
decoration. It was the mechanism by which imperfect worship from imperfect people
was made welcome.

> **Why it matters for prayer.** Hebrews argues at length that we now have a
> great high priest who has gone into heaven itself and appears before God on our
> account. What Aaron wore on a gold plate, the letter says Christ *is* — which
> means the acceptance of what we bring never depended on the quality of the
> bringing. See the notes on Hebrews 9:23–24 and Hebrews 4:14–16.
