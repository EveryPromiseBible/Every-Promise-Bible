Paul has just described the day coming like a thief, and here is what he says about
that.

## Who the thief surprises

*But **you, brothers, are not living in the dark**, so that the day should come upon
you the way a thief comes.*

The thief image belongs to the previous paragraph, and Paul immediately says it does
not apply to them.

A thief surprises people who are asleep in the dark. His readers are described as
neither.

## What they are

*Every one of you is **a child of the light, a child of the day**.*

The Hebrew idiom — *son of* — meaning belonging to, characterised by.

*We do not belong to the night, and we do not belong to the darkness.*

Stated as fact before anything is asked. Paul's habit everywhere: the identity first,
then the instruction.

## The instruction

*So **let us not drowse** along with everyone else; **let us stay awake, and keep our
heads clear**.*

The Greek for *keep our heads clear* is **nepho** — to be sober, unintoxicated, and
by extension clear-minded.

Peter uses the same word: *be sober, be vigilant, because your adversary the devil
walks about like a roaring lion.* See the note on 1 Peter 5:8.

## The observation about night

*Sleepers do their sleeping at night, and those who drink themselves senseless do it
at night — **but we belong to the day**.*

A plain observation about how people behave, used as an argument about identity.

## The armour

*Put on **faith and love as armour across the heart**, and **the hope of salvation as
a helmet**.*

Two pieces only. Paul gives the full set in Ephesians 6; here he names two, and both
are placed over things that need protecting.

**The breastplate** covers the heart — faith and love.

**The helmet** covers the head — hope.

## What the helmet is made of

Hope. Specifically *the hope of salvation*.

The head is where the arguments happen, and Paul's protection for it is not a
technique for managing thoughts. It is an expectation about the outcome.

## What "salvation" means here

The very next verse says: *for God did not appoint us to wrath, but to obtain
salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ.* See the note on
1 Thessalonians 5:9–11.

**Soteria** — the New Testament's wide word, covering rescue, healing, wholeness,
preservation. The same root used of a woman made well and a sinking man pulled out
of the water.

## And the line after that

*Who died for us, that **whether we wake or sleep, we should live together with
Him**.*

Paul has just spent a paragraph on waking and sleeping, and then says the outcome is
the same either way.

> **What Paul says the readers already are.** *Not in darkness. Children of light.
> Not appointed to wrath.* Three things stated flatly in five verses, and the helmet
> is what keeps them in the head.
