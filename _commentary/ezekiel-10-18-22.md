The most reluctant departure in Scripture, recorded in stages by a man watching from
Babylon.

## Where Ezekiel is

Not in Jerusalem. He is by the Kebar River among the exiles, and the temple scenes in
chapters 8 to 11 are shown to him in vision.

He is watching a building he cannot see.

## The first move

*Then the glory of the LORD **moved out from the threshold of the temple** and came to
rest **above the cherubim**.*

It had already moved once — chapter 9 has it going up from the cherub *to the threshold
of the house*.

## The stages

Threshold. Then above the cherubim. Then:

*They stopped **at the entrance of the east gate** of the LORD's house, and the glory of
the God of Israel was above them.*

And in the next chapter it goes further: *the glory of the LORD went up from the midst of
the city and stood on the mountain **which is on the east side of the city**.*

Four pauses on the way out. It stops each time.

## Why the east gate matters

The Mount of Olives is east of the temple. That is where the glory settles when it goes.

It is the hill Jesus wept over the city from, and the hill of the ascension. And Ezekiel
40 has the glory returning *from the way of the east*.

## What was in the temple by then

Chapter 8 lists what Ezekiel had been shown inside: an image of jealousy at the north
gate, seventy elders burning incense to creatures carved on the walls, women weeping for
Tammuz, and twenty-five men at the door of the temple with their backs to it, worshipping
the sun.

The glory leaves a building that has been filled with other things.

## The recognition

*These were the living creatures I had seen beneath the God of Israel **by the Kebar
River**, and **I knew now that they were cherubim**.*

He had described them in chapter 1 without a name for them. Standing in the temple in
vision, he recognises them.

Cherubim were carved into the veil, embroidered on the curtains, and hammered in gold on
the mercy seat. He had grown up with the images and now sees the originals.

## The detail he repeats

*Each had **four faces and four wings**, and under their wings **the form of human
hands**.*

Hands under the wings — Ezekiel notices it twice.

## The last line

*Each one **moved straight ahead**.*

He says it in chapter 1 as well: *they did not turn when they went.*

Nothing in the departure is hesitant in its motion, however many times it stops.

## What Ezekiel does not do

He does not comment. Chapters 8 to 11 are almost entirely description, and the
evaluation is left to the reader.

## Where the glory had come from

The tabernacle at its dedication, and Solomon's temple at its dedication, when *the
priests could not continue ministering because of the cloud*.

Ezekiel watches the same glory go back out.

> **How it leaves.** Not in an instant. Threshold, cherubim, east gate, the mountain
> east of the city — and the prophet who records the way out is the one given the vision
> of it coming back the same way.
