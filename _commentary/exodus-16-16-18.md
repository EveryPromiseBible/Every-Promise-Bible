A measured ration, and the measuring produces a result nobody arranged.

## The instruction

*"Each of you gather **only what you need** — about a day's worth for every person, **an omer a head**,
according to the number in your tent."*

The Hebrew is **omer laggulgolet** — an omer per skull.

**Omer** — a dry measure, roughly two litres. The chapter ends by explaining the unit, which
suggests it had gone out of use by the time Exodus was written down.

## The basis

*According to the number in your tent.*

Household by household. Everyone counted.

## What they did

*The Israelites did just that.*

## What happened anyway

*Some gathered much, some little.*

The Hebrew is **hammarbeh vehammam'it** — the one increasing and the one diminishing.

People are not identical. Some are stronger, faster, or greedier, and the text says so.

## The measuring

*And when they measured it out by the omer.*

Only at the measuring does anything unusual appear.

## The result

*The one who had gathered much **had nothing left over**, and the one who had gathered little **had no
lack**.*

The Hebrew is **lo hetiph… lo hechsir** — did not have excess, did not lack.

## What is being described

Not a lesson in restraint. An outcome.

Effort varied and the result did not. Whatever people brought back, it came out at an omer a
head.

## The next instruction

*Moses told them, "**Don't keep any of it until morning**."*

Some did, and it bred worms and stank. See the note on Exodus 16:19–21.

The two rules run together: no advantage in gathering more, and no advantage in keeping it.

## What Paul does with the verse

*As it is written, "**He who gathered much had nothing left over, and he who gathered little had no
lack**."* See the note on 2 Corinthians 8:13–15.

He quotes it word for word into a letter about a cash collection for the poor in Jerusalem.

## What Paul is arguing

*For I do not mean that others should be eased and you burdened; **but by an equality**.*

*Your abundance may supply their lack, that their abundance also may supply your lack.*

The Greek is **isotes** — equality, fairness.

Paul reads the manna as a pattern for how money moves between congregations.

## The daily part

*So they gathered it every morning, every man according to his need.*

The Hebrew is **babboqer babboqer** — morning by morning.

The doubling is Hebrew for *every single morning*.

## What the prayer asks

*Give us **this day our daily bread**.* See the note on Matthew 6:9–13.

The Greek is **epiousion** — a word found almost nowhere else, most likely meaning *for the coming
day*.

## Where the jar ends up

*Fill an omer with it, to be kept for your generations… and Aaron laid it up **before the
Testimony**.*

One omer of the food that would not keep, kept permanently.

And in the ark, alongside the rod and the tablets. See the note on
Hebrews 9:3–5.

> **The result.** Effort varied and the outcome did not. Some gathered more and some less, and at
> the measuring it was an omer a head either way.
