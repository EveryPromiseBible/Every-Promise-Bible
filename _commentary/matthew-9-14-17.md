A question about fasting, answered with a wedding and then two pieces of household advice.

## Who asks

*Then **John's disciples** came to Him.*

Not the Pharisees. The Baptist's own men, and their teacher is in prison at this point.

## The question

*"Why is it that **we fast, and the Pharisees fast, but Your disciples do not?**"*

The law required one fast a year, on the Day of Atonement. The Pharisees fasted twice a
week.

The question is fair, and it is asked by people who are themselves fasting.

## The answer

*"How can **the friends of the bridegroom** go around **grieving while the bridegroom is
still with them**?"*

The Greek is *the sons of the bridechamber* — the wedding party.

Jewish law released the wedding guests from religious obligations for the seven days of the
feast. Their duty during that week was to make the couple glad.

He answers a question about fasting with a rule about weddings.

## Who He says He is

The bridegroom.

John the Baptist had already used the word of Him — *he who has the bride is the bridegroom;
but the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly*.

The men asking are the friend's disciples.

## The prediction

*"The day will come when **the bridegroom is taken away from them**, and then they will
fast."*

The Greek is **aparthe** — taken away, lifted off. A violent word for it.

The first hint in Matthew of how this ends.

## The patch

*"Nobody sews **a patch of unshrunk cloth onto an old coat**, because the patch **pulls away
from the coat and the tear is worse than before**."*

New cloth has not shrunk. Wash the garment and the patch contracts and takes the old fabric
with it.

The repair makes the hole bigger. That is the whole point of the image.

## The wineskins

*"And nobody pours **new wine into old wineskins**. If they do, **the skins burst, the wine
spills, and the skins are ruined**."*

New wine ferments and gives off gas. A fresh skin has give in it; an old one has already
been stretched to its limit and gone brittle.

Two things are lost in the sentence, not one — the wine and the skin.

## The alternative

*"**New wine goes into new wineskins, and both are kept safe**."*

The Greek is **suntlerountai** — preserved together. Both.

He is not against the old skin. He is saying it cannot hold this.

## What the images are answering

The question was about a religious practice. The answer says the problem is structural.

What He has brought is not an improvement to an existing arrangement. It does not go into
the old container at all, and forcing it destroys both.

## What Luke adds

*And no one, having drunk old wine, immediately desires new; for he says, "**The old is
better**."*

Luke's extra line is about taste, and it is realistic about how the change will be received.

## What He had just done

The chapter is a run of them: a paralytic forgiven and healed, Matthew called out of a tax
booth, and a dinner at Matthew's house with *many tax collectors and sinners*.

The Pharisees' complaint about that dinner is three verses before this: *why does your
Teacher eat with tax collectors and sinners?*

The answer: *I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance.*

## Where the wedding image goes

*Let us be glad and rejoice and give Him glory, for **the marriage of the Lamb has come**,
and His wife has made herself ready.*

And *blessed are those who are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb*.

The image He uses to explain why nobody is fasting is the one the last book of the Bible
ends on.

> **What the patch does.** Makes the tear worse. He answers a question about religious
> practice by describing two repairs that destroy the thing being repaired.
