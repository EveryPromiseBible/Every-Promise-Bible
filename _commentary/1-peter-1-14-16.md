Peter names them by what they are before he says anything about behaviour.

## The name

*You are **children who listen now**.*

The Greek is **tekna hupakoes** — children of obedience. A Hebrew way of saying it: children
*characterised by* listening.

He does not say *become obedient children*. He addresses them as that, and then says what
follows.

## What came before

The verse in front of this one: *gird up the loins of your mind, be sober, and **rest your
hope fully upon the grace that is to be brought to you** at the revelation of Jesus Christ.*
See the note on 1 Peter 1:13.

Hope set fully on grace, and then this.

## The instruction

*Do not let your **old cravings** press you back **into the shape you had when you knew no
better**.*

The Greek for *conforming* is **suschematizomenoi** — from **schema**, the outward form
something is pressed into.

Paul uses the same verb: *do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the
renewing of your mind*. See the note on Romans 12:1–2.

## The ignorance

*When you **knew no better**.*

The Greek is **agnoia** — the ignorance you were in.

The old shape belongs to a period that is over, and Peter dates it by what they did not
know rather than by what they did.

## The pattern

*The One who called you is holy, so **be holy yourselves — in every last thing you do**.*

The Greek is *in all your conduct* — **anastrophe**, the whole way of life.

The standard is a person, not a code. *As He who called you is holy.*

## The quotation

*God said it Himself, and it is on record: **Be holy. I am holy**.*

Leviticus, four times over — 11:44, 11:45, 19:2, 20:7.

Peter quotes the Levitical formula to Gentile believers scattered across five Roman
provinces.

## The order in the Hebrew

The Leviticus sentence gives the reason after the command: *be holy, for I am holy*.

The holiness of God is the ground, and He states it about Himself. Nothing in the clause
asks for holiness to be manufactured first.

## What the word means

**Qadosh** in Hebrew and **hagios** in Greek — set apart, separated for a use.

The vessels in the tabernacle were holy. Nothing was done to the metal; it was designated.

## What Peter says four verses later

*You were **not redeemed with corruptible things**, like silver or gold… but with **the
precious blood of Christ**, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot.*

The argument runs: you were bought, at that price, out of *your aimless conduct received by
tradition from your fathers*.

The old shape is what the price was paid to get them out of.

## And in the next chapter

*You are **a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, His own special
people**.* See the note on 1 Peter 2:9.

Holy, stated as a fact about them, in the same letter.

The command in chapter 1 and the description in chapter 2 are about the same people.

## Where the letter is going

Suffering. *Beloved, do not think it strange concerning the fiery trial which is to try
you.*

Peter is writing to *pilgrims of the Dispersion* — people whose conduct is being watched,
which is why *all your conduct* is the phrase he uses.

> **How he addresses them.** *Children who listen* — said first, before a single
> instruction. The old shape is described as belonging to a time when they did not know.
