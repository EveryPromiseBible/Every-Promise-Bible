Paul is arguing about Abraham, and his case rests on a point of chronology
anyone can check.

## The timing argument

The promise to Abraham came in Genesis 15. The law came at Sinai, four hundred
and thirty years later. Circumcision itself came a chapter after the promise, not
before it.

So whatever made Abraham an heir, it cannot have been law-keeping — the law did
not exist yet. Paul makes this argument at length through the whole chapter, and
it is unanswerable on its own terms.

## Why the two cannot be combined

*If the people of the law are the heirs, then faith has been drained of meaning
and the promise has been cancelled.*

Two strong verbs. Faith is *emptied* — made hollow, void of content. The promise
is *abolished*.

Paul's point is not that law is bad but that the two systems cannot run at once.
A promise received by faith and an inheritance earned by performance are
different kinds of thing, and running the second empties the first.

## "What the law brings about is anger"

The Greek word is wrath. And Paul gives the mechanism in the same breath: *where
there is no law there is no breaking of it.*

The law is not accused of causing sin. It is described as the thing that turns
wrongdoing into transgression — a crossing of a marked line — and so brings the
matter under judgement. He develops this at length three chapters later.

> **What the promise actually was.** *That he would inherit the world.* Genesis
> promised Abraham a land, descendants, and blessing to all nations. Paul reads
> the whole thing as one inheritance and states it at its widest — which is the
> scale he thinks the promise was always working at.
