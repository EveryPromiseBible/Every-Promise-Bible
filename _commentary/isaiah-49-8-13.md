A servant is answered, and the answer turns out to be for a great many other people.

## The timing

*"**At just the right time I answered you**. On the day of rescue I helped you."*

The Hebrew is **be'et ratzon anitika** — in a time of favour I answered you.

**Ratzon** — acceptance, good pleasure. The word used of a sacrifice being accepted.

## Who is speaking to whom

The Servant, addressed in the second half of Isaiah.

Earlier in the chapter He says *I have laboured in vain, I have spent my strength for nothing* —
and gets this answer.

## The commission

*"I will keep you and **give you as My covenant to the people**."*

The Hebrew is **livrit am** — for a covenant of the people.

Not a covenant-maker. The covenant itself, in the form of a person.

## The work

*"To **restore the land** and **hand back the places left in ruins**."*

*"To say to **the prisoners, 'Come out**,' and to **those in the dark, 'Show yourselves**.'"*

The Hebrew is **tze'u** and **higgalu** — go out, be revealed.

Two imperatives addressed to people in cells and darkness.

## The journey

*"They will **graze along the roads and find pasture on every bare hill**."*

Pasture where there is normally none. The route itself feeds them.

## The four negatives

*"They will **never hunger or thirst again**. The **scorching heat** and the **blazing sun** will not
strike them down."*

## The reason

*"For **the One who loves them will lead them and guide them to springs of water**."*

The Hebrew is **merachamam** — the one having compassion on them. From **rechem**, womb.

## The road-building

*"I will **turn all My mountains into roads**, and My highways will be **lifted up**."*

The Hebrew is **vesamti kol-harai laderek**.

Mountains become roads. Not removed — converted.

## Where they come from

*"Look, they are coming from far away. Some from **the north**, some from **the west**, and some from
**the land of Sinim**."*

**Sinim** — location disputed. Aswan in southern Egypt, or somewhere further east; some have
argued for China.

Whatever it is, it is the edge of the map.

## The call to sing

*"**Sing, O heavens, and be glad, O earth. Break into song, you mountains**."*

Three imperatives to non-human things.

## The reason given

*"For **the LORD has comforted His people**, and **He will be tender with His afflicted ones**."*

The Hebrew is **nicham** — has comforted. The verb that opens chapter 40.

## What follows immediately

*But Zion said, "**The LORD has forsaken me, and my Lord has forgotten me**."*

The heavens are told to sing, and the next verse is Zion saying she has been forgotten.

## The answer

*"**Can a woman forget her nursing child**… Surely they may forget, **yet I will not forget you**."*

*"See, **I have inscribed you on the palms of My hands**; your walls are continually before Me."* See
the note on Isaiah 49:14–16.

## What Paul does with the verse

*For He says: "**In an acceptable time I have heard you, and in the day of salvation I have helped
you**." Behold, **now is the accepted time**; behold, now is the day of salvation.* See the note on
2 Corinthians 6:1–2.

He quotes verse 8 and applies the word *now* to his readers.

> covenant, in the form of a person.
