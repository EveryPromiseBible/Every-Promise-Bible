Written by a man who had seen Him, to people who had not.

## Who is writing

Peter. He had walked with Him for three years, watched the transfiguration, denied Him in a
courtyard and eaten breakfast with Him on a beach.

He mentions it in his second letter: *we were eyewitnesses of His majesty… we heard this voice
which came from heaven when we were with Him on the holy mountain*.

He knows exactly what he is saying here.

## The first

*You have **never laid eyes on Him, and you love Him**.*

The Greek is **ouk idontes agapate** — not having seen, you love.

## The second

*You **cannot see Him now either, and you trust Him anyway**.*

The Greek is **arti me horontes, pisteuontes de** — not seeing at present, yet believing.

Two clauses, and the second is about the present. Not only did they miss the three years; they
cannot see Him this morning either.

## What Jesus said about it

*"Thomas, because you have seen Me, you have believed. **Blessed are those who have not seen and
yet have believed**."*

Said to a man who had insisted on the nail prints.

Peter is describing the people that sentence was about.

## The joy

*And it makes you **so happy you cannot get it into words**.*

The Greek is **chara aneklaleto** — joy unspeakable. **Aneklaletos** occurs once in the New
Testament.

*There is **glory in that gladness**.*

The Greek is **dedoxasmene** — glorified. A perfect participle: the joy has been glorified and
stays so.

## Who these people were

*Pilgrims of the Dispersion in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia.*

Scattered, under pressure, and the letter is about suffering. The paragraph immediately before
this is about gold in a fire.

The joy is described in a letter written to people having a hard time, and the writer does not
suggest the two are in tension.

## The receiving

*And you are **already collecting** what all the trusting was aiming at the whole time.*

The Greek is **komizomenoi** — receiving, carrying off. A present participle: it is happening
now.

Not at the end. In progress.

## The outcome

*The **end of your faith — the salvation of your souls**.*

The Greek is **telos** — the goal, the completion, what a thing was for.

Faith is described as having an aim, and the aim is arriving.

## What comes next

*Of this salvation **the prophets have inquired and searched carefully**, who prophesied of the
grace that would come to you… searching what, or what manner of time, the Spirit of Christ who
was in them was indicating.*

And: *to them it was revealed that **not to themselves, but to us, they were ministering**…
things which **angels desire to look into**.*

The Greek for *look into* is **parakupsai** — to stoop and peer, the word for the disciples
bending to look into the empty tomb.

Prophets searching and angels craning to see, and the readers have it.

## What the letter opens with

*Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who according to His abundant mercy has
begotten us again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead.*

> **Who wrote it.** The man who had seen Him. He is telling people who never will that not
> seeing does not put them behind — and the prophets and the angels are the ones straining to
> look.
