Job's last speech, and it is six verses long. He has been talking for thirty
chapters and this is what he has left.

## He quotes the question back

*"You asked, '**Who is this who hides My counsel without knowledge?**'"*

God had put it as a question in chapter 38 and Job repeats it word for word.

## The answer

*"**It was me.**"*

Two words. There is no negotiation, no *but*, no explanation of the
circumstances.

He does not plead the ash heap, the ten graves, the boils or the friends. He
answers the question that was asked.

## What he admits

*"I spoke of things I did not understand, wonders far too great for me, things I
did not know."*

Not that he sinned in a way that caused the disaster — that was the friends'
charge and it was never true, and God will say so in five verses' time.

What he concedes is that he talked about something he could not see, and formed a
verdict on the One running it.

## What he stops doing

The last thing he says in the passage that follows is *I abhor myself, and repent
in dust and ashes*, and the Hebrew of that first phrase is difficult — it can be
read *I retract*, or *I melt away*.

Either way the case is withdrawn. Chapter 29 was a man wrapping himself in his own
righteousness; here he takes it off. See the note on Job 29:14–17.

## The sentence in the middle

*"Now I know that **You can do all things**, and that **no purpose of Yours can
ever be stopped**."*

He has agreed God is right, and the first thing he says after it is about God's
ability rather than his own failure.

He never learns why. Not one word about the accuser, the throne room, or the
wager, then or ever.

## What actually changed him

*"I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, **but now my eye sees You**."*

Not an argument. Four chapters of ostriches and rainstorms and wild goats calving,
and a Person on the other side of them.

The three friends had reasoned at him for twenty-five chapters and hardened him.
He sees Him once and the whole defence drops.

> **What comes immediately after.** Not a rebuke. God turns to Eliphaz and calls
> Job *My servant* four times in two verses, and tells the three friends that Job
> is the one who spoke rightly of Him. See the note on Job 42:7–8.
