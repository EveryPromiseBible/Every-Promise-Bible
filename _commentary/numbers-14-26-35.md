God answers the complaint, and the answer is the complaint.

## What He heard

*"**I have heard every murmur they raise against Me.**"*

The Hebrew for *murmur* is the grumbling word used all through the wilderness
accounts. It is not shouted; it is muttered among themselves.

He reports having heard all of it.

## The sentence

*"As surely as I live, declares the LORD, **I will do to you the very things you
spoke in My hearing**."*

Then He quotes them: *"In this wilderness your bodies will fall."*

Go back one page. Their words the night before were *if only we had died in Egypt —
or here in this wilderness!*

He gives them exactly what they asked for, in their own phrasing, and says so
explicitly.

## Who is excepted

*"**Except Caleb son of Jephunneh and Joshua son of Nun.**"*

The two who said something else. Caleb: *let's go up at once and take it — we can
certainly do it.* Joshua, with him, tearing his clothes: *the land is exceedingly
good.*

Two men out of a nation, and the difference between them and everybody else is what
came out of their mouths about the same country.

## The children

*"As for your children — **the ones you said would be taken as plunder** — I will
bring them in, and they will **know the land you rejected**."*

He repeats their own fear back to them and reverses it in the same sentence.

The generation that was afraid for their children lost the land; the children got
it.

## The arithmetic

*"**For each of the forty days you explored the land, you will bear your guilt a
year** — forty years."*

A day for a year. The wandering is measured against the reconnaissance — the walk
they took and then would not finish.

## Forty years of shepherding

*"Your children will be shepherds here for forty years, bearing the weight of your
unfaithfulness **until the last of your bodies lies in the wilderness**."*

The young ones spend their twenties and thirties burying the generation that would
not go in.

## What is not in the passage

Any suggestion the giants got stronger, or that the land became harder to take. It
was as takeable the next morning as it had been the day before.

Joshua and Caleb walked into it forty years later, and Caleb at eighty-five asked
for the hill country with the Anakim on it — *give me this mountain.* See the note
on Joshua 14:6–15.

The same giants. A different man's report about them.

## The rest they did not enter

*So I swore in My wrath, "**They shall not enter My rest**."*

Hebrews quotes it and then does something with it that Numbers does not: it says the offer is
still open.

*There remains therefore **a rest for the people of God**. For he who has entered His rest has
himself also **ceased from his works**, as God did from His.*

The wilderness generation did not fail to work hard enough. They died in a desert because they
would not walk into something already given.

## What kept them out, in one word

*And to whom did He swear that they would not enter His rest, but to those who did not obey? So
we see that **they could not enter in because of unbelief**.*

Not the giants. Not the walls. Not the forty days of reconnaissance.

> **What God said before all this.** *"I have forgiven them, as you asked."* Moses
> had interceded and the forgiveness is granted in verse 20, five verses before the
> sentence. Forgiven, and still not going in — the consequence and the pardon are
> both in the same paragraph.
