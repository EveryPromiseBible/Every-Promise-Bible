Only Matthew records this, and every detail in it is worth its own line.

## The request

*"Lord, **if it is You**, tell me to come to You on the water."*

He asks for a command rather than permission. *Tell me to come.*

Whatever else Peter is doing, he is not proposing to try it on his own initiative.

## The answer

*"**Come.**"*

One word.

## What he did

*So Peter **climbed down out of the boat, walked on the water**, and started toward
Jesus.*

He is the only person besides Jesus recorded as doing it, and the fact usually gets
lost behind what happened next.

Eleven men stayed in the boat.

## What changed

*But when he **felt the force of the wind** he was afraid.*

The wind had been blowing the whole time. It did not get worse when he stepped out.

The variable is what he was attending to.

## The sinking

*And **beginning to sink** he cried out, "**Lord, save me!**"*

Three words. It is one of the shortest prayers in the Bible, and it works.

He does not swim for the boat. He calls.

## The speed

*Immediately Jesus **reached out His hand and caught hold of him**.*

The Greek is *immediately*, and the verb is to take hold of firmly.

He does not let him go under first to make a point.

## The question

*"**You of little faith, why did you doubt?**"*

**Oligopistos** — Matthew's word, used five times, always to disciples.

And the Greek for *doubt* is **distazo**, to be pulled two ways, to waver between.

Said while holding him up out of the water.

## Where the faith failed

Not in the asking, and not in the stepping out. He walked.

The question is asked of a man who is being lifted back on to the surface, about the
part in the middle.

## The wind

*And when **the two of them climbed into the boat, the wind died away**.*

Matthew notes that they got in together.

## What the boat did

*Then the men in the boat **worshipped Him** and said, "**Truly You are the Son of
God**."*

The first time in Matthew that the disciples say it.

They had watched five thousand fed that afternoon and concluded nothing. A man
sinking and being caught produced the confession.

> **What Peter got wrong and what he got right.** He asked, he got out, he looked at
> the weather, and he sank. Of the twelve men on that lake, he is the only one who was
> ever on the water — and the only one who ever needed to be pulled out of it.
