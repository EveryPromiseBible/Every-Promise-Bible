Peter is writing to Christians under real persecution, and he stops to draw one
distinction.

## The list

*Just make sure none of you is suffering for being **a murderer, a thief, a
crook, or someone who cannot stay out of other people's business**.*

Three crimes and then a fourth item that is not a crime at all.

The Greek behind the last one is **allotriepiskopos** — a word that appears
nowhere else in Greek literature, and Peter may have coined it. It is built from
*belonging to another* and *overseer*: a person who supervises what is not theirs.

## Why it is on that list

He puts a busybody beside a murderer and a thief. It is startling, and it is not a
slip — the sentence is short and every item in it was chosen.

Interfering in another person's affairs produces suffering, and Peter classes the
suffering it produces with the suffering a thief brings on himself.

## What he is separating

The verse before: *if you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed.*
The verse after: *but if anyone suffers as a Christian, let him not be ashamed.*

So there are two kinds of trouble a believer can be in, and Peter will not let
them be confused. One is because of Christ. The other is because of you.

## And a third kind

Neither category covers Job.

He was not suffering for the name, and he was not suffering for anything he had
done — chapter 1 says so and God says so out loud. His friends spent twenty-five
chapters insisting it had to be the second kind, because their system had no room
for anything else.

Peter's distinction is real and it does not exhaust the possibilities. A man can
be in the ashes for neither reason.

## What Peter tells them to do

*Let those who suffer according to the will of God **commit their souls to Him in
doing good, as to a faithful Creator**.*

The Greek for *commit* is a banking word — to deposit something with someone for
safekeeping. It is the verb Jesus used on the cross: *into Your hands I commit My
spirit.*

> **The name he gives God in that sentence.** *A faithful Creator* — the only place
> in the New Testament God is called that. Peter reaches for the word for the One
> who made the thing, writing to people whose lives are being taken apart.
