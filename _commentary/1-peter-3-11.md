The second half of a quotation, and both verbs in it are active.

## Where it comes from

Psalm 34, quoted whole across two verses.

*Who among you loves being alive, who wants long days full of good? Then **keep your tongue from
evil**… **go after peace, chase it down**.* See the note on Psalms 34:11–14.

David wrote it after escaping Gath by feigning madness.

## The first half

*He who would love life and see good days, **let him refrain his tongue from evil**, and his lips
from speaking deceit.* See the note on 1 Peter 3:10.

## The turn

*Let him **turn away from evil and do good**.*

The Greek is **ekklinato de apo kakou kai poiesato agathon**.

**Ekklino** — to turn aside, to swerve away from.

Two verbs, and the second is not implied by the first. Turning away from evil does not
automatically produce good.

## The chase

*Let him **seek peace and pursue it**.*

The Greek is **zetesato eirenen kai dioxato auten**.

**Zeteo** — to seek, to look for.

**Dioko** — to pursue, to chase, to hunt. The verb used of persecution: *blessed are those who are
**persecuted** for righteousness' sake*.

## What the second verb adds

Seeking is looking. Pursuing is going after something that is getting away.

Peter keeps both from the psalm, and the escalation is the point.

## Who is being told this

Believers scattered across five Roman provinces, under pressure, whom Peter has just told:

*Finally, all of you be of one mind, having compassion for one another; love as brothers, be
tenderhearted, be courteous.*

*Not returning evil for evil or reviling for reviling, but on the contrary blessing.*

## Why the psalm

Because David wrote it while a fugitive in enemy territory, having escaped by pretending to be
mad.

Peter quotes a man who knew what it cost.

## What follows the quotation

*For **the eyes of the LORD are on the righteous, and His ears are open to their prayers**; but the
face of the LORD is against those who do evil.*

Also from Psalm 34, verse 15, continuing the quotation.

## The question

*And **who is he who will harm you if you become followers of what is good?**"*

The Greek is **kakoson** — will harm.

## The concession

*But even if you should suffer for righteousness' sake, **you are blessed**.*

Peter asks the rhetorical question and then answers it honestly. Usually nobody. Sometimes
somebody.

*And do not be afraid of their threats, nor be troubled.* See the note on
1 Peter 3:14.

## The instruction that follows

*But sanctify the Lord God in your hearts, and **always be ready to give a defence** to everyone who
asks you a reason for the hope that is in you, **with meekness and fear**.*

The Greek is **apologian** — a defence, the root of *apologetics*.

And the manner is specified: **meta prautetos kai phobou** — with gentleness and reverence.

## What Paul says

*If it is possible, **as much as depends on you, live peaceably with all men**.* See the note on
Romans 12:17–21.

Two qualifications in one clause: *if possible*, and *as much as depends on you*.

> **The two verbs.** Seek, and then pursue. Peter keeps the escalation from the psalm — peace is
> something you have to chase, which assumes it is moving.
