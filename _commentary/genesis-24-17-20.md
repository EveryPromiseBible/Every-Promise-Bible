She offers something nobody asked for, and it takes her most of the evening.

## The request

*The servant **hurried to meet her**. "Please," he said, "let me have **a little water from your
jar**."*

The Hebrew is **me'at-mayim** — a little water.

A small ask. Anyone at a well would grant it.

## The answer

*"**Drink, my lord**," she said, and **quickly lowered the jar into her hands** and gave him a
drink.*

The Hebrew is **vattemaher** — and she hastened.

The jar comes down off the shoulder into her hands so he can drink from it. She holds it for him.

## The offer

*When he had finished, she said, "**I'll draw water for your camels too, until they've had
enough**."*

The Hebrew is **ad im-killu lishtot** — until they have finished drinking.

## What she has just volunteered for

Ten camels, off a five-hundred-mile crossing.

A thirsty camel takes twenty to thirty gallons. The jar she is carrying holds a few. The well is
below her and the trough is beside it.

She has committed herself to something like two hundred gallons, drawn by hand, in the evening,
for a stranger.

## The sign

The servant had prayed: *let it be that the young woman to whom I say, "Please let down your
pitcher," and she says, "Drink, and I will also give your camels a drink"*. See the note on
Genesis 24:12–14.

The exact sentence, unprompted.

## What kind of sign it was

Not arbitrary.

He was looking for a wife for Isaac. The test he devised identified generosity that goes past
what is asked, and stamina, and willingness to serve somebody with no claim on her.

The sign is the character.

## The work

*So she **quickly emptied her jar into the trough**, then **ran back to the well again and again**,
drawing for all his camels.*

The Hebrew has three verbs of speed in four verses: **vattemaher** twice, and **vattaratz** — and
she ran.

## The repetition

*Again and again.*

The Hebrew is **vattaratz od el-habber lish'ov** — and she ran again to the well to draw.

The text does not compress it. She goes back, and back, and back.

## What is not said

That she complained, slowed, or asked who he was.

The questions come later, from him.

## What he does while she works

*The man **watched her in silence**.* See the note on Genesis 24:21–23.

He does not help. He does not explain. He watches.

## Why he does not speak

He is checking whether the sign completes. Interrupting would spoil the test.

The Hebrew says he was **mishtaeh** — gazing, wondering — and the verb suggests astonishment held
in.

## What she gets

*He took out **a gold nose ring** weighing half a shekel and **two gold bracelets** weighing ten
shekels.*

Given before he knows who she is, and before any negotiation.

## What she says next

*"I am the daughter of Bethuel, Milcah's son, whom she bore to Nahor."*

And: *"We have both straw and feed enough, and room to lodge."*

More hospitality, offered again without being asked.

## What her family sees

*So it came to pass, when he saw the nose ring, and the bracelets on his sister's wrists… that he
went to the man.*

Laban's first recorded reaction to anything is jewellery.

> **What she volunteered for.** Two hundred gallons, drawn by hand, at the end of the day, for a
> man she had never seen — and the servant had prayed for exactly that sentence.
