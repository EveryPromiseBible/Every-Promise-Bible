The refrain of the psalm, and the two verbs under it.

## The refrain

*Let them **thank the LORD for His steadfast love**, for the wonders He works for the children
of men.*

Said four times in Psalm 107 — after the lost in the desert, the prisoners, the sick and the
men in the storm. Four different rescues, one identical sentence.

**Chesed** — steadfast love, covenant loyalty. It is what the psalm says every one of the four
groups was rescued by, and none of them is described as deserving it.

## The two souls

*He **fills up the thirsty soul**, and the hungry soul **He loads with good things**.*

The Hebrew for *loads* is **mille** — fills to capacity. Neither verb is about a portion; both
are about a container being filled.

## Who says the same thing

*"**Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they shall be filled.**"*

**Jesus**' word is **chortasthesontai** — the verb for feeding animals until they can eat no
more. The qualification in His sentence is the appetite, not the merit.

*"**He has filled the hungry with good things**, and the rich He has sent away empty."*

Mary sings it in the Magnificat, and Luke's Greek is the Greek Old Testament's rendering of
this verse.

## Who fills it

*"**I am the bread of life. He who comes to Me shall never hunger**, and he who believes in Me
shall never thirst."*

And *"if anyone thirsts, let him come to Me and drink."*

Two of the four groups in Psalm 107 are hungry and thirsty. **Jesus** answers both in the first
person.

## What the whole psalm is for

*Let **the redeemed of the LORD say so**.* See the note on Psalms 107:1–3.

The four rescues are told so that the people they happened to would talk about them.

> **The two verbs.** Fills, and loads. Neither is a measured portion — both describe a
> container being filled up, and the only qualification named anywhere in the psalm is that
> the soul was empty.
