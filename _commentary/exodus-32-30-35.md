The greatest intercession in the Old Testament, and it does not work.

## The word he uses

**32:30** *"But now I will go up to the LORD. **Perhaps** I can make atonement for what you
have done."*

**Perhaps.** He does not know whether it can be done, and he says so before he
climbs.

That single word is the distance between Moses and the One he is a picture of.

## What he offers

**32:32** *"And yet — **if you would only forgive their sin**. But **if not, then blot me out of
the book you have written**."*

The sentence breaks off mid-thought in the Hebrew — *if You will forgive their
sin—* and then nothing. He cannot finish it.

And then the offer: erase me instead.

It is the same instinct Paul had, **Romans 9:3**: *I could wish that I myself were accursed from
Christ for my brethren, my countrymen according to the flesh.* Both men offer, and
neither is able.

## The answer

**32:33** *"**Whoever has sinned against Me, that one I will blot out of My book.**"*

The offer is declined. A man cannot substitute for another man's sin; God says so
plainly.

Moses is willing and not qualified.

## What is given instead

**32:34** *"Now go, lead the people to the place I told you of. **My angel will go before
you**."*

An angel rather than His presence, and Moses will spend the next chapter arguing
about exactly that, **Exodus 33:15**: *if Your Presence does not go with us, do not bring us up from
here.*

## The last line

**32:35**: *And the LORD struck the people because of what they had done with the calf.*

The chapter ends there. No resolution, no forgiveness pronounced, a plague, and a
deferred reckoning, **32:34** again: *when the day of reckoning comes, I will hold them to account.*

## The comparison the sermon is built on

Moses came down from forty days on the mountain to a golden calf, broke the tablets,
went back up with a *perhaps*, and came down again with a face so bright he had to
veil it.

Jesus came out of forty days in the wilderness and, **Luke 4:14**: *returned in the power of the
Spirit*, and went straight to a synagogue in Nazareth to read Isaiah 61 out loud.

Moses ended his forty days with a plague on the people. Jesus ended His forty days
after the resurrection with His hands raised in blessing, and, **Luke 24:51**: *while He blessed them,
He was parted from them.*

## What Moses could not say

*Perhaps.*

What was said from the cross was **tetelestai** — it is finished. One word, and no
conditional in it. See the note on John 19:28–30.

> **What Moses got right.** He stood between God and a people who deserved nothing,
> and offered himself. The offer was refused because he was not the one who could
> make it — but the shape of it is exactly right, and somebody else made it later and
> was accepted.
