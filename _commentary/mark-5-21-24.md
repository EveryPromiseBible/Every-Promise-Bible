A synagogue ruler on the ground in front of an itinerant preacher, in public.

## Who he is

*"**One of the leaders of the synagogue** came, a man named Jairus."*

**Archisynagogos** — the official responsible for the building, the services and who was allowed to
speak in them.

He is, in his own town, the man who would decide whether **Jesus** was permitted to teach. **Mark**
has already recorded the Pharisees taking counsel with the Herodians *how they might destroy Him*.

## The name

**Mark** gives it. He rarely names the people **Jesus** heals — the leper, the paralytic, the woman
with the haemorrhage are all anonymous — and **Jairus** is one of the few exceptions.

The Hebrew behind it is **Ya'ir** — *he will enlighten*, or *he will awaken*.

## What he does

*"When he saw Jesus **he fell down at His feet** and begged Him over and over."*

**Piptei pros tous podas autou** — he falls at His feet. And **parekalei polla** — he kept on
begging.

An official prostrate in the road in front of the crowd he presides over on Saturdays.

*"**Blessed are the ones who have nothing to bring**, who come with empty hands and know it."* See
the note on Matthew 5:3–12.

The desperation is the qualification, and it is the only one in the story.

## What he asks

*"**My little daughter is dying.** Please, come and lay Your hands on her, so she will live."*

The Greek is **to thygatrion mou** — a diminutive. My little girl.

**Mark** tells us at the end that she was twelve years old — the same number of years the woman in
the next paragraph had been bleeding. He puts the two stories inside each other on purpose, and the
number is the hinge.

## What He does

*"**So Jesus went with him.**"*

Four words. No question about the man's theology, his colleagues, or what the synagogue had been
saying.

*"**The one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out.**"*

## The crowd

*"And a great crowd followed along, **pressing in on Him from every side**."*

The Greek is **synethlibon** — squeezing, crushing.

Which is the setup for the interruption. A woman in that crowd touches His clothes, He stops, and
while He is talking to her the message arrives that the girl has died. See the note on
Mark 5:30–34.

## What He says when the news comes

*"**Do not be afraid; only believe.**"*

**Me phobou, monon pisteue.** The present imperative — keep on believing.

Jairus is told this after the death, not before it. What he asked for was a healing; what he is
told to go on believing in is something he had not asked for.

*Now believing is the substance of things hoped for, **the evidence of things not seen**.*

*"**Talitha, koumi**" — "Little girl, I say to you, arise."*

**Mark** keeps the Aramaic. It is two words in the language the family spoke, said in a room He had
cleared of everybody except the parents and three disciples.

> **What the official does.** Falls on his face in the street, in front of the congregation he leads,
> and asks. Mark does not record him being questioned about anything before *so Jesus went with
> him*.
