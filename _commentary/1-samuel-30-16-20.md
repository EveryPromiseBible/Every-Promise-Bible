The battle, and the accounting afterwards is the point of the passage.

## The scene

*He led David down, and there they were — **spread out across the whole land, eating, drinking, and
reveling** over the great haul of plunder.*

The Hebrew is **netushim al-penei kol-ha'aretz** — scattered over the face of all the land.

**Chogegim** — celebrating, keeping festival.

No perimeter, no watch. They are having a party in open country.

## Where the plunder came from

*From **Philistia and from Judah**.*

They had raided both sides. Nobody's territory was exempt.

## The attack

*David fell on them **from twilight until the evening of the next day**.*

The Hebrew is **minnesheph ve'ad-ha'erev lemocharatam**.

Roughly twenty-four hours of fighting, by four hundred men who had already marched for days.

## The escape

*Not one of them escaped, **except four hundred young men who leapt onto camels and fled**.*

The narrator counts them. Four hundred escaped from a force large enough that four hundred
escaping is a footnote.

## The recovery

*David **recovered everything the Amalekites had taken** — and **rescued his two wives**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyatzel David et kol-asher laqechu Amaleq**.

Ahinoam and Abigail, named in the chapter's opening as taken.

## The inventory

*Nothing was missing, **small or great, son or daughter, plunder or anything they'd carried off**.*

The Hebrew is **velo nedar lahem min-haqqaton ve'ad-haggadol**.

Four categories, and the narrator itemises them because the point is completeness.

## The word

*David **brought it all back**.*

The Hebrew is **hakkol heshiv David** — the whole, David returned.

## What was promised

*"Chase them. You will surely overtake them, and **you will surely rescue them all**."* See the note
on 1 Samuel 30:7–10.

The infinitive absolute doubled, and the result matches it clause for clause.

## The extra

*He took **all the flocks and herds**, and his men drove them ahead of the other livestock, calling
out, "**This is David's plunder!**"*

The Hebrew is **zeh shelal David**.

Livestock the Amalekites had taken from somewhere else. It was not David's before the raid.

He goes out to recover what was stolen and comes back with more than left.

## What he does with it

Sends portions to the elders of Judah — to Bethel, Ramoth, Jattir, Aroer, and a dozen other towns
listed by name.

*"**Here is a present for you from the spoil of the enemies of the LORD**."*

## The other dispute

Two hundred men had stayed at the Besor brook, too exhausted to cross.

*"They shall not share in the spoil."*

*But David said, "**You shall not do so, my brethren**… as his part is who goes down to the battle,
so shall his part be who stays by the supplies; **they shall share alike**."*

*So it was, from that day forward; **he made it a statute and an ordinance for Israel**.*

A national law, made about men who could not cross a stream.

## What is happening elsewhere

The next chapter: the battle on Mount Gilboa, and Saul and Jonathan dead.

*The beauty of Israel is slain on your high places! **How the mighty have fallen!***

David recovers everything on the same days Saul loses everything.

## Nothing missing

*And **nothing of theirs was lacking**, either small or great, sons or daughters, spoil or
anything which they had taken from them; **David recovered all**.*

The inventory is spelled out so nobody can wonder. Not most of it. Not the people but not the
property. All.

## And more than all

*They had also taken away the sheep and the cattle… and they said, "**This is David's
spoil**."*

He went out to get back what was stolen and came home with more than had been taken.

*"I have come that they may have life, and that they may **have it more abundantly**,"* Jesus
said — in the same breath as calling Himself the good shepherd.

*Now to Him who is able to do **exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think**.*

## Who gets a share

The two hundred who were too exhausted to cross the brook, and the men who fought want to cut
them out.

*"**As his part is who goes down to the battle, so shall his part be who stays by the
supplies**; they shall share alike."*

And David makes it a statute in Israel from that day.

> **The extra.** He went out to get back what was taken and came home with flocks that had never
> been his — and the first thing he did with them was send portions to towns in Judah.
