The turning point of the flood account is a verb about memory.

## "God remembered"

*But **God remembered Noah**, and all the wild animals and livestock with him in
the ark.*

The Hebrew is **zakar**. It does not mean God had forgotten — it is the word for
acting on a commitment, turning attention toward someone in order to do something
about them.

It is used of God remembering His covenant with Abraham when Israel groaned in
Egypt, and of Him remembering Hannah, and Rachel.

Every time it appears, something moves immediately afterwards.

## What moved

*And God sent **a wind** over the earth, and the waters began to subside.*

The Hebrew for wind is **ruach** — also breath, also Spirit. The same word as
Genesis 1:2, where *the Spirit of God hovered over the face of the waters.*

A world under water, and a wind moving over it. Genesis is deliberately repeating
its own opening.

## The closing

*The springs of the deep and the floodgates of the heavens **were closed**.*

The two sources that were split open in chapter 7 are shut. Both directions, in
reverse order.

## The date

*On **the seventeenth day of the seventh month**, the ark came to rest on the
mountains of Ararat.*

Hold that date. The flood began on the seventeenth day of the second month.

Israel's calendar was reset at the exodus — *this month shall be your beginning of
months* — which moved the seventh month to first place. The seventeenth of that
month is three days after Passover on the fourteenth.

Three days after the lamb is killed, the ark comes to rest.

## "Came to rest"

The Hebrew verb is **nuach** — to rest, to settle. It is the root of **Noah**'s own
name.

The man called rest, in a boat that finally rests, on the seventeenth day.

## Ararat

The mountains of Ararat, in the region of ancient Urartu.

The ark does not land in a valley or on a shore. It comes to rest on high ground,
above everything the water had covered.

## What Noah does about it

Nothing, for months. The waters *kept receding until, on the first day of the tenth
month, the tops of the mountains appeared* — and he waits, and sends a raven, and
then a dove, three times.

He does not open the door. He did not close it either. See the note on
Genesis 7:11–16.

> **The first thing God did in this chapter.** Remembered. Not calculated, not
> relented, not reconsidered. The Hebrew word for what turns the flood is the word
> for keeping somebody in mind.
