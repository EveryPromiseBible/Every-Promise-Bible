Her first direct question, and it is about where to find Him in the middle of the day.

## The address

*Tell me, **you whom my soul loves**.*

The phrase runs through the book. She uses it four times in chapter 3 alone, looking for Him
in the streets at night.

She does not use His name. The Hebrew is *whom my soul loves* — **she'ahavah naphshi**.

## The occupation

*Where do you **graze your flock**?*

He is a shepherd in her question. The book has already had Him as a king with chariots and
as a man in a garden.

## The hour

*Where do you **rest them in the heat of noon**?*

The Hebrew is *where do you make them lie down at noon* — **tarbits**, the causative of the
verb for lying down.

Noon is when a Palestinian flock cannot graze. The shepherd's job at midday is not to lead
them to food but to get them into shade and make them stop.

Same verb, same idea, in the psalm: *He **makes me to lie down** in green pastures*. See the
note on Psalms 23:1–3.

## What she is asking for

Not the pasture. The resting place.

## The reason

*Why should I **wander veiled** among the flocks of your companions?*

The Hebrew is difficult. **Ke'otyah** can mean *as one veiled* or, with a small change,
*as one who wanders*.

A veiled woman beside the flocks of other men is a picture with an edge to it — Tamar
covered her face by the roadside in Genesis 38, and Judah took her for a prostitute.

She is saying: tell me where you are, so I am not looking for you among everybody else's
sheep.

## The friends' answer

*If you do not know, loveliest of women, then **follow the tracks of the flock**.* See the
note on Song of Songs 1:8.

## Where the noon question is answered

Later in the book: *my beloved has gone down to his garden, to the beds of spices, to feed
his flock in the gardens and to gather lilies.*

And *I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine; he feeds his flock among the lilies.*

## What the book is

Eight chapters of dialogue with no narrator, no plot, and no mention of God by name.

It was read at Passover, and Rabbi Akiva said of it that all the writings are holy but the
Song of Songs is the Holy of Holies.

## The question underneath the question

She knows he is a shepherd and she knows he has a flock. What she does not know is the
location.

Everything she asks in the book is some version of *where*.

> **What she asks for.** Not the grazing ground — the place he makes them lie down when the
> sun is at its worst. The same verb the psalm uses of green pastures.
