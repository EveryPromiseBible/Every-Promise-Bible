Paul builds an argument twice, in the same shape, and the shape is the point.

## The form

*If, while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of His
Son, **how much more**, now that we have been reconciled, shall we be saved by
His life.*

It is an argument from the harder case to the easier one. If the difficult thing
has already been done under the worst conditions, the lesser thing is not in
doubt.

He uses it twice in three verses, and it runs all through this chapter.

## What the harder case was

*While we were enemies.*

Paul has already said it two verses earlier — *while we were still sinners,
Christ died for us* — and he keeps escalating the description: weak, ungodly,
sinners, enemies.

The reconciliation happened at the worst point of the relationship. That is the
foundation the whole argument stands on.

## "Saved by His life"

The contrast is deliberate. The reconciling was accomplished by His **death**;
the saving that follows is by His **life**.

He is not dead now, and Paul treats that as the guarantee of everything still to
come. Hebrews makes the same move — *He always lives to intercede for them.*

## The odd word at the end

*We rejoice in God through our Lord Jesus Christ.*

The verb is the boasting word. Paul has spent two chapters demolishing every
ground for human boasting, and then permits exactly one: boasting **in God**.

Not in the salvation, or the security, or the change in circumstances. In Him.

> **What has already been said in this chapter.** *Since we have been put right by
> faith, we have peace with God… and we stand in this grace.* Standing is the
> posture Paul assigns to a person who has received it — which is why the *how
> much more* argument can be made at all.
