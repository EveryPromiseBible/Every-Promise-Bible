An army is drawn up, and the man who steps out in front of it is not the general.

## Who speaks

*"As the battle draws near, **the priest is to step forward and speak to the army**."*

Not the commander, not the king, and not Joshua. A priest — a man whose whole training is
sacrifice and blood, standing in front of soldiers.

Deuteronomy calls him *the priest anointed for war*. The officers speak afterwards, about who
may go home; the first word belongs to him.

## What he says first

*"**Listen, Israel.**"*

**Shema Yisrael** — the two words that open the sentence Israel says twice a day. See the note
on Deuteronomy 6:1–3.

The battle speech begins the same way the creed does.

## The four negatives

*"**Do not lose heart. Do not be afraid, do not tremble, do not panic** before them."*

Four, stacked. The Hebrew piles up verbs for fear — soften, dread, hurry, be terrified — and
denies each one.

Nothing in the four is about tactics, numbers or terrain.

## The reason

*"**The LORD your God goes with you — He will fight for you** against your enemies, and **He
will save you**."*

Three clauses, and the subject of all three is God. Goes with, fights for, saves.

The Hebrew for *save* is **hoshia** — from **yasha**, the root behind **Yeshua**. See the note
on Exodus 14:10–14.

## What the soldiers are told to do

Nothing, in this speech. The instructions come later, from the officers, and they are mostly
about who is excused: a man with a new house, a new vineyard, a new wife, or a fearful heart.

*If God is for us, who can be against us?*

## The one they were fighting toward

*"For the LORD your God is He who goes with you, **to fight for you against your enemies, to
save you**."*

*Having disarmed principalities and powers, He made a public spectacle of them, **triumphing
over them** in it.*

Paul says that about the cross, and the battle language in it is deliberate — a victory
procession, over enemies already stripped.

> **Who gives the speech.** Not the general. A priest, whose trade is blood, telling an army
> four times not to be afraid and giving one reason: the LORD your God goes with you.
