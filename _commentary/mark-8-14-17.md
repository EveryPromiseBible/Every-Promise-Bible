A warning about leaven, misheard as a complaint about catering.

## The setting

*"The disciples had forgotten to bring bread along. **All they had in the boat was one loaf.**"*

**Mark** puts the detail in deliberately, and it is funny: there is one loaf in the boat with them,
and the man sitting there had fed four thousand people with seven a few hours earlier.

## The warning

*"**Keep your eyes open. Watch out for the yeast of the Pharisees, and for the yeast of Herod.**"*

**Blepete, horate** — two verbs for looking, stacked.

Leaven in Scripture is almost always a picture of something small that spreads and changes the whole
batch. It is what had to be cleared out of the house before Passover.

*Your glorying is not good. Do you not know that **a little leaven leavens the whole lump**?*

*A little leaven leavens the whole lump.*

**Paul** uses the proverb twice — in **1 Corinthians** about tolerated sin, and in **Galatians** about
adding circumcision to the gospel. The second is closer to what **Jesus** means here.

## What each leaven is

**Matthew** identifies the Pharisees' as *the teaching of the Pharisees and Sadducees*. **Luke** calls
it *hypocrisy*.

Herod's is the other kind: power, expediency, the man who beheaded a prophet because of an oath made
at a party.

Both had just asked **Jesus** for something. The Pharisees, in the paragraph immediately before,
*began to dispute with Him, seeking from Him a sign from heaven, testing Him.*

*But He sighed deeply in His spirit, and said, "Why does this generation seek a sign? Assuredly, I
say to you, **no sign shall be given to this generation**."*

## What they hear

*"They started talking it over among themselves, saying, '**He says this because we have no
bread**.'"*

He warns them about a doctrine and they audit the picnic.

## The seven questions

*"**Why are you talking about having no bread? Do you still not see it? Do you still not understand?
Are your hearts really so slow to open?**"*

And then: *having eyes, do you not see? And having ears, do you not hear? And do you not remember?*
— followed by the arithmetic of both feedings, basket by basket.

Seven questions in a row, and not one of them is answered in the passage.

## What He does not do

Send them ashore for bread, or explain the metaphor.

The chapter ends with a blind man at Bethsaida healed in two stages — the only two-stage healing in
the gospels — who first sees *men like trees, walking*, and then sees clearly.

**Mark** puts it immediately after this conversation, and the disciples' partial sight is the reason.

## And what Peter says next

*"But who do you say that I am?" Peter answered and said to Him, "**You are the Christ**."*

Eight verses later. The men who could not follow a metaphor about bread produce the confession the
whole gospel has been building to, and **Matthew** adds what **Jesus** says about where it came from:
*flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but My Father who is in heaven.*

> **The joke Mark leaves in.** One loaf in the boat, and the anxiety is about bread — in a
> conversation with the man who had just fed four thousand from seven. He does not correct the
> catering. He asks whether they remember.
