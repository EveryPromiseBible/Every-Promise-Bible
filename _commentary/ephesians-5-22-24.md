Three verses that get quoted on their own, and almost everything about them
depends on the sentence before and the twenty-five verses after.

## The verb is not in the Greek

*Wives, submit to your own husbands.*

There is no verb in verse 22 in the oldest manuscripts. It is carried over from
the previous sentence — *submitting to one another in the fear of God* — which is
addressed to everybody.

The paragraph begins with mutual submission and then works out what it looks like
in a household.

## "As to the Lord"

The measure is set immediately. Not *because he has earned it*, and not *however
he asks for it*.

## Head

*For the husband is **head** of the wife, as Christ also is **head of the
church**.*

The comparison is what fills the word. Whatever headship means here, it means what
Christ's headship means, and Paul spends the next twelve verses saying what that
is:

*Husbands, love your wives, **just as Christ also loved the church and gave
Himself for her**.*

The pattern set for the husband is a man on a cross. Nothing in the passage
authorises anything else.

## The clause in the middle

*— and **He Himself is the Saviour of the body**.*

Paul breaks his own comparison to say it. He has just lined up husband/wife with
Christ/church, and then he stops and notes the point at which the parallel does
not run: Christ is the Saviour of the body, and no husband is.

**The body.** Not the soul, not the spirit. Paul is talking about the church as a
body and he says outright that Christ saves it.

He says the same thing a few verses later: *no one ever hated his own flesh, but
nourishes and cherishes it, **just as the Lord does the church** — for we are
members of His body.*

**Nourishes and cherishes.** The second Greek word is used of a bird warming her
young under her.

## What the whole passage is for

*This is a great mystery, but I speak concerning **Christ and the church**.*

That is Paul's own summary, and it comes at the end. The marriage was never the
subject. It is the picture, and he says which way the picture points.

> **What the husband is not given.** No authority to command, correct, or require
> anything. Every instruction in the passage addressed to him is about what he
> gives: love her, give yourself for her, nourish her, cherish her, love her as
> your own body. The list is one-directional.
