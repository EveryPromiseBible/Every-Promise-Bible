Four hundred years of silence end at an incense altar, and the man it happens to is
terrified.

## The rota

*Zechariah's division came up for **its turn of service**.*

David had divided the priests into twenty-four courses, each serving twice a year for a
week. Luke says earlier that Zechariah was *of the division of Abijah* — the eighth of the
twenty-four.

## The lot

*And **the lot fell to him** — as the custom of the priesthood decided such things — to **go
into the sanctuary of the Lord and burn the incense**.*

The Greek is **elache** — obtained by lot.

There were thousands of priests. Burning the incense was the most desirable duty in the
temple, and rabbinic tradition holds that a man who had done it once was not eligible
again.

For most priests it never came round. Zechariah is old.

## What he was doing

Burning incense on the golden altar, immediately outside the veil. Four spices in equal
parts, salted, unlawful to make for private use. See the note on Exodus 30:34–38.

## Who was outside

*Outside, **at the hour of incense**, the whole gathered crowd **was praying**.*

The hour of incense was the hour of the morning or evening sacrifice — and the incense on
the altar and the prayers of the people are the same thing in Scripture: *let my prayer
stand before You like incense*. See the note on Psalms 141:1–2.

Luke puts the crowd praying outside and the angel arriving inside, in the same sentence.

## Where the angel stood

*Standing at **the right side of the incense altar**.*

Luke gives the position. The right side is the place of favour.

## The reaction

*Zechariah **went rigid at the sight of him**, and **fear came over him**.*

The Greek is **etarachthe** — troubled, agitated.

## What the angel says

*"Do not be afraid, Zechariah, **for your prayer is heard**."*

Which prayer is not said. He and Elizabeth are *both well advanced in years*, and Luke has
already recorded that *they had no child, because Elizabeth was barren*.

The prayer for a son had presumably been abandoned decades earlier.

## What Luke says about them

*And they were **both righteous before God, walking in all the commandments and ordinances
of the Lord blameless**.*

Luke clears the obvious inference before anyone draws it. The childlessness was not a
verdict.

## What is promised

A son named John, who will *turn many of the children of Israel to the Lord their God*, and
go before Him *in the spirit and power of Elijah*.

Malachi's last words, four hundred years earlier: *behold, I will send you Elijah the
prophet.*

## What happens next

Zechariah asks how he can be sure, and is struck dumb until the day it happens. See the
note on Luke 1:18–20.

The crowd outside waits, and when he comes out he cannot pronounce the blessing.

## How Luke's Gospel ends

*And He led them out as far as Bethany, and **He lifted up His hands and blessed them**.*

A priest who could not give the blessing at the start, and a blessing completed at the
end.

> **Where the silence breaks.** In the one room a priest might enter once in a lifetime,
> at the hour the nation was praying outside, to a man old enough to have stopped asking.
