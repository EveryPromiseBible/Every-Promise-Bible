Probably an early hymn, quoted by Paul, and it is the highest sustained statement
about Christ in his letters.

## "Firstborn of all creation"

The phrase has been misread for centuries as making Him part of what was created
— and the next sentence rules that out: *in Him everything was created.*

**Prototokos** in the Old Testament is a title of rank, not chronology. God calls
Israel His firstborn, and David — the youngest of eight sons — is called firstborn
in Psalm 89. It means the one with the rights of the heir.

## The prepositions

The hymn turns on four small words, and they cover everything:

- created **in** Him
- created **through** Him
- created **for** Him
- and He is **before** all things

Then the line that holds the passage together: *in Him all of it holds together.*
The verb means to stand together, to cohere. The universe is described as
continuously held rather than wound up and released.

## The two firstborns

He is *firstborn of all creation* and *firstborn from among the dead*. The hymn
puts the two in parallel — first place in the old creation and in the new one.

And the stated purpose: *so that in everything He might hold the first place.*

## The last line

*Through Him He should bring everything back into peace with Himself, having made
that peace by the blood of His cross.*

After thrones, lordships, rulers and authorities, after all things visible and
invisible held together — the hymn ends at a cross.

The blood is what the cosmic language was building toward, which is what keeps
the passage from being an abstraction.

> **Where it lands next.** *And you, who once were alienated and enemies in your
> mind… He has now reconciled.* Having described the whole universe being brought
> back, Paul turns immediately to the readers and applies it to them personally.
> See the note on 2 Corinthians 5:18–19.
