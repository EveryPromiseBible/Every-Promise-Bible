---
key: 1 Peter 3:18
title: "Once, and to bring you in"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
One verse, and Peter packs the entire gospel into it — including the reason for it, which is not the
reason most people would give.

## Once

*Christ suffered for sins **one time**.*

**Hapax** — once, once for all. It is the word Hebrews builds a whole argument on, and it is the
difference between the two covenants: a system where a priest went in every year, and an event that
does not need doing again.

If it were repeatable it would not have been sufficient. The single occurrence is the proof of what
it accomplished.

## The just for the unjust

*The **innocent one on behalf of the guilty**.*

**Dikaios hyper adikon** — righteous for unrighteous. The preposition is **hyper**, on behalf of, in
the place of.

Peter states substitution in four Greek words and does not soften either end of it: one party had no
guilt, the other party had nothing else. There is no third category, and nobody in the sentence is
being met halfway.

## Why He did it

*To **walk us right up to God**.*

**Prosagage** — to lead someone in and present them. The related noun, **prosagoge**, is the word for
being introduced at a royal court, and it is what Paul says we now have: *through Him we both have
**access** by one Spirit to the Father*, and *we have **access** by faith into this grace in which we
stand*.

Read the purpose clause again, because it is the surprising part. The cross is not described here as
aimed at forgiveness, or at escaping punishment, or at moral improvement. Those all happened. But the
stated destination is a Person: *to bring us to God*.

He did not die to get us out of hell first and foremost. He died to get us in.

## Put to death, made alive

*In the flesh He was put to death; **by the Spirit He was made alive**.*

Two passives in sequence, and everything hangs between them. Death was real and the raising was
done to Him — the same Spirit Paul says *raised Jesus from the dead* and *will also give life to your
mortal bodies*.

> **The purpose was access.** Every barrier in the Old Testament — a veil, a fence around a
> mountain, a court no Gentile could pass, a room one man entered once a year — existed to say that
> God was not approachable. One suffering, once, by the righteous for the unrighteous, and the
> stated result is that you are walked up and introduced.
