Elijah arrives in the narrative with no introduction — no father named, no
calling scene, no commission recorded. One sentence and he is in front of the
king.

## What he says about himself

*As surely as the LORD lives — the God of Israel, **the One I stand before and
serve**.*

That is his whole credential. Not *the prophet of Israel*, not a title or an
office. A description of where he stands.

The Hebrew *to stand before* is the language of a servant attending a king in his
throne room — the phrase used of courtiers waiting on Solomon. Elijah is saying
he is in attendance somewhere else, and speaking from there.

## What he says about the weather

*There will be no dew and no rain in these years, not a drop, **until I say the
word**.*

Two things a farming nation could not live without, and the shutting of both is
put on his word. Baal was the storm god — the one whose whole portfolio was rain
and fertility.

So the drought is not a general judgment. It is aimed precisely at the thing Baal
was supposed to supply, in a country that had gone over to him.

## Where the authority came from

James is the one who tells us what happened before the sentence: *he prayed hard
that the rain would stop, and it stopped.* The declaration in front of Ahab is
the outcome of praying, not a substitute for it.

He prayed it first, then announced it, and then lived on a brook and ate what
ravens brought him for the duration.

> **"Elijah" means "my God is the LORD."** In a kingdom that had spent a
> generation deciding otherwise, the man's own name is the argument. And on
> Carmel the nation shouts it back — *the LORD, He is God* — without necessarily
> noticing whose name they are saying.
