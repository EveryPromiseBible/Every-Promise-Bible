The worst day of David's life, in one verse.

## What has happened

David and his six hundred men had been living in Philistine territory, at Ziklag. Sent home from
the Philistine army because their commanders did not trust him, they came back after three days.

*Ziklag had been **burned with fire**, and their wives, their sons, and their daughters had been
taken captive.*

*Then David and the people who were with him lifted up their voices and wept, **until they had no
more power to weep**.*

## The distress

*David was in **terrible distress**.*

The Hebrew is **vattetzer leDavid me'od** — and it was very narrow for David.

**Tsarar** — to bind, to press, to be in a tight place. The physical image of a space closing in.

## The threat

*The men were talking of **stoning him**.*

The Hebrew is **lisqolo** — to stone him.

His own six hundred. The men who had come to him at Adullam *in distress, in debt, and discontented*
and had followed him for years.

## Why

*Each one **bitter in his soul over his lost sons and daughters**.*

The Hebrew is **marah nephesh kol-ha'am** — the soul of all the people was bitter.

Their grief is not unreasonable, and the narrator states it as their reason without dismissing it.

## What he has lost

Two wives taken. A town burned. An army that wants to kill him. Saul still hunting him. No
sanctuary, no allies, and no plan.

## The turn

*But David — **David strengthened himself in the LORD his God**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyitchazzeq David baYHWH Elohav**.

**Chazaq** — to be strong, to seize, to hold fast. Here in the reflexive: he made himself strong.

## What the reflexive means

Nobody strengthened him.

There is no prophet in the scene, no Jonathan, no Samuel — Samuel is dead. He does it to himself,
and the verse names where he did it from.

## The two words at the end

*The LORD **his God**.*

**Elohav** — his God. Possessive.

Not *the God of Israel* or *the LORD of hosts*. The relationship is named in the same clause as
the strengthening.

## What the Illumination adds

*At the bottom of everything, with nothing left in his hands, he reached for **the one thing no
raider could carry off**: his God.*

Everything portable had been taken. The verse names the one asset that was not on the inventory.

## What he does next

*Then David said to Abiathar the priest, "**Bring me the ephod**."*

*So David enquired of the LORD, saying, "**Shall I pursue this troop? Shall I overtake them?**"* See
the note on 1 Samuel 30:7–10.

The strengthening comes first, and then the question.

## The answer

*"**Pursue, for you shall surely overtake them and without fail recover all**."*

## What happens

Two hundred men too exhausted to cross a brook. An abandoned Egyptian slave found in a field. A
raiding party spread out eating and drinking.

*So David recovered all… **nothing of theirs was lacking, either small or great, sons or daughters,
spoil or anything which they had taken**.* See the note on 1 Samuel 30:16–20.

## What the psalms do with this material

*Out of the depths I have cried to You, O LORD.*

*Why are you cast down, O my soul? And why are you disquieted within me? **Hope in God, for I shall
yet praise Him**.* See the note on Psalms 42:9–11.

A man talking to himself, which is what the reflexive verb describes.

> **The verb.** Reflexive. Nobody strengthened him — no prophet, no friend, and Samuel already
> dead. He did it to himself, and the verse names what he did it from.
