Peter and John have looked into the tomb and gone home. Mary stays and cries.

## She stayed

*But **Mary stayed outside by the tomb, crying**.*

The two disciples had run there, looked in, seen the linen cloths, and *went away
again to their own homes*.

She has nowhere to go. The Greek for her weeping is the loud kind — wailing.

## What she does

*And while she was crying **she bent down and looked into the tomb**.*

She looks in after the men had already looked in and found it empty. There is no
reason to expect anything.

## What she sees

*Two angels in white, seated where the body of Jesus had been laid, **one at the
head and one at the feet**.*

That is the detail, and it is a description of a piece of furniture.

The mercy seat sat on top of the ark, with *two cherubim of gold… one cherub at one
end, and the other cherub at the other end*, facing each other across it.

Once a year the high priest went in and sprinkled blood on the space between them,
and God said: *there I will meet with you, and I will speak with you from above the
mercy seat, from between the two cherubim.* See the note on Exodus 25:17–22.

Two angels, one at each end, and between them the place where a body had lain and
blood had been shed.

Mary is looking into the holiest place, from outside, and it is empty.

## What they ask

*"**Woman, why are you crying?**"*

Angels are asking a question they can hardly need the answer to, in a tomb that is
empty because of good news.

## Her answer

*"**They have taken away my Lord, and I do not know where they have put Him.**"*

She says almost the same words three times in this chapter — to Peter and John, to
the angels, and then to the gardener.

Her grief is not that He is dead. It is that she has lost the body and cannot
perform the last service she came to do.

## What happens next

She turns round, sees Him, and takes Him for the gardener — in a garden, on the
first day of the week.

He says one word. *Mary.*

And she is the first person on earth to see Him alive, and the first commissioned to
tell anybody: *go to My brethren and say to them…*

> **Where the angels are sitting.** *Where the body of Jesus had been laid.* John
> notes the position because it is the point. The room in the tabernacle that nobody
> could enter is now a place a weeping woman is leaning into, and the seat is empty.
