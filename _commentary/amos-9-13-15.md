A picture of a farming year that will not fit into a year.

## The image

*"The days are coming when **the plowman will overtake the reaper**, and **the one who
treads the grapes the one who plants the seed**."*

In an ordinary season these are months apart.

You reap in spring, thresh, and then wait until the autumn rains soften the ground
before you can plough again. You harvest grapes in late summer and sow after them.

Amos describes a harvest so long that the man ploughing for next year catches up with
the man still bringing in this one — and a vintage still being trodden when the next
sowing starts.

The seasons overlap and stop being a queue.

## The wine

*"**New wine will drip from the mountains** and flow down every hill."*

The Hebrew is that the mountains will drip sweet wine.

Vineyards were terraced on hillsides and the presses cut into the rock. The picture
is of so much fruit that the hills themselves run with it.

Joel says the same thing in almost the same words: *the mountains shall drip with new
wine, the hills shall flow with milk.* See the note on Joel 2:25–27.

## The four things restored

*They will **rebuild the ruined cities** and live in them. They will **plant vineyards
and drink their wine**. They will **make gardens and eat their fruit**.*

The point of each clause is who gets the benefit.

That is the exact reversal of the curse in Deuteronomy 28: *you shall build a house
and not dwell in it; you shall plant a vineyard and not gather its grapes.*

And it is the same promise as Psalm 128 — *you will eat the fruit of your labour.* See
the note on Psalms 128:1–2.

## The planting

*"**I will plant them in their own land**, and never again will they be uprooted from
the land I have given them."*

The gardener language is applied to the people. They plant vineyards; He plants them.

## Where this sits

Directly after the promise to raise up David's fallen shelter — the verse James
quoted at the Jerusalem council to settle whether Gentiles could come in without the
law. See the note on Amos 9:11–12.

The acceleration follows the rebuilding.

## What the book was before this

Amos was a shepherd from Tekoa, and the book is nine chapters of judgement on Israel,
Judah and six surrounding nations, with five visions of ruin.

*"Woe to you who are at ease in Zion."* *"I hate, I despise your feast days."*

And then the last five verses.

> **The last three words.** *Says the LORD **your God**.* The possessive is the final
> word of a book that spent nine chapters describing what He was going to tear down.
