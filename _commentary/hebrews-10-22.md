The first of three *let us* sentences, and everything the letter has argued for nine
chapters lands on it.

## What comes before

*Therefore, brethren, having **boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus**, by a
new and living way which He consecrated for us, **through the veil, that is, His flesh**.*

And *having a High Priest over the house of God*.

The *therefore* carries the whole letter. Everything in this verse is a consequence.

## The invitation

*So **let us come close**.*

The Greek is **proserchometha** — let us draw near. The word for approaching an altar or a
throne.

It is the exact thing forbidden at the bush: *do not draw near here*. See the note on
Exodus 3:4–6.

And forbidden at Sinai: *set bounds for the people all around*.

## With nothing held back

*With **nothing held back**.*

The Greek is **alethines kardias** — a true heart.

Not a good heart. A genuine one, without pretence.

## The certainty

*Completely certain of what we trust.*

The Greek is **en plerophoria pisteos** — in full assurance of faith.

**Plerophoria** — full carrying, complete conviction. The word Paul uses of Abraham being
*fully convinced that what He had promised He was also able to perform*.

## The first washing

*Hearts **sprinkled clean of a bad conscience**.*

The Greek is **rherantismenoi** — a perfect passive participle. Having been sprinkled, and
standing sprinkled.

Sprinkling is the priestly action. Blood was sprinkled on the altar, on the mercy seat, on
the people at Sinai, and on Aaron's garments.

What is sprinkled here is the conscience.

## What the letter has said about that

*How much more shall the blood of Christ… **cleanse your conscience** from dead works to
serve the living God?*

And the argument at the start of chapter 10: *for then would they not have ceased to be
offered? For the worshippers, once purified, would have had **no more consciousness of
sins**.* See the note on Hebrews 10:1–2.

The repeated sacrifice was the problem, because a sacrifice repeated annually is an annual
reminder.

## The second washing

*Bodies **rinsed in clean water**.*

The Greek is **lelousmenoi** — bathed, washed all over. A different verb from the sprinkling.

The priests were washed once at their consecration, entirely, and after that only their hands
and feet at the laver.

Jesus makes the same distinction: *he who is bathed needs only to wash his feet, but is
completely clean*.

## The two halves

Inside and outside. Conscience and body. Sprinkled and bathed.

Both participles are perfect and passive. Both were done to the reader before the invitation
was issued.

## The three sentences

*Let us **draw near** with a true heart.*

*Let us **hold fast** the confession of our hope without wavering, for **He who promised is
faithful**.* See the note on Hebrews 10:23–25.

*Let us **consider one another** in order to stir up love and good works.*

Faith, hope, love, in that order, in three consecutive verses.

## What is not asked for

Nothing about worthiness. The two participles have already settled it, and the letter spends
nine chapters establishing that they are settled by somebody else's work.

## What the veil was

*Through the veil, that is, His flesh.*

The curtain in front of the Holy of Holies, which nobody but the high priest passed, once a
year, with blood.

*And behold, the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom.* See the note on
Mark 15:33–36.

> **The verb.** *Draw near* — the one thing Moses was told not to do at the bush, issued as
> an invitation, on the strength of two things already done to the reader.
