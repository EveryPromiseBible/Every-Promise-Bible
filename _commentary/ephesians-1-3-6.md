The opening of the longest sentence in the New Testament, and it begins with a
claim in the past tense.

## "Has blessed us"

Not *will bless*. The verb is aorist — done, complete, a settled fact by the time
Paul writes.

And the extent is total: *with every blessing the Spirit has to give*. Nothing is
being held in reserve pending performance. Whatever the Christian life turns out
to involve, Paul's starting position is that the giving has already happened.

## Where it is kept

*Up where heaven is* — in the heavenly places. Twice more in this letter Paul
uses the phrase, and it is not a description of somewhere distant. It is where
Christ is seated, and where he later says believers are seated with Him.

So the blessings are located in the same place as the person they are held in.
That is the force of the phrase Paul repeats through this whole passage: **in
Him**, in Christ, in the One He loves. Eleven times in twelve verses.

## "Before the world was ever laid down"

The choosing is dated earlier than creation, and the purpose is stated: *so that
we would stand before Him holy, without a single flaw in us, in love.*

Two things about that. It is the goal of the choosing, not its condition. And the
last two words attach either to the standing or to the choosing itself — Greek
allows both, and either way love is in the sentence rather than obligation.

> **The reason given.** *This was simply what pleased Him, and what He wanted.*
> Paul offers no cause outside God's own good pleasure, and then says the point
> of the whole thing is *the praise of the shining beauty of His grace*. When he
> comes to explain why any of it happened, he does not point at anything in us.
