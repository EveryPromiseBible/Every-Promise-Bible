The tribe that got no land is told what it got instead.

## The arrangement

*"As for their inheritance, **I am their inheritance**. You are to give them no
possession in Israel. **I am their possession**."*

Said twice, in two forms, in one verse.

Every other tribe received territory when the land was divided. Levi received
towns scattered among the others and no ground of their own.

Joshua puts it the same way: *the LORD God of Israel was their inheritance.*

## What they lived on

*"They will eat the grain offering, the sin offering, and the guilt offering, and
**every devoted thing in Israel will be theirs**."*

Their income was what came to the altar. If the nation stopped bringing, they
stopped eating — which is exactly what happened in Nehemiah's day, and the Levites
went back to their fields.

## The firstfruits

*"**The best of all the firstfruits** of everything… will belong to the priests."*

**Reshith** again — the first and the choicest. The same word as Proverbs 3:9. See
the note on Proverbs 3:9–12.

## The dough

*"You are to give the priests **the first of your dough**, so that **a blessing may
rest on your house**."*

The instruction goes back to Numbers 15 — a cake from the first of the kneading
trough, given as a heave offering.

It is the smallest possible offering. Not a herd, not a harvest — a piece of the
dough before the bread is baked, from an ordinary kitchen.

And the reason attached is domestic: *that a blessing may rest on your house.*

## Where Paul takes that idea

*If the firstfruit is holy, the lump is also holy; and if the root is holy, so are
the branches.*

Romans 11:16, and he is using the dough offering as an image. A portion given
sanctifies the whole batch it came from. See the note on Romans 11:13–16.

## Whose priesthood this is

The chapter is about *the sons of Zadok*, who *kept charge of My sanctuary when the
children of Israel went astray from Me.*

Ezekiel's temple vision assigns the closest service to the family that stayed at the
post when everybody else drifted.

## What the priests could not do

The last verse is a food regulation — nothing that died of itself or was torn by
beasts.

And earlier in the chapter: no wine when they enter the inner court, linen only, no
wool, *for they shall not clothe themselves with anything that causes sweat.*

No sweat in the sanctuary. Whatever else the service was, it was not to be laboured
at.

> **What the tribe with nothing ended up holding.** The tabernacle, the ark, the
> law, the teaching of it, and the altar. No field anywhere, and everything that
> mattered passed through their hands.
