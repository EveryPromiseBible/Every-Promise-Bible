Three appointments a year, and one instruction about what to bring.

## The three

*"At the **Feast of Unleavened Bread**, the **Feast of Weeks**, and the **Feast of
Tabernacles**."*

The three pilgrim feasts, the ones every man travelled for. They cluster at the two ends of
the harvest year — spring, early summer, autumn.

Passover and Unleavened Bread in the first month. Weeks — **Shavuot**, Pentecost — fifty days
later. Tabernacles in the seventh month, when everything is gathered in.

## What they turned out to be

*Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us.*

*He has become **the firstfruits** of those who have fallen asleep.* Firstfruits is waved
during Unleavened Bread, on the day after the Sabbath.

*When **the Day of Pentecost** had fully come, they were all with one accord in one place.*

The first two sets of appointments were kept to the day. See the note on
Genesis 1:14–19, where the calendar is hung in the sky on the fourth day.

## Where they go

*"At **the place He chooses**."*

Repeated all through Deuteronomy and never named. The book will not say Jerusalem; it says the
place He will choose.

## The one instruction

*"**No one is to come before the LORD empty-handed.**"*

The Hebrew is **reiqam** — empty, void.

It is the same word used of the freed servant three chapters earlier: *do not send him away
empty-handed*. See the note on Deuteronomy 15:12–18.

Nobody leaves God's house empty and nobody arrives at it empty, and the law uses one word for
both.

## The measure

*"Each is to bring a gift **in keeping with the blessing the LORD your God has given him**."*

The Hebrew is *according to the gift of his hand, according to the blessing which He has
given you*.

No figure. The measure is what was received, and it is calculated afresh by each man.

*So let each one give as he purposes in his heart, not grudgingly or of necessity, for **God
loves a cheerful giver**.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 9:6–7.

## What is not in it

Any suggestion that the gift buys the appearance.

They are commanded to come, three times a year, and the giving is what people do who have been
given something — measured by the harvest that arrived, not by a rate.

> **The word used twice.** **Reiqam** — empty. The servant is not sent away with it, and the
> worshipper does not arrive with it.
