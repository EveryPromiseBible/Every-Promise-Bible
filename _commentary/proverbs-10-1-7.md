This is where the long run of two-line proverbs begins, and the pattern is fixed
from here on: one line, then its opposite. Each verse is a small before-and-after.

## The pair about hands

*A lazy hand leaves a person poor. A diligent hand quietly makes him rich.*

The Hebrew for **diligent** is a sharp word — it is used of a threshing sledge,
the tool that cuts through grain. Not busyness; something decisive that gets
through work.

The Illumination's *quietly* is picking up the sense of the verb, which is about
steady accumulation rather than a sudden windfall. Nothing dramatic is being
promised. Something is being built by a hand that keeps going.

## The one nobody quotes

*Wealth won by wrong does no good in the end. Righteousness pulls a soul back
from death.*

It arrives before the industry proverbs and sets the terms for them: the book is
not simply in favour of getting more. Two verses later comes gathering in summer
versus sleeping through harvest — timing, not just effort.

> **On reading these as promises.** They are observations about how life
> generally runs, not contracts. The same book says elsewhere that the poor can
> be swept away by injustice regardless of how hard they work, and Ecclesiastes
> exists partly to press on the exceptions. Proverbs tells you where to put your
> weight; it does not promise the floor never gives.
