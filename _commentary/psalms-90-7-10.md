The best-known number in the Bible about lifespan, and it sits in the middle of a
paragraph about being under God's anger.

## Read the verses it lives in

- *Our short lives feel Your fire; **in Your anger** we grow weary and afraid*
- *You set our failings before You, our secret faults lit up in the light of Your
  face*
- *Our days pass under Your gaze and end like a sigh*
- **then** — *our years may reach seventy, and if we are strong, eighty, yet even
  the best of them are toil and sorrow*

The number is the last line of a lament about living under displeasure. It is
description, not decree — what life measured out to that generation, in those
conditions.

## Who is speaking, and about whom

The heading calls this *a prayer of Moses*, and the wilderness is all over it.
He is watching the generation that refused at Kadesh die off across forty years,
exactly as had been said they would.

That is the seventy and eighty. Not a limit God set on human beings in general —
the span of a people under judgement, written down by the man watching it
happen.

## What the same Bible records elsewhere

- **Moses himself**, at a hundred and twenty, *his eyes were not weak and his
  strength had not left him*
- **Caleb**, at eighty-five, asking for a hill country full of giants and saying
  he was as strong as the day Moses sent him out
- **Hezekiah**, given fifteen years after being told to put his house in order

The man who wrote the seventy-year line outlived it by fifty years. That is worth
holding in mind before handing anyone the number as though it were an
appointment.

> **What Moses does next.** He does not settle for the description. The psalm
> turns into a request — *teach us to count our days… satisfy us in the morning
> with Your unfailing love… make us glad for as many days as You have afflicted
> us.* He is asking for something better than what he has just described. See
> the note on Psalms 90:11–12.
