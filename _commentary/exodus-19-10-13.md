Three days of preparation, and the whole point of it is a fence.

## Where they are

Sinai, three months out of Egypt. The mountain Moses was sent from, at the bush, with the sign
attached: *when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God on this
mountain*. See the note on Exodus 3:1–3.

The sign has now been fulfilled.

## What was said two days earlier

**19:4** again: *"You have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I **bore you on eagles' wings and brought
you to Myself**."*

**19:6** again: *"You shall be to Me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation."*

The offer, and then the people's answer, **19:8** again: *all that the LORD has spoken we will do*.

## The instruction

**19:10** *"Go to the people and **set them apart today and tomorrow**."*

The Hebrew is **qiddashtam** — sanctify them, make them holy.

The verb's first use in Scripture is of a day, not a people: *God blessed the seventh day and
**sanctified** it*. See the note on Genesis 2:1–3.

## The washing

Still **19:10**: *"Have them **wash their clothes** and be ready by the third day."*

Visible, physical preparation, and it is the kind that can be checked.

Moses adds one more thing when he passes it on, **19:15**: *do not come near your wives*.

## The day

**19:11** *"Because on that day **the LORD will come down on Mount Sinai where all the people can see
him**."*

The whole nation is to be a witness. Nothing about it is private.

## The boundary

**19:12** *"**Mark off a boundary around the mountain** for them."*

The Hebrew is **hagbalta** — set limits.

Three days of getting ready, and the readiness is for a fence.

## The prohibition

Still **19:12**: *"**Be careful not to go up the mountain or even touch its edge**."*

Not the summit. The edge.

## The penalty

The end of **19:12**: *"Whoever touches the mountain must surely be **put to death**."*

And the method, **19:13**: *no hand may touch the offender; they are to be **stoned or shot with
arrows**.*

Because whoever had touched the mountain was now himself untouchable. The execution had to be
carried out at a distance.

## The extent

Still **19:13**: *"Whether animal or human, they may not live."*

A cow that wanders onto the slope.

The holiness described here is not a moral quality. It is a property of the ground, and it does
not distinguish between a rebel and a straying goat.

## The one permission

The end of **19:13**: *"Only when the **ram's horn sounds a long blast** may they come up to the mountain."*

The Hebrew is **yovel** — the ram's horn, and the word behind *jubilee*.

There is a signal at which they may come. It is in the passage, and Exodus does not record it
being sounded.

## What the day was like

**19:16**: *Thunderings and lightnings, and a thick cloud on the mountain; and the sound of the trumpet
was very loud, so that all the people who were in the camp trembled.*

**19:18**: *And Mount Sinai was completely in smoke, because the LORD descended upon it in fire.*

**20:19** again: *"Let not God speak with us, lest we die."*

## What Hebrews does with it

*For you have not come to the mountain that may be touched and that burned with fire, and to
blackness and darkness and tempest… **and so terrifying was the sight that Moses said, "I am
exceedingly afraid and trembling."***

*But you have come to Mount Zion… to Jesus the Mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood
of sprinkling that speaks better things than that of Abel.* See the note on
Hebrews 12:18–24.

The whole argument turns on touchability. That mountain could not be approached; this one is
described as a city people have already arrived at.

> **What three days of sanctifying was for.** A boundary. The people wash their clothes, and
> the instruction they are being prepared for is not to touch the edge.
