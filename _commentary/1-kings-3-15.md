The dream ends, and what he does next is the whole verse.

## What happened

At Gibeon, where the tabernacle stood, God appeared to him in the night: *ask! What shall I give
you?*

And Solomon asked for **lev shomea** — a hearing heart — to judge the people.

*Because you have asked this thing, and have not asked long life for yourself, nor riches… **I
have also given you what you have not asked**.* See the note on 1 Kings 3:5–14.

## The waking

*Then Solomon **woke up — and realized it had all been a dream**.*

The Hebrew is **vehinneh chalom** — and behold, a dream.

## Why that matters

The narrator says so plainly. It was a dream.

Which raises the obvious question about whether it counted, and the rest of the verse answers it
by what Solomon does.

## The journey

*He **went back to Jerusalem**.*

Gibeon was about six miles north-west. He had gone there to sacrifice because *the high places
were still in use, for there was no house built for the name of the LORD*.

He gets up and travels.

## The place

*Stood **before the ark of the LORD's covenant**.*

The ark was in Jerusalem, in the tent David had pitched for it. The bronze altar and the
tabernacle were at Gibeon.

The two had been separated since the ark came up from Obed-Edom's house. See the note on
2 Samuel 6:12–15.

## What that means

He had been at the altar. He goes to the ark.

Not because it was required — Gibeon was the sanctioned place for sacrifice at that moment. He
goes to where the covenant chest was.

## The offerings

*And offered up **burnt offerings and peace offerings**.*

The **olah** — wholly consumed, nothing kept back. See the note on Leviticus 1:10–13.

The **shelamim** — the peace offering, the only sacrifice the worshipper ate. A meal shared in
God's presence.

Total surrender, and then a shared table.

## The feast

*Then he **threw a feast for his whole court**.*

The Hebrew is **mishteh lechol-avadav** — a drinking-feast for all his servants.

The peace offering was eaten. The private dream becomes a public dinner.

## The shape of the verse

Woke, went, stood, offered, feasted.

Five verbs, and every one of them is Solomon acting on something that happened while he was
asleep.

## What comes next

*Now two women who were harlots came to the king, and stood before him.*

The living child, the dead child, the sword, and *give her the living child, and by no means kill
him*.

*And all Israel heard of the judgment which the king had rendered; and they feared the king, for
they saw that **the wisdom of God was in him** to administer justice.*

The gift is tested in the next paragraph, on the two least important people in the kingdom.

## What the ark was

*And there I will meet with you, and I will speak with you **from above the mercy seat, from between
the two cherubim**.*

## Where he ends up

*But King Solomon loved many foreign women… and **his wives turned away his heart***.

*So the LORD became angry with Solomon, because his heart had turned from the LORD God of Israel,
**who had appeared to him twice***. See the note on 1 Kings 10:23–25.

The narrator counts the appearances. This is the first.

## Where he goes when he wakes

*He came to Jerusalem and **stood before the ark of the covenant** of the LORD.*

He had been sacrificing at Gibeon, where the tabernacle was. The ark was somewhere else —
David had brought it to Jerusalem and left the tent behind.

The dream sends him to the box with the blood on the lid. See the note on
Exodus 25:10–16.

## What he does there

*Offered up **burnt offerings**, offered **peace offerings**, and **made a feast for all his
servants**.*

Three things, and the third is the one to notice. The peace offering is the only sacrifice the
offerer ate — God takes the fat, the priest takes the breast and thigh, and the rest is
dinner. See the note on Leviticus 7:28–34.

He answers a promise of wisdom by throwing a party for his staff.

Paul: *the kingdom of God is not eating and drinking, but **righteousness and peace and joy in
the Holy Spirit**.*

## What is not in the verse

Any resolution, vow or programme. He does not go away and make himself wise.

*Now to Him who is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think, according
to **the power that works in us**.*

> **What he does with a dream.** Gets up, travels six miles, and stands in front of the covenant
> chest. The verse is five verbs long and every one of them treats the dream as binding.
