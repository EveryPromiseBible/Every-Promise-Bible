The writer's argument turns on a door that has not been shut.

## The opening

*So **let us be afraid**.*

The Greek is **phobethomen** — let us fear.

It is a strange way to open a paragraph about rest, and he means it: the danger is real
enough to be afraid of missing.

## What is open

*The promise of entering His rest **is still standing open**.*

The Greek is *while a promise remains*. It has not been withdrawn or used up.

## Who it is for

*And **not one of you should turn out to have missed it**.*

The Greek is *lest any of you seem to have come short* — **hustereo**, to fall short, to
arrive too late.

## The comparison

*The good news came to us **just as it came to them**.*

The Greek is *we have had the gospel preached to us, as well as they*.

The wilderness generation had good news preached to them. The writer says so plainly.

They stood at Kadesh, twelve spies came back, two said go up, and the nation refused.
That was the sermon and that was the response.

## Why it did not work

*But **hearing it did them no good, because it never mixed with any faith in the ones who
heard it**.*

The Greek is *not being mixed with faith in those who heard*.

The word is **sunkerannumi** — blended, compounded together, the way ingredients are
combined into one substance.

The message was not deficient. It sat in them without combining.

## Who enters

*We who have believed **are the ones who enter that rest**.*

Present tense. The entering is described as happening.

## The quotation

*Just as He said, "**So I swore in My anger: they will never enter My rest**."*

Psalm 95, and the writer quotes it three times across these two chapters. See the note on
Psalms 95:8–11.

## The oddity at the end

*And that, **though the work had been finished ever since the world was laid down**.*

The rest they were barred from had been ready since Genesis 2.

The writer says as much in the next verse: *God rested on the seventh day from all His
works.*

Nothing had to be built. The rest existed before the people who missed it.

## What the argument produces

*There remains therefore **a Sabbath rest for the people of God**… let us **be diligent to
enter that rest**.* See the note on Hebrews 4:8–11.

An exertion aimed at a rest, and the writer puts both words in one sentence.

## Where the chapter ends

*Seeing then that we have **a great High Priest** who has passed through the heavens…
**let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace**, that we may obtain mercy and find
grace to help in time of need.*

The chapter about a rest that was missed ends at a throne with an open invitation.

> **What the failure was.** Not that the news was withheld or unclear. The writer says
> the same gospel reached them, and names the missing element as the thing that would
> have combined with it.
