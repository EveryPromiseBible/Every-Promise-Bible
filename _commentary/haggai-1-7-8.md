The instruction is three verbs long, and none of them requires a permit.

## The repeat

*This is what the LORD Almighty says. "**Give careful thought to your ways**."*

The second time in three verses. See the note on Haggai 1:5.

The first was followed by five observations about their harvest. This one is followed by an
instruction.

## The three verbs

*"**Go up into the hills. Bring down timber and build the house**."*

The Hebrew is **alu… vahavetem… uvenu** — go up, bring, build.

## What is not in the list

Quarrying stone. Gold. Vessels. Craftsmen. Money.

Timber, from the hills nearby, carried down by hand.

Solomon's temple had cedar shipped from Lebanon by sea on rafts, and thirty thousand conscripted
labourers. This one starts with men walking uphill with axes.

## The scale

The work is described as something the people standing there can begin this week.

Nothing in the instruction depends on Persian permission, a better economy, or the opposition
going away — all three of which had been genuine obstacles for sixteen years.

## The promise attached

*"Then **I will take pleasure in it, and I will be honored**," says the LORD.*

The Hebrew is **veertzeh-bo** — and I will be pleased with it.

**Ratzah** is the word for God accepting a sacrifice. It is what a worshipper hoped for at the
altar.

## The second word

**Ve'ekkaved** — and I will be glorified, honoured, given weight.

**Kavod** — glory, weight, heaviness. The root behind the noun for the presence that filled the
tabernacle.

## What that is answering

The old men had wept when the foundation was laid, because they had seen the first house.

*Who is left among you who saw this temple in its former glory? **And how do you see it now? In
comparison with it, is this not in your eyes as nothing?***

They knew this building would be smaller. God's promise is not that it will look better — it is
that He will be pleased with it.

## What the timber was for

The same material that had gone into their own panelled walls. See the note on
Haggai 1:3–4.

The instruction sends them back to the hills for the thing they had already been fetching for
sixteen years, for a different building.

## What happens

*So the LORD stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel… and the spirit of Joshua… and the spirit of all
the remnant of the people, and **they came and worked on the house of the LORD of hosts, their
God**.*

The Hebrew is **vaya'ar YHWH et-ruach** — the LORD stirred up the spirit.

They obeyed, and the text says God moved them to.

## The word before the work

*"**I am with you**," says the LORD.*

Between the obedience and the stirring, four words.

## What this house became

Rebuilt by Herod, and standing when a boy of twelve was found in it asking questions, and when a
man overturned tables in it.

*"**The glory of this latter temple shall be greater than the former**."* See the note on
Haggai 2:6–7.

## Where the glory ended up

*And the Word became flesh and **dwelt among us**, and we beheld His glory.* See the note on
John 1:14–18.

The Greek is **eskenosen** — pitched His tent.

> **What the instruction leaves out.** Money, stone, craftsmen, permission. Go up the hill, fetch
> wood, build — three things men standing in the crowd could start that week.
