The other direction the chapter's principle runs, and it is the one nobody quotes.

## The turn

*"But if a wicked man **turns away** from all the sins he has committed… **he will surely live. He
will not die**."*

The Hebrew is **shuv** — to turn, to return. The chapter has already established that a righteous
man's record does not save his violent son. This is the same rule pointed the other way.

## The clause

*"**None of the offenses he has committed will be remembered against him.**"*

**Kol-pesha'av asher asah lo yizzakheru lo.**

Not forgiven in instalments and not weighed against the good. Not remembered.

*"For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and **their sins and their lawless deeds I will
remember no more**."*

**Hebrews** quotes Jeremiah's new covenant twice, in chapter 8 and again in chapter 10, and both
times the not-remembering is the last clause — the thing the argument has been building toward.
And **Hebrews** draws the conclusion: *now where there is remission of these, **there is no longer
an offering for sin**.*

## What remembering means

**Zakar** in Hebrew and **mimneskomai** in Greek are both about acting on what is held. To remember
somebody is to do something for them — *remember me when You come into Your kingdom.*

To remember sins no more is a decision about how He will act, not a lapse in the record.

## The question

*"**Do I take any pleasure in the death of the wicked?** declares the Lord GOD. Rather, **am I not
pleased when they turn from their ways and live?**"*

God asks it about His own disposition, and the chapter asks it twice — again at verse 32, *for I
have no pleasure in the death of one who dies.*

*The Lord is not slack concerning His promise, as some count slackness, but is longsuffering
toward us, **not willing that any should perish** but that all should come to repentance.*

*Who **desires all men to be saved** and to come to the knowledge of the truth.*

## And the answer, told as a story

*"**Likewise, I say to you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who
repents.**"*

**Luke** 15, and **Jesus** tells three parables in a row to answer the same question Ezekiel puts
— a shepherd who leaves ninety-nine, a woman who lights a lamp and sweeps the house, and a father
who runs. Each ends with somebody calling the neighbours in.

*"It was right that we should make merry and be glad, for **your brother was dead and is alive
again**, and was lost and is found."*

Ezekiel asks whether God is pleased when a man turns. The parables answer with a party, three
times.

## Why the record is not consulted

*"**Because of the righteous things he has done, he will live.**"*

That is as far as Ezekiel can go, and it leaves a man's life resting on his new record.

*For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of
God, **not of works**, lest anyone should boast.*

*But to him who does not work but believes on Him who justifies the ungodly, **his faith is
accounted for righteousness**.*

**Paul**'s phrase is the one Ezekiel 18 could not write: God justifies **the ungodly** — not the
man who has stopped being ungodly.
