One verse dropped into a chapter about drunk officials and a bad treaty.

## What is happening around it

Isaiah has been describing the leaders of Jerusalem. *The priest and the prophet have erred
through intoxicating drink… they stumble in judgment.*

And their policy: *we have made a covenant with death, and with Sheol we are in agreement.
When the overflowing scourge passes through, it will not come to us, for we have made lies our
refuge.*

They had arranged a treaty with Egypt against Assyria, and Isaiah calls it a covenant with
death.

## The counter

*Therefore this is what the Lord GOD says: **See, I lay in Zion a stone**.*

The Hebrew is **hineni yissad** — behold, I am founding.

Against their arrangement, a foundation.

## The four descriptions

*A **tested stone**.*

The Hebrew is **even bochan** — a stone of testing, or a touchstone. A stone used to test the
purity of metal by the mark it leaves.

*A **precious cornerstone**.*

**Pinnat yiqrat** — a cornerstone of value. The stone at the angle, which fixes the line of
two walls.

*For a **sure foundation**.*

**Musad musad** — the noun doubled: a founded foundation.

## The promise

*The one who trusts will **never be dismayed**.*

The Hebrew is **lo yachish** — from **chush**, to hurry, to make haste.

The older versions have *he who believes will not make haste*. The Greek Old Testament renders
it *shall not be put to shame*, and Paul and Peter both quote that version.

Both readings survive in the tradition. The Hebrew says the man who trusts is not in a rush;
the Greek says he is not disgraced.

Behind both is the same picture: somebody standing on something that holds.

## Why haste is the word

The chapter is about panicking politicians cutting a deal because Assyria is coming.

Haste, in Isaiah, is what people do when they do not believe there is a foundation. *In
returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and confidence shall be your strength. But
you would not. And you said, "No, for we will flee on horses."*

## Who quotes it

Paul, in Romans, twice — *whoever believes on Him will not be put to shame*.

And Peter: *behold, I lay in Zion a chief cornerstone, elect, precious*. See the note on
1 Peter 2:6.

Both use the Greek reading, and both apply the stone to Christ.

## The other stone verses

*The stone which the builders rejected has become the chief cornerstone.* Psalm 118, quoted by
Jesus at the end of the vineyard parable.

And the two together in Peter: a cornerstone for those who believe, and *a stone of stumbling
and a rock of offence* for those who do not.

## What follows in Isaiah

*Also I will make justice the measuring line, and righteousness the plummet.*

Builder's tools. The line and the plumb bob, used against the walls the officials had built.

*The hail will sweep away the refuge of lies, and the waters will overflow the hiding place.*

## The end of the chapter

A long agricultural passage: the ploughman does not plough forever, and different seeds are
threshed differently — dill with a stick, cumin with a rod, and bread grain is not crushed
endlessly.

*This also comes from the LORD of hosts, who is wonderful in counsel and excellent in
guidance.*

> **The Hebrew word.** *Chush* — to hurry. The one who trusts a founded foundation is
> described, first of all, as somebody who is not in a rush.
