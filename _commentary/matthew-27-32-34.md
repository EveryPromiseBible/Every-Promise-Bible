Three verses of logistics, and two details in them are worth stopping on.

## The man

*On the way out they **came across a man from Cyrene named Simon**, and they **forced him
to carry the cross**.*

**Cyrene** is in North Africa, in what is now Libya. There was a large Jewish community
there, and Cyrenians are on the list of nationalities at Pentecost. See the note on
Acts 2:9–11.

He is in Jerusalem for Passover, a long way from home.

## "Forced"

The Greek is **angareuo** — a Persian loan-word for the right of a courier or soldier to
commandeer a civilian for a task.

It is the same verb in the Sermon on the Mount: *whoever **compels** you to go one mile,
go with him two.*

Simon is conscripted. He did not volunteer and could not refuse.

## Why it was needed

The condemned man carried the crossbeam to the site. John records that Jesus went out
*bearing His cross*, so this happens partway.

He had been scourged, and the Gospels do not explain further.

## What Mark adds

*Simon, **the father of Alexander and Rufus**.*

Mark names the sons, which only makes sense if the readers knew them.

Paul closes Romans with *greet **Rufus**, chosen in the Lord, and his mother and mine.*

## The place

*They came to a place called **Golgotha**, which means **the Place of the Skull**.*

The Aramaic is **gulgolta**. Matthew translates it for his readers, as he does with a few
other terms.

## The drink

*There they **offered Jesus vinegar mixed with gall**, and **He tasted it and would not
drink it**.*

Mark says wine mixed with myrrh. It was a sedative, offered to dull what was coming —
tradition holds that the women of Jerusalem prepared it as an act of mercy.

## The two actions

*He **tasted** it* — so He knew what it was.

*And **would not drink it**.* The Greek is *He was not willing*.

He declines the anaesthetic knowingly.

## The psalm underneath

*They also gave me **gall for my food**, and for my thirst they gave me **vinegar to
drink**.*

Psalm 69, and Matthew's wording follows it.

## The other drink

Later, at the end: *"I thirst"… immediately one of them ran and took a sponge, filled it
with sour wine and put it on a reed, and offered it to Him.*

That one He takes. John says *when Jesus had received the sour wine, He said, "It is
finished!"*

He refuses the drink that would dull it and accepts the one that lets Him speak.

## What follows in the chapter

The dividing of the garments, the sign over His head, the two robbers, the darkness from
the sixth hour, the cry, and the veil torn from top to bottom. See the note on
Matthew 27:51–53.

> **What Simon got.** A conscription notice, in a foreign city, on a feast day. Mark
> assumes his sons are known to the church in Rome thirty years later.
