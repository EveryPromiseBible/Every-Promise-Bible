A sentence of death is delivered by a prophet and reversed before he can get out of
the building.

## The message

*"**Put your house in order, for you are going to die; you will not recover.**"*

Not a warning or a diagnosis. It is delivered as the word of the LORD, with no
conditions attached and no *unless*.

## What Hezekiah does

*Hezekiah **turned his face to the wall** and prayed.*

He does not argue with Isaiah or ask for a second opinion. He turns away from the
room.

## What he says

*"Remember, LORD, how **I have walked before You faithfully and with a whole heart**,
and have done what is good in Your eyes."*

He pleads his record. It is the only argument he makes.

And it was a genuine record — Hezekiah tore down the high places, broke the sacred
pillars, and smashed the bronze serpent when Israel started burning incense to it.

## The weeping

*And **he wept — bitter, heavy tears**.*

The Hebrew is *he wept a great weeping*.

## The speed

*Isaiah **had not even left the middle court** when the word of the LORD came to
him.*

The prophet is still inside the palace complex. He delivered a death sentence and is
sent back up the stairs with the opposite message.

## What God says He responded to

*"**I have heard your prayer, I have seen your tears.**"*

Two things, and the second is not speech.

The tears are named separately, as though they were their own petition.

## What is added

*"**I will add fifteen years to your life.**"*

A number, given in advance. Not *you will live* — a figure.

And two more things: *on the third day you will go up to the house of the LORD*, and
the city delivered from Assyria.

## Why

*"**For My own sake and for the sake of David My servant.**"*

The reasons given are not Hezekiah's record, which is what he had argued from.

## The figs

*"**Bring a lump of figs.**" They brought it and laid it on the boil, and Hezekiah
recovered.*

A poultice. Isaiah has just announced a miraculous reversal and then calls for a
fruit compress.

The account puts the promise and the poultice in consecutive sentences without any
apparent tension between them.

> **What Hezekiah did with the fifteen years.** Manasseh, the worst king Judah ever
> had, was born three years into them. And Hezekiah showed the Babylonian envoys
> everything in his treasury, and was told that all of it would one day be carried to
> Babylon — to which he answered *the word of the LORD is good*, because *there will
> be peace and truth in my days.*
