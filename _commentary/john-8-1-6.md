They bring a case to trap Him, and He kneels down and writes.

## Where He had been

*But Jesus **went out to the Mount of Olives**.*

The previous verse: *and everyone went to his own house*.

Everybody had a house. He went to the hill.

## The morning

*Early in the morning He came back into the temple, and all the people came to Him, and **He sat down
and taught them**.*

Sitting was the teaching posture. He is mid-lesson when the interruption arrives.

## The interruption

*Then the teachers of the law and the Pharisees **brought in a woman who had been caught in
adultery**.*

## The position

*They **stood her in the middle of them all**.*

The Greek is **stesantes auten en meso** — having set her in the midst.

A courtyard full of people who had come to hear a teacher, and a woman placed in the centre of it.

## The charge

*"Teacher, this woman was **caught in the very act** of adultery."*

The Greek is **ep' autophoro** — in the act itself.

Which raises the question the passage never answers out loud.

## The man

Adultery requires two.

Leviticus is explicit: *both the adulterer and the adulteress shall surely be put to death*. And
Deuteronomy: *both of them shall die, the man that lay with the woman, and the woman*.

If she was caught in the act, so was he. He is not in the courtyard.

## The law cited

*"In the law **Moses commanded us to stone such women**. So what do You say?"*

The stoning penalty in Deuteronomy applies specifically to a betrothed virgin. The general case
names death without specifying the method.

They are citing the law selectively, and they have brought half the defendants.

## The purpose

*They said this **to trap Him**, so that they would have **something to accuse Him of**.*

The Greek is **peirazontes auton** — testing Him.

## The trap

Say stone her, and He breaks Roman law, which reserved capital punishment to the governor.

Say release her, and He is teaching against Moses.

The same shape as the question about the tax. See the note on Mark 12:13–17.

## What He does

*But Jesus **bent down and began to write on the ground with His finger**.*

The Greek is **kupsas kato to daktulo kategraphen** — stooping down, He wrote with the finger.

## What He wrote

Nobody knows.

It is the only recorded instance of Jesus writing anything, and John does not say what it was.

## The other finger

*And He gave Moses… **two tablets of the Testimony, tablets of stone, written with the finger of
God**.* See the note on Exodus 31:18.

The men quoting the law watch somebody write on the ground with a finger.

## The clause

*As though **He had not heard them**.*

He does not answer, and He does not look up.

## What happens

*So when they continued asking Him, He raised Himself up and said to them, "**He who is without sin
among you, let him throw a stone at her first**."*

*And again He stooped down and wrote on the ground.* See the note on
John 8:7–11.

He writes twice.

## The exit

*Then those who heard it, being convicted by their conscience, **went out one by one, beginning with
the oldest even to the last**.*

## What is left

*And Jesus was left alone, and the woman standing in the midst.*

The same phrase as verse 3 — **en meso**, in the middle. She has not moved.

Everybody else has.

> **The missing defendant.** Caught in the act requires two, and the law they quote names both. One
> of them is not in the courtyard.
