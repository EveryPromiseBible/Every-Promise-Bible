The last speech of Joshua's life, and it ends with a choice put to a nation.

## Where this happens

Shechem. Joshua has gathered all the tribes, elders, heads, judges and officers.

He is *old, advanced in age*, and the chapter closes with his death at a hundred and ten.

The whole speech before this is God speaking in the first person, retelling the history from
Terah onward: *I took your father Abraham… I gave him Isaac… I sent Moses… I brought you out…
I gave you a land for which you did not labour, and cities which you did not build.*

## The instruction

*"So now, **hold the LORD in awe and serve Him with all your heart**, in **complete sincerity
and truth**."*

The Hebrew is **tamim ve'emet** — in wholeness and in truth.

## What has to go

*"**Throw away the gods your ancestors served beyond the River and in Egypt**."*

Two locations, and both are historical.

*Beyond the River* is Mesopotamia — Terah's household, before Abraham left. Joshua has already
said it: *your fathers dwelt on the other side of the River in old times, and they served
other gods*.

*In Egypt* is four hundred years of it.

They have been in the land for a generation and are still carrying idols from two countries
they left.

Rachel had stolen her father's household gods on the way out of Haran, and Jacob had buried
foreign gods under an oak — at Shechem, where this assembly is standing.

## The alternative

*"And **if serving the LORD seems undesirable to you, then choose this very day whom you will
serve**."*

The Hebrew is *if it is evil in your eyes*.

He does not soften it. If you do not want this, pick something.

## The two options

*"Whether the gods your fathers served **beyond the River**, or the gods of the Amorites **in
whose land you now live**."*

Behind them, or around them. The past or the neighbours.

Those are the two live idolatries, and he names both.

## The declaration

*"But **as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD**."*

The Hebrew is **anochi uveiti**.

He does not wait for the vote. He announces where he stands and lets the assembly answer.

The verse is on doorposts and kitchen walls all over the world, usually detached from the
sentence before it, which is *choose today whom you will serve*.

## What the people say

*"**Far be it from us that we should forsake the LORD** to serve other gods!"*

## What Joshua says to that

*"**You cannot serve the LORD, for He is a holy God**. He is a jealous God; He will not forgive
your transgressions nor your sins."*

He argues against their profession. He is trying to make them count the cost before they
promise.

*And the people said to Joshua, "No, but we will serve the LORD!"*

*So Joshua said, "**You are witnesses against yourselves**."*

## The stone

*And Joshua took a large stone, and set it up there under the oak that was by the sanctuary of
the LORD. And Joshua said to all the people, "**Behold, this stone shall be a witness to us,
for it has heard all the words of the LORD which He spoke to us**."*

A stone that has heard something.

## What happens after he dies

*Israel served the LORD all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived
Joshua.*

And then Judges: *another generation arose after them who did not know the LORD nor the work
which He had done for Israel*.

> **What is offered.** Not a recommendation. He tells them to choose, names the two realistic
> alternatives, and then states his own household's position without waiting for theirs.
