Jesus prays for a specific group of people, and the way He describes them is
worth reading slowly.

## Whose they are

*The ones You have given Me, because they are Yours… Everything that is Mine is
Yours, and everything Yours is Mine.*

They are described as a gift from the Father to the Son, and the ownership is
mutual. Nothing in the passage describes them as having attached themselves to
Him.

Four times in these verses they are *the ones You have given Me*.

## What He asks for

*Holy Father, keep them safe by Your own name.*

Not by their vigilance. By the Father's name — the same name Jesus says He had
been keeping them safe by while He was with them.

The request is for continuity: I am leaving, You keep doing what I have been
doing.

## The report He gives

*Those You gave Me I guarded, and not one of them is lost.*

He is hours from the cross, giving an account of the men He was given — men who
are about to scatter, one of whom will deny Him three times before morning.

He counts Peter among the not-lost while knowing exactly what Peter is going to
do that night.

## The exception

*Except the one doomed to be lost.*

John does not soften it and neither should any reading of it. Judas is named as
an exception, and the sentence attaches it to Scripture being fulfilled rather
than to a failure of keeping.

> **Why it matters that He prayed it out loud.** The disciples were in the room.
> Jesus could have prayed this privately; John records it because it was said in
> front of them. Men who were about to fail badly heard themselves described as
> given, guarded, and kept — before the failing happened.
