Peter quotes Joel, and the small change he makes is the whole claim.

## The change

Joel wrote *and it shall come to pass **afterward***. See the note on Joel 2:28–29.

Peter says *and it will happen **in the last days**, says God*.

He names which afterward. The era Joel pointed at is the one they are standing in, and
Peter says so in the first line of the quotation.

## The pouring

*I will **pour out My Spirit on all people**.*

The Greek is **ekcheo** — to pour out, empty. The same verb the Greek Old Testament uses
in Joel.

**All flesh.** In front of him is a crowd from fifteen nations. See the note on
Acts 2:9–11.

## The four groups

*Your **sons and your daughters** will prophesy, your **young men** will see visions, and
your **old men** will dream dreams.*

Peter reverses Joel's order of the last two — Joel has old men dreaming first. Neither
order changes the point: both ends of a life are included.

## The servants

*Yes, and on **My servants — the men and the women both** — in those days I will pour out
My Spirit.*

Joel says *the servants*. Peter says **My** servants, twice.

## The addition

*And **they will prophesy**.*

That clause is not in Joel. Peter adds it to the second group.

Joel had said the sons and daughters would prophesy; Peter extends it to the slaves as
well.

## Why he is quoting it

Some in the crowd had said *they are full of new wine*, and Peter answered with a clock —
*it is only nine in the morning*. See the note on Acts 2:14–16.

Then this. He does not explain the phenomenon; he identifies it.

## What he quotes next

The rest of Joel, unabridged: *wonders in heaven above and signs in the earth beneath:
blood and fire and vapour of smoke. The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon
into blood, before the coming of the great and awesome day of the LORD.*

He does not stop at the convenient part.

## Where he stops

*And it shall come to pass that **whoever calls on the name of the LORD shall be saved**.*

That is the last line of the quotation and the hinge of the sermon.

Paul quotes the same line in Romans 10 and draws the same conclusion: *there is no
distinction between Jew and Greek, for the same Lord over all is rich to all who call
upon Him.* See the note on Romans 10:1–4.

## What the sermon does next

Three Old Testament passages: Joel, then Psalm 16 on the resurrection, then Psalm 110 on
the seating. See the note on Psalms 110:1.

Almost the whole sermon is quotation.

## The result

*Then those who gladly received his word were baptized; and that day about **three
thousand souls** were added to them.*

## The other three thousand

The day the law was given at Sinai, three thousand fell. See the note on
Exodus 32:25–29.

Two givings, and the same number counted on each day.

> **The two words Peter changes.** Joel's *afterward* becomes *in the last days*, and
> *the servants* becomes *My servants*. He is dating the prophecy and naming who the
> slaves belong to.
