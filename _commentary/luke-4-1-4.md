He goes into the desert full of the Spirit, and the first thing recorded is hunger.

## Who leads Him there

*"**Jesus, full of the Holy Spirit**, came back from the Jordan, and **the Spirit led Him out into
the wilderness**."*

The temptation is not a lapse in the itinerary. **Luke** has the Spirit doing the leading, three
verses after the dove and the voice.

*"**You are My beloved Son; in You I am well pleased.**"*

That is said first. The testing comes to somebody who has already been told what He is.

## The forty

Israel: forty years in a wilderness, fed with manna, and failing. Moses: forty days on the mountain
without bread. Elijah: forty days to Horeb on one meal.

*"And you shall remember that the LORD your God led you all the way these forty years in the
wilderness, to humble you and test you… **So He humbled you, allowed you to hunger, and fed you with
manna**… that He might make you know that man shall not live by bread alone."*

Deuteronomy 8, and it is the verse **Jesus** answers with.

## The three answers

All three quotations come out of Deuteronomy 6 to 8 — the chapters about Israel's forty years.

He is standing in the same test the nation failed, in the same terrain, with the same scriptures,
and the outcome is different.

*For as by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so also by **one Man's obedience many will
be made righteous**.*

*For we do not have a High Priest who cannot sympathise with our weaknesses, but One who **was in all
points tempted as we are, yet without sin**.*

## The first temptation

*"**If You are the Son of God, tell this stone to become bread.**"*

The **if** is not doubt — it is the Greek conditional of assumption: *since* You are. The attack is
not on whether He is the Son. It is on whether the Son should be hungry.

*"**Man shall not live by bread alone.**"*

He answers with a sentence about the manna, and refuses to feed Himself. Two chapters later He feeds
five thousand.

> **Who leads Him in.** The Spirit, three verses after the voice from heaven. He goes into the test
> already told what He is — and every answer He gives comes out of the three chapters about Israel's
> forty years.
