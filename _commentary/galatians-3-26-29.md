Paul has spent three chapters arguing that the promise to Abraham came before the
law and does not run through it. Here is where the argument lands on the reader.

## The three divisions

*There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is
neither male nor female.*

Those were the three great dividing lines of the ancient world — race, status,
sex. A common Jewish morning prayer of the period thanked God for not making the
speaker a Gentile, a slave, or a woman.

Paul takes the same three categories and says that in Christ they do not
determine standing. He is not abolishing the differences; he is saying they no
longer decide who inherits.

## "Clothed yourselves with Christ"

The image is putting on a garment. In the Roman world a boy coming of age
exchanged his child's toga for the *toga virilis* — the moment he became a legal
adult with rights of inheritance.

Paul has just used exactly that picture in the verses before: an heir who is a
child is no different from a slave until the appointed time.

## The conclusion

*And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham's seed, and heirs according to
the promise.*

Not *like* Abraham's seed. Paul has already argued that the promise was made to
Abraham and to his *seed*, singular, and that the seed is Christ. So the route
runs: Christ is the heir; you belong to Christ; therefore the promise is yours.

The inheritance arrives by belonging, not by descent and not by performance.

> **What is inherited.** In Genesis it was land, descendants and blessing for all
> nations. Paul has already named the piece he is most interested in three verses
> earlier — *so that the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles*, and the
> promise of the Spirit received through faith. The inheritance he is arguing for
> is not smaller than Genesis. It is wider.
