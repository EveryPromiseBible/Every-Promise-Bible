Jesus is at a Pharisee's dinner. A woman with a reputation has just come in and
wept on His feet, and Simon has said nothing out loud.

## Why the story is told

Simon's objection was internal: *if this man were a prophet, He would know what
kind of woman this is.* Luke tells us he said it to himself.

Jesus answers the thought. *Simon, there is something I want to tell you* — which
is itself the reply to the doubt about whether He knew.

## The two figures

Five hundred denarii and fifty. A denarius was a day's wage, so roughly two years
of work against two months.

The gap is real and Jesus does not flatten it. One debt is genuinely ten times
the other.

## The detail that decides it

*Neither of them had anything to pay with.*

That is the equaliser, and it is easy to read past. The difference between the
two men is the size of the debt, not their ability to clear it. Both are at zero.

Ten times nothing and one times nothing come to the same thing.

## The question

*Which of the two will love him more?*

Simon answers correctly, and slightly grudgingly — *I suppose*. Jesus says he has
judged rightly, and then applies it: you gave Me no water, no kiss, no oil; she
has not stopped.

## The direction of the sentence

*Her many sins have been forgiven — for she loved much.* Read alone that can
sound like love earning the forgiveness.

The parable Jesus has just told settles the order. In the story, the cancelling
comes first and the loving follows from it. The love is the evidence of the debt
released, not the price of it.

> **The last thing He says to her.** *Your faith has saved you. Go in peace.* Not
> your love. Luke has Him say the same words to the woman in the crowd who touched
> His clothes. See the note on Mark 5:30–34.
