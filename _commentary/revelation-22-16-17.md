The last invitation in the Bible, and four voices give it.

## Who is speaking

*I, Jesus, sent My angel to bring you this testimony **for the churches**.*

The Greek is **ego Iesous** — I, Jesus.

The only place in the book where He names Himself this way. The whole revelation has come through
angels and visions, and here He speaks directly.

## The first title

*I am **the Root and the Offspring of David**.*

The Greek is **he rhiza kai to genos David**.

Both at once. The root a tree grows from, and the shoot it produces.

*And there shall come forth a Rod from the stem of Jesse… **there shall be a Root of Jesse**.* See
the note on Isaiah 11:1–3.

His question to the Pharisees turned on the same paradox: *if David then calls Him Lord, how is He
his Son?*

## The second

*I am **the bright morning star**.*

The Greek is **ho aster ho lampros ho proinos**.

Balaam's oracle: *a Star shall come out of Jacob*.

The morning star rises last, in the dark, just before the sun.

## What Peter says about it

*And so we have the prophetic word confirmed, which you do well to heed as a light that shines in
a dark place, **until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts**.* See the note on
2 Peter 1:19.

## The first voice

*And **the Spirit and the bride say, Come**.*

The Greek is **erchou** — singular imperative.

Two voices in one clause, and they say the same word.

## The second

*And **whoever hears it, let him say, Come**.*

The invitation is handed on. Anyone who hears it is instructed to repeat it.

## The third

*And **whoever is thirsty, come**.*

The Greek is **ho dipson erchestho**.

The only qualification named in the whole book's closing offer.

*"If anyone thirsts, let him come to Me and drink."* See the note on John 7:37–39.

*"Ho! Everyone who thirsts, come to the waters."* See the note on Isaiah 55:1–3.

## The fourth

*Whoever wants it, **take the water of life, and take it free**.*

The Greek is **labeto hudor zoes dorean**.

**Dorean** — as a gift, gratis, without payment. The word Jesus used to the twelve: *freely you
received, freely give*. See the note on Matthew 10:5–10.

## Where the water comes from

*And he showed me a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, **proceeding from the throne of
God and of the Lamb**.* See the note on Revelation 22:1–2.

## The two directions of *come*

The Spirit and the bride say **come** — which reads first as the church calling Christ back.

And then the same word is turned outward: whoever hears, say it; whoever thirsts, come.

One imperative, aimed both ways in a single verse.

## What follows

*"Yes — I am coming, and soon." Amen. **Come, Lord Jesus**.*

*The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.* See the note on
Revelation 22:20–21.

## Where the Bible's offers end

Genesis 3 has a garden shut and a sword at the gate.

Revelation 22 has a river, a tree, an open gate, and a free drink for anybody who is thirsty.

> **The one imperative.** **Erchou** — come. Said by the Spirit and the bride, repeated by whoever
> hears, and offered to whoever is thirsty, in the same verse and in both directions.
