Near the last words of the Bible, and He is doing the talking.

## The announcement

*I am coming, and **soon**.*

The Greek is **tachu** — quickly, swiftly.

It is said three times in this final chapter: verse 7, verse 12, and verse 20. And the book's
closing exchange is *surely I am coming quickly* — *Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus!*

The word is about the manner as much as the timing: when it happens it will be sudden.

## The reward

*And **I have My reward with Me**.*

The Greek is **ho misthos mou** — my wage, my payment.

He is carrying it. Not sending for it, not calculating it on arrival.

Isaiah has the same picture twice: *behold, the Lord GOD shall come with a strong hand… behold,
His reward is with Him, and His work before Him*.

## The measure

*To give every man **what his work comes to**.*

The Greek is **hos to ergon estin autou** — as his work is.

## The three titles

*I am **the first letter and the last**.*

Alpha and Omega. The first and last letters of the Greek alphabet, and the phrase is used four
times in Revelation.

The Hebrew equivalent — aleph and tav — sits in the second sentence of Genesis as an
untranslatable grammatical marker. See the note on Genesis 1:1–2.

*The place everything **starts** and the place everything **ends**.*

The Greek is **he arche kai to telos** — the beginning and the end.

*The **first and the last**.*

**Ho protos kai ho eschatos**.

Three pairs, saying the same thing three ways: as letters, as points, as ordinals.

## Who said it first

*"I, the LORD, am the first; and with the last I am He."*

And *"I am the First and I am the Last; besides Me there is no God."*

Isaiah, twice, in the mouth of God. Revelation puts the same words in the mouth of Christ, and
the first chapter has already done it: *I am the First and the Last. I am He who lives, and was
dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore.*

## What is on either side of it

*Blessed are those who do His commandments, that they may have the right to the tree of life,
and may enter through the gates into the city.*

And two verses on, the last invitation in the Bible:

*And the Spirit and the bride say, "**Come!**" And let him who hears say, "Come!" And let him
who thirsts come. **Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely**.*

The Greek is **dorean** — as a gift, without payment.

## The two sentences together

Verse 12 says every man is paid according to his work.

Verse 17 says the water is free to anyone who wants it.

Revelation puts both in the same paragraph and does not reconcile them.

## The last warning

*If anyone adds to these things, God will add to him the plagues that are written in this book;
and if anyone takes away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his
part from the Book of Life.*

## The last words

*The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.*

Sixty-six books, and the closing word before the amen is *grace*.

> **What He is carrying.** The wage, already with Him. And five verses later the water is
> offered *freely* — both sentences in the same paragraph.
