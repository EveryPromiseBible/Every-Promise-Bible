The title of the book, and the noun in it is the technical word for a prophetic burden.

## The word

*"This is **the weight of Nineveh**."*

The Hebrew is **massa** — a load, something carried. From **nasa**, to lift.

It is the standard heading for an oracle against a nation — *the burden of Babylon*, *the burden of
Moab*, *the burden of Egypt* — and the translation keeps the physical sense, because the verse goes
on to say *it is a burden laid on his heart*.

The prophet carries the message before he delivers it.

## The name

**Nahum** means **comfort**.

A book of three chapters describing the destruction of a city, by a man whose name is the word
Isaiah 40 opens with: *comfort, comfort My people.*

The comfort is not for Nineveh.

## What Nineveh was

The capital of Assyria: the empire that deported the northern kingdom, besieged Jerusalem, and
whose royal reliefs advertise flaying, impalement and heaps of heads.

*"Woe to the city of blood, full of lies, full of stolen things, never without a victim."*

## What had already happened there

About a hundred and fifty years earlier, a prophet who did not want to go had walked into this city
with five words, and the whole population had put on sackcloth.

*"The men of Nineveh will rise up in the judgment with this generation and condemn it, because
**they repented at the preaching of Jonah**."* See the note on Jonah 3:10.

Nahum is what happened next. The repentance under Jonah was real and it did not hold for six
generations, and this book is addressed to the same city.

## Why both books are in the canon

Jonah is God relenting toward Nineveh. Nahum is God finishing with Nineveh. The Bible keeps them
both, forty pages apart, and does not reconcile them.

*"**The Lord is not slack** concerning His promise, as some count slackness, but is longsuffering
toward us, not willing that any should perish."*

**Peter** puts the two together: the delay is mercy, and it is not indefinite.

## What the book is for

It is written to Judah, not to Assyria. Nineveh is the subject and Judah is the audience — people
who had spent a century under the boot of the empire being described.

*"Look, there on the mountains, **the feet of one who brings good news**."* See the note on
Nahum 1:15.

*"But the LORD is good. **He is a stronghold in the day of trouble**."* See the note on Nahum 1:7.

## The other burden

*"Come to Me, all you who labour and are **heavy laden**, and I will give you rest… for **My yoke is
easy and My burden is light**."*

**Matthew** 11, and the Greek is **phortion** — the load a person carries. Nahum's **massa** is laid
on a prophet's heart until he delivers it; the one **Jesus** offers is described by weight and the
weight is the point.

> **The prophet's name.** *Comfort.* Three chapters on the fall of a city, written by a man called
> Comfort, to people the city had been standing on for a hundred years.
