The verse is about vegetables and holy days, and it ends with a definition of sin.

## The subject

The whole chapter is about a dispute over food and calendar in the Roman church.

*One person believes he may eat anything, while the weak person eats only vegetables.* And *one
person esteems one day above another; another esteems every day alike*.

## The instruction

*Do you have faith about such things? **Keep it between yourself and God**.*

The Greek is **kata seauton eche enōpion tou Theou** — have it according to yourself, before God.

Not: hide your convictions. Do not make your liberty the other man's problem.

## The word

*Happy is the one who **does not condemn himself over what he approves**.*

The Greek is **makarios** — blessed. And **ho mē krinōn heauton en hō dokimazei** — the one not
judging himself in what he approves.

**Dokimazō** — to test and approve after testing. A metallurgy word.

## What the blessing is

Doing a thing you have actually settled, and not going home uneasy about it.

## The other man

*But **whoever has doubts is condemned if he eats**.*

The Greek is **ho de diakrinomenos** — the one wavering, divided, drawn two ways.

**Katakekritai** — perfect passive. He stands condemned.

## Why

*Because he is **not eating from faith**.*

Not because the food was wrong. The same plate is lawful for the man in the previous line.

## The principle

*And **whatever is not from faith is sin**.*

The Greek is **pan de ho ouk ek pisteōs hamartia estin**.

## What it is not saying

The sentence is not a general definition of every sin in the world. It sits at the end of a chapter
about disputed matters.

## What it is saying

In an area where Scripture has not spoken, the test is not the act. It is whether the man is
persuaded.

Acting against your own conscience trains you to act against it.

## Where the chapter started

*Receive one who is weak in the faith, **but not to disputes over doubtful things**.* See the note on
Romans 14:1–4.

*Who are you to judge another's servant? **To his own master he stands or falls**.*

## The two rules of the chapter

The strong are told not to despise. The weak are told not to judge.

Neither is told to change position on the food.

## What matters instead

*For **the kingdom of God is not eating and drinking, but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy
Spirit**.* See the note on Romans 14:17–21.

*Therefore let us pursue the things which make for peace.*

## The obligation on the stronger

*We then who are strong ought to **bear with the scruples of the weak**, and not to please
ourselves.* See the note on Romans 15:1–4.

*For even Christ did not please Himself.*

The argument is settled by pointing at Him, not by settling the menu.

## The word for full persuasion

*Let each be **fully convinced in his own mind**.*

The Greek is **plērophoreisthō** — to be carried to full measure.

The same word Paul uses of Abraham: *being fully convinced that what He had promised He was also
able to perform*. See the note on Romans 4:20–22.

> **The scope.** The sentence closes a chapter about food and days. In the areas Scripture leaves
> open, the test is not the act — it is whether you are persuaded.
