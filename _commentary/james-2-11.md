The argument for why the law cannot be kept in parts, and it is about a person rather than a list.

## What has just been said

*For whoever shall **keep the whole law, and yet stumble in one point, he is guilty of all**.* See the
note on James 2:10.

The Greek is **enochos panton** — liable for all of it.

## The proof

*The One who said **do not sleep with another man's wife** is **the same One who said do not kill**.*

The Greek is **ho gar eipon** — for the one having said.

## The argument

Not that the commandments are equally serious.

That they have one author. Breaking any of them is a refusal of the same person.

James does not compare the offences. He identifies the speaker.

## The illustration

*So **keep your hands clean on the first and take a life**, and you have **broken the law all the
same**.*

The Greek is **gegonas parabates nomou** — you have become a transgressor of law.

**Parabates** — one who steps across a line.

## The two commandments chosen

Adultery and murder — the seventh and the sixth, and in the Septuagint's ordering the seventh
comes first, which is the order James uses.

Two capital offences. He is not using trivial examples.

## What the passage is actually about

Not sexual ethics or violence.

The paragraph begins: *my brethren, do not hold the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of
glory, **with partiality***.

A man with a gold ring and fine clothes given the good seat; a poor man told to stand or sit by
the footstool.

James calls that a breach of the law, and this verse is the argument for why it counts.

## The royal law

*If you really fulfil **the royal law** according to the Scripture, "You shall love your neighbour as
yourself," you do well.*

*But **if you show partiality, you commit sin**, and are convicted by the law as transgressors.*

The Greek is **nomon basilikon** — the king's law.

## What follows

*So speak and so do as those who will be judged by **the law of liberty**.*

*For **judgment is without mercy to the one who has shown no mercy**. Mercy triumphs over judgment.*

The Greek is **katakauchatai eleos kriseos** — mercy boasts over judgment.

## What Paul says with the same logic

*For as many as are of the works of the law are under the curse; for it is written, "**Cursed is
everyone who does not continue in all things which are written in the book of the law, to do
them**."* See the note on Galatians 3:10–14.

Deuteronomy 27, quoted. The same all-or-nothing structure, argued to the same conclusion.

## Where the argument arrives in Paul

*Therefore **by the deeds of the law no flesh will be justified** in His sight, for by the law is the
knowledge of sin.* See the note on Romans 3:19–20.

## Where James goes

*What does it profit, my brethren, if someone says he has faith but does not have works? **Can faith
save him?***

*If a brother or sister is naked and destitute of daily food, and one of you says to them, "Depart
in peace, be warmed and filled," but you do not give them the things which are needed…* See the
note on James 2:14–17.

The chapter that argues the law is indivisible ends by asking what a claim looks like when nothing
follows from it.

> **The argument.** Not that the commandments weigh the same — that they have one author. The
> question is not which line was crossed but whose it was.
