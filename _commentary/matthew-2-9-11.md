Foreign astrologers arrive with the right conclusion and the wrong address.

## Who they are

*Behold, **wise men from the East** came to Jerusalem.*

The Greek is **magoi** — magi. A Persian priestly caste, and by this period the word covered
astrologers and dream-interpreters generally.

Not kings. The number is not given; three gifts are listed, and the tradition supplied three men.

## What they had asked

*"**Where is He who has been born King of the Jews?** For **we have seen His star** in the East and have
come to worship Him."*

They go to a palace, which is where kings are born.

## What that produced

*When Herod the king heard this, **he was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him**.*

The whole city. Herod's paranoia was well known — he had already killed a wife and three sons.

## Where the answer came from

The chief priests and scribes, asked where the Christ was to be born, quoted Micah: *in Bethlehem
of Judea*.

The men who knew the text did not go the five miles. See the note on Micah 5:2.

## The departure

*They listened to the king and set out.*

Herod had told them privately to *go and search carefully for the young Child, and when you have
found Him, bring back word to me, that I may come and worship Him also*.

## The star

*And there was the star they had seen rising in the east, **going ahead of them, until it came to
rest over the place where the child was**.*

The Greek is **proegen autous** — went before them.

**Estathe epano** — stood over.

Whatever it was, Matthew describes it moving ahead of travellers and stopping over one building,
which no ordinary star does.

## The joy

*The sight of the star filled them with **a joy almost too large to hold**.*

The Greek is **echaresan charan megalen sphodra** — they rejoiced with exceedingly great joy.

Three intensifiers stacked. It is the strongest expression of joy in Matthew.

## The house

*They went **into the house** and saw the child with Mary His mother.*

The Greek is **oikian** — a house.

Not a stable. By the time they arrive the family is in a house, and Herod's later order covers
boys *from two years old and under, according to the time which he had determined from the wise
men*.

## Who is named

*The child with **Mary His mother**.*

Joseph is not mentioned in the sentence. Matthew's infancy narrative is otherwise Joseph's — the
dreams are all his — and here the mother is named.

## What they do

*And they **knelt down and worshiped Him**.*

The Greek is **prosekunesan** — prostrated themselves.

Gentile astrologers, on the floor, in front of a toddler.

## The gifts

*Gold, and frankincense, and myrrh.*

**Chrusos** — gold, the tribute for a king.

**Libanos** — frankincense, burned in the temple, the priest's material.

**Smurna** — myrrh, used in embalming. John records seventy-five pounds of it at the burial.

## What happens next

*Then, being **divinely warned in a dream** that they should not return to Herod, they departed for
their own country another way.*

And the flight into Egypt, and the killing of the children in Bethlehem, and *Rachel weeping for
her children*.

## Who else is warned in a dream

Joseph, four times.

Matthew's first two chapters run on dreams, and the men in them are a carpenter and a group of
foreign astrologers.

> **The address.** They had the right conclusion and went to a palace. The men who could quote the
> verse did not walk the five miles to check.
