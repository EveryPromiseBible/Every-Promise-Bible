Seven proverbs, and most of them are about what an action does to the person doing it.

## The surety

*Guarantee a stranger's debt and **you will suffer for it**. Refuse to shake on it, and **you are
safe**.*

The Hebrew is **ra-yeroa ki-arav zar** — he will surely suffer harm who is surety for a stranger.

**Toqe'im** — those who strike hands. The gesture that sealed a guarantee.

Proverbs returns to this five times, more often than almost any other practical warning.

## The gracious woman

*A **gracious woman holds onto honor**, as **ruthless men hold onto their riches**.*

The Hebrew is **eshet-chen** — a woman of grace. And **ariztim** — the violent, the terrifying.

Two kinds of holding, set side by side. One holds honour, the other holds money, and the parallel
does not flatter the second.

## The kindness

*A **kind person does good to his own soul**. A **cruel one brings trouble on his own flesh**.*

The Hebrew is **gomel naphsho ish chased** — the man of **chesed** deals well with his own soul.

**Chesed** — the covenant word. Loyal kindness.

## What the proverb claims

Not that kindness is rewarded. That it is self-directed.

The kind man is doing something to himself while he is doing it to somebody else.

The cruel man is doing the same, in the same sentence, to his own body.

## The wages

*The wicked **earns wages that deceive**. Sow righteousness and **the reward is sure**.*

The Hebrew is **pe'ullat-sheqer** — wages of falsehood, work that lies.

**Sekher emet** — a wage of truth.

Both men get paid. One payment turns out not to be there.

## The direction

*Hold to righteousness and **you move toward life**. Chase after evil and **you chase your own
death**.*

The Hebrew is **ken-tzedaqah lechayyim** — so righteousness to life.

Both verbs are about pursuit. The evil man is not caught; he is chasing, and what he catches is
his own.

## The two states of heart

*The **crooked in heart** are something the LORD hates. Those **blameless in their way** are His
delight.*

The Hebrew is **iqqeshei-lev** — twisted of heart. And **temimei derek** — complete, whole in
their way.

**To'avat YHWH** — an abomination to the LORD. And **retzono** — His delight, His pleasure.

## The last one

*Be sure of it, **the wicked will not go free**, but **the children of the righteous will be
delivered**.*

The Hebrew is **yad leyad lo-yinnaqeh ra** — hand to hand, the evil shall not be unpunished.

**Yad leyad** — hand to hand. An idiom, probably meaning *depend upon it*, or possibly *from
generation to generation*.

## The reach forward

*The children of the righteous.*

The proverb extends past the man to the next household.

Proverbs says it again: *a good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children*. See the
note on Proverbs 13:17–25.

## What the chapter opened with

*Dishonest scales are an abomination to the LORD, but a just weight is His delight.*

The same two words — abomination and delight — used of weights in verse 1 and of hearts in verse
20.

The book treats the shop counter and the interior life with the same vocabulary.

## The verse in the middle of the chapter

*There is one who scatters, yet increases more; and there is one who withholds more than is right,
but it leads to poverty.*

*The generous soul will be made rich, and **he who waters will also be watered himself**.*

The same logic as verse 17, applied to money.

> **The claim.** *A kind person does good to his own soul.* Not that kindness gets rewarded — that
> it lands on the one doing it, at the time.
