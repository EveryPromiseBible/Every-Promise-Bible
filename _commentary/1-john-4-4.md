One sentence, and every clause in it is a completed fact.

## Who he is writing to

*You belong to God, **little children**.*

John's habitual address — **teknia**, small children. He uses it seven times in this
short letter, and he was an old man when he wrote it.

The Greek for *belong to God* is *you are of God* — out of Him, sourced in Him.

## What has already happened

*And **you have beaten them**.*

Perfect tense: you have overcome, and stand having overcome.

Not *you will*, not *you can*. It is reported as done, and it is said to people John
has just been warning about false prophets.

## Who "them" are

The verses before: *many false prophets have gone out into the world* — and John gives
a test, *every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of
God.*

So the overcoming is of a specific thing: teaching that denies the incarnation.

## The reason

*Because **the One in you is bigger than the one in the world**.*

The Greek is a straightforward comparative — greater.

Two beings are named and located: one **in you**, one **in the world**. The
comparison is not between you and the world.

Nothing about the believer's strength enters the sentence. The victory is credited to
a difference in size between two others, and the believer's only contribution is
being the place one of them is.

## What the letter does with this elsewhere

*This is the victory that has overcome the world — **our faith**.* And: *who is he who
overcomes the world, but he who believes that Jesus is the Son of God?*

The same perfect tense, the same completed overcoming, and the mechanism named as
believing.

## Where the chapter goes

Ten verses later: *there is **no fear in love**, but perfect love casts out fear,
because fear involves torment.*

And: *we love Him **because He first loved us**.*

The chapter that says the One in you is greater also says the whole thing started
with being loved first.

## The letter's habit

John states facts about his readers before he asks anything of them. *You are of
God. You have overcome. You know the truth. Your sins are forgiven. You have an
anointing.*

The commands come after the indicatives, every time.

> **Who wrote it.** The disciple who leaned on His chest at supper, watched Him die,
> ran to the tomb, and lived long enough to be the last one left. *That which we have
> heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and **our hands
> have handled**.*
