A man who cannot get to the temple watches birds nesting in it.

## Who wrote it

*A Psalm of the sons of Korah.*

Descendants of the man the ground swallowed in Numbers 16, who became temple singers.

They wrote eleven psalms, and this is the one about wanting to be there.

## The opening

*How lovely is Your dwelling place, **O LORD of hosts**.*

The Hebrew is **mah-yedidot mishkenotecha** — how beloved are Your dwellings.

**YHWH Tzeva'ot** — the LORD of armies. The title appears four times in twelve verses.

## The longing

*My soul **longs, yes, faints**, for the courts of the LORD.*

The Hebrew is **nikhsephah vegam-kaletah** — it longs and also is spent.

**Kalah** — to be consumed, to come to an end. The longing exhausts him.

## The whole person

*My **heart and my flesh** cry out for the living God.*

The Hebrew is **libbi uvesari yerannenu** — my heart and my flesh sing out.

Not the soul only.

## What he sees

*Even the sparrow has found a home, and the **swallow a nest for herself, where she may lay her
young**.*

The Hebrew is **tzippor** — a small bird, and **deror** — a swallow or swift.

## Where the nest is

*At **Your altars**, O LORD of hosts, my King and my God.*

The Hebrew is **et-mizbechotecha**.

Birds nesting in the temple rafters, above the altars, in the place he cannot get to.

## What is being said

The psalmist is not there.

The birds live where he wants to be, and they are not aware of it. He is envying a sparrow.

## The word for the birds

The smallest and least valuable creatures in the landscape — the ones sold two for a copper coin.
See the note on Luke 12:4–7.

They have permanent residence.

## The blessing

*Blessed are those who **dwell in Your house**. **They never stop praising You**.*

The Hebrew is **ashrei yoshevei veitecha od yehaleluka** — blessed are the dwellers in Your house;
they will still be praising You.

## What comes next

*Blessed is the one **whose strength is in You**, in whose heart are the highways to Zion.*

*As they pass through **the Valley of Weeping, they make it a place of springs**.* See the note on
Psalms 84:5–7.

The psalm moves from a man who cannot get there to pilgrims on the road.

## The arrival

*They go **from strength to strength**. Each one appears before God in Zion.*

Nobody is lost in the valley.

## What he would settle for

*Better is **one day in Your courts than a thousand anywhere else**. I would rather **stand at the
threshold**.* See the note on Psalms 84:10–12.

The threshold, not the inner court. He is asking for the doorway.

## What Jesus says about the same birds

*"Are not two sparrows sold for a copper coin? And **not one of them falls to the ground apart from
your Father**."* See the note on Matthew 10:28–31.

The bird the psalmist envies is the one Jesus uses as the least valuable thing on the market.

> **The image.** He is envying a sparrow. It has a nest above the altar and no idea where it is
> living, and he cannot get there.
