The psalmist asks what he can give back, and the answer he arrives at is that he
will take something.

## The question

*What can I **give back** to the LORD for all His goodness to me?*

The Hebrew is *what shall I return to the LORD for all His benefits toward me?*

It is the instinct of anybody who has been given something large. You start looking
for what to send in the other direction.

## The answer

*I will **lift the cup of salvation** and call on the name of the LORD.*

He asks what to give and answers by taking a cup.

The one thing he can do about the goodness is receive more of it, publicly, and say
whose it is.

## The cup

At the Passover meal, cups were lifted and blessed at set points, and this psalm is
part of the Hallel — Psalms 113 to 118, sung at Passover.

Jesus sang this psalm in the upper room, on the night He took a cup and said *this is
My blood of the new covenant.* See the note on Matthew 26:26–30.

The Hebrew is **kos yeshuot** — the cup of salvations, plural.

## The vows

*I will **keep my promises to the LORD in front of all His people**.*

Public. Twice in the psalm he says it that way — *in the presence of all His people*,
and again in the verses following, *in the courts of the LORD's house, in the midst of
you, O Jerusalem.*

## Where the psalm has come from

The opening: *I love the LORD, because He has heard my voice and my supplications…
The pains of death surrounded me, and the pangs of Sheol laid hold of me; I found
trouble and sorrow.*

And in the middle: *I believed, even when I said, "I am badly shaken."* — the line
Paul quotes as *the spirit of faith*. See the note on Psalms 116:8–11.

The man asking what he can give back is a man who has just been rescued from
something.

## The verse in the middle of the psalm

*Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of His saints.*

## What he calls himself

*O LORD, truly I am Your servant; I am Your servant, the son of Your maidservant.
**You have loosed my bonds.***

He says *servant* three times in one verse, and then names what was done to his
chains.

It is the language of Exodus 21 — a man freed, who stays. See the note on
Exodus 21:2–6.

> **How the psalm ends.** *I will offer to You the sacrifice of thanksgiving.* The
> peace offering that had to be eaten fresh, the same day, with none left until
> morning. See the note on Leviticus 7:11–18.
