Mary asks a question, gets an answer, and says one sentence.

## The question

*"**How can this happen? I have never been with a man**."*

The Greek is *how shall this be, since I do not know a man*.

She is betrothed and not yet married. Betrothal in that world was legally binding — Joseph
would have needed a divorce to end it — but the couple did not live together.

## Why this is not Zechariah's question

He asked *how can I be sure of this?* and was struck dumb. See the note on
Luke 1:18–20.

She asks *how can this happen?*

One asks for evidence; the other asks for the mechanism. Gabriel treats them differently
and Luke sets them four hundred words apart so the comparison is unavoidable.

## The answer

*"**The Holy Spirit will come over you**, and **the power of the Most High will overshadow
you**."*

The Greek for *overshadow* is **episkiasei** — to cast a shadow over, to envelop.

It is the word the Greek Old Testament uses of the cloud covering the tabernacle: *the
cloud covered the tent of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle*.

And it is the word used at the transfiguration: *a bright cloud overshadowed them*. See the
note on Matthew 17:1–8.

## The conclusion

*"So the holy child who is born of you **will be called the Son of God**."*

## The second thing

*"And here is something else: **your relative Elizabeth is carrying a son in her old age**.
The woman **everyone called childless** is **six months along**."*

Gabriel volunteers a sign she did not ask for.

Zechariah asked for one and received nine months of silence. Mary does not ask and is
handed one.

## The reason

*"Because **nothing at all is impossible for God**."*

The Greek is *no word from God shall be void of power* — **ouk adunatesei para tou theou pan
rhema**.

The same sentence, in the Greek Old Testament, is what the LORD says to Abraham when Sarah
laughs behind the tent door: *is anything too hard for the LORD?*

Gabriel quotes the Sarah passage to Mary.

## Her answer

*"**I am the Lord's servant. Let it happen to me exactly as you have said**."*

The Greek is **doule** — a female slave, a bondservant. It is the feminine of the word Paul
uses of himself at the head of his letters.

**Let it happen** — the Greek is **genoito**, an optative: may it be.

It is the same verb form as the *me genoito* Paul uses for his strongest denials, here
without the negative.

## What she is agreeing to

An unmarried pregnancy in a village of a few hundred people, and a betrothed husband who
would be entitled to end it.

Matthew records what Joseph decided before he was told: *not wanting to make her a public
example, was minded to put her away secretly*.

## The last clause

*Then **the angel left her**.*

Nothing else is said. No further instruction, no assurance, and no plan.

## What she does next

*Mary got ready and **went quickly** up into the hill country.* See the note on
Luke 1:39–45.

She goes to find the one other person on earth in a comparable position.

## What Elizabeth says about her

*"**How happy you are, because you believed** that what the Lord said to you would actually
be done!"*

> **The two questions in one chapter.** A priest in the temple asking how he can be sure,
> and a girl in Nazareth asking how it will work. Luke records both and lets the answers
> speak.
