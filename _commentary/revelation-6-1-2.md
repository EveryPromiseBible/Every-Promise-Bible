The scroll is opened, and everything in the rest of the book follows from it.

## Who opens it

*I watched **the Lamb** break one of the seals.*

The same Lamb who was found worthy in chapter 5, standing as though slain, when nobody in three
realms could be found to take the scroll. See the note on Revelation 5:1–2.

Nothing rides in chapter 6 that was not released in chapter 5.

## Who calls

*I heard **one of the four creatures** speak, and its voice came out **like thunder**.*

The four living creatures round the throne — lion, calf, man, eagle, covered in eyes. See the
note on Revelation 4:5–6.

One creature per seal, for the first four.

## The summons

*Come and take a look.*

The Greek is **erchou** — come. Two words, and it is disputed whether it is addressed to John or
to the rider.

## The rider

*There was **a white horse**. Its rider was **holding a bow**, and **a crown was handed to
him**.*

Three details: the colour, the bow, and the crown.

**Stephanos** — the victor's wreath, not **diadema**, the royal crown.

*Was handed to him* — the Greek is **edothe**, it was given. That passive runs through the whole
passage: given to him, given to the second rider, given to the fourth.

Nothing here takes anything. Everything is issued.

## The riding

*And out he rode, **already winning and riding out to win more**.*

The Greek is **nikon kai hina nikese** — conquering and that he might conquer.

## Who he is

Argued over for centuries, and the arguments are worth stating plainly.

**Christ**: chapter 19 has a rider on a white horse, crowned, called Faithful and True. The
colour and the conquering match.

**A counterfeit**: the crown is the wrong word, the weapon is a bow rather than a sword from
the mouth, and He is the one opening the seal — which makes it odd for Him to ride out of it.
And the company he rides in is war, famine and death.

The text does not adjudicate, and the note does not either.

## The bow

A bow with no arrows mentioned.

Conquest at a distance, without close engagement.

## The order

White, red, black, pale. Four riders, and the last three are unambiguous: war, scarcity, and
death. See the notes on Revelation 6:3–4, Revelation 6:5–6 and
Revelation 6:7–8.

## What Jesus said in the same order

*"Take heed that no one **deceives** you. For many will come in My name, saying, 'I am the
Christ,' and will deceive many."*

*"And you will hear of **wars** and rumours of wars."*

*"And there will be **famines**, pestilences, and earthquakes."*

Deception, war, famine, death — in that sequence, in the Olivet discourse. See the note on
Matthew 24:4–8.

The correspondence is why the first rider is so often read as deception rather than as Christ.

## What He called it

*"All these are the **beginning of sorrows**."*

The Greek is **odinon** — birth pangs.

## What the seals are attached to

A scroll that nobody could open, held in the right hand of the One on the throne, and opened by
somebody who had been killed.

The whole sequence is under that hand.

> **The verb that governs the passage.** *Was given.* The crown, the sword, the authority — all
> passives. Nothing in the chapter seizes anything.
