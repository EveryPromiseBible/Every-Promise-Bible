The angel does not open the tomb to let Him out. He opens it so people can see in.

## The timing

*When the Sabbath was over, as **the first day of the week was beginning to grow light**.*

The Greek is **te epiphoskouse** — as it was dawning.

## Who goes

*Mary Magdalene and **the other Mary**.*

The other Mary is *the mother of James and Joses*, named at the crucifixion and at the burial.

Matthew has them watching the tomb on Friday: *sitting opposite the tomb*. They know where they
are going.

## Why they go

*Went out **to look at the tomb**.*

The Greek is **theoresai ton taphon** — to view the grave.

Matthew does not mention spices. In his account they come to look.

## The problem they should have

Matthew alone records the guard: *command that the tomb be made secure*, and *they went and made
the tomb secure, sealing the stone and setting the guard*.

A Roman seal and a watch.

## The earthquake

*And there was **a great earthquake**.*

The Greek is **seismos megas**.

Matthew records two: one at the death, when *the veil of the temple was torn in two* and *the earth
quaked, and the rocks were split*, and this one.

## The cause

*Because **an angel of the Lord came down out of heaven**.*

The earthquake is the effect, not the setting. Matthew gives the reason.

## The three actions

*And went to the stone and **rolled it back and sat down on it**.*

Rolled it back. Sat on it.

## What the sitting says

Nothing needs guarding any more.

The stone that sealed the tomb becomes a seat, and the angel occupies it.

## What the opening is for

Not to release Him.

He is already gone — the other accounts have Him out before the women arrive. The stone is moved
so that people can go in and see that it is empty.

## The appearance

*He shone like lightning, and his clothing was **white as snow**.*

The Greek is **hos astrape** — like lightning.

The same description as the transfiguration: *His face shone like the sun, and His clothes became
as white as the light*.

## The guards

*The guards saw him and **shook, and dropped to the ground like dead men**.*

The Greek is **egenethesan hos nekroi** — became as dead.

The only people in the account who see the angel arrive are professional soldiers, and they are
the ones who end up on the floor.

## The reversal

The dead man walks out; the guards fall down like corpses.

Matthew records the irony and does not comment on it.

## What the angel says

*"Do not be afraid, for I know that you seek Jesus who was crucified. **He is not here; for He is
risen, as He said**. Come, see the place where the Lord lay."*

*"And go quickly and tell His disciples."*

## What happens to the guards

*They assembled with the elders, and… gave a large sum of money to the soldiers, saying, "Tell them,
'His disciples came at night and stole Him away while we slept.'"*

A story requiring sleeping witnesses to testify about what happened while they slept.

*And this saying is commonly reported among the Jews until this day.*

> **What the stone is for.** Not to let Him out — He is gone before the women arrive. It is rolled
> back so people can look in, and the angel sits on it.
