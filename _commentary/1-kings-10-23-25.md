The summary of Solomon's reign at its height, and the reason people came is stated.

## The claim

*So King Solomon **grew greater than every king on earth**, both in **riches and in wisdom**.*

The Hebrew is *and King Solomon was greater than all the kings of the earth*.

Riches and wisdom, in that order here, and in the opposite order in the request that produced
them.

## What he had asked for

*"Give to Your servant **an understanding heart to judge Your people**, that I may discern
between good and evil."*

The Hebrew is **lev shomea** — a hearing heart.

And the answer: *because you have asked this thing, and have not asked long life for yourself,
nor riches for yourself… **behold, I have also given you what you have not asked**: both riches
and honour*. See the note on 1 Kings 3:5–14.

The riches arrived because they were not the request.

## The traffic

*And **the whole world wanted an audience with him** — everyone came **seeking Solomon's
face**.*

The Hebrew is *all the earth sought the face of Solomon*.

**Seeking the face** is the idiom used of seeking God: *seek My face*, and *seek the LORD and His
strength; seek His face evermore*.

## The reason given

*Longing **to hear the wisdom God had put in his heart**.*

Not the buildings, the army, or the gold.

And the phrasing is careful: *which God had put in his heart*. The narrator does not let the
wisdom belong to the man.

## The gifts

*Every one of them brought his gift — **objects of silver and gold, robes, weapons, spices,
horses, mules**.*

Six categories, and the list is the standing inventory of international diplomacy.

*A **steady stream of tribute, year in and year out**.*

## What the queen of Sheba had said

*"It was a true report which I heard in my own land… **however I did not believe the words until
I came and saw with my own eyes; and indeed the half was not told me**."*

And: *happy are your men and happy are these your servants, who stand continually before you and
hear your wisdom*.

## What Jesus does with her

*"**The queen of the South will rise up in the judgment with this generation** and condemn it,
for she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and indeed **a greater
than Solomon is here**."*

She travelled from the far end of the known world on a rumour.

## What happens after this chapter

Chapter 11 opens: *but King Solomon loved many foreign women*.

Seven hundred wives, three hundred concubines, and *his wives turned away his heart*.

*So the LORD became angry with Solomon, because his heart had turned from the LORD God of
Israel, **who had appeared to him twice**.*

The man who asked for a hearing heart ends the book with a turned one.

## What he wrote about it

*Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and **lean not on your own understanding**.* See the
note on Proverbs 3:5–8.

And *he who trusts in his own heart is a fool*. See the note on
Proverbs 28:21–28.

## The chapter's own note

Everything in the list — silver, gold, horses, wives — is on Deuteronomy's list of what a king
must not accumulate.

*But he shall not multiply horses for himself… neither shall he multiply wives for himself…
nor shall he greatly multiply silver and gold for himself.*

The narrator sets the splendour down without comment and lets the next chapter do the work.

> **What they came for.** Not the gold. *To hear the wisdom God had put in his heart* — and the
> narrator will not let the wisdom belong to him even at the height of it.
