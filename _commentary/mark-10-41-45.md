Ten men are furious with two, and He uses the argument to say why He came.

## What started it

James and John had asked for the seats on His right and left in glory.

They asked it immediately after the third and most detailed prediction of the
crucifixion. Mark puts the request straight after *they will mock Him, and scourge
Him, and spit on Him, and kill Him.*

## The indignation

*When the other ten heard about this, **they were indignant**.*

Not because the request was wrong. Because two of them got in first.

## How the world works

*"You know how it works among the nations. Their rulers **lord it over** people, and
the important ones **make sure everyone feels their weight**."*

Two Greek verbs, both compounds built on *down* — to dominate from above, to exercise
authority against.

He describes it accurately and without heat. That is simply how power operates.

## "It is not to be like that among you"

The Greek is blunt: *but not so is it among you.*

He does not abolish greatness. The next sentence assumes people will want it.

## The inversion

*"Whoever wants to be **great** among you must be **the one who serves you**, and
whoever wants to be **first** must be **the servant of all**."*

Two words, and the second is stronger. **Diakonos** is a servant, a waiter at a
table. **Doulos** is a slave — owned, with no standing at all.

Great → servant. First → slave of everyone.

## The reason

*"For even the Son of Man **did not come to be served, but to serve**."*

He does not argue the principle. He gives Himself as the case.

## The ransom

*"And **to give His life a ransom for many**."*

The Greek is **lutron** — the price paid to free a slave or a prisoner of war. It
appears twice in the New Testament, here and in Matthew's parallel.

And the preposition is **anti** — in place of, instead of. Not *on behalf of* but
*in the position of*.

Job's friend Elihu had said it in his own words: *deliver him from going down to the
pit; **I have found a ransom**.* See the note on Job 33:23–24.

## What Mark's whole Gospel is

The shortest and fastest of the four, with *immediately* used more than forty times,
and no genealogy or birth narrative — a book about a servant, and servants do not
need pedigrees.

This verse is generally taken as its key sentence.

## Where He was going when He said it

Up the road to Jerusalem, six days out. Bartimaeus is in the next paragraph, and the
triumphal entry in the next chapter. See the note on Mark 10:46–52.

## The two words at the end

*"For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and **to give His life a ransom
for many**."*

The Greek is **lytron anti pollon**.

**Lytron** — the price paid to free a slave or redeem a captive. It occurs twice in the New
Testament, here and in the parallel in **Matthew**, and both times it is **Jesus** saying it about
Himself.

**Anti** — in place of, instead of. Not *for the benefit of*; the preposition is one of exchange.

*Who gave Himself **a ransom for all**, to be testified in due time.*

**Paul** to **Timothy**, and his compound is **antilytron** — the preposition built into the noun.

## What it was not paid in

*Knowing that you were **not redeemed with corruptible things, like silver or gold**, from your
aimless conduct received by tradition from your fathers, but with **the precious blood of Christ**,
as of a lamb without blemish and without spot.*

*In Him we have **redemption through His blood**, the forgiveness of sins, **according to the riches
of His grace**.*

## The descent the paragraph describes

The disciples are arguing about seats. His answer is a job description and then a price.

*Who, being in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal with God, but **made
Himself of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant**… and being found in appearance as a
man, He humbled Himself and became obedient to the point of death, even the death of the blood covenant.*

**Philippians** 2's word for *bondservant* is **doulos** — the noun **Jesus** uses two verses
earlier: *whoever desires to be first among you shall be **slave** of all*.

He does not hold the standard over them. He states it and then says He is about to meet it.

*"**I am among you as the One who serves.**"*

> **What the ten and the two had in common.** All twelve wanted position. The
> difference between James and John and the other ten is that two of them asked out
> loud.
