The first thing He says to her is a request.

## Who is asking

A Jewish man, alone, tired, sitting on the edge of a well at noon with no bucket.

*Jesus was worn out from the journey, and He sat down just as He was beside the
well.* See the note on John 4:1–6.

## What He asks for

*"**Would you give Me a drink?**"*

Not an announcement, a rebuke, or an offer. A need, stated to a stranger, from a
position of not having.

He begins the conversation by putting Himself in her debt.

## What she hears in it

*"**How is it that You, a Jew, are asking me for a drink — me, a Samaritan
woman?**"*

She names three problems in one sentence: He is a Jew, she is a Samaritan, and she is
a woman.

John adds the fourth as an aside: *for Jews and Samaritans have nothing to do with
each other.*

The Greek is that they do not use vessels in common — a Jew would not drink from a
cup a Samaritan had handled.

He asked her for a drink knowing whose jar it was.

## Where the disciples are

*His disciples had **gone off into the town to buy food**.*

John notes it because it explains the privacy. Twelve men present and this
conversation does not happen.

When they come back they *marvelled that He talked with a woman*, and John says *yet
no one said, "What do You seek?" or "Why are You talking with her?"*

## Why she was there at noon

The sixth hour, the hottest part of the day. Water was drawn in the cool of the
morning and evening, by the women together.

A woman with five husbands and a sixth man who is not her husband draws water when
nobody else is at the well.

He is sitting there at exactly that hour.

## What He does with the request

He never gets the drink. The passage does not record her giving Him one.

Within a few exchanges He has moved from her water to *living water*, and then to
*whoever drinks of the water that I shall give him will never thirst.*

The request was the way in.

## What she leaves behind

*The woman then **left her waterpot**, went her way into the city, and said to the
men, "Come, see a Man who told me all things that I ever did."*

She came for water and walked off without the jar.

## And what He tells the disciples

They come back with lunch and He will not eat. *"My food is to do the will of Him
who sent Me."*

Then: *"Lift up your eyes and look at the fields, for they are already white for
harvest."*

He is looking at a Samaritan town walking toward Him because of a woman with no
reputation.

> **What He said He would have given.** *"If you knew the gift of God, and who it is
> who says to you, 'Give Me a drink,' you would have asked Him, and He would have
> given you living water."* The one asking for the drink was carrying the well.
