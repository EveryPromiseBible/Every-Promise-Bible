The interpretation of the vision knocks on the door while he is still working on it.

## The state he is in

*While Peter was **still turning the vision over in his mind, wondering what it could
mean**.*

The Greek is **dieporei** — thoroughly perplexed, at a complete loss.

He does not understand it. Luke says so twice in four verses.

The sheet full of animals has not yet produced a conclusion about people.

## What is happening downstairs

*The men Cornelius had sent **had asked their way to Simon's house and were standing at the
gate**.*

They had walked thirty miles from Caesarea. Luke has been running the two scenes in
parallel since the chapter opened.

## The question they ask

*They called out, asking **whether Simon, also called Peter, was staying there**.*

The exact name the angel gave Cornelius. See the note on Acts 10:1–8.

## The instruction

*Peter was still thinking about the vision when **the Spirit said to him**.*

## The four parts

*"Look, **three men are asking for you**."*

Information he could not have had. He is on the roof.

*"**Get up, go downstairs**."*

*"And **go with them without hesitating**."*

The Greek is **meden diakrinomenos** — making no distinction, doubting nothing.

The same root Peter uses at the council: *He made **no distinction at all** between us and
them.* See the note on Acts 15:6–9.

*"**Because I have sent them**."*

## The claim in the last clause

The men were sent by Cornelius, who was told by an angel.

The Spirit says *I have sent them*.

## The sequence of the chapter

An angel to a Roman at three in the afternoon. A sheet to a Jew at noon the next day. Three
men at a gate. And a sentence from the Spirit joining them.

Four separate movements, none of which the human parties arranged.

## What Peter does

*Then Peter went down to the men and said, "Yes, I am he whom you seek. **For what reason
have you come?**"*

He asks. He still does not know.

They tell him about Cornelius — *a just man, one who fears God and has a good reputation
among all the nation of the Jews* — and that an angel told him to send.

*Then he invited them in and lodged them.*

A Jew gives lodging to two Gentile servants and a Roman soldier, in a tanner's house.

That is the first thing that changes, and it happens before he leaves Joppa.

## Who goes with him

*And some brethren from Joppa accompanied him.*

Six of them, as he says later in Jerusalem — witnesses he brought deliberately. See the
note on Acts 11:4–14.

## What the vision meant

He works it out on the road, and states it on arrival: *God has shown me that I should call
no one unholy or unclean.* See the note on Acts 10:24–29.

The sheet had animals in it. The conclusion is about people, and Peter reaches it somewhere
between Joppa and Caesarea.

## What Peter preaches when he gets there

The Spirit says go. **Luke** records what he says on arrival, and the sentence has a phrase in it
nobody expected from a Galilean:

*"**The word which God sent to the children of Israel, preaching peace through Jesus Christ — He is
Lord of all.**"*

**Houtos estin panton kyrios** — this One is Lord of all. Said in a Roman officer's house, about a
man executed by Rome.

*For **He Himself is our peace**, who has made both one, and has broken down the middle wall of
separation.*

## Doubting nothing

*"**Go with them, doubting nothing, for I have sent them.**"*

**Meden diakrinomenos** — making no distinction, hesitating not at all. The same verb **Peter** uses
at the council: *God made no distinction between us and them.*

*"**For God so loved the world.**"*

## The one who arranged both ends

An angel to Cornelius in Caesarea, a sheet to **Peter** in Joppa, and three men at a gate at the
moment the vision finishes.

*"**All that the Father gives Me will come to Me.**"*

> **When the answer arrives.** Not in the vision. Peter is still perplexed when three men
> turn up at the gate — and the Spirit's instruction is simply to go with them without
> making distinctions.
