He asks the question Himself, in public, and nobody answers it.

## Where He is

*While Jesus was **teaching in the temple courts**.*

Passion week. He has been questioned by four delegations in two days and each has
gone away with nothing.

Now He asks.

## The question

*"**How is it that the teachers of the law say the Messiah is the son of David?**"*

Not a denial. Every Jew held it, and it is true — Matthew opens his Gospel with the
genealogy proving it.

He is asking why they stop there.

## The quotation

*"David himself, **speaking by the Holy Spirit**, said: '**The Lord said to my Lord**:
sit at My right hand until I make Your enemies a footstool under Your feet.'"*

Psalm 110:1, the most-quoted Old Testament verse in the New Testament.

Two different words. The first **Lord** is YHWH. The second is **Adonai** — my Lord.

## The problem

*"**David himself calls Him Lord — so how can He be David's son?**"*

In that culture the honour ran upward. A father does not address his own descendant
as *my Lord*.

Unless the descendant existed before him.

## Who He credits with the psalm

*Speaking by the Holy Spirit.*

He attributes the authorship in the middle of the argument, and the whole case
depends on the wording being exact.

## The sitting

*"**Sit at My right hand until** I make Your enemies a footstool."*

Hebrews builds a chapter on that posture: every priest *stands ministering daily*,
and this one *sat down*. See the note on Hebrews 10:11–13.

The sitting is not idleness. It is what you do when the work is finished, and the
subduing that follows is somebody else's.

## Who answered

Nobody. Matthew's parallel adds: *no one was able to answer Him a word, nor from that
day on did anyone dare question Him anymore.* See the note on Matthew 22:41–46.

## The reaction

*And **the great crowd listened to Him with delight**.*

Mark alone records this. The Greek is that they heard Him gladly.

The scholars were silenced and the crowd enjoyed it.

## What He says next

*"Beware of the scribes, who **desire to go around in long robes**, love greetings in
the marketplaces, the best seats in the synagogues… **who devour widows' houses**."*

And then He sits down opposite the treasury and watches a widow put in two mites.

> **What Psalm 110 says next.** *The LORD has sworn and will not relent: You are a
> priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.* A king on David's throne who
> is also a priest of a line older than Aaron's — and Hebrews takes two chapters over
> it. See the note on Genesis 14:17–20.
