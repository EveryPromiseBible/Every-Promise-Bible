The answer to the pit, and the tense changes.

## Where he calls from

*I called on Your name, O LORD, **from the deepest pit**.*

The Hebrew is **mibbor tachtiyyot** — from the lowest cistern. See the note on
Lamentations 3:52–54.

Not from the far side of the rescue. From inside it.

*"**I cried out to the LORD because of my affliction, and He answered me. Out of the belly of
Sheol I cried**, and You heard my voice."*

Jonah, from inside the fish, and the same two verbs in the same order — cried, heard.

*"For as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the great fish, so will the Son of
Man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth."*

## What he calls on

*I called on **Your name**.*

*For "**whoever calls on the name of the LORD shall be saved**." How then shall they call on Him
in whom they have not believed?*

**Paul** quotes Joel in **Romans** 10 and applies it to **Jesus** — *if you confess with your
mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead, you will be
saved.* The name that answers from the bottom of a cistern is given a name in the New Testament.

## The four verbs

*You **heard** my voice… do not **close** Your ears… You **came near** on the day I called… You
**said** to me, Do not be afraid.*

Heard, listened, drew near, spoke. The rescue is described as a sequence of things God does, and
none of them is *pulled me out*. That is not in the passage.

What the man reports is company.

## Came near

*Qaravta beyom eqra'eka* — You drew near in the day I called.

*But now in **Christ Jesus** you who once were far off **have been brought near by the blood of
Christ**.*

**Ephesians** 2, and **Paul**'s verb is **engys egenethete** — you became near. In Lamentations
God does the approaching; in **Ephesians** the distance is closed from the same side.

*"**Do not be afraid**; I am the First and the Last. I am He who lives, and was dead, and behold,
I am alive forevermore."*

**Revelation** 1. The last person in Scripture to hear the sentence Lamentations 3:57 ends on
hears it from somebody who has been in a tomb.

## The tense

Every verb in these three lines is past. He is still in the pit when the poem is written and the
hearing is reported as done.

*And this is the confidence that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to His will, He
hears us. And **if we know that He hears us, whatever we ask, we know that we have the petitions
that we have asked of Him**.*

> **What the rescue consists of.** He is not lifted out in these verses. God hears, listens, comes
> near, and speaks — and the sentence He speaks is the one the risen Christ says to John: *do not
> be afraid.*
