---
key: Hebrews 2:5–8
title: ""But we do not see it yet""
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The writer quotes a psalm about the dignity of man, and then does something unusual: he admits it is
not true of anybody you can currently look at.

## The psalm

*"**What is man, that You keep him in mind?** … You put him in charge of everything Your hands have
made. You put it all under his feet."*

Psalm 8, written by a shepherd looking up at a night sky and concluding that the astonishing thing
is not the size of the universe but the attention God pays to the small thing standing under it.

And the psalm is about dominion. Everything under his feet, nothing left outside — which is Genesis
1 restated as praise.

## A little under the angels

*You set him **a little under** the angels.*

**Brachy ti** — the Greek can mean *a little in rank* or *a little while in time*, and the writer is
about to use both senses in the next sentence.

## The honest admission

*But **we do not see everything under him yet**.*

That is a remarkable thing to put in a sermon. He has just quoted Scripture on man's dominion, and
he stops to say that a look around does not confirm it.

Nobody has to be told. The world is plainly not under the feet of the human race — it is more often
the other way round, and the letter is written to people being pushed out of their livelihoods for
following Jesus.

The writer will not pretend. He never says *look harder* or *claim it by faith*. He says: not yet,
and you can see that for yourselves.

## Where he takes it

The sentence does not end in disappointment. The very next clause is the turn the whole letter runs
on — *what we do see is **Jesus***, crowned with the glory and honour the psalm described, having
gone under the angels a little while in order to die.

The dominion Adam lost is not being rebuilt in us. It is held, intact, in a Man — and He is
*bringing many sons to glory*.

> **What you cannot see yet is already true in Him.** Psalm 8 is not an unfulfilled promise; it is
> fulfilled, in a Person, at the right hand of God. The reason it is not yet visible in your
> circumstances is timing, not failure — and the writer's cure for the delay is not more effort but
> a different direction to look.
