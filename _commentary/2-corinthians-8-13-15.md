---
key: 2 Corinthians 8:13–15
title: A matter of evening out
updated: 2026-08-07
---

The word he uses is not charity. It is equality.

## What he rules out

*I am **not asking that others be given relief while you are pressed down**.*

The Greek is **hina allois anesis, humin thlipsis** — that for others relief, for you pressure.

The collection is not a transfer of hardship.

## The word

*But that **things be evened out**.*

The Greek is **isotēs** — equality, fairness, evenness.

The word appears twice in three verses and once elsewhere in Paul, of masters treating slaves
*justly and fairly*.

## What it is not

Not a levelling by decree. The whole appeal is voluntary — *I speak not by commandment*.

## The present arrangement

*At this present moment **your plenty covers what they lack**.*

The Greek is **to humōn perisseuma eis to ekeinōn husterēma**.

**Perisseuma** — surplus, what is left over. **Husterēma** — deficit, what is short.

Two accounting terms.

## The reciprocity

*So that one day **their plenty may cover what you lack**.*

Not stated as a debt to be called in.

The clause allows for the direction to reverse, and names no date.

## What is being collected

Money from the Gentile churches of Greece for the poor believers in Jerusalem.

Paul spent years on it and carried it there himself, which is what got him arrested.

## Who he holds up as the example

*The churches of Macedonia… **in a great trial of affliction, the abundance of their joy and their
deep poverty abounded in the riches of their liberality**.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 8:1–4.

They were poor, and they asked to be included.

## The theological ground

*For **you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He
became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich.* See the note on
2 Corinthians 8:9.

The argument for the collection is a sentence about the incarnation.

## The quotation

*"**The one who gathered much had nothing left over, and the one who gathered little had no lack**."*

## Where it comes from

The manna.

*So when they measured it by omers, he who gathered much had nothing over, and he who gathered
little had no lack.* See the note on Exodus 16:16–18.

## What happened there

Everyone was told to gather an omer per person. Some collected more and some less, and when it was
measured out at the tent, it came to the same.

The text does not explain the mechanism.

## What kept it honest

*But some of them left part of it until morning, and **it bred worms and stank**.*

Hoarding was made impossible by the substance itself.

## What Paul does with it

He applies a wilderness food-distribution story to a first-century relief fund, and the connecting
word is **isotēs**.

## The care taken with the money

*Avoiding this: that anyone should blame us in this lavish gift… **providing honourable things, not
only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men**.* See the note on
2 Corinthians 8:20–21.

Two other men travel with it. Paul will not carry the money alone.

## The principle stated

*He who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly… **God loves a cheerful giver**.* See the note on
2 Corinthians 9:6–7.

The Greek is **hilaron** — the root of *hilarious*.

> **The word.** Not charity — **isotēs**, evenness. And the proof text is the morning the manna
> measured out the same for everyone regardless of who gathered what.
