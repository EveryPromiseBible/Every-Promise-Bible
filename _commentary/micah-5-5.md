The verses after Bethlehem, and the first clause of the second one is quoted in Ephesians.

## What has just been said

*"But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, small among the clans of Judah… out of you will come One who will
be ruler over Israel, whose goings forth are from of old, from everlasting."* See the note on
Micah 5:2.

## What He does

*"And he will **stand and shepherd his flock in the strength of the LORD**."*

Two verbs, and the first is unexpected. **Ve'amad** — and he will stand. A king is enthroned; a
shepherd stands, because standing is what you do all day with animals.

*"**I am the good shepherd.** The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep."*

*Now may the God of peace who brought up our Lord **Jesus** from the dead, **that great Shepherd of
the sheep**.*

## The security

*"Then **his people will settle in and stay**."*

The Hebrew is **veyashavu** — and they shall dwell, sit, remain. The verb of settling rather than
visiting.

## The reach

*"For now **his greatness will stretch to the ends of the earth**."*

Said of a man from a village Micah has just called too small to count.

*"**All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.** Go therefore and make disciples of
all the nations."*

## The clause

*"**And he himself will be their peace.**"*

The Hebrew is **vehayah zeh shalom** — and this One shall be peace.

Not *he will bring peace*, and not *he will make peace*. He will **be** it.

*For **He Himself is our peace**, who has made both one, and has broken down the middle wall of
separation… so as to create in Himself one new man from the two, thus making peace.*

**Ephesians** 2, and the Greek is **autos gar estin he eirene hemon** — for He Himself is our peace.
**Paul** is quoting Micah, and the wall he names is the barrier in the temple court that Gentiles
could not pass.

*And by Him to reconcile all things to Himself, by Him, whether things on earth or things in
heaven, **having made peace through the blood of His cross**.*

**Colossians** says where the peace was made, and it is not a treaty.

*"**Peace I leave with you, My peace I give to you**; not as the world gives do I give to you."*

## The Assyrian

*"When the Assyrian marches into our land… **we will raise up against him seven shepherds, eight
leaders of the people**."*

A numerical idiom — seven, and then eight — meaning more than enough. Assyria was the terror of
Micah's lifetime and the empire that took the northern kingdom.

The passage answers the largest army in the world with a shepherd from a village and an
over-sufficient count of others.

> **The three words.** *He Himself is peace.* Not a peacemaker and not a peace treaty — Micah puts
> the noun where the verb should be, and Ephesians 2 quotes it back with a name attached.
