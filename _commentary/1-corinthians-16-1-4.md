Paul has just finished the great resurrection chapter, and the next sentence is about
money.

## What comes before it

Chapter 15 ends *therefore, my beloved brethren, be steadfast, immovable, always
abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labour is not in vain in the
Lord.*

Then: *now about the collection.*

Paul puts the two next to each other with nothing in between.

## The instruction is not new

*Do **just as I directed the churches of Galatia** to do.*

He is repeating an arrangement already running elsewhere. Corinth is being brought into
a scheme, not given a personal appeal.

## The day

*On **the first day of the week**.*

One of the earliest references to Christians organising anything around Sunday — this
letter predates the Gospels.

It is the day of the resurrection, the day the Emmaus road happened, the day the
disciples were gathered when He came through the door, and the day Troas met to break
bread. See the note on Acts 20:7–12.

## The four parts of the instruction

*Let **each one of you** **set something aside**, **storing it up** **in keeping with how
well he has done**.*

- **Each one** — nobody exempt, nobody named.
- **Set aside** — the Greek is *put by himself*, at home, personally.
- **Storing it up** — accumulating, not a single gesture.
- **In keeping with how well he has done** — the Greek is *as he may prosper*.

No sum is stated. No percentage is stated. The measure is each person's own account of
how the week went.

## Why weekly

*So that **there will be no collections to make when I come**.*

Paul does not want to arrive and start fundraising. He wants it already done, so that
his visit is about something else.

It also means nobody gives under the pressure of an apostle standing in the room.

## Who carries it

*Whomever **you approve by your letters** I will send to carry your gift to Jerusalem.*

They choose the couriers, not him, and the appointment is in writing.

Paul keeps his own hands off the money. He says why in 2 Corinthians: *avoiding this,
that anyone should blame us in this lavish gift… providing honourable things, not only
in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men.*

## "And if it is fitting that I go as well"

He treats his own involvement as optional and puts it last.

## Where it was going

Jerusalem — the same relief effort that runs across four of Paul's letters, from Gentile
churches to the Jewish believers in Judea. The famine had come as Agabus said it would.
See the note on Acts 11:27–30.

## What the chapter is doing

Chapter 15: the dead are raised, death is swallowed up, your labour is not in vain.
Chapter 16: put something aside on Sunday.

The resurrection argument ends in a practical arrangement about a collection.

## What the collection was

Money from Gentile churches to poor Jewish believers in Jerusalem. **Paul** organises it across four
letters and spends two chapters of **2 Corinthians** arguing for it.

*For you know **the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He
became poor, that you through His poverty might become rich**.*

**2 Corinthians** 8:9, and it is the argument. Not duty, not proportion — the incarnation applied to
a budget.

*Now concerning **the grace of God bestowed on the churches of Macedonia**: that in a great trial of
affliction, the abundance of their joy and their deep poverty abounded in the riches of their
liberality.*

**Paul** calls their giving **charis** — grace — five times in those two chapters, of both the gift and
the giving.

## The arrangement

*On the first day of the week let each one of you lay something aside, **storing up as he may
prosper**, that there be no collections when I come.*

No amount, no percentage, and no collection taken in the meeting. **Paul** sets up a standing order
so that nothing is raised under pressure.

*So let each one give as he purposes in his heart, not grudgingly or of necessity; for **God loves a
cheerful giver**.*

> **What is missing from the instruction.** Any figure. Paul, who could simply have set
> one, tells each person to weigh their own week and put something by.
