Two images, and the grammar under both of them is the thing to notice.

## Not "become"

*You **are** the salt of the earth. You **are** the light of the world.*

Present indicative, stated as fact. Jesus is not setting a target. He is telling
a crowd — who at this point in Matthew have done nothing but climb a hill and sit
down — what they already are.

Everything He says afterwards follows from the statement rather than earning it.

## What can actually go wrong

The two images fail in different ways, and neither failure is about quantity.

- **salt** can go flat — lose the thing that made it useful. What is left is not
  weaker salt; it is something that gets walked on
- **light** can be hidden — nobody in the illustration puts out the lamp. They
  put a bucket over it

So the warnings are about losing distinctiveness and about concealment. Not about
shining harder.

## Where the attention goes

*So that when they see the good you do, they will give the glory to your Father
in heaven.*

The visibility is deliberate — a town on a hill, a lamp on a stand, light filling
a house. But the sentence ends somewhere other than where it started: what people
see is the good; who they credit is the Father.

> **A page later.** In chapter 6 Jesus warns against doing good *to be seen by
> people*. It looks like a contradiction of this and is not — the difference is
> in the last clause of each. Let them see the good so they glorify God; do not
> perform it so they admire you. Same visibility, opposite destination.
