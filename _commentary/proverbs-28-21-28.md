Eight couplets about money, and the sharpest of them is about a man in a hurry.

## The hurry

*The **stingy man hurries after wealth** and never knows that want is coming for
him.*

The Hebrew behind *stingy* is *a man with an evil eye* — the idiom for a grudging,
tight, envious way of looking at things. Jesus uses the same expression: *if your
eye is bad, your whole body will be full of darkness.*

Two things are said about him: he hurries, and he does not know what is coming.

## What is coming

*Want.* The Hebrew is lack, poverty — and the verb has it arriving at him, coming
upon him.

He is running toward money with the thing he is running from already on the road.

## Trust and enrichment

*The **greedy heart stirs up strife**, but **whoever trusts in the LORD will be
enriched**.*

The two halves are the alternative to each other. A grasping heart produces
conflict; trust produces increase.

The Hebrew for *enriched* is to be made fat — the word for prosperity and fullness.

## The gift that does not empty

*Whoever **gives to the poor will lack nothing**, but whoever shuts his eyes to
them gathers many curses.*

The Hebrew is *shall not lack*. Nothing at all.

And the opposite is not *will be poor* — it is *gathers many curses*. The man who
shuts his eyes is still gathering; the chapter simply names what he is collecting.

## Rebuke and flattery

*Whoever **rebukes** a man will find more favour at last than the one who
**flatters** with his tongue.*

*At last* — afterward, in the end. The flatterer is preferred now.

Set that beside a king who *never once crossed* his son, never asked *why do you do
this?* See the note on 1 Kings 1:5–10.

## Trusting your own heart

*Whoever **trusts his own heart is a fool**, but whoever walks in wisdom will be
kept safe.*

It is the flat contradiction of the usual advice.

## The last couplet

*When the wicked rise, people go into hiding, **but when they perish, the righteous
increase**.*

The chapter says it twice — the same line appears seven verses earlier.

> **What the same chapter says about interest.** *Whoever swells his wealth by
> interest and gain **gathers it for one who is kind to the poor**.* He is still
> gathering. The proverb only disagrees with him about who the pile is for. See the
> note on Proverbs 28:7–13.
