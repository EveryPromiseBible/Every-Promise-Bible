Four attributes, arranged as architecture: two underneath, two walking in front.

## The foundation

*Righteousness and justice are **the ground beneath Your throne**.*

The Hebrew is **tzedeq umishpat mekon kis'eka** — the fixed place, the base, of Your throne.

**Makon** — an established site, a foundation.

## Why that matters

A throne resting on power can do anything.

This one is described as resting on two things that constrain what it can do — and they are named
first.

## The two in front

*Steadfast love and faithfulness **walk out before Your face**.*

The Hebrew is **chesed ve'emet yeqaddemu paneika** — go before Your face.

**Chesed** — covenant loyalty. **Emet** — truth, reliability.

## The arrangement

Righteousness and justice hold the throne up. Mercy and truth go ahead of it, as heralds do.

What is met first is not the foundation.

## The pairing elsewhere

*And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us… **full of grace and truth**.* See the note on
John 1:14–18.

The Greek is **charitos kai alētheias** — the New Testament's rendering of **chesed ve'emet**.

## The people

*Happy are the people who **know the glad shout**.*

The Hebrew is **teru'ah** — the blast of the trumpet, the shout at a coronation or when the ark
moved.

**Yode'ei teru'ah** — those who know the shout. Who recognise the sound and what it means.

## The walking

*Who walk, O LORD, **in the light of Your face**.*

The Hebrew is **be'or-paneika**.

The blessing of Aaron: *the LORD make His face shine upon you*.

## Where the gladness is

*In Your name they are glad all day long, and **in Your righteousness they are lifted high**.*

The Hebrew is **uvetzidqateka yarumu** — in Your righteousness they are exalted.

Not their own. The word that holds up the throne is the word they are raised by.

## The strength

*For **You are the glory of their strength**, and by **Your favor our head is raised**.*

The Hebrew is **birtzoneka** — in Your **ratzon**, Your favour, good pleasure.

The same word as the shield of favour in Psalm 5. See the note on Psalms 5:11–12.

## The last line

*For **our shield belongs to the LORD**, our king to the Holy One of Israel.*

The Hebrew is **ki laYHWH maginnenu** — for to the LORD is our shield.

The king is spoken of as a shield, and the shield is stated to be on loan.

## What the psalm is

The longest treatment of the Davidic covenant in the Psalter.

*"I have made a covenant with My chosen… **your seed I will establish forever**."* See the note on
Psalms 89:3–4.

## How it ends

*But **You have cast off and abhorred**… You have profaned his crown by casting it to the ground.*

The psalm that spends fifty verses on the promise spends the last twenty on its apparent collapse,
and closes: *Lord, where are Your former lovingkindnesses, which You swore to David in Your truth?*

## The two words in that question

**Chasadeika** and **be'emunateka** — the same pair from verse 14.

The psalmist ends by holding God to the two attributes he said walk out in front.

> **The architecture.** Righteousness and justice hold the throne up; mercy and truth go ahead of it.
> What anyone approaching meets first is not the foundation.
