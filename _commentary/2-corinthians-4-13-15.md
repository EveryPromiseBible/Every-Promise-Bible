Paul names something he shares with a psalmist who lived a thousand years before
him.

## The phrase

*We have **the same spirit of faith** as the one who wrote, "**I believed, and so I
spoke**."*

He is quoting Psalm 116. And he does not say *the same faith* — he says the same
**spirit** of faith, as though it were a disposition, a way of operating, that both
men were working out of.

## The order in it

**Believed**, then **spoke**. The speaking comes out of the believing, not the other
way round.

*We too believe, and so we speak.* Paul repeats the pattern about himself in the
same sentence, which is the whole point of the quotation.

## What the psalmist was in

Psalm 116 is not a victory song written afterwards. Read the line Paul is quoting
in its own setting:

*I believed, even when I said, "**I am badly shaken**." In my alarm I said, "Every
person is a liar."*

He believed and he said he was shaken, in one breath. The psalm has him with *the
pains of death* around him and *the sorrows of Sheol*.

So the spirit of faith Paul is claiming is not one that denies the state it is in.
The psalmist says both things — I am badly shaken, and I believed. See the note on
Psalms 116:8–11.

## What Paul was in

The same chapter, a few verses earlier: *hard pressed on every side… perplexed…
persecuted… struck down.*

And his own list later in the letter: beaten five times with thirty-nine lashes,
three times with rods, once stoned, three shipwrecks, a night and a day in the
open sea.

That is the man saying *we too believe, and so we speak.*

## What he believes

*Knowing that **the One who raised up the Lord Jesus will raise us up as well**,
and stand us in His presence together with you.*

The content of the faith is a resurrection — one that has happened, and one that
will.

Paul's confidence is not about the next few weeks. It reaches past the outcome of
whatever is currently pressing on him.

## Who it is for

*All of it is **for your sake**, so that the grace reaching more and more people may
make **thanksgiving overflow**, to the glory of God.*

He turns even his own beatings outward. The chapter has already said it: *death is
working in us, but life in you.*

> **The verses on either side.** Before: *we have this treasure in earthen vessels,
> that the excellence of the power may be of God and not of us.* After: *though our
> outward man is perishing, yet the inward man is being renewed day by day.* The
> spirit of faith sits between a clay pot and a body wearing out, and speaks anyway.
