The last lines of a prayer prayed underwater, and the final sentence is the centre of the book.

## Where he is

Inside the fish, having gone down *to the moorings of the mountains* with *seaweed twined about my
head*. See the note on Jonah 1:15–17.

## The prayer

It is almost entirely quotation. Line after line comes out of the psalms — *the waters surrounded
me*, *I went down*, *You brought up my life from the pit*, *when my soul fainted within me, I
remembered the LORD*.

A man at the bottom of the sea praying in borrowed words, which is what the psalms are for.

## The tense

*"**But I, with a song of thanks, will offer myself to You.**"*

**Toda** — thanksgiving. Not a petition. Jonah gives thanks for a rescue that has not visibly
happened, from inside the thing he needs rescuing from.

The fish does not vomit him out until the verse after the prayer ends.

## The line about idols

*"Those who **cling to worthless idols forfeit the mercy that could be theirs**."*

The Hebrew is **meshammerim havlei-shav ya'azovu chasdam** — those who keep vain vanities forsake
their **chesed**.

**Hevel** — vapour, the Ecclesiastes word. And **chasdam** — their covenant-love, the mercy that
belonged to them.

Said by a man who had just spent a chapter running from God while pagan sailors prayed.

## The last sentence

*"**Salvation belongs to the LORD.**"*

**Yeshuatah la-YHWH.**

Three words in Hebrew, and it is the theological centre of the book. Rabbinic tradition marks it as
the middle verse.

The noun is **yeshuah** — the word behind the name **Yeshua**.

*"And you shall call His name **JESUS**, for He will save His people from their sins."*

*"**Salvation belongs to our God** who sits on the throne, and to the Lamb!"*

**Revelation** 7, and the Greek is **he soteria to theo hemon** — the same sentence, sung by a crowd
nobody could number, out of every nation.

## Why the sentence matters here

Jonah says it, and then spends chapter 4 furious that God acted on it.

He is angry precisely because salvation belongs to the LORD and not to Jonah. If it were his, he
could withhold it from Nineveh. It is not, and he cannot.

*So then it is **not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who shows mercy**.*

**Romans** 9, and **Paul**'s verb for running is the book of Jonah in one word.

## What happens next

*Then the LORD spoke to the fish, and it delivered Jonah up onto the dry land.*

The rescue follows the thanksgiving, and the fish is described as obeying.

> **The three words.** *Yeshuatah la-YHWH* — salvation belongs to the LORD. Jonah says it at the
> bottom of the sea and then spends the last chapter angry about it, because a salvation that
> belongs to God is one he cannot keep from anybody.
