An invitation shouted at the edges of the world, and then an oath.

## The invitation

*Turn to Me and be saved, **all the ends of the earth**.*

The Hebrew for *turn* is to look, to face toward. The older rendering is *look
unto Me*.

It is the same posture as the bronze serpent in the wilderness — a nation dying of
snakebite, told to look at something lifted up.

That is the whole instruction. Not climb, not fetch, not achieve. Turn your face
this way.

## Who it is addressed to

*All the ends of the earth.* Written to Israel, in exile, and aimed past them at
everybody.

*For I am God, and there is no other.* The chapter says it repeatedly — Isaiah 45 is
the most concentrated statement of monotheism in the Old Testament, and it is
addressed partly to Cyrus, a Persian king named a century and a half before he was
born.

## The oath

*By My own self I have sworn.*

There is nobody higher to swear by. Hebrews makes the point: *because He could
swear by no one greater, He swore by Himself.*

## What was sworn

*My mouth has spoken **righteousness**, a word that **will not come back empty**.*

The Hebrew is that it will not return — the same idea as chapter 55: *my word shall
not return to Me void, but it shall accomplish what I please.*

Righteousness is what the sworn word is about, and it is spoken out of His own
mouth rather than required from anybody's hands.

## The result

*Before Me **every knee will bow**, by Me **every tongue will swear**.*

Paul quotes it twice — in Romans 14, of the judgement seat, and in Philippians 2,
where it is the climax of the passage about Christ emptying Himself:

*That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow… and every tongue should confess
that Jesus Christ is Lord.*

Paul takes a verse where God swears by Himself and applies it, without comment, to
Jesus.

## The knee and the tongue

The Hebrew of the second clause is that every tongue will *swear* — will make its
own declaration.

The two go together in the verse: a knee bent and a mouth open. And the next verse
in Isaiah is what the mouth says: *surely in the LORD I have righteousness and
strength.*

Not in myself. In Him, and said out loud. See the note on Romans 10:5–8.

> **What comes right after.** *In the LORD all the descendants of Israel shall be
> justified, and shall glory.* Justified — the same word Paul builds Romans on, in
> a prophet writing seven centuries earlier.
