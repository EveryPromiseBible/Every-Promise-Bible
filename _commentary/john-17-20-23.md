The last section of the longest prayer of Jesus recorded anywhere, and it widens to
include people who did not exist yet.

## Who He is praying for

*"I am **not praying for these alone**. I am praying also for **everyone who will
believe in Me through their word**."*

Eleven men in a room, and He prays past them to everybody who would believe on the
strength of what they said.

That is every Christian who has ever lived, prayed for by name of category, hours
before the arrest.

## The request

*"**That they may all be one**."*

Said four times in these verses.

## The standard

*"**Just as You, Father, are in Me and I am in You**."*

Not one in the sense of an alliance or an agreement. The comparison offered is the
union of the Father and the Son.

## What it is for

*"So that **the world may believe** that You sent Me."*

And again in the next verse: *"so that **the world may know** that You sent Me."*

Twice, and both times the reason for the unity is what people outside would conclude
from it.

## What He gave them

*"**The glory You gave Me I have given to them**."*

Past tense, said the night before the crucifixion.

Whatever that glory is, He describes it as already handed over.

## The last clause

*"…and have **loved them just as You have loved Me**."*

That is the sentence.

Not loved them well, or loved them greatly. *Just as* — the same measure as the
Father's love for the Son.

He asks the world to know two things: that He was sent, and that these people are
loved on that scale.

## Where the prayer has come from

Chapter 17 has three movements: He prays for Himself, then for the eleven, then for
everybody who will believe.

For the eleven: *I have kept them; not one of them is lost except the son of
perdition.* See the note on John 17:9–12.

## Where He goes next

*When Jesus had spoken these words, **He went out with His disciples over the Brook
Kidron**, where there was a garden.*

The prayer ends and He walks straight to Gethsemane.

## What He does not pray

Nothing here asks for their circumstances to improve. Earlier in the chapter: *I do
not pray that You should take them out of the world, but that You should keep them
from the evil one.*

> **What He says about being heard.** Standing at Lazarus's tomb: *Father, I thank You
> that You have heard Me. And I know that You always hear Me.* This prayer was prayed
> by somebody who had said that.
