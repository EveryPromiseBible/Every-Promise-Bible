A governor with a half-built temple and an empty treasury gets one sentence.

## The situation

Zerubbabel had laid the foundation of the second temple sixteen years earlier. Then
the work stopped — opposition, discouragement, a letter to the Persian court, and a
building site standing open.

The old men who remembered Solomon's temple had wept when they saw the foundation.

## The word

*"**Not by might nor by power, but by My Spirit**, says the LORD Almighty."*

**Might** is *chayil* — an army, a force, collective strength.

**Power** is *koach* — individual strength, the capacity of one man. It is the same
word in Deuteronomy 8: *He gives you the power to gain wealth.*

Both are ruled out — the group's resources and the man's own — and one thing is put
in their place.

## The mountain

*"**What are you, mighty mountain?** Before Zerubbabel you will become level
ground."*

The mountain is addressed directly, not prayed about.

Whatever the obstacle was — Persian bureaucracy, hostile neighbours, no money — it is
spoken to and told what it will become.

## The capstone

*"And he will bring out the capstone while the people shout, '**Grace, grace to
it**.'"*

The final stone, the one that completes a building.

And the shout that goes up as it is set is not *well done*, or *at last*, or a
tribute to Zerubbabel. It is one word, said twice: **chen, chen** — grace, grace.

The house began with grace and is finished with the same word shouted over the last
stone.

## The hands

*"**The hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation** of this house, and **his hands
will complete it**."*

The same pair of hands at both ends. Sixteen years of stoppage in between, and the
promise is that the man who started it finishes it.

Paul says the same of a different work: *He who has begun a good work in you will
complete it.*

## The day of small things

*"**Who dares despise the day of small things?**"*

The Hebrew is literally *who has despised the day of small beginnings*.

It is asked as a rebuke, and the answer in context is: the people who saw the
foundation and wept because it was not Solomon's.

*For they will rejoice when they see **the plumb line** in the hand of Zerubbabel.*

A plumb line is a weight on a string. That is the small thing — one man holding a
builder's tool.

## The seven eyes

*"These seven are **the eyes of the LORD, which range through the whole earth**."*

The chapter's vision is a lampstand with seven lamps, and this is what the seven
are.

Revelation picks it up: the Lamb with *seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of
God sent out into all the earth.* See the note on Revelation 5:5–6.

> son of Iddo**.* Ezra 6 records the finish, and names the prophet who gave this
