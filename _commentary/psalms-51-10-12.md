Three verses, six requests, and every verb has God as its subject.

## The verb

*Create in me a clean heart, O God.*

The Hebrew is **bara**.

It is the verb of Genesis 1:1, and in the Hebrew Bible its subject is always God. No human being
ever **bara**s anything.

David does not ask for the heart to be cleaned. He asks for one to be made.

## What the word implies

Something that is not there.

He has spent five verses on washing and blotting out — the vocabulary of laundry and of erasing
a debt. Then he changes register.

## The second

*And **renew a steady spirit** within me.*

The Hebrew is **ruach nakhon** — a firm, established, right spirit.

**Nakhon** is used of things that are set up and will hold: a throne established, a house
founded on rock.

He is asking for stability, which is exactly what the last year of his life had lacked.

## The fear

*Do not **cast me away from Your presence**, do not **take Your Holy Spirit from me**.*

David had watched it happen.

*But the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul, and a distressing spirit from the LORD troubled
him.*

He had been in the room. He was the boy brought in to play the harp when it happened.

This is not an abstract anxiety.

## What is different under the new covenant

*I will pray the Father, and He will give you another Helper, **that He may abide with you
forever**.* See the note on John 14:15–17.

And *in whom also, having believed, **you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise**, who is
the guarantee of our inheritance*.

The request in this psalm is the one prayer a believer is told not to need to pray.

## The restoration

*Give me back **the joy of Your salvation**.*

Not the salvation. The joy of it.

The Hebrew is **hashivah li** — return to me.

He does not ask to be re-saved. He asks for something that has gone missing to come back.

## The last

*And **hold me up with a willing spirit**.*

The Hebrew is **ruach nedivah** — a willing, generous, noble spirit.

**Nadiv** is the word for a freewill offering, and for a prince.

Third *spirit* in three verses: steady, holy, and willing.

## What he promises next

*Then I will teach transgressors Your ways, and sinners shall be converted to You.*

The teaching is conditional on the restoring. A man in his condition had nothing to say until
this had happened.

## Where the psalm ends

*The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and a contrite heart — these, O God, You
will not despise.*

And then, oddly, two verses about rebuilding the walls of Jerusalem and offering bulls on the
altar — which most readers take as a later addition, or as the man's mind moving from himself to
the city.

## Where the new heart is promised

*I will give you a new heart, and put a new spirit within you; I will take the heart of stone out
of your flesh and give you a heart of flesh.* See the note on Ezekiel 36:26.

David asks for it. Ezekiel is told it will be given.

> **The verb he chooses.** *Bara* — the Genesis verb, whose subject is never a human being. Not
> clean this one. Make one.
