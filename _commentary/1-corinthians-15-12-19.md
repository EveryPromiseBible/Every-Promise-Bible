Paul takes the position he is arguing against and follows it all the way down.

## The problem in Corinth

*How is it that **some among you are saying there is no resurrection of the dead?**"*

Not that Christ did not rise. That the dead in general are not raised.

Greek thought had no difficulty with the soul surviving; a body coming back was the
offensive part. Paul had been laughed off the Areopagus in Athens over exactly this.

## The first move

*If there is **no resurrection of the dead**, then **Christ Himself has not been raised**.*

He runs their principle backwards. A general rule with no exceptions cannot have one.

They wanted to keep the resurrection of Christ and drop the rest. Paul says the two stand
or fall together, and he says it twice.

## The chain

Each consequence follows from the last:

- *Our **preaching is empty*** — the Greek is **kenon**, hollow, containing nothing.
- *And **your faith is empty** as well.*
- *We are found to be **false witnesses about God***, because we testified that He raised
  Him.
- *Your faith is **futile***, and *you are **still in your sins***.
- *Those who have **fallen asleep in Christ have perished***.

## The word for futile

**Mataia** — useless, pointless, without effect. It is a different word from the *kenon*
of verse 14: that one means empty of content, this one means empty of result.

## "Still in your sins"

The clause carries the theology of the chapter. If the resurrection did not happen, the
death did not settle anything.

Paul says it in Romans the other way round: *delivered up because of our offences, and
**raised because of our justification**.* See the note on Romans 4:23–25.

## The dead

*And those who have **fallen asleep in Christ have perished**.*

He includes the people they had buried. If he is wrong, the ones who died believing are
simply gone.

## The last line

*If our hope in Christ belongs to this life only, then **there is no one on earth more to
be pitied than we are**.*

The Greek is *most pitiable of all men*.

He does not say Christians would still be better off, or that the ethics would remain
worth having. He says they would be the most wretched people alive.

Paul had been beaten five times, stoned once, shipwrecked three times, and was writing
from Ephesus where he says he *fought with beasts*.

The life only made sense on one condition, and he says so.

## What follows

*But now **Christ is risen from the dead, and has become the firstfruits** of those who
have fallen asleep.*

The Greek is **aparche** — the first sheaf brought in, which is not a sample but a
guarantee. See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:23–28.

## The witnesses he had already listed

Cephas, the twelve, over five hundred at once — *of whom the greater part remain to the
present, but some have fallen asleep* — then James, then all the apostles, and last of
all himself.

He names people who could be asked.

> **What Paul refuses to do.** Salvage anything. He takes the denial, applies it
> consistently, and lists everything that goes with it — including the people already
> buried.
