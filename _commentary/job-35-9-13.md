Elihu names a question nobody thinks to ask, and the description of God inside it
is the best line in his speech.

## The situation

*People **groan under the weight of oppression**. They cry out under the crushing arm
of the powerful.*

The crying is real and the oppression is real. Elihu is not disputing either.

## What is missing

*But **no one thinks to say, "Where is God my Maker?"**"*

They cry out **because** of the pressure. Nobody cries out **to** anyone.

That is the distinction the passage turns on — the difference between a noise pain
makes and a question addressed to somebody.

## The phrase

*"The One who **fills the night with songs**."*

The Hebrew is *gives songs in the night*.

Not takes the night away. Not explains it. Gives something to sing in the middle of
it.

Songs are what this book's God is said to hand out in the dark, and Elihu offers it
as a reason to ask where He is.

## The comparison

*"Who **teaches us more than the beasts of the field** and **makes us wiser than the
birds of the sky**."*

Animals cry when they are hurt. That much requires nothing.

Elihu's point is that people were given something the animals were not, and in
trouble they use the part they share with the cattle and neglect the part that is
theirs alone.

## Why the answer does not come

*"They cry out, but **no answer comes** — because of the arrogance of wicked people.
**It is all empty noise**, and God will not listen to emptiness."*

The Hebrew is *vanity* — the same root as Ecclesiastes' *hebel*, vapour.

Elihu is describing a cry with nothing in it and nobody in mind.

## Who is speaking

Elihu, the young man who waited through nine chapters for the three friends to
finish, and who is never rebuked by God at the end of the book.

He is also the one who says *if there is a messenger at his side, one who speaks for
him, one in a thousand… I have found the price for his release.* See the note on
Job 33:23–24.

## Where songs in the night turn up

Paul and Silas, backs opened by rods, feet in the stocks in the inner prison: *at
midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were
listening to them.* See the note on Acts 16:29–31.

And the psalm: *the LORD will command His lovingkindness in the daytime, and **in the
night His song shall be with me**.*

> **What the whole book has been about.** Job groaning, and Job's friends explaining
> it, and Job asking for an umpire. Elihu's contribution is that the groaning could
> be addressed to somebody — and God answers out of the whirlwind the moment he stops
> talking.
