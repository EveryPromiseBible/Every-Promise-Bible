The leper's question is the one everybody actually asks, and Jesus answers it in
two words.

## What the man was sure of, and what he was not

*Lord, if You are willing, You can make me clean.*

He has no doubt about the power. **Can** is not in question — he says it flatly.
What he does not know is whether Jesus *wants* to.

That is where most people stand. Not wondering whether God is able. Wondering
whether He is willing, in their case.

## The answer

*Jesus reached out His hand and touched him. "I am willing," He said. "Be
clean."*

He takes the man's own word and hands it straight back. The uncertainty is not
left hanging for a moment.

And He touches him first. Under the law a leper was untouchable, and anyone
touching one became unclean until evening. Jesus reaches out before He speaks.
The cleanness travels the other way.

## What the touch says

This is the first healing Matthew records after the Sermon on the Mount, and he
places it deliberately. Jesus has just finished teaching with authority; now He
puts a hand on the one person in Israel nobody would touch.

*Instantly the leprosy left him.* No process, no waiting.

> **Why He sends him to the priest.** Leviticus 14 sets out a whole ceremony for
> cleansed lepers that had, so far as anyone knew, never been performed — two
> birds, cedar, scarlet, hyssop. Jesus tells the man to go and present himself
> *so they can see it for themselves*. The priests were about to have to open a
> chapter of the law that had lain unused.
