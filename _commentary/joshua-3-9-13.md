Joshua tells them what is about to happen, and why it is being done this way.

## The summons

*So Joshua called to the people, "**Come here. Listen to the words of the LORD your God**."*

The Hebrew is **geshu henah** — draw near here.

He has been in command for three days. See the note on Joshua 3:1–4.

## The purpose

*"**This is how you'll know that the living God is right here among you**."*

The Hebrew is **El chai** — the living God. The phrase appears rarely, and always where the
question is whether God is actually present and active.

The crossing is not primarily transport. It is evidence.

## The seven

*"And that He will surely drive out before you **the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Hivites, the
Perizzites, the Girgashites, the Amorites, and the Jebusites**."*

Seven nations, named individually.

Moses had told them: *seven nations greater and mightier than you*. Joshua names each one in
front of a river they cannot cross.

The land is occupied, and the list is not shortened for morale.

## The ark

*"Look — **the ark of the covenant of the LORD of all the earth is going ahead of you into the
Jordan**."*

**Adon kol-ha'aretz** — Lord of all the earth. The title is used twice in this short passage, and
it is the point: the God of Israel is the owner of the land they are entering, not a rival deity
crossing a border.

## Why the ark goes first

Because they have not been this way before.

The order given three days earlier: *when you see the ark… then you shall set out from your
place and go after it*, with two thousand cubits between.

The people follow something they can see, at a distance, and do not lead.

## The twelve

*"So now **choose twelve men from the tribes of Israel, one from each tribe**."*

They are not told yet what for. Chapter 4 explains: each man lifts a stone from the riverbed and
carries it out, and the twelve stones are set up at Gilgal.

*When your children ask in time to come, "What do these stones mean?"*

The memorial is arranged before the miracle happens.

## The timing

*"And this will happen: **the moment the priests carrying the ark set their feet in the waters of
the Jordan, the river will be cut off**."*

The Hebrew is **kenoach kapot raglei** — as the soles of the feet rest.

Not before. The water goes when the feet are wet.

## What the river was doing

*The Jordan overflows all its banks during the whole time of harvest.*

Flood stage. The narrator says so twice, in case anyone thinks they picked an easy season.

## The heap

*"The water flowing down from upstream will **stand still, piling up in a heap**."*

The Hebrew is **ned echad** — one heap.

The same word in the Song of the Sea: *the waters were gathered together, the floods stood
upright like a heap*.

Forty years apart, the same noun. The generation that saw the Red Sea has died in the
wilderness, and this generation gets the same word.

## What happens

*The waters which came down from upstream stood still and rose in a heap very far away at Adam,
the city that is beside Zaretan.* See the note on Joshua 3:14–17.

And the priests stood on dry ground in the middle of the Jordan until all the people had crossed
over.

> **The order.** *The moment the priests set their feet in the waters, the river will be cut off.*
> Wet feet first, dry riverbed second — and the memorial stones were commissioned before either.
