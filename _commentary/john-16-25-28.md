The last thing He tells them before He prays, and it is about who they will be asking.

## Where this comes

The end of the upper-room discourse. Four chapters of it, and the next words out of His
mouth are *Father, the hour has come* — the prayer of John 17. See the note on
John 17:1–5.

## The pictures

*"I have been saying all this to you **in pictures**."*

The Greek is **paroimiais** — figures, veiled sayings, proverbs. John's word where the other
Gospels say *parables*.

The vine, the branches, the woman in labour, the sheepfold, the grain of wheat.

## The hour

*"The hour is coming when I will **no longer speak to you in pictures**, but will **tell you
about the Father plainly**."*

The Greek is **parresia** — openly, with freedom of speech, without concealment.

The same word John uses for the confidence believers have in approaching God. The plain
speaking runs both ways.

## The day

*"**On that day** you will ask in My name."*

Not now. That day is after the resurrection, and the disciples do exactly this from Acts
onward.

## The correction

*"And **I am not saying to you that I will ask the Father on your behalf**."*

He heads off the assumption before they make it.

They have just watched Him say *I will pray the Father, and He will give you another
Helper* — and He now removes the picture that would leave them with: a reluctant Father who
has to be worked on.

## The reason

*"**Because the Father Himself loves you**."*

The Greek is **autos gar ho pater philei humas** — *for the Father Himself is fond of you*.

**Phileo**, not agapao. The warm word. The word for the affection between friends.

The intercession is not needed for the Father's willingness. It never was.

## The ground given

*"**Since you have loved Me** and have **believed that I came from God**."*

Two things, and both are things the disciples did — loved Him, and believed where He came
from.

That is the whole qualification listed.

## The four-clause summary

*"**I came from the Father** and **entered the world**; now I am **leaving the world** and
**going back to the Father**."*

Out, in, out, back. The shortest account of the incarnation in the Gospels, said by the one
doing it.

The disciples' reaction is immediate: *see, now You are speaking plainly, and using no
figure of speech! Now we are sure that You know all things… by this we believe that You
came forth from God.*

## What He says to that

*"**Do you now believe? Indeed the hour is coming, yes, has now come, that you will be
scattered**, each to his own, and will leave Me alone. And yet I am not alone, because the
Father is with Me."*

Then the last sentence of the discourse: *these things I have spoken to you, that in Me you
may have peace. In the world you will have tribulation; but be of good cheer, **I have
overcome the world**.*

## Where the same thing is said elsewhere

*He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all, how shall He not with
Him also freely give us all things?* See the note on Romans 8:31–34.

*God so loved the world that He gave.* The giving followed the love; it did not produce it.

> **The sentence He wanted them not to misread.** Not that He talks the Father round. *The
> Father Himself loves you* — and the verb is the fond one.
