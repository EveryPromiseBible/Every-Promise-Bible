key: 2 Kings 5:9–10
title: He did not even come out
updated: 2026-08-09
---
A general arrives with a fortune and an escort, and the prophet sends a servant to the door.

## What is standing outside

*Naaman came with **his horses and chariots** and stood at the door of Elisha's house.*

Ten talents of silver, six thousand shekels of gold, ten changes of clothing, a letter from
his king, and a military escort. He is commander of the army of Aram and he has brought the
purchase price.

## What comes out

Nobody.

*And **Elisha didn't even come out**. He sent a messenger.*

The one detail Naaman cannot get past. His complaint two verses later is not about the river:
*"I said to myself, **He will surely come out to me**, and stand and call on the name of the
LORD his God, and wave his hand over the place."*

He had written the scene in advance and it did not include a servant at the door.

## Why it had to be that way

Because the moment Elisha comes out and performs, the healing has a price and a
performer.

*Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but **according to His mercy He saved
us**.*

*Where is boasting then? **It is excluded.***

## The instruction

*"Go, **wash yourself seven times in the Jordan**, and your flesh will be restored, and you
will be clean."*

Free. Repeatable by anybody. Requiring nothing he had brought, and nothing he could do
impressively.

The Jordan is muddy, and he says so — *are not the rivers of Damascus better?* Abana and
Pharpar come down clean off the mountains behind his own city.

## Seven times

Not once, and not until he felt something. A stated number, with nothing to show for the first
six.

*By faith the walls of Jericho fell down after they were encircled for seven days.* See the
note on Joshua 6:1–5.

## Who talked him into going

*"**If only my master were with the prophet who is in Samaria!**"*

A girl taken captive in a raid on Israel, working in the house of the man whose army took her,
telling his wife where he could be healed.

The chain that saves the general starts with a slave with no name in the text.

## What he had to become first

*So he went down and dipped seven times.*

The Hebrew is **vayyered** — he went down. The last thing recorded before it is his servants
persuading him not to go home.

Jesus states the rule the chapter runs on: *"whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and **he
who humbles himself will be exalted**."*

> **What offended him.** Not the river, first. That nobody came out. He had brought a fortune
> and an escort to a door, and the answer was handed to him by a servant.
