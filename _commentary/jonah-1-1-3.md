Four verbs of descent in three verses, and the last one is where the book's geography starts.

## The command

*"**Get up**," God said. "**Go to Nineveh**, that great and troubled city, and speak against it."*

**Qum lekh** — arise, go. And Nineveh is the capital of Assyria: the empire that will destroy the
northern kingdom, and whose reliefs show prisoners led by hooks through the jaw.

Jonah is a northern prophet. He is being sent to the people who will end his country.

## What he does

*"But Jonah **got up and went the other way**."*

The Hebrew keeps the verb: **vayyaqom Yonah livroach** — Jonah arose to flee. He obeys the first
word of the command and reverses the second.

## The direction

*"He **came down to Joppa**… He paid his fare, **climbed aboard**."*

The Hebrew is **vayyered** — and he went down. It is used again in the next paragraph — *Jonah had
gone down into the lowest parts of the ship* — and again in his prayer: *I went down to the
moorings of the mountains.*

Down to Joppa, down into the ship, down into the sea, down to the roots of the mountains. The book
tracks him descending until there is nowhere further.

*Now this, "He ascended" — what does it mean but that **He also first descended into the lower parts
of the earth**?*

## The fare

*"He **paid his fare**."*

The one detail in the paragraph that costs him something. Running away is the only thing in the
book Jonah pays for.

## What he was running from

*"**Running from the presence of the LORD.**"*

The Hebrew is **millifnei YHWH** — from before the face of the LORD. Twice in one verse.

*"**Where can I go from Your Spirit?** Or where can I flee from Your presence?… If I take the wings
of the morning, and dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea, even there Your hand shall lead me."*

Psalm 139 answers the plan before it is made, and the specific case it names is the far side of
the sea. See the note on Psalms 139:1–6.

## Why he ran

The book does not say here. It says in chapter 4, and the reason is not fear:

*"Isn't this what I said while I was still at home? That is why I fled… for **I knew that You are a
gracious and merciful God, slow to anger and abundant in lovingkindness, One who relents from doing
harm**."*

He ran because he thought God would forgive them, and was right.

## The greater one

*"**The men of Nineveh will rise up in the judgment with this generation and condemn it**, because
they repented at the preaching of Jonah; and indeed **a greater than Jonah is here**."*

**Matthew** 12. **Jesus** compares Himself to Jonah twice, and the comparison is never flattering to
Jonah.

*"**Lo, I come — in the volume of the book it is written of Me — to do Your will, O God.**"*

> **The verb the book repeats.** *Down.* To Joppa, into the ship, into the sea, to the roots of the
> mountains. And the only thing Jonah is recorded paying for in the whole book is the ticket away.
