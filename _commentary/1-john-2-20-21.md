John is writing about people who had left the church claiming superior knowledge,
and this is his answer to the readers who stayed.

## The word

**Chrisma** — an anointing, and the noun is built on the same root as **Christos**,
the Anointed One. John is making the connection deliberately: the Anointed has
anointed them.

In Israel the oil went on prophets, priests and kings. John says the ordinary
believers reading his letter have it.

## What it does

*And you know all things.*

The phrase is not a claim to omniscience — the next verse makes that clear:
*I have not written to you because you do not know the truth, but because you **do**
know it.*

He is not adding to their information. He is telling them they already have what
the departing teachers claimed to be selling.

## Why he says it

The verses just before name the problem: people had gone out from among them,
teaching that Jesus was not the Christ. The whole passage is about a congregation
being told they were missing something.

John's response is not a counter-argument. It is: you have the anointing, and you
know.

## "No lie ever comes out of the truth"

A short, blunt line, and it is the test he gives them. Truth does not generate
lies. If a teaching leads away from what they already knew about Christ, its
source is settled by that fact alone.

> **Where it goes.** A few verses on: *the anointing you received from Him
> remains in you… as it has taught you, remain in Him.* John does not tell them to
> seek a fresh experience. He tells them what they have stays, and to stay in the
> One who gave it.
