Peter tells suffering Christians to be glad — and of everybody in the New Testament, he is the one
who had least right to say it easily.

## Sharing, not paying

*You are **getting in on what Christ went through**.*

**Koinoneite** — you are having fellowship with. The great word for partnership, attached here to
His sufferings.

Which settles what this is and is not. Nothing is being added to the cross; Peter has already said
in this letter that Christ *suffered once for sins*, and *once* is the whole point. What is on offer
is not a share of the payment but a share of the company.

Paul wanted the same thing and used the same word: *that I may know Him… and **the fellowship of His
sufferings***.

## Who is saying it

Peter is the disciple who took Jesus aside and *began to rebuke Him*, saying *this shall not happen
to You* — and was answered with the sharpest sentence Jesus ever spoke to a friend.

He is the one who swung a sword in the garden to prevent it, and the one who denied he knew Him
rather than share any of it.

And here he is, writing the New Testament's clearest instruction to rejoice in suffering for the
name. Nothing produced that but grace: a resurrection, a charcoal fire on a beach, and three
questions that undid three denials.

## When the cover comes off

*When **His glory is pulled out into the open** you will be beside yourselves with joy.*

**En te apokalypsei tes doxes autou** — at the unveiling of His glory. An apocalypse is not a
catastrophe; it is the removal of a covering.

The glory is not being manufactured later. It is real now and hidden, and one day the cloth comes
off. Peter had seen a preview of it once on a mountain, and never got over it — he brings it up in
his second letter as the thing he cannot stop testifying to.

## And what the next verse says

*If you are reproached for the name of Christ, blessed are you, for **the Spirit of glory and of God
rests upon you**.*

**Anapauetai** — rests, settles, comes down and stays. It is the word used of the Shekinah filling a
tabernacle.

Peter's picture of a reviled believer is not a person struggling on alone. It is a person with the
glory sitting on them.

> **Rejoicing is not pretending it does not hurt.** It is knowing what the fire is: not the Father
> being against you, but fellowship with Jesus Christ in the one thing He went through for you — and
> a glory that is already real and only waiting for the cloth to come off.
