The first day of the church, and Luke gives the number.

## What Peter kept saying

*He said much more than this, and he kept urging them, "Be rescued from this
crooked generation."*

Luke tells us the sermon we have is a summary — there was much more. And the plea
he records is passive: *be rescued*, be saved. Not save yourselves.

The crowd's own question had been *what shall we do?*, and the answer they were
given was to turn and be baptised, and to receive.

## The number

*That day about three thousand people were added to them.*

The figure is not chosen at random by history. Exodus 32 records three thousand
falling on the day the law was given at Sinai — and Pentecost was, by long
tradition, the anniversary of that giving.

Same feast, same number, and the verb reversed. Three thousand fell there; three
thousand were **added** here.

Luke does not comment. He was writing for people who knew the story.

## "Added"

The word is the ordinary one for putting something to an existing total, and Luke
will use it repeatedly through the early chapters — the Lord added to their
number daily.

Nobody in Acts is described as joining. They are added, and the one doing the
adding is named.

## What the day cost them

Nothing is said about qualifications. They had, seven weeks earlier, been part of
the city that called for a crucifixion — Peter says so to their faces two
paragraphs before this.

The response to that sermon was three thousand baptisms.

## The verb in the exhortation

*"**Be saved from this perverse generation.**"*

**Sothete** — an aorist passive imperative. Not *save yourselves*; be saved, by somebody else.

## What they were being added to

*"**Added**."* **Prosetethesan** — a passive again.

*And **the Lord added to the church daily those who were being saved**.*

Six verses later, and **Luke** names the agent. The three thousand did not join; they were added, and
the one doing the adding is the one the sermon was about.

*"**All that the Father gives Me will come to Me, and the one who comes to Me I will by no means cast
out.**"*

## What the sermon had asked for

*"**Repent, and let every one of you be baptised in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of
sins**; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit."*

*"For **the promise is to you and to your children, and to all who are afar off, as many as the Lord
our God will call**."*

The offer is extended to the people who had shouted for the crucifixion seven weeks earlier, and
**Peter** has just said so to their faces.

> **What follows immediately.** The famous four: the apostles' teaching, the
> fellowship, the breaking of bread, and the prayers. The number is not left as a
> headline; Luke goes straight on to what those three thousand then did every day.
