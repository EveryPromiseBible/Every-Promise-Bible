Five verses, and every move in them is about what God is like.

## The description

*Now the serpent was **more crafty than any creature the LORD God had made**.*

The Hebrew is **arum** — shrewd, prudent, subtle. Proverbs uses it approvingly of the
prudent man.

There is a pun in the Hebrew: the previous verse ends *they were both naked* — **arummim**
— and this one opens **arum**.

## The opening move

*He came to the woman with **a question that twisted things**: "**Did God really say** you
must not eat from **any tree** in the garden?"*

The Hebrew is *has God indeed said*.

Two distortions in one sentence:

- It is a **question** rather than a claim, so nothing is asserted and everything is
  suggested.
- **Any tree** — the actual command was one tree out of the whole garden. He inverts the
  proportion.

## What God had said

*"**Of every tree of the garden you may freely eat**; but of the tree of the knowledge of
good and evil you shall not eat."* See the note on Genesis 2:15–17.

The Hebrew doubles the permission: *eating you may eat*. It is emphatic generosity, with
one exception.

The serpent quotes the exception and drops the generosity.

## Her answer

*"We may eat from the trees — only not from the tree in the middle. God said we must not
eat it **or even touch it**, or we will die."*

Three changes:

- **Freely eat** becomes simply *we may eat* — the generosity flattens.
- **Or even touch it** is not in the command. She adds a restriction.
- The Hebrew of *you shall surely die* — the doubled form — becomes *lest we die*.

The permission shrinks, the prohibition grows, and the certainty weakens.

## The contradiction

*"**You will not die**," the serpent said.*

The first flat denial in Scripture, and it is a direct negation of the sentence in
Genesis 2.

## The motive supplied

*"**God knows** that the moment you eat it, **your eyes will open, and you will be like God,
knowing good and evil**."*

The move is not to deny the command. It is to explain the motive behind it.

The suggestion is that the restriction exists to keep something back.

## What was already true

Genesis 1: *God created man **in His own image**; in the image of God He created him.*

They were already like Him. The offer is of something they had.

## What was true in the promise

*Your eyes will be opened.* And the next verse: *then the eyes of both of them were
opened.*

The serpent's factual claim was accurate. What they saw was that they were naked.

## Where the pattern repeats

In the wilderness, three temptations, and two of them open *if You are the Son of God* —
which is the same move, aimed at what had just been said out loud at the Jordan: *this is My
beloved Son*. See the note on Matthew 3:16–17.

Jesus answers all three with Scripture, quoted accurately.

## What Paul says about the method

*But I fear, lest somehow, **as the serpent deceived Eve by his craftiness**, so your minds
may be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ.*

Same word — **panourgia**, craftiness.

> **What is attacked first.** Not the rule. The opening question misquotes the generosity,
> and the closing sentence supplies a motive — and by the end the only thing in dispute is
> what kind of God gave the command.
