The verdict, and it goes the opposite way from everything the previous forty
chapters seemed to be building toward.

## Who is in trouble

*"**My anger burns against you and your two friends.**"*

The three men who spent twenty-five chapters defending God's justice are the ones
He is angry with.

Eliphaz is named. He spoke first and he is addressed first.

## The charge

*"Because **you have not spoken of Me what is right**, as My servant Job has."*

Said twice, once at the start and once at the end, framing the whole instruction.

The offence is not how they treated Job — though they crushed him with words for
twenty-five chapters. It is what they said about **God**.

They had made Him the kind of God who breaks a man to punish hidden sin, who
operates a formula, who has to be defended by accusation. Every sermon they
preached misrepresented Him, and that is the thing He answers.

## "As My servant Job has"

Job, who said God had netted him, bent him down, and would not answer — Job is the
one who spoke rightly.

He said hard things and he said them to God's face. They said correct-sounding
things about Him behind His back.

And *My servant Job* is used four times in these two verses. God keeps repeating
the title over the man his friends had been treating as a defendant.

## What they have to do

*"Take **seven bulls and seven rams**, and go to My servant Job, and offer them up
as a burnt offering for yourselves."*

Seven and seven — the largest sacrifice named anywhere in the book.

And they have to carry it to Job. Three older men driving fourteen animals across
to the ash heap of the man they had been lecturing.

## And who has to pray

*"**And My servant Job will pray for you. I will accept his prayer** and not deal
with you as your foolishness deserves."*

The sacrifice is not enough on its own. God makes their forgiveness run through
the man they wronged.

Job has to intercede for Eliphaz, Bildad and Zophar. They cannot approach without
him.

## What that makes him

A man standing between God and three guilty people, praying, with a burnt offering
on the fire — which is precisely the office Elihu had described back in chapter
33: *a messenger at his side, one who speaks for him, one in a thousand.* See the
note on Job 33:23–24.

## And what it does to Job

The very next verse: *when Job prayed for his friends, the LORD restored everything
he had lost.*

Not when he repented. Not when he understood. When he prayed for them. See the
note on Job 42:10.

## Who has to pray

*"**Now therefore, take for yourselves seven bulls and seven rams, go to My servant Job, and
offer up for yourselves a burnt offering; and My servant Job shall pray for you. For I will
accept him**, lest I deal with you according to your folly."*

The three men who spent thirty chapters telling Job to repent have to bring the sacrifice — and
they cannot present it themselves. It has to go through the man they accused.

*For there is one God and **one Mediator between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus**.*

## The title used four times

*My servant Job.* In one paragraph, over a man who had just said *I abhor myself*.

It is the same title Isaiah gives to Somebody else: *behold, **My Servant** whom I uphold, My
Elect One in whom My soul delights.*

## What the offering is

Seven bulls and seven rams — the largest private sacrifice recorded outside the temple system,
required of men whose fault was speaking wrongly about God.

*For **without shedding of blood there is no remission**.*

## What the verdict does not contain

An answer. God rebukes the friends for what they said about Him and never explains what He was
doing, to any of them.

And He vindicates the man who shouted at Him over the three who defended Him with true-sounding
sentences.

> **What is not in the verdict.** Any answer. God rebukes the friends, vindicates
> Job, and never once explains the boils to anybody. The book ends with the
> question unanswered and the man restored.
