The grain runs out, and the argument the family had been avoiding has to happen.

## The pressure

*The famine **still lay heavy on the land**. When they had **eaten all the grain** they'd
brought from Egypt.*

Nothing has been decided since chapter 42. Simeon is still in an Egyptian prison and Jacob
has refused to send Benjamin.

They have simply been eating.

## The instruction

*"**Go back and buy us a little more food**."*

Jacob says it as though nothing else is in the way. *A little more* — as if a small trip
were possible.

## Judah's answer

*But Judah **reminded him**, "The man warned us plainly, '**You will not see my face unless
your brother is with you**.'"*

He quotes it twice in four verses. There is no way round it and he says so by repeating
the sentence.

## The terms

*"If you send our brother along, **we'll go down and buy food for you**. But if you won't
send him, **we can't go**."*

Judah puts the decision back where it belongs. He does not plead and does not decide for
his father.

## Jacob's complaint

*Israel said, "**Why did you bring this trouble on me by telling the man you had another
brother?**"*

The Hebrew is *why did you deal so wrongfully with me*.

He is looking for somebody to blame for a famine and a stranger's condition.

## What they answer

*"**The man questioned us closely** about ourselves and our family. '**Is your father still
alive? Do you have another brother?**' We simply answered what he asked."*

They are telling the truth. Chapter 42 records the interrogation.

## What they could not know

*"**How could we know he'd say, 'Bring your brother down here'?**"*

Joseph had been asking about his father and his mother's other son. The brothers took it
for a Egyptian official's suspicion.

## What Judah offers next

*"**I myself will be surety for him; from my hand you shall require him**. If I do not bring
him back to you and set him before you, then let me bear the blame forever."*

Reuben had offered his own two sons in chapter 42 and Jacob did not answer him. See the
note on Genesis 42:29–38.

Judah offers himself, and Jacob agrees.

## What Judah does later

When the cup is found in Benjamin's sack, Judah makes the longest speech in Genesis and
ends: *now therefore, please **let your servant remain instead of the lad as a slave to my
lord**, and let the lad go up with his brothers.*

He keeps the offer.

## Who Judah had been

The brother who proposed selling Joseph in the first place: *what profit is there if we
kill our brother and conceal his blood? Come, let us sell him.*

Genesis puts twenty-two years and one chapter about Tamar between the two speeches.

## Jacob's surrender

*"Take double money in your hand… **take your brother, and arise, go back to the man**. And
may God Almighty give you mercy before the man, that he may release your other brother and
Benjamin. **If I am bereaved, I am bereaved**."*

**God Almighty** — **El Shaddai**, the name given to Abraham at ninety-nine. See the note
on Genesis 17:1–2.

> **What breaks the deadlock.** Not persuasion. The food ran out, and Judah put the terms
> in front of his father twice and then offered to stand for the boy himself.
