The fourth attempt, and this time the diviner gives up on his own trade.

## What has happened three times already

Balak has moved Balaam from hilltop to hilltop, built seven altars each time, offered a
bull and a ram on each, and got a blessing every time. See the note on
Numbers 23:18–20.

*"I called you to curse my enemies, and look, you have kept on blessing them these three
times!"*

## What changed

*Now when Balaam **saw that it pleased the LORD to bless Israel**.*

He works it out. Three rounds of evidence and he stops arguing with it.

## What he stopped doing

*He **did not go off, as at other times, to look for omens**.*

The Hebrew is **nechashim** — enchantments, divination. It is the same root as the word for
serpent.

The previous chapters describe his method: he would withdraw to a bare height and wait for
a sign, and *the LORD met Balaam*.

Here he abandons the professional apparatus.

## What he did instead

*He **turned his face toward the wilderness**.*

Toward the camp. He simply looks at what is in front of him.

## What he saw

*And Balaam **lifted his eyes and saw Israel camped tribe by tribe**.*

The Hebrew is *dwelling according to their tribes*.

Numbers 2 gives the arrangement: four divisions of three tribes each, camped on the four
sides, with the tabernacle in the middle.

From a hilltop it would have read as a pattern.

## What came

*And **the Spirit of God came upon him**.*

The Hebrew is **ruach Elohim**.

It is said of Balaam — a hired diviner, on his fourth attempt to curse a nation, who has
already been rebuked by his own donkey.

Scripture says the Spirit came on him, and the fourth oracle is the finest of them.

## What he says about himself

*"The word of the man **whose eye is opened**, the word of one who **hears the words of God,
who sees the vision of the Almighty**, falling down, but **with his eyes uncovered**."* See
the note on Numbers 24:3–9.

The Hebrew for *vision* is **machazeh** — from **chazah**, to see.

**Falling down, but with his eyes uncovered** — the standard description of a prophet under
the hand of God, and Balaam applies it to himself.

## The donkey

Two chapters earlier the animal saw the angel three times and Balaam did not, and was
beaten for stopping.

*Then the LORD **opened Balaam's eyes**, and he saw the Angel of the LORD standing in the
way.*

The professional seer is the last one in the story to see anything.

## What follows

*"**How lovely are your tents, Jacob**."*

The line that opens Jewish morning prayer to this day, spoken by a man on Balak's payroll.

## How it ends for Balaam

Badly. When cursing failed, he advised Balak to seduce Israel into idolatry at Peor — and
Numbers 31 records his death in battle.

The New Testament names it three times: the way of Balaam, the error of Balaam, and the
doctrine of Balaam.

> **What he stopped doing.** Looking for omens. Three failed attempts with the full
> apparatus, and on the fourth he turns his face toward the camp and looks — and that is
> the verse the Spirit arrives in.
