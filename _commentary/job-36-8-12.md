The fourth speaker in the book, and the only one God does not rebuke by name.

## Who Elihu is

He appears in chapter 32, out of nowhere, after the three friends *ceased answering Job,
because he was righteous in his own eyes*.

He is young — *I am young in years, and you are very old; therefore I was afraid, and dared
not declare my opinion to you* — and he has been waiting through thirty-one chapters.

His anger is stated twice: against Job *because he justified himself rather than God*, and
against the three friends *because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned Job*.

He speaks for six chapters and nobody answers him.

## What he is describing

*"And if any of them end up **bound in chains, caught tight in the cords of affliction**."*

The Hebrew is *held in cords of affliction* — the picture of a man tied.

## What he says God does with it

*"Then God **shows them plainly what they have done**, the way **their pride carried them too
far**."*

*"He **opens their ears to correction** and **calls them to turn back**."*

The Hebrew is *He opens their ear to instruction*.

Two verbs about hearing.

## The two outcomes

*"**If they listen and serve Him**, they will **live out their days in good things**, their
years in **real contentment**."*

The Hebrew is *in pleasantness* — **neimim**.

*"But **if they will not listen**, they **cross over by the sword** and **die still knowing
nothing**."*

The Hebrew is *they die without knowledge*.

## What Elihu gets right

The three friends had argued backwards from Job's suffering to his guilt. Elihu's argument
is different: he says affliction can be instructional rather than punitive.

*"He **delivers the poor in their affliction**, and **opens their ears in oppression**."*

And earlier: *"For God may speak in one way, or in another, yet man does not perceive
it."*

## What Elihu also does

Applies the general principle to a man the reader knows is not guilty.

The reader has read chapter 1 and 2. Job's friends have not, and neither has Elihu.

Every speech in the book is made by men working without the information the reader was
given on the first page. See the note on Job 1:6–12.

## What God says at the end

To Eliphaz: *My wrath is aroused against you and your two friends, for **you have not spoken
of Me what is right**, as My servant Job has.*

**Your two friends** — three men are named. Elihu is not among them, and he is not
addressed in the epilogue at all.

The book leaves him alone, which readers have argued about for a very long time.

## What comes immediately after his speeches

*Then the LORD answered Job **out of the whirlwind**.*

Elihu's last chapter is a description of a gathering storm — lightning, thunder, clouds
turning — and then the storm speaks.

## What God's answer consists of

Questions. Seventy of them, about wild goats, ostriches, the war horse, the hawk, the sea,
snow, and Orion.

Not one of them is about suffering.

> **What Elihu contributes.** A category the other three did not have — that affliction can
> open an ear rather than settle an account. And like all of them, he applies it to a man
> whose file he has not read.
