They can forecast the weather to the afternoon and cannot say what year they are standing in.

## Who He turns to

*He also said **to the crowds**.*

The chapter has been addressed to the disciples — *my friends*, *little flock*. Here He turns to
the crowd.

## The first sign

*"When you see **a cloud coming up in the west**, you say at once, '**Rain is on the way**' — and it
is."*

The west is the Mediterranean. Cloud off the sea means rain, every time.

Elijah's servant saw *a little cloud, like a man's hand, rising out of the sea*, and the sky went
black.

## The second

*"And when **the south wind blows** you say, '**It is going to be hot**' — and it is."*

The south is the desert. The **sirocco** off the Negev.

## What the two have in common

Both forecasts are correct. Luke says so twice — *and it is*.

The rebuke that follows is not that they read the sky badly.

## The name He calls them

*"**You pretenders**."*

The Greek is **hupokritai** — actors, stage-players. One wearing the mask of another.

The charge is not stupidity. It is that they can do the reading and are behaving as though they
cannot.

## The question

*"You can **read the face of the sky and the look of the land**, so how is it that **you cannot read
this moment you are living in**?"*

The Greek is **ton kairon touton** — this **kairos**.

**Kairos**, not **chronos**. Not the date. The moment with a character to it, the opportune hour.

## What the moment was

The Messiah was standing in the crowd asking the question.

## The second question

*"And **why can you not judge for yourselves what is right**?"*

The Greek is **aph' heauton** — of yourselves.

He does not offer them the answer. He tells them the capacity is already theirs.

## The turn

*"If you are **on your way to court** with someone who has a case against you, **do your best to
settle it with him along the road**."*

The Greek is **dos ergasian apellachthai** — give effort to be released from him.

## The chain

*"Or he will **drag you in front of the judge**, and the judge will **hand you over to the officer**,
and the officer will **throw you in prison**."*

Three handovers, each one further from the point where anything could be done about it.

## The road

The whole opportunity is the walk to the courthouse.

The **kairos** He has just named is that stretch of road. Once the parties reach the judge, the
window that existed is gone.

## The last line

*"I tell you, you will **not get out of there until you have paid the very last coin**."*

The Greek is **to eschaton lepton** — the last **lepton**, the smallest coin in circulation, half a
quadrans.

The same coin the widow puts in the treasury. See the note on Mark 12:41–44.

## What He had just said

*"**I came to send fire on the earth**… Do you suppose that I came to give peace on earth? I tell
you, not at all, but rather division."* See the note on Luke 12:49–53.

The urgency of the road to court is set immediately after that.

## The other place He weeps over it

*"If you had known, even you, especially in this your day, the things that make for your peace! But
now they are hidden from your eyes… **because you did not know the time of your visitation**."* See
the note on Luke 19:41–44.

The Greek there is **kairon tes episkopes sou** — the same word.

> **The competence.** He does not tell them they cannot read the signs. He tells them they read the
> sky perfectly and are acting as though they cannot read the hour.
