God offers a blank cheque, and the interest of the story is entirely in what
Solomon does with it.

## What he actually asked for

The English usually says *an understanding heart*. The Hebrew is **lev shomea** —
a **listening** heart. A heart that hears.

That is a striking thing for a king to request. Not brilliance, not judgement in
the abstract, but the capacity to take in what is being said to him — by God and
by the people he is about to govern.

## How he gets there

Read the run-up. He talks about David, then says two things about himself:

- *I am only a child* — he was a grown man with a throne
- *I do not know how to go out or come in*

The request comes out of an admission, and the admission comes before the ask.
Then the reason: *who on his own could ever shepherd this great people of Yours?*
He is not asking for wisdom to be impressive. He is asking because he has looked
at the job and knows he cannot do it.

---

## What God says back

*Because you asked for this — not long life for yourself, not riches, not the
death of your enemies…*

God lists the things Solomon did not ask for, which tells you what the offer was
understood to include. And then: *what you did not even ask for, I give you too.*

The riches and honour arrive as an unrequested extra. They are not the reward for
a clever request; they come because of what the request revealed about the man
making it.

## The conditional at the end

*And if you walk in My ways… I will give you long life as well.*

Worth reading precisely. The wisdom is given outright, no condition. The riches
and honour are given outright. Only the long life carries an *if* — and Solomon's
own story, which turns badly in his later years, is the reason that detail is in
the text.

> **What this passage is not.** It is not a formula for getting more by asking
> for the right thing. God's pleasure is in what the request showed about
> Solomon's estimate of himself and the size of the task. The story records a man
> who knew he was out of his depth, and the whole answer follows from that.
