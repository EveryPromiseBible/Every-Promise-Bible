Two verses at the head of the psalm, and they name four things about God in the
space of a breath.

## "Makes a home"

*Whoever **makes a home** in the secret shelter of the Most High.*

The Hebrew is to sit, to remain, to dwell — the word for taking up residence, not
visiting.

Everything the rest of the psalm promises runs off this verb. It is not a place
you go to in an emergency; it is an address.

## The secret place

**Sether** — a covering, a hiding place, somewhere concealed.

It is secret because it is hidden, not because it is hard to find. Nobody outside
can see you there.

## The shadow

*Will **rest in the shadow** of the Almighty.*

A shadow requires being close. You cannot be in the shade of something at a
distance from it.

And the Hebrew for *rest* here is to lodge overnight — to spend the night. The
first line is about living there; the second is about sleeping there.

## The four names, in order

The psalm stacks them:

- **Most High** — *Elyon*, the highest, the one above all
- **Almighty** — *Shaddai*, the all-sufficient
- **the LORD** — *YHWH*, the covenant name from the burning bush
- **my God** — *Elohim*, and the possessive is the point

It moves from the highest and most distant title down to *my*. The psalm ends the
sentence closer than it started it.

## Who says it

*I will say of the LORD, "**You are** my refuge and my fortress."*

The first verse is a statement about anybody. The second is spoken, in the first
person, and addressed to Him directly — *You are*.

The psalm changes from third person to second person inside two lines. The saying
of it is part of it.

## Refuge and fortress

Two words that are not the same. **Machseh** is a shelter you run into from a
storm. **Metsudah** is a stronghold — a fortified place, a mountain hold, the word
used of David's strongholds in the wilderness.

Somewhere to run, and somewhere that holds.

> **Who quotes this psalm.** The devil, in the wilderness, and he quotes verses 11
> and 12 accurately — *He shall give His angels charge over you.* He leaves out the
> line *to keep you in all your ways*. The one place in Scripture where Psalm 91 is
> cited by name, it is being misused, and the answer is a different verse.
