Paul describes his job and then says whose energy does it.

## The subject

*He is the One **we announce**.*

The Greek is **hon hemeis katangellomen** — whom we proclaim.

The pronoun is emphatic and comes first: not a system, a person.

## What has just been said

*The mystery which has been hidden from ages and from generations, but now has been revealed to
His saints… which is **Christ in you, the hope of glory**.* See the note on
Colossians 1:26–27.

## The two activities

*Warning every person and **teaching every person with wisdom of every kind**.*

The Greek is **nouthetountes… didaskontes**.

**Noutheteo** — to put in mind, to admonish. What a parent does.

**Didasko** — to teach.

## The repetition

*Every person… every person… every person.*

The Greek is **panta anthropon** — three times in one verse.

Paul is writing to a church troubled by teachers offering advanced knowledge to an inner circle.
The tripled *every* is the answer.

## The aim

*So that we may **present every person fully grown in Christ Jesus**.*

The Greek is **teleion** — complete, mature, having reached the goal.

Not *knowledgeable*. Finished.

## The word for present

**Parastesomen** — to set alongside, to present formally.

He uses it earlier in the chapter of what Christ does: *to present you holy, and blameless, and
above reproach in His sight*.

## The labour

*This is **what I labor for**.*

The Greek is **kopio** — to work to exhaustion.

## The struggle

*Struggling on.*

The Greek is **agonizomenos** — from **agon**, the contest. The root of *agony*.

The athletic word. Paul uses it of a race and of a fight.

## The correction

*With **the working He works in me**.*

The Greek is **kata ten energeian autou ten energoumenen en emoi**.

**Energeia** and **energeo** — the noun and the verb from the same root, side by side.

The working that works.

## What that clause does

Everything before it is Paul's effort — labouring, struggling, exhausting himself.

The clause names the source, and does not remove the effort.

## The last words

*And it works in me **with power**.*

The Greek is **en dunamei**.

## What Paul says elsewhere in the same shape

*I laboured more abundantly than all of them — **yet not I, but the grace of God which was with
me**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:9–11.

*I can do all things through Christ **who strengthens me**.* See the note on
Philippians 4:11–13.

Three statements, all with the same structure: real work, and a named source that is not the
worker.

## What follows

*For I want you to know **what a great conflict I have for you** and those in Laodicea, and for as
many as have not seen my face in the flesh.*

The Greek is **agona** — the same word as *struggling* in the previous verse.

He has never met them.

## What the conflict is for

*That their hearts may be encouraged, being knit together in love… **in whom are hidden all the
treasures of wisdom and knowledge**.*

## The warning that follows

*Beware lest anyone cheat you through philosophy and empty deceit… **for in Him dwells all the
fullness of the Godhead bodily; and you are complete in Him**.* See the note on
Colossians 2:8–10.

The Greek is **pepleromenoi** — having been filled full. The same root as *fullness*.

> **The clause.** **Energeian energoumenen** — the working that works. It names the source without
> removing any of the labour that precedes it.
