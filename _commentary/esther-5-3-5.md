She is offered anything, and she asks him to come to a meal.

## What has just happened

*On the third day Esther put on her royal robes and stood in the inner court.*

Three days of fasting, and then the approach that could have killed her. See the note on
Esther 5:1–2.

The sceptre went out. She is still alive, and the king is speaking first.

## The offer

*"**What is it, Queen Esther?** What is your request? **Even to half the kingdom, it will be given to
you**."*

The Hebrew is **ad-chatzi hamalkut** — up to half the kingdom.

## What that phrase is

A royal formula, not an accounting figure. The same offer Herod makes to a dancing girl, with the
same words, in Mark's Gospel — and that one ends with a head on a platter.

Here the woman using it is asking for the opposite: lives, not a death.

## What she asks for

*"If it please the king, let **the king and Haman come today to a banquet I have prepared for him**."*

The Hebrew is **mishteh** — a drinking-feast. From **shatah**, to drink.

## The word that runs through the book

**Mishteh** appears about twenty times in Esther, more than in the rest of the Old Testament
combined.

The book opens with the king's six-month feast, and it will end with a feast the Jews keep every
year. Everything decisive in it happens over food.

## The pronoun

*A banquet I have prepared **for him**.*

Singular. Haman is invited; the banquet is stated to be for the king.

Haman will read it differently, and boast to his friends that evening that he alone was invited
with the king. That reading is what puts up the gallows.

## The king's response

*"**Bring Haman quickly**," the king said, "**so we may do as Esther asks**."*

The Hebrew is **maharu** — hurry.

The king is impatient to grant a request he has not yet heard.

## What she does at the banquet

Asks them both to a second banquet, the next day.

She is offered half a kingdom and asks for another dinner. See the note on Esther 5:6–8.

## The night in between

*That night the king could not sleep.*

He calls for the chronicles, and reads about a man who saved his life and was never rewarded. See
the note on Esther 6:1–3.

The delay is the whole mechanism. Had she spoken at the first banquet, Mordecai's record would
still have been unread and the gallows unbuilt.

## The third day

*Now it happened on the third day.*

Abraham lifted his eyes on the third day and saw the place. Hosea says *on the third day He will
raise us up*. See the note on Hosea 6:1–3.

## What her request finally is

*"**Let my life be given me at my petition, and my people at my request**."* See the note on
Esther 7:3–4.

Not half the kingdom. Her own life, and theirs.

> **The pause.** Offered half a kingdom, she asks for dinner — and the gap between the two banquets
> is the night the king cannot sleep.
