Not *so that* they may be forgiven. *Because* they are.

## The conjunction

*I am writing to you, little children, **because** for the sake of His name your sins are forgiven
you.*

The Greek is **hoti**.

It can mean *that* or *because*. Here it heads a series of six clauses, all of them stating
something already true of the readers.

## The tense

*Your sins **are forgiven** you.*

The Greek is **apheōntai** — perfect passive.

The perfect describes a completed action with a standing result. Not *were forgiven* and not *will
be*. Forgiven, and remaining so.

## The ground

*For the sake of **His name**.*

The Greek is **dia to onoma autou** — on account of His name.

Not on account of their confession, their progress, or their record. The name.

## Whose name

The one just given: *Jesus Christ, the righteous One*, our advocate with the Father. See the note
on 1 John 2:1.

## What the letter is doing here

Six clauses, three groups, each addressed twice.

*Little children, because your sins are forgiven.*
*Fathers, because you have known Him who is from the beginning.*
*Young men, because you have overcome the wicked one.*

## The pattern

Every one of them is a **hoti** clause. Every one of them states something accomplished.

John writes to a church on the basis of what is already the case about them. He does not write to
make it the case.

## The three groups

Whether they are ages or stages has been argued both ways.

What is not in dispute is that the first group — the whole church, in John's habitual address — is
told the thing that is true of all of them: forgiven.

## The word for children

**Teknia** — little born-ones. The same address as verse 1.

Later in the verse-series he uses **paidia**, a different diminutive.

## What comes just before

*If anyone does sin — **we have someone speaking for us** in front of the Father.* See the note on
1 John 2:1.

And *He Himself is the propitiation for our sins, and not for ours only but also for the whole
world*. See the note on 1 John 2:2.

The advocate is stated first, and then the readers are told their sins stand forgiven.

## What comes just after

*Do not love the world or the things in the world.* See the note on 1 John 2:15.

The instruction follows the statement. In this letter the indicative always comes before the
imperative.

## The other perfect

*He has **perfected forever** those who are being sanctified.* See the note on Hebrews 10:14.

The Greek is **teteleiōken** — the same tense doing the same work.

## The phrase in the Gospel

*"Your sins are forgiven you."* Spoken to a paralysed man on a mat, before he was healed and before
he had said anything. See the note on Mark 2:1–5.

Luke uses the identical form: **apheōntai soi hai hamartiai sou**.

> **The conjunction.** Six clauses, all *because*. John writes to the church on the ground of what
> is already true of them, not to bring it about.
