A prisoner stands up in a sinking ship and tells the crew what is going to happen.

## The situation

Fourteen days in a storm. Luke, who is aboard, describes it: they had thrown the cargo
over, then the ship's tackle with their own hands, and *when neither sun nor stars appeared
for many days, and no small tempest beat on us, all hope that we would be saved was finally
given up*.

## The hunger

*When they had **gone a long time without food**.*

Not fasting by choice. Nobody could cook or keep anything down.

## Who stands up

*Paul **stood up among them**.*

A prisoner in chains, on his way to trial in Rome, addressing the crew, the soldiers and
the centurion.

## The reproach

*"Men, **you should have listened to me** and not put out from Crete."*

He had warned them: *I perceive that this voyage will end with disaster and much loss.*

*But the centurion was more persuaded by the helmsman and the owner of the ship than by the
things spoken by Paul.*

Which was the reasonable decision. A tentmaker's opinion against the captain's.

He mentions it once and moves on.

## The turn

*"**But now I urge you: take heart**."*

## The promise

*"**Not one life among you will be lost — only the ship**."*

Two hundred and seventy-six people aboard, by Luke's count.

## The source

*"**Last night an angel of the God whose I am and whom I serve stood beside me**."*

Two clauses about ownership and service, in a sentence to a shipful of pagans.

## What the angel said

*"**Do not be afraid, Paul. You must stand before Caesar**."*

He had been told this in Jerusalem two years earlier: *as you have testified for Me in
Jerusalem, so you must also bear witness at Rome.*

## The gift

*"And look, **God has granted you the lives of all who are sailing with you**."*

The Greek is **kecharistai** — from **charis**, grace. Has graciously given.

The two hundred and seventy-five other people are described as given to Paul.

## The confidence

*"So **take heart**, men. **I trust God that it will turn out exactly as I was told**."*

He says *take heart* twice in six verses.

## The one detail

*"But **we are going to run aground on some island**."*

He does not promise a smooth ending. The ship is lost and they hit land somewhere
unspecified.

## What happens later that night

The sailors try to escape in the lifeboat under pretence of laying anchors, and Paul tells
the centurion: *unless these men stay in the ship, **you cannot be saved**.*

The soldiers cut the ropes and let the boat fall away.

Both are true in the chapter: God has granted the lives, and the men have to stay aboard.

## The breakfast

*And while day was coming on, Paul implored them all to take food… **and he took bread and
gave thanks to God in the presence of them all**, and when he had broken it he began to
eat.*

*Then they were all encouraged, and also took food themselves.*

He says grace over a meal in a wrecking ship, and everybody eats.

## The count

*And so it was that **they all escaped safely to land**.*

## Where they land

Malta. See the note on Acts 28:1–6.

> **Who is giving the orders.** A prisoner, after a fortnight of storm, on the strength of
> a night visit — and Luke, who was on the ship, records that the count came out exactly
> as he said.
