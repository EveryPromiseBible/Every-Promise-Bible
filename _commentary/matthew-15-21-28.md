The hardest conversation Jesus has with anyone who came to Him for help, and it
ends with the highest compliment He pays anybody in Matthew.

## Where it happened

*Jesus went out from there and departed to the region of **Tyre and Sidon**.*

That is the far north-west coast — the border of **Asher**, whose whole blessing in
Genesis 49 is one line about food:

*Asher's food will be rich; he will provide **delicacies fit for a king**.*

The only recorded time He left Israel's territory, and the conversation He has
there is about bread and a table. See the note on Genesis 49:20.

## The three obstacles

She meets a wall three times:

- *He did not answer her a word*
- *I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel*
- *It is not right to take the children's bread and toss it to the little dogs*

Silence, then a limit, then what sounds like an insult. And in between, the
disciples asking Him to get rid of her.

## What she does with each one

She keeps coming. After the silence she comes closer; after the limit she kneels;
after the dogs remark she takes the image and finishes it.

*Yes, Lord — and even the little dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their
master's table.*

She does not dispute the order He has stated. She accepts it entirely and points
out that it does not exclude her: if the children are being fed, there is
something under the table.

## The word He used

**Kunarion** — a diminutive. Not the scavenging street dogs, but the little ones
inside a house. It is a household word, and she hears the household in it — *their
master's table*, not somebody else's.

## What a crumb did

She asked for a crumb and she got what she asked for. *Let it be done for you
just as you desire* — and a demon left her daughter that hour, at a distance.

That is what fell off the edge of the table, unnoticed by the people eating at
it: enough to break a demonic hold on a child in another house.

The children's bread is what the crumb broke off. Whoever is at that table is not
reaching for fragments — they are handed the loaf, torn and put into their hands,
and told to eat it.

## What He says

*Woman, your faith is great.* He says this to exactly two people in Matthew, and
both are Gentiles; the other is the centurion.

*Let it be done for you just as you desire.* And her daughter was healed from that
hour, at a distance, with the mother still standing in front of Him.

## What she called Him first

*Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David.* A Canaanite woman, from the country Israel
was told to drive out, addressing Him by His messianic title.

She had the doctrine right before the conversation started.

> **Why the delay.** Nothing here suggests reluctance in Him — Matthew has already
> shown Him healing a Roman's servant on request. What the exchange produced was
> an answer from her that has been quoted ever since, and a public verdict on her
> faith in front of disciples who had just asked Him to send her away.
