Women arrive with burial spices and are asked a question they cannot answer.

## The timing

*Very early on **the first day of the week**, at first light.*

The Greek is **te de mia ton sabbaton orthrou batheos** — at deep dawn.

They had rested on the Sabbath *according to the commandment*, and set out the moment it was
legal to walk that far.

## What they brought

*Bringing **the spices they had prepared**.*

Prepared on the Friday, before sunset. They had watched the burial: *and the women who had come
with Him from Galilee followed after, and they observed the tomb and how His body was laid*.

They know exactly where they are going and what they expect to do there.

## The stone

*They found **the stone rolled away** from the tomb.*

Mark records their conversation on the way: *who will roll away the stone from the door of the
tomb for us?*

The problem they had discussed was already solved.

## The absence

*And when they went inside they **did not find the body of the Lord Jesus**.*

## The reaction

*They were **standing there at a loss** over it.*

The Greek is **aporeisthai** — to be without a way, perplexed.

Not joy. Confusion.

## The two men

*Two men appeared beside them in **clothing that blazed like lightning**.*

The Greek is **en esthesi astraptouse** — in flashing apparel.

Luke has two, and describes them as men. Matthew has one angel; Mark has a young man in a white
robe.

## The fear

*The women were terrified and **bowed their faces to the ground**.*

## The question

*"**Why are you looking for the Living One among the dead?**"*

The Greek is **ti zeteite ton zonta meta ton nekron**.

**Ton zonta** — the Living One. A title, used of God in the Old Testament.

The question is not a rebuke about their reasoning. It is a statement about a category error.

## The two clauses

*"**He is not here. He has risen**."*

The Greek is **ouk estin hode, alla egerthe** — passive. He was raised.

## The instruction

*"**Remember what He told you while He was still in Galilee**."*

The Greek is **mnesthete** — remember.

Not new information. Something they already had.

## What He had told them

*"That the Son of Man **had to be handed over into the hands of sinful men, and be crucified, and
rise again on the third day**."*

Three clauses, and Luke has recorded Him saying them three separate times.

The last one was always in the sentence, and nobody in the story ever reacted to it.

## The result

*And **they remembered His words**.*

The Greek is **kai emnesthesan ton rhematon autou**.

The angels' entire message is: you were told.

## What the women do

*Then they returned from the tomb and told all these things to the eleven and to all the rest.*

Luke names them: *Mary Magdalene, Joanna, Mary the mother of James, and the other women*.

## What the men do

*And **their words seemed to them like idle tales, and they did not believe them**.*

The Greek is **leros** — nonsense, delirium. A medical word for the ravings of the very ill.

Luke, a physician, uses it.

## What Peter does

*But Peter arose and ran to the tomb… and he departed, **marveling to himself at what had
happened**.*

## Who is told next

Two people walking to a village seven miles out, who do not recognise Him for most of the
afternoon. See the note on Luke 24:13–16.

> **The angels' message.** Not new information — *remember what He told you*. He had said it three
> times, with the third day in the sentence every time.
