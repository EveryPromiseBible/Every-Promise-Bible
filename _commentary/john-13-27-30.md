Four words end this paragraph, and John did not put them there for the weather.

## The bread

*As soon as **Judas took the bread**, Satan entered into him.*

The morsel had just been handed to him by Jesus, dipped and given — which at that
table was a gesture of honour, offered to a favoured guest.

The last thing Judas receives from Him is a mark of friendship.

## What Jesus says

*"**What you are about to do, do quickly.**"*

Not *don't*. Not an exposure in front of the others — John says nobody at the table
understood it.

He knows exactly what is happening and releases him to go and do it.

## What the others assumed

*Some of them thought that, **because Judas kept the money bag**, Jesus was telling
him to buy what they needed for the festival, **or to give something to the poor**.*

Eleven men, three years in, and not one of them suspects him. Judas is the
treasurer, and the natural assumption about him leaving is that he has gone to give
money away.

Earlier in the chapter Jesus had said *one of you will betray Me*, and they looked
at one another *perplexed*, and each asked *is it I?*

## He went out at once

*Judas took the piece of bread and **went out at once**.*

He obeys immediately. It is the last recorded thing he does in the upper room.

## The four words

*And it was night.*

John writes the way a man writes who has been thinking about it for sixty years.

He could have written nothing. The time is not needed — nobody is going to be
confused about the hour of the Last Supper.

He has spent his whole Gospel on light and darkness: *the light shines in the
darkness*, *men loved darkness rather than light*, *I am the light of the world*, *he
who follows Me shall not walk in darkness*.

A man walks out of the room where the Light is sitting, and John records the weather
of it.

## What happens the moment he is gone

The very next verse: *"Now the Son of Man is glorified, and God is glorified in
Him."*

And then: *"A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another as I have
loved you."*

The door closes behind Judas and Jesus starts the longest and warmest passage in the
Gospels — chapters 14 to 17, the upper room discourse, and the prayer.

## What He had done to Judas' feet

Earlier the same evening He washed them. See the note on John 13:1–3.

He knew, and He knelt down in front of him anyway.

> warming himself at a charcoal fire — and is restored over another charcoal fire on
> a beach. Two men fail on the same night. Only one comes back to breakfast. See the
> note on John 21:1–3.
