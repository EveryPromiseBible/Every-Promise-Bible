The instruction that organises the whole book, and then the first thing it explains.

## Where John is

*I, John, both your brother and companion in the tribulation… **was on the island that is called
Patmos** for the word of God and for the testimony of Jesus Christ.*

He has just seen the figure among the lampstands and *fell at His feet as dead*, and been told
*do not be afraid*. See the note on Revelation 1:17–18.

## The instruction

*So **write it down**.*

The Greek is **grapson** — aorist imperative. A single decisive act.

It is the twelfth of twelve occurrences of the command to write in this book. He is told to
write to each of the seven churches, and told at the very end *these words are true and
faithful*.

## The three tenses

*What you have just seen, what is going on now, and what is going to happen after this.*

The Greek is **ha eides, kai ha eisin, kai ha mellei genesthai meta tauta**.

Seen, are, about to be.

## What the three correspond to

Chapter 1 is what he has seen — the figure among the lampstands.

Chapters 2 and 3 are what is: seven letters to seven functioning congregations with real
problems.

Chapter 4 opens with the same phrase: *come up here, and I will show you things which **must take
place after this***.

The book states its own outline in one verse.

## The word for secret

*Here is **the secret** behind the seven stars… and behind the seven gold lampstands.*

The Greek is **musterion** — mystery. In the New Testament, not something unknowable but
something previously hidden and now disclosed.

Paul uses it the same way: *the mystery which has been hidden from ages and from generations, but
now has been revealed*. See the note on Colossians 1:24–27.

## The first explanation

*The seven stars are **the angels belonging to the seven churches**.*

The Greek is **angeloi** — messengers. The word can mean a heavenly being or a human envoy, and
each of the seven letters is addressed *to the angel of the church in*.

## The second

*And the seven lampstands you saw are **the seven churches**.*

Which is the interpretation that matters most, because of where the figure was standing.

*In the midst of the seven lampstands One like the Son of Man.*

## What that means

He is standing among them, holding their messengers in His right hand, before a word of
correction is spoken.

Five of the seven letters contain a rebuke. Two of them are severe. The position was established
first.

## The lampstand

In the tabernacle there was one lampstand with seven branches, and the priest tended it every
morning and evening.

Here there are seven separate stands, and the one walking among them is not a priest trimming
wicks but the figure of chapter 1.

## What is threatened

*Repent, or else I will come to you quickly and **remove your lampstand from its place***. — to
Ephesus.

The stands can be moved.

## What the right hand was doing

*He laid His right hand on me, saying, "Do not be afraid."*

The same hand that holds the seven stars. John felt it on his shoulder before he was told what
was in it.

## Where the writing ends

*And he said to me, "**Do not seal the words of the prophecy of this book**, for the time is at
hand."*

Daniel was told the opposite — *shut up the words, and seal the book until the time of the end*.

> **The outline.** Seen, are, about to be — the book stating its own three-part shape in one
> sentence, before a single seal is opened.
