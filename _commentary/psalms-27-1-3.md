Two questions with no answer given, because the answer is nobody.

## The three titles

*The LORD is my **light** and my **rescue**… the LORD is the **stronghold** of my life.*

The Hebrew is **ori** — my light. **Yish'i** — my salvation. **Ma'oz** — my stronghold, my place of
refuge.

## The first

**Or** — light.

The only place in the Psalter where God is called *my light*.

## The two questions

*Whom shall I fear? Of whom shall I be afraid?*

The Hebrew is **mimmi ira** and **mimmi efchad**.

Both are rhetorical. Neither is answered.

## What form they take

*Whom* — a person, not a circumstance.

The psalm's threat throughout is people: the wicked, an army, false witnesses.

## The first scene

*When the wicked close in on me **to devour my very flesh**.*

The Hebrew is **le'ekol et-besari** — to eat my flesh. Predator language.

## What happens to them

*These enemies, these foes of mine, **it is they who stumble and fall**.*

The Hebrew is **hemmah kashelu venafalu** — they stumbled and fell. Perfect tense.

Written as something that has already happened.

## The escalation

*Though **an army camps against me**, my heart will not be afraid. Though **war rises up around me**,
even then I will trust.*

The Hebrew is **machaneh** — an encampment. And **milchamah** — war.

## The structure

Each line names a larger threat and gives the same answer.

## What he asks for

*One thing I have desired of the LORD… that I may **dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my
life**, to **behold the beauty of the LORD**.* See the note on Psalms 27:4–6.

The Hebrew is **no'am** — pleasantness, favour, delight.

## The one request

A man surrounded by an army asks for one thing, and it is not the army's removal.

## Where the psalm turns

*When You said, "Seek My face," my heart said to You, "**Your face, LORD, I will seek**."* See the note
on Psalms 27:7–10.

And *when my father and my mother forsake me, then the LORD will take care of me*.

## How it ends

*Yet I am sure of this: **I will see the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living**. **Wait for the
LORD**.* See the note on Psalms 27:11–14.

## The two halves

The psalm's first half is confident; the second half is a plea — *do not hide Your face from me*.

Both are in it, and the editor did not smooth them out.

## Where the light goes

*"**I am the light of the world**. He who follows Me shall not walk in darkness, but have the light of
life."* See the note on John 8:12–14.

And *the city had no need of the sun or of the moon to shine in it, for **the glory of God
illuminated it**, and the Lamb is its light*. See the note on Revelation 21:22–23.

> **The form of the question.** *Whom* shall I fear — not *what*. The psalm's threats are all
> people, and it asks about them by name.
