A woman notices something about a passing prophet and builds him a room.

## The town

*One day Elisha was passing through **Shunem**.*

A village in the Jezreel valley, on the route between Carmel and the Jordan. Elisha walked
it regularly.

## The woman

*Where **a well-to-do woman** lived.*

The Hebrew is **ishah gedolah** — a great woman. It can mean wealthy, prominent, or of
standing.

She is never named. Through four chapters she is *the Shunammite*.

## The first move

*She **urged him to stay for a meal**.*

The Hebrew is *she constrained him* — pressed him to.

*And after that, **whenever he came by, he would stop there to eat**.*

One meal became a habit.

## Her reasoning

*She said to her husband, "**I know that this man who often passes our way is a holy man of
God**."*

She works it out herself. Nobody tells her.

The husband is not the one who notices, and he does not object.

## The proposal

*"Let's **make a small room on the roof** for him and put **a bed in it, a table, a chair,
and a lamp**."*

Four items. The Hebrew lists them one by one.

A bed to sleep, a table to work, a chair to sit, and a lamp to see. It is the minimum
furniture for a life, and she thought through each piece.

## The reason

*"Then **whenever he comes to us, he'll have a place to stay**."*

Not for an occasion. For every time.

## What she gets

Elisha sends Gehazi to ask what can be done for her — *would you like to be spoken for to
the king or to the commander of the army?*

Her answer: *I dwell among my own people.*

She wants nothing. It is Gehazi who observes that she has no son and her husband is old.

*"About this time next year you shall embrace a son."*

And her reply: *"No, my lord. **Man of God, do not lie to your maidservant**."*

She had not asked and did not want the hope.

## What happens later

The boy grows, goes out to the harvesters, cries *my head, my head*, and dies on her lap
at noon.

She carries him up to the roof room, lays him on the prophet's bed, shuts the door, and
rides to Carmel. See the note on 2 Kings 4:18–25.

## Where she puts him

On the bed she built.

## What she says on the road

To her husband: *it is well.* To Gehazi, sent to meet her: *it is well.*

Only when she reaches Elisha and takes hold of his feet does she say anything else.

## Where the story ends

*And he called Gehazi and said, "Call this Shunammite woman." So he called her. And when she
came in to him, he said, "**Pick up your son**." So she went in, fell at his feet, bowed to
the ground, **picked up her son, and went out**.*

## The last we hear of her

Chapter 8: Elisha warns her of a seven-year famine, she leaves for Philistine country, and
comes back to petition the king for her house and land — arriving at the exact moment
Gehazi is telling the king the story of her son.

She gets everything back, *and all the proceeds of the field from the day that she left the
land until now*.

## What she noticed

*"Look now, **I know that this is a holy man of God**, who passes by us regularly."*

She works it out from a man walking past her house on his rounds. Nothing dramatic is recorded
— no miracle, no sermon. She watches him go by and draws a conclusion.

## What she does about it

Builds a room. Bed, table, chair, lampstand — the four items, listed.

Jesus makes it a rule: *"**he who receives a prophet in the name of a prophet shall receive a
prophet's reward**… And whoever gives one of these little ones only a cup of cold water in the
name of a disciple, assuredly, I say to you, he shall by no means lose his reward."*

## What she asks for

*"**I dwell among my own people**."*

Elisha offers to speak for her to the king or the commander of the army, and she declines. She
is not building the room to be owed anything.

She gets a son anyway, and later gets him back from the dead, and later still gets her house,
her land and seven years of its produce restored by royal order. See the note on
2 Kings 8:6.

*"But when you do a charitable deed, **do not let your left hand know what your right hand is
doing**… and your Father who sees in secret will Himself reward you openly."*

## Where the room is

*On the wall* — a roof chamber, built onto the outside of the house.

It costs her something and it is for one guest who is not there most of the time.

> **What she built.** Four pieces of furniture in a room on a roof, for a man who came
> through occasionally — and it is the bed she lays her dead son on years later.
