The reading goes well, the response is tears, and three sets of leaders tell them to stop.

## What is happening

The wall is finished. The people gather in the square before the Water Gate and ask Ezra to bring
the Book of the Law.

*So Ezra the priest brought the Law before the assembly of men and women and all who could hear
with understanding… **and the ears of all the people were attentive**.* See the note on
Nehemiah 8:1–3.

From morning until midday, on a wooden platform, with Levites moving among them explaining.

*So they read distinctly from the book… **and helped them to understand the reading**.*

## The response

*Now **the people were weeping as they listened to the words of the Law**.*

They have been in exile. Most of them have never heard the whole thing read.

The weeping is not confusion. They understood it, which is the problem.

## Who stops them

*And **Nehemiah the governor, and Ezra the priest and scribe, and the Levites who were teaching**,
said to them all.*

The civil authority, the religious authority, and the teachers, together.

Three offices, one sentence. It is the only place in the book where all three speak as one.

## What they say

*"**This day is holy to the LORD your God. Do not mourn, and do not weep**."*

The Hebrew is **qadosh hu** — it is holy.

The day is the first of the seventh month, the Feast of Trumpets. A festival day.

## What they are told to do instead

*"Go your way. **Eat rich food and drink something sweet**."*

The Hebrew is **ichlu mashmannim ushtu mamtaqqim** — eat fat things, drink sweet things.

An instruction to have a good meal, given to people crying over Scripture.

## The third thing

*"And **send portions to those who have nothing ready**."*

The Hebrew is **manot** — portions, shares.

Anyone who has not prepared gets sent food. The celebration is not allowed to be private.

## The reason

*"**For this day is holy to our LORD. Do not be grieved, for the joy of the LORD is your
strength**."*

The Hebrew is **chedvat YHWH hi ma'uzchem**.

**Chedvah** — gladness. A rare word, Aramaic in flavour, used only here and in Chronicles.

**Ma'oz** — a stronghold, a fortified place, a refuge.

## What the sentence says

Not *be joyful and you will feel stronger*.

The joy belongs to the LORD — **of the LORD** — and it functions as a fortress.

## The order of the day

Read the law, understand it, weep, be told not to, eat well, send food out, and be told the joy
is the stronghold.

Nothing in that sequence is what the reading naturally produced.

## What happens next

*And all the people went their way to eat and drink, to send portions and rejoice, **because they
understood the words that were declared to them**.*

The same understanding that produced the weeping produces the rejoicing, one verse later.

## The next day

The heads of the households come back to study further, and find the command about booths — and
they keep the Feast of Tabernacles.

*Since the days of Joshua the son of Nun until that day **the children of Israel had not done so**.
And there was very great gladness.*

A thousand years, and nobody had kept it.

## Where the confession comes

Chapter 9. *They stood and confessed their sins and the iniquities of their fathers*, with
sackcloth and dust on their heads.

The mourning does happen. Three weeks later, on a day appointed for it.

> **The word.** **Ma'oz** — a stronghold. The joy is not described as a feeling that helps but as
> a fortified place, and the sentence says whose it is.
