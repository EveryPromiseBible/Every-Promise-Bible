The last twenty-two verses of Proverbs, and the first word sets the terms.

## What it is

An acrostic. Twenty-two verses, each beginning with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet.

A finished, formal poem — the book's closing set piece.

## Who wrote it

*The words of King Lemuel, the utterance which his mother taught him.*

Lemuel is unidentified. And the poem before the acrostic is his mother's instruction to him:
*do not give your strength to women… open your mouth for the speechless, in the cause of all
who are appointed to die*.

A queen mother telling a son what to look for, and what to be.

## The word

*A **woman of valor**, who can find her?*

The Hebrew is **eshet chayil**.

**Chayil** — strength, ability, worth, army, wealth. The whole range sits in one noun.

It is the word for Gideon — *mighty man of valour*. It is the word for the *capable men* of
Obed-edom's household, *strong for the work*. See the note on
1 Chronicles 26:6–11.

Translating it *virtuous* narrows it to morals. The Hebrew is closer to *formidable*.

## The question

*Who can find her?*

A rhetorical opening, the same shape as *who has believed our report?*

## The value

*She is worth **far more than rubies**.*

The Hebrew is **peninim** — rubies, or corals, or pearls. The comparison is standard in the
book: wisdom is *more precious than rubies*.

## The trust

*Her husband's heart **rests safe in her**, and **he is never left wanting**.*

The Hebrew is **batach** — trusts, leans on, is confident. The security word.

*He is never left wanting* — the Hebrew is *he shall have no lack of gain*, and **shalal** is
plunder, spoil. A military word.

## The duration

*She brings him **good and not harm all the days of her life**.*

Not most days.

## What she does in the rest of the poem

Buys a field and plants a vineyard from her own earnings. Trades. Works wool and flax. Gets up
before dawn. Reaches out to the poor. Makes and sells linen garments. Speaks with wisdom.

*Strength and honour are her clothing; she shall rejoice in time to come.*

## Where it is read

In observant Jewish homes the husband sings **Eshet Chayil** to his wife at the table on Friday
night, before the Sabbath meal.

A poem about competence, sung weekly, for centuries.

## Who is called this elsewhere

Ruth. Boaz says it to her at the threshing floor: *all the people of my town know that you are
**a virtuous woman***.

The same phrase, **eshet chayil** — said to a Moabite widow gleaning in a field.

And the book of Ruth sits immediately after Proverbs in the Hebrew ordering, so the poem's
question — *who can find her?* — is followed by a book about a man who did.

## The last verse

*Charm is deceitful and beauty is passing, but **a woman who fears the LORD, she shall be
praised**.*

*Give her of the fruit of her hands, and **let her own works praise her in the gates**.*

The gates are where the elders sat and the town's business was done.

The book ends by sending a woman's reputation into the public square on the strength of what
she made.

## Who found her

*A woman of valour, **who can find her?***

The book ends on a question, and the New Testament answers it with a Bridegroom.

*Husbands, love your wives, just as **Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her**,
that He might sanctify and cleanse her with the washing of water by the word, that **He might
present her to Himself a glorious church**, not having spot or wrinkle or any such thing.*

**Paul**'s point is the order. She is not found glorious. She is made glorious by the one who
came for her, and then presented.

## The linen

She *makes and sells linen garments*, and *strength and honour are her clothing*.

*"Let us be glad and rejoice and give Him glory, for the marriage of the Lamb has come, and
**His wife has made herself ready**." And to her it was granted to be arrayed in **fine linen,
clean and bright** — for the fine linen is the righteous acts of the saints.*

**Revelation** dresses the bride in exactly what the acrostic has her weaving, and adds one
verb: *it was granted to her*. The linen is her work and it is given to her to wear.

## The valour

**Chayil** — the word for an army.

*"**I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.**"*

## And what the last verse rests on

*Charm is deceitful and beauty is passing, but **a woman who fears the LORD, she shall be
praised**.*

*Whose praise is not from men but **from God**.*

> **The word the poem opens with.** *Chayil* — the word used of Gideon and of an army. Not
> *virtuous* in the narrow sense; closer to *formidable*.
