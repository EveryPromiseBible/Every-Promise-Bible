Three verses, and the Trinity is in all three of them.

## Where He came from

*Jesus came from **Nazareth in Galilee**.*

Mark has no birth narrative and no genealogy. The first thing he says about Jesus'
origins is a village nobody rated — *can anything good come out of Nazareth?*

Thirty years, summarised as a place name.

## The baptism

*And **John baptized Him** in the Jordan.*

Mark does not record the objection Matthew includes — *I need to be baptized by You, and are
You coming to me?* — or the answer, *permit it to be so now, for thus it is fitting for us
to fulfil all righteousness*.

John's baptism was *for the remission of sins*, and the one man in the queue with none goes
into the water anyway.

## The moment

*And **the moment He came up out of the water**.*

Mark's favourite word is **euthus** — immediately. He uses it more than forty times, eleven
in this first chapter alone.

## The tearing

*He saw the heavens **split wide open**.*

The Greek is **schizomenous** — torn, rent. Matthew and Luke have the milder *opened*.

Mark uses this verb exactly twice. The other is at the crucifixion: *and the veil of the
temple was **torn** in two from top to bottom.*

The heavens at the start, the veil at the end, and the same word for both.

Isaiah had asked for it: *Oh, that You would rend the heavens! That You would come down!*

## The dove

*And the **Spirit coming down on Him like a dove settling**.*

The Greek is *as a dove descending upon Him*.

A dove appears once before in a story about water receding, carrying an olive leaf.

## The voice

*And a voice came out of heaven.*

Three parties present, doing three different things: the Son in the river, the Spirit
descending, the Father speaking.

## What it says

*"**You are My Son, the One I love. My delight rests on You**."*

Mark's version is second person — *You are*. Matthew's is third — *this is My beloved Son*.

Mark has it said to Him.

The Greek is **ho agapetos** — the beloved. And **eudokesa** — I am well pleased, an aorist:
the pleasure is settled.

## What He had done at this point

Nothing recorded. No sermon, no miracle, no disciple called.

The delight is announced before the ministry starts.

## The two Old Testament sentences behind it

*You are My Son* — Psalm 2, the coronation psalm.

*In whom My soul delights* — Isaiah 42, the first Servant Song, which continues *I have put
My Spirit upon Him*.

King and Servant, in one sentence, over a man standing in a river.

## What happens next

*Immediately the Spirit **drove Him into the wilderness**.*

The Greek is **ekballei** — the word for casting out demons. Mark's verb is violent where
Matthew's is *led up*.

Forty days, and the tempter's opening line is *if You are the Son of God*. See the note on
Matthew 4:1–4.

The words tested are the ones just spoken from the sky, and the word not repeated is
*beloved*.

## Where the voice comes back

On the mountain of transfiguration: *this is My beloved Son. **Hear Him!***

Twice in the Gospels the Father speaks about the Son, and both times the middle word is the
same.

> **What Mark tore.** The heavens here and the veil at the cross — one verb, used twice in
> the whole Gospel, at the two ends of it.
