*So Abraham prayed to God, and God healed Abimelech.* It is the first time
anyone in scripture prays for someone else's healing, and the circumstances are
uncomfortable.

## Who is praying for whom

Abraham had just told a foreign king that Sarah was his sister — the same lie he
had told in Egypt — and Abimelech had taken her into his household on the
strength of it.

So the man praying is the man whose deception caused the crisis. The man being
prayed for is the injured party who behaved better throughout: God Himself says
Abimelech acted *with a clear conscience and clean hands*.

## What God says to do about it

Earlier in the chapter God tells Abimelech to return the woman, and adds: *he is
a prophet, and he will pray for you, and you will live*.

The instruction is not that Abraham deserves to be heard. It is simply what is to
happen. God assigns the intercession to the person in the wrong.

## The detail at the end

*For the LORD had closed up every womb in Abimelech's household.* The reason for
the healing is only explained after it is granted — the writer holds it back to
the last line of the chapter.

And Abraham, who has been childless for decades and is one chapter away from
Isaac, prays for another household's fertility and is answered.

## Who he prayed for

*So Abraham prayed to God, and **God healed Abimelech**, his wife, and his female servants.*

The first recorded prayer for healing in Scripture, and Abraham prays it for the king who had
taken his wife into a harem — a situation Abraham had personally caused by lying about her, for
the second time in his life.

The wronged party is God's. The man doing the praying is the one at fault. And the healing lands
on the household of the man he deceived.

*But I say to you, love your enemies, bless those who curse you… and **pray for those who
spitefully use you**.*

*Then Jesus said, "**Father, forgive them**, for they do not know what they do."* Said from a
cross, about the men driving the nails, and it is the same shape: intercession offered by the
one with the standing to press a charge.

## What is not in the chapter

Any suggestion that Abraham had earned the right to be heard.

He is called *a prophet* by God in the same paragraph in which he is caught out, and told
*he will pray for you, and you shall live*. The title and the failure sit in one sentence.

> **What the passage will not support.** It is not a model of a righteous man
> whose prayers were effective because of his conduct. Genesis records the lie in
> full, without excusing it, and then records the prayer being answered. Both
> things happened, and the text is not embarrassed by either.
