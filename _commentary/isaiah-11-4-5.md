A ruler is described by what he judges with and what he wears.

## Where this comes

*There shall come forth a Rod from the stem of Jesse, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots.*

*The Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon Him — the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of
counsel and might, the Spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD.* See the note on
Isaiah 11:1–3.

Six qualities in three pairs, and then how He judges.

## Who He judges for

*With righteousness he will judge **the poor**, and with fairness decide for **the humble of the
earth**.*

The Hebrew is **dallim** — the weak, the poor. And **anvei-aretz** — the humble of the land.

The two groups with no standing in any ancient court.

## Why that is the test

Verse 3 has just said He will not judge *by what His eyes see, nor decide by what His ears hear*.

A judge who cannot be swayed by appearance is a judge the poor can use.

## The weapon

*He will **strike the earth with the rod of his mouth**, and with **the breath of his lips** he will slay
the wicked.*

The Hebrew is **beshevet piv** — with the rod of His mouth. And **beruach sephatav** — with the
breath of His lips.

Not a sword. Speech.

## Where Paul takes it

*And then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord will **consume with the breath of His
mouth**.*

He quotes the line into 2 Thessalonians.

## The belt

*Righteousness will be **the belt around his waist**, and faithfulness **the sash around his hips**.*

The Hebrew is **ezor motnav** — the girdle of His loins.

## What a belt did

Held the tunic up and the sword on. A working man or a soldier girded himself before doing
anything.

*Let your waist be girded and your lamps burning.*

The garment nearest the body, and the one that makes movement possible.

## The two words

**Tzedeq** — righteousness. **Emunah** — faithfulness, reliability.

Not ornaments. The thing that holds everything else together.

## Where Paul puts it

*Stand therefore, having **girded your waist with truth**, and having put on **the breastplate of
righteousness**.* See the note on Ephesians 6:14–17.

Paul splits Isaiah's line into two pieces of armour and gives them both to the reader.

The belt the Messiah wears becomes the belt the soldier wears.

## What the chapter goes on to describe

*The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, the leopard shall lie down with the young goat… **and a
little child shall lead them**.*

*The nursing child shall play by the cobra's hole.*

*They shall not hurt nor destroy in all My holy mountain, **for the earth shall be full of the
knowledge of the LORD as the waters cover the sea**.* See the note on Isaiah 11:6–9.

## The root

*"And in that day there shall be a **Root of Jesse**, who shall stand as a banner to the people; **for
the Gentiles shall seek Him**."*

## What He calls Himself at the end of the Bible

*"I am the **Root and the Offspring of David**. I am the bright morning star."* See the note on
Revelation 22:16–17.

Both at once — the root the tree grows from and the shoot it produces.

> **The weapon.** Not a sword — *the rod of His mouth* and *the breath of His lips*. And the belt
> that holds the working clothes together is righteousness.
