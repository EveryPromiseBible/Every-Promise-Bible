He arrives with an escort, and two men in the region are unhappy about it before he
has laid a stone.

## The letters

*So I came to the governors of the province **Beyond the River**, and **I gave them the
king's letters**.*

*Beyond the River* is the official Persian name for the satrapy west of the Euphrates —
Nehemiah uses the imperial term, as a court official would.

The letters are produced immediately. His authority is established at the border, in
writing, before anyone can question it.

## The escort

*The king had also sent **army officers and horsemen** with me.*

Ezra, going the same road a few years earlier, had deliberately refused an escort —
*I was ashamed to request of the king an escort of soldiers, because we had spoken to
the king saying, "The hand of our God is upon all those who seek Him."*

Nehemiah takes the cavalry. Ezra declines it. The book records both without ranking
them.

## The two names

*But when **Sanballat the Horonite** and **Tobiah the Ammonite official** heard of it.*

**Sanballat** — governor of Samaria; a Babylonian name meaning something like *Sin has
given life*, after the moon god. Papyri from Elephantine name him as governor, so he is
a documented figure outside the Bible.

**Tobiah** — a Hebrew name meaning *the LORD is good*. He is called *the Ammonite
servant*, and he keeps turning up.

A man called The-LORD-is-good is the standing opponent for thirteen chapters.

## The connections Tobiah had

He is married into a Jerusalem family, his son married the daughter of another, and
nobles in the city were *sending letters to him*. In chapter 13 he is given a room in
the temple itself. See the note on Nehemiah 13:4–5.

He never attacks from outside. He is inside the correspondence the whole time.

## What displeased them

*They were **deeply displeased** that someone had come **to seek the good of the people
of Israel**.*

The Hebrew is *it grieved them exceedingly with a great grief*.

The offence named is not the wall, the money, or the royal authority. It is that
somebody had come to seek Israel's good.

## What Nehemiah does next

He tells nobody. Three days after arriving he goes out at night with a few men and one
animal, and rides round the ruins in the dark: *I told no one what my God had put in my
heart to do at Jerusalem.*

Then he calls the assembly, and only then does he say *let us build.*

## The reply when they mock

*"**The God of heaven Himself will prosper us**; therefore we His servants will arise
and build, **but you have no heritage or right or memorial in Jerusalem**."*

Short, and not defensive.

> **What arrives with him.** Letters, timber, cavalry, and two enemies. All four are in
> the same paragraph, and Nehemiah lists them without treating the last as a surprise.
