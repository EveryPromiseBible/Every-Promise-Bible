Eight proverbs, and the last one ends the run on a table.

## The sacrifice and the prayer

*The LORD **hates the sacrifice of the wicked**, but **the prayer of the upright delights
Him**.*

The Hebrew for *delights* is **ratson** — favour, acceptance, good pleasure. The word used
of a sacrifice being accepted.

One man brings an animal and it is detestable. The other says nothing but words and it is
the thing God takes pleasure in.

Samuel says the same to Saul: *to obey is better than sacrifice*.

## The way and the pursuer

*The LORD **hates the way of the wicked**, but **He loves the one who pursues what is
right**.*

The Hebrew is *he loves him who follows after righteousness* — **radaph**, to chase, to
pursue hard. The verb for an army in pursuit.

Not the one who has arrived. The one still running after it.

## The correction

*Stern correction waits for the one who **leaves the path**. Whoever **hates rebuke will
die**.*

Two halves, and the second is the harsher.

Proverbs keeps the pair together throughout: *he who regards a rebuke will be honoured*, and
*he who hates correction is stupid*.

## The seeing

*Death and destruction **lie open before the LORD**. **How much more the hearts of men**.*

The Hebrew names them: **Sheol and Abaddon**. The grave and the place of ruin, personified,
transparent to Him.

The argument runs from the greater to the lesser. If the underworld is open, a chest cavity
is not a difficulty.

Hebrews says it the same way: *all things are naked and open to the eyes of Him to whom we
must give account.*

## The mocker

*A mocker will **not love the one who corrects him**. He **will not go near the wise**.*

The Hebrew is **lets** — the scoffer, a fixed character type in Proverbs.

He does not argue with wisdom. He keeps away from it.

## The face

*A **glad heart makes a cheerful face**. **Heartache crushes the spirit**.*

The Hebrew for *cheerful* is *makes the face shine* — **yetiv panim**.

The proverb reads inside to outside: the interior is the cause and the face is the report.

And Aaron's blessing runs the same direction from above: *the LORD make His face shine upon
you*.

## The appetite

*The understanding heart **goes looking for knowledge**. The **mouths of fools feed on
folly**.*

Both are eating. One goes looking; the other grazes on what is at hand.

## The last one

*Every day is **grim for the oppressed**, but a **happy heart keeps a feast going on and
on**.*

The Hebrew is *all the days of the afflicted are evil, but he who is of a merry heart has a
continual feast*.

**Mishteh tamid** — a banquet, perpetual.

No change of circumstance is mentioned between the two halves. The afflicted man and the
merry man could be the same man.

## Where the feast image goes

*And on this mountain the LORD of hosts will **make for all peoples a feast***. See the note
on Isaiah 25:6–9.

And the other proverb about the interior and the bones: *a cheerful heart is good medicine*.
See the note on Proverbs 17:22–28.

> **What the last line measures.** Not the day. *All the days of the afflicted are evil* —
> and the merry heart is at a banquet that does not stop, in the same weather.
