He asks where conflict comes from and answers his own question in three words.

## The question

*Where do all these **fights and running feuds** among you come from?*

The Greek is **polemoi kai machai** — wars and battles.

Military vocabulary, applied to a congregation.

## The answer

*From **inside you**.*

The Greek is **ek ton hedonon humon** — out of your pleasures.

**Hedone** — pleasure, the root of *hedonism*.

## The image

*From **the wanting that never shuts up, camped in your own body, at war**.*

The Greek is **ton strateuomenon en tois melesin humon** — campaigning in your members.

**Strateuomai** — to serve as a soldier, to wage a campaign. Not a skirmish; an occupation.

Paul uses the same picture: *I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind*.
See the note on Romans 7:21–23.

## The chain

*You want, and you do not get it, so you kill. You crave, and it stays out of reach, so you brawl
and you go to war.*

The Greek is **epithumeite kai ouk echete, phoneuete** — you desire and do not have, you murder.

The word **phoneuete** has troubled readers since the earliest manuscripts; some scribes changed
it. James is writing to congregations, and he does not soften it.

## The pattern

Want, fail to get, escalate.

The desire is not the problem in itself. The route taken when it is not met is.

## The sentence

*And **you still do not have it — because you never asked**.*

The Greek is **ouk echete dia to me aiteisthai humas**.

Every method has been tried except the one that was available.

## The second case

*Or you ask and nothing comes, and that is because of **what you are asking for**.*

The Greek is **kakos aiteisthe** — you ask badly, wrongly.

## The diagnosis

*You only want to **blow it on yourself**.*

The Greek is **hina en tais hedonais humon dapanesete** — that you may spend it on your pleasures.

**Dapanao** — to spend, to squander. The verb used of the prodigal: *he wasted his possessions
with prodigal living*, and of the woman who *had spent all her livelihood on physicians*. See the
note on Luke 8:43–48.

The same **hedone** as verse 1. The thing that starts the war is the thing the prayer is aimed at.

## What is not said

That asking for yourself is wrong.

The letter says *if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all liberally*. And
*you do not have because you do not ask* is a rebuke for not asking.

The problem named is the spending, not the asking.

## What follows

*You adulterous men, you adulterous women — has it not dawned on you yet? **Being this world's
friend means being God's enemy**.*

*But **He gives more grace**.* See the note on James 4:4–6.

The paragraph that names murder and war among believers turns, in three verses, to *He gives more
grace*.

## The instruction

*Therefore **submit to God. Resist the devil and he will flee from you**.*

*Draw near to God and He will draw near to you.*

*Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and **He will lift you up**.* See the note on
James 4:7–10.

## What the earlier chapter said

*But if you have bitter envy and self-seeking in your hearts, do not boast and lie against the
truth.*

*For where envy and self-seeking exist, **confusion and every evil thing are there**.* See the note
on James 3:13–16.

The same diagnosis, one chapter earlier, and the same interior location.

> **The line.** *You still do not have it — because you never asked.* Every escalation has been
> tried, and the one thing available was not.
