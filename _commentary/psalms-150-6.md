The last verse of the Psalter, and the qualification is a pulse.

## The line

*Let **everything that has breath** praise the LORD. **Hallelujah!***

The Hebrew is **kol hanneshamah tehallel Yah**.

## The word

**Neshamah** — breath.

It is the word in Genesis: *the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and **breathed into
his nostrils the breath of life***. See the note on Genesis 2:7–9.

Not the general word for spirit or wind. The specific noun for the breath God put in.

## The qualification

Everything that has it.

Not everything that understands, believes, deserves, or has got its life in order. Everything
that is breathing.

The bar is set at the lowest possible point on purpose.

## What comes before it

Twelve verses of instruments: trumpet, lute, harp, timbrel, strings, flutes, loud cymbals,
clashing cymbals.

And dance. And *praise Him in His mighty firmament*, and *praise Him for His mighty acts*.

Every method the ancient world had, and then a verse that needs no equipment.

## The last word

**Hallelu-Yah** — praise Yah. The shortened divine name.

The Psalter's final word is the imperative that gave English *hallelujah*.

## Where the book started

*Blessed is the man who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly.*

It opens with a single man deciding where to walk. It closes with everything that breathes.

## The five books

The Psalter is arranged in five books, each ending with a doxology.

The last of them is not a verse but five whole psalms — 146 to 150, every one beginning and
ending with *hallelujah*.

The book that contains *my God, my God, why have You forsaken Me* and *out of the depths I have
cried* is arranged to end this way.

## What the psalm does not mention

Any circumstance.

There is no reason given in verse 6, no *because*, and no condition. Just the breath and the
instruction.

## The other place breath is decisive

*Then He said to me, "Prophesy to the breath… **Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe on
these slain, that they may live**."* See the note on Ezekiel 37:9–10.

A valley of bones, and what makes the difference is breath.

## And the upper room

*He breathed on them, and said to them, "**Receive the Holy Spirit**."* See the note on
John 20:21–23.

The Greek is **enephusesen** — the same verb the Septuagint uses in Genesis 2:7. It occurs twice
in the whole Greek Bible: the making of Adam, and this.

## What Paul says the breath became

*All Scripture is **given by inspiration of God**.* — **theopneustos**, God-breathed. See the note
on 2 Timothy 3:16–17.

> **The bar.** Breath. Not understanding, not worthiness, not a good year — the Psalter ends by
> setting the qualification as low as it can go and then saying *everything*.
