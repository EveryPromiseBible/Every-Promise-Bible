The instruction is not about how to bear it. It is about what not to conclude from it.

## The address

*Friends I love.*

The Greek is **agapētoi** — beloved.

Peter uses it four times in the letter, and always immediately before something hard.

## The word for the trial

*When **the fire arrives to test you**.*

The Greek is **tē en humin purōsei pros peirasmon** — the burning among you for testing.

**Purōsis** — a burning, a smelting. The word for what happens in a furnace.

## What the furnace is for

**Pros peirasmon** — toward testing.

Not destruction. The purpose is stated in the same phrase.

## The instruction

*Do not act like **something bizarre is happening to you**.*

The Greek is **mē xenizesthe** — do not be made strange, do not be astonished as at a foreign thing.

**Xenos** — a stranger, a foreigner. The root of *xenophobia*.

## What the command actually forbids

Not the pain. The interpretation.

The verb is about a reaction of *this does not belong here*. Peter says the reasoning is wrong, not
that the fire does not hurt.

## What he had already said

*In this you greatly rejoice, though now for a little while, if need be, you have been grieved by
various trials, **that the genuineness of your faith, being much more precious than gold that
perishes, though it is tested by fire**, may be found to praise*. See the note on 1 Peter 1:6–7.

The same metallurgical picture, at the top of the letter.

## The reason he gives next

*But **rejoice to the extent that you partake of Christ's sufferings**, that when His glory is
revealed, you may also be glad with exceeding joy.* See the note on 1 Peter 4:13.

The Greek is **koinōneite** — you share, you have fellowship in.

## The measure

*To the extent that.*

Not: rejoice about the pain. The joy is proportioned to whose sufferings they turn out to be.

## The distinction he insists on

*But let none of you suffer as a murderer, a thief, an evildoer, **or as a busybody in other people's
matters**.*

The Greek is **allotriepiskopos** — one who oversees what belongs to others.

He lists three crimes and then adds meddling, in the same sentence.

## What the letter is written into

*Beloved, I beg you as sojourners and pilgrims.* The recipients are scattered across five provinces
of Asia Minor, and the pressure on them is social before it is legal.

## Where it ends

*But **may the God of all grace**, who called us to His eternal glory by Christ Jesus, **after you have
suffered a while, perfect, establish, strengthen, and settle you**.* See the note on 1 Peter 5:10.

Four verbs, and the last one — **themeliōsei** — means to lay on a foundation.

## The three in the furnace

*"I see **four men, loose, walking about in the middle of the fire**, and they are not harmed."* See
the note on Daniel 3:24–25.

They went in bound. What burned first was the rope.

> **The forbidden reaction.** Not *do not flinch* — **mē xenizesthe**, do not treat it as something
> foreign. The command is about the conclusion drawn, not the pain felt.
