The church's first administrative decision, and the choice of men is the interesting part.

## The problem

*Now in those days… there arose **a complaint against the Hebrews by the Hellenists, because
their widows were neglected in the daily distribution**.*

Greek-speaking Jewish believers versus Aramaic-speaking ones. A cultural fault line, and food
was going to one side.

The first recorded internal dispute in the church is about fairness to widows.

## The apostles' answer

*"It is not desirable that we should leave the word of God and serve tables… **seek out from
among you seven men of good reputation, full of the Holy Spirit and wisdom**, whom we may
appoint over this business."*

*"But we will give ourselves continually to prayer and to the ministry of the word."*

They do not take it on and they do not dismiss it. They delegate it with a job specification.

## Who chooses

*This suggestion **pleased the whole gathering, and they chose**.*

The congregation picks the men. The apostles set the criteria and appoint them afterwards.

## The seven names

*Stephen, Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolas.*

Every one is Greek.

The complaint came from the Greek-speaking side, and the entire committee is drawn from it.
Nobody split the seats.

*And **Nicolas, a convert from Antioch**.*

A proselyte — a Gentile who had converted to Judaism first. He is on the list too.

## The first two

*Stephen, a man **full of faith and of the Holy Spirit**.*

Within one chapter he is doing signs and arguing in the synagogue, and by chapter 7 he is dead.
See the note on Acts 6:8–11.

*Philip.*

By chapter 8 he is in Samaria, and then on the Gaza road with an Ethiopian official and a
scroll. See the note on Acts 8:26–29.

Two men appointed to organise a food distribution, and both end up as the two most significant
evangelists in the early chapters.

## The commissioning

*They **presented these men to the apostles**, who **prayed and laid their hands on them**.*

Prayer and hands, the same pattern as Antioch later. See the note on
Acts 13:1–3.

## The result

*And **the word of God kept spreading**.*

The Greek is **euxanen** — grew.

Luke's marker sentence, and he uses it after trouble every time. See the note on
Acts 12:24–25.

Here the trouble was a complaint about lunch, and the growth clause follows the fix.

## The number

*The number of disciples in Jerusalem **multiplied greatly**.*

## The last clause

*And **a large company of the priests became obedient to the faith**.*

The Greek is **polus ochlos ton hiereon**.

Priests. The men whose livelihood was the temple system, and whose colleagues had condemned
Jesus.

Luke drops it in as the third item on a list and does not elaborate.

## What the passage models

A real grievance, named honestly. A structural fix rather than a rebuke. The complaining party
given the whole committee. And the leadership protecting what only they could do.

> **How the seats were allocated.** All seven to the side that raised the complaint — and two of
> them turn out to be Stephen and Philip.
