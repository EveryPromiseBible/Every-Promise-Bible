Four verses of setting, and every detail in them is doing something.

## The crossing

*After this Jesus **crossed to the far side of the Sea of Galilee**, which is also called
**the Sea of Tiberias**.*

John gives both names. **Tiberias** was the Roman name, after the emperor — Herod Antipas
had built a city on the shore and named it for him.

John writes for readers who would know the Roman name and not the Jewish one.

## The crowd

*A large crowd **kept following Him**, because they had **seen the signs He was doing for the
sick**.*

The Greek is imperfect — they were following, continuously.

John states the reason plainly and without flattery: the signs.

By the end of the chapter almost all of them are gone.

## The mountain

*Jesus **went up on the mountainside and sat down there with His disciples**.*

Sitting is the posture of a teacher.

## The date

*The Passover, the festival of the Jews, was near.*

John marks three Passovers in his Gospel, and it is how the ministry is usually dated to
about three years.

This one matters for what follows. Passover is the feast of the lamb and of unleavened
bread, and the chapter is about to be entirely about bread.

## Why John gives the date

The discourse that follows runs on Passover material: manna in the wilderness, bread from
heaven, and *unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no
life in you*.

The feast is in the background of every line.

## What happens next

Five thousand men, besides women and children. Five barley loaves and two small fish from
a boy in the crowd.

*Then Jesus took the loaves, and **when He had given thanks** He distributed them.*

Twelve baskets of fragments left over — one for each disciple who had said it could not be
done.

It is the only miracle in all four Gospels.

## What the crowd tried to do

*Therefore when Jesus perceived that they were about to **come and take Him by force to make
Him king**, He departed again to the mountain by Himself alone.*

## What He does that night

Walks out to the disciples on the water, three or four miles from shore, in a storm. See
the note on John 6:16–21.

## What He says the next day

*"Most assuredly, I say to you, **you seek Me, not because you saw the signs, but because
you ate of the loaves and were filled**."*

He tells the crowd why they came, and it is not the reason John gave in verse 2.

## Where the chapter ends

*"This is a hard teaching. Who can listen to it?"* — and *from that time many of His
disciples went back and walked with Him no more*. See the note on John 6:60–65.

The chapter opens with a large crowd following and ends with twelve men and a question:
*do you also want to go away?*

> **What the four verses set up.** A lake with two names, a crowd there for the healings, a
> teacher sitting on a hill, and Passover coming — and then a boy's lunch feeds five
> thousand and a discourse about bread empties the room.
