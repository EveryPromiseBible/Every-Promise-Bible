Aaron's death is unlike any other in the Old Testament. He is told where, told
when, and walks up the mountain to it in front of the whole nation.

## The order of events

*Take off Aaron's priestly garments and put them on Eleazar his son… Aaron will
be gathered to his people, and he will die there.*

The transfer comes **before** the death. Moses strips his brother of the robes on
the mountain top, dresses the son in them, and only then does Aaron die.

The priesthood is not interrupted for a moment. There is never a point at which
Israel has no high priest.

## Why it was done in public

*They went up the mountain in the sight of the whole community.* Three men climb;
two come down, one of them wearing the vestments everybody recognised.

Nothing had to be explained afterwards. The nation watched the office change
hands.

## What it exposes

A priest dies and the robes go to somebody else, over and over, generation after
generation. The system depends on replacement because the men wearing it keep
running out.

> **The comment the New Testament makes.** Hebrews puts its finger on exactly
> this: there had to be many priests, *because death kept stopping them from
> staying in office* — but He continues forever, so His priesthood does not pass
> to anyone else. The writer had scenes like this mountain in mind. See the note
> on Hebrews 2:14–16, where the same argument runs the other way: He took a body
> in order to die.
