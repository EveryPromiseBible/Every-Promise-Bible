Paul is being asked for credentials, and he says the people asking are the credentials.

## The situation

Somebody had arrived in Corinth with letters of recommendation, and had used them against him.

Letters of introduction were the normal machinery of the ancient world. Paul writes one himself
for Phoebe, and one for Onesimus.

He is not against the practice. He is against needing one here.

## The question

*Are we **starting to recommend ourselves all over again**?*

The Greek is **sunistanein** — to commend, to introduce.

It is a weary question. He has been defending himself since chapter 1 and is tired of it.

## The second

*Or do we need, **as some people do**, letters of recommendation to you, **or from you**?*

The last three words are the sharp end.

Some of the newcomers had apparently asked Corinth for a reference. Corinth — the congregation
Paul founded.

## The answer

*You are **our letter, written on our hearts**, there for anyone to know and read.*

The Greek is **anaginoskomene hupo panton anthropon** — read by all men.

Their existence is the document.

He makes the same point in the first letter: *if I am not an apostle to others, yet doubtless I
am to you. For **you are the seal of my apostleship** in the Lord*.

## The correction

*It is plain that **you are a letter from Christ, delivered by us**.*

He shifts the authorship mid-sentence.

Not his letter. Christ's, and Paul is the courier.

The Greek is **diakonetheisa** — served, ministered, delivered by us.

## The ink

*Written **not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God**.*

## The tablets

*And **not on tablets of stone but on tablets that are human hearts**.*

The Greek is **plaxin lithinais** — stone tablets.

That is Sinai, and everybody reading knew it.

And **plaxin kardiais sarkinais** — tablets that are hearts of flesh.

## Where the phrase comes from

*I will take out of you the heart of stone, and I will give you **a heart of flesh**.* See the
note on Ezekiel 36:26.

And Jeremiah: *I will put My law in their minds, and write it on their hearts*.

Paul is quoting both prophets in one clause, and he does not stop to say so.

## What the chapter goes on to argue

*Our sufficiency is from God, who also made us sufficient as ministers of the new covenant, not
of the letter but of the Spirit; for **the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life**.* See the
note on 2 Corinthians 3:4–6.

And then the two ministries compared — the one engraved on stones, which came with glory that
faded, against the one that remains. See the note on
2 Corinthians 3:7–11.

And Moses' veil, and the unveiling. See the note on 2 Corinthians 3:16–18.

The whole chapter unfolds from the stone-and-flesh contrast in verse 3.

## What Corinth was like

Factions, lawsuits, drunkenness at the table, a scandal in the membership.

That is the letter Paul is holding up as his reference.

He does not pretend otherwise, and he still says it.

> **Whose letter it is.** He calls them his in one clause and corrects himself in the next —
> *a letter from Christ, delivered by us*. He is the courier, not the author.
