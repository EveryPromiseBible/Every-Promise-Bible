Paul quotes the law against itself and then says what was done about it.

## Who is under a curse

*Everyone who **rests on the works of the law** is under a curse.*

Not everyone who breaks it. Everyone who rests on it — who stands on their own
keeping of it as their position before God.

## Why

*"**Cursed is everyone who does not continue in ALL the things** written in the
book of the law, to do them."*

Deuteronomy 27, the words Israel shouted *Amen* to from Mount Ebal.

The killing word is *all*. Not most, not the important ones, not on balance.
Everything, continuously, without a gap.

James puts the same thing another way: *whoever keeps the whole law and yet
stumbles in one point is guilty of all.*

## The two systems, side by side

Paul sets the operating principles next to each other:

- *the one who is right with God **will live by faith*** — Habakkuk
- *the law is not of faith; its word is rather, **the person who does these things
  will live by them*** — Leviticus 18

One says believe. The other says do. They are not two routes to the same place;
they run on incompatible currency.

## What Christ did with the curse

*Christ has **bought us out from under the curse** of the law **by becoming a curse
for us**.*

The Greek for *bought us out* is **exagorazo** — to purchase out of the market, the
word used of buying a slave in order to free him.

And He did not lift the curse off. He took the position: *becoming a curse for
us.*

## The proof text

*"**Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree.**"*

Deuteronomy 21. The body of an executed criminal hung on a tree was under God's
curse and had to be taken down before nightfall so the land would not be defiled.

Paul reaches for the one verse in the law that puts a curse on a hanged man, and
says that is the transaction.

## What it was for

Two purpose clauses, and both are given:

*So that **the blessing of Abraham** might come to the nations in Christ Jesus, and
so that through faith we might **receive the promise of the Spirit**.*

The curse was taken so the blessing could go somewhere it had never been —
*to the nations.*

And the blessing of Abraham is the one credited in Genesis 15:6, before there was
any law to keep. See the note on Genesis 15:6.

> **What Paul asks them at the top of the chapter.** *Did you receive the Spirit by
> the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?* He does not argue the
> theology first. He asks them to remember how it started.
