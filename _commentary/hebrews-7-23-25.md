---
key: Hebrews 7:23–25
title: "The priesthood that never changes hands"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The argument is almost comically simple, and it turns entirely on the fact that one Priest did not
stay dead.

## Why there had to be so many

*There had to be a great many of those priests, because **death kept stopping them from staying in
office**.*

Aaron's line ran for roughly fourteen centuries, and every man in it was interrupted the same way.
The high priesthood of Israel was a succession of funerals with a robe passed on afterwards.

Whatever comfort a worshipper took from his priest, he took it knowing the man would be replaced.

## A priesthood that cannot be handed on

*But He goes on forever, so **His priesthood never passes to anyone else**.*

**Aparabatos** — untransferable, not passing to a successor. It is a legal term, and it appears once
in the Bible.

There is no next one. Nobody will ever be appointed to the position Jesus holds, because the only
thing that has ever vacated it is death, and He has been through that already and come back.

## Completely, and for good

*Which is why He is able to save, **completely and for good**, everyone who comes to God through
Him.*

**Eis to panteles** — to the uttermost. The phrase carries both dimensions at once: all the way
through, and all the way on. Nothing about the saving is partial, and nothing about it expires.

And the guarantee named is not the strength of the one coming. It is the deathlessness of the One
being come to. Your salvation is as secure as His pulse.

## What He is doing now

*He is always alive, and **He lives to speak to God on their behalf**.*

**Entynchanein** — to approach someone on another's behalf, to petition.

Paul puts it beside the cross and refuses to separate them: *it is Christ who died, and furthermore
is also risen, who is even at the right hand of God, **who also makes intercession for us***. The
work that saved you is finished; the Person who did it has not gone quiet.

> **Nobody is coming to replace Him.** Every priest you could have had before this one was a man
> with a funeral in his future, and the whole system was built around that fact. This one is *able
> to save to the uttermost*, and the reason given is not your grip, your record or your consistency
> — it is that He always lives.
