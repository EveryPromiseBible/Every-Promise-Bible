The sharpest thing He ever said to a man, and Mark records where He was looking when He said it.

## What just happened

*"But who do you say that I am?" Peter answered and said to Him, "**You are the Christ**."*

The high point of the first half of Mark's Gospel, and it is Peter who says it.

Six days from here is the transfiguration, and Peter is on that mountain too.

## The turn

*Then **He began to teach them plainly** that the Son of Man had to suffer a great deal.*

The Greek is **erxato didaskein** — He began to teach. Mark marks it as a new phase.

Up to this point He has spoken in parables and told people to say nothing. From the confession
onward, He teaches this.

## The four things

*Suffer a great deal, be **rejected by the elders and the chief priests and the teachers of the
law**, be **killed**, and **after three days rise again**.*

The three groups named are the Sanhedrin — the whole council, itemised.

The resurrection is in the sentence. It is always in the sentence, in all three predictions, and
nobody in the story ever reacts to that half.

## The manner

*He said it **openly, holding nothing back**.*

The Greek is **parresia** — plainly, with freedom of speech, in public.

Mark uses the word once in his Gospel, here.

## Peter

*And Peter **took Him aside** and began to **argue Him out of it**.*

The Greek is **proslabomenos** — taking Him to himself, taking Him by the arm.

**Epitiman** — to rebuke. The same verb Mark uses of Jesus silencing demons and stilling the
storm.

Peter rebukes Him. Matthew records the words: *far be it from You, Lord; this shall not happen to
You!*

## Why he does it

He has just called Him the Christ, and the Christ was not supposed to be executed.

Peter's objection follows directly from his confession. He is right about the title and wrong
about the job.

## The look

*But Jesus **turned around, looked at His disciples**, and spoke sharply to Peter.*

The Greek is **epistrapheis kai idon tous mathetas autou**.

Peter had taken Him aside. Jesus turns back to the group before answering.

Mark alone records the look. Matthew has the rebuke without it.

## What the look does

The correction is spoken to Peter, in front of the others, because the others are thinking it
too.

Peter said out loud what eleven men were assuming.

## The words

*"**Get behind Me, Satan**."*

The Greek is **hupage opiso mou, Satana**.

The same word He used in the wilderness — *away with you, Satan* — when the offer was the
kingdoms of the world without a cross. See the note on Matthew 4:8–11.

The same offer, from a friend.

## The diagnosis

*"**Your mind is not on the things of God at all. It is on the things of men**."*

The Greek is **ou phroneis** — you are not minding, not setting your thinking on.

Not *you are evil*. Your mind is set on the human side of the question — which is where anybody's
would be.

## What follows immediately

*"**Whoever desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow
Me**."* See the note on Mark 8:34–38.

Peter tried to keep Him off the cross and is told about carrying one.

## Where Peter ends up

*"I do not know this Man of whom you speak."* And the rooster, and the look. See the note on
Luke 22:54–62.

*And the Lord turned and looked at Peter.* The same verb, the same evangelists' interest in where
He was looking.

## And afterwards

*"**But go, tell His disciples — and Peter** — that He is going before you into Galilee."*

Mark's angel names him separately.

> **What Mark adds.** The turn. Peter took Him aside to say it privately; He turned back to the
> group before He answered, because the correction was not only Peter's.
