A death sentence is reversed, and the sign given is a shadow moving the wrong way.

## What has just happened

Isaiah had come with a message that left no room: *set your house in order, for you shall die and
not live*.

Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and wept bitterly. See the note on
Isaiah 38:1–3.

## The turnaround

*Then **the word of the LORD came to Isaiah**: "Go and tell Hezekiah…"*

The parallel account says Isaiah had not yet reached the middle court before the word came.

The prophet who delivered the sentence is sent back with the reversal before he is out of the
building.

## How God identifies Himself

*"This is what the LORD, **the God of your father David**, says."*

Not *the God of Israel*. The God of his father David — which is a covenant address, aimed at a
man in the line the promise ran through.

## The two things heard

*"**I have heard your prayer and seen your tears**."*

The Hebrew is **shamati… ra'iti** — I have heard, I have seen.

Both are counted. The tears are named separately from the prayer, as if they were a second thing
said.

*You number my wanderings; **put my tears into Your bottle**; are they not in Your book?* See the
note on Psalms 56:8–11.

## The grant

*"**I am going to add fifteen years to your life**."*

The Hebrew is **yosef** — I will add.

A specific number, given in advance. Hezekiah is told exactly how long he has.

## The second promise

*"And **I will rescue you and this city** from the hand of the king of Assyria. **I will defend
this city**."*

Sennacherib is at the gates. The personal healing and the national deliverance come in one
sentence, and the king's life and the city's are tied together.

## The sign

*"And this is the sign to you from the LORD… **Look, I will make the shadow that has gone down on
the stairway of Ahaz go back ten steps**."*

The Hebrew is **ma'alot Achaz** — the steps of Ahaz. A stairway that told the time by the
shadow's descent.

Built by his father, the king who *sacrificed his own son in the fire* and shut the doors of the
temple.

## Why a shadow

Of every possible sign, the one given is time running backwards on his father's instrument.

The man has just been given fifteen years. The sign is the sun conceding ten steps.

## What happens

*So **the sunlight went back the ten steps it had descended**.*

The account in Kings adds that Hezekiah was offered the choice — forward ten or back ten — and
chose back, because *it is an easy thing for the shadow to go down ten degrees*.

He asks for the harder one.

## What he wrote afterwards

*The writing of Hezekiah… when he had been sick and had recovered:*

*I said, "In the prime of my life I shall go to the gates of Sheol"… **O LORD, by these things
men live**… **You have cast all my sins behind Your back***. See the note on
Isaiah 38:15–20.

## What the fifteen years contained

*Then Berodach-Baladan sent letters and a present to Hezekiah, for he heard that he had been
sick.*

And Hezekiah showed the envoys everything in the treasure house, and Isaiah came and told him it
would all be carried to Babylon.

*Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king* — born inside the fifteen, and the worst king
Judah had.

The narrative gives the extension and then records what filled it, without softening either.

## The other time the sun did something

Of every possible sign, the one given is the sun conceding ten steps on a dying man's behalf.

*Now it was about the sixth hour, and **there was darkness over all the earth until the ninth
hour. Then the sun was darkened**, and the veil of the temple was torn in two.*

**Luke** records the second occasion. A king is given fifteen years and the shadow goes
backwards; a King is put to death and the light goes out at noon.

## Heard and seen

*"I have **heard your prayer** and **seen your tears**."*

*"And **God will wipe away every tear** from their eyes; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow,
nor crying."*

**Revelation** 21 finishes what Isaiah 38 starts. The tears counted separately in the message to
Hezekiah are, at the end, wiped off individually.

## The God of your father David

Not *the God of Israel*. A covenant address, to a man in the line the promise ran through.

*"He will be great, and will be called the Son of the Highest; and the Lord God will give Him
**the throne of His father David**."*

**Luke** uses the same phrase, of somebody else, and adds *of His kingdom there will be no end* —
which is the one thing nobody could say about Hezekiah's fifteen years.

> **The sign.** Time running backwards on his father's stairway. A man given fifteen years, and
> the shadow conceding ten steps to say so.
