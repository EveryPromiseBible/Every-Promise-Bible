Two verses in the middle of an attack on Judah's shepherds, and they contain a name.

## Where it sits

The chapter opens *woe to the shepherds who destroy and scatter the sheep of My pasture* — the
kings and priests who had run the country into the ground.

*"I will **set up shepherds over them who will feed them**, and they shall fear no more."*

And then the Branch.

*"**I am the good shepherd.** The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep… I know My sheep,
and am known by My own."*

**John** 10 is the answer to Jeremiah 23, and **Jesus** makes the contrast the chapter makes — the
hireling who flees, against the one who does not.

## The Branch

*"I will raise up for David **a righteous Branch**."*

The Hebrew is **tsemach tsaddiq** — a sprout, a shoot, something that comes up out of the ground.

It is a title, used by four prophets:

- *Behold, I will bring forth My Servant **the BRANCH**.* — Zechariah 3:8
- *Behold, the Man whose name is **the BRANCH**!* — Zechariah 6:12
- *In that day **the Branch of the LORD** shall be beautiful and glorious.* — Isaiah 4:2
- and here, *a righteous Branch* raised up **for David** — a king.

A King, a Servant, a Man, and the Branch of the LORD. Readers have long noticed that the four
gospels present Him in those four ways, in that order.

## What he does

*"He will **reign as king**, and he will **act wisely**, and he will do **justice and righteousness
in the land**."*

The Hebrew for *act wisely* is **hiskil** — to act with insight, prudently, so that it succeeds. It
is the word used of the servant in Isaiah 52:13: *behold, My Servant shall deal prudently.*

## The name

*"And this is the name by which he will be called: **The LORD Our Righteousness**."*

**YHWH Tsidkenu.**

The divine name and a possessive pronoun, joined by a noun. It is not a description of his
character — it is a statement about whose righteousness it is and who has it.

Not *the LORD is righteous*. **Our** righteousness.

## Who says the same thing

*But of Him you are in Christ Jesus, who became for us wisdom from God — and **righteousness** and
sanctification and redemption.*

**Paul**'s verb is **egenethe** — He became it. Not supplied it.

*For He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, **that we might become the righteousness of God
in Him**.*

*And **be found in Him, not having my own righteousness**, which is from the law, but that which
is through faith in Christ — **the righteousness which is from God by faith**.*

**Philippians** 3, written by a man who had just itemised the finest religious record available in
his generation and called it rubbish.

## Why the name is the whole point

Jeremiah's contemporaries had a king called Zedekiah — **Tsidqiyahu**, *the LORD is my
righteousness*. He is the one on the throne while the city falls, who breaks the covenant about
the slaves, and who watches his sons killed before he is blinded. See the note on
Jeremiah 34:17–20.

The prophet announces a coming king whose name is a rearrangement of the reigning king's, and puts
**our** in the middle of it.

## And the same promise, transferred

*"In those days Judah will be saved and Jerusalem will dwell safely. And **this is the name by
which she will be called: THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS**."*

Jeremiah 33:16, ten chapters on, and the name has moved from Him to the city. See the note on
Jeremiah 33:6–9.

What is His becomes hers, and the prophet does it by repeating the sentence with the pronoun
changed.

> **The name.** *YHWH Tsidkenu* — the LORD our righteousness. Not that He is righteous, which
> nobody doubted. That His righteousness has an owner, and the pronoun is plural, and it is not
> His.
