The only miracle in all four Gospels, and the multiplication is never described.

## What preceded it

*When the apostles came back, **they told Him everything they had done**.*

They have just returned from being sent out two by two with power over demons and to heal.

*He took them away with Him, **quietly**, to a lonely stretch of country near the town called
Bethsaida.*

He is trying to get them a rest. Mark records the reason: *there were many coming and going, and
they did not even have time to eat*.

## The interruption

*But the crowds found out where He had gone and **came after Him**.*

The rest is cancelled before it starts.

## What He does with the interruption

*He **welcomed them**, spoke to them about the kingdom of God, and **healed all who needed
healing**.*

The Greek is **apodexamenos** — received them gladly.

Not tolerated. Welcomed.

## The disciples' proposal

*"**Send the crowd off** so they can go to the villages and farms around here and find food and
somewhere to sleep. **We are out in the middle of nowhere**."*

It is a sensible suggestion. The day is going, the place is empty, and the numbers are large.

Their solution is dispersal.

## The reply

*"**You give them something to eat**."*

The Greek puts **humeis** — *you* — in the emphatic position, which Greek only does deliberately.

Men who have just come back from a successful mission are handed something they cannot do.

## The inventory

*"**We have five loaves and two fish, and that is all**," they said, "unless You want us to go and
buy food for this whole crowd."*

John names the source: *there is a boy here with five barley loaves and two small fish*. See the
note on John 6:5–9.

Barley was the cheap grain. The whole supply is one child's packed lunch.

## The number

*There were about **five thousand men** there.*

**Andres** — males. Matthew adds *besides women and children*.

## The seating

*"Have them **sit down in groups of about fifty**."*

Which turns a crowd into a countable arrangement, and means the distribution can be tracked.

Mark says they sat *in ranks, by hundreds and by fifties*, on green grass.

## The four verbs

*He **took** the five loaves and the two fish, **looked up to heaven**, **gave thanks** and **broke**
them.*

Took, blessed, broke, gave — the same four verbs Luke uses at the last supper, and again at
Emmaus, where *He took bread, blessed and broke it, and gave it to them* and their eyes were
opened.

## Where the multiplication happens

*And **kept handing the pieces to the disciples** to set in front of the people.*

The Greek imperfect: **edidou** — he was giving, continuously.

The text never says the bread multiplied. It says He kept handing it over.

Whatever happened, happened between His hands and theirs, and the disciples walked back to Him
for more, again and again, all evening.

## The result

*Everyone ate, and **everyone had enough** — and when they gathered up what was left over, there
were **twelve baskets full**.*

The Greek is **echortasthesan** — were filled, satisfied. The word used of feeding animals to
fullness.

Twelve baskets, and twelve men who had said *that is all*.

## What He asks straight afterwards

*"**Who do the crowds say that I am?**"… "But who do you say that I am?"*

Peter: *the Christ of God*. See the note on Luke 9:18–20.

The question comes immediately after the meal.

> **The tense.** **Edidou** — he kept on giving. The text never describes bread multiplying; it
> describes a man handing pieces over, and twelve men coming back for more all evening.
