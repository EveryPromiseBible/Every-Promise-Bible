One verse, and it closes forty days on a mountain.

## The moment

*And when the LORD **finished speaking** with Moses on Mount Sinai, He gave him **the
two tablets of the covenant**.*

Everything from chapter 20 to here — the law, the ordinances, the tabernacle
pattern, the priesthood, the Sabbath — has been spoken. This verse ends it.

## Stone

*Tablets of **stone**.*

Not scroll, not clay. The hardest available writing surface, and the one you cannot
amend.

## The finger

*Written by **the very finger of God**.*

The Hebrew is *written with the finger of God* — the same phrase the magicians of
Egypt used when they gave up: *this is the finger of God.*

Luke uses it too: *if I cast out demons **with the finger of God**, surely the kingdom
of God has come upon you* — where Matthew's parallel has *by the Spirit of God*.

## Two tablets

The usual picture is a division of subject — God on one, neighbour on the other.
Ancient treaty practice suggests something else: two copies of the same document, one
for each party, both kept in the shrine.

Both tablets went into the ark. Neither party took theirs home.

## What is happening at the foot of the mountain

The next verse: *when the people saw that Moses delayed coming down… they said, "Make
us gods that shall go before us."*

While the finger of God is writing, Aaron is taking the earrings.

## What happens to them

Moses comes down, sees the calf, and *cast the tablets out of his hands and broke them
at the foot of the mountain.*

The document written by God's own finger did not survive the walk down. See the note
on Exodus 32:15–20.

## The second set

*Cut two tablets of stone like the first ones, **and I will write on them** the words
that were on the first tablets which you broke.*

This time Moses does the cutting and God does the writing. And these are the ones that
went into the ark and lasted.

> **Where the writing moves to.** *I will put My laws inside their hearts, and I will
> write them on their minds.* Same author, same verb, different surface — and a
> surface that does not shatter on the way down a hill. See the note on
> Hebrews 10:15–17.
