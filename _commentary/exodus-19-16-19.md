The day the law is given, described entirely by sound and smoke.

## The day

*On the morning of **the third day**.*

The day named in the preparation. Wash your clothes, be ready by the third day, and do not
touch the mountain. See the note on Exodus 19:10–13.

Three days of getting ready, and this is what they were getting ready for.

## What arrives

*Thunder cracked and lightning flashed, **a thick cloud settled over the mountain**, and a
**trumpet blast sounded so loud that everyone in the camp trembled**.*

The Hebrew is **qolot** — voices, and it is the ordinary word for thunder.

The trumpet is not blown by anybody. No priest is mentioned, and the shofar was not yet part of
the worship — Aaron has not been consecrated.

The sound has no visible source.

## The people

*Everyone in the camp trembled.*

The Hebrew is **vayyecherad** — they shook.

Two million people, and the text says the whole camp.

## What Moses does

*Then Moses **led the people out of the camp to meet with God**, and they **stood at the foot
of the mountain**.*

He walks them toward it. That is the remarkable line: the trembling comes first and then he
brings them out.

*At the foot* — the Hebrew is *at the lowest part*. Right up to the boundary and no further.

## The smoke

*Mount Sinai was **wrapped in smoke, because the LORD had come down on it in fire**.*

The reason is given. Not weather — a descent.

*The smoke rose **like smoke from a furnace**.*

The Hebrew is **kivshan** — a kiln, a smelting furnace.

The same word Genesis uses of Sodom the morning after: *and he looked toward Sodom and
Gomorrah… and saw the smoke of the land ascend like the smoke of a furnace*.

## The shaking

*And **the whole mountain shook violently**.*

Some manuscripts have *the people trembled greatly*; the Hebrew as it stands has the mountain.

## The escalation

*As the trumpet grew **louder and louder**.*

The Hebrew is *going and growing very strong*.

Ordinary trumpets fade. This one increases for the duration.

## The exchange

*Moses spoke, and **God answered him in the thunder**.*

The Hebrew is **bekol** — with a voice, in thunder. The same word doing both jobs.

## What is said next

The ten commandments, and then the people's reaction:

*Now all the people witnessed the thunderings, the lightning flashes, the sound of the trumpet,
and the mountain smoking; and when the people saw it, **they trembled and stood afar off**.*

*Then they said to Moses, "**You speak with us, and we will hear; but let not God speak with
us, lest we die**."*

They had said, three days earlier, *all that the LORD has spoken we will do*.

## What Moses said afterwards

*"I am exceedingly afraid and trembling."*

Not in Exodus — Hebrews supplies it, and it is not recorded anywhere in the Old Testament.

## What Hebrews does with the scene

*For you have **not come to the mountain that may be touched** and that burned with fire, and to
blackness and darkness and tempest, and the sound of a trumpet and the voice of words…*

*But **you have come to Mount Zion**, and to the city of the living God… to Jesus the Mediator
of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling that speaks better things than that of
Abel.* See the note on Hebrews 12:18–24.

Every feature of this paragraph appears in that one, listed as what the reader has not come
to.

## What the sound was for

Nothing in the account is decorative. The fire, the smoke, the shaking and the trumpet all say
the same thing as the fence: do not come up.

> **The one detail that does not fade.** The trumpet gets louder for as long as it sounds — and
> nobody in the account is blowing it.
