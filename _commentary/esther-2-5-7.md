Two people are introduced, and everything the book needs is in their description.

## The genealogy

*Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of **Kish**, a **Benjamite**.*

Kish, a Benjamite, is the name of Saul's father. The writer is pointing at
something.

Saul was the Benjamite king commanded to destroy Amalek, and he did not. Haman is
introduced two chapters later as *the Agagite* — Agag being the Amalekite king
Saul spared.

A descendant of Kish and a descendant of Agag, in the same court, four hundred
years later. The unfinished business of 1 Samuel 15 is the plot of this book.
See the note on Deuteronomy 25:17–19.

## Carried away

*His family had been carried away from Jerusalem among the captives.*

Mordecai is in Susa because his people lost their land. He is not there by
ambition. He is there because of the exile, in the middle of the season Hosea
called *not My people*.

## Two names

*Mordecai was raising **Hadassah, that is, Esther**.*

**Hadassah** is Hebrew — the myrtle. **Esther** is the Persian name she is known
by in the palace, and it is the name on the book.

The myrtle is a fragrant shrub that gives off its scent most strongly when it is
crushed, and Isaiah 55 puts it in a promise: *instead of the brier shall come up
the myrtle tree.*

Her Hebrew name is hidden under a foreign one, in a book where God's name is
hidden under an ordinary story.

## "She had neither father nor mother"

Said twice in three lines. The writer wants it registered.

The girl who will stand between a nation and its extermination is an orphan in a
foreign city, with no standing, no family, and no claim on anybody.

*Mordecai had taken her as his own daughter.* The Hebrew is literally *took her
for a daughter*.

> **What is already in place.** Before Haman is promoted, before the decree is
> written, before anyone knows there is a danger — a Benjamite of Kish's line is in
> the fortress and the girl in his house is beautiful. The pieces are set out
> before the game starts.
