Four men dig through a roof, and the sentence that follows is one of the most
remarkable in the Gospels.

## The room

*Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there, men who had come in from
every village of Galilee and from Judea and from Jerusalem.*

Luke is describing an inspection. Religious authorities have travelled from
three regions to watch this man, and they are sitting down — the posture of
people who intend to stay.

And in the same sentence: *the power of the Lord was present with Him to heal.*

## The roof

Houses had flat roofs of beams, brushwood and packed mud, reached by an outside
stair. Luke, writing for a Greek audience, says *tiles* — describing it in terms
his readers would picture.

Either way, four men take somebody's roof apart with a room full of dignitaries
underneath, and lower a man on a mat into the gap.

## "Their faith"

*And when Jesus saw **their** faith.*

Plural. Luke does not say the paralysed man believed anything. Whatever he
thought, the faith Jesus responds to is the faith of the men on the roof.

That is worth sitting with. Somebody else's conviction got him in front of Jesus,
and it is credited.

## What He says first

*Friend, your sins are forgiven.*

Not what anybody in the room came for. They had dug through a ceiling for legs,
and He addresses something nobody mentioned.

The word is literally *man*, but warm — the Illumination's *friend* catches the
tone.

> **What follows.** The scribes reason that only God can forgive sins, and Jesus
> agrees with the premise: *so that you may know the Son of Man has authority on
> earth to forgive sins* — He said to the paralysed man, get up. The healing is
> offered as evidence for the forgiveness, which tells you which of the two He
> considered the greater work.
