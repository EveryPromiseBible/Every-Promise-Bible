---
key: 2 Samuel 7:12–17
title: God turns it around
updated: 2026-08-07
---

David offers to build a house. He is told he will be given one.

## What he had proposed

*"See now, I dwell in a house of cedar, but **the ark of God dwells inside tent curtains**."*

Nathan approves it on the spot. That night the word comes to him, and it is different. See the note
on 2 Samuel 7:1–3.

## The reversal

*"Would you build a house for Me to dwell in?… **Also the LORD tells you that He will make you a
house**."*

The Hebrew is **bayit** — house, in both senses. A building, and a dynasty.

David means a temple. The answer uses the same word to mean a family line.

## The timing

*"When your days are done and you **lie down with your ancestors**."*

The promise's first clause presumes his death. It is not a promise for his lifetime.

## The offspring

*"I will raise up **your own offspring after you, one who comes from your own body**."*

The Hebrew is **zar'aka** — your seed.

## The immediate reference

*"**He is the one who will build a house for my Name**."*

Solomon, who does.

## The clause that outruns him

*"And I will establish the throne of his kingdom **forever**."*

The Hebrew is **ad-olam**.

Solomon's kingdom did not last. The word is used anyway, three times in the passage.

## The relationship

*"**I will be a Father to him, and he will be a son to me**."*

The Hebrew is **ani ehyeh-lo le'av vehu yihyeh-li leven**.

## Where that sentence goes

*For to which of the angels did He ever say… "**I will be to Him a Father, and He shall be to Me a
Son**"?* See the note on Hebrews 1:4–5.

The letter quotes this verse to establish who Christ is.

## The discipline clause

*"**When he does wrong, I will correct him with the rod**, the way any father disciplines a son."*

The Hebrew is **beshevet anashim** — with the rod of men.

The promise includes provision for the son's failure. It is written into the covenant, not treated
as a breach of it.

## The clause that changes everything

*"**But my faithful love will never leave him**, the way I stripped it from Saul."*

The Hebrew is **vechasdi lo-yasur mimmennu** — My **chesed** shall not depart from him.

## What Saul is doing in the sentence

A worked example of what will not happen.

Saul was measured and found wanting and the kingdom was torn away. The covenant here is explicitly
of a different kind.

## The security

*"Your house and your kingdom will **stand secure before me forever**; your throne will be established
for all time."*

Three words for permanence in one line: **ne'eman**, faithful, established; **ad-olam**; **nakon**,
firm.

## The messenger

*Nathan told David **every one of these words, all that he had seen**.*

Including the correction of his own advice from the day before.

## What David does with it

*Then King David went in and **sat before the LORD**, and he said: "Who am I, O Lord GOD?"* See the
note on 2 Samuel 7:18–21.

He sits. Not a posture of prayer; the posture of someone who has been given something and has to
take it in.

## Where the promise is invoked

*"The LORD God will give Him **the throne of His father David**. And He will reign over the house of
Jacob forever, and of His kingdom there will be no end."* See the note on Luke 1:31–33.

Gabriel quotes it to Mary.

And Peter at Pentecost: *being a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him that
of the fruit of his body… He would raise up the Christ*. See the note on Acts 2:29–32.

> **The switch.** He offers to build God a house and is told God will build him one. The same
> Hebrew word, meaning a building in the offer and a dynasty in the answer.
