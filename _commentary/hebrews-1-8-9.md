The fourth of seven quotations, and the Father addresses the Son as God.

## The frame

*But **to the Son He says**.*

The Greek is **pros de ton huion**.

Each quotation in the chapter is introduced by asking which angel was ever addressed this way. This
one is the strongest.

## The address

*"**God, Your throne stands forever and ever**."*

The Greek is **ho thronos sou ho Theos eis ton aiona tou aionos**.

**Ho Theos** — the vocative. The Father calls the Son God.

## Where it comes from

Psalm 45, a royal wedding song.

*My heart is overflowing with a good theme; I recite my composition concerning the King.*

A court poem for a royal marriage, addressed to the king — and Hebrews reads it as addressed to
the Son.

## The Hebrew problem

**Kisacha Elohim olam va'ed** — Your throne, O God, is forever and ever.

The Hebrew can also be read *your throne is God, forever*, or *God is your throne*. Translators
have divided since antiquity.

Hebrews follows the Septuagint's vocative reading and builds an argument on it.

## The sceptre

*"And **the scepter You rule by is a scepter of what is right**."*

The Greek is **rhabdos euthutetos** — a rod of uprightness.

The instrument of rule is described by its moral character rather than its power.

## The two verbs

*"You **loved what is right** and **hated what is lawless**."*

The Greek is **egapesas dikaiosunen kai emisesas anomian**.

Both aorist. A settled disposition, stated as completed action.

The pair is the point: loving one and hating the other are the same posture.

## The consequence

*"**That is why** God, Your God, has poured over You the oil of gladness."*

The Greek is **dia touto** — on account of this.

The anointing is presented as a response to the two verbs.

## The oil

**Elaion agalliaseos** — oil of exultation.

**Agalliasis** — the strong word for rejoicing, from the verb Peter uses of joy inexpressible. See
the note on 1 Peter 1:6–7.

## What anointing with oil was

Kings and priests were anointed. So were guests at a feast — *You anoint my head with oil; my cup
runs over*.

The image here is festal rather than official: oil poured over somebody at a celebration.

## The measure

*"And **given You more of it than anyone beside You**."*

The Greek is **para tous metochous sou** — beyond Your companions.

**Metochoi** — partners, sharers. The same word Hebrews uses of believers: *we have become
partakers of Christ*.

The comparison is with people who also received it.

## What is being said

The one described as *a Man of sorrows and acquainted with grief* is also the one anointed with
gladness above everyone else. See the note on Isaiah 53:1–3.

Hebrews does not treat those as competing descriptions.

## What the psalm goes on to say

*All Your garments are scented with myrrh and aloes and cassia.*

The three spices, and two of them are burial spices — the myrrh brought to the tomb, and the aloes
Nicodemus carried.

## Where the chapter is going

*But to which of the angels has He ever said: "**Sit at My right hand, till I make Your enemies Your
footstool**"?* See the note on Hebrews 1:13–14.

> **The connective.** *That is why.* The anointing with gladness is given as the consequence of two
> aorist verbs — loving one thing and hating another.
