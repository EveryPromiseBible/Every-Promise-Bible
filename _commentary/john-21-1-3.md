After the resurrection, seven men go back to work, and it is the same lake and the
same result as the first time.

## Where

*By the **Sea of Tiberias*** — the Sea of Galilee, in the territory of Zebulun and
Naphtali.

Zebulun's blessing was *a harbour for ships*, and Zebulun's name means dwelling. He
had lived there, taught there, and done most of His work on that shoreline. See
the note on Genesis 49:13.

## Who was there

*Simon Peter, Thomas, Nathanael, the sons of Zebedee, and two others.*

Seven of them. Peter first, and Thomas next — the one who had said he would not
believe unless he put his hand in the side.

## "I am going fishing"

Three words in the Greek, and John gives no explanation for them.

It is the trade Peter left when he pulled two boats onto the shore, full of fish,
and walked away from the catch of his life.

*They said to him, "We will come with you."* Nobody argues.

## "All that night they caught nothing at all"

The same lake, the same trade, the same night hours, the same empty nets.

It is exactly what Luke 5 recorded before any of them followed Him: *we worked hard
all night long and came up with nothing at all.* See the note on Luke 5:4–7.

Three years of walking with Him, and the fishing goes the same way it always did.

## What is waiting on the beach

*When the morning had now come, Jesus stood on the shore; yet the disciples did
not know that it was Jesus.*

He asks whether they have any food, tells them which side to cast on, and the net
comes up with a hundred and fifty-three fish and does not break.

*And when they had come to land, they saw a **fire of coals** there, and **fish
laid on it, and bread**.*

He had breakfast cooking before they got there. He did not need their catch.

## The charcoal fire

The Greek word for it, **anthrakia**, occurs twice in the whole New Testament, both
in John.

The other one is in the high priest's courtyard, where *the servants and officers
had made a fire of coals… and Peter stood with them and warmed himself*, and denied
Him three times.

Peter's restoration happens over the same kind of fire, and he is asked three
questions.

## What He handled that morning

The work — a net so full they could not haul it in. The body — bread and fish
already on the coals. And the thing Peter could not say out loud.

All three, and He did not raise the third until the man had eaten.

> **What He does not mention.** The denial. Not once. He asks *do you love Me?*
> three times and never says why He is asking three times.
