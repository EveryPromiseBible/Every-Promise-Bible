Two verses, and the first one is the condition the second one rests on.

## The condition

*Because you have made the LORD **your refuge**, the Most High **your dwelling
place**.*

Two words, and they are not the same.

**Refuge** — *machseh*, a shelter you run into from a storm. Somewhere you go when
something is happening.

**Dwelling place** — *maon*, a habitation, a lair, a home. Somewhere you live.

The psalm's opening line says the same: *whoever **makes a home** in the secret
shelter of the Most High.* See the note on Psalms 91:1–2.

## The promise

*No evil will overtake you, **no plague will come near your tent**.*

The Hebrew for *plague* is **nega** — a stroke, a blow, a mark. It is the word
Leviticus uses of the mark of leprosy, and the word for the strokes God said would
fall on Egypt.

And *your tent* — the older rendering is *your dwelling*. The protection is described
around a residence rather than a person.

## The verse before them

*A thousand may fall at your side, and ten thousand at your right hand; **but it
shall not come near you**.* See the note on Psalms 91:5–8.

The psalm does not describe an untroubled world. It describes falling on every side.

## Where the same phrase turns up in Exodus

*The blood shall be a sign for you on the houses where you are. And when I see the
blood, I will pass over you, and **the plague shall not be on you to destroy you**
when I strike the land of Egypt.*

The same noun, over doorframes, on a night when it was on everybody else. See the
note on Exodus 12:1–13.

## Who quoted this psalm

The devil, in the wilderness, and he quoted the two verses immediately following:

*"He shall give His angels charge over you… in their hands they shall bear you up."*

He got the words right and left out the clause *to keep you in all your ways.*

And Jesus did not correct the psalm. He answered from Deuteronomy: *you shall not
tempt the LORD your God.*

## How the psalm ends

The last three verses are the only ones spoken by God rather than about Him:

*"Because he has set his love upon Me, therefore I will deliver him… **He shall call
upon Me, and I will answer him; I will be with him in trouble**."*

**In** trouble. Not instead of it. See the note on Psalms 91:14–16.

> **What the psalm is doing structurally.** It has three voices — a teacher describing
> the refuge, a believer saying *I will say of the LORD, "You are my refuge"*, and
> God Himself at the end. The promises are stated, confessed, and then confirmed by
> the One making them.
