A man asks about willingness, not power, and the answer takes his own word back.

## The man

*A man came along who was **covered with leprosy**.*

The Greek is **pleres lepras** — full of leprosy.

Luke is a physician, and he is the only one who grades it. Matthew and Mark say *a leper*.

## Where he is

*While He was **in one of the towns**.*

Which he should not have been. *He shall dwell alone; his dwelling shall be outside the camp.*

## The approach

*When he saw Jesus **he fell face down and begged Him**.*

## The request

*"Lord, **if You are willing, You can make me clean**."*

The Greek is **ean theles, dunasai** — if You will, You are able.

He has no doubt about the power. The uncertainty is entirely about the desire.

## Why the question is not strange

Leprosy was widely read as judgment. Miriam got it. Gehazi got it. Uzziah got it in the temple.

A man in that condition had reason to wonder whether anybody, including God, wanted him well.

## The touch

*Jesus **reached out His hand and touched him**.*

The Greek is **hepsato autou** — touched him.

Under the law this makes the toucher unclean. Nobody had touched this man in however long he had
been ill.

The healing did not require it. The word alone healed the centurion's servant at a distance.

## The answer

*"**I am willing**," He said. "**Be clean**."*

The Greek is **thelo, katharistheti**.

Two words, and the first one is the man's own word handed back.

He asked *if You are willing*. The answer is **thelo** — I am willing.

## The order

Touch first, then the word.

The contact happens while the man is still a leper.

## The result

*And **immediately the leprosy left him**.*

## The instruction

*Then Jesus told him **not to speak of it to anyone**.*

*"**Go and show yourself to the priest**, and make the offering Moses commanded for your cleansing,
**so that they can see for themselves**."*

Leviticus 14 sets out the ritual — two birds, cedar, scarlet, hyssop, and the priest's inspection.

## What that means

The priesthood has to certify a cleansed leper.

There is no record of the ritual ever being performed before this. The procedure existed on the
page and had nowhere to be used.

## What Mark records the man doing

*However, he went out and began to proclaim it freely, and to spread the matter, **so that Jesus
could no longer openly enter the city**, but was outside in deserted places*.

The leper goes into the towns. Jesus stays outside them.

## What happens instead

*But **word about Him spread even further**, and enormous crowds came together to listen and to be
healed.*

## The last verse

*And **He would slip away to the quiet places, where He prayed**.*

The Greek is **hupochoron en tais eremois kai proseuchomenos** — imperfect. Habitually.

Luke records this repeatedly: *He often withdrew*, *He went out to the mountain to pray, and
continued all night*.

The busier the crowds get in Luke, the more often he mentions the withdrawing.

## The other ten

*They stood off at a distance and raised their voices… "Go and show yourselves to the priests."*
See the note on Luke 17:11–14.

No touch that time.

> **The word.** He asked *if You are willing*. The answer is his own word, first: **thelo** — I am
> willing — and the hand was already on him.
