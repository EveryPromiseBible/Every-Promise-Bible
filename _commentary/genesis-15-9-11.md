He asks how he can know, and is told to fetch five animals.

## The question behind it

*"Lord GOD, **how shall I know that I will inherit it**?"*

The verse before. He has just been counted righteous for believing — and then asks for
confirmation. See the note on Genesis 15:6.

The request is not rebuked. It is answered with a ceremony.

## The list

*"Bring Me **a heifer three years old, a goat three years old, a ram three years old, along with a
turtledove and a young pigeon**."*

Five creatures.

Every one of them turns up later in the Levitical sacrificial code, which will not be written for
four hundred years.

## The age

Three years old, three times. Full-grown, at the height of value.

## What he does

*Abram brought them all. He **cut the larger animals in two and laid each half opposite the other**.*

The Hebrew is **vayebatter otam battavek** — he cut them in the middle.

## The birds

*But **he did not divide the birds**.*

Leviticus later says the same of a bird offering: *he shall split it at its wings, but shall not
divide it completely*.

The exception is preserved without explanation, here and there.

## What the ceremony is

The standard form of a covenant in the ancient Near East.

The Hebrew idiom is not *make* a covenant. It is **karat berit** — to *cut* a covenant.

## How it worked

The animals are halved and the halves set opposite each other, with a path between.

The parties walk down the aisle between the pieces, and in doing so say: let this be done to me if I
break it.

## The witness to that

*"**I will treat them like the calf they cut in two to walk between its pieces**… the officials of
Judah… who walked between the pieces of the calf."* See the note on Jeremiah 34:17–20.

Jeremiah describes the ritual and its penalty, and applies it.

## The waiting

*When **birds of prey came down on the carcasses, Abram drove them away**.*

The Hebrew is **ha'ayit** — birds of prey, collective.

## The gap

Nothing else happens for hours. He has laid out the pieces and has to keep the vultures off them
all afternoon.

## What comes next

*And when the sun was going down, **a deep sleep fell upon Abram**; and behold, horror and great
darkness fell upon him.* See the note on Genesis 15:12–16.

**Tardemah** — the word used of the sleep that fell on Adam.

He is put to sleep before the ceremony begins.

## Who walks

*A **smoking firepot and a blazing torch** appeared and **passed between the pieces**.* See the note on
Genesis 15:17–21.

One party goes down the aisle. Abram is unconscious on the ground.

## What that means

A covenant sworn with a self-maledictory oath, in which only one of the two parties takes the oath.

The man it is made with is asleep, and never walks.

## What he had been promised

*"**Look now toward heaven, and count the stars** if you are able to number them… **So shall your
descendants be**."* See the note on Genesis 15:1–5.

## Where the same fire turns up

The pillar of fire by night, and *the LORD descended upon it in fire, and its smoke ascended like
the smoke of a furnace*. See the note on Exodus 19:16–19.

> **The absent signatory.** Two parties should walk the aisle. God puts Abram to sleep first, and
> goes down it alone.
