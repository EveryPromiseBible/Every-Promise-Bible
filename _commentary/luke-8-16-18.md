Three sayings in a row, and the last one is about the ear rather than the message.

## The lamp

*"**No one lights a lamp and then covers it with a jar or shoves it under a bed**."*

A first-century lamp is a clay dish of oil with a wick. Put a jar over it and it goes out;
put it under a bed and the bed catches.

Neither option in the sentence is a sensible way to store a lamp. Both are absurd on
purpose.

## The stand

*"**You put it up on a stand**, so that **everyone who comes in can see by its light**."*

Luke's version says who benefits — the people entering the house.

Matthew's has *it gives light to all who are in the house*. Luke is thinking about arrivals.

## The rule

*"**Nothing is hidden that will not be brought out into the open**, and nothing is covered up
that will not be uncovered and made plain."*

Said twice, in two ways, in one verse.

The same sentence appears elsewhere as a warning: *what you have spoken in the ear in inner
rooms will be proclaimed on the housetops*.

Here it is attached to the lamp, which makes it a promise about the message rather than a
warning about secrets. What is being taught in private will end up public.

## The instruction

*"So **pay attention to how you listen**."*

The Greek is **blepete oun pos akouete** — take heed *how* you hear.

Mark's version has *take heed **what** you hear*. Luke has *how*.

Between them the two Gospels cover both: what goes in, and the manner of the listening.

## The measure

*"**Whoever has will be given more, and whoever has nothing will lose even what he thinks he
has**."*

Luke's addition is in the last clause — **ho dokei echein**, what he *seems* to have, what he
*thinks* he has.

Matthew has *even what he has will be taken away*. See the note on Matthew 13:10–17.

Luke's version says the thing lost was never really held.

## What is being measured

Mark makes it explicit: *with the same measure you use, it will be measured to you; and to
you who hear, more will be given*.

The attention given is the size of the container.

## Where this sits

Immediately after the parable of the sower and its explanation, and immediately before His
mother and brothers arrive outside.

The four soils, and then three sayings about hearing, and then: *My mother and My brothers
are **these who hear the word of God and do it***.

Luke arranges the whole passage around one subject.

## The lamp and the soil

Both images are about receiving. The soil takes seed in; the lamp gives light out.

Between them is the sentence about hearing, and it belongs to both.

## What Mark does with the same material

Mark 4 has the lamp, the measure, and then two more seed parables — the one about the man who
sleeps and does not know how it grows, and the mustard seed. See the note on
Mark 4:26–29.

## Where the light image goes

*You are the light of the world. A city that is set on a hill cannot be hidden.*

And *let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your
Father in heaven*.

Same lamp, same stand, applied to people.

> **The word Luke changes.** Mark says take heed *what* you hear; Luke says take heed *how*.
> And the thing lost, in his version, is what a man only thought he had.
