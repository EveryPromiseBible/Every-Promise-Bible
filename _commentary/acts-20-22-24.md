In the middle of his farewell to the Ephesian elders, Paul says where he is going and
what he expects there.

## The compulsion

*"I am going to Jerusalem **under a compulsion in the spirit I cannot shake**."*

The Greek is **dedemenos to pneumati** — bound in the spirit.

The same word for being in chains. He is describing himself as already tied.

## The ignorance

*"**Not knowing what will happen to me there**."*

## The one thing he does know

*"Except that **the Holy Spirit testifies to me in every city that chains and hardships are
waiting for me**."*

City after city, the same message.

Luke records one instance in the next chapter: Agabus takes Paul's belt at Caesarea, binds
his own hands and feet, and says *so shall the Jews at Jerusalem bind the man who owns
this belt*. And the whole company begs him not to go.

His answer there: *what do you mean by weeping and breaking my heart? For I am ready not
only to be bound, but also to die at Jerusalem.*

## The response

*"But **none of these things move me**."*

The Greek is *I make my life of no account* — the older versions have *none of these
things move me*.

## The valuation

*"**Nor do I count my life dear to myself**."*

The Greek is **timian** — precious, valuable. He declines to price it.

## The purpose

*"**So that I may finish my course with joy**."*

The Greek is **dromon** — a race course, a track. Paul uses it of himself three times, and
the last is in his final letter: *I have fought the good fight, **I have finished the
race**, I have kept the faith.* See the note on 2 Timothy 4:6–8.

**With joy.** He puts the two in the same clause — a course he expects chains at the end
of, finished with joy.

## The commission

*"And **the ministry I received from the Lord Jesus** — to testify to **the good news of
the grace of God**."*

He names his own commission in six words, and the noun at the centre of it is grace.

## What he does not say

Nothing about the outcome, the outcome's fairness, or a request for it to change.

He has been told what is waiting. He states it and keeps going.

## What happened

He reached Jerusalem, was seized in the temple, nearly beaten to death, rescued by Roman
troops, and stopped a flogging by asking one legal question. See the note on
Acts 22:25–26.

Then two years in Caesarea, an appeal to Caesar, a shipwreck, and Rome — where Acts leaves
him *preaching the kingdom of God and teaching the things which concern the Lord Jesus
Christ **with all confidence, no one forbidding him***.

He got to Rome in chains, as promised, and Luke's last word about it is *no one forbidding
him*.

> **What he was told and what he did.** Chains and hardships, confirmed in every city he
> passed through — and he says the only thing he wants is to finish the course and deliver
> the message he was given.
