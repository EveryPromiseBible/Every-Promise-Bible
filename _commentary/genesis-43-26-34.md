Joseph gives a dinner for the brothers who sold him, and two things happen at it
that they do not understand.

## The room he went into

*Joseph hurried out, for his heart was overflowing at the sight of his brother,
and he was about to weep. He went into his private room and wept there. Then he
washed his face, came out, controlled himself, and said, "Serve the meal."*

He has been the second most powerful man in Egypt for years. He sees his full
brother for the first time since he was seventeen and has to leave his own house
to cry.

Genesis records the washing and the composure. It is one of the most human
paragraphs in the book.

## The seating

*The brothers were seated in front of him in order, from the oldest to the
youngest, and they looked at one another in amazement.*

Nobody had told the steward their ages. Ten men are placed in birth order by a
household they have never visited, and they cannot account for it.

The narrator gives no explanation either. He simply reports them staring at each
other.

## Benjamin's portion

*Benjamin's portion was five times larger than anyone else's.*

Joseph does it deliberately, and it is a test as much as a gift. These are the
men who once could not stand their father's favouritism toward one son and threw
him in a pit for it.

Now, in front of them, the youngest is singled out again — and this time nobody
objects. *So they drank and were merry together with him.*

> **Where it is going.** The next chapter is the silver cup planted in Benjamin's
> sack, and Judah — the brother who proposed selling Joseph — offering himself as
> a slave in the boy's place. The dinner is the setup. Joseph is finding out
> whether they are the same men, and they are not.
