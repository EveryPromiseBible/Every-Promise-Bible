Peter quotes Isaiah, and the man quoting it was given a name meaning rock.

## What comes before

*Coming to Him as to a living stone, rejected indeed by men, but chosen by God and precious,
**you also, as living stones, are being built up a spiritual house**, a holy priesthood.*

A building made of people, on a stone that is alive.

## The quotation

*Watch — **I am setting a stone down in Zion, the one the whole corner rests on**.*

Isaiah 28:16. See the note on Isaiah 28:16.

The Greek is **akrogoniaion** — the extreme corner stone. The block at the angle that sets
the line for both walls and carries the weight where they meet.

## The two verbs

*I chose Him.*

The Greek is **eklekton** — chosen, elect. The same word Peter uses of his readers in the
letter's first line: *elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father*.

*He is **worth everything**.*

**Entimon** — held in honour, precious, of high value.

## The promise

*And **whoever believes on Him will not be put to shame**.*

The Greek follows the Septuagint reading of Isaiah — **ou me kataischunthe**.

The Hebrew has *will not make haste*. Peter uses the Greek Bible, as Paul does.

## What follows

*Therefore, to you who believe, **He is precious**; but to those who are disobedient, "**the
stone which the builders rejected has become the chief cornerstone**," and "**a stone of
stumbling and a rock of offence**."*

Three stone quotations in a row: Isaiah 28, Psalm 118, and Isaiah 8.

The same stone does three different things depending on who is standing near it.

## The one about the builders

Psalm 118. Jesus quoted it at the end of the parable of the vineyard, to the chief priests,
and *they sought to lay hands on Him*.

Peter had quoted it himself before the Sanhedrin: *this is the stone which was rejected by
you builders, which has become the chief cornerstone*.

The men he said it to were the builders in question.

## The one about stumbling

Isaiah 8: *He will be as a sanctuary, but a stone of stumbling and a rock of offence to both
the houses of Israel*.

Sanctuary and stumbling block, in one sentence, in the original.

## Peter's own name

*You are Simon the son of Jonah. **You shall be called Cephas**, which is translated, A
Stone.*

The Greek is **petros** — a stone, a piece of rock.

The man who was renamed Rock spends this passage arranging every stone text in the Old
Testament around somebody else.

He never applies the word to himself in either letter.

## What the house is for

*A holy priesthood, **to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus
Christ**.*

And then the sentence that follows the three quotations:

*But you are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, His own special people.*
See the note on 1 Peter 2:9.

## What the building is elsewhere

*Having been built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, **Jesus Christ Himself
being the chief cornerstone**, in whom the whole building, being fitted together, grows into a
holy temple in the Lord.*

Paul, in Ephesians, with the same word.

> **What the writer does not do.** Peter is the man who was renamed Rock, and he spends the
> paragraph putting every stone verse in the Old Testament under somebody else's feet.
