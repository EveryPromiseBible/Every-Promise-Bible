Paul calls in a second witness, and the witness is a psalm.

## Why David is here

The chapter's argument runs on Abraham: *Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to
him for righteousness.*

Paul then reaches for a second case, four hundred years later, from a different kind of
man. Abraham was the founder; David was a king who had a man killed to cover an
adultery.

## The word

*The person **to whose account God puts righteousness**.*

The Greek is **logizomai** — to reckon, to credit, to enter in a ledger. Paul uses it
eleven times in this one chapter.

It is a bookkeeping word, and the entry is made by somebody else.

## The qualifier

*Apart from **anything accomplished**.*

The Greek is *apart from works*. Paul has already set it up in verse 4: *now to him who
works, the wages are not counted as grace but as debt.*

Wages are owed. This is not that.

## The quotation

Psalm 32, and Paul quotes it in full — three clauses, and every one is passive.

## "Whose wrongs are lifted off"

The Hebrew is **nasa** — to lift, to carry away. The word used of the scapegoat bearing
the iniquities into the wilderness.

## "Whose sins are buried out of sight"

The Hebrew is **kasah** — covered.

## "Against whom the Lord counts no wrong"

The third is the one Paul is really after, because it uses his word: *the Lord does not
**impute** iniquity*. Same verb, **logizomai**.

Two clauses about what is done with the sin, and one about what is not written down.

## The happiness

*Oh, **the happiness**.*

The Greek is **makarios** — blessed, happy. It is how the Beatitudes open.

Twice in two verses, at the front of each sentence, which is the Hebrew word order of
the psalm.

## What Psalm 32 is

David's own account of the year he said nothing:

*When I kept silent, my bones grew old through my groaning all the day long. For day and
night Your hand was heavy upon me.*

*I acknowledged my sin to You, and my iniquity I have not hidden… **and You forgave the
iniquity of my sin**.*

Paul quotes the opening of it, and the psalm behind the quotation is a man describing
what the silence cost.

## The point Paul makes next

*Does this blessedness then come upon the circumcised only, or upon the uncircumcised
also?*

His whole use of the two witnesses turns on chronology: Abraham was counted righteous in
Genesis 15, and circumcised in Genesis 17. Fourteen years apart, in that order.

## What Paul concludes

*Now it was not written for his sake alone… **but also for us**. It shall be imputed to
us who believe in Him who raised up Jesus our Lord from the dead.*

The ledger entry is described as transferable, and the reason given is the resurrection.

> **What the three clauses have in common.** Nobody in them does anything. Lifted off,
> covered, not counted — and Paul picks the psalm precisely because the man singing it
> had nothing to plead.
