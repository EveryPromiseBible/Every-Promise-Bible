He waits until the last day of the feast, and then He shouts.

## The day He chose

*On the **last day of the festival, the great day**, Jesus **stood up and called
out in a loud voice**.*

The Feast of Tabernacles ran seven days, and every morning a priest carried a
golden pitcher down to the pool of Siloam, filled it, and poured it out at the
base of the altar while the people sang from Isaiah 12 — *with joy you will draw
water from the wells of salvation.*

By most accounts the pouring did not happen on the eighth day. The pitcher stayed
empty.

Into that silence, a man stands up in the temple court and says: if anyone is
thirsty, come to Me.

## "Let him come to Me and drink"

Two verbs, and both are simple. Not qualify, not prepare. Come, and drink.

The invitation is addressed to a condition — *if anyone is **thirsty*** — and
thirst is not an achievement.

## Where the rivers come out

*Out of his **innermost being** will flow **rivers** of living water.*

Not a trickle and not a cistern. **Rivers**, plural.

The Greek is *out of his belly* — the deepest inside of a person, the part
nothing reaches.

And note the direction. He drinks, and what comes out of him is more than he took
in, and it comes out for other people.

## "As the Scripture has said"

John does not tell us which Scripture, and no single verse matches word for word.
The picture runs through the prophets — water from the rock in the wilderness,
Ezekiel's river out from under the temple threshold, Zechariah's living waters
going out from Jerusalem.

He is drawing on a whole current, not quoting a line.

## John's own footnote

*He was speaking about the Spirit, whom those who believed in Him were going to
receive; for the Holy Spirit had not yet been given, **because Jesus had not yet
been glorified**.*

John, writing decades later, explains it for us. The Spirit waits on the cross.

And the sequence is stated plainly: glorified first, then given. What was
announced in a temple courtyard at a feast arrived in an upper room at Pentecost.

> **Where the water had been coming from.** The pool of Siloam — the same pool
> Jesus sends the blind man to wash in two chapters later. John keeps his images
> and comes back to them.
