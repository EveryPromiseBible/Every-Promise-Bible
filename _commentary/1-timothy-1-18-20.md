Two things are to be held. The letter says that when one is let go, the other goes down with it.

## The handover

*So **this is the charge I am placing in your hands**, Timothy, my son.*

The Greek is **parathēkē**-language — **paratithemai soi**, I commit to you.

A deposit entrusted to somebody for safekeeping. The letter ends on the same image: *guard what was
committed to your trust*.

## The backing

*In keeping with **the words once spoken over you**.*

The Greek is **kata tas proagousas epi se prophēteias** — according to the prophecies previously
made about you.

Referred to again later: *do not neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you by
prophecy with the laying on of hands*. See the note on 1 Timothy 4:14–16.

## What they are for

*Let those words **arm you for the good fight**, and fight it well.*

The Greek is **strateuē tēn kalēn strateian** — that you may wage the good warfare.

The prophecies are treated as equipment, not decoration. Something to fight with on a bad day.

## The two things

*Keep your grip on **faith**, and on **a conscience that is clear**.*

The Greek is **echōn pistin kai agathēn suneidēsin** — having faith and a good conscience.

The pairing appears three times in this letter.

## The order

Faith first, conscience second. But the sentence that follows runs the failure the other way.

## The wreck

*For there are men who **shoved that conscience aside**, and **their faith went down like a ship on the
rocks**.*

The Greek is **apōsamenoi** — having thrust away. A violent verb; the same word Stephen uses of
Israel pushing Moses aside.

And **enauagēsan** — they were shipwrecked. From **naus**, ship.

## The sequence

The conscience is pushed away first. The faith is what founders afterwards.

Paul does not describe men who lost an argument about doctrine. He describes men who overrode
something and found the rest went with it.

## What that means about the conscience

It is not the source of faith. It is the hull.

A man who trains himself to sail past his own conscience has removed what kept the water out.

## The names

*Hymenaeus and Alexander are among them.*

Hymenaeus appears again: *who have strayed concerning the truth, saying that the resurrection is
already past*. See the note on 2 Timothy 2:16–18.

## The action

*I **handed them over to Satan**, so that they would be **taught not to speak against God**.*

The Greek is **paredōka tō Satana**. And **paideuthōsin** — that they may be instructed,
disciplined, trained as a child is.

## What that is

The same phrase as the Corinthian case: *deliver such a one to Satan for the destruction of the
flesh, **that his spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord Jesus***. See the note on
1 Corinthians 5:3–5.

Removal from the church's protection, with a stated purpose that is remedial.

## The word

**Paideuō** — the verb behind *paedagogy*. Teaching by discipline.

The sentence names the object as instruction, not destruction.

## What the letter says about the law

*The law is not made for a righteous person, but for the lawless.* See the note on 1 Timothy 1:8–11.

And immediately before this charge, Paul's own case: *I obtained mercy because I did it ignorantly
in unbelief… **Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am chief***. See the note
on 1 Timothy 1:15–17.

He gives the warning about shipwreck straight after calling himself the worst case there is.

> **The order of the sinking.** The conscience is shoved aside first, and the faith goes down after
> it. Paul writes the wreck in that sequence.
