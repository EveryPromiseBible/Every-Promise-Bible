The verse **Jesus** said last, and what David meant by it.

## Where the line ends up

*And when **Jesus** had cried out with a loud voice, He said, "**Father, into Your hands I commit
My spirit**." Having said this, He breathed His last.*

**Luke** records it as the seventh and final saying from the cross. It is a quotation of this
verse, with one word added: **Father**.

## What David meant by it

Not dying words. Psalm 31 is a prayer of a man under pressure and expecting to live — *You have
seen my affliction, You have known the troubles of my soul.*

**Paqad** — to deposit, to entrust, to place something with somebody for safekeeping. It is a
banking word. He is putting his life on deposit.

*For I know whom I have believed and am persuaded that **He is able to keep what I have
committed to Him** until that Day.*

**Paul** uses the same image in Greek — **parathēkē**, a deposit lodged for safekeeping — and
he is writing from prison.

## The clause in the middle

*You have redeemed me, O LORD, **God of faithfulness**.*

**Padah** — to ransom, to buy out. David says it in the past tense before any of the rescue in
this psalm has happened.

*In whom we have **redemption through His blood**.*

## What God has already seen

*You have **seen my affliction**, You have **known the troubles of my soul**. You did not shut me
in the hand of the enemy. You set my feet **in a wide open place**.*

**Merchav** — a broad place. The Hebrew for distress means narrow; the rescue is described as
its opposite. See the note on Psalms 18:16–24.

## Who else said it

Stephen, being stoned: *"**Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.**"*

He is quoting the same psalm, and where **Jesus** said *Father*, Stephen says **Lord Jesus** —
addressing to the Son the words the Son had addressed to the Father.

> **What the word means.** **Paqad** — a deposit lodged with somebody for safekeeping. David
> says it in the middle of his life. Jesus said it at the end of His, and added one word to the
> front.
