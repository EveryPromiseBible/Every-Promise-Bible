Seven items in a chain, and the chain is fastened at one end before Peter says anything
about adding.

## What comes first

*On top of **all that**.*

The Greek is *for this very reason*. Everything in the list is a response to what has
just been stated:

*His divine power has given to us **all things that pertain to life and godliness**…
exceedingly great and precious promises, that through these you may be **partakers of the
divine nature**.*

The supply is announced before the work.

## The verb

*Set goodness down beside it.*

The Greek is **epichoregeo** — to supply, to furnish. It comes from the **choregos**, the
wealthy citizen in Athens who paid for the chorus in a play: costumes, training, the
whole production, out of his own pocket.

The word carries lavishness, and Peter has just used the same root of God in verse 3.

## What is already there

*You have **trust already**.*

Faith is not on the list of things to add. It is the thing everything else is set beside.

## The seven

- **Goodness** — **arete**, moral excellence, a thing performing what it is for
- **Understanding** — **gnosis**, knowledge
- **A firm hand on your own appetites** — **enkrateia**, self-control
- **Staying power to keep going when it costs you** — **hupomone**, remaining under
- **A life bent toward God** — **eusebeia**, godliness
- **Real warmth toward the family** — **philadelphia**, brotherly affection
- **Love** — **agape**

## The shape of the list

Each item is set beside the one before it. The Greek preposition is *in* — supply in your
faith, virtue; in virtue, knowledge.

It is not a ladder of stages, with one finished before the next begins. Peter is
describing things laid alongside each other.

## Where it starts and ends

Faith at one end, love at the other.

Paul's ordering is the same: *faith working through love*, and *the greatest of these is
love*.

## The effort

*Do not sit still. **Put everything you have into this**.*

The Greek is **spouden pasan pareisenenkantes** — bringing in all diligence alongside.

Peter asks for real exertion, and he asks for it on the strength of a supply he has
already said was given.

## The two words for family and love

**Philadelphia** and **agape** are listed separately and in that order.

The warmth toward the household comes second-to-last, and love is set beside even that.

## What the list is for

The next two verses give the reason, and it is about sight. See the note on
2 Peter 1:8–9.

## Who is writing

Peter, in what he says is his last letter — *knowing that shortly I must put off my
tent, just as our Lord Jesus Christ showed me.*

## The promise attached

*For if you do these things **you will never stumble**.*

> **The order Peter uses.** God's supply first, stated in the fullest terms available.
> Then *for this very reason*, and then a list of things to bring alongside a faith the
> reader is told they already have.
