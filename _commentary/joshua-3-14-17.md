The crossing into the land, and the detail that matters is when the water stopped.

## The order of march

*With **the priests carrying the ark of the covenant walking ahead of them**.*

At the sea, forty years earlier, Moses stretched out a rod and the water divided before
anyone moved.

Here the ark goes first and nothing happens until it arrives at the edge.

## The season

*Now **the Jordan overflows its banks all through harvest season**.*

Spring, at the barley harvest, with snowmelt off Hermon coming down. The river is at its
widest and fastest.

The narrator puts the clause in so nobody reads it as a summer crossing.

## The timing

*But **the moment the priests who carried the ark reached the river and their feet touched
the water's edge**, the water flowing down from upstream **stood still**.*

The water stopped when the feet went in.

They had been told in advance: *when the soles of the feet of the priests rest in the
waters of the Jordan, the waters shall be cut off.*

Nothing was visible until somebody stepped into a flooded river.

## Where it stopped

*It rose up in a heap, **far upstream, near a town called Adam, beside Zarethan**.*

The Hebrew names the place. Adam is about sixteen miles upstream.

The stopping happened out of sight of the people crossing. What they saw was a dry bed;
the event itself was a day's walk north.

## The Hebrew for "heap"

**Ned** — a heap, a mound. It is the same word used of the sea in the Song of Moses: *the
waters were gathered together, the floods stood upright **like a heap**.*

## Where the rest went

*And the water flowing down toward **the Salt Sea, the sea of the plain, was completely cut
off**.*

Everything downstream drained away and left the bed dry.

## Where they crossed

*So the people crossed over, **right in front of Jericho**.*

In full view of the city they were about to besiege.

Rahab had already told the spies what the news was doing: *we have heard how the LORD
dried up the water of the Red Sea for you… and as soon as we heard these things, our
hearts melted.*

## The priests

*And the priests who carried the ark **stood firm on dry ground in the middle of the
Jordan**, and **all Israel crossed** on dry ground, **until the whole nation had finished
passing over**.*

The Hebrew for *stood firm* is **kun** — to be fixed, established, made ready. It is the
word for something set in place and holding.

They went in first and came out last. The whole nation walked past them.

## The stones

Twelve men, one from each tribe, carried twelve stones out of the riverbed where the
priests' feet had stood, and Joshua set them up at Gilgal.

And twelve more were set in the river itself, where the priests stood — *and they are there
to this day*.

*That this may be a sign among you when your children ask in time to come, "**What do these
stones mean to you?**"*

## What happened when they came up

*And it came to pass, when the priests who bore the ark came from the midst of the Jordan
and the soles of their feet touched the dry land, that **the waters of the Jordan returned
to their place and overflowed all its banks as before**.*

## The date

*The people came up from the Jordan **on the tenth day of the first month**.*

Nisan 10 — the day the Passover lamb was selected and taken into the house.

Forty years earlier, on that day in Egypt, they were choosing lambs.

> **What had to happen first.** The feet. The river was in flood and the promise was that
> it would stop when the soles of the priests' feet rested in it — so the first men into
> the water went in while it was still running.
