Paul compares Adam and Christ and then insists the comparison does not balance.

## "Not two of a kind"

*The free gift and the trespass are not two of a kind.*

He has just drawn the parallel and now he breaks it. The two sides look
symmetrical and are not — the second is larger, and he says so three times with
*much more*.

## Where the asymmetry is

The sharpest line is the middle one:

*The judgment followed a **single** trespass and brought condemnation, but the
free gift followed **many** trespasses and brought a verdict of righteousness.*

One sin was enough to bring the verdict down. The gift had to deal with an
accumulation — every trespass since — and it still came out the other side with
an acquittal.

## The switch nobody expects

*If by the trespass of the one man **death reigned**… much more will those who
receive the overflow of grace and the gift of being put right with God **reign in
life**.*

The symmetry would require: death reigned, so life will reign. Paul does not
write that. He says **they** will reign — the people, not the abstraction.

Death is a monarch in his sentence. Grace does not merely replace it with a
better monarch; it puts the formerly condemned on the throne.

## "Those who receive"

The one active verb on the human side of the passage. Not those who achieve or
maintain — those who **receive**, and what they receive is described twice as
overflowing and as a gift.

> **What "in life" means.** Not after it. Paul says reign *in life* — the sphere
> is this one. The whole argument since chapter 3 has been that the standing is
> given now, which is why he can put the reigning in the present tense of a
> Christian's existence.
