The third friend, and he brings a procedure.

## The shape of it

*"**If** you would only set your heart right… **if** there is sin in your grip,
put it far away… **then** you could lift up your face without a stain."*

Two conditions and a result. It is a formula, and it is the oldest one there is:
do this, and God will do that.

## The steps

- *set your heart right*
- *stretch out your hands to Him*
- *if there is sin in your grip, put it far away*
- *let no wrong live in your tent*

Four items on a checklist, handed to a man covered in boils scraping himself with
a piece of broken pot.

## Then the promise

*"Then you could **lift up your face without a stain**. You would stand firm and
unafraid."*

And a few verses on: *your life would be brighter than noonday; its darkness would
become like the morning.*

The words are beautiful. The offer is genuine. And everything in it is conditional
on Job's performance.

## Why it is the wrong word

Every clause depends on something Job has to produce, and the whole premise is
that his darkness is being caused by sin he is holding on to.

The reader knows it is not. Chapter 1 said so, and God Himself said so out loud
in the throne room: *there is no one on earth like him.*

Zophar is telling a man to clean up something that is not there, and then
promising him light on the other side of it.

## What it sounds like now

*Your darkness is because of sin — deal with it, and the brightness will come
back.*

It is still preached. And it still lands on people who are already on the floor,
and it still asks them to produce a righteousness of their own before God will
lift their face.

The face is lifted at Calvary, and the sin was put far away by Somebody else.

## What Zophar had said just before this

*"Know then that God exacts from you **less than your iniquity deserves**."*

That is his opening position. He begins by telling Job he is getting off lightly.

## What he does not have

Not one of the three friends has a word from God. They have observation,
tradition, and a formula, and between them they cover every way of speaking to a
sufferer that leaves him to sort himself out.

> **The verdict.** All three are named in chapter 42, and the same sentence is
> spoken over all three: *you have not spoken of Me what is right.* And what they
> have to do about it is bring seven bulls and seven rams and stand there while Job
> prays for them. See the note on Job 42:7–8.
