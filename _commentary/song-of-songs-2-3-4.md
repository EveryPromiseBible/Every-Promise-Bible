She answers his description of her with one of her own, and it is a tree.

## What he had just said

*Like a lily among thorns, so is my love among the daughters.*

## Her reply

*Like **an apple tree among the trees of the wood**, so is my beloved among the young men.*

The Hebrew is **ketappuach** — the identification of the fruit is uncertain; apple, quince, or
apricot.

The point is not botany. It is a fruit tree standing in a forest of trees that bear nothing you
can eat.

## The comparison she makes

He compared her to a flower among thorns. She compares him to the one tree in a wood with fruit
on it.

Both images are of one thing among many, and both are the speaker's own choice of picture.

## The sitting

*In his shade **I longed to sit**, and **there I sat**.*

The Hebrew is **chimmadti veyashavti** — I desired and I sat.

Two verbs, and the second one answers the first. She wanted to, and then she did.

Nothing intervenes. The Song does not put an obstacle between the longing and the sitting.

## The shade

**Betsillo** — in his shadow.

In that climate shade is not a comfort, it is survival. The psalms use the word for God's
protection: *the LORD is your shade at your right hand*, and *under the shadow of Your wings*.

## The taste

*And **his fruit was sweet to my taste**.*

The Hebrew is **matoq lechikki** — sweet to my palate.

The same word used of the scroll Ezekiel ate, and of the word in Psalm 119. See the note on
Ezekiel 3:1–3.

## The two things a fruit tree gives

Shade and fruit. Rest and food.

She names them in that order — she sat first.

## The house of wine

*He brought me to **the house of wine**.*

The Hebrew is **bet hayyayin** — literally the house of wine. A banqueting hall, or a wine
cellar.

The verb is **hevi'ani** — he brought me. She is taken there.

## The banner

*And **his banner over me was love**.*

The Hebrew is **vediglo alai ahavah**.

**Degel** — a standard, an ensemble, the flag a division of an army camped under. Numbers uses it
of the tribes: *everyone of the children of Israel shall camp by his own standard*.

## What a banner does

It identifies whose you are, from a distance, to everybody.

She is under one, and it says love.

## What she asks for next

*Sustain me with cakes of raisins, refresh me with apples, **for I am lovesick**.*

*His left hand is under my head, and his right hand embraces me.*

And then the refrain that runs three times through the book: *do not stir up nor awaken love
until it pleases*.

## The voice

*The voice of my beloved! Behold, **he comes leaping upon the mountains, skipping upon the
hills**.*

*Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away.* See the note on Song of Songs 2:10–13.

The winter is past, the rain is over and gone, and the fig tree is putting out green figs.

## What she says about belonging

*My beloved is mine, and I am his.*

And later, reversed: *I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine*.

And at the end, only the one direction: *I am my beloved's, and his desire is toward me*.

The Song moves the pronouns three times and lands on his desire.

> **The two verbs.** *I longed to sit, and there I sat.* Nothing comes between them, which is the
> whole point of the line.
