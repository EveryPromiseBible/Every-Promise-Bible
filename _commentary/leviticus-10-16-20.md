Moses is angry about a technical breach, and a grieving father talks him out of it.

## What day this is

The day Nadab and Abihu died. Their bodies were carried out of the camp that morning, and Aaron
and his two remaining sons were told not to mourn publicly. See the note on
Leviticus 10:12–15.

The chapter has not moved on. This is still the same afternoon.

## The breach

*When Moses inquired about **the goat of the sin offering** and found that **it had already been
burned up**, he was angry.*

The rule was plain, and Moses states it: *it was given to you **to take away the guilt of the
community**… Since its blood was not brought inside the Holy Place, **you should have eaten it
there, as I commanded**.*

The priests were to eat the sin offering. Their eating was part of how the guilt was carried.
They burned it instead, and Moses is right that it was not what he had said.

## Who he is angry with

*Eleazar and Ithamar, **Aaron's remaining sons**.*

He goes at the two young men who had buried their brothers that morning.

## The answer

*But **Aaron** replied.*

The father steps in front of his sons and takes the question himself.

*"Today they offered their sin offering and their burnt offering before the LORD, **yet such
things as these have happened to me**. Would the LORD have been pleased if I had eaten the sin
offering today?"*

He does not dispute the rule and he does not claim an exemption. He asks a question about what
God is like.

## What he is really saying

That God would not have wanted a man to sit down and eat a fellowship portion in the sanctuary a
few hours after his two eldest sons were carried out dead.

He had held his peace all day — *and Aaron held his peace* is the whole of his response to the
deaths. This is the only thing he says in the chapter.

## The verdict

*And when Moses heard this, **he was satisfied**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyitav be'enav** — and it was good in his eyes.

No penalty. No correction. No sacrifice required to cover the irregularity. The letter of the
instruction was not kept, and the case is closed by a grieving man asking whether God would have
liked it.

## What that tells you about the God of Leviticus

Twenty-six chapters of exact procedure — cubits, portions, which finger, how many times — and
here the procedure yields to a father who could not eat.

*A bruised reed He will not break, and smoking flax He will not quench.*

*For He knows our frame; **He remembers that we are dust**.*

## The one Aaron could not be

He carried the guilt of the community in a portion of meat he could not swallow that day.

*But this Man, after He had offered **one sacrifice for sins forever**, sat down at the right hand
of God.* See the note on Hebrews 9:11–12.

> **How it ends.** Not with a ruling, a fine or a sacrifice. Aaron asks whether God would have
> been pleased, and Moses — who wrote the rule down — decides that he would not have been.
