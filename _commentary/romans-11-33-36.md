Eleven chapters of argument end, and Paul stops arguing.

## Where this comes

The end of Romans 9 to 11 — the hardest stretch in the letter, on Israel, election, and the
Gentiles.

*For God has committed them all to disobedience, **that He might have mercy on all**.*

The doxology follows immediately.

## The exclamation

*Oh, **the depth of the riches and the wisdom and the knowledge of God**!*

The Greek is **o bathos ploutou kai sophias kai gnoseos Theou**.

Three genitives. The Greek can read *the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge*, or
three separate things. Translators divide.

## The two adjectives

*How far past our searching are **His judgments**, and **His paths** — no one can trace where He has
walked!*

The Greek is **anexeraunetos** — unsearchable, past finding out.

**Anexichniastos** — untraceable. From **ichnos**, a footprint. Nobody can follow the tracks.

Both words are rare, and both are compounds Paul may have coined.

## The three questions

*For **who has known the mind of the Lord?***

Isaiah 40:13. See the note on Isaiah 40:12–14.

*Or **who has been His counselor?***

Same verse.

*Or **who has ever given Him anything first, so that it would have to be paid back?***

Job 41:11: *who has preceded Me, that I should pay him?*

## What the three rule out

Knowing His mind. Advising Him. Putting Him under obligation.

Information, counsel, and credit. Three ways a person might have standing with God, and Paul
closes all of them with quotations.

## The reason

*For **from Him and through Him and to Him are all things**.*

The Greek is **ex autou kai di' autou kai eis auton ta panta**.

Three prepositions: source, agency, and goal.

Everything comes out of Him, holds together by Him, and is heading toward Him — which is why
nobody can put Him in their debt.

## The parallel

*Yet for us there is one God, the Father, **of whom are all things**… and one Lord Jesus Christ,
**through whom are all things**.*

*For **by Him all things were created**… **and He is before all things, and in Him all things
consist**.* See the note on Colossians 1:15–20.

## The last line

*To Him be **the glory forever**. Amen.*

The Greek is **auto he doxa eis tous aionas** — to Him the glory unto the ages.

## What follows

*I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, **that you present your bodies a living
sacrifice**, holy, acceptable to God, which is your reasonable service.* See the note on
Romans 12:1–2.

The **therefore** carries eleven chapters.

Paul's ethics start where his theology stops, and the hinge is a doxology.

## What the doxology does to the argument

He does not resolve the tension in chapters 9 to 11.

Election and human responsibility, Israel's hardening and Israel's future, mercy on all — he
states them all and then says the paths cannot be traced.

The praise is not a substitute for the argument. It is what the argument arrives at.

## The other closing

*Now to Him who is able to establish you according to my gospel… **to God, alone wise, be glory
through Jesus Christ forever**. Amen.* See the note on Romans 16:25–27.

Two doxologies, at the end of the theology and at the end of the letter.

> **The three questions.** Known His mind, advised Him, given Him something first — information,
> counsel, and credit. Paul closes all three with quotations, and then says why.
