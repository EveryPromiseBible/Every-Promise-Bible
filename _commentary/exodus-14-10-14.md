Water in front, an army behind, and Moses tells them to stop moving.

## Where they were

God had brought them there. Two chapters back He had told Moses to have them turn
and camp by the sea — deliberately, in a place with no exit, *for Pharaoh will
say, they are wandering in confusion.*

The trap was arranged. They were exactly where they had been led.

## What they said

*"**Were there no graves in Egypt**, that you brought us to the wilderness to
die?"*

It is bitter and it is sarcastic. And then: *"Better to serve them than to die in
the desert."*

Within days of coming out, they are asking to go back in. The mind had not left
Egypt when the body did.

## What they had just done

*They were terrified **and cried out to the LORD**.*

The text records both. The terror and the crying out are in the same sentence,
and neither is criticised.

## Four instructions

*"Do not be afraid. **Stand still**, and **watch** the salvation the LORD will
work for you today… The **LORD will fight for you**. You need only to **be
still**."*

Every one of them is a command to stop.

The Hebrew for *stand still* is to take a position and hold it — plant yourself.
And *be still* is the verb for silence: hold your peace.

They are given nothing to do. Not a weapon, not a formation, not a plan.

## The timing

*"These Egyptians you see today, **you will never see again**."*

The rescue arrives at the last possible hour, with the chariots visible on the
horizon. It could have come at any point in the previous three days. It comes
when they can see the dust.

The sea does not open until they are standing on the shore of it with nowhere
else to be.

## The line the Illumination adds

*The battle is not yours to win. God will fight, and you may rest.*

It is what Moses has just said, put plainly. And it is the whole difference
between this passage and every strategy a frightened person reaches for.

> **What He says to Moses next.** *Why are you crying out to Me? Tell the
> Israelites to go forward.* The stillness is not passivity — it is that the
> fighting is His. When the word comes to move, they move into a road that was not
> there a minute earlier.
