The wall is finished, and Nehemiah looks at a city that is still mostly empty.

## The problem

*The city was **large and spacious, but few people lived in it**, and the houses
had not yet been rebuilt.*

He has spent fifty-two days rebuilding the walls against organised opposition, with
a trowel in one hand and a weapon in the other.

And now there is a defended city with almost nobody inside it.

## The sentence

*So **my God put it into my heart** to gather the nobles and the rulers and the
people to be registered by families.*

The Hebrew is *my God gave into my heart*. It is Nehemiah's characteristic phrase —
he uses it earlier in the book too, of the work on the wall.

He does not report a vision, a prophet or a voice. He reports a thought arriving,
and he says where it came from.

## What it looked like from inside

Like his own idea. Like an administrative instinct — a governor deciding a census
is the sensible next step.

Nehemiah's habit through the whole book is to name the source of his instincts as
he goes. *The good hand of my God was upon me.* *What my God had put in my heart to
do for Jerusalem — I told no one.*

## What came of it

*And **I found the record of the genealogy** of those who had come back first.*

He set out to organise a registration and found an existing document — the list of
the first return under Zerubbabel, ninety years earlier.

The chapter that follows is that list, name by name, family by family, down to the
singers, the gatekeepers and the temple servants. The same list appears in Ezra 2.

## Why the record mattered

The genealogies decided who could serve as a priest — the chapter goes on to
describe men who *sought their listing among those who had been registered by
genealogy, but it was not found, so they were excluded from the priesthood.*

A thought in a governor's heart, and a census that recovered a lost document and
settled who could stand at an altar.

## What he does with it next

Chapter 8. *All the people gathered together as one man… and Ezra the priest
brought the Law before the assembly*, and read it from morning until midday, and
the Levites *gave the sense, and helped them to understand the reading.*

The registration led straight to the Book being opened in the square.

> **What he was before this.** Cupbearer to Artaxerxes — a servant with a wine cup,
> in a Persian palace, four months' journey from a ruined city he had never seen.
> The first thing recorded of him is that he asked how Jerusalem was, and sat down
> and wept.
