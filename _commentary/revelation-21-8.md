A list of eight, and the order is not the one anybody would predict.

## Where it sits

In the middle of the best news in the book.

*And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow,
nor crying. There shall be no more pain, for the former things have passed away.*

*Behold, I make all things new.*

*I will give of the fountain of the water of life freely to him who thirsts. **He who overcomes
shall inherit all things, and I will be his God and he shall be My son**.*

And then verse 8.

## The first two

*But **the cowards**, and **the ones who would not believe**.*

The Greek is **deilois** — the fearful, the timid. And **apistois** — unbelieving, faithless.

These head the list. Before the murderers, before the sorcerers, before the idolaters.

**Deilos** is not the ordinary word for fear. It occurs three times in the New Testament, and
one of the others is in the boat: *why are you so **fearful**? How is it that you have no
faith?* See the note on Mark 4:35–41.

The same pairing there — fearful, and no faith.

## What the word is not

Not the fear anybody feels. Scripture is full of frightened people who are commended.

The word describes the fear that governs — that decides. In a book written to churches under
Roman pressure, it means the man who will not say the name when asked.

## The remaining six

*The ones whose lives are **vile**.*

**Ebdelugmenois** — the abominable, from the root of **bdelugma**, the abomination.

*The **murderers**.*

*The **sexually immoral**.*

**Pornois** — the standard word.

*The ones who **deal in magic**.*

**Pharmakois** — sorcerers, and the word behind *pharmacy*. Drugs and spells were the same
trade.

*The ones who **worship idols**.*

*And **every liar**.*

The Greek is **pasin tois pseudesin** — all the liars.

Ending on the smallest-sounding item, and *every*.

## The verdict

*Their part is in **the lake that burns with fire and sulfur**. That is **the second death**.*

The Greek is **to meros auton** — their portion, their share.

The same word used of an inheritance. The previous verse had *he who overcomes shall inherit
all things*, and this one assigns a different portion.

## The second death

Revelation defines it three times, and this is one of them.

*Blessed and holy is he who has part in the first resurrection. **Over such the second death has
no power**.*

## Who is being written to

Seven churches, most of them under pressure, some of them being killed.

*Do not fear any of those things which you are about to suffer… be faithful until death, and I
will give you the crown of life.*

The letter to Smyrna, and the two words in it are the two at the head of this list, in
reverse.

## What the chapter goes on to

The city. Twelve gates, twelve foundations, a wall of jasper, a street of gold, and no
temple — *for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple*.

*And there shall be no night there: they need no lamp nor light of the sun, for the Lord God
gives them light.*

## The invitation two chapters later

*And let him who thirsts come. Whoever desires, **let him take the water of life freely**.* See
the note on Revelation 22:12–13.

The book's last invitation is unrestricted, and it is four verses from the end.

> **What heads the list.** Not murder. The fearful and the unbelieving — written to
> congregations who were being asked, under threat, whether they knew Him.
