Two verses, and the shortest of them names who did it.

## Who struck him

*So **the adversary** went out from the presence of the LORD and **struck Job**
with painful boils.*

The sentence names the agent. It was not God who put the boils on him — the text
says the accuser went out and did it.

That is worth holding on to, because Job spends the next thirty-five chapters
convinced it was God, and his friends spend the same thirty-five chapters
agreeing with him about that and only arguing over why.

The reader knows something the whole cast does not.

## "From the soles of his feet to the crown of his head"

The phrase is a merism — the two ends stated to mean everything in between. No
part of him was untouched.

Deuteronomy uses the identical phrase for a specific curse: *the LORD will strike
you with painful boils… from the sole of your foot to the top of your head.* Any
reader who knew that verse would draw the conclusion Job's friends draw.

The book puts that conclusion in the reader's hand and then spends forty chapters
showing it is wrong in this case.

## The pottery

*Job took a **piece of broken pottery** to scrape himself.*

The greatest man in all the East, sitting with a shard of a broken pot.

He had seven thousand sheep a chapter ago. What he has now is a fragment of
something that was already thrown away.

## The ashes

*And he **sat down among the ashes**.*

The ash heap outside a town was the rubbish dump, where the sweepings and the
burnt refuse went. It is where the destitute sat.

## What he still had

His wife tells him to curse God and die. His three friends arrive, do not
recognise him from a distance, and sit with him on the ground for seven days
without a word.

*And no one spoke a word to him, for they saw that his suffering was very great.*

The seven silent days are the best thing the three of them do in the entire book.

> **What he says at the end of chapter 2.** *Shall we accept good from God, and not
> trouble?* And the writer adds: *in all this Job did not sin with his lips.* He is
> holding, and he is holding without knowing one thing about why.
