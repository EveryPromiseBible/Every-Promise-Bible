Paul's account of how a civilisation ends up worshipping animals, and it starts with a failure to say thank you.

## Where this comes

*For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ, for **it is the power of God to salvation**… For in it
**the righteousness of God is revealed from believing to believing**.* See the note on
Romans 1:16–17.

And then, immediately: *for the wrath of God is revealed*.

Two revelations, in consecutive sentences, using the same verb.

## The wrath

*From heaven **the wrath of God is being revealed**.*

The Greek is **apokaluptetai** — present tense, continuous.

Not stored up for later. Being disclosed now.

## Against what

*Every kind of **godlessness and wrongdoing** of people who **hold the truth down and keep it from
being heard**.*

The Greek is **katechonton** — holding down, suppressing, restraining.

Something is pressing upward and being held under.

## What is available

*Whatever can be known of God **lies open before them**, because **God Himself has made it plain to
them**.*

The Greek is **ho Theos gar autois ephanerosen** — God manifested it to them.

The disclosure is His act.

## Through what

*Ever since the world was made, His **invisible qualities** — His **eternal power** and **His divine
nature** — have been **clearly seen, understood through the things He has made**.*

The Greek is **ta aorata… kathoratai** — the invisible things are clearly seen. A deliberate
paradox.

**Nooumena** — being understood by the mind.

## The verdict

*So that **no one has any excuse**.*

The Greek is **anapologetous** — without a defence, without an apology in the legal sense.

The same word Paul uses at the start of chapter 2, of the person who judges others.

## The two failures

*They knew God — and still they would not **give Him the honor that is His**, or **so much as thank
Him**.*

The Greek is **ouch hos Theon edoxasan e eucharistesan** — they did not glorify Him as God, nor
give thanks.

## What the sequence is

Not atheism. Knowledge, followed by a refusal to honour and a refusal to thank.

Paul names ingratitude as the second step in the sequence that ends in idolatry.

## What follows

*Their thinking **drifted into emptiness**, and their **undiscerning hearts went dark**.*

The Greek is **emataiothesan** — were made futile, empty. And **eskotisthe** — was darkened.

Both passive. It happened to them.

## The reversal

*They **advertised themselves as wise, and turned into fools**.*

The Greek is **phaskontes einai sophoi emoranthesan** — claiming to be wise, they were made
foolish.

**Moraino** — the root of *moron*.

## The exchange

*And they **traded the glory of the immortal God** for **images shaped like mortal man, and like birds
and animals and creatures that crawl on the ground**.*

The Greek is **ellaxan** — exchanged.

The list descends: man, birds, quadrupeds, reptiles.

## The three handings-over

*Therefore **God also gave them up** to uncleanness.*

*For this reason **God gave them up** to vile passions.*

*And even as they did not like to retain God in their knowledge, **God gave them over** to a debased
mind.*

The Greek is **paredoken** — three times.

The judgment described is being allowed to have what was chosen.

## Where the argument is going

*Therefore you are inexcusable, O man, whoever you are who judge.* See the note on
Romans 2:6–11.

Chapter 1 closes the case against the pagan world, and chapter 2 turns on the reader who has been
agreeing.

## Where it lands

*For **all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God**, being justified freely by His grace.* See
the note on Romans 3:23–24.

> **The second step.** Not disbelief — *they would not give Him the honour, or so much as thank
> Him*. Paul puts ingratitude in the sequence that ends in a statue of a reptile.
