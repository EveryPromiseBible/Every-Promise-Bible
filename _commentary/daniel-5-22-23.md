The charge is not ignorance. Daniel's word to him is *though you knew all this*.

## The night

A thousand nobles, a banquet, and a hand writing on the plaster of the wall.

Belshazzar's knees knock together. The wise men cannot read it. The queen remembers a man from the
previous reign.

## The setup

Before the charge, Daniel spends five verses on Nebuchadnezzar: the greatness given him, the pride,
the grass, the restoration. See the note on Daniel 4:34–35.

Then the turn.

## The charge

*"And you, Belshazzar, his son, **you have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this**."*

The Aramaic is **kol-qovel di kol-denah yeda'ta** — inasmuch as you knew all this.

Nebuchadnezzar published his own account. It was in the archives, and the grandson had read it.

## What he did instead

*"You **lifted yourself up against the Lord of heaven**."*

The Aramaic is **hitromamta** — you exalted yourself.

## The specific act

*"**The vessels of His house were brought before you**, and you and your nobles, your wives and your
concubines, **drank wine from them**."*

The vessels his grandfather took from the temple in Jerusalem and put in a treasury.

Nebuchadnezzar stored them. Belshazzar drinks out of them.

## What that is

Not theft — the theft was two generations old. Deliberate desecration, performed in front of a
thousand people, of the vessels of a defeated God.

## What they toasted

*"You **praised gods of silver and gold, of bronze and iron, of wood and stone**."*

Five materials. The metals of the statue in chapter 2, with wood and stone added.

## The clause about them

*"Which **do not see or hear or know anything**."*

The Aramaic is **la chazayin vela shame'in vela yad'in**.

The psalm behind it: *they have mouths, but they do not speak; eyes, but they do not see… those
who make them are like them*. See the note on Psalms 115:4–8.

## The contrast

*"But **the God who holds your breath in His hand and owns all your ways**, Him you have not
honoured."*

The Aramaic is **nishmetak bideh** — your breath in His hand. And **vekol-orchatak leh** — and all
your ways, His.

## The reversal

The gods he praised cannot see, hear or know. The God he ignored is holding his breathing.

Daniel does not argue that the idols are inferior. He says the King's next inhalation is in
somebody else's hand.

## The writing

*Mene, Mene, Tekel, Upharsin.*

Numbered, numbered, weighed, divided. Three of the four are commercial weights.

*You have been **weighed in the balances and found wanting**.* See the note on Daniel 5:24–25.

## That same night

*That very night Belshazzar, king of the Chaldeans, was slain.*

The breath in the hand, and the hand closed before morning.

## Where the phrase goes

*In Him we live and move and have our being.* Paul, on the Areopagus, to a city of statues. See
the note on Acts 17:26–28.

And *He is before all things, and in Him all things consist*. See the note on Colossians 1:15–20.

> **The inventory.** He praised gods that cannot see, hear or know — while the God who was holding
> his breath went unmentioned all evening.
