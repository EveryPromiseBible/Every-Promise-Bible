The moment the church stopped being a Jewish movement, and it happens before the
sermon finishes.

## The interruption

*Peter was still speaking when the Holy Spirit fell on everyone who was
listening.*

He is cut off mid-sentence. Nothing has been required of these people — no
baptism, no circumcision, no probation, no instruction on keeping the law.

Luke tells us what Peter had just said: *everyone who believes in Him receives
forgiveness of sins through His name.* The Spirit falls on that sentence.

## Who was astounded

*The believers of the circumcision who had come with Peter were astounded that
the gift of the Holy Spirit had been poured out on the Gentiles as well.*

Not the Gentiles. The Jewish Christians who had travelled with him — men who had
needed a vision on a rooftop and a direct instruction to walk into that house at
all.

They believed in Jesus and were still astonished at this.

## Peter's question

*Can anyone refuse water to keep these people from being baptized, when they have
received the Holy Spirit just as we did?*

The logic runs backwards from what God has already done. The evidence came first;
the church's practice has to catch up with it.

It is the argument Peter will use again in Jerusalem when he is called to account
for eating with Gentiles — *if God gave them the same gift, who was I to think I
could stand in God's way?*

## The last line

*Afterward they asked him to stay on with them a few days.*

A Jewish apostle stays in a Roman officer's house. In the culture of the time
that sentence is nearly as significant as the Spirit falling.

> **How the chapter opens.** Cornelius is described as devout, God-fearing,
> generous and prayerful — and none of it saved him; an angel still had to send
> for Peter. Luke gives him every religious credential available and then has the
> gospel arrive anyway, unearned.
