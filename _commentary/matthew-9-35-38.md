A summary of everything He had been doing, and then a sentence about what He felt looking
at the crowd.

## The three things

*Teaching in their synagogues, **telling the good news of the kingdom**, and **healing every
disease and every weakness**.*

Matthew gives the same three-part summary in chapter 4, before the Sermon on the Mount.
This one closes the section, and the two summaries bracket everything in between.

## The scope

*All through **the towns and the villages**.*

*Every disease and every weakness* — the Greek is **pasan noson kai pasan malakian**, and
Matthew repeats *every* twice.

## What moved

*And when He looked at the crowds, **something moved deep inside Him**.*

The Greek is **esplanchnisthe** — from **splanchna**, the inward parts, the guts.

It is the strongest word for compassion in the language, and in the Gospels it is used
almost exclusively of Jesus. The exceptions are three parable characters: the father
seeing his son a long way off, the Samaritan seeing the man in the road, and the master
forgiving the debt.

## What He saw

*Because they were **worn ragged and thrown down**.*

The Greek is **eskulmenoi** — flayed, harassed, torn — and **errimmenoi**, thrown down,
cast aside like something discarded.

## The comparison

*Like **sheep with no shepherd anywhere**.*

The phrase is old. Moses used it asking for a successor: *that the congregation of the
LORD may not be **like sheep which have no shepherd***.

And Ezekiel, on Israel's leaders: *My sheep wandered through all the mountains… and no one
was seeking or searching for them.*

Israel had shepherds. There were priests, scribes, elders and Pharisees in every town he
walked through.

Matthew says He looked at the crowd and saw no shepherd.

## The turn

*"**The harvest is enormous, but the workers are few**."*

He changes the picture mid-sentence — from sheep to a field.

Sheep need a shepherd; a harvest needs hands. The crowd is both things in two verses.

## The instruction

*"So **beg the Lord of the harvest to send workers out into His harvest field**."*

The Greek for *beg* is **deomai** — to plead out of need.

**Send out** is **ekbale** — to thrust out, drive out. It is a forceful word; Matthew uses
it of casting out demons.

## What happens next

Chapter 10, verse 1: *and when He had called His twelve disciples to Him, He gave them
power.*

They are told to pray for workers and are immediately sent as the answer.

The chapter break obscures it — the sending follows the instruction with nothing in
between.

## Whose harvest

*His harvest.* The field belongs to the one being asked.

> **What He was looking at.** Crowds that had teachers, courts, synagogues and a temple —
> and the word Matthew uses is that they were thrown down, like sheep with nobody over
> them.
