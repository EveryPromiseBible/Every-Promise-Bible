Seven questions with no answers, and the New Testament quotes the hardest of them twice.

## The measurements

*Who has **measured the waters in the hollow of His hand**, or **marked off the sky with the span
of His fingers**?*

The Hebrew is **sha'al** — a handful, the water a cupped palm holds. And **zeret** — the span from
thumb to little finger, about nine inches.

The oceans in a cupped hand and the sky measured with an outstretched hand.

*Who has **held the dust of the earth in a basket**, or **weighed the mountains on scales**?*

The unit for the dust is **shalish** — a third of some larger measure, a small dry-goods
container.

Isaiah takes the four largest things anybody could name — sea, sky, earth, mountains — and puts
each into a kitchen implement.

## The turn

*Who has **fathomed the Spirit of the LORD**, or stood beside Him as His counsellor? Whom did He
ask for advice? **Who taught Him the right way**, who showed Him understanding?*

Three questions about creation, then four about His mind. The second set is the harder one, and
the expected answer to all seven is nobody.

## Who quotes it

*"For **who has known the mind of the LORD? Or who has become His counselor?** Or who has first
given to Him and it shall be repaid to him?" For of Him and through Him and to Him are all
things.*

**Paul** ends **Romans** 11 with it — eleven chapters of argument about mercy, and he closes by
quoting the unanswerable question rather than summarising.

## And who quotes it again

*For "**who has known the mind of the LORD that he may instruct Him?**" **But we have the mind of
Christ**.*

**1 Corinthians** 2, and the same verse gets the opposite treatment. **Paul** asks Isaiah's
question, concedes it, and then adds a sentence Isaiah could not have written.

Not that anyone has climbed up to God's understanding. That it was handed down.

*"**No longer do I call you servants**, for a servant does not know what his master is doing; but
I have called you friends, **for all things that I heard from My Father I have made known to
you**."*

## The counsellor question, answered elsewhere

*And His name will be called **Wonderful, Counselor**.*

Isaiah asks who advised God and, nine chapters earlier, has already put the word in a name.

## Why the chapter asks any of this

*Comfort, comfort My people.*

The measurements are not there to make a reader feel small. They are the argument for the
promise at the end of the chapter — *those who wait on the LORD shall renew their strength.* See
the note on Isaiah 40:29–31.

The size of Him is the reason the tired are told to expect something.

> **The one Paul answers.** *Who has known the mind of the LORD?* He quotes it in Romans and
> leaves it standing; he quotes it in 1 Corinthians and adds five words — *but we have the mind of
> Christ.*
