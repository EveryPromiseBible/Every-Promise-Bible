title: "Throw me into the sea"

Pagan sailors refuse to do what the prophet tells them, and row instead.

## The question

*"**What must we do to you**, so that the water will grow calm for us?"*

They ask him. They have already cast lots, established that the storm is his, and heard him say *I
am a Hebrew, and I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land* — and their
response is to ask his advice.

## The answer

*"**Pick me up and throw me into the sea, and it will grow calm. I know this storm has come upon you
because of me.**"*

It is the one moment in the first half of the book where Jonah tells the truth about himself.

## What the sailors do

*"Still the men would not. They **bent to the oars and tried to row back to the land**, but they
could not."*

The Hebrew is **vayyachterru** — and they dug, they rowed hard.

Foreign sailors, on a ship they are about to lose, working to save the man whose god is sinking
them. The narrative is quietly making a comparison, and it is not in the prophet's favour.

## Their prayer

*"**Please, LORD, do not let us die for taking this man's life. Do not hold his innocent blood
against us.**"*

They use the covenant name. And **dam naqi** — innocent blood.

*When Pilate saw that he could not prevail at all… he took water and washed his hands before the
multitude, saying, "**I am innocent of the blood of this just Person.**"*

**Matthew** 27, and it is the same sentence in the mouth of a Roman governor: a man outside the
covenant, disclaiming responsibility for a death he is about to authorise.

The sailors mean it. Pilate does not, and the crowd answers *His blood be on us.*

## One man for the ship

*"Throw me into the sea, and it will grow calm."*

*"**Nor do you consider that it is expedient for us that one man should die for the people**, and
not that the whole nation should perish."*

**John** 11, said by Caiaphas — and **John** adds that *he did not say this on his own authority;
but being high priest that year he prophesied.*

The arithmetic is the same. One man overboard, and the storm stops.

## Where the difference is

Jonah is the reason for the storm. He offers himself because the sentence is his.

*Who Himself bore our sins in His own body on the tree.*

*For **Christ also suffered once for sins, the just for the unjust**, that He might bring us to
God.*

**Peter**'s phrase is **dikaios hyper adikon** — righteous on behalf of unrighteous. Jonah goes into
the sea as the guilty man; the comparison **Jesus** draws to him is about three days, not about
innocence.

## What happens to the sailors

*"Then they lifted Jonah up and threw him into the sea, and the raging waters grew still. And the
men were filled with a deep and quiet fear of the LORD. **They offered a sacrifice to Him and made
their promises to Him.**"*

The first conversions in the book are the crew. Jonah has not preached a sermon yet.

> **What the sailors do first.** Row. They are told the remedy, refuse it, and try to save the man
> who caused the storm — and when they finally do it, they pray about his blood before they lift
> him.
