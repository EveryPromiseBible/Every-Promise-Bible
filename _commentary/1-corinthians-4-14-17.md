Four chapters of correction, and then he tells them why he wrote it.

## What he says he is not doing

*I am **not writing these things to shame you**, but to warn you as **my beloved children**.*

The Greek is **entrepon** — to make ashamed, to turn a face away.

He has just finished a paragraph of heavy irony: *you are already full, you are already rich,
you have reigned as kings without us*, and *we are fools for Christ's sake, but you are wise*.

Then he stops and says the point was never humiliation.

## The word for warn

**Nouthetein** — to put in mind, to admonish. It is the word for what a parent does, and it is
paired here with **beloved children**.

## The comparison

*For even if you had **ten thousand guardians** to keep you in line in Christ, **you do not have
many fathers**.*

The Greek is **murious paidagogous** — ten thousand pedagogues.

A **paidagogos** was a household slave who walked a boy to school, watched his conduct, and had
authority to discipline him. Not the teacher. The escort.

Paul uses the same word of the law: *the law was our tutor to bring us to Christ*. See the note
on Galatians 3:23–25.

## What the contrast is

Guardians can be multiplied. Fathers cannot.

The Corinthians had plenty of instruction available — Apollos, Cephas, and whoever else the
factions were forming around. Paul does not deny any of them their competence. He says the
category is different.

## The claim

*It was **in Christ Jesus that I became your father through the good news**.*

The Greek is **egennesa** — I begot you.

He grounds the claim in the gospel, not in office. He was there when it happened.

## The request

*So I beg you, **be imitators of me**.*

The Greek is **parakalo** — I urge, I appeal. Not a command from a position.

He repeats it later with the qualification: *imitate me, just as I also imitate Christ*.

## Why Timothy

*That is why I have sent Timothy to you, **my beloved and faithful child in the Lord**.*

The same two words he has just used of the Corinthians — beloved, child.

Paul addresses Timothy this way in both letters to him: *my own true son in the faith*, and *a
son I love dearly*. See the note on 2 Timothy 1:1–2.

## What Timothy is for

*Who will **call to your mind my ways in Christ**, just as I teach them **everywhere in every
church**.*

The Greek is **anamnesei** — will remind you.

Nothing new is being brought. And the last clause is the check: the same teaching everywhere,
so Corinth cannot claim a private version.

## What the factions were

*Each of you says, "I am of Paul," or "I am of Apollos," or "I am of Cephas," or "I am of
Christ."* See the note on 1 Corinthians 1:10–17.

A church sorting itself by teacher. Paul's answer, four chapters later, is that they are sorting
by the wrong category.

## What he says about the workers

*I planted, Apollos watered, **but God gave the increase**.* See the note on
1 Corinthians 3:5–9.

*Neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase.*

He empties the office before he claims the fatherhood, which is what keeps the claim from being
a bid for a faction of his own.

> **The two categories.** Guardians escort and correct, and you can have ten thousand of them.
> Fathers beget, and Paul's claim is not to the office but to having been there when it happened.
