The apostle who leaned on His chest at supper falls down like a corpse.

## What he has just seen

Seven golden lampstands, and one like the Son of Man among them — hair white as snow, eyes like a
flame of fire, feet like fine brass, a voice like many waters, a sharp two-edged sword out of His
mouth, and *His countenance was like the sun shining in its strength*.

## The collapse

*The sight of Him **put me on the ground**. I went down at His feet and **lay there like a dead man**.*

The Greek is **epesa pros tous podas autou hos nekros** — I fell at His feet as dead.

## Who is falling

John. The disciple who had leaned back on His chest at the supper table and asked who the traitor
was.

Sixty years later, the same man cannot stand up.

## What Daniel did

*Therefore I was left alone when I saw this great vision, and no strength remained in me… **then I
fell into a deep sleep on my face, with my face to the ground**.*

And Ezekiel: *so when I saw it, I fell on my face*.

Nobody in Scripture stays upright.

## The hand

*Then He **laid His right hand on me**.*

The Greek is **etheken ten dexian autou ep' eme**.

The same right hand that held the seven stars, one verse earlier.

## What He says first

*Do not be afraid.*

The Greek is **me phobou**.

Before any title, before the commission, before the letters to the churches.

## The first title

*I am **the first and I am the last**.*

The Greek is **ho protos kai ho eschatos**.

Isaiah's phrase, spoken by God: *I am the First and I am the Last; besides Me there is no God*.

## The second

*I am **the One who is alive**.*

The Greek is **ho zon** — the living One.

The same construction the angels use at the tomb: *why do you seek **the Living One** among the
dead?* See the note on Luke 24:1–8.

## The three states

*I **was dead** — and look at Me, **I am alive**, and **I will be alive for all time to come**.*

The Greek is **egenomen nekros kai idou zon eimi eis tous aionas ton aionon**.

Was dead. Am alive. Will be alive to the ages of the ages.

Past, present, future — the same three-part shape as *who is and who was and who is to come*. See
the note on Revelation 1:8.

## The keys

*The **keys to hell and to death** are in My hand.*

The Greek is **tas kleis tou thanatou kai tou hadou** — the keys of death and of Hades.

**Hades** — the realm of the dead, the Septuagint's word for **Sheol**.

## What a key means

Authority to open and to shut.

Whoever holds the keys decides who goes in and who comes out. The claim is that the doors are no
longer under their own management.

## Where the keys turn up again

*And **Death and Hades delivered up the dead who were in them**.*

*Then Death and Hades were cast into the lake of fire.* See the note on
Revelation 20:13–15.

## What He says next

*"**Write the things which you have seen, and the things which are, and the things which will take
place after this**."* See the note on Revelation 1:19–20.

The commission comes after the hand and the *do not be afraid*.

## What He had said before, on a mountain

*But Jesus came and touched them and said, "**Arise, and do not be afraid**."*

The transfiguration. Peter, James and John *fell on their faces and were greatly afraid*.

The same hand, the same words, and John was there for both.

> **The order.** The hand first, then *do not be afraid*, then the titles. Nothing is explained
> until the man on the floor has been touched.
