The bleakest paragraph in Isaiah, and the three chapters around it are why it is bearable.

## The three words

*Terror and **the pit** and **the snare**.*

The Hebrew is **pachad va-pachat va-pach** — three words that rhyme. Isaiah is making a sound, not
just a list, and the sound is a trap closing.

*Whoever flees from the sound of the terror will **fall into the pit**. Whoever climbs out of the
pit will be **caught in the snare**.*

Three exits, all of them into each other.

## The verbs

*The earth is **broken open**, the earth is **torn apart**, the earth is **shaken violently**.*

Three again, in the same construction, and the Hebrew stacks them with the same root sounds:
**ro'ah hitro'a'ah, por hitporerah, mot hitmotetah**.

## The shaking

*The foundations of the earth are shaking… it **falls, and it will not rise again**.*

*"Yet once more **I shake not only the earth, but also heaven**." Now this, "Yet once more,"
indicates the removal of those things that are being shaken… **that the things which cannot be
shaken may remain**. Therefore, since we are receiving a kingdom which cannot be shaken, let us
have grace.*

**Hebrews** 12 quotes the prophets on the shaking and draws the opposite conclusion from despair.
The point of a shaking is the discovery of what does not move — and the sentence ends with the
word *grace*.

## What the same section says four verses on

Isaiah 24 to 27 is one unit, and this paragraph is inside it.

*And on this mountain He will destroy the covering cast over all people… **He will swallow up
death forever**, and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from all faces.*

*"Death is swallowed up in victory." "O Death, where is your sting?"… **thanks be to God, who
gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ**.*

**Paul** quotes Isaiah 25:8 in **1 Corinthians** 15 as the last word on the subject. The chapter
that says the earth falls and will not rise is four verses from the promise that death itself is
the thing swallowed.

## And the same section on the resurrection

*Your dead shall live; together with my dead body they shall arise. **Awake and sing, you who
dwell in dust!***

Isaiah 26:19, in the same little apocalypse.

## Who came out of the pit

*He lifted me out of **the pit** of ruin, out of the miry clay. He set my feet upon a rock.* See
the note on Psalms 40:1–3.

*Our soul has escaped as a bird from **the snare** of the fowlers; the snare is broken, and we
have escaped.*

Isaiah names the pit and the snare as inescapable. The psalms name both as things somebody was
lifted out of, and **Hebrews** says why: *that through death He might destroy him who had the
power of death.*

> **What the shaking is for.** Hebrews quotes it and reads it forward: the things that can be
> shaken go so that what cannot be shaken is seen. And the conclusion drawn from the most
> frightening image in the prophets is a single word — *let us have grace*.
