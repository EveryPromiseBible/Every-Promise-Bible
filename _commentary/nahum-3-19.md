key: Nahum 3:19
title: "There is no healing for your wound"
updated: 2026-08-09

The last verse of the book, and it ends without a promise.

## The sentence

*"**There is no healing for your wound. The blow is fatal.**"*

The Hebrew is **ein-kehah leshivrekha** — there is no easing for your fracture. **Nachlah
makkatekha** — your wound is grievous, incurable.

## What that is not

Every other prophetic book against a nation leaves a door somewhere. Isaiah has Egypt worshipping
alongside Israel. Jeremiah promises to restore the fortunes of Moab, Ammon and Elam. Even Obadiah
ends with the kingdom belonging to the LORD.

Nahum ends with a wound that does not close, and Nineveh was never rebuilt. The site was so
thoroughly lost that when Xenophon marched an army past the mound two hundred years later he did
not know what it was.

## The clapping

*"Everyone who hears the news of you **claps their hands with relief**, for who has not felt your
endless cruelty, again and again, without end?"*

The last clause is the book's justification and it is a question. Nahum does not defend the verdict;
he asks whether anybody in the ancient world had been spared.

## The word

**Makkah** — a blow, a wound, a stroke.

*But **He was wounded** for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; the chastisement
for our peace was upon Him, and **by His stripes we are healed**.*

Isaiah's noun for the stripes is **chaburah** — a bruise, a welt. The one incurable thing in Nahum
is answered in the New Testament by a wound somebody else took.

*Who Himself bore our sins in His own body on the tree, that we, having died to sins, might live
for righteousness — **by whose stripes you were healed**.*

**Peter** quotes it in the past tense.

## The other incurable wound

*"For thus says the LORD: '**Your affliction is incurable, your wound is severe**… there is no one
to plead your cause, that you may be bound up; you have no healing medicines.'"*

Jeremiah says it about Judah — the same vocabulary, about God's own people.

And then, eleven verses later: *"**For I will restore health to you and heal you of your wounds**,
says the LORD, because they called you an outcast."*

The same diagnosis, and a different ending, and the difference is not in the patient.

## What the last verse leaves

Nahum stops. There is no *yet*, no *but*, and no verse 20.

The book that opened with a prophet called Comfort ends with a wound that does not heal, and the
comfort it contains is all in one verse near the front: *the LORD is good; He is a stronghold in
the day of trouble.* See the note on Nahum 1:7.

*"But **I say to you, love your enemies**, bless those who curse you, do good to those who hate
you."*

**Matthew** 5. Nahum's readers had watched Assyria for a century and the book gives them a verdict.
The Sermon on the Mount is addressed to people under a different empire, and it gives them
something else.

> **How it ends.** With no door. Every other oracle against a nation leaves one somewhere; this one
> does not, and Nineveh was never rebuilt. The only healing word in the book is the one Isaiah
> attaches to somebody else's wounds.
