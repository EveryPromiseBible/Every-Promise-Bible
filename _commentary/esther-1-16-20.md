The most powerful court in the world panics over one woman not coming to dinner,
and legislates.

## The escalation

*"Queen Vashti has not only wronged the king, but **every official and every
people in all the provinces**."*

Memucan takes a private embarrassment and makes it an imperial crisis in one
sentence. A hundred and twenty-seven provinces are now involved.

## What they were afraid of

*"Word of what the queen has done will get out to all the women, and they will
look down on their own husbands."*

The fear is of a story travelling. Not of Vashti — of what people will say about
her.

*"There will be no end of contempt and quarreling."* The men who ruled from India
to Cush are worried about arguments at home.

## The remedy

*"Let him send out a royal decree, written into the laws of Persia and Media **so
it cannot be revoked**."*

The irrevocability of Persian law is introduced here, casually, in chapter 1. It
will matter enormously in chapter 8, when a death sentence on a whole people
cannot be cancelled either.

The writer plants it early, in a trivial context, so that the reader already knows
the rule when everything depends on it.

## The sentence that makes room

*"Let the king give her royal place **to another woman better than she**."*

Memucan does not know what he has just said. He is drafting a decree about
household order, and the clause he adds at the end opens the throne of Persia.

Four chapters later a Jewish girl sits in that place, and the empire's entire
Jewish population is alive because of it.

## What is not in the chapter

Nobody prays. Nobody consults a prophet. Nobody mentions God.

A drunk king, a proud queen, and a nervous adviser between them arrange the one
thing that will be needed. See the note on Esther 1:9–12.

> **The last line of the decree.** *Then every wife will honor her husband, from
> the greatest to the least.* An empire-wide law was passed to make women
> respectful, and the historical result of it, so far as the book records, is that
> the Jews were not exterminated.
