Jesus opens by asking a Samaritan woman for a drink, which is the least likely
sentence in the chapter. He is thirsty and has nothing to draw with. The whole
exchange runs from a position of need.

## "If you knew the gift God is holding out"

Two things she does not know: *what* is being offered and *who* is doing the
offering. He names both as missing, then says what she would do if she had them —
*you would have asked Him*.

Not qualified, not prepared. Asked.

## Living water

In ordinary speech *living water* meant running water — a spring or a stream,
as opposed to still water in a cistern. Her reply takes it that way, which is
why she points out He has no bucket and the well is deep.

She is not being obtuse. She is answering the plain sense of the words, and John
lets the misunderstanding stand for four verses because the second meaning
arrives better after the first has been exhausted.

## What He promises

*Everyone who drinks this water will be thirsty again.* True of the well, and
true of everything else that has to be gone back to.

*The water I give will become a spring inside him.* The location changes. Not a
better well to keep returning to — a source relocated inside the person, welling
up.

> **Her answer.** *Give me this water, so that I will not be thirsty and will not
> have to keep coming all this way to draw.* She is still thinking about the
> walk, and He does not correct her or make her understand first. The next thing
> He says is about her husband, and the conversation goes somewhere else entirely.
> He answers people mid-misunderstanding.
