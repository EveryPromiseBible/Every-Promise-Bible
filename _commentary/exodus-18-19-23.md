The best management advice in the Bible, and it comes from a Midianite priest.

## Who is speaking

Jethro — Moses' father-in-law, priest of Midian, who has just arrived at the camp bringing
Moses' wife and two sons back to him.

He is not an Israelite. He hears what God has done, says *now I know that the LORD is greater
than all the gods*, and offers a burnt offering.

## What he saw

The day before: *Moses sat to judge the people, and the people stood before Moses from
morning until evening.*

And his question: *"**Why do you alone sit, and all the people stand before you from morning
until evening?**"*

Moses' answer: they come to me, and I judge, and I make known the statutes.

## The verdict

*"**The thing that you do is not good. Both you and these people who are with you will surely
wear yourselves out**. For this thing is too much for you; **you are not able to perform it
by yourself**."*

Blunt, and to the man who had just parted a sea.

## The opening

*"Listen to me, and let me give you some advice, **and may God be with you**."*

He offers counsel and immediately submits it to God's approval — and says so again at the
end.

## What Moses keeps

*"**You stand before God for the people** and bring their cases to him."*

*"**Teach them his decrees and instructions**, and **show them the way to live and the work
they are to do**."*

Two things: representing them upward, and teaching them.

Both are irreducible. Jethro does not propose delegating either.

## The qualifications

*"Look for **capable men** from among all the people — men who **fear God**, who are
**trustworthy** and **hate dishonest gain**."*

Four, and only the first is about ability.

**Hate dishonest gain** — the Hebrew is *hating covetousness*. The test is not whether a man
would take a bribe but whether the idea disgusts him.

## The structure

*"**Set them over the people as leaders of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens**."*

Four tiers. A man over ten families answers to a man over fifty, and so on up.

Nothing in the arrangement requires literacy, a building, or a budget.

## The rule

*"Have them **bring every hard case to you**, but **let them decide the simple ones
themselves**."*

The escalation runs one way, and the threshold is difficulty.

## The purpose

*"That will **lighten your load**, for **they will carry it with you**."*

The Hebrew is *and they shall bear the burden with you*.

Not instead of. With.

## The condition

*"**If you do this, and God so commands**."*

He puts the whole proposal under God's authorisation. Twice in five verses.

## The outcome

*"You'll **be able to hold up**, and **all these people will go home in peace**."*

Two results, and the second is about the people waiting in the queue.

Standing from morning to evening was costing them as much as it was costing Moses.

## What Moses did

*So **Moses heeded the voice of his father-in-law and did all that he had said**.*

The most humble man on the face of the earth takes advice from his wife's father.

## What Deuteronomy adds

Moses tells it again forty years later and adds one detail: *and you answered me and said,
"The thing which you have told us to do is good."*

He put it to the people first.

> **Who gives the advice.** A Midianite priest, on a visit, who had watched one day's work
> and told the deliverer of Israel that what he was doing was not good.
