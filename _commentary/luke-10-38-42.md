Two sisters, one guest, and a correction that is gentler than it is usually
read.

## Who opened the door

*A woman named **Martha welcomed Him into her home**.*

It is her house and her invitation. Nothing in the passage criticises that, and
without her there is no scene.

## Where Mary is

*Mary **sat down at Jesus' feet** and listened to what He was saying.*

Sitting at a teacher's feet is the posture of a disciple — Paul says he was *brought
up at the feet of Gamaliel*.

It was not a woman's place in that culture. She has taken a student's seat in a room
where women served.

## What was happening to Martha

*But Martha was **pulled in every direction** by all there was to do.*

The Greek is **perispao** — to be dragged around, drawn away, distracted. It is a
word about being pulled off course.

She is not lazy and she is not doing anything wrong. She is being torn.

## What she says

*"**Lord, do You not care** that my sister has left me to do all the serving by
myself? **Tell her to come and help me.**"*

Three things in one sentence: an accusation about His caring, a complaint about her
sister, and an instruction to Him.

It is the same accusation the disciples made in the boat — *do You not care that we
are going down?*

## The name, twice

*"**Martha, Martha.**"*

The doubled name is how affection is spoken in Scripture. *Abraham, Abraham. Moses,
Moses. Simon, Simon. Saul, Saul. Jerusalem, Jerusalem.*

Every time, it precedes something serious, and every time it is warm.

## The diagnosis

*"You are **worried and pulled about** by so many things."*

Two words: anxious, and troubled — the second means to be in a tumult, disturbed.

He names her state before He names the remedy, and He does not tell her she should
not have been cooking.

## "Only one thing is really needed"

The contrast is between *many things* and **one**.

Not many things done better, or more efficiently. One.

## What Mary chose

*"**Mary has chosen the good portion, and no one is going to take it away from
her.**"*

The Greek is **meris** — a portion, a share, the word for what you are allotted at a
meal.

Martha is preparing portions. Jesus says Mary has already taken hers.

*No one is going to take it away* — the one thing about the afternoon that is
permanent.

## What Martha is not told

She is not told to stop serving, leave the kitchen, or be like her sister. She is
told what she is being pulled apart by, and what the one necessary thing is.
