Three men turn down a dinner, and none of them has anything against the host.

## What prompted it

*"**How happy the one will be who sits down to eat in the kingdom of God!**"*

A pious remark from a fellow guest at a Pharisee's table, and it is the kind of thing
nobody expects to be tested.

Jesus answers it with a story about people who did not come.

## The two invitations

*He **sent invitations out to a long list** of guests. When everything was ready, he
**sent his servant around to tell them, "Come now."**"*

That is how it was done. A first invitation to reserve the day, and a second when the
food was on the table.

Every one of these men had already accepted. The refusals come after they had said
yes.

## What is ready

*"**The table is set and it is all waiting for you.**"*

Nothing is asked of them. The cost, the preparation and the timing are the host's.

## The three excuses

*"I have just **bought a field** and I have to go out and look it over."*

Nobody buys a field unseen. The inspection happens before the purchase, not after.

*"I have just **bought five yoke of oxen** and I am on my way to try them in the
harness."*

Ten oxen, unexamined, already paid for. The same problem.

*"I have just **been married**. I cannot come."*

The third does not even say *please excuse me*. He states it as settled.

## What the excuses have in common

Land, livestock, and a wife. All three are good — a field, working animals, a
marriage.

Nothing sinful is named. The dinner is lost to ordinary property and ordinary
happiness.

And every excuse is in the past tense: *I have just bought, I have just bought, I have
just been married.* Things already acquired, keeping them from something being given.

## The one legal exemption

Deuteronomy 24 does exempt a newly married man — from military service, for a year.

The third man may be quoting it. He is producing a valid reason for not going to war,
about a dinner invitation.

## What the master does

*The master of the house **was angry**.*

And then: *"Go out quickly into the streets and the back lanes… **bring in the poor
and the crippled and the lame and the blind**."*

See the note on Luke 14:21–24.

## Who Jesus was talking to

He is at a Pharisee's house on the Sabbath, having just healed a man with dropsy and
told the guests to stop taking the best seats.

And He had said, a few verses earlier: *when you give a feast, invite the poor, the
maimed, the lame, the blind — and you will be blessed, because they cannot repay you.*

Then He tells a story in which the master does exactly that.

> **What the first men lose.** *"Not one of those men on the first list will ever taste
> this dinner of mine."* Not because they were refused. Because they were busy, and
> the food went to people who were not.
