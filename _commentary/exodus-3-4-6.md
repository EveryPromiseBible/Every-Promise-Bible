The first thing God says to Moses is his name twice, and the second thing is stop.

## The trigger

*When the LORD **saw him turn aside to look**, God called out to him from the middle of the
bush.*

The turning came first. See the note on Exodus 3:1–3.

## The doubled name

*"**Moses! Moses!**"*

The doubling happens a handful of times in the Bible, and it always marks the same kind of
moment: *Abraham, Abraham* on the mountain with the knife raised; *Samuel, Samuel* in the
dark; *Saul, Saul* on the Damascus road; *Simon, Simon, Satan has asked for you*.

And *Martha, Martha*, over dinner.

## The answer

*"**Here I am**."*

The Hebrew is **hinneni** — behold me. One word.

The same word Abraham says three times in Genesis 22, and Isaiah says in the temple.

## The instruction

*"**Do not come any nearer**," God said.*

The Hebrew is *do not draw near here*.

He had said *I will now turn aside*, and he is stopped before he arrives.

## The sandals

*"**Take off your sandals**, for the ground where you are standing is **holy ground**."*

The Hebrew for *holy* is **qodesh** — the word's first appearance applied to a place in
Scripture.

In Israelite law the sandal carried a man's claim. Boaz's redemption is sealed by a shoe
handed over; the ceremony in Deuteronomy for a man who refuses to redeem is to have his
sandal pulled off in public.

To take the sandals off is to stand there with no claim on the ground.

## The name

*"**I am the God of your father** — the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of
Jacob."*

Singular: *your father*. He starts personal and then goes back three generations.

Four hundred years since Jacob went down to Egypt, and He introduces Himself by the men
He walked with.

## The reaction

*At that Moses **hid his face**, because he was **afraid to look at God**.*

He came to see, and ends up covering his eyes.

Later he asks *please, show me Your glory*, and is put in a cleft of the rock with a hand
over his face.

## What the other son heard

A younger son comes home in rags with a rehearsed speech, and his father *ran and fell on
his neck and kissed him* — then *put shoes on his feet*. See the note on Luke 15:17–20.

One is told not to come near and to take his sandals off. The other is run at, and sandals
are put on him.

## Where the boundary goes

Sinai keeps the same rule. *Set bounds for the people all around, saying, "Take heed to
yourselves that you do not go up to the mountain or touch its base."*

And Hebrews sets it against the other mountain: *for you have not come to the mountain that
may be touched… but you have come to Mount Zion*. See the note on Hebrews 12:18–24.

## What tore

At the crucifixion, *the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom* — and the
writer of Hebrews calls it *a new and living way which He consecrated for us, through the
veil, that is, His flesh*, with the invitation *let us draw near*.

The same verb the bush forbade.

> **The two sentences.** *Do not draw near*, at the bush; *let us draw near with a true
> heart*, in Hebrews. Nothing changed about the holiness of the ground — something changed
> about the standing of the man on it.
