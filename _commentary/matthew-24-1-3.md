The disciples point at a building and He tells them it is coming down.

## Where He has just come from

*Jesus **left the temple** and was walking away.*

The chapter before ends with the seven woes and *Jerusalem, Jerusalem… your house is
left to you desolate.* See the note on Matthew 23:37–39.

He walks out, and the disciples change the subject to architecture.

## What they were pointing at

Herod's temple was one of the wonders of the ancient world. Josephus describes stones
of enormous size, and some of those still in the retaining wall weigh several hundred
tons.

They had been working on it for forty-six years and were not finished. See the note on
John 2:18–22.

Mark records what one of them said: *Teacher, see what manner of stones and what
buildings are here!*

## The answer

*"**Not one stone here will be left sitting on another. Every one of them will be
thrown down.**"*

In AD 70 Titus took the city. The temple burned, and the gold in it melted down
between the stones — which, according to the accounts, is why they were prised apart
afterwards.

## Where He sits down

*As He was sitting on **the Mount of Olives**.*

Across the Kidron valley, facing the temple. They are looking at the building He has
just described being demolished.

Zechariah says His feet will stand on that hill.

## The three questions

*"**When will these things happen?** And **what will be the sign of Your coming**? And
**of the end of the age**?"*

They ask three things and assume they are one event.

That assumption is what makes the discourse that follows difficult — Jesus answers
all three, and the answers are interleaved rather than sorted.

## What He starts with

Not a date. *"Take heed that no one deceives you."*

The same opening as Luke's account: *be careful that no one leads you astray.* See the
note on Luke 21:7–9.

## And where He ends

*"But of **that day and hour no one knows**, not even the angels of heaven, but My
Father only."*

And then three parables about being ready rather than being informed. See the note on
Matthew 24:36–41.

## The one thing they got right

They asked privately. Matthew notes it — *the disciples came to Him privately.*

Whatever else this is, it is a conversation with four men on a hillside rather than a
public announcement.

> **What the building meant to them.** Not a landmark. The temple was where God had
> said He would put His name, where sin was dealt with, and where a nation's identity
> was held. He told them it would be rubble, and then sat down opposite it to explain.
