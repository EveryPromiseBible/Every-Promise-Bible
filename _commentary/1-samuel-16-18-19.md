Six things are said about a boy nobody had thought to fetch, and the last one is the point.

## Who speaks

*One of the young men spoke up.*

The Hebrew is **echad mehanne'arim** — one of the servants.

Not a prophet, not an official. A member of the household staff who has been somewhere and
remembered something.

## Why they are looking

*A distressing spirit from the LORD troubled him*, and Saul's servants suggest finding a man who can
play.

## The description

*"I've seen a son of Jesse of Bethlehem **who knows how to play**."*

The Hebrew is **yodea naggen** — knowing how to play.

## The list

*"He's **brave**, **a fighting man**, **careful with his words**, and **fine to look at**."*

**Gibbor chayil** — a mighty man of valour. **Ish milchamah** — a man of war. **Nevon davar** —
discerning of speech. **Ish to'ar** — a man of form.

## What is odd about it

He is a teenager who has not been in a battle.

The servant describes him as a soldier before he has fought anything, and the next chapter is
Goliath.

## The last item

*"**And the LORD is with him**."*

The Hebrew is **vaYHWH immo**.

## Where it comes in the list

Last, after skill, courage, speech and appearance.

The four human qualities are real. The clause added at the end is what the chapter has just been
about.

## What had happened days earlier

*"The LORD does not see as man sees; for man looks at the outward appearance, **but the LORD looks
at the heart**."* See the note on 1 Samuel 16:6–7.

Seven brothers passed over, and the youngest not even called in from the field.

*Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward.*

## Saul's message

*"Send me **your son David, the one who's with the sheep**."*

The Hebrew is **asher batzon** — who is among the flock.

## What that identifies him as

The one who is not in the house.

Jesse's own answer to Samuel had been *there remains yet the youngest, and there he is, keeping the
sheep*. The same location is how the king now finds him.

## The irony

Saul is sending for the man anointed to replace him, and asks for him by his job.

## What the sheep were for

*"Your servant used to keep his father's sheep, and when **a lion or a bear** came and took a lamb
out of the flock, I went out after it."* See the note on 1 Samuel 17:34–37.

*"The LORD, who delivered me from the paw of the lion… He will deliver me from the hand of this
Philistine."*

The military reputation the servant gave him turns out to be accurate, and it was earned in a field
with no witnesses.

## What the music does

*And Saul loved him greatly, and he became his armourbearer.*

*Whenever the spirit from God was upon Saul, David would take a harp and play it with his hand.
Then Saul would become refreshed and well.*

## Where the shepherd line goes

*He also chose David His servant, and **took him from the sheepfolds**… **So he shepherded them
according to the integrity of his heart**.* See the note on Psalms 78:70–72.

> **The order of the list.** Skilled, brave, well-spoken, good-looking — and then the clause that
> the whole chapter turned on: *and the LORD is with him*.
