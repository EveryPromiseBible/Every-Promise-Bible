A man decides how to handle a scandal quietly, and is interrupted.

## What he had decided

*Joseph her husband, being **a just man**, and not wanting to make her a public example, **was
minded to put her away secretly**.*

The Greek is **dikaios** — righteous. And **lathra** — privately.

Betrothal in that culture was legally binding; ending it required a divorce. The law permitted
a public accusation.

He is described as righteous, and the righteous act he settles on is the quiet one.

## The timing

*But while he was **turning all of this over in his mind**.*

The Greek is **enthumethentos** — pondering, deliberating.

The angel arrives after the decision and before the action.

## The address

*"**Joseph, son of David**."*

Which is how nobody has addressed a village carpenter in his life.

Matthew's genealogy has just traced the line through David to this man. The angel uses the title
the genealogy established.

## The reassurance

*"**Do not be afraid to take Mary home as your wife**."*

The Greek is **me phobethes** — do not fear.

He was not afraid of scandal; he had already planned around it. What is being addressed is
whether he may go through with the marriage at all.

## The explanation

*"**The child within her was conceived by the Holy Spirit**."*

The Greek is **ek Pneumatos estin Hagiou** — is of the Holy Spirit.

Matthew tells the reader this in verse 18. Joseph is told it in verse 20.

## The instruction

*"She will give birth to a son, and **you are to give Him the name Jesus**."*

Naming a child was the father's act, and it was the legal acknowledgment of paternity.

Joseph is asked to name a son who is not his, which is precisely how the boy becomes son of
David.

## The name

**Iesous** — the Greek form of **Yeshua**, a shortened **Yehoshua**: *the LORD saves*.

A common name. There are several men called Jesus in Josephus, and one in Colossians — *Jesus who
is called Justus*.

## The reason

*"Because **He will save His people from their sins**."*

The Greek is **sosei ton laon autou apo ton hamartion auton**.

## What the reason narrows

*His people* — which a first-century hearer would take as Israel.

*From their sins* — which is not what anybody in Israel was expecting to be saved from.

The expectation was Rome. The angel names something else.

## The name Matthew adds

*"**And they shall call His name Immanuel**," which is translated, "**God with us**."* See the note
on Matthew 1:22–23.

**Immanu-El** — with us, God.

Two names in three verses. One is what He will do. The other is who He is.

## What Joseph does

*Then Joseph, being aroused from sleep, did as the angel of the Lord commanded him and **took to
him his wife**.*

No recorded words. Joseph does not speak anywhere in the New Testament.

He obeys four times in two chapters, each time after a dream: take her, go to Egypt, come back,
turn aside to Galilee.

## What Luke records instead

Gabriel to Mary: *you will conceive in your womb and bring forth a Son, and shall call His name
Jesus*, and her answer, *let it be to me according to your word*. See the note on
Luke 1:30–33.

Both parents are told the name separately.

## The other thing Matthew says

*And he **did not know her till she had brought forth her firstborn Son. And he called His name
Jesus**.*

The last four words are the obedience.

> **The naming.** It was the father's legal act, the acknowledgment of a son. Joseph is told to
> perform it over a child that is not his, and that is how the line reaches David.
