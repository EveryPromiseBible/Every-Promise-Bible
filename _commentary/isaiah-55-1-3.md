An invitation shouted like a market trader's cry, and the price is the strangest
thing about it.

## Who is called

*Come, all you who are **thirsty**… and you who **have no money**.*

Two qualifications, and both are lacks. Thirst, and an empty purse.

Nothing is required except the absence of what would normally be required.

## The impossible instruction

*Come, **buy** and eat! Come, **buy wine and milk, with no money and no cost**.*

Buy, with nothing to buy with. The verb is the ordinary word for a commercial
purchase, and Isaiah puts it next to *without money and without price*.

It is deliberately absurd. There is a transaction and there is no payment.

## What is on offer

Water, wine, milk, and *the richest food* — the Hebrew is *fatness*, the choicest
part.

Not rations. Wine and milk are what you serve at a feast.

## The question

*Why **spend your silver on what is not bread**, and your labour on **what never
satisfies**?*

Two currencies wasted: money and work.

Isaiah does not say the things bought are evil. He says they are *not bread* — they
do not do what food does.

Ecclesiastes ran the same experiment at length and reported the same finding. See the
note on Ecclesiastes 3:11.

## The verbs

*Listen well… **eat what is good**… let your soul delight… **Turn your ear and come
to Me. Listen, and your soul will live.***

**Listen** three times in two verses.

And *incline your ear* — the Hebrew is to stretch the ear toward, to lean in.

## What the listening produces

*Listen, and **your soul will live**.*

Life, in exchange for attention.

## The covenant

*I will make **an everlasting covenant** with you, **the sure mercies promised to
David**.*

The Hebrew is the faithful, dependable mercies — **chasdei David**, the covenant
loyalties sworn to David in 2 Samuel 7.

Paul quotes exactly this phrase at Antioch, and applies it to the resurrection: *God
has fulfilled this for us… "I will give you the sure mercies of David."*

## Where the chapter sits

Isaiah 53 is the suffering servant. Isaiah 54 is the barren woman told to sing and
enlarge her tent. Isaiah 55 is the free invitation.

Three chapters in sequence: a price paid, a household enlarged, and a meal offered
at no cost. See the note on Isaiah 54:1–3.

## How the chapter ends

*My word… shall not return to Me void, but it shall accomplish what I please.*

And: *the mountains and the hills shall break forth into singing before you, and all
the trees of the field shall clap their hands.*

> **The last word of the invitation.** *Seek the LORD **while He may be found**, call
> upon Him **while He is near**.* The offer is free and it is not indefinite — and
> Paul says the same in his own words: *now is the accepted time; now is the day of
> salvation.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 6:1–2.
