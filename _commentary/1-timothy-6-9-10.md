The most misquoted sentence in the New Testament, and the wording is precise.

## What the verse says

*For **the love of money** is a root, and every kind of evil grows up out of it.*

The Greek is **philarguria** — literally *silver-love*. One word, and it is the subject
of the sentence.

Not money. The love of it.

## "A root"

The Greek has no definite article: *a root of all kinds of evil*.

Paul is not claiming this is the single origin of everything wrong in the world. He is
saying it is a root, and that a great variety grows off it.

## Who is described

*Men **set on getting rich**.*

The Greek is *those who are determined to be rich* — **boulomai**, wanting, resolving,
setting the will on.

Paul is describing an aim, not a bank balance. Two verses earlier he was talking about
contentment; a few verses later he addresses *those who are rich in this present age*
and does not tell them to stop being rich. He tells them to be generous and ready to
give. See the note on 1 Timothy 6:17–19.

The rich are given instructions. The ones determined to get rich are given a warning.

## The trap

*Walk **straight into the bait and the trap**.*

The Greek is **peirasmos** (testing, enticement) and **pagis** (a snare, a bird trap).

A snare works by having something in it worth walking toward.

## What is in it

*And into **a whole crowd of cravings that make no sense and do no good**, the kind that
**drag a person down into ruin and destruction**.*

Two words for the ending — **olethros** and **apoleia**. Paul doubles it.

## The two consequences

*Some have **reached out for it** and been **led away from the faith**, and have **run
themselves through with sorrow upon sorrow**.*

- **Led away from the faith** — the Greek is *wandered away*, drifted off course.
- **Run themselves through** — **peripeiro**, to impale, to pierce all the way through.
  A violent word, and it is reflexive. They did it to themselves.

## What Paul is not saying

He is not saying money is evil, that the poor are safer, or that wealth disqualifies
anybody. He says none of those things in the passage, and eight verses later he tells
Timothy that God *gives us richly all things to enjoy*.

The object of the warning is a craving, and the injury described is self-inflicted.

## The context

The chapter opens on false teachers *who suppose that godliness is a means of gain* —
men using the ministry to get money.

That is who Paul is writing about. The warning has a specific target in the room.

## What he offers instead

*Now **godliness with contentment is great gain**. For we brought nothing into this
world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out.*

He uses the profit word — **porismos**, gain — and applies it to contentment.

> **The clause people drop.** *The love of.* Two words, and without them the sentence
> says something Paul does not say anywhere in his letters.
