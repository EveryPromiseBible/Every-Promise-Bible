title: "Put it down to my account"

The clearest picture of imputation in the New Testament, and it is in a private note about a
domestic theft.

## The concession

*"**And if he has done you wrong, or owes you anything.**"*

**Paul** does not minimise it. The **if** is not doubt about the facts; it is the polite Greek
conditional, and he goes straight on to settle the sum, which he would not do for a debt he thought
imaginary.

The wrong is real, the amount is real, and the owner has a case.

*For **all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God**.*

## The verb

*"**Put it down to my account.**"*

The Greek is **touto emoi ellogei** — charge this to me.

**Ellogeo** is a bookkeeping term. It appears twice in the New Testament: here, and in **Romans**
5:13 — *sin is not imputed when there is no law*.

The same technical verb, in a letter about a runaway slave and in the chapter about Adam and
**Christ**.

## The handwriting

*"**That is my own handwriting there — Paul — and I will settle it.**"*

*Ego Paulos egrapsa te eme cheiri* — I, Paul, wrote it with my own hand.

**Paul** normally dictated and signed at the end. Here he takes the pen mid-letter to write out a
promissory note, in his own hand, which under Roman law made it enforceable.

*Having wiped out **the handwriting of requirements that was against us**… and He has taken it out of
the way, having nailed it to the cross.*

**Colossians** 2, written at the same time, to the same town, carried by the same man — and
**Paul**'s word there is **cheirographon**, a certificate of debt written by hand.

One document is torn up. One is written out. Both are in **Paul**'s hand and both are about somebody
else's debt.

## The three transfers

*Receive him **as me**.* The standing goes one way. See the note on Philemon 1:17.

*Charge his debt **to me**.* The liability goes the other.

*I will repay.* And the payer is the one with neither the wrong nor the account.

*For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes **He
became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich.*

*Even so **one Man's righteous act** resulted in justification of life to all men. For as by one
man's disobedience many were made sinners, so also **by one Man's obedience many will be made
righteous**.*

## The sentence he adds

*"**And I will not mention that you owe me your very self.**"*

The Greek is **hina me lego soi** — that I may not say to you. A rhetorical figure: he mentions it
by saying he will not.

Philemon's own conversion came through **Paul**. The debt he is being asked to waive is smaller than
the one he is standing in.

*"**Should you not also have had compassion on your fellow servant, just as I had pity on you?**"*

**Matthew** 18, and the parable of the unforgiving servant is the same arithmetic told as a story.

> **The verb.** *Ellogei* — charge it to my account. It occurs twice in the New Testament: here,
> about a stolen sum, and in Romans 5 about sin being reckoned. Paul writes out the guarantee in
> his own hand, which is what made it collectable.
