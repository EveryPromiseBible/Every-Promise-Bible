key: Micah 7:8
title: "Though I sit in darkness"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Three verses in the first person, spoken from the bottom of the book.

## What comes before

*"The faithful have vanished from the land, and there is no honest person left… **do not trust a
neighbour, do not rely on a friend**. Guard the words of your mouth even from the one who lies in
your arms."*

And then the family: *son treats father with contempt, daughter rises against mother.*

*"For **I have come to set a man against his father, a daughter against her mother**… and a man's
enemies will be those of his own household."*

**Matthew** 10, and **Jesus** is quoting Micah 7:6 word for word.

## The turn

*"**But as for me**, I will keep watch for the LORD."*

The Hebrew is **va'ani** — and I. The same construction Micah used in chapter 3 against the hireling
prophets.

**Atzappeh** — I will watch, as a sentry on a wall watches for a dawn he cannot yet see.

## The three clauses

*"I will wait for the God who saves me. **My God will hear me.**"*

Said in a country he has just described as having no honest person left in it.

## The rising

*"Do not gloat over me, my enemy. **Though I have fallen, I will rise again. Though I sit in
darkness, the LORD will be my light.**"*

Both halves concede the situation. He has fallen; he is sitting in the dark.

*Though the righteous fall seven times, he rises again.*

*"**I am the light of the world.** He who follows Me shall not walk in darkness, but have the light
of life."*

*The people who sat in darkness have seen a great light, and upon those who sat in the region and
shadow of death Light has dawned.*

**Matthew** 4, quoting Isaiah, and the verb is Micah's: **sat**.

## The confession

*"**Because I have sinned against Him, I will bear the LORD's anger** until He takes up my cause and
does me justice."*

He does not claim to be the innocent party. The whole hope in the paragraph is expressed by a man
who says the trouble is deserved.

*If we confess our sins, **He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins** and to cleanse us from
all unrighteousness.*

## Who takes up the case

*"Until **He takes up my cause** and does me justice."*

The Hebrew is **yariv rivi** — until He contends my contention. It is a courtroom phrase, and the
striking thing is who is on which side: the offended party is asked to act as the defence.

*My little children, these things I write to you, so that you may not sin. And if anyone sins, **we
have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous**.*

**John**'s word is **parakletos**. The judge's Son is counsel for the defence.

*Who shall bring a charge against God's elect? **It is God who justifies.**"*

## The last clause

*"He will bring me out into the light, and **I will look upon His righteousness**."*

Not his own vindication. His.

*And be found in Him, **not having my own righteousness**, which is from the law, but that which is
through faith in Christ.*

> **Who is asked to prosecute.** The one he sinned against. *I will bear the LORD's anger until He
> takes up my cause* — and 1 John names the Advocate, who turns out to be the Judge's Son.
