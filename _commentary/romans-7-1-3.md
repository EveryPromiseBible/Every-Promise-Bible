Paul stops and makes sure of one legal principle before he builds anything on it.

## The principle

*The law has authority over a person **only for as long as that person is
alive**.*

He says it flatly, and he says it to people who *know how law works* — Jewish
readers in Rome who would grant the point without argument.

A law can speak to the living. It has nothing to say to a corpse. No court
prosecutes a dead man, and no statute binds one.

## The illustration

*By law she is bound to her husband while he lives; but if her husband dies, she
is released from the law that bound her to him.*

The law that bound her did not change. It was not repealed, softened, or
amended. It is exactly as binding as it was.

She simply is no longer inside its jurisdiction, because a death occurred.

## "No adulteress at all"

*She is **no adulteress at all** if she marries another man.*

Paul presses the same case twice — once with the husband living, once with him
dead — so that nobody can mistake which fact does the work.

The same act is a crime in one paragraph and blameless in the next. Nothing about
the woman changed. A death changed everything.

## Where this is going

The next verse applies it: *you were made dead to the law through the body of
Christ, so that you could belong to Another.*

Note who died in Paul's application. Not the law — us. *Through the body of
Christ*, which is the phrase he will use again over a table three letters later.

The law is still the law, holy and just and good, and Paul says so a few verses
on. It simply has no claim on somebody who died and was raised.

> **Why he pauses to establish this.** Chapter 6 has just been answering *shall we
> go on sinning that grace may abound?* Paul's answer is not tighter rules. It is a
> funeral: you died with Him, and what you are joined to now is a Person, not a
> code. See the note on Romans 7:4–6.
