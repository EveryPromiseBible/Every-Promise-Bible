Three conditions and three responses, and the third one is the longest.

## The pattern

*Is one of you **having a hard time**? Let him **pray**.*
*Is one of you **in good spirits**? Let him **sing**.*
*Is one of you **sick**? Let him **send for the elders**.*

Every state gets an action, and none of them is to do nothing.

The first two are things you do alone. The third is the only one that requires
somebody else.

## Who sends

*Let **him** send for the elders.*

The initiative is the sick man's. Nobody in the passage is told to go looking for the
sick.

## What they do

*Let them **pray over him** and **put oil on him** in the name of the Lord.*

Two actions, in that order.

The Greek for *pray over* is literally that — praying over the person, and it implies
being physically present.

## The oil

The Greek verb is **aleipho**, which is the ordinary word for rubbing oil on — what
you do after a bath, or to an athlete, or to a wound.

It is not **chrio**, the ceremonial anointing word from which *Christ* comes. James
uses the everyday one.

Olive oil was the standard medical application of that world. The Samaritan poured
oil and wine on the man's wounds on the Jericho road.

Whatever else is happening, the elders are doing something ordinary and physical, in
a name.

## What does the healing

*And **the prayer that trusts God** will make the sick man well, and **the Lord** will
get him back on his feet.*

Not the oil, and not the elders. The Greek is *the prayer of faith*, and then the
Lord is named as the one who raises him.

## The last clause

*And **if he has committed sins**, he will be forgiven.*

**If.** James does not assume it, and he does not connect the sickness to the sin.

He puts the forgiveness in a conditional and moves on.

## Where the twelve did it

*They drove out many demons, and **they rubbed oil on many who were sick and healed
them**.*

Mark 6, on the first mission, with no bread, no bag, no money and one stick. See the
note on Mark 6:7–13.

The same verb, and it is the only occasion in the Gospels the disciples are recorded
using oil.

## What comes next in James

*Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be
healed. **The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much.***

And then Elijah, *a man with a nature like ours*, who prayed and it did not rain for
three and a half years. See the note on James 5:16–18.

> **What the letter is like.** James is the most practical book in the New Testament —
> the tongue, the poor, favouritism, patience, oaths. This paragraph is in character:
> somebody is ill, so send for the elders, and let them use oil.
