Thirty-five chapters of silence, and then He speaks — and what He says is not
what anyone in the book was expecting.

## Out of the storm

*He spoke **out of the storm**, out of the wind and the wild weather.*

The Hebrew is the whirlwind, the tempest. It is how He came for Elijah too.

## What He does not do

He does not answer a single one of Job's questions. Not one.

He does not explain the boils. He does not mention the throne room, the accuser,
or the wager. He does not defend Himself against the charge Job has been laying
for thirty chapters.

And He does not confirm the friends' diagnosis either. There is no list of Job's
sins, because there is no list.

## What He does instead

*"**I have questions for you**, and it is your turn to answer Me."*

He asks. Four chapters of questions — seventy-odd of them — and almost every one is
about something Job has never had anything to do with.

Where were you when I laid the earth's foundations. Have you entered the springs
of the sea. Have you commanded the morning. Who provides food for the raven. Do
you know when the mountain goats give birth. Can you bind the Pleiades.

Not one of them is about Job's conduct.

## "Brace yourself like a strong man"

The Hebrew is *gird up your loins* — pull your robe through your belt so you can
move.

It is not gentle and it is not a rebuke either. He is telling Job to stand up. Job
has been on an ash heap for thirty-five chapters and God's first instruction is to
get on his feet.

## "Words that carry no knowledge"

*"Who is this who **muddies My counsel** with words that carry no knowledge?"*

The Hebrew for *muddies* is to darken, to obscure — the same picture as silt
stirred up in clear water.

Job's answer, when it comes, is to agree: *it was me.* See the note on
Job 42:1–3.

## What the questions are actually doing

The whole speech is about the ostrich and the wild donkey and the storehouses of
snow and the horse that laughs at fear. It is a tour of things being cared for
that no one is watching.

The raven's chicks are fed. The mountain goats calve without a midwife. The rain
falls *on a land where no one lives, on a desert with no one in it.*

He answers a man who thinks he has been abandoned by showing him how much is being
looked after where nobody can see.

## The question He does not answer

Answer the question. Not one line of the whirlwind speeches explains the suffering, mentions
the accuser, or refers to chapters 1 and 2.

Job asked *why*, repeatedly, for thirty chapters. He is asked about ostriches instead.

## What He does

Turns up. *Then **the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind**.*

Job had said *oh, that I knew where I might find Him, that I might come to His seat* — and the
seat comes to the ash heap.

## What Job says it gave him

*"**I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees You.**"*

Not information. Sight.

*"**Philip, have you been with Me so long, and yet you have not known Me? He who has seen Me
has seen the Father.**"*

*He is the image of the invisible God.* **Paul**'s answer to the same longing is a Person
rather than an explanation.

## Where the storm turns up again

*And suddenly a great tempest arose on the sea, so that the boat was covered with the
waves — but **He was asleep**… Then He arose and rebuked the wind.*

God speaks to Job **out of** a storm. In the gospel He is asleep in one, and then tells it to
stop.

> **What Job says he gets out of it.** *I have heard of You by the hearing of the
> ear, but now my eye sees You.* Not an explanation. He never gets one. He gets
> the One he had been asking to see.
