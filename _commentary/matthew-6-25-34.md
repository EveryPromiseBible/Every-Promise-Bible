Three times He says do not worry, and in between He points at two things anybody
in that field could see.

## The birds

*They **do not plant, they do not harvest, they do not store anything away in
barns**, and your heavenly Father feeds them.*

Three verbs, all denied. And they are the three stages of agriculture — the whole
economy of that hillside.

Birds are not idle; a bird works hard all day. What it does not do is the thing the
crowd was doing: plan the year, secure the surplus, guarantee the future.

*Are you not worth far more than they are?*

## The flowers

*Look how the wildflowers grow out in the open field. **They do not work at it,
they do not spin a thread**.*

Two verbs again, and they are women's work — the crowd is being addressed in
halves.

*Not even Solomon in all his splendour was dressed like one of these.*

The richest man Israel produced, out-dressed by a weed.

## The argument

*If God clothes **the grass of the field** like that — grass that is here today and
**tossed into the oven tomorrow** — how much more will He clothe you.*

Dried grass was fuel. He is comparing a person to kindling, and arguing from the
lesser to the greater.

## "You of little faith"

The Greek is one word, **oligopistos**, and Matthew uses it five times, always to
disciples. It is not a rebuke about doctrine.

He attaches it to worry, which tells you what He thinks worry is a shortage of.

## What worry cannot do

*Which of you, **by worrying, can add a single hour** to your life?*

He does not argue that worry is wrong. He argues that it is ineffective, which is
harder to dispute.

## Who chases

*People who **do not know God** chase after all of that, and **your heavenly Father
knows perfectly well that you need it**.*

Two clauses, and the second is the answer to the first. The need is not denied — it
is granted, and the point is that it is already known about.

## The one instruction

*Seek first the kingdom of God and **His righteousness**, and **all these other
things will be given to you as well**.*

Note whose righteousness is to be sought. Not one of your own — that is the whole
argument of the Sermon, which has just spent a chapter showing nobody has one. See
the note on Matthew 5:27–30.

*All these other things* — the food, the drink, the clothes. Not withheld and not
despised; put second, and added.

The Greek for *will be given* is to add on top, to throw in besides. They arrive as
an extra.

## Today

*Do not worry about tomorrow. Tomorrow will worry about itself. **Today has enough
trouble of its own**.*

He does not say tomorrow has no trouble in it. He says today's ration is sufficient
and there is no provision for carrying a second day's.

> **The verse before all this.** *No one can serve two masters… you cannot serve God
> and mammon.* Then *therefore I say to you, do not worry.* The passage about birds
> is an answer to a question about who owns you.
