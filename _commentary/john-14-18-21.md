He is hours from leaving and uses the word for a child with no father.

## The promise

*"**I will not leave you like orphans**; I am coming to you."*

The Greek is **ouk apheso humas orphanous**.

**Orphanos** — bereft of parents. It occurs twice in the New Testament, here and in James, of *the
fatherless*.

## What the word carries

In that world an orphan had no protector, no inheritance, and no standing.

The disciples are about to lose their teacher, their movement, and their future in a single night.

## The tense

*"**I am coming to you**."*

The Greek is **erchomai pros humas** — present tense.

Not *I will come*. He is already on the way while saying it.

## What He means by coming

The chapter holds three arrivals together and does not separate them: the Spirit, whom He has just
promised; His own appearances after the resurrection; and the final return.

John lets all three stand in the same paragraph.

## The seeing

*"**In a little while the world will not see Me any more, but you will see Me**."*

The Greek uses one verb — **theorei** — for both.

The world will not observe Him. They will.

## The reason

*"**Because I live, you will live too**."*

The Greek is **hoti ego zo kai humeis zesete**.

Said the night before the crucifixion, in the present tense, about a life that has not been
interrupted yet.

## The three prepositions

*"On that day you will know that **I am in My Father**, and **you are in Me**, and **I am in you**."*

Three relations, in a chain: Son in Father, disciple in Son, Son in disciple.

The chain closes on itself. Whatever the first link is, the last one is the same kind.

## The day

*"On that day."*

The Greek is **en ekeine te hemera**.

Not defined. It has been read as the resurrection morning, as Pentecost, and as the last day.

## The condition

*"**Whoever holds My commands and keeps them is the one who loves Me**."*

The Greek is **ho echon tas entolas mou kai teron autas**.

Two verbs: having, and keeping. Possessing the instructions and doing them.

## The test offered

Not feeling. Conduct.

He said the same thing three verses earlier: *if you love Me, keep My commandments*, and says it
again after: *if anyone loves Me, he will keep My word*.

Three times in one discourse.

## The consequence

*"And the one who loves Me **will be loved by My Father, and I will love him and show Myself to
him**."*

The Greek is **emphaniso auto emauton** — I will manifest Myself to him.

**Emphanizo** — to make visible, to exhibit.

## The question Judas asks

*"Lord, **how is it that You will manifest Yourself to us, and not to the world?**"*

Not Iscariot. John says so.

It is a fair question, and the answer is about dwelling: *We will come to him and make Our home
with him*.

The Greek is **monen** — a dwelling place. The same noun as *in My Father's house are many
mansions*, at the start of the chapter.

## What the disciples got

*Then, the same day at evening… **Jesus came and stood in the midst**, and said to them, "Peace be
with you."*

Three days later.

> **The word.** **Orphanos** — a child with no father, no protector and no inheritance. Used by a
> man leaving that night, in a sentence whose second half is in the present tense.
