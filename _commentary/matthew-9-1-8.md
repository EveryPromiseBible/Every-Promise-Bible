Matthew's version of the paralytic, and it is stripped down to the exchange.

## What Matthew leaves out

The crowd at the door, the four carriers, the roof, the digging, the ropes.

Mark gives all of it. See the note on Mark 2:1–5.

Matthew keeps the faith and the words and cuts everything else — which is how he handles
most miracles.

## Where

*Jesus got into the boat, crossed over, and came to **His own town**.*

Capernaum. Matthew calls it His own city; He had moved there from Nazareth.

## What He saw

*When Jesus saw **their faith**.*

Plural, as in Mark. Whatever it took to get the man in front of Him, Jesus reads it as
faith and says so.

## The first sentence

*"**Take heart, son. Your sins are forgiven**."*

The Greek for *take heart* is **tharsei** — be of good cheer, courage.

**Son** — **teknon**, child.

Nobody had raised the subject of sins. The men came about a body.

## The objection

*Some of the teachers of the law said among themselves, "**This man is insulting God**."*

Their reasoning is correct: sin is against God, so the pardon is God's to give.

## What He knew

*Jesus **knew what they were thinking** and said, "**Why are you turning over such dark
thoughts in your hearts?**"*

Nothing had been said aloud. Answering the interior objection is itself the answer to it.

## The question

*"**Which is easier to say** — 'Your sins are forgiven,' or 'Get up and walk'?"*

Easier **to say**. Forgiveness is unverifiable; legs are checkable in seconds.

He says the checkable one to establish the other.

## The broken sentence

*"But **so that you will know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive
sins**" — and here **He turned to the paralyzed man** — "**get up, pick up your mat, and go
home**."*

The sentence is addressed to the scribes and finishes as a command to somebody else.

The healing happens inside a clause about authority.

## The result

*And the man **got up and went home**.*

Matthew gives it in five words.

## The reaction

*When the crowds saw it, they were **filled with wonder**, and they **praised God, who had
given such authority to human beings**.*

The Greek is *to men* — **tois anthropois**, plural.

Matthew alone records that detail, and it is the strange one. The crowd does not conclude
that a unique person is present. They conclude that this kind of authority has been given
to human beings.

Matthew's Gospel ends with *all authority has been given to Me… **go therefore***.

## What happens in the next verse

*As Jesus passed on from there, He saw a man named **Matthew** sitting at the tax office,
and He said to him, "Follow Me."*

The writer puts his own call immediately after this scene, and then a dinner at his house
with *many tax collectors and sinners* — and the Pharisees asking why.

The answer: *those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick… **I
did not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance**.*

> **What He addressed first.** Not the legs. Five men went to great trouble about a body,
> and the first sentence spoken over the man is *take heart* — and then something nobody
> had asked about.
