One verse, and the whole of Hebrews 7 comes out of it.

## The oath

*The LORD **has sworn**, and **He will not take it back**.*

The Hebrew is *the LORD has sworn and will not repent*.

An oath from God is unusual — He has nothing higher to swear by, so He swears on Himself.
The only other place in the Old Testament He does it about a person is Genesis 22, after
the knife. See the note on Genesis 22:15–18.

Hebrews makes the point twice: the Levitical priests were made priests *with no oath
sworn over them*, and this one was. See the note on Hebrews 7:20–22.

## The address

*"**You are** a priest forever."*

Second person. The psalm's first verse has David overhearing one Lord speak to another —
*the LORD said to my Lord, sit at My right hand*. See the note on Psalms 110:1.

This is the same conversation, three verses on.

## The two offices

A king on David's throne, seated at the right hand — and a priest.

Under the law those could not be the same person. Kings came from Judah and priests from
Levi, and the wall between them was absolute. Uzziah went into the temple to burn incense
and came out a leper.

The psalm puts both on one man, and David wrote it about a descendant of his own.

## "Forever"

The Hebrew is **le'olam**.

Levitical priests served from thirty to fifty and died. Hebrews says it plainly: *there
have been many priests, because they were prevented by death from continuing.*

## The order

*"In the order of **Melchizedek**."*

The Hebrew is *after the manner of*.

Melchizedek appears in four verses of Genesis, brings out bread and wine, blesses Abram,
receives a tenth, and is never mentioned again in the Old Testament until this line. See
the note on Genesis 14:17–20.

Four hundred years before Aaron, and a thousand before David.

## Why Genesis is silent about him

No father, no mother, no genealogy, no birth, no death. Hebrews notices all four
omissions and treats the silence as deliberate: *made like the Son of God, remaining a
priest continually.*

In a book that gives genealogies for everybody, the absence is the point.

## What the psalm is used for

It is the most-quoted Old Testament verse in the New Testament, and Hebrews quotes this
line five times.

## Where the argument goes

If a different order of priesthood was promised under oath, after the Levitical one was
running, then the Levitical one was not final: *if perfection were through the Levitical
priesthood… what further need was there that another priest should rise according to the
order of Melchizedek?*

And a change of priesthood requires a change of law: *for the priesthood being changed,
of necessity there is also a change of the law.*

The whole argument of Hebrews 7 to 10 is built on one verse in a psalm.

## The rest of the psalm

*The Lord is at Your right hand; He shall execute kings in the day of His wrath.*

And it ends: *He shall drink of the brook by the wayside; therefore He shall lift up the
head.*

> **What is sworn.** Not a promise about what He will do. A statement about what He is —
> and Hebrews says the oath exists because the office was never going to be reviewed.
