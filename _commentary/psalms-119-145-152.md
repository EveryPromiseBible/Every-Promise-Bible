Eight verses beginning with the letter **qoph**, and half of them are about hours.

## The structure

Psalm 119 has twenty-two sections of eight verses each, one for every letter of the Hebrew
alphabet.

Every line in this section begins with **qoph**, and almost every verse names the word of God by
one of eight terms.

## The call

*I've called with all my heart; **O LORD, answer!***

The Hebrew is **qarati bekol-lev anneni** — I called with the whole heart, answer me.

**Qara** — to call, and it begins with the section's letter.

## The exchange offered

*I will **hold fast to all the ways You keep**.*

Not a bargain. The keeping is described as what the answer would free him for.

## The second call

*I've cried to You; oh, **save me, be my rescue**.*

The Hebrew is **qeraticha hoshi'eni**.

## Before dawn

*Before the sun comes up **I rise and call**.*

The Hebrew is **qiddamti vannesheph va'ashavve'ah** — I anticipated the dawn and cried out.

**Qadam** — to go before, to meet in advance. He gets there first.

## What he is leaning on

*I've **hung my hope upon the word You spoke**.*

The Hebrew is **lidvarcha yichalti** — for Your word I have waited.

## The night watches

*My eyes are open **through the watch of night**.*

The Hebrew is **qiddemu einai ashmurot** — my eyes anticipated the night watches.

The same verb again. He is ahead of the watchmen too.

## What he does with the hours

*To **turn Your promise over in my mind**.*

The Hebrew is **lasiach be'imratecha** — to meditate on Your word.

**Siach** — to muse, to talk to oneself, to go over something.

## The two ends of the day

Before dawn, and through the night watches.

The section brackets the whole clock and names the same activity at both ends.

## The plea

*Hear my voice, LORD, **out of Your steadfast love**; revive me by the justice of Your judgments.*

The Hebrew is **kechasdecha** — according to Your **chesed**.

The appeal is to covenant love, and the reviving is asked for by way of His judgments.

## The pursuers

*The ones who **chase down mischief draw in close**; they've **wandered far away from what You teach**.*

The Hebrew is **qarevu rodphei zimmah** — the pursuers of wickedness have drawn near.

Two distances in one verse: near to him, far from the law.

## The answer

*But **You are near, O LORD, so near to me**.*

The Hebrew is **qarov attah YHWH**.

The same root as *drawn near* in the previous line.

They are close. He is closer.

## The age of it

*Long, long ago I learned from Your firm words that **You have founded them to stand forever**.*

The Hebrew is **qedem yadati me'edotecha ki le'olam yesadtam** — of old I have known… that You
founded them forever.

**Qedem** — ancient time, and the same root as *anticipate* earlier in the section.

## What the psalm says elsewhere about the night

*At midnight I will rise to give thanks to You, because of Your righteous judgments.*

*My eyes are awake through the night watches, that I may meditate on Your word.*

## The section that follows

**Resh**: *Consider my affliction and deliver me, for I do not forget Your law.*

*Great are Your tender mercies, O LORD; **revive me according to Your judgments**.*

## The watches

*My eyes are awake through **the night watches**, that I may meditate on Your word.*

He divides the night by them — *I rise before the dawning of the morning and cry for help*.
The psalm is written by somebody who is up early and awake late, and both are spent on the
same thing.

*Now in the morning, having risen a long while before daylight, **He went out and departed to a
solitary place; and there He prayed**.*

**Mark** records it of **Jesus** after a night of healing that had run late.

## What he is sure of

*Concerning Your testimonies, I have known of old that **You have founded them forever**.*

*"**Heaven and earth will pass away, but My words will by no means pass away.**"*

## The nearness

*You are near, O LORD… **they draw near who follow after wickedness**.*

Both are said in the same breath. The trouble is approaching and so is He, and the psalm does
not pretend only one of them is real.

*"**Lo, I am with you always**, even to the end of the age."*

> **The verb.** **Qadam** — to arrive ahead of. He uses it of the dawn and of the night watches:
> in this section he is early at both ends of the day.
