A servant goes out early and finds the town surrounded.

## What led to it

The king of Aram had been raiding Israel, and every ambush failed because Elisha kept
telling Israel's king where the camps were.

The Aramean king assumed a traitor, and was told: *Elisha, the prophet who is in Israel,
**tells the king of Israel the words that you speak in your bedroom**.*

His response was to send an army after one man.

## The morning

*When the **servant of the man of God rose early** the next morning and went out.*

He goes out first, before Elisha.

## What he sees

*There it was — **an army with horses and chariots ringed all around the city**.*

The Hebrew is *the city was surrounded*.

The verse before says the Aramean king *sent horses and chariots and a great army* by
night, and they came and surrounded Dothan while everyone slept.

Nothing about his report is wrong.

## The question

*"**Oh, my master**," the servant said, "**what shall we do?**"*

The Hebrew is *alas, my master*.

Two men in a town with an army round it, and one of them is a prophet with no army at all.

## The answer

The next verse: *"**Do not fear, for those who are with us are more than those who are with
them**."*

Which is, on the visible evidence, absurd. There are two of them.

## The prayer

*And Elisha prayed, and said, "**LORD, I pray, open his eyes that he may see**."*

He does not pray for the army to leave, or for an escape, or for the servant's courage.

He prays about eyes.

## What was there

*Then the LORD opened the eyes of the young man, and he saw. And behold, **the mountain was
full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha**.*

They had been there the whole time. Nothing was added; something was shown.

## The second prayer

Elisha prays again, and this time for the opposite: *strike this people, I pray, with
blindness*.

Then he walks into the middle of the blinded army and says *this is not the way, nor is
this the city; follow me* — and leads them into Samaria.

## What he does with them

The king of Israel asks *shall I kill them?* twice.

*"You shall not kill them… **set food and water before them**, that they may eat and drink
and go to their master."*

*Then he prepared a great feast for them; and after they ate and drank, he sent them away.*

An army that came to arrest him is fed and released.

## The result

*So the bands of Syrian raiders **came no more into the land of Israel**.*

## Where the chariots turn up again

Elisha's own master had gone up in one: *suddenly a chariot of fire and horses of fire swept
between them*. See the note on 2 Kings 2:11–12.

And what Elisha shouted then — *the chariots and horsemen of Israel!* — is what the servant
is now shown a hillside of.

## What the servant was not told

To be braver. The prophet's whole response to the panic is one prayer about sight.

> **What changed on the hill.** Not the odds. The Aramean army was there and stayed there.
> One man saw what else was on the ground, and the prayer that got him there was about
> eyes.
