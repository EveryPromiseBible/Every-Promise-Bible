One verse to husbands, and it ends with a consequence nobody expects.

## "Your turn"

Peter has just spent six verses on wives. This verse is the answering half, and it
is shorter — but everything in it is a command and there is no let-out.

## Live with her thoughtfully

*Live with her **thoughtfully, with real understanding**.*

The Greek is *according to knowledge* — **gnosis**. To dwell with her knowledgeably.

It assumes study. A man is expected to know this particular woman: what she is
carrying, what she is like, what things cost her. Not women in general.

## "Give her honour"

**Time** — the word for worth, value, price. It is the noun used of the price paid
for something.

To honour her is to treat her as expensive.

## "As the weaker of the two"

The Greek is *the weaker vessel*, and **skeuos** is a vessel — a container, and in
that world often a valuable one.

The comparison is between two vessels. Both are vessels; one is described as
weaker, and the instruction that follows is to handle it with more care, not less
respect.

It is the reasoning you apply to the fine china rather than the tin mug.

## The equality clause

*And as someone who comes into **the grace of life right alongside you**.*

Greek: **sunkleronomos** — co-heir, joint inheritor. The same word Paul uses of
believers being *joint heirs with Christ*.

Whatever *weaker vessel* means, Peter rules out the obvious misreading in the very
next clause. She inherits everything he inherits, equally, and *alongside* is his
word.

## The consequence

*— **so that nothing gets in the way of your prayers**.*

The Greek is to cut into, to hinder — it was used of breaking up a road to stop an
army advancing.

Peter attaches a man's prayer life to how he treats his wife, and puts it in the
same sentence rather than a separate paragraph.

There is no equivalent warning to the wives in the passage. It is aimed at him.

## What the whole passage is doing

Peter is writing to scattered believers under pressure, and the household section
runs servants, wives, husbands. Every part of it is about voluntary honour given
where the world of that time gave none.

The husband is the one told his access to God is on the line.

> **What Paul says in his version.** *Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also
> loved the church and gave Himself for her.* Two apostles, two letters, and neither
> gives a husband anything to require — only things to give. See the note on
> Ephesians 5:22–24.
