Paul has spent a chapter arguing that the dead are raised. Here he deals with the people
who are not dead.

## The problem

*Flesh and blood **cannot inherit the kingdom of God**, and **what decays cannot inherit
what never decays**.*

The Greek is **phthora** (corruption, decay) against **aphtharsia** (incorruption).

Not that the body is bad. That it is the wrong material for the destination.

## The announcement

*Listen, **I am telling you a mystery**.*

The Greek is **musterion** — not a puzzle, but something previously undisclosed and now
told.

Paul uses it of things that could not have been worked out and had to be revealed.

## The mystery

*Not all of us will fall asleep, **but all of us will be changed**.*

Two clauses, and the second is the universal one. Dying is optional in the sentence;
changing is not.

**Fall asleep** is the New Testament's usual word for a believer's death. Paul uses it
throughout this chapter.

## The speed

*In **a moment**, in **the twinkling of an eye**.*

The Greek for *moment* is **atomos** — indivisible, uncuttable. The word behind *atom*.
The smallest unit of time there is.

**The twinkling of an eye** — **rhipe**, a flick, a dart. It is used of the beat of a
wing.

## The signal

*At **the last trumpet**. For **the trumpet will sound**.*

Trumpets in Israel called the assembly, moved the camp, and announced the new moon and
the Day of Atonement.

Paul says the same in Thessalonians: *the Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a
shout, with the voice of an archangel, and with the trumpet of God.* See the note on
1 Thessalonians 4:16–18.

## The two groups

*And **the dead will be raised beyond the reach of decay**, and **we will be changed**.*

Paul says *we*. He wrote as a man who expected to be in the second group.

## What follows

*For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on
immortality.*

The Greek for *put on* is **endusasthai** — to get dressed, to put on clothing.

Paul uses the same verb elsewhere of putting on Christ, and of the armour.

## The taunt

*"**O Death, where is your sting? O Hades, where is your victory?**"*

He quotes Hosea, and turns a prophet's line into a mockery of the thing being quoted
about.

*The sting of death is sin, and the strength of sin is the law. **But thanks be to God,
who gives us the victory** through our Lord Jesus Christ.*

## The last verse of the chapter

*Therefore, my beloved brethren, **be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work
of the Lord**, knowing that your labour is not in vain in the Lord.*

Fifty-seven verses on the resurrection, and the application is to keep working.

And the next sentence in the letter is *now concerning the collection*. See the note on
1 Corinthians 16:1–4.

> **What is promised to everybody.** Not that nobody dies. Paul says plainly that some
> will and some will not, and then says all of them are changed — in a unit of time he
> describes as indivisible.
