A prophet is told to put a legal question to the experts, in public, and let them answer it themselves.

## The date

*On **the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, in the second year of Darius**.*

Haggai dates every oracle. This is the fourth and last, delivered in Kislev — December, 520 BC.

Three months to the day since the work restarted.

## The precision

The book has five dated messages across four months. Nothing else in the prophets is timestamped
this closely.

## The instruction

*This is what the LORD Almighty says. "**Ask the priests for a ruling**."*

The Hebrew is **she'al-na et-hakohanim torah** — ask the priests for *torah*, for instruction.

**Torah** here is not the five books. It is a priestly ruling on a point of law — the technical
sense the word carries in Leviticus and Deuteronomy.

## Whose job this was

The priests'. *They shall teach Jacob Your judgments, and Israel Your law*, and *the priest's
lips should keep knowledge, and people should seek the law from his mouth*.

Deciding clean from unclean was the priest's daily function.

## What the method is

Haggai does not tell them the answer. He asks them a question about their own subject and lets
them state the law out loud.

He does it twice, and both times the priests answer correctly.

## The first question

*"If someone carries **consecrated meat in the fold of his garment**, and that fold touches some
bread or stew, some wine or oil or any other food, **does the food become holy?**" The priests
answered, "**No**."* See the note on Haggai 2:12.

## The second

*"If someone **made unclean by a dead body** touches any of these things, **does it become
unclean?**" And the priests answered, "**Yes**."* See the note on Haggai 2:13.

## The asymmetry

Holiness does not transfer at second hand. Defilement does.

That is the law, correctly stated by the men whose job it was to know it.

## What it is applied to

*"**So is this people, and so is this nation before Me**," says the LORD, "and so is every work of
their hands; and what they offer there is unclean."*

The ruling is turned on the congregation. Offerings made on the altar of a people whose priority
was elsewhere did not sanctify the rest of their lives.

## What is being said about the sixteen years

They had been sacrificing. Ezra records the altar rebuilt and the burnt offerings resumed years
before the temple was.

The worship was going on. The ruling says it was not making anything else holy.

## The date the promise attaches to

*"**From this day forward** I will bless you."

Same verse, same day.

The blessing is dated from the day the question was asked, not from the day the temple was
finished — which was still four years away.

## What the other direction looks like

A woman with an issue of blood, unclean for twelve years, touching the hem of a garment in a
crowd. See the note on Mark 5:25–29.

And a dead girl's hand taken by a man who then told them to give her something to eat. See the
note on Mark 5:38–43.

Under Haggai's ruling both contacts run one way. In Mark they run the other.

> **The method.** He does not preach the law at them. He asks the men whose job it is, and they
> state it correctly — and then he turns it round.
