Eight proverbs in pairs, and the third one is about a sentry.

## The listener

*A wise child **leans in when a parent teaches**. A mocker **never hears the warning at all**.*

The Hebrew is **ben chakham musar av** — a wise son, a father's instruction.

**Letz** — the mocker, one of the book's three standing characters, alongside the fool and the
simple.

The difference named is not intelligence. It is whether the rebuke registers.

## The fruit

*Good words are **fruit you get to taste**.*

The Hebrew is **mipperi phi-ish yochal tov** — from the fruit of a man's mouth he eats good.

The image recurs in the book: *a man's stomach shall be satisfied from the fruit of his mouth*.

What you say comes back as something you eat.

## The guard

*Guard your mouth and **guard your life**.*

The Hebrew is **notzer piv shomer naphsho** — the one guarding his mouth keeps his soul.

Two different verbs for guarding, one for the mouth and one for the life, in the same clause.

## The opposite

*Fling words wide and **watch things break**.*

The Hebrew is **posheq sephatav mechittah-lo** — one opening wide his lips, ruin to him.

**Pasaq** — to part, to open wide. The image is of a gate left standing open.

## The two appetites

*The lazy **want and want and stay empty**. The steady **want and end up full**.*

The Hebrew is **mit'avvah va'ayin naphsho atzel** — the soul of the sluggard craves and has
nothing.

Both men want. The proverb does not say the lazy man lacks desire.

## The hatred

*Honest hearts **hate what is false**.*

The Hebrew is **devar-sheqer** — a word of falsehood.

## The standing

*Uprightness **keeps the blameless standing**. Wickedness **knocks the sinner flat**.*

The Hebrew is **tzedaqah titzor tam-derek** — righteousness guards the one blameless in the way.

The third guarding verb in eight verses.

## The two pretenders

*One **pretends to be rich and owns nothing**. Another **looks poor and holds a quiet fortune**.*

The Hebrew is **yesh mit'asher ve'eyn kol** — there is one making himself rich and having nothing.

**Mitroshesh vehon rav** — making himself poor, and great wealth.

Both are self-presentations, and both are false. Proverbs notices the second as well as the first.

## The last one

*Wealth **can ransom a man's life**. The poor **never even hear the threat**.*

The Hebrew is **kopher nephesh-ish oshro** — the ransom of a man's life is his riches.

A rich man can be extorted. A poor man is not worth threatening.

The proverb is not praising poverty. It notices a cost of wealth that the wealthy do not.

## Where the guarding proverb recurs

*He who guards his mouth and his tongue **keeps his soul from troubles**.*

And *whoever guards his mouth and tongue keeps himself out of trouble* — chapter 21.

The book returns to it repeatedly.

## The psalm

*Who among you loves being alive… then **keep your tongue from evil**.* See the note on
Psalms 34:11–14.

Same instruction, in answer to a question about long life.

## What James says

*If anyone does not stumble in word, he is a perfect man, **able also to bridle the whole body**.*
See the note on James 3:1–5.

> **The pairing.** *Guard your mouth and guard your life* — two different Hebrew verbs for guarding
> in one clause, and eight verses that use the root three times.
