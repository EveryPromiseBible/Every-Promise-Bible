The last verse of the chapter, and the most quoted sentence in it depends on the
clause after it.

## The two things

*No **weapon** shaped against you will succeed, and every **tongue** that rises to
accuse you, you will prove wrong.*

A weapon and a tongue. Force and accusation.

Note what is not promised: that the weapon will not be made. The verse before says
outright *I have created the blacksmith who blows the coals in the fire and brings
forth an instrument for his work.*

The forging happens. The prospering does not.

## The tongue

*Every tongue that **rises to accuse you**, you will prove wrong.*

The Hebrew is *you shall condemn* — a courtroom verb.

It is the only active thing the person does in the whole verse, and it is answering an
accusation rather than fighting a weapon.

## The clause everything rests on

*This is **the inheritance of the servants of the LORD**. This is their **vindication,
and it comes from Me**, says the LORD.*

The Hebrew for *vindication* is **tsedaqah** — righteousness.

And the last three words are the ones that hold the verse up: **from Me**.

Their righteousness is not theirs. The chapter says so plainly, and the promise about
weapons is attached to it as its consequence.

## An inheritance

**Nachalah** — the family portion, what you receive because of who you belong to.

Nobody earns an inheritance, and nobody keeps it by performance.

## Where the chapter has come from

Isaiah 54 follows Isaiah 53 — *He was wounded for our transgressions… by His stripes we
are healed.*

The chapter opens by telling a barren woman to sing, and to enlarge her tent for
children she does not have. See the note on Isaiah 54:1–3.

Then: *you will be **established in righteousness**… terror will be far from you.* See
the note on Isaiah 54:13–14.

And the guarantee in the middle: *as I have sworn that the waters of Noah would no
longer cover the earth, **so have I sworn that I would not be angry with you**.* See the
note on Isaiah 54:9–10.

A rainbow, offered as collateral.

## The order of the chapter

Sing. Enlarge. Be established in righteousness. And then, last: no weapon will
prosper.

The famous line is the conclusion, not the premise.

> **What the servants have.** Not immunity from being shot at. An inheritance, and a
> righteousness that came from somewhere else, and a promise about outcomes rather
> than about weather.
