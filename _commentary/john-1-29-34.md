John the Baptist gives Jesus a title nobody had used before, and it carries a
thousand years of Israel's worship in three words.

## Which lamb

Every lamb in Israel's memory is standing behind the phrase:

- the ram caught in the thicket in place of Isaac
- the Passover lamb whose blood went on the doorposts
- the two lambs offered every morning and evening in the temple
- the lamb Isaiah described, led to the slaughter and silent

John does not specify. He says *the Lamb of God*, and lets the whole system
answer to it.

## "Takes away"

The verb means to lift and carry off. Not covers, not compensates for — removes,
by carrying.

That is the difference between the old arrangement and this one. The sacrifices
covered sin year after year; this one is described as taking it away and going
off with it.

## What the sin offering looked like

Leviticus 4 sets out the procedure, and the order of it is worth walking through:

- the man brings the animal — **the animal is examined**, not the man
- he **lays his hand on its head**, and the Hebrew is to lean, to press down
- the animal is killed in his place
- the blood is applied by the priest
- *and it shall be forgiven him*
- **and the man goes home**

Nothing about his sincerity, his record, or his resolve enters the ceremony. The
inspection falls on the lamb.

That is why the four days of examination in Passion Week matter, and why every
delegation that came at Him went away with nothing, and why Pilate's verdict is
*I find no fault in Him.* The lamb was looked over in public and passed. See the
note on Matthew 21:23–27.

## "Of the world"

Not of Israel. John the Baptist, a Jewish prophet in the Judean wilderness,
extends it to the whole world in his first recorded description of Jesus.

## "I did not know myself who He was"

He says it twice. They were relatives — Luke has their mothers meeting before
either was born — so he knew the man. What he did not know was the identity.

The confirmation came by a sign he had been told to watch for: the Spirit coming
down and **staying**. The verb is the one John uses all through his Gospel for
abiding.

> **What he calls himself.** Nothing. Asked earlier who he is, he says he is a
> voice. Here he gives his whole testimony and it is entirely about somebody
> else — including the line *He was there long before I ever was*, said by a man
> six months older.
