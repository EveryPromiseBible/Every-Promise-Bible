Isaiah's second chapter opens with a picture that requires water to do something
water does not do.

## Streaming upward

*All the nations will **stream toward it** like rivers running home.*

The Hebrew verb **nahar** is built from the noun for a river. Rivers run down.
These are running up a mountain.

The image is deliberately impossible, and it is the first thing said about the
nations.

## The highest of the mountains

*The mountain of the LORD's house will stand as the highest of the mountains,
lifted above the hills.*

Mount Zion is not a large hill. It is overtopped by the Mount of Olives across the
valley, and by most of the ranges around it.

The verse is not measuring altitude.

## Who does the inviting

*Many peoples will come and say, **Come, let us go up**…*

Nobody is sent to fetch them in these two verses. The invitation is on the lips of
the peoples themselves, and they say it to each other.

## What they want when they get there

*He will teach us His ways, and we will walk in His paths.*

Not protection, not prosperity, not a verdict on their enemies. Teaching.

And the second clause commits them before the first is answered — *we will walk*.
They ask to be taught by somebody they have already decided to obey.

## Where it comes from

*For the teaching goes out from Zion, the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.*

**Teaching** is **torah**, which is instruction before it is legislation — the
word a parent's guidance is called in Proverbs.

> **What comes next in the chapter.** Swords into ploughshares, spears into
> pruning hooks, and nations that *will not learn war any more*. The disarmament
> is placed after the teaching, not before it — in Isaiah's order, they stop
> fighting because they have been to the mountain.
