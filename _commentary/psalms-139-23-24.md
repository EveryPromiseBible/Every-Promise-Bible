title: Search me, and lead me

The last two verses of the psalm, and they ask for the very thing the first six verses say has
already happened.

## What the psalm opened with

*O LORD, **You have searched me and You know me**.* See the note on Psalms 139:1–6.

Past tense, in verse 1. The searching is a completed fact before David says a word about it.

## What he asks for at the end

*"**Search me, O God, and know my heart.** Test me and know my anxious thoughts."*

He asks for what he has already described. Not because it needs doing, but because he is
consenting to it.

*For **the word of God is living and powerful, and sharper than any two-edged sword**… and there
is no creature hidden from His sight, but all things are naked and open to the eyes of Him to
whom we must give account.*

**Hebrews** says the same thing and then, in the very next sentence, goes to the throne of
grace — *let us therefore come boldly*.

## Why a man can ask for this

Because of what he has just spent twenty-two verses saying. *Where can I go from Your Spirit?…
If I make my bed in hell, behold, You are there.* And: *I am fearfully and wonderfully made.*

The invitation to be examined comes at the end of a poem about being completely known and
completely held. It is not the prayer of a man hoping to pass.

*There is therefore now **no condemnation** to those who are in **Christ Jesus**.*

## The last line

*"And **lead me in the way everlasting**."*

The Hebrew is **derek olam** — the ancient way, the everlasting road.

*"**I am the way**, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me."*

The psalm's final request is for a road, and **Jesus** answers it with a Person.

> **The order of it.** Verse 1 says He has already searched. Verse 23 asks Him to. Between the
> two is a psalm about being known everywhere and held anyway — which is the only ground on
> which anybody invites that examination.
