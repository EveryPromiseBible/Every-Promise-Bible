Paul asks a question he clearly thinks they should not need asked.

## "Do you not know"

He uses the phrase ten times in this letter. It is not gentle — it means *you already
have this information.*

## The claim

*You are **the temple of God**, and **the Spirit of God makes His home in you**.*

The Greek for *temple* is **naos** — not the whole temple complex but the inner
sanctuary, the holy place itself.

Not the courts. The room.

## Who "you" is

Plural, throughout. Paul is addressing the church at Corinth as a body — *you all,
together, are the sanctuary.*

He makes the individual point elsewhere in the same letter: *your body is the temple
of the Holy Spirit who is in you.* Both are true, and this one is corporate.

## Which church he says it to

Corinth. The letter's own list: division into factions, a man sleeping with his
father's wife, believers suing each other, drunkenness at the Lord's table, chaos in
the meetings, and some of them denying the resurrection.

Paul calls that congregation the sanctuary of God, in chapter 3, before correcting
any of it.

## The warning

*If anyone **ruins** the temple of God, **God will ruin him**.*

The same Greek verb both times — **phtheiro**, to corrupt, to destroy, to spoil.

What you do to it is what happens to you.

## What the ruining is, in context

The chapter is about the divisions: *I am of Paul… I am of Apollos.* And about
building on the foundation with gold and silver or with wood, hay and straw.

The thing that ruins the temple in this passage is factionalism — men splitting a
congregation around their favourite teacher.

## The reason

*For the temple of God is **holy**, and **that temple is what you are**.*

The Greek puts the emphasis at the end: *which you are.*

## What this replaced

Solomon's temple took seven years, a hundred and eighty thousand men, cedar, gold,
and stone cut silently in a quarry. See the note on 1 Kings 6:6–8.

And Solomon's own verdict at the dedication: *heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot
contain You. How much less this temple which I have built!*

Paul tells a fractious church in Greece that they are the room.

> **What Jesus said about the building.** *"Destroy this temple, and in three days I
> will raise it up."* They thought He meant the forty-six-year construction project
> they were standing in. *But He was speaking of the temple of His body.*
