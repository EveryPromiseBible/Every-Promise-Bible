The letter opens by placing the Son above the angels, and the ground given is not power. It is a
name and a relationship.

## Inherited, not awarded

*That set Him as far above the angels as **the name He has come into** is above theirs.*

**Kekleronomeken** — has inherited. A perfect tense, and an inheritance word: this is not a title
voted to Him for services rendered. It came to Him as a Son, which is the point of the two
quotations that follow.

## "You are My Son"

*"You are My Son; **today I have become Your Father**."*

Psalm 2, and the New Testament tells us where that *today* landed. Preaching in Antioch, Paul says
God fulfilled the promise *in that He has raised up Jesus, as it is also written in the second
Psalm: "You are My Son, today I have begotten You."*

The resurrection is the day the declaration was made publicly. Not the day He became the Son — the
letter has already called Him the one *through whom He made the worlds* — but the day the whole
creation was told.

## "I will be a Father to Him"

The second quotation is Nathan's word to David: *I will be a Father to him, and he shall be a son to
Me* — spoken in the first instance about Solomon, a boy who built a temple and then lost his way.

Hebrews lifts it out and asks the question that settles the argument: *did God ever say that to an
angel?* The promise was always aiming past Solomon at Someone the house was really for.

## Why any of this mattered to the readers

These were people under pressure to go back to a religion mediated by angels — *the word spoken
through angels*, as the next chapter puts it, and the law which Stephen said they *received by the
direction of angels*.

So the comparison is not an abstract ranking of heavenly beings. It is the whole question of whether
you would leave a Son to go back to servants.

> **Servants are given errands; the Son is given a name.** Every angel in Scripture arrives, does
> one thing, and goes. Jesus Christ inherited — and the difference between an errand and an
> inheritance is the difference between the two covenants this letter is comparing. You were not
> brought into the household of a message. You were brought into the family of a Son.
