Five things put down, and then an appetite.

## The list

*Strip all of this off and leave it lying there: **wanting to hurt people, working an angle,
wearing a face that is not yours, resenting what someone else has, running your mouth to tear a
person down**.*

Malice, deceit, hypocrisy, envy, and evil speaking — five, and all five are social. Not one of
them is a private vice.

## The verb

**Apothemenoi** — having laid aside, the word for taking off a garment.

The same word Paul uses in *put off the old man*, and the word Luke uses for the coats laid at
the feet of a young man named Saul.

## The image

*Be like **a baby a few days old, howling for milk**.*

The Greek is **artigenneta brephe** — newborn infants.

**Epipothesate** is the verb: crave, long for intensely. It is used elsewhere of Paul aching to
see people again.

A newborn does not need to be trained to be hungry. Nobody teaches it to cry for milk.

## What kind of milk

*What you want is **the word, unwatered and unmixed** — that is what makes you grow.*

The Greek is **adolon** — unadulterated, with nothing added.

The word is the negative of *dolos*, the *working an angle* from the list two verses earlier.
Peter has just told them to put down deceit and now describes the milk as undeceitful.

**Logikon** is the other adjective — of the word, reasonable. The milk is the word itself.

## What it produces

*That is what makes you grow.*

The Greek is **auxethete eis soterian** — that you may grow up into salvation.

Growth, not initiation. Peter is not telling them how to be born; he is telling people already
born how to get bigger.

## The reason given

*After all, **you have tasted Him already — and He is kind**.*

The Greek is **egeusasthe hoti chrestos ho Kyrios** — you have tasted that the Lord is good.

It is Psalm 34: *O taste and see that the LORD is good.* See the note on
Psalms 34:8–10.

## Why the psalm is quoted

The appetite is explained by the previous taste.

Peter does not say *crave the word because you should*. He says the craving follows a flavour
already known.

## What is being said about milk

Paul uses milk as the elementary stage — *I fed you with milk and not with solid food* — and
Hebrews uses it as a rebuke: *you have come to need milk and not solid food*.

Peter does not. Here the milk is the word itself, and the craving for it is the healthy sign,
not the immature one.

## Where it goes next

*Coming to Him as to a living stone… **you also, as living stones, are being built up a spiritual
house**.* See the note on 1 Peter 2:4–5.

And then the priesthood: *a chosen generation, a royal priesthood*. See the note on
1 Peter 2:9.

The newborn in verse 2 is a priest by verse 9.

## The pun the English loses

**Chrestos** — good, kind — sounded almost identical to **Christos** in the Greek of the day.

*You have tasted that the Lord is good* and *you have tasted that the Lord is Christ* are one
letter apart, and Peter's readers would have heard both.

> **Where the appetite comes from.** *You have tasted Him already — and He is kind.* The craving
> is not commanded into existence; it follows a flavour that has already been on the tongue.
