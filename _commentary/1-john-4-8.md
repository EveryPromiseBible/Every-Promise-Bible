One sentence, and the second half of it is unlike anything else said about God in
Scripture.

## The claim

*Because **love is what God is**.*

The Greek is *ho theos agape estin* — God is love. No article on *love*: not *God is
the love*, and not *God is loving*.

It is a statement of what He consists of.

## What it does not say

The sentence does not reverse. John is not saying that love is God, or that wherever
there is affection there is deity.

He is saying that if you took away the loving there would be no God left to describe.

## The other two of the same shape

John writes three sentences like this and they are the only ones of their kind:

- *God is **light**, and in Him is no darkness at all.*
- *God is **spirit**, and those who worship Him must worship in spirit and truth.*
- *God is **love**.*

Not attributes He has. Categories of what He is.

## The negative half

*Whoever does not love **has never known God**.*

The Greek is aorist — *did not come to know*. Not *has stopped knowing*; never started.

John does not argue it. He states the test and moves on.

## Where he says it twice

Eight verses later: *God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God, and God in
him.*

He says it, explains it for eight verses, and says it again.

## What he puts in between

*In this the love of God was manifested toward us, that God has **sent His only begotten
Son** into the world, that we might live through Him.*

*In this is love, **not that we loved God, but that He loved us**, and sent His Son to
be the propitiation for our sins.*

John will not let the word stay abstract. Every time he uses it he attaches it to an
event with a date.

And he insists on the direction: not our love reaching up, but His arriving.

## The definition he refuses

He never defines love by feeling. The nearest thing to a definition in his letters is
*by this we know love, because He laid down His life for us.*

## What it means for fear

*There is **no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear**, because fear involves
torment.*

Four verses later, and it is the practical conclusion he draws from *God is love*. See
the note on 1 John 4:18.

> **Who wrote it.** The man who called himself *the disciple whom Jesus loved* — his own
> description of himself, five times, in his own Gospel. He is not writing a definition
> from the outside.
