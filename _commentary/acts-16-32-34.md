The jailer's night, told in the order things happened.

## What had happened first

Paul and Silas had been *beaten with rods*, thrown into the inner prison and *fastened in
the stocks*.

*But at midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners
were listening to them.*

Then the earthquake, the doors open, the chains loose — and the jailer drawing his sword,
because a Roman guard who lost his prisoners took their sentence.

*"Do yourself no harm, for **we are all here**."* Nobody had run.

## The question and the answer

*"Sirs, **what must I do to be saved?**"*

*"**Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household**."*
See the note on Acts 16:29–31.

## The teaching

*And they **spoke the word of the Lord to him and to everyone in his house**.*

The household is included in the sentence, and then in the teaching, and then in everything
that follows.

## The hour

*That **same hour of the night**.*

Still the middle of the night. Nothing waits for morning.

## What he did first

*He took them and **washed their wounds**.*

The Greek is **elousen apo ton plegon** — washed them from the stripes.

He is the man responsible for those stripes, or at least for the men in the cell after them.
Luke does not remark on it.

## What was done to him

*And **he was baptized at once, he and all his family**.*

Two washings in one sentence, in opposite directions. He washes them, and then he is washed.

Luke sets the two verbs a few words apart.

## The meal

*Then he **brought them up into his house and set food before them**.*

Prisoners at the jailer's table, in the jailer's house, before dawn.

## The state he was in

*And he was **full of joy, along with his whole household, at having believed in God**.*

The Greek is **egalliasato** — exulted, rejoiced greatly. And **panoiki** — with all his
house, a word that occurs once in the New Testament.

The joy is his, and the whole house is in the clause with him.

## What happens in the morning

The magistrates send word to release them quietly, and Paul refuses to go.

*"They have beaten us openly, uncondemned Romans, and have thrown us into prison. And now do
they put us out secretly? **No indeed! Let them come themselves and get us out**."*

He had said nothing about his citizenship the night before, while they were being beaten.

The magistrates come in person and ask them to leave the city.

## Who the church at Philippi was

Lydia, a dealer in purple from Thyatira, whose *heart the Lord opened*.

A slave girl with a spirit of divination, whose owners started the riot when the income
stopped.

And a Roman jailer.

Three converts in one chapter, and no two of them share a language, a class or a country.

## What Paul writes to them later

*I thank my God upon every remembrance of you… for **your fellowship in the gospel from the
first day until now**.* See the note on Philippians 1:3–6.

And *rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice.* Written from another prison, to
a church founded in one. See the note on Philippians 4:4–5.

> **The order of the sentence.** He washed their wounds, and then he was washed. Luke puts
> both verbs in the same breath and lets them sit there.
