Eight proverbs, and one of them is about a light that goes where nobody else can look.

## The scales

*Unequal weights are hateful to the LORD, and **dishonest scales are not good**.*

The Hebrew is **even va'even** — a stone and a stone. Two sets of weights, a heavy one for buying
and a light one for selling.

Proverbs raises it four times, and the law forbids it: *you shall not have in your bag differing
weights*.

The understatement of the second clause — *not good* — is the Hebrew way of saying the opposite
very firmly.

## The steps

*A person's **steps are directed by the LORD**; how then can anyone understand his own way?*

The Hebrew is **miYHWH mitzadei-gaver** — from the LORD are a man's steps.

The question is not rhetorical despair. It follows from the first clause: if the direction comes
from outside, self-analysis will not find it.

*A man's heart plans his way, but the LORD directs his steps.*

## The vow

*It is **a trap to blurt out, "It is holy,"** and only later to weigh the vow.*

The Hebrew is **yala qodesh** — to say rashly *holy*.

Dedicating something to God in a moment and doing the arithmetic afterwards.

Ecclesiastes says the same: *do not be rash with your mouth… **better not to vow than to vow and
not pay***.

## The king

*A wise king **winnows out the wicked** and drives the wheel over them.*

The image is threshing — the cart wheel driven over grain on the floor to separate it.

## The lamp

*The human spirit is **the lamp of the LORD**, searching **every hidden room within**.*

The Hebrew is **ner YHWH nishmat adam** — the lamp of the LORD is the breath of man.

**Neshamah** — breath. The word from Genesis 2:7: *God breathed into his nostrils the breath of
life*. See the note on Psalms 150:6.

## The searching

**Chophes kol-chadrei-vaten** — searching all the chambers of the belly.

**Cheder** — an inner room, the innermost part of a house. The word used of a bridal chamber and
of a store room.

## What the proverb claims

The thing God breathed into a man is the light He searches him with.

Not an external inspection. The lamp is inside, and it is His.

## The two things that guard a king

*Steady love and faithfulness guard the king, and **by love his throne is upheld**.*

The Hebrew is **chesed ve'emet** — the pair. Covenant love and faithfulness.

The pair that becomes *grace and truth* in John's prologue. See the note on
John 1:14–18.

## The two ages

*The glory of the young is **their strength**, and the splendor of the old is **their gray head**.*

The Hebrew is **tipheret bachurim kocham** — the beauty of young men is their power.

**Hadar zeqenim sevah** — the honour of old men is grey hair.

Each age is given its own word for glory, and neither is asked to have the other's.

## The last one

*Blows that wound **scrub away evil**, and beatings reach **the hidden rooms of the heart**.*

The Hebrew for the last phrase is **chadrei-vaten** — the same words used of what the lamp
searches, four verses earlier.

The proverb pairs them deliberately: the lamp reaches the inner rooms, and so does pain.

## What the chapter opened with

*Wine is a mocker, strong drink is a brawler, and whoever is led astray by it is not wise.*

And *even a child is known by his deeds, whether what he does is pure and right*.

## What Paul says about the searching

*But God has revealed them to us through His Spirit. For **the Spirit searches all things, yes,
the deep things of God**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 2:10–13.

*For what man knows the things of a man except the spirit of the man which is in him?*

> **The lamp.** **Neshamah** — the breath God put into the man at the beginning. The proverb says
> that is the light He searches him with, and it is inside.
