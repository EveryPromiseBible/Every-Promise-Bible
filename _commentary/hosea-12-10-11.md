God describes His own method of speaking, and then names what it was spent on.

## The three verbs

*I **spoke through the prophets**, gave **vision after vision**, told **parables through their
mouths**.*

The Hebrew gives three: spoke, multiplied visions, and used **similitudes** — **damah**, to
liken, to compare.

## The multiplying

*Vision after vision.*

The Hebrew is *I have multiplied visions* — **chazon**, the same word as Proverbs 29 and
Habakkuk 2. See the note on Proverbs 29:14–18.

Not one vision. A supply of them.

## The third method

*Told parables through their mouths.*

The Hebrew is *by the ministry of the prophets I have used similitudes*.

God says He spoke in likenesses — pictures standing for something else.

Which is exactly what the prophets do. Jeremiah's almond branch and boiling pot, Amos's
plumbline and basket of summer fruit, Ezekiel's valley of bones, Zechariah's lampstand.

And Hosea's own life: he is told to marry Gomer, and the marriage is the message.

## What Jesus does with the same method

*And **without a parable He did not speak to them**.*

Matthew adds that it fulfilled a psalm: *I will open My mouth in parables; I will utter
things kept secret from the foundation of the world.*

## What Hosea's own life was

*Go, take yourself a wife of harlotry… **for the land has committed great harlotry by
departing from the LORD**.*

Three children with names that are messages: Jezreel, Lo-Ruhamah (*no mercy*), and Lo-Ammi
(*not My people*).

And then the reversal: *I will have mercy on her who had not obtained mercy, and I will say
to those who were not My people, "You are My people."*

Paul quotes both in Romans 9. See the note on Romans 9:25–26.

## The second verse

*Is there **evil in Gilead**? Then **everything there has come to nothing**.*

Gilead was a Levitical territory east of the Jordan, and by Hosea's time a byword for
bloodshed — chapter 6 calls it *a city of evildoers, defiled with blood*.

## The altars

*In **Gilgal they sacrifice bulls**, and their **altars stand like heaps of stone in a plowed
field**.*

Gilgal was where Israel first camped after crossing the Jordan and where the twelve stones
were set up.

By Hosea's day it was a shrine centre, and he mentions it three times, never approvingly.

## The image

*Like heaps of stone in a plowed field.*

The Hebrew is *heaps in the furrows of the field*.

A farmer clearing a field piles the stones at the edges. The altars are described as those
piles — numerous, made of what was in the way, and worthless.

There is a pun underneath it: **Gilgal** and **gallim**, heaps.

## What the two verses do together

The first says God spoke in every way available — prophets, visions, pictures.

The second says what the country did with it: multiplied altars.

## Where the chapter goes

*By a prophet the LORD brought Israel out of Egypt, and by a prophet he was preserved.*

Moses is not named. The point is the office.

> **What God says about His own speaking.** Three methods and a plural: vision after
> vision, through the mouths of prophets, in likenesses — and the next sentence is about
> stone heaps in a ploughed field.
