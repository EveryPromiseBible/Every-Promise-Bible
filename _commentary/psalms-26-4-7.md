title: I wash my hands in innocence
updated: 2026-08-09

A psalm of protested innocence, and the hand-washing in it is the part worth following.

## What he claims

*I do not sit with liars… I will not sit with the wicked. **I wash my hands in innocence, and
circle Your altar**.*

He is asking to be examined — *examine me, O LORD, and prove me; try my mind and my heart* —
and the ground he offers is his own conduct.

## Where the same gesture goes wrong

*When Pilate saw that he could not prevail at all… he **took water and washed his hands** before
the multitude, saying, "I am innocent of the blood of this just Person."*

The one place in the gospels somebody washes their hands to declare innocence, it settles
nothing. The water changed no fact in the case.

## What the psalm does that Pilate does not

Circle the altar.

*I wash my hands in innocence, **and circle Your altar**, O LORD.* The washing is not the end of
it — he walks round the place where blood was shed, and the next line is a song of thanks.

Even in a psalm about his own integrity, the man is standing at a sacrifice.

## Where the washing is finally located

*Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, **having our hearts sprinkled
from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water**.*

**Hebrews** keeps both halves — the sprinkling and the washing — and neither is done by the
person drawing near.

*Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to His mercy He saved us,
through **the washing of regeneration** and renewing of the Holy Spirit.*

## How the psalm ends

*But as for me, I will walk in my integrity; **redeem me and be merciful to me**.*

The last line of a psalm about integrity asks for redemption and mercy. He does not finally
rest his case on the record he has just recited.

> **What he does after washing.** Circles the altar. The psalm will not leave the clean hands
> standing on their own — it walks them round a place where something died.
