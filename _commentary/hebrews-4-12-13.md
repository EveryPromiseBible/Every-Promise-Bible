Four claims about the word, and then the sentence turns and looks back at the reader.

## Where it sits

Directly after two chapters on the wilderness generation who heard the good news and did
not enter, and the instruction *let us be diligent to enter that rest*. See the note on
Hebrews 4:8–11.

The word that did them no good is the subject of the next sentence.

## Alive

*God's word is **alive**.*

The Greek is **zon** — living. It is the first word of the sentence in Greek, placed for
emphasis.

## Force

*There is **force** in it.*

The Greek is **energes** — active, at work, operative. The root of *energy*.

## The edge

*It has **a keener edge than any blade sharpened on both sides**.*

The Greek is **distomos** — literally *two-mouthed*. Greek describes the edge of a sword
as its mouth, and a double-edged sword as one with two.

The Hebrew does the same: **peh**, mouth, is the word for a blade's edge — and Isaiah's
threshing sledge has *mouths* for teeth. See the note on Isaiah 41:14–16.

## What it separates

*It goes in until it has separated **soul from spirit** and **joint from marrow**.*

Two pairs, and each is a pair of things nobody can get between.

Joint and marrow are not adjacent; marrow is inside the bone. The picture is of something
reaching past the surface to what is sealed inside.

## What it reads

*And it **reads the heart** — **every thought it ever had, every intention**.*

The Greek is **kritikos** — able to judge, discerning. The root of *critic*.

Two objects: **enthumeseon**, the thoughts and reflections, and **ennoion**, the
intentions and purposes.

## The turn

*Nothing that was ever made **stays hidden from Him**.*

The subject changes mid-passage. Verse 12 is about the word; verse 13 is about a person.

The writer does not mark the transition, and the effect is that the two are hard to
separate.

## The image

*Everything lies **stripped bare, throat exposed**, in front of the eyes of the One we
have to answer to.*

The Greek is **tetrachelismena** — from **trachelos**, the neck. It is the root of
*trachea*.

The word was used of a wrestler forcing an opponent's head back to expose the throat, and
of a sacrificial animal with the neck laid open for the knife.

**Stripped bare** — **gumna**, naked.

## What the next verse is

*Seeing then that we have **a great High Priest** who has passed through the heavens, Jesus
the Son of God, **let us hold fast our confession**.*

And then: *for we do not have a High Priest who cannot sympathise with our weaknesses,
but was **in all points tempted as we are**, yet without sin.*

*Let us therefore **come boldly to the throne of grace**, that we may obtain mercy and find
grace to help in time of need.*

The passage about being laid open is followed directly by an invitation to walk in
without hesitating — and by a priest described as knowing what it is like.

> **The order the writer uses.** Everything exposed, and then a throne to come to boldly.
> He puts them in consecutive sentences and the second is the conclusion of the first.
