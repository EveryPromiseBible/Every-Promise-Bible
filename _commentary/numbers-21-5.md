One verse of complaint, and the last clause of it is the writer's own comment.

## Who they speak against

*They spoke **against God and against Moses**.*

Both, and God first. Numbers is precise about the order elsewhere too — the
grumbling is aimed past the leader.

## The three complaints

*"Why did you bring us up out of Egypt **to die in the wilderness**? **There is no
bread. There is no water.** And **we loathe this miserable food**."*

Read them together and the last two contradict each other.

There is no bread — and we are sick of the bread. They complain about the absence of
food and the quality of the food in the same breath.

## The word

The Hebrew for *miserable* is **qeloqel** — worthless, contemptible, light. It is a
made-up-sounding reduplicated form, and it occurs nowhere else in the Old Testament.

They coined a word for it.

## The narrator's line

*The manna that had kept them alive — **they had come to despise even that**.*

That is the verdict on the sentence, and it names what the food actually did:
**kept them alive**.

Forty years of a substance falling every morning, wrapped in dew, that nobody had to
plant or store or pay for.

## What it had been called

*The bread of angels*, in Psalm 78. *The grain of heaven*. See the note on
Psalms 78:21–25.

Nothing about it changed between Exodus 16 and here. The description in Numbers 11 —
coriander seed, resin-pale, tasting of oil — still stood.

They had simply been eating it long enough to stop seeing it, and the same complaint
appears in chapter 11 and again here, ten chapters and many years apart. See the note
on Numbers 11:4–9.

## What followed

*So the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people, and many
of the people of Israel died.*

And then the pole. See the note on Numbers 21:8–9.

## Where they were when they said it

The verse before: *they journeyed from Mount Hor by the Way of the Red Sea, **to go
around the land of Edom**, and the soul of the people became very discouraged on the
way.*

A detour. Edom had refused them passage, so they had to go the long way round, and
the complaint comes out of the extra miles.

## What the cure was

They despised the bread that had kept them alive, and what came was serpents.

And the remedy was not the removal of the serpents. Moses prayed for that and it is not
what he got. *Make a fiery serpent, and set it on a pole; and it shall be that everyone who
is bitten, when he looks at it, shall live.* See the note on Numbers 21:8–9.

The thing that was killing them, lifted up on a pole, and looking at it is the whole of the
treatment.

*And **as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness**, even so must the Son of Man be
lifted up, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have eternal life.* See the
note on John 3:14–18.

Of every picture in the wilderness He could have taken for Himself — the manna, the rock,
the cloud — that is the one He chose.

*For He made Him who knew no sin **to be sin for us**.*

> **How the same chapter ends.** With a song. *Spring up, O well! All of you sing to
> it* — sung over a well at Beer, a few verses after the snakes. Numbers puts the
> loathing and the singing on the same page.
