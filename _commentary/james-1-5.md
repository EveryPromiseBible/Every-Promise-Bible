One short verse, and two Greek words in it are doing something the English has
to work hard to carry.

## "With both hands"

The word is **haplos**. It means singly, simply, without folds — and by
extension, without a hidden second motive. It is the opposite of giving with one
hand while keeping an account with the other.

So the promise is not merely that God gives generously in quantity. It is that
He gives *straightforwardly*: no strings running back, no ledger being kept, no
part of Him withholding while another part hands something over.

## "He never once makes you feel small for asking"

The second phrase is **me oneidizontos** — not reproaching. *Oneidizo* is the
word for upbraiding somebody, throwing something in their face, shaming them
with it.

James puts it in the sentence because he knows exactly what stops people asking.
It is not doubt that God can. It is the suspicion that the asking will be
remembered — that the answer will arrive with a comment attached about why you
needed it, or how often you have needed it lately.

He rules that out in four words.

---

## Where this sits in his letter

It comes immediately after James has told them to count it joy when they fall
into various trials, and that endurance has a work to finish.

So the wisdom on offer is not academic. It is for people in the middle of
something they do not understand, who need to know what to do next. That is when
a person is least confident of a welcome, and it is precisely there that James
puts the promise about not being shamed for asking.

## What is not in the verse

There is no qualification about the asker's state. Not *if any of you is worthy*,
not *if you have been faithful*. The condition is **short on wisdom** — the
lack itself is the qualification.

And the outcome is stated with equal plainness: *it will be put right into your
hands*. Not considered. Given.

> **On the verses that follow.** James goes on to talk about asking in faith and
> not being double-minded, and that is sometimes used to load the promise back up
> with conditions. Read what he actually describes: a person in two minds about
> whether to ask God at all, pulled one way and then the other. He is not setting
> a standard of confidence to reach. He is saying: decide who you are asking, and
> then ask him.
