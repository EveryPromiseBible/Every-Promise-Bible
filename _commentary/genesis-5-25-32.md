The oldest man who ever lived, and the year he died is the year the flood came.

## The list

*When Methuselah was 187, he fathered Lamech… **969 years in all**, and then he died.*

The longest recorded lifespan in Scripture.

## The refrain

Eight times in the chapter: *and then he died*.

The genealogy is a drumbeat of deaths. One man in it does not get the line — *Enoch walked with
God, and he was not, for God took him*. See the note on Genesis 5:21–24.

Methuselah is his son.

## The arithmetic

Methuselah is 187 when Lamech is born. Lamech is 182 when Noah is born. Noah is 600 when the
flood comes.

187 + 182 + 600 = 969.

The year the oldest man in the Bible dies is the year of the flood.

## What his name may mean

**Methushelach** — the second element is **shelach**, which can be a weapon, a dart, or the verb
*to send*.

Read that way: *when he is dead, it shall be sent*.

The reading is disputed and cannot be pressed. The arithmetic stands on its own.

## What the number is doing there

The longest life in the book sits in the generation before the judgment.

Peter's word for it: *the Divine longsuffering waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was being
prepared*. See the note on 1 Peter 3:18.

## Lamech's line

*When Lamech was 182, he fathered a son and **named him Noah**, saying, "**This one will bring us
comfort** from our labor and from the painful toil of our hands, **because of the ground the LORD has
cursed**."*

**Noach** — rest. **Yenachamenu** — he will comfort us, from **nacham**.

A wordplay that is close but not exact: rest and comfort are different roots.

## What Lamech is quoting

*Cursed is the ground for your sake; in toil you shall eat of it… by the sweat of your face.* See
the note on Genesis 3:17–19.

He is naming his son against a sentence passed six chapters earlier and never lifted.

## What he expects

Relief from farming.

## What arrives

A flood, an ark, and afterwards the first vineyard.

The comfort comes, and not in the shape the father had in mind.

## The other Lamech

Five verses earlier, in Cain's line, a different Lamech: *I have killed a man for wounding me… if
Cain shall be avenged sevenfold, then Lamech seventy-sevenfold*. See the note on Genesis 4:17–24.

Two men with the same name in two lines. One boasts about a killing. One names a son for comfort.

## The last verse

*And when Noah was 500 years old, he fathered **Shem, Ham, and Japheth**.*

The chapter of deaths ends with three births, and the next verse begins the flood account.

## What comes immediately after

*And the LORD said, "My Spirit shall not strive with man forever, for he is indeed flesh; **yet his
days shall be one hundred and twenty years**."* See the note on Genesis 6:1–4.

A hundred and twenty years of notice, given to a generation, immediately after a chapter that
records one man living nearly a thousand.

> **The arithmetic.** Add the three figures the chapter gives and Methuselah's 969th year is the
> year the flood arrives. The longest life in Scripture runs out exactly when the water starts.
