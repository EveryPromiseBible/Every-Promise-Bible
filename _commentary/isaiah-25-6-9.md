Four verses, and everything in them happens on one hill.

## The location

*And **on this mountain** the LORD of hosts will make for all peoples a feast.*

*And **on this mountain** He will swallow up the covering.*

Twice, in two sentences. Isaiah has just named it in the chapter before — Mount Zion, in
Jerusalem.

Every item on the list is sited at the same address.

## The menu

*A feast of **rich food**, a feast of **well-aged wine**, of the **best cuts** and the
**finest wine, refined** and poured out.*

The Hebrew for *rich food* is **shemanim** — fat things. For *well-aged wine*,
**shemarim** — wine left on its lees, which is how the good stuff was aged.

Two pairs, and each pair says the same thing twice: the best of it, and then the best of
the best.

## Who is invited

*For **all peoples**.*

Not Judah. The nations, and Isaiah says it twice in the same breath — *the veil that is
spread over **all the nations***.

## The covering

*He will **swallow up the covering** that is cast over all peoples, **the veil** that is
spread over all the nations.*

The Hebrew for *covering* is **lot** — a wrapping, something laid over a face. The word
behind the veil Moses put on. See the note on 2 Corinthians 3:12–15.

And the second word is **massekah** — a woven covering, from the root for pouring and
overlaying.

Something is over the faces of the nations, and it is dealt with at the same table as the
food.

## The verb

**Bala** — to swallow, to gulp down.

He uses it twice in two lines. He swallows up the covering, and then:

*He will **swallow up death in victory**.*

The same verb that ate the veil eats death.

## Where that sentence goes

Paul quotes it. *Death is swallowed up in victory* — at the resurrection, when the
perishable puts on the imperishable. See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:53–55.

And John quotes the next line at the very end of the Bible: *God will wipe away every tear
from their eyes*. See the note on Revelation 21:3–4.

## The tears

*And the Lord GOD will **wipe away the tears from every face**.*

A gesture rather than a decree. The hand of God, on a face, one at a time.

## The disgrace

*He will **take away the disgrace of His people** from all the earth, for the LORD has
spoken.*

The Hebrew is *reproach* — the taunt of the watching nations. Israel's shame is removed in
front of the same audience that saw it.

## What they say

*See, **this is our God. We have waited for Him, and He has saved us**. This is the LORD.
We waited for Him.*

Twice: *we waited*. The sentence at the feast looks backwards at the waiting.

Nobody says *we found Him* or *we worked it out*.

## The command that follows

*Let us **be glad**. Let us **rejoice in His salvation**.*

The Hebrew for *His salvation* is **yeshuato**.

## Where this sits in Isaiah

Chapters 24 to 27 are one unit — the earth broken up, the city of confusion, the whole
world under judgment. And in the middle of it, without warning, a banquet.

Chapter 24 ends with the moon confounded and the sun ashamed. Chapter 25 opens *O LORD,
You are my God, I will exalt You*.

> **What is on the guest list.** All peoples. The mountain the veil is removed on is the
> mountain the meal is served on, and both sentences begin with the same three words.
