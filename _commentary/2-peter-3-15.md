Four words, and there is a whole history behind them.

## What Peter calls him

*Our **dear brother Paul**.*

Now recall what stands between these two men in the record. At Antioch, in front of the whole church,
*I stood up to him face to face, because he was in the wrong* — and Paul wrote it down, named Peter,
and sent it to churches to be read aloud. See the note on Galatians 2:11–13.

Peter, at the end of his life, calls him **ho agapetos hemon adelphos**. Beloved. Brother. Ours.

He does not defend himself, revisit the incident, or add a qualification. The man who was publicly
corrected commends the corrector in Scripture.

## And commends the letters

*Wrote to you about this as well, **out of the wisdom he had been given**.*

**Kata ten dotheisan auto sophian** — according to the wisdom given to him. Peter attributes it to a
gift rather than to Paul's ability, which is how Paul always described it himself.

And a sentence later Peter puts those letters in a category: *some things hard to understand, which
untaught and unstable people twist to their own destruction, **as they do also the rest of the
Scriptures**.*

That is one apostle classing another apostle's correspondence as Scripture, inside Paul's own
lifetime — and the correspondence in question includes the letter with Peter's worst day in it.

## What Paul wrote to them about

*Take our Lord's patience for what it is: **rescue**.*

**Makrothymian… soterian** — the longsuffering is salvation. Peter has just answered the scoffers by
saying the delay is not slowness but room, *not willing that any should perish* — and he says Paul
made the same case.

Paul certainly did: *do you despise the riches of His goodness, forbearance and longsuffering, not
knowing that **the goodness of God leads you to repentance**?*

Both apostles read the passage of time the same way. Every day the world continues is a day the door
is still open.

> **Grace between two men, and grace as the subject.** Peter was corrected by Paul and calls him
> beloved; Paul was hunted by the church and was vouched for by Barnabas. Neither of them held
> anything. And the doctrine they agree on at the end is the one that made it possible: the
> patience of the Lord Jesus Christ is not indifference or delay — it is rescue, still running.
