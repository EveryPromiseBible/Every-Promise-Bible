Two sentences, side by side, and the pairing is the point.

## The company she is in

The chapter is a list: Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph,
Moses. Patriarchs and prophets. Then the walls of Jericho — and then Rahab.

She is one of only two women named in the chapter, and the writer does not soften
her description. *Rahab the prostitute.* He could have written "Rahab of
Jericho." He did not, and neither did James.

## What her faith is said to consist of

*Since she had welcomed the spies in peace.*

Not a confession, not a sacrifice, not a long obedience. She let two men in and
did not turn them over. The writer treats a single act of hospitality toward
God's people as the visible form of faith in her case.

## The contrast in the sentence

*Did not die with those who refused to believe.* The Greek word is about
disobedience through unbelief — refusing to be persuaded.

Everyone in Jericho had the same evidence, as she says herself on the roof. The
line the chapter draws is not between the informed and the ignorant. It is
between those who were persuaded and those who were not.

> **James, on the same woman.** *Was she not shown to be right by that?* James
> uses Rahab as his second example of faith proved by action — the first being
> Abraham. Between the two writers she is cited as an example of faith and as an
> example of works, and it is the same episode both times. The two arguments are
> not in conflict; see the note on James 2:17.
