The charge Paul leaves with the Ephesian elders, and the order of it is deliberate.

## The first instruction

*"**Keep watch over yourselves** and over all the flock."*

Themselves first. The Greek is **prosechete** — pay attention to, hold your mind on.

Same order as Hezekiah's instruction to the Levites: consecrate yourselves, and then the
house. See the note on 2 Chronicles 29:3–5.

## Who appointed them

*"In which **the Holy Spirit has made you overseers**."*

The Greek is **episkopous** — overseers, bishops.

Luke uses three words for the same men in one sentence: **presbuteroi** (elders, in verse
17), **episkopoi** (overseers), and the verb **poimainein**, to shepherd. In the New
Testament they are one office.

Paul does not say the church appointed them.

## The verb

*"To **shepherd the church of God**."*

**Poimainein** — to tend, to pasture. It is what Jesus told Peter to do on the beach:
*tend My sheep*.

## The price

*"Which **He purchased with His own blood**."*

The Greek is **periepoiesato** — acquired, obtained for oneself.

The phrase is one of the strongest statements in the New Testament about who died: the
church of **God**, purchased with **His own** blood.

Some manuscripts read *the church of the Lord*; the difficulty is in the text and has been
noted since the early centuries.

## The reason for the watching

*"I know that **after I am gone, savage wolves will come in among you**, and they **will not
spare the flock**."*

The Greek is **lukoi bareis** — heavy, grievous wolves.

Jesus had used the image: *beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing,
but inwardly they are ravenous wolves*.

## The worse half

*"**Even from among your own selves men will rise up**, saying twisted things in order to
**draw the disciples away after them**."*

Not only from outside. Paul tells the men in front of him that some of them are the
problem.

**Saying twisted things** — the Greek is **diestrammena**, distorted, turned aside. The
same root Paul used of Elymas *twisting the straight paths of the Lord*. See the note on
Acts 13:9–12.

**After them** — the object of the drawing is named, and it is the teachers themselves.

## The instruction

*"So **stay awake**."*

The Greek is **gregoreite** — the word Jesus used in Gethsemane, and Peter uses in his
letter about the lion. See the note on 1 Peter 5:8.

## The record

*"And remember that for **three years, night and day, I did not stop warning every one of
you with tears**."*

Three years — the longest Paul stayed anywhere.

**Every one of you** — the Greek is *each one*, individually.

**With tears** — the third mention of them in this speech.

## What he leaves them with

The next verse: *and now, brethren, **I commend you to God and to the word of His grace**,
which is able to build you up and to give you an inheritance.* See the note on
Acts 20:32–35.

Wolves are coming, and what he hands them is a word.

> **Where the danger is located.** Outside and inside, and Paul spends more of the warning
> on the second. He is speaking to the overseers, and he tells them some of the men in
> the room will be the ones drawing people away.
