A woman gets what she came for without permission, and Jesus stops everything to
make sure she knows she has it.

## What He felt

*Jesus knew at once, in Himself, that power had gone out from Him.*

The Greek word is **dunamis** — power, ability, the word behind our *dynamite*.
Mark says Jesus registered it going out, immediately, and turned round in the
middle of a crush of people.

He was on His way to a dying child, with a synagogue ruler beside Him. He stopped
anyway.

## The disciples' objection

*Look at this crowd pressing in on You from all sides, and You ask who touched
You?*

They are right about the crowd. Hundreds of people were making contact with Him.
Their point is that the question is unanswerable — and Mark's point is that one
touch was different from all the others.

*But He kept looking around to find her.* He would not move on.

## Why it mattered

She had been bleeding twelve years — which under the law meant twelve years of
being ceremonially unclean, unable to touch or be touched without passing it on.
She had come up behind Him deliberately, hoping to take the healing and slip away
unnoticed.

He would not let her leave anonymous. And when she came forward *shaking and
afraid*, He gave her the one word that ended the twelve years socially as well as
physically:

> **Daughter.**

It is the only time in the Gospels He calls anyone that.

## What He said next

*Your faith has made you well. Go in peace, and be free of this trouble for
good.*

He credits her — not the hem, not the accident of proximity. Her faith. And He
sends her away with a blessing rather than a rebuke for taking without asking.

> **The interruption cost something.** While He was talking to her, the message
> came that Jairus' daughter had died. Jesus had stopped for one woman and a
> child died in the delay — and then He raised her. Mark tells both stories inside
> each other on purpose.
