He clears the room before he says who he is.

## What broke him

Judah has just offered himself in place of Benjamin: *let your servant remain instead of the lad
as a slave to my lord*, and *how shall I go up to my father if the lad is not with me?*

The brother who proposed selling Joseph offers to take a younger brother's punishment. See the
note on Genesis 44:30–34.

That is the speech that ends the concealment.

## The clearing

*Joseph **could no longer hold himself back** in front of all his attendants. "**Everyone leave
me!**" he cried.*

The Hebrew is **velo-yachol Yoseph lehitappeq** — he could not restrain himself.

He has restrained himself twice already, going out to weep and washing his face and coming back.

## Why the room is emptied

*So **no one else was with him** when Joseph revealed himself to his brothers.*

He is the second man in Egypt. What is about to happen would humiliate them in front of the
court.

He protects them from the disclosure at the moment of making it.

## The sound

*And he **wept so loudly that the Egyptians heard him**, and word reached Pharaoh's house.*

He sent them out and they heard anyway.

## The two words

*"**I am Joseph!**"*

The Hebrew is **ani Yoseph**. Two words.

They have known him for two chapters as **Zaphnath-Paaneah**, the Egyptian governor who speaks
through an interpreter.

## The question

*"**Is my father really still alive?**"*

They have already told him twice that Jacob is alive. He has asked before and been answered.

It is not a request for information.

## Their response

*But **his brothers could not answer him** — they were **stunned into silence** at the sight of
him.*

The Hebrew is **nivhalu mippanav** — they were terrified before his face.

The man they sold has absolute power over them and has just said his name.

## The instruction

*"**Come close to me**," Joseph said. So they came closer.*

The Hebrew is **geshu-na elai**.

He does not leave them standing where they are.

## The full sentence

*"**I am Joseph, your brother, the one you sold into Egypt**."*

He states it plainly. No euphemism, no softening.

**Your brother**, in the middle of it.

## What he says next

*"**But don't be distressed, and don't be angry with yourselves** for selling me here."*

The Hebrew is **al-te'atzvu** — do not be grieved, and **ve'al-yichar be'eineichem** — and let it
not burn in your eyes.

He forbids them the self-recrimination before they have started.

## The reason

*"**Because it was to save lives that God sent me ahead of you**."*

The Hebrew is **lemichyah shelachani Elohim** — for preservation of life God sent me.

## The arithmetic

*"For **two years now the famine has been in the land**, and there are **five more years** with no
plowing or harvest."*

Two down, five to go. He is not speaking in generalities.

## The purpose stated twice

*"God sent me ahead of you to **keep a remnant of you alive** on the earth and to **save your lives
by a great deliverance**."*

The Hebrew is **lipletah gedolah** — for a great escape, a great deliverance.

## The sentence

*"**So it was not you who sent me here, but God**."*

Which does not deny that they sold him — he has said it twice in eight verses.

Two agencies over one act, and he names both without collapsing either.

## The titles

*"He has made me **a father to Pharaoh**, **lord of all his household**, and **ruler over all of
Egypt**."*

Three offices, all held by a man who arrived as a slave and spent years in a prison.

## What he says at the end of the book

*"You meant evil against me; **but God meant it for good**, in order to bring it about as it is this
day, to save many people alive."* See the note on Genesis 50:19–21.

Said again, after Jacob dies and the brothers panic that he has been waiting.

> **The order.** He empties the room, says his name, tells them plainly that they sold him, and
> then forbids them to torture themselves about it — in that sequence.
