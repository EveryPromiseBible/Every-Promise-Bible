He tells them they already know it and says he will keep saying it anyway.

## The intention

*So I am going to **keep bringing all of this up. I will not let it slide**.*

The Greek is **melēsō aei humas hupomimnēskein** — I will always be intent on reminding you.

## The concession

*You **know it already**, and you are **standing firm in the truth you now have**.*

The Greek is **estērigmenous en tē parousē alētheia** — established in the present truth.

He grants them both knowledge and stability. Neither is in doubt.

## Why he keeps going

*And I am going to say it again anyway.*

The reason offered is not that they have forgotten. It is that reminding is what he intends to
spend his remaining time doing.

## The phrase

**Hē parousa alētheia** — the truth that is present, the truth now here.

Not a truth they might grow out of. The truth that has arrived in their era.

## The image

*As long as I am **still living in this tent**.*

The Greek is **en toutō tō skēnōmati** — in this tabernacle.

**Skēnōma** — a tent, a temporary dwelling. Peter's word for his body.

## What he says next

*Knowing that shortly **I must put off my tent**, just as our Lord Jesus Christ showed me.* See the
note on 2 Peter 1:14–15.

The Greek is **tachinē estin hē apothesis** — the laying aside is soon.

## What he was shown

*"When you are old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will gird you and carry you where
you do not wish."* And John adds: *this He spoke, signifying by what death he would glorify God*.
See the note on John 21:18–19.

He is writing under a sentence he has known about for thirty years.

## The verb

*Keep **shaking you awake** with reminders.*

The Greek is **diegeirein humas en hupomnēsei** — to stir you up by reminder.

**Diegeirō** — the verb used of waking someone from sleep. The disciples use it in the boat: *they
awoke Him*.

## What the reminders are about

The list just before: faith, virtue, knowledge, self-control, perseverance, godliness, brotherly
kindness, love. See the note on 2 Peter 1:5–7.

And the diagnosis attached: *he who lacks these things is shortsighted, even to blindness, and **has
forgotten that he was cleansed from his old sins***. See the note on 2 Peter 1:8–9.

## The word

**Lēthēn labōn** — having taken forgetfulness.

The whole reason for the reminding is stated four verses earlier. The failure the letter is guarding
against is amnesia about a cleansing.

## What the letter does after this

*For we did not follow **cunningly devised fables**… but were **eyewitnesses of His majesty**.* See the
note on 2 Peter 1:16–18.

He was on the mountain. He heard the voice.

And then: *we have the prophetic word made more sure*.

## The last line of the letter

*But **grow in the grace and knowledge** of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.* See the note on
2 Peter 3:17.

The final instruction of the man who spent the letter reminding them of what they already knew.

> **The reason given.** Not that they had forgotten, and not that they were unsteady — he says the
> opposite of both. He simply intends to spend what is left of his life saying it.
