The first time the word **grace** appears in the Bible, and it lands in the darkest
paragraph before it.

## What God saw

*Every inclination of the human heart was **only evil, all the time**.*

Three absolutes stacked: every, only, all the time. The Hebrew leaves no room —
*every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually.*

The word for *inclination* is **yetzer**, from the verb for a potter forming clay.
It is the shaping impulse, what the heart is bent toward before any act.

## What it did to God

*And it **grieved the LORD deeply** that He had made humankind; **His heart was
filled with pain**.*

The Hebrew is **atsab** — to hurt, to wound, to cause sorrow. It is the same root
used of the pain of childbirth in chapter 3.

God is described as being in pain. Genesis does not soften it, and this is four
chapters after *very good*.

## The sentence

*"I will wipe from the earth the human race I have created."*

The Hebrew is to blot out, to erase — the word for wiping a dish.

## The turn

*But Noah — **Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **chen**. This is its first occurrence in Scripture, and it arrives at
the bottom of the worst page in Genesis.

**Found.** Not earned, achieved or deserved. The verb is the one for coming across
something.

## The order of the verses

Verse 8: *Noah found grace.*

Verse 9: *Noah was a just man, perfect in his generations, and Noah walked with
God.*

Grace comes first, and the character is described afterwards. The book does not say
Noah was righteous and therefore found favour; it says he found favour, and then
tells you what he was.

## What his name means

**Noah** — rest, comfort. Lamech named him: *this one will comfort us concerning
our work and the toil of our hands, because of the ground which the LORD has
cursed.*

The man whose name is rest is the man who found grace.

## "Perfect in his generations"

The Hebrew is **tamim** — whole, sound, without blemish. It is the technical word
Leviticus uses of an animal fit for sacrifice.

## What the whole passage is doing

Everything in it is God's assessment and God's decision — He saw, He grieved, He
said, He will wipe.

And then one man is exempted, and the reason given is not his record.

> **What Noah had to build with.** No rain had ever fallen, according to the account
> in chapter 2, and he was told to build a boat inland for a flood. Hebrews puts it
> plainly: *by faith Noah, being divinely warned of things **not yet seen**, moved
> with godly fear, prepared an ark.*
