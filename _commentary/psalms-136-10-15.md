The same response is sung after the hardest lines in the psalm.

## The turn

The psalm has moved from creation — heavens, earth, sun, moon — into national history. See the
note on Psalms 136:4–9.

The refrain does not change with the subject.

## The first line

*To Him who **struck down the firstborn of Egypt**, His steadfast love endures forever.*

The Hebrew is **lemakkeh Mitzrayim bivchoreihem** — to the one striking Egypt in their firstborn.

The tenth plague. The night of the Passover.

## What the refrain says about it

**Ki le'olam chasdo** — for His covenant love endures forever.

The congregation sings the same response they sang over the sun and the moon.

## What the psalm is doing

It is not softening the judgment or explaining it. It puts the line in and answers it with the
covenant word, and moves on.

The Hebrew hymnbook does not treat the two categories as needing to be reconciled.

## The bringing out

*And **brought Israel out from among them**, His steadfast love endures forever.*

The Hebrew is **mittocham** — from among them, out of their midst.

The two lines are a pair. The striking and the bringing out are one act, told in two verses.

## The arm

*With **a strong hand and an outstretched arm**.*

The Hebrew is **beyad chazaqah uvizroa netuyah**.

The formula runs through Deuteronomy, and it is the phrase Israel was to teach its children when
they asked what the statutes meant.

## The sea

*To Him who **split the Red Sea in two**.*

The Hebrew is **legozer yam-suph ligzarim** — cutting the Sea of Reeds into pieces.

**Gazar** — to cut, to divide. The word used of dividing a thing in two, and of the covenant
Abraham made with animal halves.

## The crossing

*And **led Israel through the middle of it**.*

**Betocho** — through its midst. The same construction as *from among them* three lines earlier.

Out from among the Egyptians, through the middle of the sea.

## Pharaoh

*And **swept Pharaoh and his army into the sea**.*

The Hebrew is **veni'er** — shook off, as a garment is shaken.

Exodus 14 uses the same verb: *the LORD overthrew the Egyptians in the midst of the sea*.

## The pattern of the whole psalm

Creation, exodus, wilderness, the kings of the Amorites, the land given as an inheritance — and
then:

*Who remembered us in our lowly state… and rescued us from our enemies… **who gives food to all
flesh**.*

Cosmic, then national, then personal, then everything that eats.

## What is sung at

Passover. This is the Great Hallel, sung at the meal.

*And when they had sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives.*

The hymn at the last supper was almost certainly this psalm or the ones around it. The last thing
Jesus is recorded singing is a refrain about **chesed** enduring forever, on the night before the
crucifixion.

## What Paul does with the exodus

*All our fathers were under the cloud, all passed through the sea… **now all these things happened
to them as examples**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 10:11–13.

## What is on the other side

*And they sing **the song of Moses**, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb: "Great and
marvellous are Your works, Lord God Almighty."* See the note on
Revelation 15:2.

Standing on a sea of glass, and the song is named after the man who sang at the first one.

> **What the psalm does not do.** Separate the categories. The striking of the firstborn and the
> making of the sun get the same one-line answer from the same congregation.
