One man clears a courtyard the size of several football pitches, and nobody stops
Him.

## What He objected to

The money changers and the dove sellers were operating in the Court of the
Gentiles — the only part of the temple a non-Jew was permitted to enter.

The one space in the building where the nations could come and pray had been turned
into a livestock market with an exchange rate.

## The quotation

*"It is written, '**My house will be called a house of prayer**,' but you have
turned it into **a hideout for robbers**."*

The first half is Isaiah 56, and Mark keeps the rest of the line: *a house of
prayer **for all nations**.*

The second half is Jeremiah 7 — the temple sermon, where the people were chanting
*the temple of the LORD, the temple of the LORD* while robbing each other.

A **hideout** is where thieves go after the theft. It is not where the robbery
happens; it is where they feel safe.

## What He did not damage

He drove out people and overturned furniture. Nobody is reported hurt, and no
animal is reported killed.

*And He would not allow anyone to carry wares through the temple*, Mark adds. He
shut the shortcut down as well.

## What happened next in the same courtyard

*Then **the blind and the lame came to Him right there in the temple, and He healed
them**.*

Only Matthew records this, and the placement is the point. The tables go over and
the room fills with people who could not have got near it before.

Under the old rule, *the blind and the lame shall not come into the house.* They
came in.

## The children

*The children in the temple courts **shouting, "Hosanna to the Son of David!"**
And they were furious.*

The chief priests object to the children, and He answers them with Psalm 8: *out of
the mouths of children and nursing infants You have brought forth praise.*

The Hebrew of that psalm says *You have ordained **strength**.* He quotes the Greek,
which has praise. Both are in the line.

## Where He slept

*Then He left them, went out of the city to **Bethany**, and spent the night
there.*

He cleared the temple and then walked two miles out of the city to sleep at
Lazarus's house. Every night that week.

> **What week this is.** Nisan 10, the day the Passover lamb was brought into the
> house and kept until the fourteenth. He comes into the temple on the day the lamb
> comes in, and the next three days are spent being questioned. See the notes on
> Matthew 21:23–27 and Exodus 12:1–13.
