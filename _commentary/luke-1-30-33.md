The angel's second sentence to Mary, and it is a reassurance before it is an
announcement.

## The reassurance

*"**Do not be afraid, Mary**."*

Gabriel says the same thing to Zechariah in the same chapter, and to Daniel twice.

Luke has just recorded her reaction to the greeting: *she was troubled at his saying, and
considered what manner of greeting this was.* See the note on Luke 1:26–29.

## The statement

*"**You have found favor with God**."*

The Greek is **charin para to theo** — grace with God.

The greeting a few verses earlier used the related verb: **kecharitomene**, one who has
been graced. The same verb appears once more in the New Testament, in Ephesians, of every
believer: *He made us accepted in the Beloved*. See the note on Ephesians 1:3–6.

## What "found favour" means

The Hebrew equivalent is **matsa chen** — the phrase used of Noah before the flood, of
Joseph in Potiphar's house, of Esther before the king. See the notes on Genesis 6:5–8 and
Genesis 39:1–6.

In every case it is stated about the person and not earned by them.

## The announcement

*"You are going to **conceive and give birth to a son**, and you are to **name Him Jesus**."*

**Yeshua** — the LORD saves. Matthew gives the reason: *for He will save His people from
their sins.*

Mary is told the name. In that culture the father named the child, and Joseph is told the
same name separately in Matthew's account.

## The four things said about Him

*"**He will be great**."*

Of John, Gabriel had said *he will be great in the sight of the Lord*. Here the
qualification is dropped.

*"He will be called **the Son of the Most High**."*

*"The Lord God will give Him **the throne of His father David**."*

*"And He will **reign over the house of Jacob forever**. **His kingdom will never come to an
end**."*

## The throne

*His father David.*

The promise to David: *I will set up your seed after you… and I will establish the throne of
his kingdom forever.*

Judah had not had a king on that throne for six hundred years. The last one, Zedekiah, was
blinded and taken to Babylon.

## The two "forevers"

*Reign forever*, and *a kingdom that will never come to an end*.

Gabriel says it twice in one sentence.

## Where the words come from

Isaiah 9: *of the increase of His government and peace **there will be no end**, upon the
throne of David and over His kingdom, to order it and establish it with judgment and
justice from that time forward, even forever.*

Gabriel is quoting a prophecy seven hundred years old to a teenager in Nazareth.

## Who is hearing it

*A virgin betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David. **The virgin's
name was Mary**.*

Nazareth was a village of perhaps a few hundred people, in a region Judeans looked down on.

## What she says next

*"**How can this happen?**"* — and Gabriel answers with the mechanism rather than a rebuke.
See the note on Luke 1:34–38.

> **What is stated before anything is asked.** That she has found favour. The
> announcement follows it, and nothing in the passage presents the favour as a result of
> her answer.
