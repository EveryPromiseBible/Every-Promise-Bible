title: "Not on the strength of anything we did"

Four verses that state the gospel from start to finish without an imperative in them.

## The turn

*"**But then the kindness of God our Saviour came out into the open, and His love for people with
it.**"*

The Greek is **he chrestotes kai he philanthropia tou soteros hemon Theou epephane**.

**Chrestotes** — kindness, usefulness, benignity. **Philanthropia** — love of human beings, and it
is where English gets *philanthropy*. It occurs twice in the New Testament.

**Epephane** — appeared, shone out. The verb behind **Epiphany**. **Paul** used it in chapter 2 as
well: *the grace of God has come out into the open*.

Both times the subject is an abstract noun and the verb is one used of a sunrise or a star. Grace
and kindness are described as things that became visible, which happens when they arrive in a
person.

## The clause

*"And He saved us — **not on the strength of anything we did, nothing we could call our own
righteousness**, but because of His own mercy."*

**Ouk ex ergon ton en dikaiosyne ha epoiesamen hemeis** — not from works in righteousness which we
did.

**Paul** rules out not merely bad works but *works done in righteousness*. The best available human
material is named and excluded.

*For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God,
**not of works**, lest anyone should boast.*

*Who has saved us and called us with a holy calling, **not according to our works**, but according to
His own purpose and grace.*

Three letters, three times, the same **not**.

## What He did instead

*"**Through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit.**"*

**Loutrou palingenesias kai anakainoseos Pneumatos Hagiou.**

**Loutron** — a bath, a washing. **Palingenesia** — being born again; the word occurs twice in the
New Testament and the other is **Jesus** on the renewal of all things.

*"Do not marvel that I said to you, '**You must be born again**.'"*

*"**I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean**… I will give you a new heart and put
a new spirit within you."*

## Poured out

*"**Which He poured out on us generously through Jesus Christ our Saviour.**"*

**Hou execheen eph' hemas plousios** — which He poured out on us richly.

**Ekcheo** is the Pentecost verb: *I will pour out of My Spirit on all flesh.* And **plousios** —
richly, the adverb **Paul** uses of God being *rich in mercy*.

## The verdict

*"**So that, having been put right with God by His grace, we would become heirs**, with the hope of
life that never ends in front of us."*

**Dikaiothentes te ekeinou chariti** — having been justified by His grace.

An aorist passive. Done, done to us, and the instrument named is grace.

*Being **justified freely by His grace** through the redemption that is in **Christ Jesus**.*

## What the sentence contains

No command. Four verses, one long Greek sentence, and every verb has God as its subject: He
appeared, He saved, He poured out, He justified.

The imperatives resume in verse 8 — *that those who have believed in God should be careful to
maintain good works* — and **Paul** puts them after, and calls the four verses before them *a
faithful saying*.

> **What is excluded.** Not sin — *works done in righteousness which we did*. Paul names the best
> thing a person could bring and rules it out by name, and then lists four verbs that are all God's.
