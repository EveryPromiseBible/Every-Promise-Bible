Paul had written to Corinth before, and they had taken his instruction and turned
it exactly the wrong way round.

## The earlier letter

*I wrote to you in my letter not to keep company with people who are sexually
immoral.*

A letter we do not have. This is one of the places where the New Testament refers
plainly to correspondence that was not kept.

## What he did not mean

*I did not mean the immoral of this world **at all** — otherwise you would have to
leave the world altogether.*

They had read it as a rule about outsiders. Paul says the opposite was intended,
and his reason is practical to the point of dryness: you would have to leave the
planet.

There is no instruction anywhere in Paul to withdraw from unbelievers. The
separation he asks for runs in the other direction.

## What he did mean

*Not to keep company with anyone **who is called a brother** and is…*

The qualifier is the hinge of the paragraph. The list that follows applies inside
the church, to someone claiming the name.

## The list

Six items, and they are not sorted by how shocking they are:

- sexually immoral
- **greedy**
- an idolater
- **someone who tears people down with his words**
- a drunkard
- a swindler

A reviler sits between an idolater and a drunkard. Paul puts the sins of the
mouth and the wallet in the same sentence as the sin that started the chapter,
without ranking them.

Given that chapter 5 began with the church being *swollen with pride*, the
inclusion of the reviler is not accidental.

## "Not even to sit down and eat"

The table was where fellowship was expressed. In Corinth it was also where the
Lord's Supper was eaten.

Withholding the meal is the specific action named — and read against chapter 11,
where the rich shamed the poor at that same table, the instruction is not about
keeping the meal pure. It is about not letting the meal say something untrue.

> **How the chapter ends.** *God judges those outside.* Paul draws the line
> explicitly: the church's business is the church. The verse most often used to
> police the world is, in its own context, a refusal to do that.
