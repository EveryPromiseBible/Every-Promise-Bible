Jesus is hours from arrest, and He spends the time telling frightened men what
will happen after He is gone. Two promises, and both are narrower and more
practical than they are usually quoted as.

## What the Helper is called

**Parakletos** — from *para*, alongside, and *kaleo*, to call. One called to
your side. In ordinary Greek it was used of an advocate: the person who stands
with you when you are in trouble, and speaks for you.

English has never found a single word for it. *Comforter* is too soft,
*Counsellor* too clinical, *Advocate* too legal. The picture underneath all of
them is someone summoned to stand next to you.

## The two things He is said to do here

Jesus is unusually specific, and both jobs point in the same direction:

- **teach you everything**
- **bring back to your mind everything I have said to you**

Both are about the words of Jesus. Not new material of the Spirit's own — He
teaches what Jesus taught, and He reminds them of what Jesus said. Two chapters
later Jesus makes it explicit: the Spirit will not speak on His own, and He will
glorify Christ rather than Himself.

That is a useful measure. Something that pulls attention away from Christ and
onto itself is not doing the work described here.

---

## A promise to men who were about to forget

There is something quietly kind in the second job. These are the men who would
scatter that night. Peter is hours from denying he knows Him.

Jesus does not say *remember everything I told you*. He says Someone will bring
it back to their minds. He is making arrangements for their forgetfulness before
it happens, without mentioning it.

## "Not the way the world gives"

*Peace I leave with you; My peace I give to you. I do not give to you the way the
world gives.*

The world's peace is conditional and it is mostly the absence of something —
quiet while nothing is going wrong, safety while the money holds. It is also
given in a particular way: with expectation attached, and withdrawn when
circumstances change.

Jesus distinguishes His gift on *both* counts. It is a different peace, and it is
handed over differently. And He says it in a room where nothing outward was about
to improve for any of them.

> **On "do not let your hearts be troubled."** It sounds like an instruction to
> manage a feeling, which nobody can do on command. In the sentence it comes
> attached to a gift just handed over — peace given, then the instruction. He is
> not asking them to generate calm. He is telling them what they now have to
> settle themselves with.
