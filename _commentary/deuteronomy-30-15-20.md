The last thing Moses says before the song and the blessing, and it is a choice put in front of a nation.

## Where this is

The plains of Moab, east of the Jordan. Moses is a hundred and twenty and will not cross.

The whole book has been his farewell address. This is the end of it.

## The setting out

*Look — **today I set before you life and good, death and evil**.*

The Hebrew is **re'eh natati lephanecha hayyom** — see, I have set before you today.

Two pairs: **chayyim vetov**, life and good; **hammavet veta-ra**, death and evil.

## What the choice consists of

*Love the LORD your God, **walk in His ways**, and keep His commands and statutes.*

The Hebrew is **le'ahavah et-YHWH Eloheicha** — to love the LORD your God.

The first item is affection, and the commands follow it.

## The outcome

*And **you will live and flourish**, and the LORD your God will bless you in the land you are entering
to possess.*

## The alternative

*But if **your heart turns away and will not listen**, if you are drawn off to bow down to other gods…
then I tell you plainly today that **you will perish**.*

The Hebrew is **yiphneh levavecha** — your heart turns.

The mechanism named is the heart turning, not a decision taken.

## The witnesses

*I call **heaven and earth as witnesses** against you this day.*

The Hebrew is **ha'idoti bachem hayyom et-hashamayim ve'et-ha'aretz**.

The two permanent things. Israel's covenant is witnessed by the sky and the ground, because no
human institution outlives it.

Isaiah opens by summoning the same two: *hear, O heavens, and give ear, O earth*.

## The restatement

*I have set before you **life and death, blessing and cursing**.*

## The imperative

*So **choose life — choose it, that you and your children after you may live**.*

The Hebrew is **uvacharta bachayyim** — and you shall choose life.

The verb is second person singular, addressed to a nation as one man.

## What is unusual about it

The alternatives are laid out fairly, and then the outcome is named in advance.

It is a choice with the answer written next to it, and Moses says it anyway.

## The three verbs

*By **loving** the LORD your God, **listening to His voice**, and **holding fast to Him**.*

The Hebrew is **uledovqah-vo** — and to cleave to Him.

**Davaq** — the verb from Genesis 2: *a man shall leave his father and mother and **be joined** to
his wife*.

The same verb used of Solomon clinging to his foreign wives. See the note on
1 Kings 11:1–3.

## The reason

*For **He is your life, and the length of your days**.*

The Hebrew is **ki hu chayyecha ve'orech yamecha**.

Not the giver of life. The life.

## What Moses does next

Hands over to Joshua, writes the law and gives it to the priests, and teaches the nation a song.

Then the blessing of the tribes, and the climb up Nebo. See the note on
Deuteronomy 34:1–4.

## What the New Testament does with the passage

*But the righteousness of faith speaks in this way: "Do not say in your heart, 'Who will ascend
into heaven?'"… "**The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart**."* See the note on
Romans 10:5–8.

Paul quotes Deuteronomy 30 and applies it to the message of faith.

## The other choice

*"**I have come that they may have life, and that they may have it more abundantly**."* See the note
on John 10:7–10.

> **The clause at the end.** Not *He gives you life* — *He is your life, and the length of your
> days*. The choice is between a relationship and its absence, not between two policies.
