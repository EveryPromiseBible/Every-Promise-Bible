He changes what He calls them, and gives the reason — and the reason is about
information.

## The command

*"This is My command: **love one another as I have loved you**."*

The standard is not a level of effort. It is a comparison, and the second half of it
has already happened in front of them — He washed their feet an hour earlier.

## The greatest love

*"No one has greater love than this: **to lay down his life for his friends**."*

He is hours from doing it, and He says it in the present tense while walking to the
garden.

## The word

*"I do not call you **servants** any longer."*

The Greek is **doulos** — a slave, an owned person. It is what these men had been
calling themselves and what Paul would call himself for the rest of his life.

## The reason He gives

*"Because **a servant does not know what his master is doing**."*

That is the definition offered. Not that a slave is mistreated, or that he works
harder. He is not told.

A slave receives instructions. He does not receive the plan.

## The reason for the new name

*"I have called you **friends**, because **everything I heard from My Father I have
made known to you**."*

Same test, the other way round. They are friends because they have been told.

The change of status is measured by disclosure.

## Where this is said

Between the supper and the arrest, in the middle of the longest continuous speech in
the Gospels — the upper room discourse. He spends the last free hours of His life
explaining things.

That itself is the demonstration of the verse.

## What went with it

*"You are My friends **if you do what I command you**."*

The command in question is stated in verse 12 and repeated in verse 17: love one
another.

## The friend of God before this

Abraham is the only person in the Old Testament called it — *the friend of God*, in
James and in Isaiah — and the moment it is shown is Genesis 18, when God says *shall
I hide from Abraham what I am doing?* and tells him about Sodom before it happens.

Same test again: he was told. See the note on Genesis 18:16–21.

## What follows immediately

*"**You did not choose Me, but I chose you** and appointed you that you should go and
bear fruit."*

And after that, the warning that the world will hate them.

> **The order of the sentence.** He does not say they became friends and were then let
> into the plan. He says they are friends **because** everything He heard has been
> made known — the telling is what the friendship consists of.
