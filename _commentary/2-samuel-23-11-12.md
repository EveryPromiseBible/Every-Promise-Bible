The third of David's three champions, and what he defended was a vegetable patch.

## Where this is

The end of Samuel — a list of David's mighty men, appended after the last of the narrative.

Three at the top, then thirty, then the roll of names ending, **23:39**: *Uriah the Hittite: thirty-seven in
all*.

The list of David's heroes ends with the man he had killed.

## The first two

**Josheb-Basshebeth**, **23:8**: who *lifted his spear against eight hundred, killed by him at one time*.

**Eleazar**, who stood and struck the Philistines, **23:10**: *until his hand was weary, and his hand stuck to
the sword*.

**23:10** again: *And the LORD brought about a great victory that day; and the people returned after him only to
plunder.*

## The third

**23:11** *After him came **Shammah son of Agee, the Hararite**.*

## The ground

**23:11** again: *The Philistines had banded together at **a field full of lentils**.*

The Hebrew is **chelqat hassadeh mele'ah adashim** — a plot of the field full of lentils.

Lentils. The cheapest crop in the region, and the stew Esau sold his birthright for.

## Why it was there

It was a food field in a famine-prone country. A raiding party burning or taking a lentil harvest
took a village's winter.

Chronicles has the parallel account with barley.

## What everyone else did

**23:11** again: *And **the people ran from them**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyanas ha'am** — the people fled.

They were right about the odds. One man against a Philistine raiding party over lentils is not an
argument anybody would win on paper.

## What he did

**23:12** *But Shammah **took his stand in the middle of that field**, defended it, and struck the Philistines
down.*

The Hebrew is **vayyityatzev betok-hachelqah** — he stationed himself in the middle of the plot.

**Yatsav** — to take a stand, to station oneself. The word used of the armies standing to face
each other, and of Goliath presenting himself for forty days.

## What he chose

Not a fortress, a pass, or a city gate.

A lentil field, in the middle, alone, because it was the ground he was on.

## The last line

**23:12** again: *The LORD brought about **a great victory**.*

The Hebrew is **vayya'as YHWH teshu'ah gedolah** — and the LORD wrought a great salvation.

**Teshu'ah** — deliverance, salvation.

The same sentence is used of Eleazar's stand. Both times the man does the standing and the verse
gives the victory to God.

## What the list is doing

Naming men whose entire recorded life is one refusal to move.

Shammah appears nowhere else in Scripture. This is all of him.

## The other three

The chapter's most famous entry has none of them fighting: David longs for water from the well of
Bethlehem, three men break through the Philistine camp to draw it, and he pours it out on the
ground.

**23:17** *"Far be it from me, O LORD, that I should do this! **Is this not the blood of the men who went in
jeopardy of their lives?**"*

## What Paul says about standing

*Therefore take up the whole armour of God, that you may be able to withstand in the evil day, and
**having done all, to stand**.* See the note on Ephesians 6:10–13.

The Greek is **stenai** — to stand. Four times in four verses.

> **The ground.** A lentil field. He did not choose it for its value; he chose it because it was
> where he was, and everybody else had already gone.
