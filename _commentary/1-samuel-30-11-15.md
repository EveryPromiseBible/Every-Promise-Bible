The intelligence that wins the battle comes from a man his own side discarded.

## The find

*Out in the open country they found **an Egyptian** and brought him to David.*

The Hebrew is **bassadeh** — in the field.

No name. He is identified by nationality and then by ownership.

## What they do first

*They gave him **bread to eat and water to drink**, a piece of **fig cake** and **two clusters of
raisins**.*

Four items, listed.

David's men are in pursuit, against the clock, with their families captive. They stop and feed
somebody.

## Why he needed it

*He ate, and **his strength returned** — he'd had **nothing to eat or drink for three days and three
nights**.*

The Hebrew is **vattashov rucho elav** — and his spirit returned to him.

Three days. He was left to die and had nearly finished doing it.

## The questions

*"**Whose are you? Where do you come from?**" David asked.*

The Hebrew is **lemi attah** — to whom do you belong?

The first question in that world is ownership.

## The answer

*"I'm an Egyptian, **the slave of an Amalekite**."*

## What his master did

*"My master **left me behind three days ago when I fell sick**."*

The Hebrew is **vayya'azveni** — and he abandoned me.

A slave who became ill on a raid was a liability. He was dropped where he fell.

## What he knows

*"We raided the Negev of the Kerethites, and **the territory of Judah**, and the Negev of Caleb, and
**we burned Ziklag**."*

He was on the raid. He names the target David is looking for.

## The question that matters

*"**Can you lead me down to this band?**" David asked.*

## The terms

*"**Swear to me by God that you won't kill me or hand me over to my master**," the man said, "and
I'll take you to them."*

Two conditions, and the second is the telling one.

He is more afraid of being returned to his owner than of David.

## What David does

Swears it. And the man leads them down.

## The sequence

Fed, questioned, sworn to, and then followed.

David's men are chasing the people who took their wives and children, and the route runs through
an act of kindness to a stranger they had no reason to help.

## What the raiders were doing

*And there they were — **spread out across the whole land, eating, drinking, and reveling** over the
great haul of plunder.* See the note on 1 Samuel 30:16–20.

Celebrating, unguarded, exactly where the slave said.

## The result

*David recovered everything the Amalekites had taken… **nothing was missing, small or great, son or
daughter, plunder or anything they'd carried off**.*

## What the enquiry had promised

*"**Chase them. You will surely overtake them, and you will surely rescue them all**."* See the note
on 1 Samuel 30:7–10.

The mechanism was not named in the answer. It turned out to be a dying man in a field.

## Who the Amalekites were

The nation that attacked Israel from behind in the wilderness, *all the stragglers at your rear,
when you were tired and weary*.

They discarded stragglers themselves, and it cost them everything.

## Who they found

*They found **an Egyptian in the field**, and brought him to David; and they gave him bread and
he ate, and they let him drink water.*

A slave of the enemy, sick for three days, abandoned by his own master the moment he stopped
being useful — *my master left me behind, because three days ago I fell sick.*

The first thing David's men do is feed him. No questions until after.

## What the enemy did with him and what David did

Left him in a field to die, and picked him up off the ground.

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that **while we were still sinners, Christ
died for us**.*

*For when we were still **without strength**, in due time Christ died for the ungodly.*

The Greek for *without strength* is **asthenon** — sick, feeble. It is the state the Egyptian
is found in.

## What he becomes

The guide. Everything David recovers, he recovers because of a man his enemy threw away.

*Then he said to Jesus, "Lord, remember me when You come into Your kingdom."*

> **The route.** The intelligence that recovers everybody comes from a slave his own side left in
> a field to die — fed and sworn to by men who were in a hurry.
