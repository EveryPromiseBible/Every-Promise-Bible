The one time in Matthew that Jesus is recorded as astonished, and it is by a Roman.

## He marvelled

*When Jesus heard that, **He marvelled**.*

The Greek is to wonder, to be amazed. Mark records Him marvelling at unbelief in
Nazareth; here it goes the other way.

Both times it is caused by people's faith — the absence of it, and this.

## What the officer had said

The verses before: *only speak a word, and my servant will be healed. For I also am
a man under authority… I say to this one, "Go," and he goes.*

He is a career soldier and he has worked out the whole thing from military
structure. Orders travel. The officer does not have to be present for the order to
take effect, only to have the authority behind it.

He did not ask Jesus to come. He asked Him to speak.

## The verdict

*"I have **not found faith like this anywhere in Israel**."*

Said out loud, to the crowd following Him, about a Gentile officer of the occupying
army.

## What He adds

*"Many will come **from the east and from the west** and sit down at the table with
Abraham and Isaac and Jacob… while **those who thought the kingdom belonged to them
by birth** will be put outside."*

He takes one Roman's faith and extends it to a table filled from every direction,
and warns the people who assumed the seats were theirs.

## The word to the officer

*"**Go home. Let it be done for you just as you have believed.**"*

And Matthew's next clause: *the servant was healed at that very hour.*

At a distance. Jesus never went to the house and never saw the servant.

## The measure

*Just as you have believed.*

He does not say *because you deserved it*, or *because your servant was worth it*,
or *because you are a good master* — though the passage shows he was.

The thing named as the measure is what the man believed.

Matthew records the same formula twice more in his Gospel: to the two blind men,
*according to your faith let it be to you*, and to the Canaanite woman, *let it be
done for you just as you desire.*

Three times, three different people, and every one of them an outsider of some
kind.

> **Who the officer thought he was.** *Lord, I am not worthy that You should come
> under my roof.* A commander of a hundred men, in a country he was occupying,
> saying that to a village carpenter. Jesus never contradicts the self-assessment —
> He simply speaks the word.
