Fifteen words in Hebrew, and the oldest text of Scripture ever found.

## Who says it

*"Tell **Aaron and his sons**: this is how you will bless the people of Israel."*

The priests, and the wording is given to them. They do not compose it.

*Say to them* — the Hebrew is *thus you shall bless*. The formula is fixed.

## The shape

Three lines, and in Hebrew they get longer: three words, five words, seven words.

Each line names **YHWH** and each has two verbs. The structure is built to be heard.

## The first

*The LORD **bless you and keep you**.*

**Barak** — bless. And **shamar** — keep, guard, watch over. The verb for a shepherd with a
flock and a sentry on a wall.

Something given, and then protected.

## The second

*The LORD **make His face shine upon you and be gracious to you**.*

**Ya'er panav** — cause His face to light up.

The idiom is about a face changing when somebody walks in. The nearest English is *his face
lit up*.

And **chanan** — to show favour, to be gracious. The root of **chen**, grace, and of the names
Hannah and John.

## The third

*The LORD **turn His face toward you and give you peace**.*

**Yissa panav** — lift up His face toward you.

The opposite of the hiding: *You have hidden Your face from us*. See the note on
Isaiah 64:6–7.

And **shalom** — the last word of the blessing, and the widest one in the language.

## The name

*So they will **place My name over the Israelites**, and **I Myself will bless them**.*

The priests say the words. The blessing is God's own act, and He says so.

**My name** — the blessing has YHWH in it three times, and the sentence afterwards explains
that this is the point. The name is being laid on the people.

## Where it was found

Two small silver scrolls, rolled up as amulets, excavated at Ketef Hinnom just outside
Jerusalem in 1979.

They carry these words, and they date to around the seventh century BC — which makes them the
oldest fragments of biblical text in existence, older than the Dead Sea Scrolls by four
centuries.

Somebody was wearing this blessing round their neck before the exile.

## The face

Three lines, and two of them are about a face.

Shining, and lifted toward you. The whole blessing is about attention.

## Where the face goes

*For it is the God who commanded light to shine out of darkness who has shone in our hearts to
give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God **in the face of Jesus Christ**.* See the
note on 2 Corinthians 4:6–9.

And Moses, coming down the mountain with a face he could not see and had to be told about.

## Where it is still said

At the end of synagogue services and at the end of church services, in both traditions,
continuously, for three thousand years.

## What Paul's version is

*The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Spirit
be with you all.*

Three clauses again, and it closes 2 Corinthians.

> **What the priests were doing.** Not wishing. God's own explanation is in the last sentence:
> they put His name on the people, and He does the blessing.
