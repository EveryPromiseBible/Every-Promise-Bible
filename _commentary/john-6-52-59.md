The hardest thing Jesus ever said in public, and He does not soften it when the
room turns.

## The objection

*"**How can this man give us His flesh to eat?**"*

It is a fair question, and the Greek says they were *arguing sharply* — fighting
among themselves.

Everything about the statement would have been abhorrent to that audience. Blood was
forbidden absolutely under Leviticus 17, on pain of being cut off. See the note on
Leviticus 17:10–12.

## What He does about the objection

Repeats it, four times, harder.

*"Unless you **eat the flesh** of the Son of Man and **drink His blood**, you have no
life in you."*

And then *whoever feeds on My flesh and drinks My blood* three more times in five
verses.

The Greek verb shifts partway through from **phago** (eat) to **trogo** — to gnaw, to
chew. It is a physical, unmistakable word, and He moves toward it rather than away.

## The two clauses

*"For **My flesh is true food, and My blood is true drink**."*

The Greek is **alethes** — real, genuine. Not a metaphor for something realer.

## What it produces

Four things, stated flatly:

- *has **eternal life***
- *I will **raise him up on the last day***
- *remains in Me, and I in him*
- *will **live because of Me***

## The comparison

*"As **the living Father sent Me and I live because of the Father**, so the one who
feeds on Me **will live because of Me**."*

The relationship between the Father and the Son is offered as the pattern for the
relationship between Christ and the one who eats.

That is the largest comparison in the passage, and it is easy to read past.

## The manna, again

*"It is **not like the bread your ancestors ate, and died**."*

The crowd had opened this conversation by quoting the manna at Him. He grants that
they ate it, and points out how the story finished.

See the notes on Exodus 16:13–15 and John 6:47–51.

## What it cost Him

*From that time **many of His disciples went back and walked with Him no more**.*

That is the next paragraph. He does not call them back or clarify.

Instead He turns to the twelve: *do you also want to go away?*

And Peter: *Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life.*

## Where He said it

*While He was teaching in **the synagogue in Capernaum**.*

Not privately, not to the twelve. In the synagogue of the town He lived in, to people
who knew His family.

> **What the chapter opened with.** Five thousand fed with five loaves. They came back
> the next day wanting more bread, and He told them *do not labour for the food which
> perishes.* The crowd that had eaten the miracle is the crowd that walked away from
> the sermon about it.
