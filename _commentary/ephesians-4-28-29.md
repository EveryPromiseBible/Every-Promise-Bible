Paul tells a thief what to do instead, and the answer is not simply *stop*.

## The three stages

*Let the one who has been stealing **steal no more**. Let him **work instead**, doing
something good with his own hands, **so that he has something to share with anyone in
need**.*

Stop, work, give. The instruction does not end at the middle.

A man who stole is not sent to neutrality. He is sent all the way over to the other
side — from taking, past earning, to supplying.

## The mouth

*Do not let **one rotten word** come out of your mouth.*

The Greek is **sapros** — rotten, putrid. It is the word used of bad fish and spoiled
fruit.

Not *unkind* or *coarse*. Decayed — something that has gone off and should not be
handed to anybody.

## What to say instead

*Say only what **builds someone up where they need building**.*

The Greek is *for edification of the need* — for the building up that this particular
person, at this moment, actually requires.

It assumes you have noticed what the need is.

## The last clause

*So that what they hear **leaves grace behind in them**.*

The Greek is *that it may give grace to the hearers* — **charis**, the ordinary New
Testament word for grace.

That is the test Paul applies to speech. Not whether it was true, or deserved, or
accurate about the person. Whether grace was left behind.

Grace by definition is not earned. Words that only credit people with what they have
coming are not doing this.

## The verse straight after

*And **do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God**, by whom you were sealed for the day of
redemption.*

Paul attaches that immediately to the paragraph about speech. The one thing named in
Ephesians as grieving the Spirit is what comes out of a mouth toward another person.

## What surrounds it

The whole passage is a list of exchanges, each one with a positive on the other side:

- put off lying → **speak the truth** to your neighbour
- be angry → **do not let the sun go down** on it
- stop stealing → **work**, and then **share**
- no rotten word → words that **build** and **give grace**

Not one of them stops at the prohibition.

## And what follows

*Let all bitterness, wrath, anger, clamour and evil speaking be put away from you,
with all malice. **And be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another,
just as God in Christ forgave you.**"*

The standard is not fairness. It is *just as* — the way you were treated.

> **The sealing.** *By whom you were **sealed** for the day of redemption.* Paul warns
> about grieving the Spirit in the same breath as saying He is the seal. The
> grieving is real, and it is not a threat to the sealing — a seal is not something
> you can be sad out of.
