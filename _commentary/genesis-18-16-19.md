key: Genesis 18:16–19
title: Should I hide this from Abraham
updated: 2026-08-09
---
God stops on a road and asks Himself whether to tell a man what He is about to do.

## The question

*Then the LORD said, "**Should I hide from Abraham what I am about to do?**"*

It is asked out loud, and nobody in the passage answers it but Him.

Sodom is about to be destroyed. Abraham has no official standing in the matter, does not live
there, and has not asked.

## The reason given

*"**Abraham will surely become a great and mighty nation**, and **all nations on earth will be
blessed through him**."*

The reason for telling him is not that he needs to know. It is who he is to God.

The clause about all nations is the one Paul calls *the gospel preached beforehand to
Abraham*. See the note on Genesis 12:1–3.

## What he is called elsewhere

*Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness. **And he was called the
friend of God**.* See the note on James 2:21.

Isaiah has God say it in the first person: *Abraham My friend*. Jehoshaphat prays it back:
*the descendants of Abraham Your friend forever*.

Three times in Scripture, and it is the only title of its kind given to anybody in the Old
Testament.

## What friendship consists of here

Being told.

*No longer do I call you servants, for **a servant does not know what his master is doing**;
but I have called you friends, for **all things that I heard from My Father I have made known
to you**.* See the note on John 15:12–15.

That is the same definition, given by Jesus in an upper room, and Genesis 18 is the working
example. The distinction is not affection. It is disclosure.

## What being told makes possible

*But **Abraham remained standing before the LORD**.* The next paragraph is the intercession —
fifty, forty-five, forty, thirty, twenty, ten. See the note on Genesis 18:20–22.

He could not have prayed it if he had not been told it. The disclosure is what creates the
intercession, and the whole exchange happens because God decided not to keep something to
Himself.

## The middle clause

*"For I have chosen him, so that **he will teach his children and his household after him to
keep the way of the LORD**."*

The Hebrew for *chosen* is **yada** — to know. *I have known him.*

The teaching follows the knowing, in that order. Nothing here is a condition Abraham met
first; the sentence begins with God's choice and ends with God's promise, and the household
sits in the middle.

> **Why he was told.** Not because he was needed, consulted, or entitled. God asked Himself
> whether to hide it from His friend, and decided against it — and the greatest intercession
> in Genesis happened because of what He said next.
