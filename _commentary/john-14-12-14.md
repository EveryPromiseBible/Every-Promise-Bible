Three sentences in the upper room, and the reason given for the largest claim is a departure.

## Where this is

The night before the crucifixion. He has washed their feet, named the betrayer, and said *let not
your heart be troubled*.

Thomas has asked how they can know the way, and Philip has said *show us the Father*.

*"Have I been with you so long, and yet you have not known Me, Philip?"* See the note on
John 14:8–11.

## The claim

*"Whoever believes in Me **will do the works I have been doing**."*

The Greek is **ta erga ha ego poio kakeinos poiesei**.

**Erga** — works. John's usual word for the miracles, alongside **semeia**, signs.

## The escalation

*"And **will do greater ones than these**."*

The Greek is **meizona touton poiesei**.

## What *greater* is not

Not more impressive. Nobody in Acts does anything more remarkable than raising Lazarus or feeding
five thousand.

## The reason given

*"**Because I am going to the Father**."*

The Greek is **hoti ego pros ton Patera poreuomai**.

The reason is not the disciples' ability. It is His departure, which is what makes the Spirit's
coming possible: *it is to your advantage that I go away, for if I do not go away, the Helper will
not come to you*.

## What the greater turns out to be

Extent.

One man in one country for three years, against the church across the world for two thousand.

Three thousand in a day at Pentecost, which is more than are recorded as converted during the
whole ministry.

## The promise

*"And **whatever you ask in My name, I will do it**."*

The Greek is **ho ti an aitesete en to onomati mou**.

## The purpose

*"So that **the Father may be glorified in the Son**."*

The purpose clause is the limit on the promise. It is not a blank cheque; it names what the asking
is for.

## The repetition

*"**If you ask anything in My name, I will do it**."*

Said twice in two verses, and the second is shorter and flatter than the first.

## What *in My name* means

Not a formula appended to a prayer.

In that world, acting in someone's name meant acting as their representative, on their business,
with their authority. A steward bought in his master's name.

## What He says later in the same discourse

*"If you abide in Me, and My words abide in you, you will ask what you desire, and it shall be done
for you."* See the note on John 15:7–8.

*"Until now you have asked nothing in My name. **Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be
full**."*

## What follows immediately

*"If you love Me, keep My commandments. And I will pray the Father, and **He will give you another
Helper, that He may abide with you forever**."* See the note on John 14:15–17.

The Greek is **allon parakleton** — another Helper of the same kind.

**Allos** — another of the same sort, as against **heteros**, another of a different sort.

## What the disciples did

*And they went out and preached everywhere, **the Lord working with them and confirming the word
through the accompanying signs**.*

The last verse of Mark.

> **The reason.** Not that they would be more capable — *because I am going to the Father*. The
> greater works are grounded in a departure, and in what the departure makes possible.
