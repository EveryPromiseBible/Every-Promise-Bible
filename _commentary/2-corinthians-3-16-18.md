The end of Paul's long comparison between the two covenants, and it finishes with
a mirror.

## The veil

*But whenever anyone **turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away**.*

Paul has spent the chapter on Moses coming down the mountain with his face
shining, and putting a veil over it — and Paul says why, which Exodus does not:
*so that the children of Israel could not look steadily at the end of what was
passing away.*

The glory was fading, and the veil hid the fading.

The removal is not something the reader performs. *Whenever anyone turns.* The
veil comes off in the turning.

## Freedom

*Now the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, **there is
freedom**.*

The Greek is **eleutheria** — liberty, the state of a free person as against a
slave.

## The mirror

*All of us, **with faces unveiled, gazing at the glory of the Lord as though into
a mirror**, are being changed into that same likeness.*

Two things at once. Looking at Him, and seeing it in a mirror.

Moses had to veil his face because of what looking at God had done to it. Paul says
every face is unveiled now, and what you see when you look is being reproduced in
you.

## The verb

*Are **being changed** into that same likeness.*

The Greek is **metamorphoo** — the word behind metamorphosis, and the word Matthew
and Mark use of the transfiguration.

It is passive, and it is present continuous. Something is being done to you, and it
is not finished.

## Where the change comes from

*And **all of it comes from the Spirit of the Lord**.*

Paul closes the sentence by removing the reader from the mechanism entirely. Not
by effort, not by discipline, not by resolve.

You look, and you are changed, and the changing is His work.

## "From glory to glory"

Not from failure to glory. From one degree of it to the next.

The Greek is *from glory into glory* — a movement between two of the same thing.

> **What the beholding is of.** Not yourself in the mirror. *The glory of the
> Lord.* The chapter after this says where that glory is located: *the light of
> the knowledge of the glory of God **in the face of Jesus Christ**.*
