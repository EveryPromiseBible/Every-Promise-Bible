A whole nation's excuse, quoted back to it in its own words.

## Who is speaking

*This is what **the LORD Almighty** said.*

The Hebrew is **YHWH Tzeva'ot** — the LORD of hosts, of armies.

Haggai uses the title fourteen times in two short chapters. It is a book of thirty-eight verses,
and God is named as commander of armies in almost every paragraph, addressed to a few thousand
people with no army at all.

## Where they are

Back from Babylon. Cyrus had let them return around 538 BC, and they had laid the foundation of
the temple in the second year.

Then the local opposition, the letters to the Persian court, and the work stopped.

*Thus the work of the house of God which is at Jerusalem ceased, and it was discontinued until
the second year of the reign of Darius.*

Sixteen years.

## The quotation

*"**These people** keep saying, '**The time has not come yet. It is not the season to rebuild the
house of the LORD**.'"*

The Hebrew is **lo et-bo** — not the time to come.

## The distance in the phrasing

*These people.*

Not *My people*. God quotes them at arm's length.

## What the excuse is

Not a refusal. A postponement.

Nobody in Jerusalem is saying the temple should not be built. They are saying the conditions are
not right yet — and after sixteen years the sentence still has *yet* in it.

## Why it was plausible

It was true that the opposition was real, that a decree had stopped them, and that the economy
was bad. Haggai's next paragraph confirms the last part: *you have sown much and bring in
little*.

The excuse was accurate about the circumstances. That is what makes it worth quoting.

## What the same sixteen years produced

*Is it truly the time for you to be living in **your own paneled houses**, warm and finished, while
**this house lies in ruins**?* See the note on Haggai 1:3–4.

The word *time* comes straight back at them. They said it was not the time for one thing; God
asks whether it was the time for the other.

## The two words

**Sephunim** — panelled, roofed, finished. And **chareb** — desolate, a ruin.

Both states are the result of the same sixteen years.

## What follows

*Give careful thought to your ways.* See the note on Haggai 1:5.

Repeated in verse 7, and twice more in chapter 2. The phrase is the book's refrain.

## What happens after the sermon

*Then Zerubbabel… and Joshua… **with all the remnant of the people, obeyed the voice of the LORD
their God**.*

*And the LORD stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel… and they came and worked on the house of the
LORD of hosts, their God.*

Twenty-three days from the first word to the first tool. It is the fastest response to a prophet
recorded anywhere in the Old Testament.

## What God says in between

*"**I am with you**," says the LORD.*

Four words, delivered before a single stone had moved.

## The other prophet

*In the same month, Zechariah began. Both men are named in Ezra: **the prophets, Haggai and
Zechariah, prophesied to the Jews… and they built and finished it***.

The book of Ezra records that the building resumed because two men preached.

> **What they said.** Not *no* — *not yet*. And they had been saying it for sixteen years with the
> word *yet* still in the sentence.
