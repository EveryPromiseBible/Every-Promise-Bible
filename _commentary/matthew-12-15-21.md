The Pharisees have just decided to destroy Him. Matthew's next sentence is what
Jesus does about it, and then the longest Old Testament quotation in his Gospel.

## He withdrew

*Knowing this, Jesus **withdrew** from that place. Large crowds followed Him, and
**He healed all of them**.*

He does not confront the plot and He does not stop healing. He moves, and the
healing goes with Him.

*All of them* — Matthew's habit. He does not report a percentage.

## Why the quotation is here

Matthew reaches for Isaiah 42 to explain a man walking away from a fight, and the
prophecy he chooses is about a servant who does not raise His voice.

## What the servant does not do

*He will not argue or shout. No one will hear His voice raised in the streets.*

Three negatives before a single positive. The portrait is drawn by what is absent
from it.

## The reed and the wick

*A **bruised reed** He will not break, and a **smoldering wick** He will not put
out.*

A reed grew by the Jordan and was used for measuring, for writing, and cut into
a simple pipe. Bruised, it is worthless — you throw it away and cut another,
because reeds cost nothing.

A wick with no flame left is smoke and smell. You pinch it out.

Both images are of something that has stopped doing the one thing it exists for,
and is worth less than the effort of keeping.

Neither is discarded.

## Until

*…**until He carries justice through to victory**.*

The gentleness has a destination. It is not resignation or a mild temperament; it
is restraint held on purpose, and the sentence says where it is going.

The word for *justice* in Isaiah is **mishpat** — the right ordering of things,
what ought to be.

## The last line

*And in His name the nations will place their hope.*

Matthew, writing to Jewish readers, ends the quotation on the Gentiles — placed
here, immediately after Israel's teachers have resolved to kill Him.

> **Why Matthew put it here.** The chapter's argument is about the Sabbath and a
> withered hand. A man whose hand had stopped working was, by any practical
> measure, a bruised reed. He was not broken; he was told to stretch it out. See
> the note on Matthew 12:9–14.
