The love chapter ends by comparing what we have now with what is coming, and the last line quietly
moves the whole subject out of our hands.

## In part

*What we know is only a part, and what we prophesy is only a part.*

**Ek merous** — out of a portion. Twice, and then a third time at the end of the paragraph. Paul is
not embarrassed about it; he is dismantling the Corinthian assumption that knowledge is what makes
a Christian senior. Everybody's portion is a portion.

*But when what is complete arrives, the partial thing is finished with.* **To teleion** — the
finished, the whole-grown thing. Not better information. A different order of seeing altogether.

## The mirror the readers made

*What we see now is a **dim reflection in a polished piece of metal**.*

Corinth's bronze mirrors were an industry; some of the people reading this letter made them for a
living. Paul picks the local product to make his point, and the point is that even the best mirror
in the empire gives you an image, not a face.

*Then we will see **face to face**.* The phrase is Moses' — the man who asked to see the glory and
was put in a cleft of a rock and shown a back. What Moses was refused, Paul says the whole church
is scheduled for.

And the reason it can now be promised to anybody is in his other letter: *we all, with unveiled
face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image.*

## The clause that turns the sentence around

*What I know now is only a part; then I will know fully, **even as I have been fully known**.*

Read that last verb again. **Epeginosthen** — aorist passive. Not *as I will know*. As I *have
been* known. Past tense, finished, and the knower is not us.

The full knowledge Paul is waiting for already exists. It simply runs the other direction. He is
already completely known — every hidden thing, every half-motive, the parts of himself he cannot
see in the metal — and he says it as a comfort.

That is only a comfort if the One who knows him fully is not against him. And he is writing to
people whose scandals fill this letter.

> **He knew, and chose you anyway.** *O Lord, You have searched me and known me… such knowledge is
> too wonderful for me.* Nothing about you will come to light on that day that is news to Him. The
> blood covenant was not a response to His best estimate of us; it was cut in answer to complete
> information. Which is why Paul can write *now that you have come to know God — or rather to be
> known by God*, and correct himself in the direction of grace every time.
