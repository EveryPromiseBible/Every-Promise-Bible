A father tells his son what to do with words, and then works down the body part
by part.

## What the words are called

*They are life to those who find them, and **health to the whole body**.*

The Hebrew for *health* is **marpe** — healing, a cure, and by extension
soundness. It is used of a physician's remedy.

And the phrase is literally *to all his flesh*. Not to his mind or his character.
His flesh.

## How to keep them

Three verbs in two lines: *pay attention… lean your ear in close… do not let them
slip from your sight*, and then the fourth, which is the destination — *keep them
deep within your heart.*

Ear, eye, and then inside. The route is specified.

## Then the body, in order

- **the heart** — *above all else, guard it, for from it flow the springs of life*
- **the mouth** — put away twisted speech
- **the eyes** — look straight ahead
- **the feet** — give careful thought to where you step

Heart first, and everything after it flows from that. The Hebrew for *springs*
is the word for the outgoings or sources of a thing.

## "Guard your heart"

The verb is a sentry's word — to watch over, to keep under guard. It assumes
something is trying to get in, or out.

The reason given is not sentimental. It is hydraulic: whatever is in there is
what will come out everywhere else.

> **How the passage ends.** *Do not turn to the right or the left.* The same
> phrase Joshua was given about the book of the law. The chapter is not describing
> a mood or an atmosphere — it is describing a road, walked deliberately, by
> somebody watching where their feet go.
