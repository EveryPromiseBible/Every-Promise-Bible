The psalm turns halfway through, and God speaks in the first person for the rest of it.

## What comes before

*Oh come, let us sing to the LORD! Let us shout joyfully to the Rock of our salvation…
Oh come, let us worship and bow down; let us kneel before the LORD our Maker.*

Seven verses of invitation. Then, without warning, the voice changes.

## The warning

*"**Do not harden your hearts** as at **Meribah**, as on that day at **Massah** in the
desert."*

Two place names, and both are nicknames rather than map locations.

**Meribah** — *quarrelling, contention*. **Massah** — *testing, proving*.

Moses named the place after what happened there: *he called the name of the place Massah
and Meribah, because of the contention of the children of Israel, and because they
tempted the LORD, saying, "Is the LORD among us or not?"*

That is the question at the bottom of it.

## What happened there

Rephidim, in Exodus 17. No water, the people ready to stone Moses, and God saying *strike
the rock, and water will come out of it.*

They got the water. The place kept the name of the argument.

## The charge

*"Where **your ancestors tested Me**, tried Me, **though they had seen My work**."*

The Hebrew is *they proved Me, they saw My work*.

They had seen the plagues, the sea, the pillar, the manna. The complaint is not
ignorance.

## Forty years

*"For **forty years I grieved over that generation**."*

The Hebrew for *grieved* is to be disgusted with, to loathe. It is a strong word and it
is in the first person.

## The diagnosis

*"And I said, '**Their hearts keep wandering, they do not know My ways**.'"*

The Hebrew is *a people who err in heart*.

**They do not know My ways** — not His acts. They had seen the acts for forty years.

## The oath

*"So **I made a promise in My anger**, '**They will never enter My rest**.'"*

The Hebrew is *I swore in My wrath*, and the form of the sentence is the strongest
negative available.

**My rest** — **menuchah**, the same word Isaiah uses. See the note on
Isaiah 28:11–13.

## What the rest was

Canaan. The land, the end of the marching, the place with the wells already dug.

And the generation that came out of Egypt did not go in. Only Caleb and Joshua.

## What Hebrews does with it

The writer quotes these verses twice, in full, and builds two chapters on them.

His argument: the word *Today* in the psalm means the offer was still open when David
wrote it, four hundred years after Joshua brought them in — so the rest in question is
not only the land. See the note on Hebrews 4:8–11.

*Today, if you will hear His voice, do not harden your hearts.*

## The pivot word

*Today.* It is in the verse immediately before this passage, and Hebrews hangs the whole
case on it.

> **How the psalm is built.** An invitation to come and kneel, and then God's own voice
> naming the last time they were invited and would not. The warning is inside the call
> to worship.
