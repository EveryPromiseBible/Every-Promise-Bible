The worst king in the book gets six verses of introduction, and the last of them is about
somebody else.

## The escalation

*Ahab son of Omri **did more evil in the eyes of the LORD than any who came before him**.*

Said at the start, and again at the end of the paragraph: *he did more to provoke the LORD to
anger than all the kings of Israel before him.*

The writer brackets the whole introduction with the same verdict.

## The clause in the middle

*"**As if walking in the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat were a small thing**, he went and
married Jezebel."*

Every king before him had kept Jeroboam's calves. Ahab treats that as a starting position.

## What the marriage brought

Jezebel is the daughter of the king of Sidon — a priest-king of Baal. The marriage is a treaty,
and the temple in Samaria comes with it.

*Do not be unequally yoked together with unbelievers. For what fellowship has righteousness
with lawlessness?*

## The last verse, which looks unrelated

*In his days **Hiel of Bethel rebuilt Jericho**. He laid its foundations at the cost of Abiram
his firstborn, and he set up its gates at the cost of his youngest son Segub.*

## Why it is there

*Then Joshua charged them at that time, saying, "**Cursed before the LORD is the man who rises
up and builds this city Jericho**; he shall lay its foundation with his firstborn, and with
his youngest son he shall set up its gates."* See the note on Joshua 6:1–5.

Five hundred years earlier. The writer adds: *just as the LORD had spoken through Joshua son of
Nun.*

Jericho was the first city given to Israel, taken without a sword, and put under a permanent
ban. Hiel rebuilds it and it costs him his eldest son and his youngest.

## What the placement is saying

Not that God struck the children. The chronicler's point is the date — *in his days*. The
climate of Ahab's reign is one in which a man will rebuild what God cursed and pay for it out
of his own family, and nobody stops him.

## Where the account is settled

*Christ has **redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us**, for it is
written, "Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree."* See the note on Galatians 3:10–14.

Hiel paid the curse on Jericho with his sons. There is one other place in Scripture where a
curse is paid by a firstborn, and there the Father is the one who does not spare Him.

## Who arrives in the next verse

*And Elijah the Tishbite… said to Ahab, "As the LORD God of Israel lives, before whom I stand,
**there shall be neither dew nor rain these years, except at my word**."*

No introduction, no genealogy, no call narrative. The worst reign in the book gets the biggest
prophet, and he walks in off the page.

> **Where the two halves meet.** A king who treats the old sins as a starting point, and a
> builder who puts up what God had banned. The chapter names both in one breath and then sends
> Elijah in.
