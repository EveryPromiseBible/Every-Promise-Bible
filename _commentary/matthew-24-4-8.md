The first thing He says in answer to the disciples' question is a warning about being
misled.

## What was asked

They had shown Him the temple buildings and He had said *not one stone shall be left here
upon another*.

Then, on the Mount of Olives, privately: *when will these things be? And what will be the
sign of Your coming, and of the end of the age?*

Three questions. The chapter is the answer.

## The first instruction

*"**Be careful that no one leads you astray**."*

The Greek is **planese** — deceive, cause to wander. The root of *planet*.

Before any sign is named, the first thing said is about deception.

He returns to it: *many false prophets will rise up and deceive many*, and *false christs
and false prophets will rise and show great signs and wonders, so as to deceive, if
possible, even the elect*.

Three times in one chapter.

## Who comes

*"**Many will come using My name**, saying, '**I am the Messiah**,' and they will lead many
astray."*

Not opponents. People using the name.

## The wars

*"You will **hear of wars and rumors of wars**, but **do not let yourselves be alarmed**."*

The Greek is **throeisthe** — be frightened, be startled into panic.

## The clause that governs the paragraph

*"**These things have to happen, but the end is still to come**."*

Two halves. They must occur, and they are not the marker.

He says it again at the end of the section: *all of this is only the beginning*.

The warning is against reading the news as a countdown.

## The list

*"**Nation will rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom**, and there will be
**famines and plagues and earthquakes in place after place**."*

The Greek is *in various places* — **kata topous**.

## The image

*"All of this is only **the beginning of the birth pains**."*

The Greek is **odinon** — labour pains.

The image is precise, and Jesus chose it. Labour pains are real, they intensify, they come
in a pattern, and they are not the disaster they feel like — they are the beginning of
something arriving.

Paul uses the same word of creation: *the whole creation groans and labours with birth
pangs together until now.*

## What He says will be happening at the same time

Fifteen verses later: *and **this gospel of the kingdom will be preached in all the world as
a witness to all the nations**, and then the end will come.* See the note on
Matthew 24:9–14.

The two run together in the same chapter.

## Where the chapter ends

*"But of **that day and hour no one knows**, not even the angels of heaven, but My Father
only."* See the note on Matthew 24:36–41.

And then three parables about being ready — the servant, the ten virgins, and the talents —
and the sheep and the goats.

## What the whole discourse repeats

*Watch therefore, for you do not know what hour your Lord is coming.*

## Where He was sitting

Opposite the temple He had just walked out of, two days before the arrest.

> **The first instruction of the chapter.** Not to watch the news. To make sure nobody
> misleads you — and then a list of alarming things with *do not be alarmed* attached to
> it.
