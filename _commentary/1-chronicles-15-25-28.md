The ark finally moves, and Chronicles records one detail Samuel leaves out.

## Who goes

*David and **the elders of Israel and the commanders of thousands**.*

Not thirty thousand handpicked men this time. The leadership, the priests, the Levites and the
musicians.

## Where from

*From the house of Obed-edom, **with great rejoicing**.*

Three months earlier the procession had stopped there in fear. See the note on
2 Samuel 6:9–11.

## The clause

*And **because God had helped the Levites who carried the ark**, they sacrificed **seven bulls
and seven rams**.*

The Hebrew is **be'zor ha'elohim** — in God's helping.

Chronicles states it as the reason for the sacrifice. They got the ark to Jerusalem and the
thing they marked was that they had been helped to carry it.

Nobody died. Last time somebody had.

## What the sacrifice was

Seven bulls and seven rams. Samuel has *an ox and a fatted calf* at six steps; Chronicles has
the fuller count.

## What David wore

*David was clothed in **a robe of fine linen**, as were **all the Levites who carried the ark,
the singers, and Kenaniah** the leader of the music.*

The Hebrew is **butz** — fine white linen, the expensive kind.

Everybody in the procession is dressed the same. The king, the carriers, the singers and the
choirmaster.

*David also wore **a linen ephod**.*

The ephod is priests' clothing. He is not a priest, and he wears it.

Samuel notes the same detail and adds what his wife thought of it. See the note on
2 Samuel 6:12–15.

## The noise

*With **shouts of joy**, with the sound of **the ram's horn and trumpets and cymbals**, with
**harps and lyres ringing out**.*

Five instruments and a shout. Chronicles is written by somebody interested in the music
department and lists the players by name in the previous verses.

## Who Kenaniah was

*Chief of the Levites, was instructor in charge of the music, because he was skilful.*

The Hebrew is *he instructed about the carrying, because he was skilful*.

A named professional with a job title, in a passage about doing it properly.

## The difference between the two attempts

First: a new cart, thirty thousand men, no consecration, nobody consulted, and a death.

Second: shoulders and poles, Levites consecrated, instructions checked, sacrifices, uniform
linen, an appointed music director — and God's help noted as the reason for the thanksgiving.

## What the Chronicler is doing

Writing after the exile, for people rebuilding, and his interest is consistently in how worship
was ordered.

Samuel tells the story. Chronicles tells the arrangements.

## What David does at the end of the day

Chapter 16: appoints Asaph and his brethren to minister before the ark *continually, as every
day's work required*, and gives them a psalm.

*Give thanks to the LORD! Call upon His name… **seek the LORD and His strength; seek His face
evermore**.*

And then everybody gets a loaf, a piece of meat and a raisin cake, and goes home.

## Where the ark stays

In the tent David pitched for it, in Jerusalem, with singers around it — while the tabernacle of
Moses and the altar stayed at Gibeon.

That arrangement is what Amos calls the fallen tent of David, and what James quotes at the
Jerusalem council. See the note on Acts 15:13–18.

> **What they sacrificed for.** Not the arrival. Chronicles says it plainly: *because God had
> helped the Levites who carried the ark* — the help with the lifting is what got the seven
> bulls.
