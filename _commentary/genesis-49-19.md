Eleven words in English, and in Hebrew it is a piece of wordplay Jacob almost
certainly meant.

## The name inside the sentence

**Gad** means a troop, a raiding band. And the Hebrew line runs *gad gedud
yegudennu, ve-hu yagud aqeb* — the same consonants hammered five times over.

A troop shall troop upon him, but he shall troop upon their heel.

## What it says

*"Gad will be **attacked by raiders**, but he will **turn and strike at their
heels**."*

The trouble is not denied. He gets raided, and the raiders are described as a
troop — more than he can meet head-on.

And the last clause is the last event. Whoever hits first, Gad has the final
word.

## The ground Gad was given

East of the Jordan, on the frontier — the most exposed territory any tribe held,
open to Ammon, Moab and every raiding party out of the desert.

Jacob's line was not decorative. It described the border they would live on.

## Where Jesus walked into it

Across that lake, on the eastern shore, He met a man who could not be bound with
chains, living among the tombs, cutting himself with stones.

*"What is your name?" "**Legion**," he answered, because a great many demons had
gone into him.*

A Roman legion was some thousands of men. It is the largest concentration of the
enemy named anywhere in the Gospels, and it is standing on Gad's side of the
water.

He speaks, and it comes out. One word against a troop, and the man is found
sitting, dressed and in his right mind. See the note on Luke 8:30–33.

## The heel

*He will strike at their heels.*

The heel is where Genesis started. Chapter 3: *He shall bruise your head, and you
shall bruise His heel.*

Jacob's fourteenth son-blessing uses the word from the promise made in the garden,
and the man who grabbed his brother's heel at birth is the one saying it.

> **The shortest blessings carry the most.** Gad, Asher and Naphtali get one line
> each. That is the whole inheritance in words — a troop turned back, food fit for
> a king, and a doe set free — and every one of them turns up as ground Jesus stood
> on.
