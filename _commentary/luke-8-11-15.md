The one parable Jesus explains line by line, and He says elsewhere that it is the key to the
others.

## The key

Mark records the aside: *do you not understand this parable? **How then will you understand all
the parables?***

This one is the pattern.

## The identification

*"**The seed is the word of God**."*

Luke's phrasing. Matthew has *the word of the kingdom*; Mark has *the sower sows the word*.

One thing sown, four outcomes, and the seed is identical in every case.

## The footpath

*"The people who hear, and then **the devil comes and lifts the word right out of their
hearts**, so that they will not believe and be saved."*

Luke names the agent and the purpose. Matthew has *the wicked one*; Mark has *Satan*.

Nothing wrong with the seed and nothing wrong with the sowing. It was taken.

## The rock

*"The people who **hear the word and welcome it with real gladness, but they have no root**."*

The Greek is **meta charas** — with joy.

The problem is not the reception. It is enthusiastic.

*"They **believe for a little while**, and when the testing comes **they fall away**."*

The Greek is **pros kairon pisteuousin** — for a season they believe.

And **aphistantai** — they stand off, withdraw. The root of *apostasy*.

Luke says *in time of testing*; Mark says *tribulation or persecution arises for the word's
sake*.

## The thorns

*"The people who hear, but **as they go on their way**, the **worries and the money and the
pleasures of this life crowd in and choke them**."*

Three chokers named: **merimnon**, cares; **ploutou**, riches; **hedonon**, pleasures.

Mark adds a fourth: *the desires for other things*.

The Greek for *choke* is **sumpnigontai** — throttled, strangled. The same verb Luke uses of
the swine drowning and of the crowd pressing round Jesus.

*"And **nothing ever ripens**."*

The Greek is **ou telesphorousin** — they do not bring to completion.

Growth happens. Maturity does not.

## What thorns are

The ground's first product after the curse. See the note on Genesis 3:17–19.

And what the soldiers plaited into a wreath. See the note on John 19:1–3.

## The good ground

*"The people who **hear the word with an honest and generous heart**, and **hold on to it**,
and **by patient endurance bring in a harvest**."*

Three things, and Luke is the only one who lists them this way.

The Greek is **kardia kale kai agathe** — a noble and good heart. It is the Greek phrase for a
gentleman's character, and Luke, writing for a Greek reader, uses it.

**Katechousin** — hold fast, retain, keep a grip.

**En hupomone** — in endurance, remaining under.

## What is missing from Luke's version

The yields.

Matthew and Mark have *thirtyfold, sixtyfold, a hundredfold*. Luke has *a hundredfold* in the
parable itself and drops the numbers from the explanation.

## The four differences

The seed is the same, the sower is the same, and the soil is the only variable.

Three of the four hear. Two of the four start well. One finishes.

## Where the enemy features

Only in the first soil.

The second is undone by shallowness, the third by ordinary life. Nothing supernatural is named
in either.

## What follows immediately

*"No one lights a lamp and covers it with a jar… **so pay attention to how you listen**."* See
the note on Luke 8:16–18.

The parable about soils is followed by an instruction about the manner of hearing.

> **What is choking the third soil.** Not persecution and not the devil. Worries, money and
> pleasures — and Luke says the plant grows and simply never finishes.
