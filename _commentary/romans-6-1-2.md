Paul asks the question his own preaching provokes, and the fact that he has to ask
it tells you what he had been preaching.

## The question

*Shall we **keep on sinning, so that there will be all the more grace**?*

He has just written *where sin increased, grace overflowed all the more.* This is
the obvious next move, and Paul makes it himself before anyone else can.

Nobody ever asks this question of a message about behaving better. It only arises
if the grace preached was extravagant enough to sound dangerous.

He is accused of it elsewhere too: *why not say — as we are slanderously reported
and as some claim that we say — "Let us do evil that good may come"?*

That is the charge, and Paul reports that people were making it about him.

## The answer

*Certainly not.*

The Greek is **me genoito** — the strongest negative available, roughly *may it
never be*. Paul uses it ten times in Romans.

## What the answer is not

He does not say *because you will lose your salvation*. He does not say *because
God will punish you*. He does not reintroduce a rule, or a threat, or a condition.

He asks a different question back.

## The reason he gives

*We **died to sin**; how can we go on living in it any longer?*

Not *we should die to sin*. Not *we are dying to sin daily*. Past tense, done.

The argument is about impossibility of a sort — the absurdity of a dead man
continuing to keep house in the place he was carried out of.

Paul spends the rest of the chapter on that one fact: buried with Him, raised with
Him, the old man crucified, freed from sin. And only after all of it does an
instruction appear — *count yourselves dead indeed to sin, but alive to God.* See
the note on Romans 6:8–11.

The reckoning comes after the reality, and is based on it.

## Which way round the chapter runs

Every command in Romans 6 arrives after a statement of what is already true. Paul
never asks anyone to die to sin in order to become dead to it.

> **What he says at the end of the chapter.** *For sin shall not have dominion over
> you, **for you are not under law but under grace**.* The reason given for sin not
> ruling is the very thing the objector thought would let it rule.
