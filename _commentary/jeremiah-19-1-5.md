A prophet is told to buy a piece of pottery and take the leadership out to a rubbish dump.

## The purchase

*Go and **buy a clay jar from a potter**.*

The Hebrew is **baqbuq** — a narrow-necked flask, and the word is onomatopoeic: it is the gurgle
the liquid makes coming out.

He is told to buy it, which means it is his and it is whole.

The chapter before had him at the potter's house watching a spoiled vessel reworked *while the
clay was still soft*. See the note on Jeremiah 18:1–4.

A jar that is already fired cannot be reworked. It can only be broken, and that is the point of
the two chapters standing next to each other.

## The witnesses

*Take with you some of **the elders of the people** and some of **the elders of the priests**.*

Not a crowd. He is told to bring the men whose signatures would matter.

## The place

*The **Valley of Ben Hinnom**, near the entrance of **the Potsherd Gate**.*

**Ge-Hinnom** — the valley on the south side of the city, where children had been burned to Baal
and Molech, and where the city's refuse was tipped and burned.

The Greek of the name is **Gehenna**, and it is the word **Jesus** uses for hell.

*"And if your hand causes you to sin, cut it off. It is better for you to enter into life maimed,
rather than having two hands, to go to **hell**, into the fire that shall never be quenched."*

**Mark** 9, and every time He says it the word is this valley.

The Potsherd Gate is the gate the broken pottery went out through.

## The charge

*They have **filled this place with the blood of the innocent**.*

**Dam neqiyyim** — innocent blood.

*"**I have sinned by betraying innocent blood.**" And he threw down the pieces of silver in the
temple and departed.*

*But the chief priests took the silver pieces and said, "It is not lawful to put them into the
treasury, because they are the price of blood." And they consulted together and **bought with them
the potter's field**.*

**Matthew** 27. Chief priests, thirty pieces of silver, and a potter's field bought as a burial
ground outside the city — and **Matthew** says it fulfilled *what was spoken by Jeremiah the
prophet*.

The elements of Jeremiah 19 reassemble in one afternoon: the priests, the potter, the field, the
innocent blood.

## The clause at the end

*Something I never commanded or spoke of, **something that never entered My mind**.*

The Hebrew is **velo altah al-libbi** — it did not come up upon My heart.

Said of child sacrifice. It is the strongest disavowal in the prophets, and it is worth holding
next to the one thing that did:

*For **God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son**.*

*He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all.*

What Israel did to their children in that valley never crossed His mind. What He did with His own
Son was, as **Peter** says at Pentecost, *by the determined purpose and foreknowledge of God*.

## What Jeremiah does with the jar

He smashes it in front of them: *even so I will break this people and this city, as one breaks a
potter's vessel, which cannot be made whole again.*

And then he goes and stands in the temple court and says it again, and Pashhur the priest has him
beaten and put in the stocks.

> **The two chapters together.** In 18 the clay is soft and the potter reworks it. In 19 the jar
> is fired, and the only thing left to do with it is break it. Jeremiah is sent to the valley
> whose Greek name Jesus would use for hell — and centuries later a potter's field beside it is
> bought with the price of innocent blood.
