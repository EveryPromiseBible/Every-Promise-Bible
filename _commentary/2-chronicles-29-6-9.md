The young king explains to the Levites why the building is in the state it is in.

## Who he is talking about

*"For **our fathers** were unfaithful and did evil in the eyes of the LORD our God."*

**Our** fathers. He includes himself, standing there as the son of the man who did it.

Ahaz was his father. Hezekiah does not name him and does not exempt himself.

## The four verbs

*"They **forsook Him**, **turned their faces away** from the dwelling of the LORD, and
**turned their backs** on Him."*

Forsook, turned away, turned the back. The chronicler stacks them, and each one is a
movement of the body.

## What they did to the building

*"They also **shut the doors of the porch** and **put out the lamps**."*

Two acts, both by hand.

The lamps were the seven-branched lampstand, which was to burn *from evening until
morning before the LORD* — a continual statute, forever, in Leviticus.

Somebody put it out.

## What stopped

*"They **did not burn incense** or **offer burnt offerings** in the holy place to the God
of Israel."*

The incense on the golden altar and the offering on the bronze one. See the note on
Exodus 30:34–38.

Both daily. Both stopped.

## The consequence

*"So **the anger of the LORD came upon Judah and Jerusalem**, and He made them **an object
of terror, of horror, and of scorn**."*

Three words for what the nation became to the countries around it.

## The clause

*"**As you see with your own eyes**."*

He is not arguing from theory. He points at the room they are standing in and the state
of the country outside it.

Ahaz's reign had brought invasion from Aram, from Israel, and finally from the Assyrians
he had paid to protect him — *Tiglath-Pileser king of Assyria came to him and distressed
him, and did not assist him.*

## The cost, stated plainly

*"This is why our fathers have **fallen by the sword**, and **our sons and daughters and
wives are in captivity**."*

He names the families in the room.

## What he does not do

He does not blame the Levites, the previous priesthood, or the political situation. He
gives one cause and traces the whole list of effects to it.

And he does not stop there — the next verse begins *now it is in my heart to make a
covenant with the LORD God of Israel.*

## Where the diagnosis goes

Straight into work. The Levites rise, consecrate themselves, and start carrying things
out to the Kidron Valley the same day. See the note on 2 Chronicles 29:12–17.

## What follows the cleansing

The offerings, the Levites with cymbals and harps *according to the commandment of
David*, the song of the LORD beginning, and the whole assembly worshipping while the
burnt offering burned.

And then: *Hezekiah rejoiced, and all the people, that God had prepared the people, **for
the thing came about suddenly**.*

> **How he opens.** By naming what went wrong, in the first person plural, in front of
> the men who are about to do the work. Nobody is told the temple got that way by
> accident.
