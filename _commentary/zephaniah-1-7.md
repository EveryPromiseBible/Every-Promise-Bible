Two sentences, and the second one turns the metaphor round.

## The command

*"**Be still before the Lord GOD**, for the day of the LORD is near."*

The Hebrew is **has mippenei Adonai YHWH** — hush, from before the Lord GOD. **Has** is not a word;
it is a sound, the noise you make to silence a room.

*"**Be silent, all flesh, before the LORD**, for He is aroused from His holy habitation."*

*Then **the Lord is in His holy temple. Let all the earth keep silence before Him.**"*

Three prophets use it, and it is the same instruction each time: stop talking.

*"**Be still, and know that I am God.**"*

## The sacrifice

*"The LORD **has made ready a sacrifice**; He has **set apart His guests**."*

**Hekhin YHWH zevach** — the LORD has prepared a slaughter. **Hiqdish qeru'av** — He has sanctified
His invited ones.

The vocabulary is a feast. A **zevach** was the offering you ate: the animal killed, the portions
burned, and the rest served to the guests who had been ritually prepared to eat it.

## Who is on the menu

The next verse: *"On the day of the LORD's sacrifice I will call to account the officials and the
king's sons."*

The guests are not the ones eating. Zephaniah's feast has the nation as the offering and the
invited ones are whoever God has summoned to consume it — in the historical reading, Babylon.

Ezekiel makes it explicit: *speak to every sort of bird… assemble yourselves to **My sacrificial
meal** which I am sacrificing for you.*

## What the New Testament does with the same two nouns

*Then he said to me, "Write: '**Blessed are those who are called to the marriage supper of the
Lamb**!'"*

**Revelation** 19 keeps a prepared feast and invited guests, and the one being eaten is described
as a **Lamb** who was slain.

*"**Come, for all things are now ready.**"*

**Luke** 14, and **Jesus**' parable uses the identical construction — the meal prepared, the guests
called — and the trouble in the story is that the invited ones will not come.

## The two suppers

**Revelation** 19 has both in one chapter. The marriage supper of the Lamb, and then, twenty verses
later, *come and gather together for the supper of the great God*, where the birds are called to
eat the flesh of kings.

Zephaniah 1:7 is the second one. The New Testament sets the first beside it.

*For indeed **Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us**. Therefore let us keep the feast.*

**Paul**'s sentence resolves the ambiguity in Zephaniah's: at the Passover the sacrifice and the
meal are the same animal, and the guests eat what died in their place.

## Sanctified guests

*"He has **set apart** His guests."* **Qadash** — consecrated, made ready to approach.

*But you were washed, but **you were sanctified**, but you were justified in the name of the Lord
**Jesus** and by the Spirit of our God.*

The preparation of the guests is something done to them in both books.

> **The two readings of one metaphor.** A sacrifice prepared and guests consecrated. In Zephaniah
> the nation is what is served. At the Passover the guests eat the lamb that died instead of them,
> and Paul says which one Christ is.
