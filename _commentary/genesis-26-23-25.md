---
key: Genesis 26:23–25
title: For My servant Abraham’s sake
updated: 2026-08-09
---
God appears to Isaac the night he arrives at Beersheba, and the reason He gives for blessing
him is somebody else.

## What he had just finished doing

Digging wells and being driven off them. Esek — *contention*. Sitnah — *hostility*. And then
Rehoboth, *for now the LORD has made room for us*. See the note on Genesis 26:22.

He moved four times and did not fight once.

## The arrival

*From there Isaac **went up to Beersheba**. **That night the LORD appeared to him**.*

The same night. He does not have to settle in first.

Beersheba is his father's ground — the well Abraham dug and the tamarisk tree he planted, where
he *called on the name of the LORD, the Everlasting God*.

## What is said first

*"**I am the God of your father Abraham. Do not be afraid, for I am with you.**"*

Three things, and none of them is a requirement.

**Do not be afraid** is the first command in the sentence, and it is the commonest thing God
says to people in Scripture when He appears.

## The reason

*"I will bless you and multiply your descendants **for the sake of my servant Abraham**."*

Not for Isaac's sake. Not because of the wells, the restraint, the hundredfold harvest in a
famine year, or anything else the chapter has recorded about him.

He is blessed on account of a man who is already dead.

## What that is

It is the whole mechanism of grace, stated in Genesis in five words.

*And be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, **even as God in Christ
forgave you***. See the note on Ephesians 4:30–32.

*For His sake* is the ground everybody who has ever been blessed by God has stood on. Isaac gets
it for Abraham's sake; we are told plainly whose sake ours is for.

Nothing in the verse depends on Isaac's performance, which is convenient, because the paragraph
immediately before this has him telling the men of Gerar that his wife is his sister — the same
lie his father told, in the same town.

## What he does about it

*So Isaac **built an altar there and called on the name of the LORD**. He **pitched his tent**
there, and his servants **dug a well**.*

Three verbs, in that order. Altar, tent, well.

Worship first, then somewhere to live, then water. His father had marked his whole route the
same way — tent and altar, Shechem, Bethel, Hebron. See the note on Genesis 12:4–9.

## What happens the next morning

*Then Abimelech came to him from Gerar.* The men who threw him out arrive to ask for a treaty.
See the note on Genesis 26:26–29.

The night God says *I am with you*, and the morning the neighbours turn up saying they have
noticed.

> **The five words.** *For the sake of my servant Abraham.* The blessing is real, it is
> unconditional, and the reason given for it is a man Isaac had buried.
