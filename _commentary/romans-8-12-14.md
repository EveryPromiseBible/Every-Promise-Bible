Paul opens with a strange half-sentence, and the strangeness is the point.

## The sentence he does not finish

*We are in debt — but not to the old nature, as though we owed it a life lived on
its terms.*

He says who they are **not** indebted to, and then never says who they are. The
sentence is left open.

Read what he has just spent eleven verses on and the missing half is obvious: if
anyone is owed, it is the God who did what the law could not. Paul leaves the
reader to finish it.

## What "putting to death" is not

*If by the Spirit you go on putting the body's doings to death, you will live.*

Two things in the grammar keep this from becoming grim self-effort. It is
continuous — *go on* doing it, not a single crisis. And it is **by the Spirit**,
which names who is doing the work through them.

Paul has already ruled out the alternative at length: the law could not manage it,
and neither could he, as chapter 7 records in painful detail.

> **The line that follows.** *All who are led by the Spirit of God are the sons
> of God* — and then Paul goes immediately to adoption, Abba and inheritance. He
> raises the leading in order to prove sonship, not to test it. See the note on
> Romans 8:15–17, which is the same argument continuing.
