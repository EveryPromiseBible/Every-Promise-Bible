One sentence, and the two halves of it do not obviously match.

## The mismatch

*Go into the ark, **you and all your household**, for I have seen that **you
alone** are righteous in this generation.*

The reason given is about one man. The invitation covers eight people.

Nothing is said about Noah's wife, his sons, or his sons' wives — not a word
about their character in either direction. They go in on the strength of the
verdict passed on him.

## The pattern this belongs to

It happens repeatedly. A household is spared for the sake of one man's standing:
Noah's family here, Rahab's family in Jericho, Lot's family in Sodom, the
Israelite houses at Passover reckoned by household rather than by person.

The Bible keeps counting people in units of *house*, and it keeps doing it at the
moments of rescue.

## "Come into the ark"

The Hebrew verb is *come*, not *go*. It is the word you use to invite someone
toward where you already are — which means God is not sending Noah into the boat
from outside. He is calling him in from somewhere He already is.

> **The last line.** *And Noah did everything the LORD commanded him.* It is the
> fourth time Genesis says it in two chapters. The obedience is recorded flatly,
> without commentary, and then the rain starts seven days later — which is how
> long Noah had to sit in a finished boat under a clear sky.
