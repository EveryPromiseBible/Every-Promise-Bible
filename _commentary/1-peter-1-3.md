The first sentence of the letter proper, and it is one long burst.

## The opening

*Praise God, **the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ**.*

The Greek is **eulogetos ho Theos kai Pater tou Kuriou hemon Iesou Christou**.

A Jewish blessing formula — **barukh** — carried into Greek. Paul opens Ephesians and 2
Corinthians the same way.

## Who is writing

*Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ.*

The fisherman who denied Him three times in a courtyard, writing to *the pilgrims of the
Dispersion in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia*.

Five Roman provinces in what is now Turkey. Believers under pressure and scattered.

## The mercy

*He had **mercy to spare**, and He spent it.*

The Greek is **kata to polu autou eleos** — according to His great mercy.

**Polu** — much, abundant.

## The birth

*Giving us **a second birth**.*

The Greek is **anagennesas hemas** — having begotten us again.

**Anagennao** — to beget anew. The verb appears twice in the New Testament, both in this chapter.

Peter was in the room when Nicodemus was told *unless one is born again*. See the note on
John 3:1–3.

## What it is a birth into

*Into **a hope with a pulse in it**.*

The Greek is **eis elpida zosan** — into a living hope.

## The adjective

**Zosa** — living. Not *lively* or *strong*. Alive.

Peter uses the word four times in this letter: a living hope, the living word, living stones, and
*the living and enduring word of God*.

## Why living

*Jesus came up out of the grave, and **that is how**.*

The Greek is **di' anastaseos Iesou Christou ek nekron** — through the resurrection of Jesus Christ
from the dead.

The hope is alive because its ground is a man who came back.

## What Peter had before that

*Then Peter went out and **wept bitterly**.*

Between the courtyard and the empty tomb he had nothing.

The angel's message on the Sunday named him separately: *go, tell His disciples — **and Peter**.*

## What follows

*To **an inheritance incorruptible and undefiled and that does not fade away**, reserved in heaven
for you.*

Three adjectives, all negatives: **aphtharton**, **amianton**, **amaranton**.

Cannot decay, cannot be polluted, cannot fade. See the note on 1 Peter 1:4–5.

## What is guarding it

*Who are **kept by the power of God** through faith for salvation ready to be revealed in the last
time.*

The Greek is **phrouroumenous** — garrisoned, held under guard.

The same military verb Paul uses of the peace of God standing over the mind. See the note on
Philippians 4:6–7.

## The joy

*In this you greatly rejoice, though now **for a little while, if need be, you have been grieved by
various trials**.* See the note on 1 Peter 1:6–7.

## Whom they have not seen

*Whom having not seen you love. Though now you do not see Him, yet believing, **you rejoice with joy
inexpressible and full of glory**.* See the note on 1 Peter 1:8–9.

The Greek is **anekaleto** — unspeakable.

Written by a man who had seen Him, to people who had not, about a joy he says cannot be described.

> **The adjective.** **Zosa** — living. Not a strong hope or a bright one. Alive, and the reason
> given is a man who came out of a grave.
