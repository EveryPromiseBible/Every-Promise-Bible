The first miracle in John's Gospel, and the only people who know it happened are the staff.

## The situation

A wedding at Cana, and the wine has run out.

*And when they ran out of wine, the mother of Jesus said to Him, "**They have no wine**."*

*"Woman, what does your concern have to do with Me? **My hour has not yet come**."*

*His mother said to the servants, "**Whatever He says to you, do it**."* See the note on
John 2:1–5.

## The jars

*Standing there were **six stone waterpots**, the kind the Jews kept for **their ceremonial
washings**.*

The Greek is **lithinai hudriai** — stone water jars.

Stone, not clay. Stone vessels did not contract ritual impurity and so were used for purification
water; clay ones had to be broken if defiled.

## The capacity

*Each one holding **twenty or thirty gallons**.*

The Greek is **metretas duo e treis** — two or three measures.

Six jars at twenty-five gallons is around a hundred and fifty gallons.

## The number

Six.

John notes it. He does not say why.

## The instruction

*Jesus said to the servants, "**Fill the jars with water**."*

The only instruction given, and it is one anybody could carry out.

Nothing about wine. Nothing about the shortage. Fill the jars.

## The work

Around a hundred and fifty gallons, drawn and carried by hand. At eight pounds a gallon, that is
well over half a ton of water.

## The compliance

*So they filled them, **right up to the brim**.*

The Greek is **heos ano** — up to the top.

John records the detail. They did not half-fill them.

## The second instruction

*"**Now dip some out and carry it to the master of the feast**."*

The Greek is **antlesate nun** — draw out now.

They are told to serve it before anybody has tasted it.

Nothing in the text says the water had changed at the point they dipped.

## What the steward says

*When the master of the feast had tasted the water that was made wine, and **did not know where it
came from** (but the servants who had drawn the water knew).*

John's parenthesis is the point of the scene.

The man in charge has no idea. The staff, who carried it, know exactly.

## His verdict

*"**Every man at the beginning sets out the good wine**, and when the guests have well drunk, then
the inferior. **You have kept the good wine until now!**"*

He is complaining about the catering order, and he is right about the quality.

## The count

*This **beginning of signs** Jesus did in Cana of Galilee, and manifested His glory; and His
disciples believed in Him.*

The Greek is **archen ton semeion** — beginning of the signs.

John numbers this one and the official's son at Capernaum, and stops. See the note on
John 4:51–54.

## Who never finds out

The bridegroom. The guests. The steward.

The mother knows, the disciples know, and the servants know.

## What He said about His hour

*"My hour has not yet come."*

The word recurs through the Gospel — *no one laid a hand on Him, because His hour had not yet
come* — until the last week: *the hour has come that the Son of Man should be glorified*.

The first sign is performed before the hour, at a woman's request, into six stone jars for washing
that end the evening full of something else.

> **The instruction.** *Fill the jars with water* — the only thing anybody was told to do, and it
> took over half a ton of carrying before a single thing had changed.
