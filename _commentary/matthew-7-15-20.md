Near the end of the Sermon on the Mount, and it is a test for the audience rather than an
instruction.

## The warning

*"**Watch out for false prophets**."*

The Greek is **prosechete** — hold your mind toward, be on guard.

Prophets, not opponents. The people being described are inside, speaking.

## The disguise

*"They come to you **wearing sheep's wool, but underneath they are ravenous wolves**."*

The Greek is **en endumasin probaton** — in sheep's clothing.

**Harpages** — ravenous, rapacious. From the verb for snatching and plundering.

The disguise is the point. A wolf that looked like a wolf would need no warning.

## The test

*"**You will know them by their fruit**."*

The Greek is **epignosesthe** — you will recognise, know fully.

Not by their doctrine, in the first instance. Not by their following, and not by their
results.

## The rhetorical question

*"Do people gather **grapes off thornbushes, or figs off thistles**?"*

Both are absurd, deliberately. Nobody in the audience has ever tried.

Luke has the same saying with the fruits swapped — figs off thornbushes, grapes off a briar
patch. See the note on Luke 6:43–45.

## The rule

*"Every **good tree bears good fruit**, and a **rotten tree bears bad fruit**."*

The Greek is **sapron** — rotten, decayed, worthless.

*"A good tree **cannot** bear bad fruit, and a rotten tree **cannot** bear good fruit."*

Stated as an impossibility in both directions.

## What the fruit is not

Not the miracles.

The very next paragraph settles that: *many will say to Me in that day, "Lord, Lord, **have we
not prophesied in Your name, cast out demons in Your name, and done many wonders in Your
name?**"*

*And then I will declare to them, "**I never knew you**."*

Prophecy, exorcism and wonders are listed as things that can be present without the tree being
good.

## What the fruit is

The sermon has already spent six chapters on it: the meek, the merciful, the peacemakers,
turning the other cheek, loving enemies, giving without a trumpet, praying without display,
forgiving.

And Paul's list, which is singular: *the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace,
longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control*. See the note on
Galatians 5:22–24.

## The disposal

*"Every tree that does not bear good fruit is **cut down and thrown into the fire**."*

The same sentence John the Baptist used, word for word, at the Jordan.

## The repetition

*"**So then, you will know them by their fruit**."*

He says it twice, at the start and the finish, and the sentence between them is the argument.

## What the saying is for

Diagnosis, not improvement.

Nothing in the passage tells a thornbush to try harder. Thornbushes are not addressed; the
hearers are told how to identify one.

## What the sermon closes with

Two builders, two houses, one storm, and the difference is not the weather.

*"Everyone who hears these sayings of Mine **and does them**, I will liken him to a wise man
who built his house on the rock."*

And the crowd's reaction: *they were astonished at His teaching, for He taught them as one
having authority, and not as the scribes*.

## What makes a tree good

The passage says a rotten tree **cannot** bear good fruit, and never tells it to try. So the
question the parable leaves is how a tree changes species.

*"Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the
vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me… **for without Me you can do nothing**."*

**John** 15, and **Jesus** keeps the impossibility and names the remedy. It is not effort applied
to the fruit. It is attachment.

*Therefore, if anyone is in **Christ**, he is **a new creation**; old things have passed away;
behold, all things have become new.*

*For **we are His workmanship**, created in **Christ Jesus** for good works, which God prepared
beforehand that we should walk in them.*

**Paul**'s noun is **poiema** — a thing made. The good works are downstream of the making, and
**Ephesians** 2 puts them one verse after *by grace you have been saved… not of works*.

## The fruit that is singular

*The **fruit** of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness,
gentleness, self-control.*

**Karpos**, singular, and nine items. **Paul** does not call them fruits, and he does not call them
achievements. They are what grows on something the Spirit is in.

> **What the test is not.** Not power. Two paragraphs later, prophecy, exorcism and wonders are
> named as things done by people He says He never knew.
