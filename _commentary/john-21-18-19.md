Peter is restored, told how he will die, and given a two-word instruction.

## What has just happened

Three questions on a beach, over a charcoal fire — **anthrakian**, the same rare word **John** used
of the fire in the courtyard where Peter denied Him.

*"Simon, son of Jonah, do you love Me?"* Three times, for three denials, and three commissions:
*feed My lambs, tend My sheep, feed My sheep.*

He is not asked to explain himself. He is asked one question, and given work.

## The prediction

*"**When you were young, you tied your own belt and walked wherever you wanted to go. But when you
are old, you will stretch out your hands, and someone else will tie you and carry you where you do
not want to go.**"*

**John** adds the explanation: *this He spoke, signifying by what death he would glorify God.*

**Ekteneis tas cheiras sou** — you will stretch out your hands. By the time **John** wrote, the phrase
was standard for crucifixion, and early tradition has Peter crucified in Rome.

## What is being contrasted

Not youth and age. Self-direction and being carried.

*"**Lord, why can I not follow You now? I will lay down my life for Your sake.**" Jesus answered him,
"Will you lay down your life for My sake? Most assuredly, I say to you, the rooster shall not crow
till you have denied Me three times."*

**John** 13. Peter had volunteered for exactly this and could not do it that week. Here he is told he
will do it, and it is stated as a fact rather than asked as a commitment.

*Being confident of this very thing, that **He who has begun a good work in you will complete it**.*

## Glorify God

*"**Signifying by what death he would glorify God.**"*

The same verb **John** uses of the cross — *the hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified*. See
the note on John 12:20–23.

## The two words

*"**Follow Me.**"*

**Akolouthei moi** — a present imperative: keep following.

They are the first words He said to Peter, on the same lake, three years earlier. The gospel ends by
repeating the opening instruction to a man who has failed in between.

## And the next thing Peter does

*Then Peter, turning around, saw the disciple whom Jesus loved following… and said, "**But Lord, what
about this man?**"*

*"**If I will that he remain till I come, what is that to you? You follow Me.**"*

The pronoun is emphatic — **sy moi akolouthei**. He is told the same two words a second time, in one
conversation, because he had already started looking sideways.

> **The two words.** *Follow Me* — the first thing He ever said to Peter, given back to him after
> the denial, and then repeated within three verses because he immediately asked about somebody
> else.
