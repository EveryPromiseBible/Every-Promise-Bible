Before a single vision, before the seals and the trumpets, John states what the
reader already is.

## The three verbs

*He loved us. With His own blood He washed our sins off us. He made us kings and
priests serving God, His own Father.*

Three short clauses, all in the past. Loved, washed, made. Nothing here is
pending.

And the order runs from the inside out: love first, then the cleansing, then the
office.

## Kings and priests

In Israel those two were kept strictly apart. Priests came from Levi, kings from
Judah, and when King Uzziah went into the temple to burn incense he came out a
leper.

Only Melchizedek held both, and Hebrews makes much of it. Here both are given to
ordinary believers in seven small congregations in Asia, some of which the next
two chapters will criticise sharply.

## Who it is said to

Not to spiritual heroes. Read on and these are the churches that have left their
first love, tolerated false teaching, and gone lukewarm.

The status is stated at the front, before any of that, and it is never withdrawn
during the letters that follow.

## The description of Christ

*The witness who never lies, the first to be born out of the dead, the ruler the
kings of the earth answer to.*

Three titles, and they map onto the whole book: the one who tells the truth
about what is coming, the one who has already been through death, and the one to
whom every earthly power is answerable — which is the thing a persecuted church
most needed to hear.

> **"The One who is here now, who was here before, and who is on His way."** John's
> version of the divine name, stretched across all three tenses, and he puts the
> present first. Not *was, is, and will be* — but **is**, was, and is coming.
