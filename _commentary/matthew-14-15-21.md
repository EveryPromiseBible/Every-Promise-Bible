The disciples propose sending the crowd away. He tells them to feed it.

## What He had been doing

The chapter opens with John the Baptist's execution, and *when Jesus heard it, He departed by boat
to a deserted place by Himself*.

The crowd followed on foot. *And He was moved with compassion for them, and healed their sick.*

He went away to grieve, and healed the people who followed Him there.

## The suggestion

*"This is **a lonely place**, and the hour is already late. **Send the crowds away** so they can go to
the villages and **buy themselves something to eat**."*

Practical, reasonable, and the only workable plan available.

## The refusal

*"**They do not need to go away. You give them something to eat**."*

The Greek is **dote autois humeis phagein** — the pronoun is emphatic. *You* give them.

## The inventory

*"**All we have here**," they said, "**is five loaves of bread and two fish**."*

The word *all* is the whole answer. They are reporting a shortfall.

John adds that it came from a boy in the crowd, and that the loaves were barley — the poor man's
grain.

## The instruction

*"**Bring them here to Me**."*

The Greek is **pherete moi hōde autous**.

The five and two do not become enough. They change hands.

## The seating

*Then He told the crowds to **sit down on the grass**.*

Mark records the detail: in groups of hundreds and fifties, on green grass.

An unmanageable mass is turned into countable units before anything is distributed.

## The four verbs

*He **took** the five loaves and the two fish, **looked up** to heaven, **gave thanks**, and **broke** the
loaves.*

The Greek is **labōn… eulogēsen… klasas… edōken**.

Took, blessed, broke, gave.

## Where the four appear again

*And as they were eating, Jesus **took** bread, **blessed** and **broke** it, and **gave** it to the
disciples.* See the note on Matthew 26:26–28.

And on the road to Emmaus: *He **took** bread, **blessed** and **broke** it, and **gave** it to them*.
*Then their eyes were opened and they knew Him.* See the note on Luke 24:30–32.

The same four verbs, three times, and the third time they are what identifies Him.

## What He did not do

He never touched the crowd's meal.

*He handed them to the disciples, and **the disciples gave them to the people**.*

The multiplying happens somewhere between His hands and theirs. Matthew does not say where.

## The result

*Everyone **ate and had enough**.*

The Greek is **echortasthēsan** — they were filled, satisfied. A word used of feeding animals to the
full.

## The leftovers

*And afterward they gathered up what was left over — **twelve baskets full**.*

The Greek is **kophinoi** — the small wicker basket a traveller carried.

Twelve of them, and twelve disciples. Each man who had reported the shortage picks up a basket.

## The count

*About **five thousand men** had eaten, **besides the women and the children**.*

The Greek is **chōris gunaikōn kai paidiōn**.

The stated figure is the partial one.

## The only miracle in all four Gospels

Every evangelist records it. Nothing else in the ministry appears in all four except the passion
and the resurrection.

## What He says about it afterwards

*"I am the bread of life. **He who comes to Me shall never hunger**."* See the note on John 6:35–40.

The discourse the next day, to the same crowd, who had come back looking for another meal.

> **The handover.** The five loaves never become sufficient in the disciples' hands. They are told
> to bring them, and the arithmetic changes on the other side of that.
