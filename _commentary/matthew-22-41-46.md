After three days of being questioned, He asks one of His own, and it ends the
week's interrogation.

## The question

*"**What do you think about the Messiah? Whose son is He?**"*

It is the only question in the Gospels He puts to His enemies about Himself, and it
is the one everything hangs on.

## Their answer

*"David's."*

Correct, and it is what every Jew would have said. The Messiah comes from David's
line — 2 Samuel 7, Isaiah 11, Jeremiah 23.

They are not wrong. They are short.

## What He asks next

*"Then how is it that **David, speaking by the Spirit, calls Him Lord**?"*

He quotes Psalm 110:1 — *the LORD said to my Lord: sit at My right hand until I make
Your enemies a footstool.*

Two different words. The first is **YHWH**. The second is **Adonai**, my Lord —
and David is the greatest king Israel ever had, with nobody above him but God.

He calls someone else *my Lord*, and that someone is his own descendant.

## The problem

*"**If David calls Him Lord, how can He be David's son?**"*

In that culture the line ran one way. A father does not address his descendant as
*my Lord*; every honour flows upward through the generations.

Unless the descendant existed before the ancestor.

## The other time He said it

*"Before Abraham was, **I AM**."*

They picked up stones for that one. See the note on John 8:57–59.

Same claim, two forms. Once to a hostile crowd in plain words, and once here, as a
question, from their own songbook — and neither time does anybody misunderstand
what is being said.

## What Psalm 110 goes on to say

*The LORD has sworn and will not relent: **You are a priest forever according to
the order of Melchizedek**.*

A king on David's throne who is also a priest, and not of Aaron's line. Hebrews
spends two chapters on that verse.

## The silence

*No one could answer Him a word, and **from that day on no one dared ask Him
another question**.*

The questioning stops. Matthew reports it as the end of it — after this the
Sanhedrin stop debating and start planning.

> **What He does immediately after.** Turns to the crowds and the disciples and
> delivers the seven woes, and finishes with *Jerusalem, Jerusalem, how often I have
> longed to gather your children.* The last public words of His ministry, and they
> end in tears. See the note on Matthew 23:37–39.
