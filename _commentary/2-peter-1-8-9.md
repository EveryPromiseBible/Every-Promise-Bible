Two sentences, and the second one traces a problem back to something forgotten.

## The condition

*If those things are in you, and **there is more of them all the time**.*

The Greek is *being in you and abounding* — both present participles, both continuous.

Peter is not describing a completed set. He is describing something increasing.

## The two negatives

*They will keep you from **standing around useless** and from **coming up empty**.*

- **Useless** — **argos**, from *a-* and *ergon*: without work, idle. It is the word for
  the labourers standing in the marketplace all day in the parable.
- **Coming up empty** — **akarpos**, unfruitful.

Peter states the benefit as the absence of two failures.

## Where the fruitfulness is located

*In **the knowing of our Lord Jesus Christ**.*

The Greek is **epignosis** — full knowledge, knowledge that has landed.

Not in ministry, in achievement, or in general. In the knowing of a person.

## The other case

*But **whoever is missing them cannot see**.*

The Greek is **tuphlos** — blind.

## The correction

*He is **squinting at what sits right in front of his face and nothing past it**.*

The Greek is **muopazon** — the root of *myopic*. Short-sighted, screwing up the eyes.

Peter says blind and then immediately narrows it: not sightless, near-sighted. He can
see what is close.

## The diagnosis

*And he has **forgotten that his old sins were washed off of him**.*

The Greek is **lethen labon** — having received forgetfulness. The root behind *Lethe*,
the river of forgetting.

That is the cause Peter lands on. Not weak will, not bad teaching, not lack of effort.

## What was forgotten

*That his old sins were washed off of him.* The Greek is **katharismos** — cleansing,
purification. It is the word used of ritual purification, and of what Hezekiah's Levites
did to the temple. See the note on 2 Chronicles 29:12–17.

A past event, done to him.

## The direction of the argument

Peter has just given a list of seven things to add. See the note on 2 Peter 1:5–7.

Then he explains what their absence means, and the explanation runs backward — the
missing list is a symptom, and the thing underneath it is a forgotten cleansing.

## What he asks for next

*Therefore, brethren, be even more diligent to **make your call and election sure**, for
if you do these things you will never stumble.*

And then: *I will not be negligent to **remind you always of these things**, though you
know them and are established in the present truth.*

Peter's stated method against the forgetting is repetition. He says it three times in
four verses: *remind you*, *stir you up by reminding you*, *that you may be mindful*.

## Why he is in a hurry

*Knowing that shortly I must put off my tent, just as our Lord Jesus Christ showed me.*

He is writing what he wants remembered after he is gone.

> **What the near-sighted man can see.** What is in front of him. The thing Peter says
> he has lost sight of is behind him — a washing that already happened.
