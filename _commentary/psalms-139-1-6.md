title: You have searched me and You know me

Six verses about being completely known, and not one of them is a threat.

## The first line

*O LORD, **You have searched me and You know me**.*

**Chaqar** — to search out, to dig, to explore a place thoroughly. The verb used of prospecting
and of investigating a legal case.

It is in the perfect tense. The searching is finished before the psalm begins.

## What the search found

Sitting down and standing up. Thoughts from far away. The path and the resting place. Every
way.

*Before a word is on my tongue, O LORD, **You already know it whole**.*

Nothing on the list is impressive and nothing on it is hidden. It is a description of an
ordinary life examined completely.

*And there is no creature hidden from His sight, but **all things are naked and open to the eyes
of Him** to whom we must give account.*

**Hebrews** says the same and then, immediately: *let us therefore **come boldly** to the throne
of grace.* The full exposure and the confident approach are consecutive sentences.

## The hemming in

*You **hem me in, behind and before**, and You lay Your hand on me.*

**Tsur** — to besiege, to enclose. The word for an army surrounding a city.

Read as threat it is claustrophobic. Read as it stands — with the hand laid on rather than
raised — it is protection from both directions at once.

*"**Neither shall anyone snatch them out of My hand.**"*

## What he concludes

*This knowing is **too wonderful for me**, too high, I cannot reach it.*

Not *too much for me to bear*. Too high to climb up to.

*Oh, the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! **How unsearchable are His
judgments** and His ways past finding out!*

**Paul** ends eleven chapters of argument in exactly the same posture.

## Where the psalm ends

*"**Search me, O God, and know my heart.**"* See the note on Psalms 139:23–24.

Having said He has already done it, David asks Him to. That is a psalm about being fully known
inviting more of it, which nobody does unless they are also fully held.

> **The tense.** *You have searched me.* Finished, before the poem starts — and the man who
> says it spends twenty-two more verses on how far the searching reached, and then asks for
> it again.
