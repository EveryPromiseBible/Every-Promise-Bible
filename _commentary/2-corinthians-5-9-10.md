Paul names his single aim, and then the reason for it.

## The context

*So we are always confident, knowing that while we are at home in the body **we are absent from the
Lord**.*

*We are confident, yes, well pleased rather **to be absent from the body and to be present with the
Lord**.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 5:6–8.

Two locations, and he prefers the second.

## The ambition

*And so, **at home or away**, this is what we set our hearts on: **to please Him**.*

The Greek is **philotimoumetha** — we make it our ambition.

**Philotimeomai** — literally *to love honour*. The word for the drive that made a Greek citizen
pursue public distinction.

Paul takes the ambition word and points it at one thing.

He uses it three times, and once tells the Thessalonians to *be ambitious to be quiet*. See the
note on 1 Thessalonians 4:9–12.

## The independence of circumstance

*At home or away.*

Whether alive or dead, in his own framing. The aim does not change with the location.

## The reason

*For **every one of us has to be shown plainly before the judgment seat of Christ**.*

The Greek is **phanerothenai dei** — it is necessary to be made manifest.

**Phaneroo** — to bring into the open. Not merely *appear*, but be made visible.

## The seat

**Bema** — the raised platform.

In a Greek city the **bema** was where a magistrate sat to hear cases, and where prizes were
awarded at the games.

Paul stood before one in Corinth: *and Gallio drove them from the judgment seat*. His readers knew
the object.

## Who appears

*Every one of us.*

The Greek is **tous pantas hemas** — the all of us.

Paul includes himself and his readers. This is not a passage about outsiders.

## What happens

*So that each of us may **receive back what is owed for the things done through the body**.*

The Greek is **komisetai ta dia tou somatos** — may receive the things through the body.

**Komizo** — to receive back, to be repaid.

## The two categories

*Whether **the good or the bad**.*

The Greek is **eite agathon eite phaulon**.

**Phaulos** — worthless, of no account. Not the word for wickedness, but for what turns out to be
nothing.

## What is not at stake

Salvation.

Paul has just spent two chapters on it: *if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation*, and *He
made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us*. See the note on
2 Corinthians 5:20–21.

## What is at stake

The work.

*Now if anyone builds on this foundation with gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay, straw,
**each one's work will become clear**… **If anyone's work is burned, he will suffer loss; but he
himself will be saved**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 3:10–15.

## What follows immediately

*Knowing, therefore, **the terror of the Lord, we persuade men**.*

The Greek is **phobos** — the fear.

*For the love of Christ compels us… that **those who live should live no longer for themselves**, but
for Him who died for them and rose again.*

Two motives in consecutive verses, and Paul names both without ranking them.

> **The word.** **Philotimoumetha** — the Greek drive for public honour, aimed at one thing and
> declared independent of whether he is alive or dead.
