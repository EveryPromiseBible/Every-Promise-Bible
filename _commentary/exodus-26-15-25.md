The walls of the tabernacle, and what they stand on.

## What they are made of

*Upright frames of **acacia wood** to hold the tabernacle upright.*

Acacia is the desert tree — hard, thorny, slow-growing, and it does not rot. It was
the only usable timber in that wilderness.

Each frame is *ten cubits tall and a cubit and a half wide* — about fifteen feet high
and just over two across.

Elsewhere in the chapter they are overlaid with gold.

## The pegs

*With **two pegs at the bottom set side by side** for joining — every frame made the
same way.*

The Hebrew is two hands, two tenons. Each board has exactly two, and they are what
hold it in place.

## What they stand in

*Resting on **forty silver bases — two bases under each frame, one for each peg**.*

Not driven into the sand. Every board sits in its own pair of silver sockets, and
nothing touches the ground.

Ninety-six sockets in total, one under each tenon.

## Where the silver came from

Exodus 30 and 38 give it. Every man twenty and over paid a half-shekel at the
census:

*"**The rich shall not give more and the poor shall not give less** than half a
shekel, when you give an offering to the LORD, **to make atonement for yourselves**."*

It is called *the atonement money*.

And Exodus 38 records what was done with it: *the hundred talents of silver were for
casting the sockets of the sanctuary.*

The foundation the whole tent stands on is redemption money, at a flat rate, from
every man alike.

## The equal price

The rich paid the same as the poor. Nobody had a larger share of the foundation, and
nobody had a smaller one.

Peter uses the same idea in his own vocabulary: *you were not redeemed with
corruptible things, like silver or gold… but with the precious blood of Christ.*

## The frames, and what they were

Desert wood, cut, squared, covered in gold, standing upright in silver.

Nothing about the timber is impressive on its own. It is what it is standing in that
holds it.

## The number

Twenty boards on the south, twenty on the north, and eight at the back — with the two
corner frames doubled and fitted into a single ring.

Forty-eight frames, held by ninety-six sockets, carrying the curtains that made the
one place on earth God said He would meet people.

> **What it was all designed to hold up.** Ten curtains of fine linen, blue, purple
> and scarlet, with cherubim worked into them — and behind the veil, the ark. The
> boards are structure. Nobody was meant to be looking at them.
