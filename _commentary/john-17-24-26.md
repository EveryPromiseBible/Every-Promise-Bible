The last three verses of the prayer, and the first word of them is not a request.

## The verb

*Father, **I want**.*

The Greek is **thelo** — I will, I desire.

Everywhere else in the prayer He asks: *I pray for them*, *keep them*, *sanctify them*.
Here He states what He wants.

## What He wants

*The ones **You have given Me** to be **with Me where I am**.*

**Given Me** — the phrase runs through the whole prayer. He uses it seven times.

**Where I am** — the same words He used in the upper room: *I go to prepare a place for
you… that where I am, there you may be also.*

## What for

*So that they may **see My glory**.*

Not share it, in this clause. See it.

He had asked earlier in the prayer for the other thing: *the glory which You gave Me I have
given them*.

## Where the glory comes from

*The glory **You gave Me because You loved Me before the world was made**.*

The Greek is *before the foundation of the world*.

The loving is dated earlier than the creation, and the glory is attributed to it.

## The address

*Righteous Father.*

He has said *Holy Father* earlier in the prayer, and *Father* throughout. This is the only
*righteous* one, and it comes attached to a statement about the world not knowing Him.

## The three knowings

*The world **has not known You**, but **I have known You**, and **these have known that You
sent Me**.*

Three clauses in descending scale — the world, Himself, and the eleven men in the room.

What the disciples know is the smallest of the three, and it is enough to put them on the
right side of the sentence.

## The name

*I have **made Your name known to them**, and **I will go on making it known**.*

Two tenses. Done, and continuing.

He had said it at the start of the prayer: *I have manifested Your name to the men whom
You have given Me out of the world.*

## What the name is

Not a sound. In Hebrew usage a name is what somebody is.

And in the Gospels the disclosure has a specific shape: He teaches them to say **Father**,
and He says **Abba** in Gethsemane — a word so particular that Paul and Mark both leave it
untranslated. See the note on Galatians 4:4–7.

## The purpose

*So that **the love with which You loved Me may be in them, and I in them**.*

The Greek is *the love with which You loved Me* — the same love, not a comparable one.

That is the last request of the prayer, and it asks for the love named in verse 24 —
the one that predates the world — to be located inside people.

## The last four words

*And I in them.*

The prayer ends there. The next verse is *when Jesus had spoken these words, He went out
with His disciples over the Brook Kidron.*

## Who was listening

Eleven men, hours from scattering. One of them would deny Him before morning.

He prays this over them out loud.

> **What He asks for last.** Not protection and not success. That the love He was loved
> with before the world existed would be inside the people in the room — and then He goes
> out to the garden.
