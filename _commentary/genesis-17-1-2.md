Thirteen years of silence end with a new name for God.

## The age

*When Abram was **ninety-nine years old**.*

Ishmael was born when Abram was eighty-six, at the end of chapter 16.

Between that chapter and this one, Genesis records nothing. Thirteen years, and no
appearance, no word, and no conversation.

The silence begins after the attempt to produce the heir by arrangement.

## The name

*"**I am God Almighty**."*

The Hebrew is **El Shaddai**, and this is the first time it appears in Scripture.

The derivation is uncertain and has been argued over for centuries — the older sense may
be *the mountain one*, or connect to a root for sufficiency.

It becomes the patriarchs' name for God. Exodus says so explicitly: *I appeared to Abraham,
to Isaac, and to Jacob, as **God Almighty**, but by My name LORD I was not known to them.*

## Why here

A man of ninety-nine is about to be told he will have a son by a wife of ninety.

The name given at that moment is the one about sufficiency.

## The instruction

*"**Walk before Me, and be blameless**."*

The Hebrew is **tamim** — complete, whole, without blemish. It is the word used of a
sacrificial animal, and of Noah: *Noah was a just man, **perfect** in his generations*.

**Walk before Me** — the Hebrew is *walk to My face*.

Enoch and Noah *walked with*. Abram is told to walk before.

## The covenant

*"I will **make My covenant between Me and you**, and **I will multiply you greatly**."*

The Hebrew for *multiply* is doubled — *multiply you exceedingly*.

## What Abram does

*Then Abram **fell on his face**.*

## What happens in the chapter

The names change. **Abram** — exalted father — becomes **Abraham**, father of a multitude.
**Sarai** becomes **Sarah**.

Both new names have a **hei** inserted, the letter of the divine name, and Jewish
commentary has noted it for a very long time.

## The laugh

*Then Abraham **fell on his face and laughed**, and said in his heart, "Shall a child be
born to a man who is a hundred years old? And shall Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a
child?"*

And: *"**Oh, that Ishmael might live before You!**"*

He has a son already and would rather the promise attached to him.

## The answer

*"**No, Sarah your wife shall bear you a son**, and you shall call his name **Isaac**."*

**Yitzhaq** — *he laughs*. The name is given in the same conversation as the laughing.

Sarah laughs too, one chapter later, behind a tent door, and denies it.

## What is required in the chapter

Circumcision — *the sign of the covenant*, given here and not before.

Paul makes the chronology his whole argument: Genesis 15 has Abram counted righteous by
believing; Genesis 17 has the sign. Fourteen years apart, in that order. See the note on
Romans 4:6–8.

## What Hebrews says about the pair

*By faith **Sarah herself also received strength to conceive seed**, and she bore a child
when she was past the age, because she judged Him faithful who had promised.* See the note
on Hebrews 11:8–12.

> **What the name means in that room.** A man of ninety-nine, thirteen years after trying
> to arrange the heir himself, is told the name **El Shaddai** — and then told the son is
> coming through the ninety-year-old.
