Three prohibitions and one permission, and the permission is the surprise.

## The three

*Let not the wise boast in their wisdom. Let not the strong boast in their
strength. Let not the rich boast in their riches.*

Wisdom, strength, wealth — the three things every ancient society ranked people
by, and ours has not moved on much.

Note that none of the three is condemned. Solomon was given wisdom and wealth by
God directly. The prohibition is on boasting in them, which is a different thing
from having them.

## The one

*But if anyone would boast, let them boast in this alone, that they have the
understanding to know Me.*

Boasting is not forbidden. It is redirected. There is something a person is
permitted to be loud about, and it is knowing God.

The Hebrew for *understanding* here is about insight gained — a knowing that has
been worked at and arrived at, not merely information held.

## What He wants known about Himself

*That I am the LORD, and that I show steadfast love, justice, and righteousness
on the earth.*

He specifies. Of everything that could be said about God, three things are named:
**hesed** — the covenant-keeping love — along with justice and righteousness. And
all three are described as being done *on the earth*, not in the abstract.

Then: *for in these I take delight.*

## Where Paul takes it

He quotes the last line twice — in 1 Corinthians 1 after saying God chose the
foolish and weak things, and again in 2 Corinthians. Both times as *let the one
who boasts, boast in the Lord.*

He treats it as settling the question of what a Christian may be proud of.

> **When Jeremiah said it.** In a book largely about a nation about to lose its
> wise men, its soldiers and its wealth to Babylon. The three things he tells
> them not to boast in were all about to be taken away, and the one thing he
> leaves them was the one thing exile could not touch.
