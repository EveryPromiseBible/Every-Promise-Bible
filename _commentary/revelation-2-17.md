Three promises to one church, and the last one is private.

## Where the church is

Pergamos — the provincial capital of Asia, with the temple of Rome and Augustus, the great altar
of Zeus, and the healing shrine of Asclepius.

*"I know your works, and where you dwell, **where Satan's throne is**."*

## What they had done

*"And you hold fast to My name, and did not deny My faith even in the days in which **Antipas was My
faithful martyr**, who was killed among you."*

One name preserved, of a man about whom nothing else is known.

## What was wrong

*"You have there those who hold the doctrine of **Balaam**… to eat things sacrificed to idols, and to
commit sexual immorality."*

*"Repent, or else I will come to you quickly and **will fight against them with the sword of My
mouth**."*

## The call

*If you can hear at all, **listen to what the Spirit is telling the churches**.*

The Greek is **ho echon ous akousato** — he who has an ear, let him hear.

**Ear**, singular. In the Gospels the same formula is usually plural: *he who has ears to hear*.

The seven letters all end with this line.

## The first promise

*"Whoever wins, **I will feed him the manna that has been kept hidden**."*

The Greek is **tou manna tou kekrummenou** — the hidden manna.

## What that refers to

The jar of manna Aaron laid up before the Testimony — one omer of the food that would not keep,
kept permanently. See the note on Exodus 16:19–21.

Jewish tradition held that Jeremiah hid the ark before the exile and that it would reappear.

Offered to a church whose members were being pressed to eat at idol feasts.

## The second

*"And I will hand him **a white stone**."*

The Greek is **psephon leuken**.

**Psephos** — a small stone or pebble used for voting.

## What white stones did

A juror's white pebble acquitted; a black one condemned.

Victors at the games were given a token admitting them to the celebration.

Both meanings were current, and the promise is to a persecuted congregation in a city with courts
and games.

## The third

*"With **a new name cut into it**."*

The Greek is **onoma kainon gegrammenon** — a new name written.

## Whose name

The Greek is ambiguous. It can be His new name, or the recipient's.

## The clause

*"A name **nobody knows except the one who takes it**."*

The Greek is **ho oudeis oiden ei me ho lambanon**.

Private. Not announced, not shared, not usable by anyone else.

## What is unusual about it

Everything else in Revelation is public — the throne, the multitude nobody can number, the city
with its measurements.

This is one line of it that is not.

## Where new names appear elsewhere

*"He who overcomes… I will write on him the name of My God, and the name of the city of My God…
**and My new name**."* See the note on Revelation 3:12.

And the renamings: Abram to Abraham, Jacob to Israel, Simon to Peter.

## What the whole letter turns on

*"To him who overcomes."*

The Greek is **to nikonti** — to the conquering one.

The same phrase closes all seven letters, and every promise attached to it is different.

> **The last clause.** A name nobody knows except the person who receives it — one private thing in
> a book where everything else is on display.
