The second friend, and he stands on something older than himself.

## Where his authority comes from

*"**Just ask the generations who came before**; consider what their ancestors
learned."*

That is his case. Not observation, like Eliphaz — inheritance. What has always
been taught.

*"We were born only yesterday and know nothing."* He disqualifies the present in
favour of the past, and then speaks with total confidence about a man in front of
him.

Tradition does not mean truth. Something can be handed down for four hundred years
and still be the wrong word for the man in the ashes.

## The papyrus

*"**Can papyrus grow tall where there is no marsh?** Can reeds flourish without
water?"*

A proverb, and a good one. Cut the reed off from the water and it withers faster
than grass that was never green.

*"That is the way of everyone who forgets God."*

He has just called Job a man who forgot God, in a metaphor, without saying his
name.

## The spider's web

*"What he leans on is fragile; what he trusts is **a spider's web**. He leans on
his house, and it will not hold. He grabs it, and it will not last."*

The house that will not hold. Job's ten children died when a house came down on
them.

Bildad had opened his speech in this chapter with *if your children have sinned
against Him, He has cast them away for their transgression.* He is not being
careless. He means it.

## The uprooted plant

*"He is like a well-watered plant in the sunlight, spreading its shoots… gripping
the rocks. But **tear it from its place**, and that place disowns it: 'I never saw
you.'"*

The most accurate description of Job's situation in the chapter, and it is
delivered as an accusation.

The greatest man in the East, spread out over the hills, torn out. Bildad has the
picture exactly right and the diagnosis exactly wrong.

## What the traditional wisdom actually says

Bildad's inherited system is simple: God blesses the righteous and destroys the
wicked, visibly, in this life, every time.

It is neat, and much of the Old Testament reads that way at a distance. The book
of Job exists because it is not the whole story, and because a man was sitting in
ashes for whom it was not true at all.

> **The verdict on him.** *You have not spoken of Me what is right.* He quoted the
> fathers, he used the proverbs correctly, and God's summary of his theology was
> that it misrepresented Him. See the notes on Job 4:7–11 and Job 42:7–8.
