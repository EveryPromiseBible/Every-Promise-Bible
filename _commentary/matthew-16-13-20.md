Jesus asks two questions in a place chosen for the asking.

## Where He asks it

*Caesarea Philippi* — as far north as He ever took them, at the foot of Mount
Hermon. The town sat beside a cave sacred to the Greek god Pan, with a temple to
Caesar Augustus built nearby.

Surrounded by the shrines of other lords and other sons of gods, He asks who they
say He is.

## The two questions

*Who do people say the Son of Man is?* Then: *But **you** — who do you say I am?*

The first is a survey and they answer it easily. The second is emphatic in Greek
— *you*, plural, as against everybody else.

The reported opinions are all complimentary: John the Baptist, Elijah, Jeremiah,
one of the prophets. Every one of them is a category He does not fit.

## Peter's answer

*You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God.*

Both halves matter. Messiah is the office; Son of the living God is the identity.

## Where it came from

*No human being revealed this to you. My Father in heaven showed it to you.*

Jesus does not congratulate Peter on his insight. He identifies the source, and
it is not Peter.

## The rock

*You are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church.*

The wordplay is deliberate — *Petros*, a stone, and *petra*, rock. Christians
have argued for centuries over whether the rock is Peter himself or the
confession he has just made.

What is not in dispute is the builder and the outcome. *I* will build, and *the
gates of the realm of the dead will not hold out against it.* Gates are
defensive. The picture is of the church advancing on death's stronghold, not
enduring a siege.

> **What happens four verses later.** The same man, who has just been told the
> Father revealed this to him, is called Satan for objecting to the cross.
