A man panics, and his wife answers him with an argument.

## The realisation

*The angel did not appear to them again. **Only then did Manoah understand** — this had been the
angel of the LORD.*

He had asked for the name and been told it was *wonderful*. He worked it out afterwards.

## The panic

*"**We are going to die**," he said to his wife, terrified. "**We have seen God!**"*

The Hebrew is **mot namut ki Elohim ra'inu** — dying we shall die, for we have seen God.

## Why he thinks so

*"You cannot see My face; **for no man shall see Me, and live**."*

Said to Moses. Manoah is not inventing it.

Gideon reacted the same way: *alas, O Lord GOD! For I have seen the Angel of the LORD face to
face*, and had to be told *peace be with you; do not fear, you shall not die*.

## The wife

*But his wife, **steady and clear-eyed**, answered him.*

She is unnamed in the whole chapter, and she is the one the angel came to, twice.

## The argument

*"**If the LORD wanted to kill us, He would not have accepted our offering from our hands, or shown
us all this, or told us these things now**."*

Three clauses, in ascending order.

## The first

He accepted the offering.

They watched Him go up in the flame of it. A rejected sacrifice does not do that. See the note on
Judges 13:19–20.

## The second

He showed them all this.

An extended appearance, twice, with a conversation.

## The third

He told them these things *now*.

The announcement was about a son who has not been conceived yet. A God intending to kill them
would not have opened with a birth announcement.

## What kind of reasoning this is

Evidential.

She does not tell him to feel better. She takes the facts of the last hour and shows him they
cannot support his conclusion.

Every one of her three points is something he saw himself.

## The word for it

The Illumination adds: *her faith calmed them both*.

The Hebrew simply ends the speech. The narrator gives her the last word and moves on.

## What Paul argues the same way

*He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all, **how shall He not with Him also
freely give us all things?***

The Greek is **pos ouchi** — how shall He not.

The same structure: a completed act, and what it rules out. See the note on
Romans 8:31–34.

## What follows immediately

*So the woman bore a son and **called his name Samson**.*

The Hebrew **Shimshon** may derive from **shemesh**, sun.

*And the child grew, and the LORD blessed him. **And the Spirit of the LORD began to move upon him**
at Mahaneh Dan.*

The Hebrew is **lepha'amo** — to stir him, to impel him.

## What the last chapter of his life looks like

Blind, in a Philistine temple, praying *remember me, I pray! Strengthen me, I pray, just this
once*. See the note on Judges 16:28–30.

His mother's argument stood; his own life did not.

> **The argument.** Three facts from the last hour, each one something her husband saw himself,
> and none of them consistent with the conclusion he had just reached.
