A psalm about a person, and it is deliberately built to echo the one before it,
which is about God.

## The pair

Psalm 111 describes the LORD: His work is honour and majesty, His righteousness
endures forever.

Psalm 112 describes the person who fears Him — and uses the same phrases. *Their
goodness stands forever* is word for word what the previous psalm said about God.

The two are twin acrostics in Hebrew, each running through the alphabet. Read
together, the second is a portrait of someone taking after the first.

## Where it begins

*Happy is the one who holds the LORD in awe, who delights, deep down, in what He
asks.*

Not *obeys* first. **Delights** — the Hebrew is about taking pleasure in
something, wanting it. The commands are described as a pleasure before they are
described as a duty.

## What follows

*Their children will be strong across the land… Their house holds plenty and
wealth, and their goodness stands forever.*

Three generations of effect: the person, the children, and something that outlasts
both.

The last clause is the one that does not fit the pattern of the other two. Plenty
and wealth are in the house; the righteousness stands *forever* — it is the only
item on the list that time cannot reach.

> **Read on for the harder verses.** The psalm goes on: *even in darkness light
> dawns for the upright*, and *they will have no fear of bad news; their heart is
> steady, trusting in the LORD.* It does not describe a life without darkness or
> bad news. It describes someone who has light in the first and a steady heart in
> the second.
