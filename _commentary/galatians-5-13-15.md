---
key: Galatians 5:13–15
title: "Freedom, and what the flesh wanted to do with it"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul has spent four chapters demolishing the law as a way of standing before God. Now he answers the
question everybody asks next.

## A bridgehead

*Only do not turn that freedom into **an opening for the flesh**.*

**Aphorme** is a military word: a base of operations, the beachhead an army lands on before the
campaign begins.

Paul does not say freedom becomes flesh. He says the flesh will use freedom as a starting point if
it is handed one. The danger is real and he names it — and then look at what he does *not* do about
it. He does not withdraw the freedom. He does not reintroduce a fence. He has just spent the whole
letter saying that fences never produced holiness in anybody.

## What he prescribes instead

*Instead, **through love, make yourselves servants** to one another.*

**Douleuete allelois** — be slaves to one another. Paul answers the abuse of freedom with a deeper
use of it. Freedom is not cancelled here; it is spent.

Nobody can be enslaved to another by command. Only a free man can hand himself over, which is
exactly what the Lord of glory did with a towel and a basin on the last night.

## The law, already full

*For the whole law is **brought to its fullness** in one sentence.*

**Peplerotai** — perfect passive. Has been filled up and stands filled. Paul does not say *you must
fulfil the law by loving*; he says the whole thing has been brought to fullness in one sentence,
and the man who said *I did not come to destroy but to fulfil* is the one who did it.

Love is not the law returning by the back door. It is what the law was always describing, produced
now by the Spirit as fruit rather than demanded as a quota.

## Biting and devouring

*But if you keep **biting** at one another and **tearing** one another apart, watch out, or you will
be **finished off** by one another.*

Three animal words in a row: **daknete**, **katesthiete**, **analothete** — bite, eat down, be
consumed. The last one is used of fire.

This is a description of a church that has gone back under law. People measuring each other by a
standard nobody meets have to keep the standard high and their neighbour low, and it ends with a
congregation eating itself.

> **The two things freedom can be spent on.** It is the same freedom either way — that is what makes
> it freedom. The flesh wants it as a landing site; love wants it as something to give away. And
> the second is not the harder of the two once you know how you got free: *Christ has made us free*,
> and love is simply what that looks like moving outward.
