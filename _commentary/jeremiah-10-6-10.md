Set immediately against the scarecrow, and every line is a contrast with something that has to be
nailed upright.

## The opening

*There is **no one like You, LORD**.*

**Me'eyn kamokha** — from nothing like You. The phrase is the psalms' refrain and Jeremiah puts it
at the head of the paragraph.

## The question

*"**Who would not fear You, King of the nations?** This is Your due."*

The Hebrew is **ki lekha ya'atah** — for to You it belongs, it is fitting.

*And they sing **the song of Moses**, the servant of God, and **the song of the Lamb**, saying:
"Great and marvellous are Your works, Lord God Almighty!… **Who shall not fear You, O Lord, and
glorify Your name? For You alone are holy. For all nations shall come and worship before You**."*

**Revelation** 15 quotes Jeremiah's question, unaltered, in the mouths of people standing on a sea
of glass — and answers the second half of it. Jeremiah asks who would not fear the King of the
nations; **John** hears that the nations came.

## The manufacture, again

*Beaten silver from Tarshish, gold from Uphaz, **the work of the craftsman and the goldsmith's
hands**. They dress them in blue and purple, **all of it the work of skilled workers**.*

Twice in two lines: the work of hands. Jeremiah keeps saying who made whom.

*"The God who made the world and everything in it, since He is Lord of heaven and earth, **does
not dwell in temples made with hands. Nor is He worshipped with men's hands, as though He needed
anything**."*

**Paul** on Mars Hill, in a city full of the finest examples of exactly this craftsmanship, making
Jeremiah's argument to people who had never read him.

## The three titles

*But the LORD is **the true God**. He is **the living God**, **the everlasting King**.*

**Elohim emet. Elohim chayyim. Melekh olam.**

*For they themselves declare concerning us… how **you turned to God from idols to serve the living
and true God**.*

**Paul** to the **Thessalonians**, and he uses two of Jeremiah's three, in order, to describe what
conversion was.

*And we know that the Son of God has come and has given us an understanding, that we may know Him
who is true; and we are in Him who is true, in His Son **Jesus Christ**. **This is the true God and
eternal life.***

**John** ends his first letter with the third clause and puts a name in it — and the sentence
after it is *little children, keep yourselves from idols.* He has been reading Jeremiah 10.

## The living one

*He is **the living God**.*

*"I am He who lives, and was dead, and behold, **I am alive forevermore**."*

*"Why do you seek **the living among the dead**? He is not here, but is risen."*

An idol has to be carried and cannot walk. **Luke** 24 has two men told to stop looking for this
one in a grave.

## The wrath

*When He is angry, **the earth trembles**. The nations cannot endure His wrath.*

*Much more then, having now been justified by His blood, **we shall be saved from wrath through
Him**.*

*For God did not appoint us to wrath, but **to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ**.*

Jeremiah says the nations cannot endure it and stops there. **Paul** says the same and then names
where it went.

> **The question John heard answered.** *Who would not fear You, King of the nations?* Revelation
> 15 puts Jeremiah's line into a song — and adds the answer he could not have written: *all
> nations shall come and worship before You.*
