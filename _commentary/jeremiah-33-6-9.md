A promise of restoration made to a city under siege, by a prophet in prison.

## Where Jeremiah was

The chapter opens by noting he was *still confined in the courtyard of the
guard*. Babylonian siege ramps were against the walls. The city fell shortly
after.

Everything promised here is spoken into that.

## The list

*Health and healing… abundant peace and security… bring them back from exile…
rebuild them as they were before… cleanse them of every sin… forgive all their
sins of rebellion.*

Six undertakings, and the last two are about guilt rather than circumstances. The
rebuilding of walls and the cleansing of sin are in the same sentence.

The Hebrew for *abundant peace* is a phrase about an overflow of shalom and
truth — wholeness, and reliability with it.

## What the nations do

*Then this city will bring Me joy and honour and praise before all the nations of
the earth, when they hear of all the good I do for it.*

The restoration is public. The audience is everybody else, and what they hear
about is not Judah's recovery but *the good I do*.

## The line worth stopping on

*They will tremble in awe at all the good and all the peace I pour out on it.*

Trembling is normally the response to judgement. Here the nations shake at
**goodness**.

That is an unusual sentence, and Jeremiah means it: the sheer scale of the mercy
is what unsettles the onlookers.

> **What is promised three verses later.** *In this place, of which you say, "It
> is a waste"… there will be heard again the voice of joy and the voice of
> gladness, the voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride.* Jeremiah
> quotes the city's own despairing description back and reverses it.
