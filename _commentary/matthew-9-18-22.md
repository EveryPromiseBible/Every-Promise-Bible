Matthew tells both stories in five verses. Mark takes twenty-three.

## The interruption

*While He was still speaking.*

He is answering a question about fasting. The ruler comes in mid-sentence.

## The father

*One of **the leading men of the town** came and **knelt in front of Him**.*

Mark and Luke name him: Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue.

A synagogue official kneeling in public to a travelling teacher is a considerable thing.

## What he says

*"**My daughter has just died. But come and lay Your hand on her, and she will live**."*

Matthew has her already dead. Mark and Luke have her dying, with the news of the death
arriving during the delay.

Matthew compresses — he does this throughout, and it is why his miracle accounts are half
the length.

## The request

*Come, lay Your hand on her.* One gesture, and a statement about what will follow.

## The journey

*So Jesus **stood up and went with him**.*

No question asked and no condition set.

## The interruption inside the interruption

*And **on the way**.*

The second story happens inside the first. Both Matthew and Mark keep the structure, and
it is the point: a man whose daughter is dead has to stand in the street while somebody
else is dealt with.

## The woman

*A woman who had been **bleeding for twelve years**.*

The girl is twelve years old — Mark records it. For as long as one family has had a
daughter, this woman has been bleeding.

Twelve years of ceremonial uncleanness under Leviticus 15: unable to touch or be touched,
excluded from the temple.

## What she touched

*Came up **behind Him** and touched **the edge of His cloak**.*

The Greek is **kraspedon** — the tassel on the corner of the garment, the **tzitzit**
commanded in Numbers.

She reaches for the lowest, furthest part of him, from behind.

## What she kept saying

*Because she **kept saying to herself**, "If I can only touch His clothes, **I will be
well**."*

The Greek is imperfect — she was saying it, repeatedly, while pushing through.

**I will be well** is **sothesomai** — saved, made whole.

## The turn

*Jesus **turned and saw her**.*

She had planned to take it and leave. He will not let her.

## What He says

*"**Take heart, daughter. Your faith has made you well**."*

**Daughter** — the only time in the Gospels He calls anyone that.

Two verses earlier He had called a paralysed man **son**. See the note on
Matthew 9:1–8.

**Take heart** — **tharsei**, the same word.

He credits her faith, not the tassel.

## The timing

*And **from that moment the woman was well**.*

Matthew says it happened when He spoke. Mark says she felt it when she touched. See the
note on Mark 5:25–29.

## What happens next

He arrives at the house, tells the flute players and the wailing crowd that the girl is
not dead but sleeping, and they laugh at Him.

*But when the crowd was put outside, He went in and **took her by the hand, and the girl
arose**.*

> **What the delay cost.** A man with a dead daughter waited in a street while a woman who
> had planned to slip away unnoticed was found, spoken to, and given a name.
