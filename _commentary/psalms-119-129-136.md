The **Pe** stanza — eight lines, every one beginning with the same Hebrew letter,
and the middle of it says what happens when the words are opened.

## The unfolding

*The **opening** of Your words lets in the light; it gives plain folk the sense
they never had.*

The Hebrew is a noun built from the verb *to open* — an entrance, an unfolding,
a doorway. The word itself carries the picture: something shut that is opened,
and light comes through the gap.

## Who it is for

*It gives **plain folk** the sense they never had.*

The Hebrew **pethi** is the simple one — not the fool, and not the scoffer, but
the untaught, the person with no advantage.

That is the one specified as the beneficiary. Not the learned. The opening of the
words is what levels the ground.

## The panting

*I open wide my mouth and pant for breath, so hungry, longing for Your good
commands.*

It is an undignified image and the psalmist chose it. The Hebrew is the gasping
of someone who has been running, or of an animal in heat and thirst.

## The requests in between

- *steady my footsteps by the word You gave*
- *let no wrong thing hold mastery over me*
- *rescue me from the men who crush and cheat*
- *let Your bright face shine warm upon Your servant*

**Let Your face shine** is the Aaronic blessing, quoted back to God by a man
asking for what was already promised over him.

## How the stanza ends

*Rivers of tears go running from my eyes, because the people will not keep Your
teaching.*

The Hebrew is *channels of water* — the runnels that carry a stream. Not for
himself. For everybody else.

> **The stanza's shape.** It opens with the words being wonderful and closes with
> weeping over people who ignore them. The distance between those two lines is the
> whole burden of the psalm.
