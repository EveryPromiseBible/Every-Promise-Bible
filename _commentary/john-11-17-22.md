He arrives four days late, and the first thing anyone says to Him is a complaint with faith
in it.

## The delay

*When Jesus arrived, He found that Lazarus had **already been in the tomb four days**.*

John has already explained why. He was told Lazarus was ill, and *He stayed two more days in
the place where He was*. See the note on John 11:1–6.

The verse before that is the one that makes the delay strange: *now Jesus loved Martha and
her sister and Lazarus*. John puts *therefore* between the love and the staying.

## Why four matters

There was a rabbinic belief that the soul hovered near the body for three days and departed
on the fourth, after which the face was no longer recognisable.

By day four, in that culture, nobody expected anything.

## The distance

*Bethany was close to Jerusalem, only about **fifteen stadia** away.*

Under two miles. Just over the Mount of Olives.

Which means the mourners are largely from Jerusalem, and the news of what happens here
reaches the authorities the same day. It does: *then many of the Jews… believed in Him. But
some of them went away to the Pharisees and told them the things Jesus did.*

The raising of Lazarus is what triggers the decision to kill Him.

## The crowd

*Many of the people had **come out to Martha and Mary to comfort them**.*

A full house of mourners, professional and otherwise.

## The two sisters

*As soon as Martha heard that Jesus was coming, **she went out to meet Him**, while **Mary
stayed sitting in the house**.*

Exactly in character. Luke has Martha *distracted with much serving* while Mary *sat at
Jesus' feet and heard His word*.

Here Martha gets up and goes, and Mary stays sitting.

## What Martha says

*"**Lord, if You had been here, my brother would not have died**."*

Mary says the identical sentence a few verses later, word for word. They have clearly been
saying it to each other for four days.

It is a reproach and it is a statement of confidence. Both at once.

## The second half

*"But **even now I know that whatever You ask of God, God will give You**."*

The Greek is *whatever You ask** — **aiteses**, the verb for a request from an inferior to a
superior.

She is not asking for anything specific. She says something like: it is not over, and I do
not know what I am saying.

## What He tells her

*"**Your brother will rise again**."*

And she takes it as doctrine: *I know that he will rise again in the resurrection at the last
day*.

Then the sentence that corrects her tense: *I am the resurrection and the life*. See the note
on John 11:23–27.

## What she confesses

*"Yes, Lord, I believe that You are the Christ, the Son of God, who is to come into the
world."*

The same confession Peter makes at Caesarea Philippi, made at a graveside by a woman who has
just been reproaching Him.

## What she says later

At the stone: *Lord, by now there will be a smell*. See the note on John 11:38–40.

The confession in verse 27 and the objection in verse 39 come from the same person, twelve
verses apart.

## What John said the miracle was for

*"This sickness is not unto death, but **for the glory of God**, that the Son of God may be
glorified through it."*

And to the disciples, before setting out: *Lazarus is dead. **And I am glad for your sakes
that I was not there**, that you may believe.*

> **What she says in one breath.** *If You had been here* — and then *even now*. A complaint
> and a confidence, four days after the funeral.
