A Persian king is addressed by name a century and a half before he is born.

## The title

*This is what the LORD says to **Cyrus, His anointed one**.*

The Hebrew is **limshicho** — to His messiah.

It is the word used of Israel's kings, of the high priest, and of the coming deliverer.

Here it is applied to a Gentile emperor who worshipped Marduk.

## Who Cyrus was

The founder of the Persian Empire. He took Babylon in 539 BC, and issued the decree that let the
exiles go home and rebuild the temple.

*Thus says Cyrus king of Persia: "The LORD God of heaven has given me all the kingdoms of the
earth. And He has commanded me to build Him a house at Jerusalem."* — the last verse of
Chronicles, and the first of Ezra.

## The grip

*The man **He has taken by the right hand**.*

The Hebrew is **hechezaqti vimino** — I have grasped his right hand.

In Babylonian ritual, the king took the hand of the god Marduk at the new year festival to be
confirmed in office.

Isaiah reverses it. The God of Israel takes the Persian's hand.

## The clearing

*"**I will walk ahead of you and level the mountains**."*

*"I will **break down the bronze gates**. I will **shatter the iron bars**."*

Herodotus records that Babylon's river gates were left open on the night the city fell.

## The treasure

*"I will hand you **the treasures hidden in the dark, the riches tucked away in secret places**."*

Babylon's accumulated wealth, and the vessels Nebuchadnezzar had carried out of the temple —
which Cyrus returned, itemised, in Ezra 1.

## The purpose

*"Then **you will know it was I, the LORD, the God of Israel, who called you by name**."*

## The reason

*"**I did this for the sake of My servant Jacob, for Israel, My chosen**."*

The whole Persian conquest is stated as being for the benefit of a small displaced people.

## The last clause

*"I called you by name and gave you a title of honor, **though you did not know Me**."*

The Hebrew is **velo yeda'tani**.

Repeated in the next verse: *I will gird you, though you have not known Me*.

## What that says

The commissioning does not depend on the man's awareness of it.

Cyrus is named, grasped by the hand, given the treasure, and called God's anointed — and the text
says twice that he does not know who is doing it.

## When this was written

Isaiah is prophesying to a nation that has not yet gone into exile, about a king who will let them
out of one, by name.

The chapter before ends: *who says of Cyrus, "He is My shepherd, and he shall perform all My
pleasure"*.

## What follows

*"**I am the LORD, and there is no other**; there is no God besides Me. I will gird you, though you
have not known Me."*

*"**I form the light and create darkness, I make peace and create calamity**; I, the LORD, do all
these things."*

The most sweeping claim of sole agency in the Old Testament, and it comes in a paragraph about a
foreign king.

## What Paul does with the same logic

*For **there is no authority except from God**, and the authorities that exist are appointed by
God.*

*He is God's minister to you for good.* See the note on Romans 13:1–5.

The word **diakonos** applied to a pagan magistrate who does not know whose he is.

> **The clause repeated twice.** *Though you did not know Me.* The naming, the grip on the hand,
> and the title are all stated as independent of the man's awareness of them.
