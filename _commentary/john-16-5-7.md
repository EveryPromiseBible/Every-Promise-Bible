He tells eleven grieving men that His leaving is to their advantage.

## The departure

*"But now **I am going to the One who sent Me**."*

The Greek is **hupago pros ton pempsanta me**.

He has been saying it all evening: *where I am going you cannot come*, and *I go to prepare a place
for you*.

## The complaint

*"And **not one of you asks Me, 'Where are You going?'**"*

Which is odd, because Peter had asked exactly that — *Lord, where are You going?* — two chapters
earlier, and Thomas had said *we do not know where You are going*.

## What He means

They asked, and then stopped.

The questions have given way to grief, and nobody is asking about the destination any more.

## The diagnosis

*"Instead, because I have said these things to you, **sorrow has filled your hearts**."*

The Greek is **he lupe pepleroken humon ten kardian** — grief has filled your heart.

**Pleroo** — to fill completely. There is no room left for the question.

## The assurance

*"Still, **I am telling you the truth**."*

The Greek is **ten aletheian lego humin**.

He says it because what follows will not sound true.

## The claim

*"**It is better for you that I go away**."*

The Greek is **sumpherei humin hina ego apeltho** — it is advantageous for you.

**Sumphero** — to bring together, to be profitable.

## Why it is startling

Three years of walking with Him, and He tells them His removal is the better arrangement.

## The reason

*"**If I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you; but if I go, I will send Him to you**."*

The Greek is **ho parakletos** — the one called alongside.

## The exclusivity

The two are stated as alternatives. Not both.

The incarnation put Him in one place at a time. The Spirit is not limited that way, and John
elsewhere notes *the Holy Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*.

## What the Helper does

*"And when He comes, **He will show the world how wrong it has been about sin, and about
righteousness, and about judgment**."* See the note on John 16:8–11.

The Greek is **elenxei** — will convict, will expose.

## The three

Sin, because they do not believe.

Righteousness, because He goes to the Father.

Judgment, because the ruler of this world stands condemned.

## What He says next

*"**I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now**."*

*"However, when He, the Spirit of truth, has come, **He will guide you into all truth**."* See the
note on John 16:12–15.

## The last thing in the discourse

*"**In the world you will have tribulation; but be of good cheer, I have overcome the world**."*

The Greek is **tetharsete** — take courage. And **nenikeka** — I have conquered. Perfect tense,
said the night before the crucifixion.

## What happens to the grief

*"Therefore you now have sorrow; but **I will see you again and your heart will rejoice, and your joy
no one will take from you**."*

The Greek is **ouk arei aph' humon** — no one takes it away from you.

> **The claim.** *It is better for you that I go.* Said to eleven men whose grief has filled the
> room, about the arrangement that replaces having Him in one place at a time.
