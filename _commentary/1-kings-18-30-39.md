The contest on Carmel, and every detail Elijah arranges is aimed at leaving no
other explanation.

## The twelve stones

*He took twelve stones, one for each of the tribes descended from Jacob — the man
to whom the word of the LORD had come, "Israel shall be your name."*

The northern kingdom had been split from Judah for generations. Elijah rebuilds
the altar as though the nation were whole, and the writer stops the narrative to
mention the renaming at the Jabbok.

He is not addressing ten tribes. He is addressing Israel.

## The water

Four jars, poured over the offering and the wood. Then *do it again*. Then *a
third time*.

Twelve jars in total, in the middle of a drought, on a mountain — water that must
have been carried up. It fills the trench.

Every drop is there to remove an excuse. No hidden coals, no dry tinder, no
sleight of hand.

## The prayer

After the prophets of Baal had spent a day shouting, dancing and cutting
themselves, Elijah prays about sixty words:

> *Let it be known today that You are God in Israel, that I am Your servant, and
> that I have done all these things at Your word. Answer me, LORD, answer me, so
> these people will know that You, O LORD, are God — and that You are turning
> their hearts back to You.*

Three requests, and all three are about **them**: that they would know who God
is, and that their hearts are being turned back. Nothing is asked for the
prophet.

## The hour

*And it came to pass, **at the time of the offering of the evening sacrifice**, that
Elijah the prophet came near.*

Three in the afternoon — the hour the lamb was killed in Jerusalem, in a kingdom that
had not sent anyone to Jerusalem in generations.

The prophets of Baal had been shouting since morning and finished at exactly that hour.
See the note on 1 Kings 18:25–29.

## The altar he repaired

*He repaired **the altar of the LORD that was broken down**.*

There had been one on that mountain. Somebody had pulled it down, and Elijah does not
build a new one — he puts the old one back.

## The answer

*Then the fire of the LORD fell. It consumed the offering, the wood, the stones,
the very dust — and licked up all the water in the trench.*

Not just the sacrifice. The stones and the dust and the water — the fire takes
everything, including the things fire does not normally take.

> **What the people say.** *The LORD — He is God! The LORD — He is God!* In Hebrew
> that is *YHWH hu ha-Elohim*, twice. And it is the meaning of the prophet's own
> name — Eliyahu, *my God is the LORD*. The nation ends the day shouting his
> name back at him without knowing it.
