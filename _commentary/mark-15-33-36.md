Three hours of dark, and one sentence out of it.

## The hour

*At **the sixth hour, at midday**.*

Noon. The sun at its highest point in the sky.

Mark has been keeping time all through the chapter — the third hour when they crucified
Him, the sixth, the ninth. He builds the day out of clock readings.

## The darkness

*Darkness came over **the whole land**, and it **stayed until the ninth hour**.*

Three hours. Noon to three.

Not an eclipse; Passover falls at full moon, when a solar eclipse is impossible.

## When the lambs were killed

Between the ninth hour and evening, at the temple, thousands of Passover lambs were being
slaughtered — a few hundred yards from the hill.

## What darkness had meant before

The ninth plague in Egypt: *darkness which may even be felt*, three days of it, over
Egypt only — and *all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings*. The plague
immediately before the Passover.

And Amos, on the day of the LORD: *I will make the sun go down at noon, and I will darken
the earth in broad daylight. I will turn your feasts into mourning.*

## The cry

*And at the ninth hour Jesus **cried out with a loud voice**.*

The Greek is **eboesen phone megale** — the shout of a strong man. Mark has recorded almost
nothing from the blood covenant before this.

## The words

*"**Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani?**"*

Aramaic, and Mark keeps the sound before he translates it. He does this a handful of times —
*Talitha koum*, *Ephphatha*, *Abba*.

## The translation

*"**My God, My God, why have You abandoned Me?**"*

The Greek is **egkatelipes** — left behind, forsaken, abandoned.

It is the opening line of Psalm 22, and the psalm goes on: *they pierced My hands and My
feet*, *they divide My garments among them, and for My clothing they cast lots*. See the
note on Psalms 22:16–18.

Both of those had happened by the time He said the first line.

## Where the psalm ends

*You have answered Me… I will declare Your name to My brethren.*

And the last words of it in Hebrew: *ki asah* — *He has done it*.

## The misunderstanding

*Some of the men standing there heard it and said, "**He is calling for Elijah**."*

*Eloi* and *Eliyahu*. Close enough at a distance, in a crowd, from a man who had been
scourged.

Elijah was expected to come before the day of the LORD — Malachi's last sentence.

## The sponge

*One of them ran and **soaked a sponge in sour wine**, put it on a stick, and lifted it up
to His mouth.*

**Oxos** — the cheap sour wine soldiers drank.

Earlier they had offered wine mixed with myrrh, a painkiller, and He refused it. This one He
takes.

## The waiting

*"**Leave Him**," he said. "**Let us see whether Elijah is coming to take Him down**."*

The last recorded human speech before He dies is a man proposing to watch.

## What happens next

*And Jesus cried out with a loud voice, and breathed His last. Then **the veil of the temple
was torn in two from top to bottom**.*

And the centurion — the man who ran the execution — *truly this Man was the Son of God*.

Mark's Gospel opens with *the beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God*, and
the first human being in it to say so is the officer in charge of killing Him.

> **What He asked.** Not for it to stop. *Why* — and He asked it in Aramaic, in the first
> line of a psalm that ends with the words *He has done it*.
