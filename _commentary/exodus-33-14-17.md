---
key: Exodus 33:14–17
title: My Presence will go with you
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The answer to the golden calf is not a warning, a probation or a set of conditions. It is a
promise about company.

## Where this sits

After the calf. After three thousand dead, after the broken tablets, and after God had said
*I will send My angel before you* — an escort instead of Himself, which is what Moses had spent
the whole previous conversation refusing. See the note on Exodus 32:30–35.

## The answer

*"**My Presence will go with you, and I will give you rest**."*

The Hebrew is **panai yelechu** — My face will go.

Not an angel, not a representative, and no probationary terms. The thing that had just been
withdrawn is given back, and something is added to it.

## The four words on the end

*"**And I will give you rest.**"*

The Hebrew is **vahanichoti lach** — and I will cause you to rest.

It is the root of **Noah**, and of the ark coming to rest on the mountains. See the note on
Genesis 8:1–5.

*"Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and **I will give you rest**."*

Said by somebody standing in Galilee, in the same two halves: come, and I will give.

## What Moses says back

*"**If your Presence does not go with us, do not send us up from here.**"*

He would rather stay in a desert with God than go to a good land without Him. The land had been
promised, guaranteed, and sworn to three generations, and Moses says he does not want it on
those terms.

## The reason he gives

*"How else will anyone know that you are pleased with me and with your people — **unless you go
with us? That is what sets us apart**, me and your people, from every other people on the face
of the earth."*

Notice what he does not say.

Not our law, our morality, our conduct, our temple, or our record — the record at this exact
point in the book is a golden calf. The only distinguishing mark he can name is that God is
with them.

## The reply

*"**I will do the very thing you have asked, because I am pleased with you, and I know you by
name.**"*

Three clauses, and every one of them is about God's disposition rather than Moses' performance.

**I am pleased with you** — the Hebrew is *you have found favour in My eyes*. **Chen.** The
same word that was said over Noah before a single thing was recorded about his character. See
the note on Genesis 6:5–8.

## What Moses asks next

*"Please, show me Your glory."*

And what he is shown is a name read out loud: *the LORD, the LORD God, **merciful and gracious,
longsuffering, and abounding in goodness and truth**, keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving
iniquity and transgression and sin.* See the note on Exodus 34:5–7.

He asked to see glory and was given a character reference, and it is almost entirely about
mercy.

## The promise, restated

*Let your conduct be without covetousness; be content with such things as you have. For He
Himself has said, "**I will never leave you nor forsake you.**"*

The Greek stacks the negatives so heavily it is nearly untranslatable — never, no not ever,
will I leave off holding you.

> **What was asked and what was given.** Moses asked God not to send them without Him. The
> answer added something he had not asked for: *and I will give you rest.*
