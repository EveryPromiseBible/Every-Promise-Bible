The summary at the end of the campaign, and it contains a detail the writer did
not have to include.

## The claim

*Joshua took the entire land, exactly as the LORD had promised Moses… And the
land had rest from war.*

That is as complete as the book gets. The giants are cut off from the hill
country — Hebron, Debir, Anab, the highlands — and *not one of the Anakim was
left in the land of Israel*.

## The exception, stated in the same breath

*Only in Gaza, Gath, and Ashdod did any remain.*

Three Philistine cities on the coastal plain. The writer records the total
victory and the surviving pocket in consecutive sentences, without appearing to
notice a problem.

It matters later. Gath is where Goliath comes from, four centuries on — a
descendant of the people this chapter says were cleared out, living in one of the
three cities this chapter says they were not.

## What kind of claim it is

The Bible's histories are willing to say *the whole land* and then name what was
left, and to do it on the same page. The summary is describing the campaign's
outcome — the field armies broken, the hill country taken — not a census.

Two chapters later God says outright: *there is still so much land left to
possess*. The book contains its own qualification.

## What the book keeps landing on

*The land had rest from war.*

Twice in Joshua — here, and at the end of Caleb's chapter. It is not conquest the narrative
treats as the goal. It is rest.

*There remains therefore **a rest for the people of God**. For he who has entered His rest has
himself also **ceased from his works**, as God did from His.*

And the reason Hebrews can say *there remains* one is that Joshua's was not the last word:
*for if Joshua had given them rest, then He would not afterward have spoken of another day.*

## The three cities left standing

*Only in Gaza, in Gath, and in Ashdod some remained.*

Three coastal towns, named in the same breath as the claim that none were left. Goliath of
Gath is a descendant of them, and David is four hundred years away.

What is left unfinished in Joshua is what Judges is about.

> **The phrase that closes it.** *The land had rest from war* appears twice in
> Joshua, here and at the end of Caleb's chapter. It is the note the conquest
> narrative keeps landing on — not conquest as the goal, but rest as the point of
> it.
