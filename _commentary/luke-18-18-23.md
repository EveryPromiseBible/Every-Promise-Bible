A man with everything asks what he has to do, and the answer takes everything.

## The question

*"Good teacher, **what do I have to do to inherit eternal life?**"*

An inheritance is the one thing in the world nobody does anything to get. It comes by
birth, by relationship, by somebody's death.

He asks what to **do** to get one.

## The word Jesus picks up

*"**Why do you call Me good?** No one is good except God alone."*

He does not deny it. He asks the man whether he has thought about what he just said.

The man used *good* as a compliment between equals. Jesus takes it as a category with
one occupant.

## The commandments listed

*"Do not commit adultery, do not murder, do not steal, do not tell lies about people,
honor your father and your mother."*

Five, and all five are from the second table — the ones about other people.

The first table, the commandments about God, is not read out.

## The answer

*"**I have kept every one of those**," the man said, "ever since I was a boy."*

He says it without hesitation and Jesus does not contradict him. Mark's account adds
the line Luke leaves out: *then Jesus, looking at him, loved him.*

## "One thing"

*"There is **one thing you are still missing**."*

Not a list. He has kept the five, and one item remains.

## What the one thing is

*"Go and **sell everything you own** and **give the money to the poor**, and you will
have **treasure in heaven**. Then **come back and follow Me**."*

Four instructions. The first two empty him, the third relocates what he had, and the
fourth is the actual invitation.

It is the same invitation given free to fishermen and a tax collector, with a condition
attached that was attached to nobody else.

## The commandment that was left out

The first one. *You shall have no other gods before Me.*

He was asked to prove he had kept the table that was never recited.

## The response

*When the man heard it, **sorrow came over him, because he was very rich**.*

Luke ends the scene there. The man does not argue, does not bargain, and is not
recorded as leaving in Luke's wording — Matthew and Mark say he went away.

He is the only person in the Gospels who is invited to follow and does not.

## What Jesus says next

*"**How hard it is** for those who have riches to enter the kingdom of God! It is easier
for a camel to go through the eye of a needle."*

And when they ask *who then can be saved*, the answer is *the things which are
impossible with men are possible with God.*

## The next man in Luke

Zacchaeus, in the very next chapter — rich, and he gives half his goods to the poor
without being asked. Luke puts them one after another. See the note on Luke 19:1–10.

> **What he came for.** He asked how to inherit. He was told to sell, give, and come —
> and the treasure was named as already waiting in heaven before he took a step.
