The objection is never spoken aloud, and it gets answered anyway.

## Who is in the room

*Some of the teachers of the law **were sitting there**.*

Luke's parallel says they had come *out of every town of Galilee, Judea, and Jerusalem*.
This is not a casual audience; the scholars have travelled.

## The objection

*They began **turning it over in their hearts**: "Why does this man talk this way? **That
is an insult to God. Nobody can forgive sins except God alone**."*

The reasoning is correct. A third party cannot forgive a debt owed to someone else.

Sin is against God, so the pardon is God's to give — and they are right about that.

## What He knew

*Jesus knew **immediately, deep in His spirit**, what they were reasoning **among
themselves**.*

Nothing had been said. Mark says twice in one sentence that it was interior.

Answering the unspoken thought is itself an answer to the question they were asking.

## The question back

*"**Why are you turning such things over in your hearts?**"*

## The comparison

*"**Which is easier to say** to this man — 'Your sins are forgiven,' or 'Get up, pick up
your mat, and walk'?"*

Easier **to say**. That is the precise wording.

Saying *your sins are forgiven* is easier, because nothing visible follows and nobody can
check it. Saying *get up and walk* is verifiable within seconds.

So He says the checkable one, to establish the other.

## The purpose clause

*"**But so that you will know the Son of Man has authority here on earth to forgive
sins**."*

The sentence breaks off mid-way — Mark inserts *and here He turned to the paralyzed man*
— and then finishes as a command to somebody else.

The healing is inside a sentence addressed to the scribes.

## "Here on earth"

The Greek is *on the earth*. Their objection was that forgiveness belongs in heaven. He
puts it on the ground, in a house in Capernaum, in the afternoon.

## "The Son of Man"

His usual way of referring to Himself. It comes from Daniel 7: *One like the Son of Man,
coming with the clouds of heaven… to Him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom.*

It sounds modest and is not.

## The three commands

*"**Get up, pick up your mat, and go home**."*

The mat is included. He is to carry the thing that carried him.

## What happened

*And the man **stood right up, took his mat, and walked out in front of them all**.*

Mark's Greek is immediate. Through the crowd, past the scribes, out the door that had
been too full to get in by.

## The reaction

*They were beside themselves, and they **praised God** and kept saying, "**We have never
seen anything like this**."*

The Greek is *they were amazed out of their minds*.

## What was and was not proved

The legs were the visible half. The forgiveness was the point, and He said so before the
man moved.

> **What is on offer in the passage.** The scribes came to assess a claim about
> authority. They watched a man walk out carrying the bed he was brought in on, and the
> claim it was offered as proof of was the one about sins.
