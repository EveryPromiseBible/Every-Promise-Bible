The clause that ran through the first half of the chapter survives the fall and reappears
underground.

## The accusation

*When Joseph's master heard his wife's account — "**This is how your slave treated me**" — he burned
with anger.*

## The wording

*Your slave.* Not *your Hebrew*, which is how she put it to the household servants.

To the servants she said *he brought in a Hebrew to us to mock us*, which includes a complaint
against her husband.

## What Potiphar does not do

Execute him.

A slave accused of assaulting his master's wife would ordinarily be killed. Joseph goes to prison
instead, and the text lets the reader draw a conclusion it does not state.

## Where he goes

*He took Joseph and put him in prison, **the place where the king's prisoners were confined**.*

The Hebrew is **bet hassohar** — the round house.

Not a common jail. The prison for the king's men, which is why a butler and a baker turn up in it
two chapters later.

## The full stop

*And there Joseph stayed.*

## The turn

*But **the LORD was with Joseph**.*

The Hebrew is **vayehi YHWH et-Yosef**.

## Where the same clause appeared

*The LORD was with Joseph, and he was a successful man.* And *his master saw that the LORD was with
him*. See the note on Genesis 39:1–6.

Twice in the house, twice in the prison. The chapter is built on the repetition.

## What is not repeated

Anything about the circumstances improving.

He goes from head of a household to a cell, and the sentence about him does not change.

## The three things

*He showed him **steadfast kindness** and gave him **favor in the eyes of the prison keeper**.*

The Hebrew is **chesed** — covenant loyalty. And **chen** — favour.

## What the keeper does

*The keeper **put Joseph in charge of all the prisoners** and everything that was done there.*

Exactly what Potiphar did. The same delegation, one floor down.

## The detail

*He **paid no attention to anything under Joseph's care**.*

The Hebrew is **ein sar bet-hassohar ro'eh et-kol-me'umah beyado** — the keeper saw nothing at all in
his hand.

Identical phrasing to verse 6: *he did not know what he had except the bread which he ate*.

## The last line

*Because **the LORD was with Joseph and made everything he did succeed**.*

The Hebrew is **matzliach** — the same word used of him in the master's house.

## How long

Years. He interprets the dreams *after two whole years* have passed following the butler's release,
and the butler forgot him for all of them.

## What he says about it later

*"You meant evil against me; **but God meant it for good**, in order to bring it about as it is this
day, to save many people alive."* See the note on Genesis 50:19–21.

## The psalm's summary

*They **hurt his feet with fetters**, he was laid in irons. **Until the time that his word came to
pass**, the word of the LORD tested him.* See the note on Psalms 105:17–22.

The Hebrew there is **tzerafathu** — refined him. The metallurgy word.

> **The clause that does not move.** *The LORD was with Joseph* is said of a man running a great
> house and of the same man in a cell, in the same chapter, in the same words.
