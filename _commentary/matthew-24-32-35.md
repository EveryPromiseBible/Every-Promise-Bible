A short parable set inside the longest answer Jesus gives about the end, and it
is about reading a season rather than fixing a date.

## The lesson

*"As soon as its branch turns soft and puts out its leaves, you know that summer
is near."*

The Greek behind *turns soft* is about the branch becoming tender — sap moving
in wood that was dry all winter. It happens before the leaves.

Nobody is told to calculate. They are told to notice what a farmer notices.

## "Right at the door"

*"When you see all these things, you know that He is near, right at the door."*

The Greek can be read *it is near* or *He is near* — the verb has no stated
subject. The Illumination takes the personal reading, and the phrase *at the
door* elsewhere in the New Testament is always used of someone standing there.

James uses it: *the Judge is standing at the door.* Revelation uses it: *I stand
at the door and knock.*

## The generation

*"This generation will certainly not pass away until all these things have
happened."*

The word is **genea**. It has been read as the people alive then, as the Jewish
people as a race, and as the generation that sees the signs begin.

The sentence is left as He said it, and it has been argued about for two thousand
years without being softened by anybody.

## The last line

*"Heaven and earth will pass away, but My words will never pass away."*

The most permanent things a person can point at are named, and then set below His
own sentences.

The Greek for *will never pass away* is a double negative — the strongest form of
denial available in the language.

> **Where this sits.** The disciples had asked two questions on the Mount of
> Olives: when will these things be, and what will be the sign. He answers the
> second at length and, a few verses on, says of the first that no one knows the
> day or the hour.
