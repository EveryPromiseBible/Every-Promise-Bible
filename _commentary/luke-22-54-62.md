Only Luke records the look, and it is the hinge of the scene.

## The distance

*Peter followed a long way behind.*

Luke's phrasing is exact and it is not only geography. He is close enough to be
in the courtyard and far enough to deny knowing Him.

## The three denials

They escalate, and they get shorter:

- *Woman, I do not know Him*
- *Man, I am not*
- *Man, I have no idea what you are talking about*

An hour passes between the second and third. He had time to leave, and stayed.

## The timing

*And while the words were still in his mouth, the rooster crowed.*

Luke puts the two in the same instant. No gap for reflection.

## The look

*And the Lord turned and looked straight at Peter.*

Jesus was inside the high priest's house being interrogated. For this to happen
He was being moved through the courtyard, or was visible across it — bound,
already struck, in the middle of His own trial.

The verb Luke uses is for a direct, deliberate look at someone.

Nothing is said. No word is recorded, and no rebuke.

## What the look did

*And Peter remembered what the Lord had said to him.*

The remembering is what breaks him — not the servant girl, not the rooster.
Christ had told him this would happen, and had said in the same breath: *I have
prayed for you, that your faith may not fail; and when you have turned back,
strengthen your brothers.*

He had been told the failure and the recovery in one sentence, before either
happened.

> **Where it ends.** *He went outside and wept bitterly.* Luke leaves him there
> and does not resolve it in this chapter. The resolution is on a beach in John
> 21, over a charcoal fire — the only other charcoal fire in the Gospels is the
> one in this courtyard.
