A pagan king dreams the whole future of the world, and a Jewish captive tells him
what he dreamed before interpreting it.

## What Daniel had to do

Nebuchadnezzar demanded that his wise men tell him the dream itself, not just its
meaning — on pain of death for all of them.

So the description in these verses is Daniel telling the king what was in the
king's own head.

## The metals, top to bottom

- **head** — fine gold
- **chest and arms** — silver
- **belly and thighs** — bronze
- **legs** — iron
- **feet** — part iron, part clay

Daniel names the first outright: *you are that head of gold.* Babylon.

Then, in his own words, *another kingdom inferior to yours*, and another, and a
fourth as strong as iron that breaks everything.

## The direction of travel

Each metal down the statue is **less valuable** and **harder**.

Gold to silver to bronze to iron to iron mixed with baked clay. Worth decreasing,
strength increasing, and then the last one is strong and brittle at the same time.

Empires get tougher and cheaper as the statue goes down.

## What it is standing on

*Its **feet** part iron and part clay.*

Everything above rests on the weakest joint in it. Iron and fired clay do not
bind — Daniel says so a few verses later: *they will not hold together, just as
iron does not mix with clay.*

The whole weight of world history, in this dream, is balanced on a mixture that
cannot adhere.

## What the king saw it as

*Huge it was, **blazing with a bright and fearsome light**, and the look of it was
**terrifying**.*

That is how the kingdoms of men look from a throne — enormous, blazing, and
frightening.

Daniel's God shows the same thing to Daniel as a beast-vision in chapter 7. The
king saw a magnificent statue. The prophet saw animals.

> **What is not in the dream yet.** A stone, cut without hands, which strikes the
> feet and grinds the whole statue to chaff, and then becomes a mountain filling
> the earth. That is the next part of what Nebuchadnezzar saw, and it is the part
> the dream was given for. See the note on Daniel 2:44–45.
