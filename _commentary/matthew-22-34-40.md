A lawyer sets a trap and Jesus answers by quoting two verses that were not
usually put together.

## The question behind the question

*Which is the greatest commandment in the law?*

It was a live rabbinic debate — the tradition counted 613 commands, and ranking
them was a standing argument. Any answer would offend somebody, which is why it
is described as a test.

## The two He picks

- **Deuteronomy 6:5** — love the LORD your God with all your heart, soul and
  mind. This is the Shema, recited twice daily by every observant Jew. Nobody
  could object to it.
- **Leviticus 19:18** — love your neighbour as yourself. A single line, buried in
  a chapter of miscellaneous laws about gleaning, wages and honest weights.

The first was the obvious answer. Pulling the second up beside it was not.

## "The second is like it"

The word means *of the same kind*. He does not rank them as first and runner-up;
He says they are the same sort of thing.

That is the move the lawyer would not have expected. Love of God and love of the
person next to you are put in one category.

## "Hangs on"

The verb is the one used of hanging something on a peg — and of a body hanging on
a cross.

Everything else, in this image, is suspended from these two. Take them away and
the rest has nothing to hold it up.

> **Where the commanding runs from.** Jesus is answering a question about the
> law, and the answer is a command. What the New Testament adds is where the
> capacity comes from: *we love Him because He loved us first.* The order is not a
> softening of the command — it is the only thing that makes it possible. See the
> note on 1 John 4:19.
