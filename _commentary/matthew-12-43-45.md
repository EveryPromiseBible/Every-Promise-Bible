A short, strange parable about a house, and the problem with the house is not
that it is dirty.

## The three words

*When it arrives it finds the place **empty, swept out and neatly arranged**.*

Every one of them sounds like an improvement. The house is clean. Nothing is out
of place. Somebody has clearly done work on it.

And that is exactly the condition in which it gets taken over. The trouble is the
first word: **empty**. Nobody moved in.

## The wandering

*It wanders through waterless places looking for rest and finds none.*

The detail is offered without explanation and Matthew does not develop it. What
the parable does say is that the spirit's preference is to return to what it
knows: *I will go back to the house I left.*

## Reform is not the same as occupancy

That is the argument. A life can be tidied — habits stopped, behaviour corrected,
the place genuinely swept — and still be unoccupied.

The parable's ending is blunt: *that person ends up worse off than before.* A
cleaned house with nobody in it is more vulnerable than a lived-in one.

## Who Jesus was talking about

*That is exactly how it will be for this evil generation.*

He applies it to a generation, not to an individual. The context is a demand for
a sign from scribes and Pharisees who had just accused Him of casting out demons
by Beelzebul — religious people, morally scrupulous, whose reform had never
included receiving Him.

> **The alternative in the same chapter.** *Whoever does the will of My Father in
> heaven is My brother and sister and mother.* Jesus says it moments before this,
> pointing at His disciples. The empty house and the family are the two options
> Matthew 12 puts side by side.
