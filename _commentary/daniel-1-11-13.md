---
key: Daniel 1:11–13
title: "Test us for ten days"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
A teenager proposes a trial to a mid-level official, and the chapter has already said why it
works.

## What has been asked

*Daniel purposed in his heart that **he would not defile himself** with the portion of the king's
delicacies, nor with the wine which he drank.*

The Hebrew is **sam al-libbo** — he set it on his heart.

The text does not say what the defilement was. The food was probably offered to idols and probably
not slaughtered by Israelite rules, but Daniel does not explain, and neither does the narrator.

## The verse the chapter turns on

*Now **God had brought Daniel into the favour and goodwill of the chief of the eunuchs**.*

The Hebrew is **chesed verachamim** — covenant kindness and compassion. The same pair used of God
Himself.

That is verse 9, before the request is made. The resolve in verse 8 does not produce the
permission; the permission is already in place, and it is credited to God.

## The obstacle

The chief official is sympathetic and frightened: *I fear my lord the king… why should he see your
faces looking worse than the young men who are your age? Then you would endanger my head.*

He does not refuse on principle. He refuses because it will cost him his life.

## What Daniel does about it

*So Daniel turned to **the steward**.*

He goes one level down, to the man who actually carries the trays, and makes a smaller, testable
proposal to somebody with less to lose.

## The proposal

*"Please, **test us for ten days**. Give us only vegetables to eat and water to drink. Then **look
at our faces**… and **decide about your servants by what you see**."*

The Hebrew for *test* is **nasah** — to try, to prove.

Ten days. A short window, an observable outcome, and the decision handed back to the steward.

## What is not in the request

Any claim about what will happen. Daniel does not promise the result. He asks for a look.

## What happens

*And at the end of ten days **their features appeared better and fatter in flesh** than all the
young men who ate the portion of the king's delicacies.*

*As for these four young men, **God gave them knowledge and skill** in all literature and wisdom;
and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams.*

Twice in the chapter the outcome is attributed to God rather than to the diet.

## The other man who would not eat the food offered

*Then **Jesus** was led up by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil. And when He
had fasted forty days and forty nights, afterward He was hungry… "Command that these stones become
bread." But He answered and said, "It is written, '**Man shall not live by bread alone, but by
every word that proceeds from the mouth of God**.'"*

**Matthew** 4. A young man, an offer of food, and a refusal — and the sentence He answers with is
about where life actually comes from.

*"**I have food to eat of which you do not know**… My food is to do the will of Him who sent Me,
and to finish His work."*

## What the chapter is not

A diet. The narrator's own explanation is in verses 9 and 17, and both of them name God as the
actor.

> **Verse 9.** *God had brought Daniel into the favour and goodwill of the chief official* — and
> the Hebrew is **chesed verachamim**, the covenant pair. The resolve is real, and the door was
> already open before he knocked on it.
