A member of the Sanhedrin comes at night and takes a metaphor literally.

## The objection

*"**How can a man be born when he is old? He cannot climb back into his mother's womb a
second time**."*

Nicodemus is a teacher of Israel, and his response to *you must be born again* is obstetric.

It is the running feature of John's Gospel — the woman at the well asks for water so she will
not have to keep coming back, the crowd asks for the bread always, the disciples wonder
whether somebody brought Him lunch. See the note on John 4:31–34.

Every time, a literal reading gives Him the opening.

## The phrase behind it

The Greek is **anothen** — which means both *again* and *from above*.

Nicodemus hears *again* and asks about a second birth. Jesus meant *from above*, and John
chose a word that carries both.

## The answer

*"Unless a person is **born of water and of the Spirit**, he cannot enter the kingdom of
God."*

Water and Spirit. Ezekiel had put them together in a promise: *I will sprinkle clean water on
you, and you shall be clean… **I will put My Spirit within you***.

Nicodemus is being told he should have known this. Two verses later: *are you the teacher of
Israel, and do not know these things?*

## The rule

*"**What is born of human parents is human; what is born of the Spirit is spirit**."*

The Greek is *that which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the Spirit
is spirit*.

Each thing produces after its own kind. The clause built into the third day of creation. See
the note on Genesis 1:9–13.

Flesh cannot produce spirit, which is why a second physical birth would not help.

## The reassurance

*"**Do not be so astonished** that I said to you, 'You must be born all over again.'"*

The Greek is **me thaumases** — do not marvel.

## The wind

*"**The wind blows wherever it pleases**."*

The Greek is **pneuma** — the same word as *Spirit*, and it does duty for both. The Hebrew
**ruach** does the same.

The sentence is untranslatable in one direction: *the Spirit breathes where it wills*.

## What can be observed

*"**You hear the sound of it, but you cannot tell where it came from or where it is going**."*

Two things known and two unknown. The effect is audible and the mechanism is not.

Nobody has ever seen wind. They see what it moves.

## The application

*"**That is how it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit**."*

The comparison is to the person, not to the Spirit. A born-again person is like the wind:
what happened is undeniable and the process is not on display.

## What comes next

*"If I have told you earthly things and you do not believe, how will you believe if I tell
you heavenly things?"*

And then the serpent on the pole, and *for God so loved the world that He gave His only
begotten Son*. See the note on John 3:14–18.

The most quoted verse in the Bible is the end of a private conversation with a Pharisee at
night.

## Where Nicodemus turns up again

Defending Him in the council: *does our law judge a man before it hears him?*

And at the end, with a hundred pounds of myrrh and aloes, in daylight, at a burial. See the
note on John 19:38–42.

John introduces him each time as *the man who came to Jesus by night*.

> **The word that carries both.** *Anothen* — again, and from above. Nicodemus took the first
> meaning, and the answer he gets is about the second.
