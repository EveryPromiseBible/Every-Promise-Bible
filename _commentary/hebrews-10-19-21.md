The letter's argument turns here from explanation to invitation, and it opens
with a place nobody was allowed to enter.

## Where the way leads

*The way into the holiest place is open to us now, and we can walk in without a
flinch.*

The holiest place is behind the curtain — one man, one day a year, with blood and
smoke. See the note on Leviticus 16:11–14.

The word behind *without a flinch* is **parrhesia**, boldness, the freedom of
somebody with the right to speak in the room. The same word the letter used at
4:16 about the throne.

## What makes it open

*Because of the blood of Jesus.* Not because of improvement, and not because the
holiness of the place has been reduced.

## "A road that is alive"

*He opened up a road for us that no one had ever walked before, a road that is
alive.*

Two claims. It is **new** — the Greek word originally meant freshly slaughtered,
and came to mean recent, unused. Nobody has been down it.

And it is **living**. A road is not usually alive. The writer is stretching the
image on purpose, because the way is a person.

## Through the curtain

*It runs straight through the curtain, which is His own body.*

Matthew records the temple curtain torn in two from top to bottom at the moment
Jesus died. Hebrews puts the two together: the torn veil and the torn body are
one event, and what they produce is a road.

See the note on Matthew 27:51–53.

## The priest at the end

*And we have a great Priest set over God's household.*

Three things are now in place — an open way, a road that lives, and a priest
already inside. The next verse begins *let us draw near*, and the rest of the
chapter is what to do about it.

## What stood in front of the curtain

The golden altar of incense, immediately outside the veil, with smoke going over the
top of it into the room beyond.

The high priest carried a censer through on the Day of Atonement precisely so the cloud
would cover the mercy seat *lest he die*.

The incense was what made the approach survivable. See the note on Exodus 30:34–38.

## What is in the room now

*And we have **a great Priest set over God's household**.*

Not waiting outside once a year. Inside, permanently, and the way to Him is described as
open.

> **Why "flinch" is the right word.** Everything in the old arrangement taught
> people to hesitate: the curtain, the once-a-year, the incense so the priest
> would not die. The letter is not asking readers to feel casual. It is telling
> them the reason for the hesitation has been dealt with.
