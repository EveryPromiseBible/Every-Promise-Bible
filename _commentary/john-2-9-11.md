The first miracle, and almost nobody at the party knows it happened.

## Who knew

*He had **no idea where it had come from**, though **the servants who had drawn it
knew perfectly well**.*

John records both halves. The master of the feast, the man whose job was to know
about the wine, does not know. The servants do.

The people who carried the water jars are the only ones in the room who saw
anything.

## What they had done

Six stone waterpots, standing there *according to the manner of purification of the
Jews* — for washing hands and vessels, not for drinking.

*"Fill the waterpots with water." And **they filled them up to the brim**.*

Twenty or thirty gallons each. Six of them. Somewhere between a hundred and a hundred
and eighty gallons.

## The compression

Wine takes a vine, a season, a harvest, pressing, and time in a jar. Every stage of
it is time.

Between the drawing and the tasting there was none of that.

## What the master says

*"**Everyone puts out the good wine first**, and then brings on the cheaper wine once
the guests have had plenty. But **you have kept the best until now**."*

He is complaining, in the way a professional complains, that the order is wrong.

He is speaking better than he knows. It is John's habit throughout the Gospel —
Caiaphas does the same thing later, and John points it out.

## What John calls it

*This was **the first of His signs**.*

The Greek is **semeion** — a sign, something pointing beyond itself. John never
calls them miracles; he counts seven signs and this is number one.

## Where He did it

*At Cana in Galilee* — a village nobody outside that district had heard of, at a
family wedding, for people who had run out of drink.

The first sign is not in the temple, not in Jerusalem, and not in front of anybody
who mattered. It solves a social embarrassment.

## The result

*He **let His glory be seen**, and **His disciples put their trust in Him**.*

Two things. The glory was shown, and a handful of men believed.

Nothing is recorded about the crowd, the bridegroom, or the master of the feast.

## What His mother said

*"**Whatever He says to you, do it.**"*

Her last recorded words in Scripture, spoken to the servants — the only people in the
story who end up seeing anything. See the note on John 2:1–5.

> **The second sign.** A nobleman's son healed at Capernaum, twenty-odd miles away,
> at the exact hour Jesus spoke. The first sign compresses time; the second crosses
> distance. John records them as the first two, in that order. See the note on
> John 4:46–50.
