The second attempt, and this time the tempter comes with a Bible verse.

## What he quotes

*He will give His angels orders concerning You, and they will lift You up in
their hands.*

It is Psalm 91, and it is quoted accurately as far as it goes. The promise is
real; the psalm says it.

What is left out is the line in the middle — *to guard you in all your ways.*
The keeping is promised along the road God has for a person, not on a jump the
person invents.

## The place

*The highest point of the temple*, over the Kidron valley — a drop of well over a
hundred feet, in full view of the courts below.

The suggestion is a spectacular, public, self-authenticating demonstration. It
would have settled the question of who He was in an afternoon.

## The answer

*It is also written: do not put the Lord your God to the test.*

Jesus does not dispute the psalm. He puts another Scripture beside it, from
Deuteronomy 6 — the passage about Massah, where Israel demanded water and asked
whether the LORD was among them or not.

A promise is not a lever. Testing God is what you do when you are not sure of Him
and want proof; and demanding proof is the opposite of the trust the promise was
given to.

## What this settles about quoting Scripture

A verse can be quoted correctly and used wrongly. The correction is not less
Bible but more of it — Jesus answers all three temptations from Deuteronomy, and
here He answers a quotation with a quotation.

> **Read alongside Psalms 91:5–8.** The psalm's promises are real and this app
> takes them as such. What this scene rules out is treating them as a mechanism
> to be operated, which is exactly what was being suggested.
