A woman who counted herself childless is asked to explain a crowd.

## Where she is standing

*Your **ruined places**, your **desolate land**, your **country in rubble**.*

The address is to Jerusalem after the fall. Nothing in her situation has changed when the
promise is made.

## The complaint the chapter answers

*But Zion said, "The LORD has forsaken me, and my Lord has forgotten me."*

*"**Can a woman forget her nursing child?…** Surely they may forget, yet **I will not forget
you**. See, I have inscribed you on the palms of My hands."*

Nine verses earlier, and it is the reason everything after it is said.

*Then He said to Thomas, "Reach your finger here, and **look at My hands**."*

## The jewels

*"As surely as I live," says the LORD, "**you will wear them all like jewels**. You will fasten
them on like a bride."*

The children are the ornament. She is not given jewellery; she is told the people coming back
are it.

*"**They shall be Mine**," says the LORD of hosts, "**on the day that I make them My jewels**."*

Malachi uses the same picture the other way round — the people are God's treasure — and the two
verses between them describe the same possession from both ends.

## The question

*"**Who gave birth to all these? I was grieving and alone**, an exile, a wanderer. **Who raised
these children? I was left all by myself. Where did all these come from?**"*

Four questions, and she cannot answer any of them. The passage does not have her recognise her
own children. It has her stand in front of a crowd she did not produce and ask where they came
from.

*"Rejoice, O barren, you who do not bear! Break forth and shout, you who are not in labour! For
**the desolate has many more children than she who has a husband**."*

**Paul** quotes the next chapter of Isaiah in **Galatians** 4 and applies it to the church — a
family belonging to a woman with no natural claim to one.

## Where the children came from

*And not for that nation only, but also that **He would gather together in one the children of
God who were scattered abroad**.*

**John** 11, and it is Caiaphas' unwitting prophecy: it is expedient that one man die for the
people. The gathering Isaiah describes is credited to a death.

*"And **other sheep I have which are not of this fold**; them also I must bring."*

## The ones who tore her down

*Those who tore you down and left you empty **are leaving you behind**.*

They go, and the children come, in the same sentence. She is not asked to drive anybody out.

> **What she is asked.** Not to be grateful — to explain. *Who gave birth to all these? I was
> left all by myself.* The promise arrives as a question she has no answer to, standing in the
> rubble.
