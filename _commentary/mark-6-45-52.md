Mark tells the same night as Matthew and keeps two details nobody else has.

## He made them go

*Right away He **had His disciples get into the boat**.*

The Greek is compelled — He made them. They are on that water because He put them
there.

## What He does

*He went up the mountainside **to pray**. Night came on. The boat was out in the middle
of the lake, and **Jesus was alone on the shore**.*

Mark separates them deliberately: the boat in the middle, and Him alone.

## The first detail

*He **could see that they were straining hard at the oars**.*

From a mountainside, at night, on a lake several miles across.

Whatever Mark means by it, he says Jesus was watching the whole time. They could not
see Him; He could see them.

The Greek for *straining* is being tormented, harassed — the word for being tortured.

## The wind

*Because **the wind was blowing straight against them**.*

Contrary. They had been rowing since evening and were about halfway.

## The hour

*At about **the fourth watch of the night**.*

Three to six in the morning. Nine hours or so of rowing.

He comes at the end of the night, not the beginning.

## The second detail

*And **He was about to go right past them**.*

Only Mark has this, and it is the strangest sentence in the passage.

He was not going to get into the boat. He was going to pass by.

Readers have long connected it to Exodus 33, where God tells Moses *I will make all My
goodness **pass before you***, and to Elijah at Horeb, where *the LORD passed by*.

Both are occasions when God shows Himself by going past somebody.

## What made Him stop

*But when they saw Him… they thought He was a ghost, and **they screamed**.*

He stops for the scream.

The passing-by would have been a revelation. The screaming turned it into a rescue.

## What He says

*"**Take heart. It is I. Do not be afraid.**"*

The middle two words are **ego eimi** — the ordinary *it's me*, and also the name from
the bush.

## The verdict

*They were utterly stunned. **They had not yet grasped what the loaves had meant.**
Their hearts were still slow and closed.*

Mark's own comment, and it is severe.

They had watched five thousand men fed from five loaves that same afternoon and
collected twelve baskets of leftovers. See the note on Mark 6:35–41.

Mark says the storm should not have surprised them, because of the bread.

> **What Matthew adds and Mark leaves out.** Peter walking on the water and sinking.
> Mark's Gospel is traditionally associated with Peter's preaching, and it is the one
> account that does not include the part where Peter tried.
