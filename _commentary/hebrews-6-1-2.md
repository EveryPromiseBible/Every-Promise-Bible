Six items are named as foundation, and every one of them could be taught in a synagogue.

## The instruction

*So let us **leave the beginner's lesson about Christ behind** and be **carried on to full growth**.*

The Greek is **aphentes ton tēs archēs tou Christou logon** — leaving the word of the beginning of
Christ.

And **epi tēn teleiotēta pherōmetha** — let us be borne on to maturity.

## The passive

**Pherōmetha** — let us be carried.

Not *let us press on*. The verb puts the readers on the receiving end of the movement.

## Why he says it

*You have come to need milk and not solid food. **For everyone who partakes only of milk is unskilled
in the word of righteousness**, for he is a babe.* See the note on Hebrews 5:12–14.

The rebuke is that they are still on the first lesson years later.

## The foundation

*We are **not laying that foundation down a second time**.*

Six items follow, in three pairs.

## The first pair

*Turning away from **everything we did that led nowhere**, and **trusting God**.*

The Greek is **metanoias apo nekrōn ergōn** — repentance from dead works. And **pisteōs epi Theon**
— faith toward God.

## The word

**Nekra erga** — dead works. The phrase appears twice in this letter and nowhere else.

*How much more shall the blood of Christ… **cleanse your conscience from dead works** to serve the
living God.* See the note on Hebrews 9:13–14.

Not sins. Works — the kind done to establish a standing.

## The second pair

*Teaching about **the washings**, and **hands placed on people**.*

The Greek is **baptismōn didachēs** — of washings, plural. Not the word for Christian baptism used
elsewhere; the general word for ceremonial washings.

Laying on of hands: ordination, blessing, the transfer of a sacrifice's identity to the animal.

## The third pair

*The dead raised, and **a judgment whose verdict stands forever**.*

The Greek is **anastaseōs nekrōn kai krimatos aiōniou**.

## What the six have in common

Repentance, faith in God, washings, laying on of hands, resurrection, eternal judgment.

Every one of them was standard Jewish teaching. Nothing in the list is distinctively Christian.

## What that means for the argument

The letter is written to people tempted to go back. He calls the whole platform they would return
to *the foundation*, and says the foundation is not the building.

## What he wants to move on to

The subject he keeps interrupting himself to reach: *called by God as High Priest **according to the
order of Melchizedek** — of whom we have much to say, and hard to explain*. See the note on
Hebrews 5:9–11.

He returns to it in chapter 7 and does not leave it again.

## What comes between

The warning passage. *For it is impossible for those who were once enlightened… **if they fall
away**, to renew them again to repentance.* See the note on Hebrews 6:4–6.

The hardest paragraph in the letter, placed immediately after the call to go on.

## Where it settles

*But, beloved, **we are confident of better things concerning you**, yes, things that accompany
salvation.* See the note on Hebrews 6:9–10.

And *this hope we have as **an anchor of the soul**, both sure and steadfast, and which enters the
Presence behind the veil*. See the note on Hebrews 6:19–20.

## The clause

*And **this we will do if God permits**.*

The sentence after the six items. Even the going on is not put down as the readers' achievement.

> **The six.** Repentance, faith, washings, hands, resurrection, judgment — every item on the
> foundation list could be taught in a synagogue. That is his point about it.
