Three sentences built the same way, and the repetition is the argument.

## The pattern

*Different kinds of **gifts**, but the **same Spirit**.*
*Different ways of **serving**, but the **same Lord**.*
*Different things being **done**, but it is the **same God** who works all of them.*

Three times: variety on one side, sameness on the other.

And the sameness is named as Spirit, Lord, and God — Paul writes a Trinitarian
sentence without pausing to explain it, in the middle of a practical passage about
church meetings.

## Why he is saying it

Corinth was ranking each other by which gift they had. The whole chapter is written
against that, and the answer he gives is not to level the gifts but to point at
their source.

Different — and Paul never suggests they should be the same. He simply removes the
grounds for comparison.

## "To each one"

*To **each one** the manifestation of the Spirit is given.*

Not to the impressive ones, or the mature ones. Each one.

The Greek for *manifestation* is a showing, a making visible. Something invisible
becoming apparent through a person.

## What it is for

*For **the good of everyone**.*

The Greek is *toward what is profitable* — for the common advantage.

That is the criterion Paul applies all through chapters 12 to 14. Not what a gift
does for the person who has it. What it is worth to the room.

He says it again in chapter 14: *let all things be done for edification*, and *since
you are zealous for spiritual gifts, seek to excel to the edifying of the church.*

## The nine

The verses following list them: a word of wisdom, a word of knowledge, faith,
healings, the working of miracles, prophecy, discerning of spirits, tongues, and
the interpretation of tongues.

And after the list: *but one and the same Spirit works all these things,
**distributing to each one individually as He wills**.*

The distribution is His decision, not an achievement.

## The body

The rest of the chapter is the body — the foot saying it is not a hand, the ear
saying it is not an eye, and the eye unable to say to the hand *I have no need of
you.*

*Those members of the body which seem to be weaker are necessary.*

## What comes straight after

Chapter 13. *Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not
love, I have become sounding brass.*

Paul lists the gifts, describes the body, and then interrupts himself for a chapter
on love before returning to the subject.

> **The verse that opens the chapter.** *Now concerning spiritual gifts, brethren, **I
> do not want you to be ignorant**.* Paul's reason for the whole discussion is that
> they did not understand what they had.
