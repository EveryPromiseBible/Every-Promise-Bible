key: Titus 1:15
title: "To the pure, everything is pure"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
One sentence about purity, and it locates the problem somewhere unexpected.

## The claim

*"**To those who are pure, everything is pure.**"*

**Panta kathara tois katharois.**

The context is a group teaching *Jewish myths and the commands of men who turn away from the truth*
— food rules, most likely, and ritual regulations attached to them.

**Paul**'s answer is not a better rule. It is that the category has moved.

*"**Do you not perceive that whatever enters a man from outside cannot defile him**?"… thus He
purified all foods.*

**Mark** 7, and **Mark** adds the last clause himself as an explanation.

*What God has cleansed **you must not call common**.*

**Acts** 10, said three times to **Peter** on a rooftop, and the point of the vision turns out to be a
Roman soldier rather than a menu.

*I know and am convinced by the Lord **Jesus** that there is **nothing unclean of itself**; but to him
who considers anything to be unclean, to him it is unclean.*

**Romans** 14, and **Paul** grounds it in a Person.

## Where the defilement is

*"But to people who are **filthy inside and refuse to believe**, nothing is pure — the filth has run
right through their thinking and their conscience."*

**Memiantai auton kai ho nous kai he syneidesis** — both their mind and their conscience have been
defiled.

The dirt is not in the objects. It has got into the equipment used to assess them.

*"For **from within, out of the heart of men, proceed evil thoughts**… all these evil things come from
within and defile a man."*

## How anybody becomes pure

**Paul** states the principle and does not, here, say how somebody gets into the first category. The
rest of the letter does.

*Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to His mercy He saved us, **through
the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit**.* See the note on Titus 3:4–7.

*How much more shall **the blood of Christ**, who through the eternal Spirit offered Himself without
spot to God, **cleanse your conscience from dead works** to serve the living God?*

**Hebrews** 9, and the organ it names is the second of **Paul**'s two: the conscience.

*Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, **having our hearts sprinkled from an
evil conscience**.*

## The last verse

*"**They claim they know God, but everything they actually do denies Him.**"*

**Theon homologousin eidenai, tois de ergois arnountai** — they profess to know God, but in works
they deny Him.

The people insisting on the rules are the ones whose lives contradict the claim. **Paul** does not
answer their theology with a counter-rule; he points at the fruit.

> **Where the dirt is.** Not in the food. *Both their mind and their conscience have been defiled* —
> the contamination is in the instrument doing the inspecting, which is why a better rule would not
> help.
