One verse, and the grammar of it is the whole point.

## Where it sits

*"Let us be glad and rejoice and give Him glory, for **the marriage of the Lamb has come**, and His
wife has made herself ready."*

The fourfold *hallelujah* of the chapter has just been sung — the only occurrences of the word in
the New Testament.

## The clothing

*And **she was given** clean bright linen to wear.*

The Greek is **edothe aute** — it was given to her.

Aorist passive. Somebody handed it over.

## What she did

*His wife has **made herself ready**.*

The Greek is **hetoimasen heauten** — prepared herself.

Active. She did that.

## The two halves

She prepared herself; the dress was given to her.

Both statements stand in consecutive verses, and Revelation does not reconcile them.

## The linen

**Bussinon lampron katharon** — fine linen, bright and clean.

**Bussos** — the costly white linen of Egypt, used for priestly garments and royal dress.

**Lampron** — shining, radiant.

## The interpretation

*And **the linen is the right acts of God's people**.*

The Greek is **ta dikaiomata ton hagion** — the righteous deeds of the saints.

**Dikaioma** — a righteous act, or a righteous verdict. The noun is used both ways in the New
Testament.

## What that does

The dress that was given is identified as things the saints did.

Both facts are stated in one verse: the garment was handed to her, and the garment is her deeds.

## The other white robes in the book

*They washed their robes and **made them white in the blood of the Lamb**.* See the note on
Revelation 7:13–17.

Which is the only way a robe gets whiter for being washed in blood.

*He who overcomes shall be **clothed in white garments**.*

*I counsel you to buy from Me… **white garments, that you may be clothed**.*

Revelation keeps giving robes rather than describing people acquiring them.

## The contrast

Two chapters earlier, the other woman: *the woman was arrayed in purple and scarlet, and adorned
with gold and precious stones and pearls*.

Babylon dresses expensively. The bride wears linen.

## Who else wears it

*And the armies in heaven, clothed in **fine linen, white and clean**, followed Him on white
horses.*

Same fabric, eleven verses later.

## What Paul says about the preparation

*That He might sanctify and cleanse her **with the washing of water by the word**, that He might
present her to Himself a glorious church, **not having spot or wrinkle**.* See the note on
Ephesians 5:25–27.

The cleaning is done by the bridegroom, in Paul's account, and the presenting is His too.

## The invitation

*"**Blessed are those who are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb!**"*

And the angel's own correction, immediately after: *"See that you do not do that! I am your fellow
servant… **Worship God!**"*

John had fallen at his feet.

> **The two clauses.** *She made herself ready*, and *she was given* the dress — and the dress is
> identified as what the saints did. Revelation states both and leaves them standing.
