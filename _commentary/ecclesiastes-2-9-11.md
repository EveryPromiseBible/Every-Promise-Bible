He built everything a man could build, and then walked round it.

## The experiment

The chapter is a controlled test.

*I said in my heart, "Come now, I will test you with mirth; therefore enjoy pleasure."*

Wine, houses, vineyards, gardens, orchards, pools of water, servants, herds, silver and gold,
singers and musical instruments.

## The claim

*I grew great. **I grew greater than anyone who lived in Jerusalem before me**.*

The Hebrew is **vegadalti vehosaphti** — I became great and I added.

## The control

*And through all of it **my wisdom stayed with me**.*

The Hebrew is **aph chokmati amedah li**.

The point of the clause is that this is not a story of a wise man losing his head. He is watching
the experiment while running it.

## The permission

*Whatever my eyes desired, **I did not hold back from them**. **I refused my heart no joy**.*

The Hebrew is **lo atzalti** — I did not withhold.

Nothing was denied. Whatever limit anyone else's life has, this one did not have it.

## The wages

*My heart took pleasure in all my work, and **that was my reward for all my labor**.*

The Hebrew is **vezeh-hayah chelqi mikkol-amali** — this was my portion from all my labour.

The pleasure in the work was real, and he counts it as what he got.

## The turn

*But then **I turned and looked**.*

The Hebrew is **uphaniti ani** — and I turned, I myself.

Two words that change the chapter. He stops building and walks round it.

## What he sees

*I looked at **everything my hands had made**, and at **all the sweat it had cost me**.*

The Hebrew is **uva'amal she'amalti la'asot** — and at the labour I had laboured to do.

## The verdict

*And I saw it. **All of it. Vapor**.*

The Hebrew is **hakkol havel** — all of it is **hevel**.

Breath, vapour, mist. See the note on Ecclesiastes 1:1–3.

## The second image

*A **chasing after the wind**.*

The Hebrew is **ur'ut ruach** — a shepherding of wind, or a striving after wind.

The phrase runs through the book seven times, always attached to the moment he stops and looks.

## The conclusion

*There was **no lasting gain under the sun**.*

The Hebrew is **ve'eyn yitron tachat hashamesh** — and there is no profit under the sun.

**Yitron** — surplus, what is left over. The commercial word from chapter 1's opening question.
See the note on Ecclesiastes 1:1–3.

He asked the question at the start of the book and here reports the answer.

## What makes the verdict weigh

He is not describing something he read about.

The man saying it has the houses, the vineyards, the pools and the singers, and states plainly
that he enjoyed them.

## Where he lands a few verses later

*There is nothing better for a man than that he should **eat and drink, and that his soul should
enjoy good in his labour**. This also, I saw, was from the hand of God.* See the note on
Ecclesiastes 2:24–26.

Bread, work, and the day — received rather than accumulated.

## What the book concludes

*Fear God and keep His commandments, **for this is man's all**.* See the note on
Ecclesiastes 12:13–14.

## What Paul says

*Yet indeed **I also count all things loss** for the excellence of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my
Lord.* See the note on Philippians 3:7–9.

A man who had the religious equivalent, and did the same arithmetic.

> **The two words.** *And I turned.* He stops building, walks round what he has built, and the
> chapter's verdict is what he sees from there.
