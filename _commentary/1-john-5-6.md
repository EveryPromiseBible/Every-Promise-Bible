One verse, aimed at something specific that was being taught in Ephesus.

## The claim

*This is the One who came **by water and blood** — Jesus Christ.*

The Greek is **di' hudatos kai haimatos**.

## The insistence

*Not by **water alone**: by water and by blood.*

The emphatic repetition is the whole point of the verse.

Somebody was saying *water alone*, and John writes the sentence twice to close it.

## What was being taught

The early church recorded a teacher at Ephesus, Cerinthus, who held that the Christ descended on
the man Jesus at the baptism and departed before the crucifixion — so that the divine one was
not the one who died.

On that account, the Christ came *by water* and not by blood.

John's letters are full of denials aimed at that shape of teaching: *every spirit that confesses
that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God*.

## The water

The baptism in the Jordan, where the voice said *this is My beloved Son*.

## The blood

The cross.

And John alone records what came out of the side: *one of the soldiers pierced His side with a
spear, and immediately **blood and water** came out*.

*And he who has seen has testified, and his testimony is true.* See the note on
John 19:31–34.

He saw both, in that order, and writes about both here.

## The witness

*And **the Spirit is the One who stands witness**.*

The Greek is **to marturoun** — the one bearing witness. A participle, present tense: continuing.

## Why the Spirit can

*Because **the Spirit is truth**.*

Not *speaks the truth*. Is.

The same construction Jesus used of Himself: *I am the way, the truth, and the life*.

## What follows

*For there are three that bear witness: **the Spirit, the water, and the blood; and these three
agree as one**.*

The Greek is **eis to hen eisin** — they are unto the one thing.

Three witnesses, which is what the law required: *by the mouth of two or three witnesses the
matter shall be established*.

## The argument from testimony

*If we receive the witness of men, **the witness of God is greater**.*

*He who believes in the Son of God **has the witness in himself**.*

John moves from external testimony to internal, and does not present them as alternatives.

## What the witness says

*And this is the testimony: that **God has given us eternal life, and this life is in His Son**.*

*He who has the Son has life; **he who does not have the Son of God does not have life**.* See the
note on 1 John 5:11–12.

## Why the letter was written

*These things I have written to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, **that you may know
that you have eternal life**.*

The Greek is **hina eidete** — that you may know.

John's Gospel was written *that you may believe*. The letter was written to people who already do,
so that they may know they have it.

## The Spirit's other testimony

*The Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit that **we are children of God**.* See the note on
Romans 8:15–17.

The same function, in Paul's vocabulary.

> **The repetition.** *Not by water alone: by water and by blood.* John says it twice in one verse
> because a teacher in his own city was saying the first half and stopping.
