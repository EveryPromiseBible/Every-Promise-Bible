The crowd's reaction, and the thing Luke keeps in the frame is that everybody knew the
man.

## What they see

*All the people saw him **walking and praising God**.*

Two things at once. He had gone into the temple *walking, leaping, and praising God* two
verses earlier.

## The recognition

*And **they recognized him** — this was **the man who used to sit begging at the
Beautiful Gate**.*

The Greek is that they knew him thoroughly.

He was a fixture. Luke has already said he was *carried* there **daily**, and Peter's
sermon a chapter later notes he was *over forty years old*.

Everyone entering that gate had walked past him for decades.

## Why that matters

There is no question about identity. Nobody in the courtyard has to be persuaded that
this is the same man.

The Sanhedrin says as much the next day: *what shall we do to these men? For, indeed,
that a notable miracle has been done through them is evident to all who dwell in
Jerusalem, and **we cannot deny it**.*

## The gate

The Beautiful Gate — the entrance from the court of the Gentiles into the temple proper.
Josephus describes a great bronze gate on the east side that took twenty men to close.

He was placed at the door of the building every day and never went in.

## Their reaction

*And they were **filled with wonder and amazement** at what had happened to him.*

The Greek is **thambos** and **ekstasis** — astonishment, and being put out of one's
place.

## What the man does

*And **the healed man kept hold of Peter and John**.*

The Greek is imperfect: he was holding on to them, continuously, and would not let go.

An hour earlier he had asked them for money.

## What Peter had said

*"Silver and gold I do not have, but what I do have I give you: **in the name of Jesus
Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk**."*

And then: *he took him by the right hand and lifted him up, and immediately his feet and
ankle bones received strength.*

## Where the crowd runs

*All the people came running to them in **the covered walkway called Solomon's
Colonnade**, utterly astonished.*

The same portico Jesus had been walking in at the Feast of Dedication. See the note on
John 10:22–26.

Two chapters later the whole church is meeting there: *they were all with one accord in
**Solomon's Porch**.*

## What Peter does with the crowd

Turns them away from himself in the first sentence:

*"Men of Israel, **why do you marvel at this? Or why look so intently at us, as though by
our own power or godliness we had made this man walk?**"*

And then: *His name, through faith in His name, has made this man strong.*

## What it costs them

That evening Peter and John are arrested, and spend the night in custody. Five thousand
men had believed by then.

> **Why Luke keeps the man in the shot.** He is standing next to Peter throughout the
> sermon, holding on. When the council convenes the next morning the record notes that
> *the man who had been healed was standing with them* — and there was nothing to say.
