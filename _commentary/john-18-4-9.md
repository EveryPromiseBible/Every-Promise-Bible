The arrest, and the man being arrested runs it.

## What He knew

*Jesus **knew everything that was coming toward Him**.*

John says it before anything happens. There is no ambush in his account.

The same phrase opens the foot-washing: *Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things
into His hands*.

## The direction of travel

*He **walked out to meet them**.*

The Greek is **exelthon** — went out. Toward the torches.

Matthew, Mark and Luke have Judas arrive and kiss Him. John has Him step forward first.

## Who came

A detachment of soldiers with the officers of the chief priests and Pharisees, *with
lanterns, torches, and weapons*.

The Greek for *detachment* is **speira** — a cohort. At full strength that is six hundred
men, and even a maniple is two hundred.

They brought lanterns to find a man who was waiting for them at full moon.

## The question

*"**Who are you looking for?**"*

He asks it, and He asks it twice.

## The answer

*"**Jesus of Nazareth**."*

## The reply

*"**I am He**."*

The Greek is **ego eimi** — two words, and the pronoun is not required by the grammar.

It is the sentence from the burning bush. See the note on Exodus 3:4–6. John has used it all
through the Gospel — *before Abraham was, I AM*, and *I am the bread*, *the door*, *the good
shepherd*, *the resurrection and the life*.

## The detail John inserts

*And Judas, **the man who was handing Him over, was standing there with them**.*

Not doing anything. Standing.

## What happened

*They **drew back and fell to the ground**.*

An armed cohort, on its face, in a garden.

John does not explain it and does not dwell on it. One sentence, and he moves on.

## The second asking

*So He asked them **again**, "Who are you looking for?" And again they said, "Jesus of
Nazareth."*

He waits for them to get up and starts the exchange over.

## The condition

*"I have already told you that I am He. **So if I am the one you want, let these men go**."*

A demand, from the accused, at the moment of arrest.

And it is granted. Eleven men walk out of that garden.

## Why John says it happened

*He said it so that **the word He had spoken would come true: Of those You gave Me, I have
not lost one**.*

From the prayer an hour earlier: *those whom You gave Me I have kept, and none of them is
lost except the son of perdition*. See the note on John 17:11–13.

John treats a sentence about physical safety in a garden as the fulfilment of a sentence
about eternal keeping. He makes no distinction between the two.

## What Peter does next

Draws a sword and takes off a man's ear. Jesus heals it, and says *put your sword into the
sheath. Shall I not drink the cup which My Father has given Me?*

The one disciple who fights is the one who denies Him within the hour.

## What He had said about it beforehand

*"**No one takes it from Me, but I lay it down of Myself**. I have power to lay it down, and
I have power to take it again."* See the note on John 10:16–18.

Six hundred men with torches, and a sentence that put them on the ground.

> **The one thing He asks for.** Not release, and not a hearing. *Let these men go* — and
> John files it as a promise being kept.
