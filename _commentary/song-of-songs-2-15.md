One verse, four lines, and it interrupts the loveliest passage in the book.

## What comes immediately before

*"Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away. For lo, **the winter is past, the rain is
over and gone**. The flowers appear on the earth; the time of singing has come, and the
voice of the turtledove is heard in our land."*

Spring, in the finest lines in the Song. And then, without transition, pest control.

## The command

*Catch the foxes for us.*

The Hebrew is **echezu** — seize, take hold of. Plural imperative: *you*, more than one
person.

Who is being told is not stated. The book has a chorus, and it may be them.

## The size

*The **little** foxes.*

The Hebrew is **shu'alim qetannim**.

The size is specified and it is the point. Nobody is worried about a wolf getting into the
vineyard; the verse is about the small ones that get through gaps.

## The damage

*That **spoil the vineyards**.*

The Hebrew is **mechabbelim** — ruining, corrupting, acting destructively.

Foxes in a vineyard do not eat the fruit primarily. They dig under the walls, gnaw the vine
roots and the tender shoots, and the damage is not visible for a season.

## The timing

*For our vineyards are **in bloom**.*

The Hebrew is **semadar** — the vine in blossom, before the grapes set.

That is the vulnerable moment. A blossom knocked off is a bunch that never exists.

The word appears three times in the Song and nowhere else in the Bible.

## What the vineyard has meant already

*My mother's sons were angry with me; they made me the keeper of the vineyards, but **my own
vineyard I have not kept**.*

Chapter 1, the bride's own words. She has already told us there is a vineyard she has
neglected.

## And what it means later

*My own vineyard is before me. **You, O Solomon, may have a thousand**.*

The last chapter, and the vineyard is hers to give.

## Isaiah's vineyard

*My Well-beloved has a vineyard on a very fruitful hill… He expected it to bring forth good
grapes, but it brought forth wild grapes.*

And the verdict: *for the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel.*

## The one Jesus tells

*A certain man planted a vineyard and set a hedge around it, dug a place for the wine vat
and built a tower.*

Every vineyard parable in Scripture has something getting in — animals, tenants, or the
owner's own son being thrown out.

## What the verse does not say

How. No method is given, and no list of foxes.

Four lines: catch them, they are small, they ruin vineyards, and the vines are in flower
right now.

## The verse after it

*My beloved is mine, and I am his. He feeds his flock among the lilies.*

The interruption lasts exactly one verse, and the poem picks up where it left off.

> **What is specified.** The size. Not the wolves, not the boar out of the wood — the little
> ones, at the exact moment the vine is in blossom and the fruit has not set.
