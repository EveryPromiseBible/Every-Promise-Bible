He has just gone up out of sight, and Luke's next sentence is about the walk home.

## The distance

*From the hill called the Mount of Olives, which is close to the city, **a Sabbath
day's walk away**.*

Roughly two thousand cubits — a little over half a mile. Luke measures it in the unit
a Jewish reader would use.

The ascension happened close enough to the city to walk back from before lunch.

## The room

*They went up to **the upstairs room** where they were staying.*

The Greek has the definite article — **the** upper room. Luke assumes his reader knows
which one.

It is where the supper was eaten, and it is where they are again. See the note on
Mark 14:12–16.

## The names

*Peter and James and John and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James
the son of Alphaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas the brother of James.*

Eleven. Luke lists every one by name.

The order has changed from the Gospel lists — Peter, James and John are moved to the
front, and Judas Iscariot is simply gone. No comment is made about the absence.

## What they were doing

*All of them **stayed together, praying with one heart and one mind**.*

The Greek is **homothumadon** — of one accord, one passion. Luke uses it repeatedly in
the early chapters of Acts and almost nowhere else in the New Testament.

They are waiting, because they were told to: *wait for the Promise of the Father.*

## The women

*Along with **the women**, and **Mary the mother of Jesus**, and **His brothers**.*

Luke, who opened his Gospel with Mary, mentions her here for the last time — in a
prayer meeting, named among the others, with no special place.

## The brothers

*And His brothers.*

In John's Gospel: *even His brothers did not believe in Him.* They mocked Him at the
Feast of Tabernacles and told Him to go show Himself to the world.

Between that verse and this one, the resurrection happened, and Paul records one
detail: *then He was seen by James.*

James goes on to lead the church in Jerusalem and write a letter that opens *James, a
bondservant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ* — without mentioning that they grew
up in the same house.

## How many

The next verse: *the number of names together was about **a hundred and twenty**.*

That is what was left, in one room, of three years of public ministry.

## What happens in that room

Ten days later: *suddenly there came a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind,
and it filled the whole house where they were sitting.* See the note on Acts 2:1–4.

> **What they had been told to do.** Not to organise, not to plan, not to preach yet.
> *Tarry in the city of Jerusalem until you are endued with power from on high.* Luke
> describes ten days of exactly that, and lists everyone who was there.
