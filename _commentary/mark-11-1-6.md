title: "Go and find the colt"

The errand before the entry, and it is described in more detail than the entry itself.

## The instruction

*"**As soon as you enter it you will find a colt tied there that no one has ever ridden.**"*

Stated as fact. Not *look for*, not *there may be* — **heuresete**, you will find.

*"They went and found the colt out in the street, tied at a doorway."*

**Mark** confirms each element in turn, and uses the same construction again three chapters later
about the upper room: *they went, and found it just as He had said to them.* See the note on
Mark 14:12–16.

## The unridden animal

*"**That no one has ever ridden.**"*

The detail matters twice over. An unbroken colt is not something you put a rider on in the middle of
a shouting crowd, and it is the specification for an animal used for sacred purposes: the red heifer
*on which a yoke has never come*, and the cart that carried the ark, drawn by *two milk cows on
which there has never come a yoke*.

*And he laid Him in **a tomb which had been hewn out of the rock, where no one had ever lain
before**.*

The animal He rides in on and the grave He is laid in are both described the same way.

## The password

*"And if anyone asks you what you are doing, say, '**The Lord needs it, and He will send it back here
shortly**.'"*

**Ho kyrios autou chreian echei** — the Lord has need of it.

It is the only recorded occasion **Jesus** says He needs anything. The one who owns the cattle on a
thousand hills sends two men to borrow a donkey, and the arrangement He makes is a loan with a
return date.

## What they say and what happens

*"They answered just as Jesus had told them to, **and the people let them take it**."*

No explanation of who arranged it. **Mark** does not say whether the owner was a disciple, and the
sentence works either way — either it was prearranged, in which case He had planned the route, or it
was not, in which case He knew the yard.

## What He is doing

*"Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion!… **Behold, your King is coming to you; He is just and having
salvation, lowly and riding on a donkey**, a colt, the foal of a donkey."*

Zechariah 9:9, and it is the one moment in the gospels **Jesus** arranges a public demonstration
about His own identity. Everywhere else in **Mark** He tells people to keep quiet.

A king entering a city on a horse is coming to fight. On a donkey, he is coming in peace.

## The week it opens

Nisan 10, by the usual reckoning: the day the Passover lamb was selected and brought into the
house, to be kept until the fourteenth. See the note on Matthew 21:12–17.

He rides in on the day the lamb comes in, and the next three days are spent being examined.

## What He does when He arrives

*And **Jesus** went into Jerusalem and into the temple. **So when He had looked around at all things,
as the hour was already late, He went out to Bethany** with the twelve.*

**Mark**'s anticlimax. The shouting, the branches, the road covered with clothes — and He walks into
the temple, looks at everything, and leaves because it is getting dark. The tables go over the next
morning.

> **The two words He uses.** *The Lord needs it.* It is the only thing in the gospels He is recorded
> saying He needs, and it is a borrowed donkey, returnable.
