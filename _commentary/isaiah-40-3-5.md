Road-building language, used of a coming that has not happened yet.

## What the image is

In the ancient world, when a king travelled, work parties went ahead to make the
route passable — filling in the dips, cutting down the high ground, straightening
what wandered.

Isaiah applies that to God arriving. The road is not being built *to* Him. It is
being made ready *for* Him to come.

## The two directions

*Let every valley be lifted up, every mountain and hill made low.*

Both movements aim at the same result — level ground — and both are stated in the
passive. The valley does not raise itself. The hill does not step down.

That levelling shows up all through Scripture as the way God deals with people:
the low lifted, the high brought down. Mary sings it in the Magnificat; James
says the humble are exalted. Here it is described as the preparation for glory
being seen.

## Who it is spoken to

This is the opening of the second half of Isaiah, addressed to a people in exile,
and it begins two verses earlier with *comfort, comfort my people*.

The road being described leads home.

## Where it turns up again

All four Gospels quote these verses of John the Baptist. Luke quotes the longest
stretch, and ends where Isaiah does — *all people together will see it.*

> **The last line.** *The mouth of the LORD has spoken.* Isaiah adds it as the
> guarantee. The chapter goes on to say that grass withers and flowers fall, but
> the word of our God stands forever — and this is one of the things that word
> has said.
