The opening line is a quotation, and it is a marching order.

## Where it comes from

*Whenever the ark set out, Moses would say, "**Rise up, O LORD! Let your enemies scatter; let
those who hate you flee before you**."* See the note on Numbers 10:33–36.

Said every time the camp broke and the ark went to the front of the column.

The psalm opens by quoting it, and the whole poem is about a procession.

## The verb

*Let God **rise up**.*

The Hebrew is **yaqum** — arise, stand up.

Moses' version is an imperative addressed to God. The psalm turns it slightly: *let God arise*.

## The two images

*As **smoke is blown away**, blow them away.*

*As **wax melts before the fire**, let the wicked melt away.*

Both are about substances that do not survive contact. Nothing is fought in either picture.

## The turn

*But **let the righteous be glad**, let them **exult** before God, let them be **jubilant with
joy**.*

Three verbs piled up, and the Hebrew intensifies each time.

The psalm's structure is set in the first three verses: one side dispersing, the other side
celebrating, and the celebration gets more words.

## What the psalm is

A processional, and probably one sung as the ark was carried up to Jerusalem — or re-enacted
annually.

It is one of the hardest psalms in the Psalter. The Hebrew is difficult, the imagery jumps, and
scholars have called it a collection of fragments.

## What is in it

*A father of the fatherless, a defender of widows, is God in His holy habitation. **God sets the
solitary in families**; He brings out those who are bound into prosperity.*

*You, O God, sent a plentiful rain, whereby You confirmed Your inheritance, when it was
weary.*

*Blessed be the Lord, who **daily loads us with benefits**.*

*Our God is the God of salvation; and to God the Lord belong **escapes from death**.*

## The line Paul quotes

*You have ascended on high, **You have led captivity captive**; You have received gifts among
men.*

Paul uses it in Ephesians, with one change: *when He ascended on high, He led captivity captive,
and **gave gifts to men**.*

Received in the psalm; gave in the letter.

And Paul draws the conclusion: *He who descended is also the One who ascended far above all the
heavens, that He might fill all things. And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets,
some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers.*

## The processional in the middle

*They have seen Your procession, O God, the procession of my God, my King, into the sanctuary.
The singers went before, the players on instruments followed after; **among them were the
maidens playing timbrels**.*

Singers, musicians, and girls with tambourines — which is what 2 Samuel 6 describes going up
into Jerusalem. See the note on 2 Samuel 6:12–15.

## How it ends

*Ascribe strength to God; His excellence is over Israel, and **His strength is in the
clouds**.*

*Blessed be God!*

## What the first verse assumes

That He is seated.

*Let God arise* only makes sense said to somebody sitting down, and Moses said it at the start
of every march.

> **Where the line comes from.** A marching order, said every time the ark moved. The psalm
> opens with it — which means the poem is written from the moment before anything has started.
