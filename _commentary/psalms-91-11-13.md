The best-known lines in the psalm, and the most famously misused.

## The charge

*He will **give His angels orders about you**, to guard you **in all your ways**.*

The Hebrew is that He will command them concerning you.

The angels are described as under instruction rather than acting on their own
initiative, and the assignment is specific to a person.

## The last clause

*To guard you **in all your ways**.*

That is the clause the devil left out.

In the wilderness he quoted verses 11 and 12 accurately — *He shall give His angels
charge over you… in their hands they shall bear you up, lest you dash your foot
against a stone* — and stopped before *in all your ways*.

Jumping off the temple is not one of a man's ways. It is a stunt, and the psalm's
protection is over a road being walked.

## What Jesus answered with

Not a correction of the psalm. *It is written again: you shall not tempt the LORD
your God* — Deuteronomy 6, about Massah, where Israel demanded proof that God was
among them.

He answers a real quotation with another real quotation.

## The hands

*They will **lift you in their hands**, so you will not strike your foot against a
stone.*

A stubbed toe. The smallest injury the psalm could have named.

Whatever the angels are doing, the passage puts them at ankle height.

## The four animals

*You will walk over **the lion and the cobra**; **the young lion and the serpent** you
will tread beneath your feet.*

Two pairs, and each pair has one that attacks openly and one that attacks hidden.

The lion is visible and loud; the cobra is underfoot and unseen. The same division as
verse 3 — the snare you cannot see and the plague you cannot hear.

## Where the treading turns up

*"I have given you authority to **walk right over snakes and scorpions**, and over all
the power of the enemy."*

Luke 10, to the seventy — and the sentence after it is *do not let your joy rest on
that. Let your joy rest on this: your names are written down in heaven.* See the note
on Luke 10:17–20.

And Genesis 3:15: *He shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise His heel.* The
serpent under a heel is where the Bible's first promise ends up.

## What the verbs are

Walk, and tread. Neither is a fight.

> **What the psalm is doing here.** Three voices run through it — a teacher describing
> the shelter, a believer confessing *You are my refuge*, and then God Himself in the
> last three verses. These lines are the teacher's, and the devil borrowed them.
