The second half of the oldest Christian hymn we have, and it turns on the first two words.

## What comes first

Seven steps down.

*Who, being in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal with God, but **made
Himself of no reputation**, taking the form of a bondservant, and coming in the likeness of
men.*

*And being found in appearance as a man, **He humbled Himself and became obedient to the point
of death, even the death of the cross**.* See the note on Philippians 2:5–8.

The Greek for *made Himself of no reputation* is **ekenosen** — emptied.

## The hinge

*Which is why.*

The Greek is **dio kai** — therefore also.

The exaltation is a consequence. The passage does not present it as a return to a status quo;
it presents it as an answer to the descent.

## The lifting

*God has **lifted Him to the highest place**.*

The Greek is **huperupsosen** — super-exalted. A compound, and it occurs once in the New
Testament.

Every verb in the first half has Him as the subject: He emptied, He took, He humbled. Every
verb in the second half has God as the subject.

He does the going down. Somebody else does the lifting.

## The name

*And given Him **a name that stands above every name**.*

The Greek is **to onoma to huper pan onoma**.

Which name is not stated in verse 9. It arrives in verse 11.

## The bowing

*So that at the name of Jesus **every knee should bow — in heaven, on earth, and under the
earth**.*

Three realms, and the list is exhaustive by design.

The line is Isaiah's, and in Isaiah it is God speaking of Himself:

*"I have sworn by Myself; the word has gone out of My mouth in righteousness, and shall not
return, **that to Me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall take an oath**."*

Paul applies it to Christ without argument, and quotes it of God in Romans 14.

## The confession

*And **every tongue should openly say that Jesus Christ is Lord**.*

The Greek is **kurios Iesous Christos**.

**Kurios** is the Greek Old Testament's standard translation of **YHWH**. That is the name
above every name — and it is the confession Paul names as the mark of belief: *if you confess
with your mouth the Lord Jesus… you will be saved*. See the note on Romans 10:9–13.

It was also the Roman loyalty formula. *Kaisar kurios* — Caesar is Lord.

## The destination

*To the glory of God the Father.*

The hymn does not end on the Son. Every knee bowing and every tongue confessing is described
as glory going somewhere else.

## Why Paul quotes it

Not to teach christology. To settle an argument between two women.

*I implore Euodia and I implore Syntyche to be of the same mind in the Lord.*

The passage is introduced by *let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus*, and
before that: *let nothing be done through selfish ambition or conceit, but in lowliness of
mind let each esteem others better than himself*.

The most exalted passage about Christ in Paul is deployed as an argument for not pushing to the
front.

## Where He is now

*Seated at the right hand of the Majesty on high.*

And *far above all principality and power and might and dominion, and every name that is
named*. See the note on Ephesians 1:20–23.

> **The two halves.** He empties, takes, humbles. God lifts and gives. Every verb in the
> descent has Him as its subject, and not one in the ascent does.
