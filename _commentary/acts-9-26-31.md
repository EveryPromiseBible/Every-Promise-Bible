key: Acts 9:26–31
title: "Barnabas took him"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The most famous convert in history arrives in Jerusalem and nobody will see him.

## The problem

*"**And when Saul had come to Jerusalem, he tried to join the disciples; but they were all afraid of
him, and did not believe that he was a disciple.**"*

**Epeirazen kollasthai** — he kept trying to attach himself. The imperfect: this went on.

They are not being unreasonable. The last time Jerusalem saw this man he was *breathing threats and
murder*, holding coats at a stoning and going house to house.

## The one who moved

*"**But Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles.**"*

**Epilabomenos** — having taken hold of him.

**Luke** has already told us what the man's name means: *Joses, who was also named Barnabas by the
apostles (which is translated Son of Encouragement).* **Huios parakleseos** — son of the coming
alongside, from the same root as the name for the Holy Spirit.

He is introduced in chapter 4 selling a field, and reappears here vouching for somebody nobody else
will stand next to.

## What he tells them

*"**And he declared to them how he had seen the Lord on the road, and that He had spoken to him, and
how he had preached boldly at Damascus in the name of Jesus.**"*

Barnabas tells Saul's story for him. **Luke** does not record Saul saying a word in the scene.

*Therefore **receive one another, just as Christ also received us**, to the glory of God.*

*"**The one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out.**"*

## What happens next

*"**So he was with them at Jerusalem, coming in and going out.**"*

And then he argues with the Hellenists, they try to kill him, and the brethren put him on a ship to
Tarsus. He disappears from **Acts** for years — until *Barnabas departed for Tarsus to seek Saul.*

The same man goes and finds him again.

## The verse that closes the section

*"**Then the churches throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and were edified. And
walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit, they were multiplied.**"*

**Te paraklesei tou hagiou pneumatos** — the encouragement of the Holy Spirit. The same noun as
Barnabas's name.

The persecutor is inside, the churches are at peace, and **Luke** attributes the growth to the
Comforter — in the paragraph where a man called Son of Encouragement took hold of somebody's arm.

> **What Barnabas does.** Takes hold of a man everybody else is frightened of, and tells his story
> for him. Luke gave us the meaning of the name five chapters earlier and does not repeat it here.
