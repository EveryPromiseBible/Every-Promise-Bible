Two lines, and the second one moves the ground.

## The first

*Happy the people **who have it so**.*

The Hebrew is **ashrei ha'am shekakhah lo** — blessed the people to whom it is thus.

*It* is the list in the verses before: tall sons, daughters like carved pillars, full barns,
multiplying flocks, no breach in the wall, no exile, no crying in the streets. See the note on
Psalms 144:12–14.

## The second

*Happy the people **whose God is the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **ashrei ha'am she-YHWH Elohav**.

The same opening word, and a different completion.

## The two readings

Both are old, and both are defensible.

One takes them as parallel: happy is the nation with all this, and it is happy because the LORD
is its God. Hebrew poetry works in couplets, and this is what couplets usually do.

The other takes the second as a correction: happy is the nation with all that — and happier
still the one with Him, whether or not the barns are full.

The Hebrew does not adjudicate. The word **ashrei** is repeated, which is what makes the second
reading available.

## The word

**Ashrei** — the word Psalm 1 opens with. *Blessed is the man who walks not in the counsel of
the ungodly.* See the note on Psalms 1:1–3.

It is the word behind the beatitudes in the Greek, and it means something closer to *happy* or
*fortunate* than to *holy*.

## What the psalm has been

A war song. *Blessed be the LORD my Rock, who trains my hands for war… bow down Your heavens, O
LORD, and come down.*

And the plea for rescue *from the hand of foreigners, whose mouth speaks lying words*.

The list of blessings is written by a man who is asking for deliverance, not reporting one.

## What that does to the last verse

It is not a description of his circumstances. It is what he is asking for, and then what he
falls back on.

## Where the same move is made

Habakkuk, at the end of his book: *though the fig tree may not blossom, nor fruit be on the
vines; though the labour of the olive may fail, and the fields yield no food; though the flock be
cut off from the fold, and there be no herd in the stalls — **yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I
will joy in the God of my salvation**.* See the note on Habakkuk 2:4–5.

Habakkuk lists the same agricultural items and removes them one by one.

## And Paul

*Not that I speak in regard to need, for **I have learned in whatever state I am, to be
content**… I know how to be abased, and I know how to abound.* See the note on
Philippians 4:11–13.

## The psalm before it

*The LORD is gracious and full of compassion, slow to anger and great in mercy. **The LORD is
good to all**, and His tender mercies are over all His works.*

Psalm 145, immediately after, and then the last five psalms are all praise.

The Psalter ends with *let everything that has breath praise the LORD*.

## What the verse does

Names the good life accurately, and then names something that does not depend on it.

> **The word repeated.** *Ashrei*, twice. The first happiness is a list of things a man can
> lose; the second completes the sentence differently.
