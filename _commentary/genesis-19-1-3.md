The same day, the second household, and Genesis writes it as a deliberate echo.

## The two who came on

*The **two angels** arrived at Sodom **in the evening**.*

Three men visited Abraham at noon. Two go on to Sodom by evening. See the note on
Genesis 18:1–5.

The chapter before had them leaving: *then the men turned away from there and went toward
Sodom, but Abraham still stood before the LORD*.

## Where Lot is

*And Lot was **sitting at the city gate**.*

The gate is the town's council chamber, its court and its exchange. Elders sat there; business
was transacted there; Boaz calls the ten elders to the gate to settle Ruth's case.

Sitting in it means position.

Chapter 13 had him pitching his tent *as far as Sodom*. Chapter 14 had him *dwelling in
Sodom*. Now he is in the gate. See the note on Genesis 13:5–13.

Three chapters, three stages.

## What he does

*When he saw them, **he got up to meet them and bowed with his face to the ground**.*

Exactly what Abraham did. Genesis uses nearly identical wording.

Whatever else is true of Lot, his instincts about strangers are his uncle's.

## The invitation

*"My lords, please **come to your servant's house. Wash your feet, spend the night**, and go on
your way early in the morning."*

The same offer, and one addition: *early in the morning*.

He wants them off the street, and he wants them gone before the town is up.

## The refusal

*"**No**," they answered, "**we will spend the night in the square**."*

The Hebrew is **barechov** — the open place, the same word Isaiah uses for where truth stumbles.
See the note on Isaiah 59:12–15a.

Two strangers proposing to sleep in the open square of that city.

## The insistence

*But he **urged them so strongly** that they went with him.*

The Hebrew is **vayyiphtsar-bam me'od** — he pressed them greatly.

He knows what will happen if they stay out, and the rest of the chapter proves him right.

## The meal

*He prepared a meal for them, **baking unleavened bread**, and they ate.*

The Hebrew is **matstsot** — unleavened.

The quickest bread there is. No time for dough to rise.

Abraham's meal took hours — a calf killed and dressed, cakes from three seahs of meal. Lot's is
flatbread, fast.

## What the two scenes have in common

A man at a doorway, getting up, bowing to the ground, pressing visitors to come in, and putting
food in front of them.

## What is different

Noon and evening. A tent flap and a city gate. Hours of cooking and unleavened bread.

And what happens next: Abraham gets an announcement about a son and a conversation about a
city. Lot gets a mob at the door.

Genesis sets the two households side by side across a chapter break and lets the reader do the
arithmetic.

## What happens that night

*Now before they lay down, the men of the city, the men of Sodom, both old and young, all the
people from every quarter, surrounded the house.*

And Lot goes out to them, shuts the door behind him, and offers them his daughters.

*And they said, "Stand back!… This one came in to stay here, and **he keeps acting as a
judge**."*

They name his position in the gate and use it against him.

## What Peter says about the man

*Righteous Lot, who was oppressed by the filthy conduct of the wicked… **day after day their
lawlessness scraped away at his soul**.* See the note on 2 Peter 2:7–9.

> **The one line that dates the drift.** *Lot was sitting in the gate.* Three chapters earlier
> he was pitching a tent in that direction, and Genesis records each stage without a word of
> comment.
