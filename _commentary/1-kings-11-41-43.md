The obituary of the wisest king, and the last clause is about somebody else.

## The reference

*As for everything else in Solomon's story — **all he did, and all his wisdom** — it is written down
in **the book of the acts of Solomon**.*

A source the writer of Kings had and we do not.

The book cites lost records repeatedly: the book of Jasher, the chronicles of the kings of Israel,
the chronicles of the kings of Judah.

## The length

*He reigned in Jerusalem **over all Israel for forty years**.*

The same as David. The same as Saul.

**Over all Israel** — which will not be true of his son for a single week.

## The death

*Then Solomon **lay down to rest with his ancestors**, and he was **buried in the city of David his
father**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyishkav im-avotav** — and he slept with his fathers.

The standard formula, used of kings who died peacefully.

## What is not said

Anything about his end.

Ecclesiastes and Proverbs are attributed to him, and the narrative of Kings gives no deathbed
scene, no repentance, and no last word.

David's death has a chapter of instructions. Solomon's has one sentence.

## The successor

*And **Rehoboam his son reigned in his place**.*

His mother was Naamah the Ammonitess — one of the foreign wives named in chapter 11. See the note
on 1 Kings 11:1–3.

The next king is the son of the problem.

## What Rehoboam does

Goes to Shechem, is asked to lighten the yoke, consults the old men and then the young men, and
answers *my father chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scourges*.

*So Israel departed to their tents.*

## The last clause

*The kingdom stood **cracked and waiting** — yet **the lamp for David still burned**, exactly as God had
said.*

The Hebrew idiom is **nir leDavid** — a lamp for David.

## Where the lamp comes from

*"**However I will not tear away the whole kingdom**; I will give one tribe to your son **for the sake
of My servant David**, and for the sake of Jerusalem which I have chosen."*

The reason given is not Solomon, or Rehoboam. It is a man who has been dead forty years.

## The promise behind it

*"And your house and your kingdom shall be established forever before you. **Your throne shall be
established forever**."*

Nathan to David, in 2 Samuel 7. See the note on 2 Samuel 7:12–16.

*"But My mercy shall not depart from him, as I took it from Saul."*

## Where the phrase recurs

*Nevertheless for David's sake the LORD his God gave him **a lamp in Jerusalem**, by setting up his
son after him.*

*Yet the LORD would not destroy Judah, for the sake of His servant David, as He promised him to
give **a lamp to him and his sons forever**.*

Three times in Kings, always at a low point, always naming David.

## Where the line runs

*The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, **the Son of David**, the Son of Abraham.*

*Solomon begot Rehoboam, Rehoboam begot Abijah…* See the note on
Matthew 1:1–3.

Matthew's list runs straight through Rehoboam. The lamp is the reason there is a genealogy.

## What Jesus says about Solomon

*"Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow… **even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed
like one of these**."* See the note on Matthew 6:25–34.

*"And indeed **a greater than Solomon is here**."*

> **The last clause.** The kingdom cracks, and the reason it does not go out is a promise made to
> the previous king, named by name, forty years after he died.
