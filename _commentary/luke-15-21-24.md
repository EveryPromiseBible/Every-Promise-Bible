The son has a speech ready. He worked it out in the pigsty, and it had three
parts: I have sinned against heaven, I have sinned against you, **make me one of
your hired men.**

Watch what happens to the third part.

## He gets two-thirds of the way through

*Father, I have sinned against heaven, and I have sinned against you. I am not
fit to be called your son —*

And the dash is doing real work. He is cut off. The father is already talking to
the servants about a robe. The line the son rehearsed hardest — the arrangement
he was going to propose, where he earns his keep and everyone's honour is
satisfied — never gets said.

Confession is welcomed. The bargain is not allowed to be spoken.

## What the father calls for, and why

Four things, and none of them are comfort items. Every one is a marker of
standing in that household:

- **the best robe** — literally the first robe, the one belonging to the head of
  the house
- **a ring** — a signet: authority to act in the family name
- **sandals** — servants went barefoot; sons did not
- **the fattened calf** — the animal kept for the one occasion big enough to
  need it

He does not get a bath and a probationary period. He gets dressed as a son in
front of everybody, before he has done a single thing to prove himself, and the
household is fed on the best animal on the property.

---

## "This son of mine was dead"

The father gives his reason twice, and it is not that the boy repented well:

> This son of mine was dead, and he is alive again. He was lost, and he is found.

Dead and alive. Lost and found. Those are the two pairs the whole chapter has
been building — the sheep, the coin, and now this. In all three the thing that
was missing does nothing to recover itself. It is carried, swept up, run to.

The son's repentance is real and it matters. But the story is careful about what
it accomplishes: it gets him home. It does not buy the robe.

## What it is doing in this chapter

Jesus is answering a complaint about the company He keeps. The answer is a father
who runs, interrupts an apology, and throws a party — and the point of putting
the interruption in is that the son's plan to work his way back was never on the
table.

> **On the celebration.** In all three stories the finder calls the neighbours
> together. The joy is not private, and it is never at the recovered thing's
> expense. Nobody in the chapter is welcomed home quietly and then reminded
> later what they cost.
