Some Greeks make a polite request, and it triggers the hour.

## Who they are

*"**Among those who had come up to worship at the festival there were some Greeks.**"*

**Hellenes** — Gentiles, God-fearers who worshipped at the feasts and could go no further than the
outer court. The court **Jesus** had cleared of livestock. See the note on John 2:13–17.

They are standing in the one part of the temple they are allowed into, asking to see Him.

## The request

*"**Sir, we would like to see Jesus.**"*

**Kyrie, thelomen ton Iesoun idein.**

They go to Philip — a Greek name, from Bethsaida in Galilee, a bilingual border town. He goes to
Andrew, also a Greek name, and the two of them go together.

Nobody in the passage records whether the Greeks got their meeting.

## The answer

*"**The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified.**"*

**John** has been saying *His hour had not yet come* since chapter 2. Here it arrives, and what
brings it is a request from Gentiles.

The gospel has structured it so: the whole world starts asking for Him, and He says the hour is
here.

*"**And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all peoples to Myself.**" This He said,
signifying by what death He would die.*

Nine verses later. The way they see Him is not an interview.

## What glorified means here

*"**Most assuredly, I say to you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies, it remains
alone; but if it dies, it produces much grain.**"*

The next sentence. Glory, in this gospel, is defined at the cross — *now is the Son of Man
glorified*, said in the upper room the moment Judas leaves.

**Menei monos** — it remains alone. The alternative to the death is not survival; it is solitude.

## Where it lands

*Therefore remember that you, once Gentiles in the flesh… **were at that time without Christ**… **But
now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ**.*

**Ephesians** 2, and **Paul** names the barrier: *He has broken down the middle wall of separation* —
the balustrade in the temple that these men were standing behind.

> **What the request sets off.** Gentiles ask to see Him and He announces the hour. The way they were
> going to see Him is stated nine verses later — *if I am lifted up* — and John adds that He meant
> the manner of His death.
