The most economical verse in the chapter, and it is a man doing nothing.

## The watching

*The man **watched her in silence**.*

The Hebrew is **mishtaeh lah macharish** — gazing at her, keeping silent.

**Sha'ah** — to gaze, and in this stem to be astonished. **Charash** — to be deaf, to hold one's
peace.

Two words for the same thing: he does not move and he does not speak.

## What she is doing meanwhile

Running back and forth to the well with a jar, watering ten camels off a desert crossing. See the
note on Genesis 24:17–20.

It takes a long time. He watches all of it.

## Why he does not help

Because the sign is not finished.

He had prayed for a specific sentence and a specific action, and she is in the middle of the
action. Interrupting would make the result his.

## The question he is holding

*Wondering **whether the LORD had made his journey succeed**.*

The Hebrew is **hahitzliach YHWH darko** — whether the LORD had prospered his way.

**Tsalach** — to advance, to prosper, to succeed. The verb in *whatever he does shall prosper*,
and in Joshua's charge: *then you will make your way prosperous, and then you will have good
success*. See the note on Joshua 1:6–9.

## The uncertainty

The narrator says he did not know.

She has said the words. She is doing the work. And the man is still watching, because a
coincidence and an answer look identical from the outside until the family is named.

## The gift

*When the camels had finished drinking, he took out **a gold nose ring weighing half a shekel and
two gold bracelets weighing ten shekels**.*

The Hebrew is **nezem zahav** — a gold ring, for the nose.

Ten shekels of gold in the bracelets, which is a substantial weight.

## The order

He gives the jewellery **before** he asks who she is.

The gift is not a payment for information, and it is not conditional on the answer.

## The two questions

*"**Whose daughter are you?** Tell me — **is there room in your father's house for us to spend the
night?**"*

Identity, then hospitality.

The first question is the one his whole errand depends on.

## The answer

*"I am the daughter of Bethuel, Milcah's son, whom she bore to Nahor."*

Nahor is Abraham's brother. Milcah is named in Genesis 22, in a short genealogy attached to the
end of the mountain: *Milcah has also borne children to your brother Nahor*.

That list exists so this moment has a name in it.

## What he does then

*Then the man **bowed down his head and worshiped the LORD**.* See the note on
Genesis 24:26–27.

The silence ends.

## What he says

*"**Blessed be the LORD God of my master Abraham**, who has not abandoned His kindness and
faithfulness to my master."*

**Chesed ve'emet** — the pair that becomes *grace and truth*.

## The word he uses of himself

*"**As for me, the LORD has led me on the very road** to the house of my master's relatives."*

Past tense, at a well, before the family has been met or the request made.

## What she does

*So the young woman ran and told her mother's household these things.*

The same verb she used with the water jar. She runs.

## The order of it

He gives the gold **before** he asks who she is.

Not as payment for the answer, and not held back until the answer is satisfactory. The
narrator is careful about the sequence: the camels finish, the jewellery comes out, and
*then* the two questions.

Nothing she says afterwards can lose it, because nothing she said beforehand earned it.

That is the order the chapter keeps: the wealth goes out ahead of the servant, and the bride
is loaded with the father's gifts before there is any agreement to load them against. See the
note on Genesis 24:1–4.

## The gift before the answer

He gives the gold, and then he asks who she is.

Not afterwards, not on condition, and not as a fee for the information. If she had turned out
to be nobody in particular, the bracelets were already on her wrists.

*But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that **while we were still sinners, Christ
died for us**.*

That is the same order and it is the whole difference. The gift is not a response to who she
turned out to be. It went out first, and her identity was discovered afterwards.

> **The verse.** A man standing still, saying nothing, while somebody else works — because the
> sign was not finished and completing it himself would have proved nothing.
