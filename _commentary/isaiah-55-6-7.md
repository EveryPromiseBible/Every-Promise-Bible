The last clause is not *He will forgive*. It is a doubled verb.

## The invitation

*Seek the LORD **while He may be found**. Call on Him **while He is near**.*

The Hebrew is **behimmatze'o** — in His being found. And **bihyoto qarov** — in His being near.

## What the two clauses assume

That there is a window.

Not that God moves, but that the seeking has a season. The chapter opens with a market call: *Ho!
Everyone who thirsts, come to the waters*. See the note on Isaiah 55:1–3.

## The instruction

*Let the wicked **leave his road behind**, and the man of evil **leave his thoughts**.*

The Hebrew is **ya'azov rasha darko** — let the wicked forsake his way. And **ish aven
machshevotav** — and the man of iniquity his thoughts.

## The pair

Way and thoughts. Conduct and the reasoning underneath it.

Both are named, and both are to be left.

## The turn

*Let him **turn back to the LORD**, and He will **have compassion on him**.*

The Hebrew is **veyashov** — and let him return. **Shuv**, the standard word for repentance: turn
round.

And **virachamehu** — and He will have compassion. From **rechem**, the womb.

## The second turn

*Turn back to our God, for **He will forgive again and again**.*

The Hebrew is **ki-yarbeh lislo'ach**.

## The construction

**Yarbeh** — He will multiply, make abundant. **Lislo'ach** — to pardon.

Literally: He will multiply to pardon. He will abundantly pardon.

## The verb

**Salach** — to forgive. In the Hebrew Bible this verb is used only of God.

No human being in the Old Testament ever **salach**-forgives anybody. The word is reserved.

## What comes next

*"For **My thoughts are not your thoughts**, nor are your ways My ways," says the LORD.* See the note
on Isaiah 55:8–9.

## What the *for* is doing

The famous verse is a *because*. It is attached to the sentence about pardon.

The gap between His thinking and ours is stated, in context, as a statement about how far He
forgives — not about the inscrutability of providence in general.

## The measurement

*"For **as the heavens are higher than the earth**, so are My ways higher than your ways."*

The same measurement Psalm 103 uses of **chesed**, in the verse before east and west. See the note
on Psalms 103:11–12.

## The illustration that follows

*For as the rain comes down… and **do not return there, but water the earth**… **so shall My word be**
that goes out of My mouth.* See the note on Isaiah 55:10–11.

Rain does not go back up unused.

## Where the chapter ends

*You shall go out with joy, and be led out with peace; **the mountains and the hills shall break
forth into singing**.* See the note on Isaiah 55:12–13.

## The three parables built on this

*"This man receives sinners and eats with them."* And the answer: a shepherd, a woman with a lamp,
and a father who runs. See the notes on Luke 15:3–7 and Luke 15:21–24.

> **The doubled verb.** Not *He will pardon* — **yarbeh lislo'ach**, He will multiply to pardon. And
> the verb for that kind of forgiving is never used of a human being.
