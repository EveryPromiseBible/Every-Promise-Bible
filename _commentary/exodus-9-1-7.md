The fifth plague, and the king checks the claim himself.

## The title

*"**The LORD, the God of the Hebrews**, says, let my people go."*

The Hebrew is **YHWH Elohei ha'Ivrim**.

**Ivri** — Hebrew. The word outsiders used of them. Pharaoh's court would recognise it.

## The demand

*Let my people go, **so they can worship me**.*

The fifth time. The same sentence, unchanged, at every plague.

## The threat

*"If you refuse and keep holding them, **the hand of the LORD will bring a severe plague on your
livestock in the field**."*

The Hebrew is **yad-YHWH hoyah** — the hand of the LORD is being.

## The list

*The **horses, donkeys, camels, herds, and flocks**.*

Five categories, itemised.

Horses meant chariots — the army. Donkeys and camels meant transport. Herds and flocks meant
food and wealth.

Egypt's economy, listed in one verse.

## The distinction

*"But **the LORD will make a distinction between the livestock of Israel and of Egypt**, and
**nothing that belongs to Israel will die**."*

The Hebrew is **vehiphlah** — He will distinguish.

The same verb as the fourth plague at Goshen. See the note on Exodus 8:20–24.

## The appointment

*The LORD **set a time**, saying, "**Tomorrow** the LORD will do this in the land."*

The Hebrew is **vayyasem YHWH mo'ed** — the LORD set an appointed time.

**Mo'ed** — an appointed meeting, the word used of the feasts and of the tent of meeting.

A plague with a diary entry.

## What happened

*The next day it happened, and **the livestock of Egypt died, but not one animal belonging to
Israel died**.*

## The inspection

*Pharaoh **sent men to look**, and sure enough, **not one of Israel's animals had died**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyishlach Paroh** — and Pharaoh sent.

He does not take it on report. He sends people to Goshen to count.

## What that establishes

The distinction is verifiable, and it was verified by the hostile party's own inspectors.

The narrative includes the audit.

## The response

*Yet **his heart stayed stubborn**, and he would not let the people go.*

The Hebrew is **vayyikbad lev Paroh** — Pharaoh's heart was heavy.

He has the evidence, confirmed by his own men, and it changes nothing.

## What the hardening does

Exodus uses three verbs across twenty occurrences: **kaved** (heavy), **chazaq** (strong), and
**qashah** (hard).

In the early plagues Pharaoh hardens his own heart. Later the text says God hardened it. Both are
stated, and the book does not reconcile them.

## Where the livestock issue returns

Four verses later, the sixth plague falls *on man and beast*, and the seventh kills animals left
in the field.

Egyptian livestock keeps appearing after this chapter says it died — which suggests *in the
field* in verse 3 is the limiting phrase, and animals under shelter survived.

## The gods

*"Against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment."*

The bull Apis, the cow Hathor, the ram Khnum. The plague on livestock falls on a pantheon with
animals in it.

## What Moses says at the seventh

*"But I know that **you and your officials still do not fear the LORD God**."* See the note on
Exodus 9:22–35.

> **The audit.** *Pharaoh sent men to look.* The distinction was checked by the opposing side's
> own inspectors, confirmed, and changed nothing.
