key: Philippians 4:13
title: "The most misquoted verse in the Bible"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
It is printed on running shoes and gym walls, and what it actually promises is better than what
they think it says.

## Read the two verses before it

*I have learned, in whatever state I am, **to be content**… I know how to be brought low, and I know
how to abound. Everywhere and in all things I have learned both to be full and to be hungry, both
to abound and to suffer need.*

That is the sentence *all things* refers to. Not accomplishments. Circumstances — and both
directions of them.

Paul says he had to **learn** it, and the word for learning the secret is **memyemai**, the term for
being initiated into a mystery. Contentment did not come with the conversion. It was taught to him,
in prisons and on ships and in a hungry week in Thessalonica.

## Both directions need it

Notice he names abundance as often as need. Plenty is not the neutral state that requires no grace;
it is the other test, and it has ruined more people than hunger.

So the verse promises strength for the day everything goes well and you are tempted to stop
needing Him, as much as for the day nothing does.

## The verb, and who is doing it

*I can do all things **through Christ, who pours His strength into me**.*

**Ischyo** — I am strong for, I have the strength for. And **en to endynamounti me** — in the One
who keeps on empowering me. A present participle: not a deposit made once, but a supply running.

Take Him out of the sentence and there is nothing left. Paul is not claiming capacity; he is naming
a source, and the whole force of the verse is in a prepositional phrase that the poster usually
crops off.

*My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.*

> **Better than the version on the wall.** The poster promises you can achieve anything. The verse
> promises something no achievement could give you: that you can be at rest with a full plate or an
> empty one, because the strength is not yours and does not rise and fall with your circumstances.
> Christ pours it in, and He does it in the lean week as steadily as in the good one.
