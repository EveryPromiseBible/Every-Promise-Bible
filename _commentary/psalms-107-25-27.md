The fourth of four rescue stories in the psalm, and this one is at sea.

## Who sent it

*He spoke, and **a storm wind rose up**.*

The psalm attributes the storm to a word. It does not describe weather that happened
to arrive.

## The motion

*Up to the sky they went, then **down into the depths**.*

The Hebrew is that they go up to the heavens and down to the deeps.

## What melts

*Their **courage melting** in the danger.*

The Hebrew is *their soul melted because of trouble* — the same verb used of hearts
melting before an army.

## The sailors

*They **reeled and staggered like drunk men**, and **every skill they had was swallowed
up**.*

The last clause is the point of the passage.

These are professionals — verse 23 says *they that go down to the sea in ships, that do
business in great waters.* Seamanship is what they have.

The psalm says their competence was swallowed. Not overwhelmed by fear; used up, and
insufficient.

## What they do

*Then they **cried out to the LORD in their trouble**, and He brought them out of their
distresses.*

That is the refrain, and it appears four times in this psalm — after the wanderers in
the desert, the prisoners in irons, the fools who were sick because of their
transgressions, and now the sailors.

Four different kinds of trouble, one sentence in the middle of each.

## The calm

*He **calms the storm, so that its waves are still**. Then they are glad because they
are quiet; **so He guides them to their desired haven**.*

The verse before this in the same psalm is the one the disciples had every reason to
recall on a lake at night. See the notes on Mark 6:45–52 and John 6:16–21.

*Immediately the boat was at the shore they had been heading for.*

## The refrain that follows each rescue

*Oh, that men would give thanks to the LORD for His goodness, and for **His wonderful
works to the children of men**!*

Also four times.

## How the psalm opens

*Give thanks to the LORD, for He is good… **let the rescued ones say it out loud**, the
ones He bought back.* See the note on Psalms 107:1–3.

The whole psalm is an instruction to people who have been got out of something: say
so.

## How it ends

*Whoever is wise will observe these things, and they will understand the
lovingkindness of the LORD.*

> **The four groups.** Lost in a desert, sitting in darkness and irons, sick and near
> the gates of death, and going down in a storm. The psalm gives each of them the
> same three lines: they cried, He brought them out, let them give thanks.
