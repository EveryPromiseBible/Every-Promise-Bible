The first description of the church, given in one sentence with four items in it.

## The context

Three thousand people were added that morning. See the note on Acts 2:40–41.

The city had a new congregation the size of a small town, converted in a day, with twelve
leaders and no building.

This paragraph is what they did next.

## The verb

*They **gave themselves steadily** to.*

The Greek is **proskarterountes** — persisted in, held fast to, continued steadfastly.

It is used of a boat kept ready for somebody, and of the disciples in the upper room devoting
themselves to prayer.

Not attended. Persisted.

## The first

*The **apostles' teaching**.*

The Greek is **te didache ton apostolon**.

Content, and it came from named people who had been there.

## The second

*And to **sharing life together**.*

The Greek is **koinonia** — fellowship, partnership, having in common.

Not sociability. The word is commercial as much as social — a **koinonos** is a business partner,
which is what Luke calls James and John in the fishing trade.

Verse 45 spells out what it meant in practice.

## The third

*And to **the breaking of bread**.*

Used in Acts of both the Lord's table and ordinary meals, and the two were not separated at
this stage. Verse 46 has them *breaking bread from house to house*.

## The fourth

*And to **prayer**.*

The Greek has the definite article — **tais proseuchais**, the prayers.

Which may mean the set hours. Peter and John go up to the temple *at the hour of prayer, the
ninth hour* two verses later.

## What followed

*A sense of **awe** came over everyone.*

The Greek is **phobos** — fear.

*And many **wonders and signs** were done through the apostles.*

The order in the paragraph is worth noticing: the four things first, then the awe, then the
signs.

## What the fellowship meant

*All the believers were together and **held everything in common**. They **sold their property
and their possessions** and shared out the money to anyone in need.*

The Greek is **hapanta koina** — all things common.

## What it was not

Not compulsory. Peter says so explicitly to Ananias a few chapters later: *while it remained,
was it not your own? And after it was sold, was it not in your own control?*

The selling was voluntary, and the lie was the offence.

And not permanent. It is described in Jerusalem, in this period, and Paul is taking a collection
for that same church twenty years later because it had run out.

## What is missing from the list

A building, a programme, a name, and an organisation.

Four practices, and Luke lists them in the order they occurred to him.

## The summary a few verses on

*So continuing daily with one accord in the temple, and breaking bread from house to house, they
ate their food with gladness and simplicity of heart, praising God and having favour with all
the people. **And the Lord added to the church daily those who were being saved**.* See the note
on Acts 2:46–47.

The adding is His, in Luke's grammar. Nobody in the paragraph is recruiting.

## What the apostles' doctrine was

*"**They continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine.**"*

The content is on record. **Luke** has just written the sermon out, and **Paul** gives the summary:

*For I delivered to you first of all that which I also received: **that Christ died for our sins
according to the Scriptures, and that He was buried, and that He rose again the third day**.*

*And with great power the apostles gave witness to **the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. And great
grace was upon them all**.*

Verse 33 of the next chapter's paragraph, and **Luke** puts the preaching and the grace in one
sentence.

## The breaking of bread

*For as often as you eat this bread and drink this cup, **you proclaim the Lord's death till He
comes**.*

## What produced the sharing

*"**They sold their possessions and goods, and divided them among all, as anyone had need.**"*

**Luke** does not present it as a rule. Two chapters later **Peter** says to Ananias *while it
remained, was it not your own?* — the giving was voluntary and the lie about it was not.

*For you know **the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He
became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich.*

**Paul** uses that sentence, in **2 Corinthians** 8, in the middle of an appeal for a collection.

> **The verb governing all four.** *Proskartereo* — persisted, held fast. Not attended, and not
> valued. Kept at.
