A taunt-song over the king of Babylon, and five sentences in the middle of it that
readers have always taken as describing something larger.

## What it is

The chapter introduces it: *you will take up this **proverb against the king of
Babylon**.*

A **mashal** — a taunt, a mocking poem. It is addressed to a human king.

And the language in these four verses goes beyond any human king, which is why the
church has read it as describing the power behind him.

## The name

*How you have fallen from heaven, **O shining one, son of the dawn**.*

The Hebrew is **helel ben-shachar** — shining one, son of the morning.

The Latin Bible rendered *helel* as **Lucifer**, light-bearer, and the name entered
English that way. It is a description of the morning star, not a proper name in the
Hebrew.

## The five "I will"s

*"**I will** ascend to heaven. **I will** lift my throne above the stars of God.
**I will** sit on the mount of assembly, in the far reaches of the north. **I will**
rise above the tops of the clouds. **I will** make myself like the Most High."*

Five, in a row, and each one goes higher than the last.

The last is the only one that names a person: *like the Most High.*

## What is missing

Any object outside himself. Every clause is *I* and a destination.

Set it beside the other great five-fold statement in Scripture — *behold, I have
come to do Your will, O God*, and *not what I want, what You want.* See the notes on
Psalms 40:6–8 and Luke 22:39–46.

Two sets of intentions, and they run in opposite directions.

## Where it ends

*"**Yet you are brought down to the grave, to the depths of the pit.**"*

Five ascents, and one sentence in reply.

The Hebrew is **Sheol**, and *the depths of the pit* — the lowest place named in the
poem, answering the highest.

## The other passage

Ezekiel 28 does the same thing with the king of Tyre: *you were the anointed cherub
who covers… you were perfect in your ways from the day you were created, till
iniquity was found in you.*

Two prophets, two pagan kings, and both oracles reach past the man in front of them.

## What Jesus says

*"**I watched Satan fall out of heaven like a bolt of lightning.**"*

Luke 10, to the seventy coming back excited that the demons obeyed them. He speaks as
an eyewitness. See the note on Luke 10:17–20.

## The morning star, taken back

*"I, Jesus… am the Root and the Offspring of David, **the Bright and Morning Star**."*

Revelation 22, and it is the last self-description in the Bible.

The title the shining one grasped at is claimed by somebody else at the end of the
book.

> **What the taunt-song is really about.** Verse 4 says it plainly: *the LORD has
> broken the staff of the wicked… the whole earth is at rest and quiet; they break
> forth into singing.* The poem is what the nations sing when an oppressor falls.
