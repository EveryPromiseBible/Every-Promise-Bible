Peter is on trial in Jerusalem for eating with Gentiles, and his defence is a report of
something he did not do.

## The situation

The believers in Judea had heard that Gentiles had received the word, and when Peter came
up to Jerusalem *those of the circumcision contended with him, saying, "You went in to
uncircumcised men and ate with them!"*

The charge is about a meal.

## How he answers

He tells the whole story again from the beginning — the sheet, the voice, the three men
at the door, the journey to Caesarea. Luke records the entire Cornelius account twice,
in chapter 10 and again here.

## The timing

*"**As I began to speak**, the Holy Spirit fell on them **just as He did on us at the
beginning**."*

He was interrupted. His sermon in chapter 10 stops mid-flow — Luke says *while Peter was
still speaking these words*.

Nothing had been decided about these people. Nobody had been baptised, circumcised, or
instructed.

## What he remembered

*"And I remembered what the Lord had said: '**John baptized with water, but you will be
baptized with the Holy Spirit**.'"*

Words spoken to him personally before the ascension, and he recognises the event as the
one they described.

## The logic

*"So if **God gave them the same gift He gave us** when we believed on the Lord Jesus
Christ, **who was I to stand in God's way?**"*

Two clauses. The premise is the identical gift; the conclusion is a question about his
own authority to object.

The Greek is *who was I, able to withstand God?*

## What is conceded

The **same gift**. Peter does not describe a lesser or preliminary version.

He says it again at the council years later: *God, who knows the heart, testified for
them **by giving them the Holy Spirit just as He gave Him to us**. He made **no
distinction at all**.* See the note on Acts 15:6–9.

## The silence

*When they heard this, **they had nothing more to say**.*

The Greek is *they kept silent*.

## The verdict

*And they praised God. "So then," they said, "**God has given the Gentiles too a change
of mind that leads to life**."*

**A change of mind** — **metanoia**, the same word Paul uses to the Ephesian elders. See
the note on Acts 20:17–21.

And it is described as **given**.

## What it settles and what it does not

It settles the Cornelius case. It does not settle the question — Acts 15 has the whole
church convening on the same issue, and Galatians 2 has Paul confronting Peter for
withdrawing from Gentile tables at Antioch.

The same Peter who said *who was I to stand in God's way* got up and moved to another
table when men came from James.

Luke reports the argument here; Paul reports the relapse.

## Where the chapter goes

Straight to Antioch, where men from Cyprus and Cyrene *spoke to the Hellenists, preaching
the Lord Jesus*, and *a great number believed and turned to the Lord*.

Barnabas is sent to look into it, goes to Tarsus to find Saul, and the two of them teach
there for a year.

*And the disciples were first called Christians in Antioch.*

> **What his defence rests on.** Not a decision he made or a policy he adopted. Something
> that happened while he was still talking, which he says was the same thing that had
> happened to them.
