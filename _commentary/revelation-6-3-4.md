The second seal, and the description is unusually specific about mechanism.

## The summons

*I heard **the second creature** say, Come and take a look.*

One creature per seal, and Revelation keeps the count.

## The horse

*And out came another horse, and **this one was red**.*

The Greek is **purros** — fiery red, flame-coloured. From **pur**, fire.

The same adjective is used of the dragon in chapter 12: *a great, fiery red dragon*.

## The permission

*Its rider **was given leave** to take peace away from the earth.*

The Greek is **edothe auto** — it was given to him.

The third passive in four verses. Nothing is seized. See the note on
Revelation 6:1–2.

## What is removed

*To **take peace away from the earth**.*

The Greek is **labein ten eirenen** — to take the peace.

Peace is described as a thing that had been present and is withdrawn, rather than as a condition
that simply fails.

## The result

*So that **people would slaughter each other**.*

The Greek is **allelous sphaxousin** — they should slay one another.

**Sphazo** is the sacrificial word. It is the verb used of the Lamb — *a Lamb as though it had
been **slain***.

The same verb, one chapter apart. One was killed; these kill each other.

## The weapon

*And **a huge sword was put in his hand**.*

The Greek is **machaira megale** — a great sword.

**Machaira** is the short sword, the soldier's or assassin's weapon rather than the long
Thracian **rhomphaia** John uses elsewhere.

Close work.

## The order in the discourse

*"You will hear of **wars and rumours of wars**. See that you are not troubled; for all these
things must come to pass, but the end is not yet."*

*"For nation will rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom."* See the note on
Matthew 24:4–8.

Second on the list there, and second here.

## What Luke's version adds

*"When you hear of wars and commotions, **do not be terrified**; for these things must come to
pass first, but the end will not come immediately."* See the note on
Luke 21:7–9.

Both Gospels attach the same instruction: do not be alarmed.

## The one before and after

White, then red, then black, then the corpse-coloured horse.

Deception, war, scarcity, death — and the third follows the second for the obvious reason.
Armies eat the harvest.

## The clause the passage keeps

*It was given.*

Whatever else the chapter is doing, it does not present the riders as autonomous. The seals are
broken by the Lamb, the creatures issue the summons, and the authority is handed over.

## What is not said

That God causes the slaughter.

The removal of peace is what is permitted. What people do with the absence is described in the
next clause, and the subject of that verb is *they*.

> **The verb for the killing.** *Sphazo* — the sacrificial word, used one chapter earlier of the
> Lamb. He was slain; these slay each other.
