Two short verses, and they say what the sacrifices actually accomplished.

## What they did

*Instead, those sacrifices **drag the sins back into memory, year after year**.*

The Greek is **anamnesis** — a calling to mind, a remembrance.

It is the word Jesus uses of the bread: *do this **in remembrance** of Me.* See the note
on Luke 22:19–20.

Two remembrances, of two different things.

## Why the annual repetition mattered

The writer's argument in the verses before is that a treatment which worked would have
stopped. See the note on Hebrews 10:1–2.

The Day of Atonement came round every year, which means the account was reopened every
year.

## The reason

*Because there is **no way that the blood of bulls and goats can take sins away**.*

The Greek is **adunaton** — impossible. The same word the writer uses of God lying, of
pleasing Him without faith, and of renewing an apostate. See the note on
Hebrews 6:4–6.

## The bluntness of it

He says it about a system God commanded, in Leviticus, in detail, with the species and
the handling of the blood specified.

He is not accusing the law of fraud. He is saying what it was for: *the law threw a
shadow of the good that was coming.*

## What the animals could not do

**Take away** — the Greek is **aphairein**, to lift off and remove.

They covered. The Hebrew **kaphar** is to cover over, and that is what the mercy seat did
— the lid over the tablets, with blood on it.

Covered is not the same as gone.

## What John the Baptist said

*"Behold! **The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!**"*

The Greek there is **airo** — to lift up and carry off. The same root the writer says the
bulls and goats could not do.

## Where the argument goes

Straight into Psalm 40 in the mouth of Christ — *sacrifices and offerings You did not
want, but a body You prepared for Me.* See the notes on Hebrews 10:5–7 and
Hebrews 10:8–9.

Then the priest who sat down, and Jeremiah's *their sins I will remember no more*. See
the notes on Hebrews 10:11–13 and Hebrews 10:15–17.

## The two rememberings, side by side

The old arrangement produced an annual reminder of sins.

The new covenant's terms are *I will remember no more*, and the meal instituted under it
is a remembrance of a person.

## Where the chapter's argument ends

*And where those have been forgiven, **there is no offering for sin left to make**.* See
the note on Hebrews 10:18.

> **What the sacrifices were for.** Not to clear the account. The writer says they
> reopened it, once a year, and that the impossibility was in the material.
