The third of five woes, and the sentence that ends it belongs to a different scale
entirely.

## What the book is

Habakkuk opens with a complaint: *O LORD, how long shall I cry, and You will not hear?*

God answers by saying He is raising up the Babylonians — which makes the prophet's
problem worse, and he says so: *You are of purer eyes than to behold evil… why do You look
on those who deal treacherously?*

The five woes in chapter 2 are the answer, pronounced over the empire that was about to
swallow Judah.

## The woe

*Sorrow to the one who **builds a town with blood** and **founds a city on wrongdoing**.*

Babylon was built by deported labour. The bricks of it are still stamped with
Nebuchadnezzar's name.

## The question

*Is it not from the LORD of hosts that **the peoples labor only to feed the fire**, that
**the nations wear themselves out for nothing**?*

Two clauses about wasted work. The Hebrew is *the people shall labour for the very fire,
and the nations shall weary themselves for nothing*.

Everything built with blood is described as fuel.

## The turn

*For **the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, as the waters
cover the sea**.*

The **for** connects it. The reason the empire's work comes to nothing is what is going
to fill the earth instead.

## The measure

*As the waters cover the sea.*

Not water on the sea. The sea is water — the covering is total and there is nothing else
there.

## What is filling it

*The knowledge of **the glory** of the LORD.*

Isaiah has nearly the same line: *the earth shall be full of **the knowledge of the LORD**
as the waters cover the sea* — at the end of the passage about the wolf and the lamb.

Habakkuk adds *the glory*.

## Where the woes go

Five in the chapter: to the plunderer, to the man who builds his house by unjust gain, to
the city built on blood, to the man who makes his neighbour drunk, and to the idol-maker.

And the last verse of the chapter: *but **the LORD is in His holy temple. Let all the earth
keep silence before Him**.*

## The verse the chapter is famous for

*Behold the proud, his soul is not upright in him; but **the just shall live by his
faith**.*

Verse 4, eight verses before this. Paul quotes it in Romans and Galatians, and Hebrews
quotes it too — the sentence the Reformation was built on comes out of a book about
Babylon.

## How the book ends

*Though the fig tree may not blossom, nor fruit be on the vines… **yet I will rejoice in
the LORD**, I will joy in the God of my salvation.*

Habakkuk lists six failed harvests and then says that.

He got no answer to his original question. He got this.

> **The scale of the sentence.** It sits inside a woe over one empire, and it is about the
> whole earth — and what fills it is not power or peace but the knowledge of the glory.
