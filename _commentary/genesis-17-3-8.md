God changes a man's name to a claim that is not yet true, and makes him answer to
it.

## Where he is standing

Ninety-nine years old. Twenty-four years since the promise was first made. One son,
Ishmael, by a servant girl, and none by Sarah.

*Abram fell facedown.*

## The two names

**Abram** — exalted father. That name was already a stretch.

**Abraham** — *ab hamon*, father of a multitude.

*"No longer will you be called Abram; your name will be Abraham, **for I have made
you the father of many nations**."*

## The tense

*I **have made** you.* Past tense, completed, spoken to a childless man.

Not *I will make you*, though the promises around it are future — *nations will
come from you, kings will be among your descendants*. This one clause is put in the
past, and it is the clause the name is built on.

Paul quotes exactly this line in Romans 4 as his example of *the God who calls
things that do not exist as though they already do.* See the note on
Romans 4:16–17.

## What it cost him to carry

Every person in that camp — servants, herdsmen, his wife, the neighbours he traded
with — now had to call him *father of a multitude*.

For a year. Isaac is not born until chapter 21.

A man of ninety-nine, with no child by his wife, introducing himself by that name
for a full year. Every use of it was either a joke or an act of faith, and Genesis
records no complaint from him about it.

## Sarai too

A few verses on: *As for Sarai your wife, you shall not call her name Sarai, but
**Sarah** shall be her name.*

**Sarah** means princess. *And I will bless her, and she shall be a mother of
nations; kings of peoples shall be from her.*

Ninety years old, barren her whole life, and renamed princess and mother of
nations.

## The word "everlasting"

It appears three times in these six verses — an **everlasting** covenant, an
**everlasting** possession, and *I will be their God*.

The Hebrew is **olam** — perpetual, without a stated end.

## What Abraham does with it

*Abraham fell facedown **and laughed**.* That is verse 17, and Genesis reports it
without softening.

He laughs, Sarah laughs in the next chapter, and the boy is named **Isaac** — *he
laughs*. God took the laughter and made it the child's name.

> **What is not asked of him here.** Nothing. The whole passage is *I will* — I will
> make, I will keep, I will give, I will be their God. Circumcision comes in the
> next paragraph as the sign of a covenant already made, not a condition of getting
> one.
