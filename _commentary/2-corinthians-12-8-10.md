Paul asks three times and gets an answer that is not the one he asked for.

## Three times

*Three times I begged the Lord about this, **that it might leave me**.*

He asked for removal. He was specific, and he was refused.

Three times is also what happened in Gethsemane: *He went away again a second time
and prayed… so He left them, went away again, and prayed the third time.*

## What it was

*A thorn in the flesh* — the Greek is a stake, a splinter — and *a messenger of Satan
to buffet me.*

Paul says where it came from: a messenger of Satan. He does not call it God's gift.

And he says what it was doing: the Greek for *buffet* is to strike with the fist,
repeatedly.

What it was exactly, nobody knows. Every candidate has been proposed and the text
does not say.

## The answer

*"**My grace is enough for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.**"*

The Greek is present tense and continuous — *My grace is sufficient*, and goes on
being.

*Made perfect* is **teleitai** — brought to its completion, brought to full
expression. Not that weakness improves anything about the strength. That weakness is
where it comes fully into view.

## What Paul does with the refusal

*So I will **boast all the more gladly** in my weaknesses, so that **the power of
Christ may come to rest upon me**.*

The Greek for *rest upon* is **episkenoo** — to pitch a tent over, to tabernacle
upon. It is the verb behind the Word *dwelling* among us in John 1.

He asked for the thorn to leave. He got a tent pitched over him instead.

## The list

*Weaknesses, insults, hardships, persecutions, times when everything is pressed
tight.*

Five, and every one of them is something happening to him rather than something he
arranged.

*I take pleasure in* them — and the phrase attached is *for the sake of Christ*,
which is the only thing that makes the sentence anything other than strange.

## The last line

*For **when I am weak, then I am strong**.*

Not despite. **When.**

## Where the chapter started

With the third heaven, and *words beyond all speaking* he was not permitted to
repeat, fourteen years earlier and never mentioned until now. See the note on
2 Corinthians 12:1–4.

The thorn is introduced in the same paragraph as the revelation, and Paul links
them: *lest I should be exalted above measure.*

The greatest experience of his life and the standing affliction sit on the same
page, and the sentence between them is *My grace is enough for you.*

> **What he had already said about it.** *We have this treasure in earthen vessels,
> that the excellence of the power may be of God and not of us.* The clay pot is not
> an apology in this letter. It is the argument.
