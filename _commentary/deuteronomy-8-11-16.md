The danger Moses names is not poverty. It is a full stomach.

## The warning

*Be careful **not to forget the LORD your God**.*

The Hebrew verb for forgetting runs through the chapter. It appears three times, and it
is the chapter's whole subject.

## The list of conditions

*When you have **eaten your fill**, and **built fine houses** to live in, and your **herds
and flocks have grown large**, and your **silver and gold have multiplied**, and
**everything you own has increased**.*

Five clauses, and every one of them is a blessing. Nothing on the list is a sin.

They are the exact things promised a few chapters earlier.

## The moment of danger

*Then **watch your heart**.*

Not during the wilderness. After the houses are built.

## The mechanism

*Do not let it **swell with pride**, so that **you forget the LORD your God**.*

The Hebrew is *lest your heart be lifted up*.

Pride first, forgetting second. Moses gives the order.

## What is forgotten

*The One who **brought you out of Egypt, out of the house of slavery**.*

The identity given is the rescue. Forgetting Him means forgetting what state they were
found in.

## The wilderness, described

*He led you through **that vast and terrible wilderness**, with its **venomous snakes and
scorpions**, its **thirsty ground where there was no water**.*

Moses does not romanticise it. He calls it terrible and lists what was in it.

## What God did in it

*He **brought water for you out of the hard rock**.*

The rock at Rephidim and the rock at Kadesh. See the notes on Exodus 17:4–7 and
Numbers 20:10–11.

*He **fed you in the wilderness with manna your fathers had never known**.*

Neither is a thing they could have arranged.

## The purpose

*Humbling you and testing you, **all for your good in the end**.*

The Hebrew is *to do you good at your latter end*.

Moses names the purpose of the hardship as their benefit, and puts it at the end of the
sentence rather than the middle.

## What comes next

*Then you say in your heart, "**My power and the might of my hand have gained me this
wealth**."*

*And you shall **remember the LORD your God, for it is He who gives you power to get
wealth**.*

Both sentences are in the same paragraph. The forgetting produces a specific claim, and
the remedy is a specific correction.

## What the earlier part of the chapter says

*He humbled you, allowed you to hunger, and fed you with manna… **that He might make you
know that man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the
mouth of the LORD**.*

Jesus quotes that verse in the wilderness, to the first temptation, about bread.

## The other warning like it

*Great and good cities which you did not build, houses full of all good things which you
did not fill… **then beware, lest you forget the LORD**.*

Joshua uses the same list when he stands the nation at Shechem. See the note on
Joshua 24:11–13.

> **What is dangerous in the chapter.** Not the snakes and not the thirst. Moses gets
> through those in one verse and spends the rest of the paragraph on the houses.
