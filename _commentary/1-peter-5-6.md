One sentence, with two hands in it.

## The command

*So **get low under God's strong hand**.*

The Greek is **tapeinothete oun hupo ten krataian cheira tou Theou**.

**Tapeinoo** — to make low, to humble. And it is passive: *be humbled*, or *let yourself be
brought low*.

## The hand

**Krataia cheir** — the mighty hand.

The Septuagint's standard phrase for the exodus: *the LORD brought you out of Egypt with a mighty
hand and an outstretched arm*.

The hand that is above them is the one that got a nation out of slavery.

## Why *under*

Not *before*, or *toward*.

The position is beneath, which is where a hand can either press or lift.

## What comes before

*Likewise you younger people, submit yourselves to your elders. Yes, all of you be submissive to
one another, and **be clothed with humility**, for "God resists the proud, but gives grace to the
humble."*

Proverbs 3:34, quoted in the Septuagint's wording — the same verse James quotes. See the note on
James 4:4–6.

## The word for clothed

**Enkomboomai** — to tie on, as a slave tied an apron over his clothes for work.

Peter had watched somebody do exactly that in an upper room, with a towel.

## The promise

*And **He will lift you up**.*

The Greek is **hupsose** — will exalt, will raise high.

## Who does it

He does.

The command is to go down. The lifting is somebody else's action, and Peter does not make it
conditional on anything except the going down.

## The timing

*When **the moment is right**.*

The Greek is **en kairo** — in due time, at the right season.

**Kairos** — the appointed moment, not measured duration.

Peter does not say how long. He says there is a moment and it is not the reader's to schedule.

## Who is writing

The man who said *even if I have to die with You, I will not deny You*, and did.

And who was told *when you are old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will gird you
and carry you where you do not wish*.

## What follows immediately

*Casting all your care upon Him, **for He cares for you**.*

The Greek is **epirripsantes** — having thrown upon. The verb used of the disciples throwing their
garments on the colt.

The participle attaches to the humbling: getting low and throwing the anxiety over are one
action, not two.

## And then

*Be sober, be vigilant; because **your adversary the devil walks about like a roaring lion**, seeking
whom he may devour.* See the note on 1 Peter 5:8.

*Resist him, steadfast in the faith, knowing that the same sufferings are experienced by your
brotherhood in the world.*

## The last promise in the letter

*But may **the God of all grace**, who called us to His eternal glory by Christ Jesus, after you have
suffered a while, **perfect, establish, strengthen, and settle you**.*

Four verbs, and *after you have suffered a while* is inside the sentence.

## What Jesus said

*"For **whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted**."*

Both verbs passive, in Luke's account of the dinner-party seating. See the note on
Luke 14:7–11.

> **The direction.** The instruction is to go down. The lifting is in the passive, and the timing
> is **kairos** — a moment, not a schedule.
