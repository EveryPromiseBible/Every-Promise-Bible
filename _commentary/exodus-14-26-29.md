The same gesture that opened the sea closes it.

## The instruction

*"**Stretch out your hand over the sea**, so the waters flow back over the Egyptians."*

The Hebrew is **neteh et-yadecha al-hayyam**.

Word for word what he was told to do to open it. See the note on
Exodus 14:21–25.

## The symmetry

One gesture, twice. Once to open, once to close.

The staff does not do two different things. The same hand over the same water, and the water does
what it is told both times.

## The timing

*Moses stretched out his hand, and **at daybreak the sea returned to its place**.*

The Hebrew is **liphnot boqer** — at the turning of the morning.

All night the wind held it open. At dawn it goes back.

## The word

**Le'etano** — to its strength, to its normal state.

The sea is described as returning to what it usually is. The parting was the exception.

## The direction

*As the Egyptians **fled toward it**, the LORD swept them into the sea.*

The Hebrew is **nasim liqrato** — fleeing to meet it.

They are running away from the water and the water is coming to them. The escape route is the
trap.

## The verb

**Vayna'er** — He shook off, He tossed.

The word for shaking dust out of a garment. The Song of the Sea uses it too.

## The completeness

*The waters returned and covered the chariots and horsemen — **the whole army of Pharaoh that had
followed Israel into the sea**.*

*Not one of them survived.*

The Hebrew is **lo-nish'ar bahem ad-echad** — there did not remain of them even one.

## The qualification

*That had followed Israel into the sea.*

The narrator limits the claim. What is destroyed is the pursuing force, not everybody in Egypt.

## The contrast

*But **the Israelites had walked through the sea on dry ground**, a wall of water on their right and
on their left.*

The Hebrew repeats the phrase from verse 22 exactly.

Same sea, same night, same water. One nation walks through on dry ground; the other does not come
out.

## What they see in the morning

*So the LORD saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians, and **Israel saw the Egyptians
dead on the seashore**.* See the note on Exodus 14:30–31.

The bodies wash up on the side they are standing on.

## The result

*Thus Israel saw the great work which the LORD had done in Egypt; so the people **feared the LORD,
and believed the LORD and His servant Moses**.*

## What they sing

*I will sing to the LORD, for He has triumphed gloriously! **The horse and its rider He has thrown
into the sea**.* See the note on Exodus 15:1–5.

The first song in the Bible, sung with the wreckage in sight.

## What happens three days later

*For three days they traveled through the wilderness and found no water.*

*So the people grumbled against Moses.* See the note on Exodus 15:22–27.

## What Paul says about it

*All our fathers were under the cloud, all passed through the sea, all were baptized into Moses in
the cloud and in the sea.*

*But with most of them God was not well pleased, for their bodies were scattered in the
wilderness.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 10:1–5.

> **The symmetry.** The same hand over the same water, twice — and the narrator uses the identical
> phrase for Israel walking on dry ground in both halves of the account.
