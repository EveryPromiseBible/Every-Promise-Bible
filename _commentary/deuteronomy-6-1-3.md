The introduction to the Shema, and it names three generations before it says anything else.

## What the book is

**Deuteronomy** — from the Greek for *second law*. Moses' final speeches on the plains of
Moab, to the generation that grew up in the wilderness.

Everyone who heard the law at Sinai is dead except Caleb and Joshua. These people were
children then, or not born.

## The three words

*Here are **the commands, the laws, the rulings**.*

The Hebrew is **mitzvah**, **chuqqim**, **mishpatim** — commandment, statutes, judgments.

The three categories recur through the book. Roughly: the charge, the decrees, and the case
law.

## The destination

*Meant for **the land you are crossing over to possess**.*

The law is given for a place they are not in yet.

## The three generations

*Live this way, **you and your children and your children's children after them**.*

Named in the first sentence.

Nothing in the arrangement assumes it will be caught automatically. It has to be handed down
twice to reach the third name in the list.

## The posture

*Holding the LORD **in awe all the days of your life**.*

The Hebrew is **yare** — to fear, to revere.

## The result

*And **your days will be long**.*

The promise attached to the fifth commandment, applied here to the whole.

## The address

*Listen then, Israel.*

The Hebrew is **shema Yisrael** — the same two words that open the next paragraph.

Verse 3 begins it, and verse 4 is the sentence Israel has said twice daily ever since. See the
note on Deuteronomy 6:4–5.

## The instruction

*And **be careful to live it out**.*

The Hebrew is **shamarta la'asot** — take care to do.

Hearing and doing, joined in one clause. Hebrew has no separate word for *obey*; **shema**
does both jobs.

## The promises

*So that **all may go well with you** and you may **increase greatly**.*

## The description

*A land **flowing with milk and honey**.*

The Hebrew is **zavat chalav udevash**.

Milk means pasture — enough grass for herds. Honey means either wild honey or date syrup, and
either way it means flowering, fruitful country.

The phrase occurs about twenty times in the Pentateuch. Its first use is at the burning bush,
in the sentence where Moses is sent: *I have come down to deliver them… to a land flowing with
milk and honey*. See the note on Exodus 3:4–6.

Forty years between the first use and this one, and the same man is saying it.

## What comes after the Shema

*"And these words which I command you today shall be in your heart. **You shall teach them
diligently to your children**, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, when you walk
by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up."* See the note on
Deuteronomy 11:18–21.

The Hebrew for *teach diligently* is **shinnantam** — from a root meaning to sharpen, to
whet. Repeat until it has an edge.

And the four settings cover a whole day: sitting, walking, lying down, getting up.

## The warning that follows both

*"Then beware, lest you forget the LORD who brought you out of the land of Egypt."* See the
note on Deuteronomy 6:10–12.

The danger named is not persecution. It is a full house.

> **Who is in the first sentence.** Three generations. The instruction is written from the
> start on the assumption that it has to be handed on twice to get where it is going.
