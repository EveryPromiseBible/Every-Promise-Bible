Seventy men come back thrilled, and He redirects the excitement without dampening
it.

## What they say

*"Lord, **even the demons do what we say when we use Your name**."*

The Greek is emphatic — *even the demons*. It is more than they expected.

He had sent them out to heal the sick and say *the kingdom of God has come near to
you.* He had not mentioned demons.

They came back with something they were not promised.

## "In Your name"

They are careful about the mechanism. Not *we cast out demons* — *when we use Your
name.*

## What He says

*"**I watched Satan fall out of heaven like a bolt of lightning.**"*

The Greek is imperfect — I was watching. He speaks as somebody who was present.

**Like lightning** — sudden, visible, and from above to below.

## The authority

*"I have **given you authority** to walk right over snakes and scorpions, and over
**all the power of the enemy**, and nothing at all will harm you."*

The Greek for *authority* is **exousia** — delegated right, permission. It is given,
not developed.

*Snakes and scorpions* echoes Psalm 91: *you shall tread upon the lion and the cobra…
you shall trample underfoot.* See the note on Psalms 91:11–13.

## The redirection

*"**But do not let your joy rest on the fact that the spirits obey you.**"*

He does not deny it happened, correct their report, or warn them about pride.

He tells them what to be glad about.

## What to be glad about instead

*"**Let your joy rest on this: your names are written down in heaven.**"*

The Greek is perfect tense — have been written, and stand written.

Ministry success is variable. Some days the demons come out and some days they do
not; Jesus' own disciples failed at it in Matthew 17.

The name in the book does not fluctuate, and it was written before any of them cast
out anything.

## The book

The image runs through Scripture — *blot me out of Your book*, Moses said; *the book
of life*, in Philippians, Daniel and Revelation.

## What He does next

*In that hour Jesus **rejoiced in the Spirit** and said, "I thank You, Father… that
You have hidden these things from the wise and prudent and revealed them to babes."*

Luke records Jesus rejoicing exactly once, and it is here, immediately after this
conversation.

> **Who the seventy were.** Not the twelve. An unnamed larger group, sent out two by
> two ahead of Him, given no money, no bag and no sandals, and told *he who hears you
> hears Me.* They came back with authority over the enemy and were told to be glad
> about something else.
