An old man in Babylon is reading, and what he reads sends him to his knees for the rest
of the chapter.

## The date

*In the **first year of Darius** son of Ahasuerus, a Mede by descent, who was made ruler
over the kingdom of the Babylonians.*

Babylon has just fallen. The handwriting on the wall was last chapter; Belshazzar was
killed that night.

Daniel was a teenager when he was deported. He is in his eighties now.

## What he was doing

*I, Daniel, **understood from the Scriptures**.*

The Hebrew is *I understood by the books*.

He is not having a vision. He is reading, and the visions in this book come to a man
who was already in the text.

## What he was reading

*From **the word the LORD had given to Jeremiah the prophet**.*

Jeremiah's letter to the exiles — the one with *I know the thoughts that I think toward
you* in it — is addressed to Daniel's generation.

Jeremiah was alive within Daniel's lifetime. This is nearly contemporary writing being
treated as Scripture, in Scripture.

## The seventy years

*That the desolation of Jerusalem was to last **seventy years**.*

Jeremiah 25 and 29 both give the figure. Daniel does the arithmetic and works out that
the term is nearly up.

## What he does about it

The next verse: *then I set my face toward the Lord God to make request by prayer and
supplications, with fasting, sackcloth and ashes.*

He has just discovered that the thing is promised, dated and about to happen — and that
is what makes him pray.

Not the reverse.

## What the prayer is like

Twelve verses, and almost all of it is confession — *we have sinned and committed
iniquity, we have done wickedly and rebelled.*

He says **we** throughout. Daniel is the one man in the book with nothing recorded
against him, and he puts himself inside the sentence every time.

## What it is asked on

*Not because of our righteous deeds, but **because of Your great mercy**.*

He states the ground of the request explicitly, and it is not the record of the people
he is praying for.

## The answer

*While I was still speaking and praying… **Gabriel** came to me in swift flight.* See the
note on Daniel 9:20–21.

The reply was dispatched before the prayer was finished.

## What happened in history

Cyrus's decree, in Ezra 1, releases the exiles to go home and rebuild the house — *that
the word of the LORD by the mouth of Jeremiah might be fulfilled.*

Ezra names the same prophecy Daniel was reading.

> **What he did with a promise that was certain.** He prayed about it — for three
> weeks, in sackcloth. The prophecy's certainty is the reason given in the text for the
> praying, not an alternative to it.
