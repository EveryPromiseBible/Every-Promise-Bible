An army is coming, and the one thing Israel does about it is kill a very small
lamb.

## What Samuel offered

*So Samuel took a **nursing lamb** and offered it **whole** as a burnt offering to
the LORD.*

Not a bull, not a ram, not the seven bullocks that a national emergency might
seem to call for. A lamb still on its mother's milk — the smallest offering a man
could bring.

And *whole* — the Hebrew is the word for entire, offered complete, nothing kept
back and nothing eaten.

## The timing

*While Samuel was offering the sacrifice, the Philistines drew near to attack.*

The narrator puts the two clauses in the same sentence on purpose. The enemy is
closing while the lamb is on the altar.

Nobody is armed. Nobody has taken a position. Israel is at worship with an army
walking toward them.

## Who fought

*But that day the **LORD thundered** with a mighty voice against the Philistines
and threw them into such confusion that they were routed before Israel.*

The Hebrew for *confusion* is the panic-word — the same one used at the Red Sea
and against the Canaanite kings under Joshua. It is a specific act of God
recorded in a specific verb.

Israel's part comes second: *the men of Israel rushed out… and chased them.* They
pursue a battle that has already been won over an altar.

## The small thing

The size of the lamb and the size of the deliverance are not proportioned to each
other, and the chapter does not apologise for it.

What the offering did was proclaim a death. Everything after the thunder is
Israel walking into ground the sacrifice had already taken. See the note on
1 Corinthians 1:26–31 on what God chooses to confound the strong with.

> **What Samuel set up afterwards.** A stone, named **Ebenezer** — *thus far the
> LORD has helped us.* A rock in a field, with no power in it at all, put there so
> that people walking past would have to remember what happened. The chapter ends
> with a memorial as small as the lamb it commemorates.
