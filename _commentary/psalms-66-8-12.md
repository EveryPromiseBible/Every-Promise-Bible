Every verb in the hard middle of this psalm has God as its subject.

## The call

*Bless our God, **O peoples**, let the sound of His praise be heard.*

The Hebrew is **ammim** — peoples, plural. The nations, not Israel alone.

## The first mercy

*He has **kept our souls among the living**, and has **not let our feet slip**.*

The Hebrew is **hassam nafshenu bachayyim** — the one placing our life among the living.

And **velo-natan lammot raglenu** — He has not given our foot to slipping.

## The turn

*For **You tested us**, O God.*

The Hebrew is **bechantanu** — You have examined us. A metallurgical word.

## The refining

*You **refined us like silver in the fire**.*

The Hebrew is **tzeraftanu kitzraf-kasef**.

**Tzaraph** — to smelt, to purify metal by heat. The refiner sits by the crucible; the process is
watched, not left.

## The three hard lines

*You brought us **into the net**.*

*You laid **heavy burdens on our backs**.*

*You let **men ride over our heads**.*

The Hebrew is **metzudah** — a net or trap. **Mu'aqah** — pressure, constraint, on the loins.
**Hirkabta enosh lerosheinu** — You caused men to ride at our head.

## The image

A defeated army on the ground and cavalry going over them.

## Who is the subject

God, in all three lines.

The psalm does not say the enemy did these things while God looked away. It puts every verb on Him.

## The two elements

*We went **through fire and through water**.*

Both. Not one or the other.

Isaiah pairs them the same way: *when you pass through the waters, I will be with you… when you
walk through the fire, you shall not be burned*. See the note on Isaiah 43:1–3.

## The word

*Through.* Not *into*, and not *around*.

## The destination

*But You brought us out **into a place of abundance**.*

The Hebrew is **larevayah** — to saturation, to overflow.

## The word elsewhere

*You anoint my head with oil; **my cup runs over**.*

The Hebrew there is **kosi revayah** — my cup, saturation. See the note on Psalms 23:5.

The noun appears twice in the Hebrew Bible, and these are the two.

## What that means here

The place they are brought out into is described with the word for a cup that will not stop
filling.

## What the psalm has just recalled

*He turned the sea into dry land; they went through the river on foot. There we rejoiced in Him.*
See the note on Psalms 66:5–7.

The route out of Egypt went through water.

## What the psalmist does about it

*I will go into Your house with burnt offerings; **I will pay You my vows, which my lips have uttered
and my mouth has spoken when I was in trouble**.* See the note on Psalms 66:13–15.

Promises made in the fire, kept afterwards.

## The last line of the psalm

*Blessed be God, who has not turned away my prayer, nor **His mercy from me**.*

**Chasdo** — His **chesed**.

> **The subject of the verbs.** *You brought us into the net. You laid burdens on our backs. You let
> men ride over our heads.* And the same *You* is what brought them out the other side.
