Nine men do exactly what they were told, and one does something else.

## What happened

Ten lepers were sent to the priests and cleansed on the road. See the note on
Luke 17:11–14.

## The turn

*One of them, **when he saw that he had been healed, turned around and came back**, praising God
at the top of his voice.*

The Greek is **hupestrepsen** — he turned back.

He is going the wrong way. The other nine are heading for the priest, which is the instruction
he was also given.

## The volume

*Praising God at the top of his voice.*

**Meta phones megales** — with a loud voice. The same phrase Luke used four verses earlier when
the ten *raised their voices* and shouted for mercy.

The same volume, redirected.

## The posture

*He **fell on his face at the feet of Jesus** and thanked Him.*

The Greek is **eucharistōn** — giving thanks. The word behind *eucharist*.

Ten stood at a distance because the law required it. One is now face down at His feet, which
the law also would not have allowed.

## The clause Luke puts last

*And **the man was a Samaritan**.*

Luke holds it to the end of the sentence.

He does the same with the man on the Jericho road: priest, Levite, and then *a certain Samaritan,
as he journeyed, came where he was*. See the note on Luke 10:30–35.

## The three questions

*"**Were not all ten of them made clean? Where are the other nine? Was there nobody found to come
back and give the glory to God except this foreigner?**"*

The Greek is **allogenes** — of another race. A rare word.

It appears on the stone inscriptions that stood at the barrier in the temple, warning that no
foreigner might pass beyond into the inner courts on pain of death.

Jesus uses the temple's own word for the one man who came back.

## What the nine did wrong

Nothing, strictly.

They were told to go to the priests, and they went. The narrative does not accuse them of
disobedience, and it does not un-heal them.

## The word to the one

*"**Get up and go on your way. Your faith has made you well**."*

The Greek is **he pistis sou sesoken se** — your faith has saved you.

**Sozo** — the verb for both healing and salvation. Luke has used *cleansed* of all ten and
*healed* of this one, and now uses the widest word of the same man.

Something happened to him at Jesus' feet that did not happen on the road.

## The verb He starts with

*Get up* — **anastas**, the participle of the resurrection word.

He came face down and is told to rise.

## Where this sits in Luke

Immediately after: *when will the kingdom of God come?* and the answer, *the kingdom of God is
within you*. See the note on Luke 17:20–21.

And immediately before it, the disciples asking *increase our faith*, and the answer about the
mustard seed and the mulberry tree.

A question about how to get more faith, and then a Samaritan whose faith is commended for
turning round.

> **The word.** **Allogenes** — the temple's own term, carved on the barrier stone that told
> foreigners how far in they could come. Used here of the one man who came all the way.
