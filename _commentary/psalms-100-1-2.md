title: Serve the LORD with gladness

Two verses of instruction, and every verb in them is about noise.

## Who is called

*Make a joyful noise to the LORD, **all the earth**.*

Not Israel. The psalm's first line addresses the whole world, and its last line is *His truth
endures to all generations* — the frame is deliberately larger than the temple it was sung in.

*"**Go into all the world** and preach the gospel to every creature."*

## The noise

The Hebrew is **hari'u** — a shout, the noise an army makes or a crowd makes when a king
arrives. It is the word for the blast of the trumpet at Jericho.

Not a pleasant sound. A loud one.

## The adverb

*Serve the LORD **with gladness**.*

The Hebrew is **simchah** — joy. It is the only qualifier the psalm attaches to the serving, and
it is not *carefully*, *worthily* or *diligently*.

*God loves **a cheerful giver**.* **Paul**'s word is **hilaros**, and the same instinct governs
both sentences.

## Why gladness is possible

The reason arrives in verse 3, and it is not about the worshipper's performance:

*Know that the LORD, He is God. **It is He who has made us, and not we ourselves**; we are His
people and the sheep of His pasture.*

*For **we are His workmanship**, created in **Christ Jesus** for good works.*

**Paul**'s word is **poiema** — a thing made. The gladness in Psalm 100 rests on somebody else
having done the making.

## Where the psalm goes

*Enter into His gates with thanksgiving, and into His courts with praise.* See the note on
Psalms 100:4.

Come in singing, and find out on arrival that the reason is *the LORD is good; His mercy is
everlasting*.

> **The one qualifier.** Not carefully or worthily. *With gladness* — and the reason given two
> lines later is that He made us and we did not make ourselves.
