---
key: Esther 6:7–9
title: The answer he wrote for himself
updated: 2026-08-09
---
The king asks a general question and Haman answers the wrong one.

## What was asked

*"**What shall be done for the man whom the king delights to honour?**"*

No name in the question. And Haman, who has come to the palace at dawn to ask permission to
hang Mordecai, does the arithmetic out loud in his own head:

*Now Haman thought in his heart, "**Whom would the king delight to honour more than me?**"*

## What he designs

*"A **royal robe the king himself has worn**, and **a horse the king has ridden**, one with a
**royal crown** upon its head."*

Not gold, land or office. He asks for the king's own things — the robe off the king's back and
the horse from under him.

He wants to be treated as the king for an afternoon.

## And the ceremony

*"Let them **dress the man** the king delights to honour, and **lead him on horseback through
the city square**, calling out before him, '**This is what is done for the man the king
delights to honour!**'"*

A herald, a route, and a sentence to shout. He writes the whole thing including the words.

## Who wears it

*"Hasten, take the robe and the horse… and do so for **Mordecai the Jew** who sits within the
king's gate. **Leave nothing undone** of all that you have spoken."*

Every clause he composed is executed, and he leads the horse.

## What the passage is about

A man who assumed the honour was for him, describing in detail a glory he then has to hand to
somebody else and announce in the street.

*For **whoever exalts himself will be humbled**, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.*

*Let nothing be done through selfish ambition or conceit, but in lowliness of mind let each
esteem others better than himself… **Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ
Jesus**.*

**Paul** sets the opposite motion beside it: One who had a robe and a throne of His own and
went the other way.

## What Mordecai had done to earn it

Saved the king's life, years earlier, and been forgotten. The entry sat unread in the
chronicles until a sleepless night.

*For **God is not unjust to forget your work and labour of love**.*

> **What he was answering.** A question with no name in it. He filled in his own, wrote the
> ceremony down to the words of the herald, and then led the horse.
