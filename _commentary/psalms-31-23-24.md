title: Be strong, and let your heart take courage

The last two verses, and the psalm turns outward to say them.

## Who is addressed

*Love the LORD, **all you His faithful**.*

The Hebrew is **chasidav** — His covenant-loyal ones, from **chesed**. Everything up to this
point has been first person; the last two verses speak to everybody else.

A man who has spent twenty-two verses describing being surrounded, slandered and forgotten
*like a dead man out of mind* finishes by encouraging the room.

*Blessed be the God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our tribulation, **that we may be
able to comfort those who are in any trouble**, with the comfort with which we ourselves are
comforted by God.*

**Paul** describes the mechanism exactly: comfort received, and then passed on in the same
currency.

## What is promised

*The LORD **keeps the faithful**.*

**Notser** — guards, watches over, preserves. The sentry word.

*"**My sheep hear My voice**, and I know them, and they follow Me. And I give them eternal life,
and they shall never perish; neither shall anyone snatch them out of My hand."*

## The instruction

*Be strong, and **let your heart take courage**, all you who wait for the LORD.*

The Hebrew is **chizqu ve'ya'ametz levavkhem** — the same pair spoken over Joshua four times in
one chapter. See the note on Joshua 1:6–9.

*Finally, my brethren, **be strong in the Lord and in the power of His might**.*

**Paul** keeps the imperative and names where the strength is kept, which is not in the person
being told to be strong.

## Who it is said to

*All you **who wait** for the LORD.*

Not the ones who have been rescued. The psalm's last word is addressed to people still in it.

> **Where the psalm ends up.** Not with the rescue. With a man who has just described being
> forgotten like a broken pot turning round and telling everybody else to take courage.
