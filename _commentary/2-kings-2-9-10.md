An old prophet offers his successor anything, and the answer is about inheritance.

## Where they are

*When they had crossed.*

Elijah had rolled up his mantle, struck the Jordan, and the water divided. They walked
over on dry ground — the same river, the same way Israel came in under Joshua, going the
other direction.

Elisha has followed him from Gilgal to Bethel to Jericho to the Jordan, refusing three
times to be left behind: *as the LORD lives, and as your soul lives, I will not leave
you.*

## The offer

*"**Ask me — what can I do for you before I am taken from you?**"*

Elijah knows he is going. So do the sons of the prophets at every stop, who kept telling
Elisha so and being told *yes, I know; keep silent*.

## The request

*"Please, let me **inherit a double portion of your spirit**."*

The Hebrew is *let a double portion of your spirit be upon me*.

## What a double portion was

Not twice as much as Elijah had. It is the inheritance law from Deuteronomy: the
firstborn receives *a double portion of all that he has* — two shares where the other
sons get one.

Elisha is not asking to be greater than Elijah. He is asking to be treated as the heir.

The word he uses is the language of a son claiming a father's estate — and when the
chariot comes he shouts *my father, my father*.

## The verdict

*"**You have asked a hard thing**."*

The Hebrew is *you have made hard to ask*. Elijah does not refuse it and does not grant
it. It is not his to give.

## The condition

*"Still — **if you see me as I am taken from you, it will be yours. But if not, it will
not**."*

One condition, and it is about looking.

Not about worthiness, service, or preparation. Whether his eyes are on the man at the
moment he goes.

## What he had already done

Followed, refused three separations, and walked through a divided river. The condition
attached to the request is the same thing he had been doing all day.

## What happened

He saw it. *Elisha saw it and cried out.* See the note on 2 Kings 2:11–12.

## The miracles

The old count is that Elijah's recorded miracles number eight and Elisha's sixteen —
which readers have long noticed lines up with the request.

Scripture never makes the tally itself.

## What Elisha did first

Picked up the mantle that fell, went back to the Jordan, struck the water and said
*where is the LORD God of Elijah?* — and it parted.

He crossed back into the country alone, and the sons of the prophets watching from
Jericho said *the spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha.*

## What the double portion meant

The firstborn's share. *He shall acknowledge the son of the unloved wife as the firstborn by
giving him **a double portion** of all that he has.*

Elisha is not asking to be twice the prophet. He is asking to be treated as the heir.

*And if children, then heirs — **heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ**.*

## What was said about a greater succession

*"**Most assuredly, I say to you, he who believes in Me, the works that I do he will do also;
and greater works than these he will do**, because I go to My Father."*

Elijah went up and his successor did roughly twice the recorded miracles. Jesus says the same
shape about His own going, and attaches it to anybody who believes.

## What it depended on

*"**If you see me when I am taken from you**, it shall be so for you; but if not, it shall
not be."*

Not on merit and not on training. On watching — keeping his eyes on the man as he went.

*Looking unto Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith.*

> **What he asked for.** Not power and not a position. The share of a firstborn son in
> his father's house — and the only condition was to keep watching until the end.
