One verse of heading, and every word in it is doing work.

## The verb

*This is what Isaiah, son of Amoz, **saw**.*

The Hebrew is **chazah** — to see, to behold, to perceive in vision.

The noun form is **chazon** — the vision word. See the note on Proverbs 29:14–18.

A book of sixty-six chapters, almost entirely speech, is introduced as something seen.

## The name

**Yeshayahu** — *the LORD is salvation*.

The same root as **Yeshua**. The prophet's name is the book's subject.

## The father

*Son of Amoz.*

Not the prophet Amos — a different name in Hebrew. Nothing else is known about him, and
Jewish tradition holds he was of royal descent, which would explain Isaiah's access to
kings.

## The subject

*A vision **of Judah and Jerusalem**.*

The southern kingdom and its capital. Isaiah addresses the nations too — there are oracles
on Babylon, Moab, Egypt, Tyre — but the heading names the home ground.

## The four kings

*In the days of **Uzziah and Jotham, of Ahaz and Hezekiah**, the kings of Judah.*

Roughly sixty years of ministry, through four reigns.

**Uzziah** — a long and prosperous reign that ended when he went into the temple to burn
incense and came out a leper. Isaiah dates his call to the year Uzziah died: *in the year
that King Uzziah died, I saw the Lord sitting on a throne, high and lifted up.*

**Jotham** — sixteen years, and the chronicler's verdict is that he *did what was right*
but *the people acted corruptly*.

**Ahaz** — the worst of the four. He burned his own son as an offering, stripped the temple
to pay tribute to Assyria, and copied a Damascus altar. Isaiah offers him a sign and he
refuses it, and gets one anyway: *behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a Son*.

**Hezekiah** — the reformer who opened the temple doors his father had shut, and to whom
Isaiah brings both the death sentence and the fifteen added years. See the note on
2 Chronicles 29:3–5.

## What one man watched

An empire rise. Assyria took the northern kingdom and deported it during Isaiah's lifetime,
and later surrounded Jerusalem itself — the siege that ends with the angel and a hundred
and eighty-five thousand dead in the camp.

## What he says next

*"Hear, O heavens, and give ear, O earth! For the LORD has spoken: **I have nourished and
brought up children, and they have rebelled against Me**."*

*"The ox knows its owner and the donkey its master's crib, **but Israel does not know**, My
people do not consider."*

## The verse a few lines later

*"Come now, and let us reason together," says the LORD. "**Though your sins are like scarlet,
they shall be as white as snow**; though they are red like crimson, they shall be as
wool."*

## The other visions

Ezekiel opens *the heavens were opened and I saw visions of God*. Amos, Obadiah, Nahum and
Habakkuk all head their books with the same word.

The prophetic books are consistently introduced as sight rather than speech.

## The book's range

From *the ox knows its owner* in chapter 1 to *behold, I create new heavens and a new earth*
in chapter 65. See the note on Isaiah 65:21–23.

And chapter 53 in between.

> **The word the whole book stands under.** *Saw.* Sixty-six chapters of speech, four
> kings, an empire's rise and a siege — filed as a vision, by a man whose name means the
> LORD saves.
