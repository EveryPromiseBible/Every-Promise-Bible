Three verses, and the middle one describes a disaster happening around the promise.

## What comes before

*The work of righteousness will be peace, and the effect of righteousness, quietness and
assurance forever.* See the note on Isaiah 32:15–17.

The Hebrew there is **ma'aseh** and **avodah** — the work and the service of righteousness.
Peace is described as its output.

Then this.

## The three words

*My people will live in **peaceful dwellings**, in **secure homes**, in **undisturbed places
of rest**.*

The Hebrew is **neveh shalom**, **mishkenot mivtachim**, **menuchot sha'ananot**.

**Shalom** — wholeness.

**Mivtach** — from **batach**, to trust, to lean on. Security in the sense of confidence
rather than fortification.

**Sha'anan** — at ease, untroubled, quiet.

Three nouns for the same condition, stacked.

## Where the middle word goes

**Mivtach** is the trusting word. *Blessed is the man who **trusts** in the LORD, and whose
hope is the LORD.*

The security is a leaning, not a wall.

## The interruption

*Though **hail flattens the forest** and **the city is leveled completely**.*

The Hebrew is *and it shall hail, coming down on the forest, and the city shall be laid low
in humiliation*.

A verse about safety, then a verse about destruction, and then a blessing.

Isaiah does not remove the hail. He puts the dwelling in the middle of it.

## The forest and the city

Both are Isaiah's images for proud human power. He has already used them: *the LORD of hosts
shall lop off the bough with terror; the high ones of stature will be hewn down, and the
haughty will be humbled. He will cut down the thickets of the forest with iron.*

What comes down in verse 19 is what verse 17 was never resting on.

## The blessing

*How blessed you will be, **sowing your seed by every stream**, letting your **cattle and
donkeys range free**.*

The Hebrew is *blessed are you who sow beside all waters, who send out the feet of the ox and
the donkey*.

Sowing beside all waters — planting everywhere there is water, without needing to defend a
plot.

And livestock loose. You do not turn animals out to graze freely in a country under threat.

## What has to happen first

*Until **the Spirit is poured upon us from on high**, and the wilderness becomes a fruitful
field.*

Verse 15, and everything in the paragraph hangs off it.

## Where the same promise turns up

*And they shall dwell safely, and no one shall make them afraid.* Ezekiel, several times.

And Micah: *everyone shall sit under his vine and under his fig tree, and no one shall make
them afraid*.

Sitting under your own tree is the standing Hebrew image for peace, and it is agricultural
every time.

## What the chapter opened with

*Behold, a **king will reign in righteousness**, and princes will rule with justice.*

*A man will be as a hiding place from the wind, and a cover from the tempest, as rivers of
water in a dry place, as the **shadow of a great rock in a weary land**.*

The chapter starts with a king and ends with cattle grazing loose.

> **What is not removed.** The hail. Verse 18 promises undisturbed rest, verse 19 flattens
> the forest and levels the city, and verse 20 has people sowing.
