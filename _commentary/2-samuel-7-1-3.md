title: You will not build Me a house
updated: 2026-09-10

David wants to do something for God, and the answer that comes back reverses every term in the
sentence.

## What prompts it

**7:1** *Once the king was **settled in his palace**, and the LORD **had given him rest** from all his
enemies on every side.*

Both clauses are things done to him. He is settled and he has been given rest, and the idea
arrives out of that.

## What he notices

**7:2** *"Here I am living in a house of cedar, **while the ark of God sits under a tent of cloth**."*

He is right about the facts and the impulse is a good one. The ark had been under canvas since
the wilderness, and he had just brought it into the city himself.

## What Nathan says

**7:3** *"**Go, do whatever is in your heart**, for the LORD is with you."*

The prophet gets it wrong. He gives the answer that sounds obviously right, without asking —
and God corrects him that same night.

**7:5** *"Go and tell My servant David, 'Thus says the LORD: **Would you build a house for Me to dwell
in?**'"*

## The reversal

**7:11** *"**The LORD will make you a house.**"*

David proposes to build God a house. God says He will build David one — and the Hebrew word
**bayit** carries both meanings, a building and a dynasty. The sentence turns on the pun.

That is the whole shape of it. The man who wanted to give is given to.

**John 15:16**: *"**You did not choose Me, but I chose you** and appointed you that you should go and bear
fruit."*

**Romans 11:35–36**: *Or who has first given to Him and it shall be repaid to him? **For of Him and through Him and
to Him are all things**.*

## What God says He has never asked for

**7:7** *"I have not dwelt in a house since the time that I brought the children of Israel up from
Egypt… **Have I ever spoken a word** to anyone, saying, 'Why have you not built Me a house of
cedar?'"*

Four hundred years of moving from tent to tent, and He points out that He never once asked.

**Acts 17:24–25**: *"God, who made the world and everything in it, since He is Lord of heaven and earth, **does
not dwell in temples made with hands. Nor is He worshiped with men's hands, as though He
needed anything**."*

## Who does build it

Solomon builds the building. But the promise runs past him, **7:13**: *I will set up your seed after
you… **and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever**.*

**Luke 1:32–33**: *"He will be great, and will be called the Son of the Highest; and the Lord God **will give
Him the throne of His father David**… and of His kingdom there will be no end."*

> **The pun the chapter turns on.** **Bayit** — a house, and a household. David offers to
> build one and is told he will be given one, and the second kind does not fall down.
