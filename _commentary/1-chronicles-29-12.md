An old king prays in front of the nation after the largest offering in Israel's history.

## What has just happened

David has assembled everything for the temple he will not live to build — gold, silver,
bronze, iron, timber, onyx, marble — and given his own treasure on top of it.

Then the leaders gave, and the people, *and they rejoiced, for they offered willingly,
because with a loyal heart they offered willingly to the LORD*.

This prayer follows immediately.

## What he says first

*Yours, O LORD, is the greatness, the power and the glory, the victory and the majesty;
for all that is in heaven and in earth is Yours.*

Five nouns, and then a claim about everything.

## The verse

*"**Wealth and honor come from You**."*

The Hebrew is **osher** — riches — and **kavod**, glory, weight, honour.

He says it holding an inventory of what has just been donated.

## The rule

*"**You rule over everything**."*

## The two in His hand

*"**In Your hand is power and might**; in Your hand **it lies to make great and to give
strength to all**."*

The Hebrew is *to make great and to give strength*.

The verbs are causative. Whatever greatness or strength anybody has, the sentence puts
the source somewhere else.

## What he says next

*"But **who am I, and who are my people, that we should be able to offer so willingly**?
For **all things come from You, and of Your own we have given You**."*

That is the sentence the prayer turns on. The gift was made out of what had been received.

He goes further: *for **we are aliens and pilgrims before You**, as were all our fathers;
our days on earth are as a shadow, and without hope.*

Said by the king of Israel at the height of the kingdom.

## What he asks for

Not for himself. *Give my son Solomon a loyal heart, to keep Your commandments… and to
build the temple for which I have made provision.*

## What David had already said about the temple

*"The house to be built for the LORD must be exceedingly magnificent… **now for my house I
have prepared with all my might**"* — and he lists three thousand talents of gold from his
own possession, *over and above all that I have prepared for the holy house*.

He was not asking the people for what he had not given.

## Where the phrase turns up again

Solomon, asked in a dream what he wants, asks for an understanding heart to judge the
people. And God's answer: *I have also given you what you have not asked, **both riches
and honour**.*

The same two words, given to the son of the man who prayed this.

## How the chapter ends

*So David the son of Jesse reigned over all Israel. And the time that he reigned was forty
years… **and he died in a good old age, full of days and riches and honour**.*

The chronicler closes the account with the two words from the prayer.

> **When it was said.** Not in poverty. Standing in front of the largest voluntary
> offering the nation ever gave, with the inventory in front of him, David says the
> wealth came from somewhere else and that the giving was out of what was received.
