The conclusion of Paul's first recorded sermon, and it is the sentence the rest of his
letters unpack.

## Where it comes

Antioch of Pisidia, in a synagogue, after the reading of the law and the prophets, at the
invitation of the rulers. See the note on Acts 13:13–15.

He has just walked them through Israel's history — Egypt, the wilderness, the judges,
Samuel, Saul, David — and arrived at the resurrection with three quotations from the
Psalms and Isaiah.

Then this.

## The address

*"So let it be known to you, **brothers**."*

He is speaking to Jews and God-fearing Gentiles in their own building, on their own
sabbath.

## The first half

*"It is **through this One that forgiveness of sins is announced to you**."*

The Greek is **kataggelletai** — proclaimed, announced publicly. It is a herald's word.

Not offered on terms. Announced.

## The second half

*"And by Him **everyone who believes is set right from everything the law of Moses could
never set you right from**."*

The Greek verb is **dikaioutai** — justified, declared in the right. It is the word the
whole of Romans and Galatians turns on, and this is the first time Paul is recorded using
it.

## The comparison

The sentence has two halves and the second one is a concession about the first system.

Paul does not say the law justified nobody. He says there were things it could not reach,
and that from **everything** it could not reach, everyone who believes is set right.

## "Everyone who believes"

**Pas ho pisteuon** — the same construction he will use in Romans: *the righteousness of
God through faith in Jesus Christ, to all and on all **who believe**. For there is no
difference.* See the note on Romans 3:21–22.

## What he says immediately after

*"Beware therefore, lest what has been spoken in the prophets come upon you: '**Behold,
you despisers, marvel and perish!** For I work a work in your days, a work which you will
by no means believe, though one were to declare it to you.'"*

He quotes Habakkuk, and he ends the sermon on a warning.

## The response

*When the Jews had gone out of the synagogue, **the Gentiles begged them to speak these
same words to them the next Sabbath**.* See the note on Acts 13:42–43.

And the following week nearly the whole city turned up.

## What Paul urged them to do in the meantime

*Talked with them and **urged them to continue in the grace of God**.*

That is Luke's summary of the follow-up conversation.

## Where the same argument goes

Galatians, to churches Paul planted on this same journey: *knowing that a man is not
justified by the works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ… for **by the works of the
law no flesh shall be justified**.*

Same verb, same conclusion, written to the region he was standing in.

> **What is announced and what is not.** Forgiveness is proclaimed, not offered on
> conditions. And the justifying is stated as covering the ground the older system never
> could — which is the whole of Paul's later argument, in one sentence, in a synagogue.
