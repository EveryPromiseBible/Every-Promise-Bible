Two short sentences, and the second one is about somebody else entirely.

## The definition

*Anyone who sins is **breaking the law**, because **sin is lawbreaking**.*

The Greek is **anomia** — lawlessness. Not the breach of a particular statute but the state
of being without law.

John defines the word and does not soften it. Sin is not a mistake, a weakness, or a
shortfall in this sentence. It is lawlessness.

## What John does next

He does not follow it with a warning.

*And you know that **He came out into the open to carry our sins away**.*

The Greek is **ephanerothe** — He was manifested. The same verb John uses twice in the verses
just before, about the future appearing. See the note on 1 John 3:2–3.

Manifested then, to take sins away. Manifested later, so we shall be like Him.

## The verb

*To **carry our sins away**.*

The Greek is **are** — to lift, to bear off, to remove.

It is the Baptist's verb: *behold, the Lamb of God, who **takes away** the sin of the world*.
See the note on John 1:29–34.

Not covered. Lifted and carried off.

## The clause

*And **sin is not in Him**.*

The Greek is present tense — **hamartia en auto ouk estin**.

John states the definition of sin as lawlessness, then names the one person it does not
apply to.

## The three statements about it

The New Testament makes the same point three times, and each writer chooses a different
angle.

John: *in Him there is no sin* — nothing inside.

Paul: *Him who **knew no sin**, He made to be sin for us*. See the note on
2 Corinthians 5:20–21.

Peter: *He committed no sin, nor was deceit found in His mouth*. See the note on
1 Peter 2:21–22.

Nothing in Him, nothing known by Him, nothing done by Him. Between them the three cover the
inside, the experience, and the record.

Hebrews adds the fourth: *tempted as we are, yet without sin*.

## What the passage argues

Verse 6 draws the conclusion: *whoever abides in Him does not sin*.

The logic runs through the person, not through effort. The One who has no sin in Him took
sin away, and the one who stays in Him is in a place where sin has no business.

## The verse before all of it

*Behold what manner of love the Father has bestowed on us, that we should be called children
of God! And that is what we are.*

The chapter opens with status and then discusses conduct. John never runs it the other way.

## What he says a few lines later

*He who sins is of the devil, for the devil has sinned from the beginning. **For this purpose
the Son of God was manifested, that He might destroy the works of the devil**.*

The Greek is **luse** — loose, dissolve, undo.

The third *manifested* in the chapter, and this one names the purpose in terms of demolition.

## The letter's stated aim

*That you may know that you have eternal life.* See the note on 1 John 5:13.

The chapter about sin and lawlessness is in a letter written for assurance.

> **Where the sentence lands.** He defines sin as lawlessness — and the next thing he says is
> that it was carried off, by somebody who has none of it in Him.
