A woman is offered anything and asks for nothing.

## What she had done

Noticed a prophet passing through Shunem repeatedly, fed him, and then said to her husband:

*"Let us make a small upper room on the wall; and let us put a bed for him there, and a table and a
chair and a lampstand."* See the note on 2 Kings 4:8–10.

Four items, listed. She built it before anybody asked.

## The visit

*So one day Elisha came, **went up to the room, and lay down**.*

The Hebrew is **vayyasar el-ha'aliyyah** — turned aside into the upper room.

## The intermediary

*He said to his servant Gehazi, "**Call this Shunammite woman**."*

She is summoned through a servant, and Elisha speaks to her through him even when she is in the
room.

*"Tell her…"*

She is standing in front of him. He addresses her indirectly, which was the protocol.

## The offer

*"You have gone to all this trouble for us. **Now what can be done for you? Shall I speak to the king
or the commander of the army on your behalf?**"*

The Hebrew is **charadt eleinu et-kol-hacharadah hazzot** — you have shown us all this care.

## What is being offered

Access. Elisha has the ear of the palace and the army — the two powers in the country.

For a woman in that world, that is the most valuable thing available.

## Her answer

*"**I live among my own people**," she answered. "**I have all I need**."*

The Hebrew is **betok ammi anochi yoshavet** — in the midst of my people I dwell.

She declines. She is settled, respected, and needs nothing the court can give.

## What she does not say

That she is childless.

The narrator has not mentioned it either. It comes out only when Elisha asks his servant.

## The servant's observation

*Gehazi said, "**Well — she has no son, and her husband is old**."*

Two facts, and the second closes the first.

Gehazi supplies what she would not.

## The second summons

*"Call her," Elisha said. So he called her, and **she stood in the doorway**.*

The Hebrew is **vatta'amod bappatach** — and she stood in the door.

Not in the room. At the threshold, which is where a woman stood.

## The promise

*"**About this time next year**," he told her, "**you will hold a son in your arms**."*

The Hebrew is **ka'et chayyah** — at the time of life, at the reviving season.

The exact phrase used at Abraham's tent door: *I will certainly return to you according to the
time of life, and behold, Sarah your wife shall have a son*. See the note on
Genesis 18:9–15.

## Her answer

*"**No, my lord**," she said. "**Man of God, please don't lie to your servant**."*

The Hebrew is **al-tekhazzev beshiphchatecha** — do not deceive your maidservant.

Sarah laughed. This woman asks not to be lied to.

Both are the response of someone who has stopped hoping for this particular thing.

## What happens

*But the woman became pregnant, and **the next year, at that very time, she gave birth to a son** —
just as Elisha had told her.*

## What happens to the boy

*"My head, my head!"* — in the harvest field, carried to his mother, and dead on her knees at noon.

She lays him on the prophet's bed, saddles a donkey, and says only *it is well*. See the note on
2 Kings 4:18–25.

## What she gets back

The child, and years later, a court hearing in which the king awards her seven years of harvests
off land she had abandoned. See the note on 2 Kings 8:6.

The woman who declined an introduction to the king ends up in front of him.

> **The offer and the answer.** He offers her the king and the commander of the army. She says *I
> live among my own people* — and the one thing she lacks has to be supplied by his servant.
