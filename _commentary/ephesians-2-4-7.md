Two of the most consequential words in the New Testament, and then three verbs
that all say the same thing about location.

## "But God"

The sentence before this is a description of the dead: following the course of
this world, by nature children of wrath. Then the turn.

Paul does not say *but they tried harder*, or *but God saw potential*. He says
*But God — so rich in mercy, and loving us with a love so great*, and the reason
given for everything that follows is entirely on His side.

## The three verbs

Each one in Greek is a compound built with *syn* — together with:

- **made us alive together** with Christ
- **raised us up together** with Him
- **seated us together** with Him

Not alongside as spectators. The same three things that happened to Christ —
resurrection, ascension, enthronement — described as having happened to us with
Him.

And all three are aorist: completed. Not a process under way.

## "Even while we were dead"

The timing is stated twice for emphasis. The making-alive happened while the
condition was death, not after any improvement. Dead men do not cooperate with
their own resurrection, which is Paul's point — hence the parenthesis he cannot
help inserting: *by grace you have been saved.*

## What it is for

*So that in the ages still to come He could show the overflowing wealth of His
grace.*

The purpose is exhibition. We are the evidence God intends to display, and the
thing being displayed is the size of His kindness — with the ages to come as the
audience.

> **Where "up where heaven is" leads.** Paul has already put Christ there in
> chapter 1, seated far above every ruler and authority. Now the same location is
> given to the church, because a head and a body are not in two places. See the
> note on Ephesians 1:20–23.
