Paul asks for something impossible and then names the two words that make it ordinary.

## The instruction

*So **become imitators of God**.*

The Greek is **mimetai** — the root of *mimic*. It occurs here about God and nowhere else
in the New Testament.

Paul asks people to imitate God. It is the largest instruction in his letters.

## The qualifier

*The way **dearly loved children copy their own father**.*

The Greek is **tekna agapeta** — beloved children.

That is what makes it possible. Imitation of a father by a small child is not an
achievement; it is what happens.

Nobody teaches a toddler to copy. It follows from being in the house and being loved
there.

## "So"

The Greek is **oun** — therefore.

It looks back at the end of chapter 4: *and be kind to one another, tenderhearted,
**forgiving one another, just as God in Christ forgave you**.*

The imitating is already under way in the sentence before.

## The walk

*And **walk in love**.*

The Greek is **peripateite** — the ordinary word for going about, the one Paul uses
throughout Ephesians for how a life is conducted.

## The measure

*The same love **with which Christ loved us and gave Himself up on our behalf**.*

Not love in general. A specific act, named, with a date.

The Greek is **paredoken** — handed over, delivered up. It is the word used of Judas
handing Him over, and the word Paul uses in Romans: *He who did not spare His own Son,
but **delivered Him up** for us all.*

## What it is called

*An **offering and a sacrifice** to God, **a fragrance sweet to Him**.*

Two words from Leviticus — **prosphora** and **thusia** — and then the phrase that runs
through the whole sacrificial system.

**A sweet-smelling aroma** is the Greek Old Testament's standard rendering of the Hebrew
**reyach nichoach**, the restful smell that goes up from an accepted offering. It appears
first over Noah's altar. See the note on Genesis 8:20–22.

## Who it was for

The offering was *on our behalf*, and the fragrance was *to God*.

The direction of the two clauses is different, and both are in the sentence.

Paul uses the same word of a gift of money sent to him in prison — *a fragrance of
sweetness, a sacrifice that is welcomed, a delight to God*. See the note on
Philippians 4:17–18.

## What comes immediately after

*But fornication and all uncleanness or covetousness, **let it not even be named among
you**, as is fitting for saints.*

Paul goes straight from the sweet aroma to a list of what should not be around. The two
verses are the standard the list is measured by.

## What the letter has already said about the love

*That you, being rooted and grounded in love, may be able to comprehend… **the width and
length and depth and height** — to know the love of Christ which passes knowledge.*

Then the doxology. See the note on Ephesians 3:20–21.

> **Why the instruction is not crushing.** *The way dearly loved children copy their own
> father.* Paul puts the impossible command and the reason it is natural in the same
> sentence.
