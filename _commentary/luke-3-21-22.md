Luke alone records what He was doing when the sky opened.

## The crowd

*When **all the people were being baptized**, Jesus was baptized as well.*

The Greek is **hapanta ton laon** — all the people.

He is in a queue. Luke puts Him among them rather than apart.

## What Luke adds

*And **while He was praying**, heaven opened.*

The Greek is **proseuchomenou**.

Matthew and Mark both record the baptism. Only Luke records the prayer, and he attaches the opening
of heaven to it.

## Luke's habit

He does this repeatedly. Before choosing the twelve, *He continued all night in prayer*. Before
Peter's confession, *as He was alone praying*. On the mount of transfiguration, *as He prayed, the
appearance of His face was altered*.

The decisive moments in this Gospel are preceded by the same detail.

## The opening

*Heaven opened.*

The Greek is **aneōchthēnai ton ouranon**.

Isaiah's cry: *Oh, that You would rend the heavens and come down*.

## The descent

*And the Holy Spirit came down on Him **in bodily form, like a dove settling**.*

The Greek is **sōmatikō eidei** — in bodily appearance.

Luke, the physician, is the one who insists it was visible.

## The dove

The Spirit hovering over the water at the beginning. See the note on Genesis 1:1–2.

And the dove that came back to Noah with an olive leaf, having found nowhere to land.

Here it lands and stays. John's account: *I saw the Spirit descending from heaven like a dove, **and
He remained upon Him***.

## The voice

*"**You are My Son, the One I love. My delight rests on You**."*

The Greek is **su ei ho huios mou ho agapētos, en soi eudokēsa**.

## The address

*You are.* Second person, spoken to Him.

Matthew has *this is* — addressed to the onlookers. Luke and Mark have the direct address.

## The two Old Testament lines behind it

*You are My Son, today I have begotten You* — the coronation psalm. See the note on Psalms 2:7–9.

*Behold! My Servant whom I uphold, **My Elect One in whom My soul delights**.* See the note on
Isaiah 42:1–4.

A king's psalm and a servant's song, joined in one sentence.

## The middle word

**Ho agapētos** — the beloved.

The Septuagint uses it of Isaac: *take now your son, your only son, **whom you love***. See the note
on Genesis 22:1–2.

## The timing

Before a single sermon, miracle or disciple.

The delight is stated over thirty years of ordinary life in a carpenter's shop.

## What comes next in Luke

*Then Jesus, being filled with the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan and **was led by the Spirit
into the wilderness**.* See the note on Luke 4:1–4.

And the first words of the tempter: *"**If You are the Son of God**, command this stone to become
bread."*

The challenge opens by picking up the sentence from the sky and putting an *if* in front of it —
and dropping *beloved*.

## What Luke puts immediately after

The genealogy, run backwards from Jesus to *Adam, the son of God*.

> **The detail only Luke keeps.** Heaven opens *while He was praying*. It is the same author's habit
> throughout — the turning points in this Gospel all have prayer in front of them.
