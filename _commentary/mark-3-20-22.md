His family think He is mad and the theologians think He is possessed, and Mark puts both in
one paragraph.

## The pressure

*The crowd gathered again **so thickly that He and His followers could not even get a meal
down**.*

Mark notes this twice in his Gospel. It is the detail he keeps.

He has just appointed the twelve on a mountain, and come back into a house.

## The family

*When **His own family heard about it**, they set out to **take charge of Him**.*

The Greek is **hoi par' autou** — those belonging to Him, His own people.

And **kratesai** — to seize, to take hold of forcibly. The same verb used of arresting Him
later.

They are coming to take Him home by force.

## Why

*Because people were saying, "**He has lost His mind**."*

The Greek is **exeste** — He is beside Himself, out of His senses.

The subject is ambiguous. It can be *people were saying* or *for they said* — the family
themselves.

Either way, the family is acting on it.

John says it plainly elsewhere: *for even His brothers did not believe in Him*.

## The delegation

*And the teachers of the law **who had come down from Jerusalem**.*

Not the local scribes. An official party, sent from the capital.

## Their verdict

*"**Beelzebul is in Him**. He drives out demons by the power of the ruler of the demons."*

**Beelzeboul** — probably from **Baal-zebul**, lord of the exalted dwelling, twisted in Hebrew
into **Baal-zebub**, lord of the flies, the god of Ekron whom Ahaziah sent to consult.

## What they concede

The exorcisms.

They do not say He is a fraud or that nothing happens. Their explanation is about the source,
and it requires the power to be real.

That is the same shape as Nazareth — *where did this man get such wisdom, and these mighty
works?* See the note on Matthew 13:53–58.

Nobody in the Gospels disputes that things happen.

## The answer

*"How can Satan cast out Satan? If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot
stand… **and if Satan has risen up against himself, and is divided, he cannot stand, but has an
end**."*

And then the parable that answers the charge properly:

*"No one can enter a strong man's house and plunder his goods, **unless he first binds the
strong man**. And then he will plunder his house."*

He does not deny being in somebody's house. He says He has tied the owner up.

## What follows

*"Truly I say to you, all sins will be forgiven… but he who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit
never has forgiveness."* See the note on Mark 3:28–30.

## Where the family thread finishes

*Then His brothers and His mother came, and standing outside they sent to Him, calling Him.*

*And He looked around in a circle at those who sat about Him, and said, "**Here are My mother
and My brothers! For whoever does the will of God is My brother and My sister and mother**."*

The people who came to take Him home end up standing outside the house.

## Where they end up

*These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women and Mary the
mother of Jesus, and **with His brothers**.*

Acts 1, in the upper room.

And James runs the Jerusalem church by chapter 15, and writes a letter opening *James, a
bondservant of God*.

> **What the two verdicts have in common.** Both accept that something real is happening. His
> family called it madness; the men from Jerusalem called it the wrong power — and neither
> denied the exorcisms.
