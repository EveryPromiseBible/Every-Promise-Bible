The immediate reaction to Paul's first sermon, and the instruction he gives afterward.

## Who asks for more

*When the Jews had gone out of the synagogue, **the Gentiles begged them to speak these
same words to them the next Sabbath**.*

The Greek is **parekaloun** — begged, urgently requested.

**These same words.** Not more, and not something adapted. The same ones.

## Who is asking

The Gentiles in the building — God-fearers who attended synagogue without becoming
proselytes. Paul had addressed them directly in the sermon: *men of Israel, **and you who
fear God***.

They had been sitting at the edge of Israel's worship for years, and the sentence about
being *set right from everything the law of Moses could never set you right from* was
addressed to them too. See the note on Acts 13:38–39.

## Who followed

*And when the gathering broke up, **many of the Jews and of the devout converts to Judaism
followed Paul and Barnabas**.*

Both groups. Luke is careful to record that Jews responded as well.

**Devout converts** — the Greek is **proselytoi**, full converts who had taken on
circumcision and the law.

## The conversation

*Who **talked with them**.*

The Greek is **proslalountes** — kept talking to them. It continued after the service.

## The instruction

*And **urged them to continue in the grace of God**.*

The Greek is **epimenein te chariti tou theou** — to remain in, to stay put in.

That is Luke's one-line summary of the follow-up teaching, and it is worth noticing what
it is not. Not *be careful now*, not a list of next steps.

**Continue** — the same verb Paul uses in Colossians: *if indeed you continue in the
faith, grounded and steadfast.*

## The word Luke uses for the message

**Charis** — grace. Luke uses it of the message repeatedly in Acts: *the word of His
grace*, *the gospel of the grace of God*, and the church at Antioch commending Barnabas
and Paul *to the grace of God for the work*.

Paul's own summary of his commission, to the Ephesian elders, uses the same phrase: *the
ministry I received from the Lord Jesus — to testify to **the good news of the grace of
God**.* See the note on Acts 20:22–24.

## What happened next

*The following Sabbath **nearly the whole city gathered** to hear the word of God.*

And with the crowd came the opposition. See the note on Acts 13:44–47.

## The pattern

Luke records the same shape in city after city: a synagogue, a hearing, a large response,
and then trouble from the same building.

> **What they were told to do.** Not to add anything, and not to guard against the
> message. To stay in it — and Luke gives that as the whole of the follow-up instruction.
