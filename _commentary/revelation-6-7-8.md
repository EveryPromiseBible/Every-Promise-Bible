The fourth seal, and it is the only rider who is given a name.

## The colour

*There was a horse **the color of a corpse**.*

The Greek is **chloros** — pale green, yellowish-green.

It is the ordinary word for vegetation. Mark uses it of the grass the five thousand sat on:
*to sit down in groups on the **green** grass*.

The same adjective for spring grass and for a dead face. Translators choose between *pale* and
*green*, and the Greek carries both.

## The rider

*The rider on it was **named Death**.*

The Greek is **Thanatos**.

The first three riders are described. This one is named, and the name is the thing itself.

## The follower

*And **the Grave came along behind him**.*

The Greek is **Hades** — the realm of the dead, and the standard Septuagint rendering of the
Hebrew **Sheol**.

Two figures for one rider: the killing, and where the killed go.

Paul puts the pair together too: *O Death, where is your sting? O Hades, where is your
victory?* See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:53–55.

And Revelation ends with both of them dealt with: *then Death and Hades were cast into the lake
of fire*.

## The limit

*The two of them were **handed a quarter of the earth**.*

The Greek is **edothe autois exousia epi to tetarton** — authority was given to them over the
fourth part.

The fourth passive in eight verses, and the most explicit boundary in the chapter. A fraction is
named.

## The four means

*And turned loose to kill in it — with **the sword**, with **hunger**, with **plague**, and with
**the wild animals of the earth**.*

The Greek is **rhomphaia** for the sword — the long Thracian blade, not the **machaira** of the
second rider.

**Limos** — famine. **Thanatos** again, here meaning pestilence, which is how the Septuagint
uses it. And **therion** — wild beasts.

## Where the four come from

Ezekiel, four times over: *when I send **My four severe judgments** on Jerusalem — the sword and
famine and wild beasts and pestilence — to cut off man and beast from it*.

The list is exact and the order is close. John is quoting a prophet's standing formula.

And Leviticus 26 has the same set as the covenant curses.

## Why the wild animals

It sounds archaic and it is not. When population collapses, cultivation stops and predators
move back in.

2 Kings records it happening after the Assyrian deportation: *the LORD sent lions among them,
which killed some of them*.

## What the four riders add up to

Conquest, war, scarcity, death — and the last summarises the first three. The sword is the
second rider, the hunger is the third, and the plague and the animals are what follows both.

## What comes next

The fifth seal, and it is not a rider.

*I saw under the altar **the souls of those who had been slain for the word of God**… and they
cried with a loud voice, "**How long, O Lord, holy and true, until You judge and avenge our
blood?**"*

And they are given white robes and told to rest a little while longer.

## Where the sequence began

The Lamb, opening the seals. See the note on Revelation 6:1–2.

Everything in the chapter proceeds from a hand that had been pierced.

> **The only rider with a name.** Death, with the Grave behind him — and the one thing the text
> specifies about their authority is the fraction of it.
