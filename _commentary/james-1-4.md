One sentence, and the two words at the end of it are not about moral perfection.

## The words

*You will come out grown up and whole, with no piece of you left unfinished.*

- **teleios** — brought to its end, complete, mature. Used of a full-grown adult
  as against a child, and of a thing that has reached what it was for.
- **holokleros** — literally *whole-lot*, having all its parts. It was used of a
  body with nothing missing, and of a stone fit for building because it had no
  defect.

Neither word means sinless. Both mean **nothing lacking**.

James then says it a third way in case it was missed: *lacking in nothing*.

## What produces it

*So let that endurance run all the way to the finish.*

The verse before names the mechanism — testing produces endurance — and this one
says: now let the endurance finish its work.

The instruction is not to generate anything. It is to let something already
underway complete rather than cutting it short.

## What is being described

Not a person who has suffered and become tougher. A person with no piece missing
— the parts that only get built under pressure now present.

That is why James opens the letter by telling scattered, harassed believers to
count trials as joy. Not because the trial is good, but because of what it is in
the middle of making.

## The word, in the other letters

**Teleios** — the word rendered *perfect* here — means brought to completion, full-grown. James
wants it worked out in experience. What is easy to miss is that the New Testament also says it is
already true of you, in Christ, before any patience has done anything:

*And **you are complete in Him**, who is the head of all principality and power.*

*For by one offering He has **perfected forever** those who are being sanctified.*

That last sentence puts both halves in one line: perfected, perfect tense, finished — and *being
sanctified*, present, still under way. James is describing the second. The first was done at Calvary
and is not waiting on your endurance.

> **The next verse.** *If any of you is short on wisdom, ask God.* It follows
> directly, and the connection is easy to miss: a person in the middle of an
> unfinished process, told to let it run, is exactly a person who will not
> understand what is happening. So the immediate provision is wisdom, given
> generously and without being made to feel small for asking. See the note on
