A promise about opposition disappearing, and it ends with a hand.

## What happens to the opposition

*All who rage against you will be ashamed and disgraced. Those who oppose you
will be as nothing and perish. **You will look for your enemies and not find
them.***

The middle line is the striking one. Not that the enemies are defeated in front
of you — that you go looking and they are not there.

Twice the phrase *as nothing* appears. The Hebrew is a word for non-existence,
nothingness. What was threatening is described as having no substance left to
locate.

## The reason given

*For I am the LORD your God, who takes hold of your right hand.*

The promise is not grounded in Israel's strength or in the enemies' weakness. It
is grounded in a grip.

And it is specific: the **right** hand — the working hand, the sword hand. God is
described as holding the hand a fighter would need free.

## What He says while holding it

*Do not fear. I Myself will help you.*

The emphatic pronoun is in the Hebrew. Not *help will come* — I, Myself.

Isaiah uses this construction repeatedly in these chapters, and each time the
comfort is anchored in who is speaking rather than in the circumstances
improving.

## Who it was said to

Exiles, or people about to be. The chapter opens with God summoning the nations
to a courtroom and asking who has directed history, and it addresses Israel as
*My servant, whom I have chosen, the offspring of Abraham My friend.*

That is the standing from which the promise is made — a relationship named before
a single enemy is mentioned.

## Looking for enemies and not finding them

*You will look for your enemies and not find them.*

*Having **disarmed principalities and powers, He made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over
them in it**.*

**Paul**'s verb is **apekdysamenos** — stripped them off, as you strip armour from a defeated
man. The place he says it happened is the cross.

*There is therefore now **no condemnation** to those who are in **Christ Jesus**.*

*Who shall bring a charge against God's elect?… **Who shall separate us from the love of
Christ?***

**Romans** 8 goes looking for the accuser through a list — tribulation, distress, persecution,
famine, nakedness, peril, sword, death, life, angels, principalities, height, depth — and does
not find one.

## The hand that is held

*I am the LORD your God, **who takes hold of your right hand**.*

*"And I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish; **neither shall anyone snatch them
out of My hand**. My Father, who has given them to Me, is greater than all; and no one is able to
snatch them out of My Father's hand."*

**John** 10 has two hands and the same grip. The security in both texts is located in who is
holding, not in who is held.

> **The verse just before this passage.** *Do not fear, for I am with you; do not
> be dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you and help you; I will
> uphold you with My righteous right hand.* His right hand holding theirs. The
> image runs through the whole section.
