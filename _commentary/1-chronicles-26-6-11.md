The roster continues, and the descriptions attached to one family are the notable part.

## The grandsons

*Obed-edom's son **Shemaiah** also had sons who became **leaders in their father's house, for
they were capable men**.*

The Hebrew is **gibborei chayil**.

**Chayil** — strength, ability, worth, and also wealth and an army. The range of the word is
the point.

It is the word in *a virtuous woman* — **eshet chayil**, a woman of chayil. See the note on
Proverbs 31:10–12.

And in the angel's greeting to Gideon: *the LORD is with you, **mighty man of valour**.*

Capable, worthy, strong, and effective, all in one noun.

## The names

*Othni, Rephael, Obed, and Elzabad*, and two relatives, *Elihu and Semachiah*.

Six named grandsons, and four of the six have God's name inside them — Rephael, *God heals*;
Elzabad, *God has given*; Elihu, *He is my God*; Semachiah, *the LORD has sustained*.

## The count

*All these were descendants of Obed-edom — they, their sons, and their brothers — **sixty-two of
them, capable men, strong for the work**.*

Sixty-two, from one household.

The Chronicler has already explained the number: *for God had truly blessed him*. See the note
on 1 Chronicles 26:1–5.

## The phrase repeated

**Strong for the work** — the Hebrew is *strength for the service*.

Chayil twice in three verses, of the same family.

## The comparison

*Meshelemiah had sons and brothers, able men — **eighteen** in all.*

*Hosah's sons and brothers came to **thirteen**.*

Eighteen and thirteen. Obed-edom's line is sixty-two, and the Chronicler gives all three
figures on the same page.

## The other detail

*Hosah, of the Merarites, had sons: **Shimri the chief (his father made him chief though he was
not the firstborn)**.*

An aside about a family arrangement that nobody had to record, and the Chronicler records it.

The pattern runs through the whole Bible — Isaac over Ishmael, Jacob over Esau, Judah over
Reuben, Ephraim over Manasseh, David over seven brothers.

## What the job was

Gatekeepers.

The chapter goes on to assign them by lot: east, north, south, and the storehouse. Twenty-four
posts, staffed continuously.

*They were trusted, and were in charge of the storerooms and treasuries.*

Doorkeepers with the keys to the treasury.

## The psalm about the post

*For a day in Your courts is better than a thousand. I would rather **be a doorkeeper in the
house of my God** than dwell in the tents of wickedness.*

Psalm 84, written by the sons of Korah — the other family on this roster.

## Where the family started

A house six miles outside Jerusalem, and three months of storing a box nobody else would
take. See the note on 1 Chronicles 13:12–14.

Four hundred years on, a scribe is counting sixty-two of his descendants and calling them
strong for the work.

## The word

**Chayil** — strength, capability, valour. It is the word in *a woman of valour* at the end of
Proverbs, and the word for an army.

Used of Obed-Edom's sons and grandsons twice in a few verses: *mighty men of valour*, and
*able men with strength for the work*.

## Whose family this is

The man who took the ark in when nobody else would, and whose eighth son is called *my
reward*. See the note on 1 Chronicles 26:1–5.

Sixty-two of them, counted in this passage.

## The post itself

Gatekeeper. Standing at a door, on a rota, checking who comes in.

The Chronicler describes them with the vocabulary of soldiers and applies it to men whose
work is answering a gate.

*And whatever you do, **do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men**, knowing that from the
Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance. For you serve the Lord **Christ**.*

**Paul** writes that to household slaves.

## Where the strength came from

Not from the family line — his people were Philistine.

*For **God had truly blessed him**.* The Chronicler gives the reason once and lets it stand
over the whole roster.

*Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and comes down from the Father of
lights.*

> **The word used twice of one family.** *Chayil* — capable, worthy, strong, and wealthy, all
> in one noun. Sixty-two of them, against eighteen and thirteen in the two families listed
> alongside.
