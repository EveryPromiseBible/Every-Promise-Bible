The aftermath on the road, and Luke gives the details in the order they happened.

## The men with him

*The men traveling with him **stood there speechless. They heard the voice but saw no
one**.*

Luke's account here and Paul's own retellings differ on the detail — chapter 22 has them
*seeing the light but not hearing the voice of Him who spoke to me*.

The likeliest reading is that they heard a sound without hearing words. The Greek allows
it, and the point in both accounts is the same: something happened that they registered and
could not receive.

They are witnesses to an event they cannot interpret.

## The escort

They were there to help arrest people. Saul had letters from the high priest and a party to
execute them.

## Getting up

*Saul got up from the ground, and **when he opened his eyes he could see nothing**.*

He had fallen at the light. Luke notes that he opened his eyes deliberately.

## The reversal

*So **they took him by the hand and led him into Damascus**.*

The Greek is **cheiragogountes** — hand-leading.

He set out at the head of a party with warrants and arrives being led like a child.

He uses the same word himself of Elymas the sorcerer, struck blind at Paphos, *groping about,
looking for someone to lead him by the hand*. See the note on Acts 13:9–12.

Paul pronounces on another man what had happened to him — and includes the time limit that
he had been given.

## The three days

*For **three days he was without sight**, and **he neither ate nor drank**.*

Luke gives all three: blind, no food, no water.

Three days in a house on Straight Street, in the city he had come to raid, with nothing to
do.

## What he was doing

The Lord tells Ananias: *"**he is praying at this moment**."* See the note on
Acts 9:10–14.

And: *"he has seen in a vision a man named Ananias coming in and laying hands on him."*

He is blind and being shown something.

## What he lost in those days

Everything he had been. His letters put it this way: *what things were gain to me, these I
have counted loss for Christ.* See the note on Philippians 3:2–3.

The seven credentials he lists there were all intact on the road out of Jerusalem.

## What ended it

Ananias, a man who had to be argued into coming, laying hands on him and calling him
**brother**.

*Immediately there fell from his eyes **something like scales**, and he received his sight at
once.*

The Greek is **lepides** — flakes, scales. Luke, a physician, chooses the word.

## What he did next

*And immediately he **preached the Christ in the synagogues, that He is the Son of God**.*

*Then all who heard were amazed, and said, "**Is this not he who destroyed those who called
on this name in Jerusalem?**"*

## The three days elsewhere

The pattern is not commented on in the text. Luke reports the count and moves on.

> **What the escort saw.** Enough to stand still and say nothing. They heard something and
> saw nobody — and then took the man who had been leading them and walked him into the city
> by the hand.
