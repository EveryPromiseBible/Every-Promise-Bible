Two examples from the same room in Nazareth, and they are what gets Him thrown out.

## Where this is said

The synagogue in Nazareth, immediately after He read Isaiah 61, closed the scroll and
said *today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing.* See the note on
Isaiah 61:1–3.

Luke says the reaction to that was favourable: *all bore witness to Him and marvelled at
the gracious words.*

Then He says this.

## The first case

*"There were **plenty of widows in Israel** in the days of Elijah, when the sky was shut
up for three and a half years."*

Luke's figure of three and a half years matches James's. 1 Kings says *in the third
year*.

## Where the prophet went

*"But Elijah was **not sent to a single one of them** — he was sent to **a widow in
Zarephath, in the region of Sidon**."*

Sidon is Phoenicia — Gentile territory, and specifically Jezebel's country. Her father
was king of the Sidonians.

The prophet who spent his life opposing Jezebel was fed for three years by a widow from
her homeland. See the note on 1 Kings 17:8–16.

## The second case

*"And there were **plenty of people with leprosy in Israel** in the days of the prophet
Elisha, and **not one of them was made clean** — only **Naaman, the Syrian**."*

Naaman was commander of the army of Aram, the nation Israel was at war with. He had been
raiding Israelite territory; the girl who told him about the prophet was a slave taken in
one of those raids.

He was healed and Israel's own lepers were not.

## What both examples have in common

An Israelite prophet, a national crisis, a country full of people with the need — and the
one person helped is a foreigner.

Both stories are in Israel's own Scriptures. Nothing He says is new information.

## Why it enraged them

*So all those in the synagogue, when they heard these things, **were filled with wrath**.*

They had just asked *is this not Joseph's son?* and were expecting the local boy to do at
Capernaum's miracles here.

He answers by naming the two occasions when the prophets were sent past Israel
altogether.

## What they did

*And rose up and thrust Him out of the city; and they led Him to the brow of the hill on
which their city was built, **that they might throw Him down over the cliff**.*

## What He did

*Then **passing through the midst of them, He went His way**.*

Luke gives no explanation.

## What He said before the examples

*"Assuredly, I say to you, **no prophet is accepted in his own country**."*

## Where the same theme goes in Luke

The Gospel is full of it — the good Samaritan, the ten lepers where only the Samaritan
returns, the centurion whose faith is greater than any in Israel.

And Acts, by the same author, is the story of the message going out to exactly the people
these two examples name.

> **What is quoted at them.** Their own history. He does not tell them anything they had
> not read — he points at the two occasions in it when the help went elsewhere.
