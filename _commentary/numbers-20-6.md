One verse, and it is entirely made of movements.

## The turn

*So Moses and Aaron **turned away from the crowd**.*

They do not answer. The assembly has just accused them of dragging the nation out of
Egypt to die. See the note on Numbers 20:2–5.

Neither man argues.

## Where they go

*And went to **the door of the tent of meeting**.*

The **ohel moed** — the tent of appointment, the place God had said He would meet them.

Away from the people and toward the tent. That is the whole geography of the verse.

## The posture

*And they **fell on their faces**.*

Moses does this repeatedly in Numbers. At Korah's rebellion he does it twice — once when
the accusation is made and once when the judgment is announced, both times on behalf of
the people accusing him. See the notes on Numbers 16:1–15 and Numbers 16:16–35.

## What happens

*And **the glory of the LORD appeared to them**.*

The Hebrew is **kavod** — weight, glory.

The same phrase appears in chapter 16 and again in chapter 14, both times in the middle
of a rebellion.

## What is not recorded

Any words. Numbers does not give a prayer, a request, or a complaint. Two men walk to a
door and lie down, and the glory appears.

## The pattern this establishes

The verse before has the accusation. The verse after has the instruction: *take the staff…
speak to the rock.* See the note on Numbers 20:7–9.

Between the problem and the answer there is one verse, and it consists of leaving the
crowd and going to the tent.

## Who is missing

Miriam. She died and was buried at Kadesh in the verse before this chapter's crisis
begins.

Aaron dies at the end of the same chapter, on Mount Hor. And Moses is told in verse 12
that he will not enter the land. See the note on Numbers 20:12.

All three leaders are gone or under sentence by the end of Numbers 20.

## What Moses does elsewhere with the same posture

At the golden calf: *I fell down before the LORD, as at the first, forty days and forty
nights.*

When the spies' report caused the rebellion: *then Moses and Aaron fell on their faces
before all the assembly.*

He does it more than anyone else in Scripture.

## What the verse is made of

Turn away from the crowd. Walk to the door. Go face down.

No prayer is recorded, no request, no defence, and no answer to the accusation. Numbers gives
three movements and then: *and the glory of the LORD appeared to them.*

*But when you pray, **go into your room, and when you have shut your door**, pray to your Father
who is in the secret place; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly.*

## Who else did it

Matthew, of Jesus in Gethsemane: *He went a little farther and **fell on His face, and
prayed**.*

In a garden, on the last night, with a crowd coming for Him — and what He said there is the only
prayer in Scripture that ends *nevertheless, not as I will, but as You will.*

Moses does this more often than anyone else in the Old Testament. It is the posture of a man who
has been accused and will not answer the accusation.

> **The sequence.** Accused, they turn their backs on the crowd, walk to a door, and go
> face down. And the next thing recorded is that the glory showed up.
