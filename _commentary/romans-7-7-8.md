Paul asks the question his argument invites, and defends the law in the same breath as
describing what it did to him.

## The question

*Is **the law itself sin**? **Certainly not**.*

The Greek is **me genoito** — may it never be.

He has just said the passions were *stirred up by the law* and that believers are *delivered
from the law*. Somebody was going to ask.

## The concession

*On the contrary, **I would never have recognized sin except through the law**.*

The Greek is *I would not have known sin*.

Not *would not have sinned*. Would not have known.

## The example

*I would not have known **what wanting-what-is-not-mine was**, if the law had not said,
**"You shall not covet."**"*

The tenth commandment. Paul reaches for the only one of the ten that is entirely internal
— no court can try it, and nobody else can see it.

The man who said he was *blameless* concerning the righteousness in the law names the
commandment that got past his record.

## The mechanism

*But **sin found its opening in that very commandment** and **stirred up in me every kind
of craving**.*

The Greek for *opening* is **aphorme** — a base of operations, a bridgehead. It was a
military term for the place a campaign is launched from. See the note on
Romans 7:9–12.

The prohibition became the staging ground.

## The dormancy

*Apart from the law, **sin lies there as good as dead**.*

The Greek is *sin is dead* — inert, not roused.

Paul is not saying it is absent. He says it is lying still until something wakes it.

## What the paragraph protects

The law, every time. He says it twice more before the chapter turns:

*Therefore **the law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good**.*

And: *we know that **the law is spiritual**, but I am carnal, sold under sin.*

Three defences in a dozen verses.

## Where he puts the fault

*It was **sin — sin showing itself for exactly what it is**.* See the note on
Romans 7:13.

Paul repeatedly personifies sin in this chapter as an agent with intentions: it finds an
opening, it deceives, it kills, it works.

## What the parallel in Genesis is

A command, a suggestion about the command, and a death.

Paul uses **exapatao** for what sin did — *deceived me* — and it is the verb used of the
serpent: *the serpent deceived me, and I ate*.

## Where the chapter goes

*For what I am doing, I do not understand… the good that I will to do, I do not do.* See
the note on Romans 7:18–20.

And the cry, and then chapter 8: *there is no condemnation now — none at all.* See the note
on Romans 8:1–4.

## What Paul says the law does

*By the law is **the knowledge of sin**.* See the note on Romans 3:19–20.

The same conclusion, stated flatly in the earlier chapter and worked out autobiographically
here.

> **The two things held together.** The commandment is holy, and it is what sin used as a
> bridgehead. Paul states both and puts the blame on the second party — not on the
> statute, and not on the giver of it.
