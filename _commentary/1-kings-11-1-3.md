The turn in the story, and the translation marks it in the first clause.

## The hinge

*But here is where **the light dims**.*

The Hebrew opens with a plain **vehammelek Shelomoh ahav** — and King Solomon loved.

Chapter 10 ended with silver as common as stones in Jerusalem and the queen of Sheba saying *the
half was not told me*. See the note on 1 Kings 10:23–25.

Chapter 11 begins here.

## The verb

*King Solomon **loved** many foreign women.*

The Hebrew is **ahav** — the ordinary word for love.

The narrator does not use a euphemism, and does not use a word for lust.

## The list

*Not only **Pharaoh's daughter**, but **Moabite** women, **Ammonites**, **Edomites**, **Sidonians**,
**Hittites**.*

Six nations. Every one of them a treaty.

Royal marriage in the ancient world was diplomacy. Each wife came with an alliance and a
household shrine.

## The warning

*Women from **the very nations the LORD had warned Israel about**, saying, "**Do not go among them, and
do not let them come among you, because they will surely turn your heart toward their gods**."*

Deuteronomy 7, and the reason is stated in the law itself: *for they will turn your sons away from
following Me*.

The prohibition was never about ethnicity. The stated ground is the gods.

## The clinging

*Solomon **held fast to these women; he loved them**.*

The Hebrew is **davaq** — clung, was joined to.

The word from Genesis 2: *a man shall leave his father and mother and **be joined to his wife***.

The verb for covenant loyalty, and it is used here of the wrong attachment.

## The numbers

*Seven hundred wives, princesses, and three hundred concubines.*

A thousand women.

## The law he broke

*Neither shall he **multiply wives for himself**, lest his heart turn away.*

Deuteronomy 17, the law of the king — with the reason attached, in the same words the narrator
uses. See the note on Deuteronomy 17:14–20.

Horses, wives, silver and gold. Solomon has now done all three.

## The last clause

*And **his wives turned his heart**.*

The Hebrew is **hittu et-libbo** — inclined his heart.

The exact outcome the law predicted, in the law's own vocabulary.

## The heart again

The book has tracked one word through the whole reign.

*Give Your servant **a hearing heart**.* — the request.

*A **heart** as wide as the sand on the seashore.* — the gift. See the note on
1 Kings 4:26–34.

*His wives turned **his heart**.* — the end.

## What follows

*For it was so, when Solomon was old, that his wives turned his heart after other gods; and **his
heart was not loyal to the LORD his God, as was the heart of his father David**.*

*Then Solomon built a high place for Chemosh… and for Molech.*

On the Mount of Olives, opposite Jerusalem, and they were still standing three centuries later
until Josiah tore them down.

## The verdict

*So the LORD became angry with Solomon, because his heart had turned from the LORD God of Israel,
**who had appeared to him twice**.*

The narrator counts the appearances. See the note on 1 Kings 3:15.

## What is left

*"**However I will not tear away the whole kingdom; I will give one tribe to your son for the sake
of My servant David**."*

*Nevertheless, the lamp for David still burned, exactly as God had said.* See the note on
1 Kings 11:41–43.

> **The verb.** **Davaq** — clung, was joined to. The word Genesis uses of a man and his wife and
> the prophets use of covenant loyalty, applied here to a thousand women and their gods.
