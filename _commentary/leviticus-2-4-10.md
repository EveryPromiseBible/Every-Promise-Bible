The same offering, three ways of cooking it, and the oil is in all three.

## The three methods

- **2:4**: **baked in an oven** — loaves or thin wafers, the oil mixed in or spread on
- **2:5**: **cooked on a griddle** — an open plate over the fire, the hottest and most
  exposed method. This one is, **2:6**, *broken into pieces with oil poured over it.*
- **2:7**: **prepared in a pan** — a covered vessel, the gentlest of the three

Household kitchens had all three. Whatever equipment a family owned, there was a
version of the offering they could bring.

## The one that is broken

The griddle offering is the only one with the breaking specified (**2:6** again): **broken into
pieces**, and then oil poured on again afterwards.

The fiercest heat, the most exposure, and the oil applied a second time — after
the breaking rather than only before it.

## What is constant

Fine flour in every case. Oil in every case. No yeast in any of them.

Only the fire changes.

## The memorial portion again

**2:9–10**: *He lifts out the memorial portion and burns it on the altar — a fragrance that
pleases the LORD. The rest belongs to Aaron and his sons.*

Same pattern as the uncooked version: a portion into the fire, the remainder
feeding the priests. However it was prepared, it ended up as somebody's meal.

> **On the oil.** In Scripture oil is the standing figure for the Spirit —
> priests and kings were anointed with it, and the word *Messiah* means the
> anointed one. In this chapter the oil is mixed in, spread on, and poured over,
> and it is never optional. Whatever the fire does to the offering, the oil is
> already through it.
