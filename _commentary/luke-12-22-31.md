Two examples from outside the house, and both of them are chosen carefully.

## Why He says it

*Then He said to His disciples, "**This is why** I tell you not to spend your life worrying."*

The Greek is **dia touto** — because of this.

The reason is the previous paragraph: a man whose barns were full and who died that night. *So is
he who lays up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God.* See the note on
Luke 12:16–21.

## The two subjects

*Not about **what you will eat**, and not about **clothes for your body**.*

Food and clothing. The two things everybody needs and nobody can guarantee.

## The principle

*Life is **more than food**, and the body is **more than what you put on it**.*

## The first example

*Think about **the ravens**.*

The Greek is **korakas**.

Matthew's version has *the birds of the air*. Luke names the species.

## Why ravens

They are unclean under the law — listed in Leviticus among the birds not to be eaten.

And Job asks: *who provides food for the raven, when its young ones cry to God?*

Of every bird available, the one chosen is the one an Israelite could not put on the table.

## What they do not do

*They **do not plant and they do not harvest**, they have **no storeroom and no barn**.*

The Greek is **tameion oude apotheken** — no storeroom, no barn.

The barn is the word from the previous parable. The rich man built bigger ones.

## The comparison

*How much more precious are you than birds?*

## The uselessness of worry

*And **which one of you, by worrying about it, can add a single inch to his height?**"*

The Greek is **pechun** — a cubit, about eighteen inches. Applied to **helikia**, which can mean
either height or lifespan.

Either reading makes the point: worry adds nothing.

## The argument from the small

*And **if you cannot manage even the smallest thing, why worry yourself over the rest?**"*

If the least item on the list is beyond you, the anxiety is misdirected.

## The second example

*Think about **the wildflowers**, how they grow.*

They do not labour and do not spin — the two verbs for a man's work and a woman's.

## The comparison

*Not even **Solomon in all his splendor** was dressed like one of them.*

The richest wardrobe in the national memory, and a weed beats it.

## The lifespan

*Grass that is **in the field today and thrown into the oven tomorrow**.*

Dried grass was fuel. The flower is not being praised for lasting.

## The address

*You with your **small, anxious faith**.*

The Greek is **oligopistoi** — little-faiths. One word.

Luke uses it once. Matthew uses it four times, always of the disciples.

## The instruction

*Do not go chasing after what you will eat and what you will drink, and **do not let your mind be
tossed about by it**.*

The Greek is **me meteorizesthe** — do not be raised up, do not be suspended in the air.

**Meteorizo** — the root of *meteor*. The image is of something hanging with nothing under it.

## The reason

*Your **Father already knows that you need them**.*

## The redirection

*Set your heart on the kingdom of God instead, and **all these things will be given to you along with
it**.*

The Greek is **prostethesetai** — will be added.

## What He says immediately after

*"**Do not be afraid, little flock. It is your Father's delight to give you the kingdom**."* See the
note on Luke 12:32–34.

> **The two examples.** A bird nobody could eat and a weed that ends up as oven fuel. He picks the
> two least valuable things in the landscape.
