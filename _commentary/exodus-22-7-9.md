A small piece of civil law about stolen property, and the word *double* appears twice in
it.

## Where it sits

The book of the covenant — the block of case law that follows the ten commandments. The
surrounding rules are about oxen, wells, borrowed animals, and fire spreading through a
field.

Ordinary disputes, decided in ordinary courts.

## The case

**22:7** *If a man **entrusts his neighbor with money or goods for safekeeping**, and **they are
stolen** from that neighbor's house.*

Bailment. Something is left with somebody for keeping and disappears.

## If the thief is found

Still **22:7**: *Then if the thief is found, **he repays double**.*

The Hebrew is **shenayim** — twofold.

Restitution in the law is nearly always more than the loss. Elsewhere in the same chapter
it runs to four and five times for an ox or a sheep.

The principle is that theft costs the thief more than it cost the owner.

## If he is not

**22:8** *The **owner of the house is brought before God** to swear he did not lay hands on his
neighbor's property.*

The Hebrew is *brought to the judges*, and the word is **elohim** — the same word for
God, used of those who judge in His name.

The oath settles it when the evidence cannot.

## The general rule

**22:9** *In every case of dispute — over **an ox, a donkey, a sheep, clothing, or any lost thing
that one claims is his**.*

The Hebrew for *that one claims is his* is *of which one says, "This is it"* — a man
pointing at an object and saying it belongs to him.

## The procedure

Still **22:9**: *Both parties **bring their case before God**.*

Neither side is presumed. Both come.

## The verdict

The end of **22:9**: *And **the one God declares guilty repays his neighbor double**.*

The Hebrew for *declares guilty* is **rasha** — to condemn, to pronounce wicked.

The verb appears twice in the passage: once as what the court does, and once naming the
party it lands on.

## What the ruling produces

Somebody is condemned, and the other party ends up with twice what he lost.

The two outcomes are the same sentence.

## Where doubling turns up in the prophets

**Isaiah 61:7**: *Instead of your shame you shall have **double honour**, and instead of confusion they
shall rejoice in their portion. Therefore in their land **they shall possess double**.*

Isaiah 61, in the chapter Jesus read in Nazareth. See the note on Isaiah 61:1–3.

And Zechariah, **9:12**: *return to the stronghold, you prisoners of hope. Even today I declare that
**I will restore double** to you.*

## What Job received

**Job 42:10**: *Indeed the LORD **gave Job twice as much as he had before**.*

The book counts it: fourteen thousand sheep against seven, six thousand camels against
three, and the same doubling through the list.

## Where the accuser stands

Zechariah 3 has Joshua the high priest in filthy garments, **3:1**: *and Satan standing at his right
hand to oppose him*, and the LORD saying, **3:2**: *the LORD rebuke you*.

The Hebrew for *oppose* is **satan** — to accuse, to stand against in court.

## What Proverbs says about the reverse

*Clearing the guilty and condemning the just, **the LORD hates them both alike**.* See the
note on Proverbs 17:15–21.

Both errors, equally.

> **The shape of the law.** Two parties come, God decides, and the party He condemns pays
> twice. The restoration and the condemnation are one ruling.
