Two men run to a grave, and what convinces one of them is the laundry.

## The start

*So Peter set out, and the other disciple with him, and **the two of them ran together**.*

Mary had come from the tomb saying *they have taken the Lord out of the tomb*. See the note on
John 20:1–2.

## The race

*And **the other disciple ran faster than Peter and reached the tomb first**.*

John records that he won, which is an odd detail to preserve unless it is a memory rather than a
point.

## What he does

*He **bent down and looked in**, and he saw the linen wrappings lying there, **but he did not go
inside**.*

The Greek is **parakupsas** — stooping to peer.

He stops at the entrance. Grave-impurity, or hesitation, or both.

## Peter

*Then Simon Peter came up behind him and **went straight in**.*

The Greek is **eiselthen** — entered.

Which is characteristic. Peter is the one who gets out of the boat and the one who cuts off an
ear.

## What is inside

*And he saw **the linen wrappings lying there**.*

The Greek is **othonia** — the strips of linen the body was bound in, with about seventy-five
pounds of myrrh and aloes between the layers.

**Keimena** — lying. Not thrown, not scattered.

## The cloth

*And **the cloth that had been around His head was not lying with the wrappings but folded up by
itself, in a place of its own**.*

The Greek is **soudarion** — the face cloth. And **entetuligmenon eis hena topon** — wrapped
together into one place.

## What that rules out

Robbery. A thief takes the body wrapped, or leaves the wrappings in a heap.

What is described is order. The wrappings where the body was, the head cloth separately, folded,
in its own place.

## What it might describe

The wrappings still lying in the shape they had held, collapsed, and the face cloth by itself.

John does not say. He records the arrangement and lets it stand.

## Lazarus

*And **he who had died came out bound hand and foot with graveclothes**, and his face was wrapped
with a cloth.*

*Jesus said to them, "**Loose him, and let him go**."* See the note on
John 11:41–44.

Lazarus needed unwrapping. Somebody had to do it for him.

Nothing in this tomb needed unwrapping.

## The belief

*Then the other disciple… went inside too, and **he saw, and he believed**.*

The Greek is **eiden kai episteusen**.

He believes on the strength of folded cloth. Not an appearance, not an angel, not a voice.

## What they did not yet know

*For **until that morning they had not yet understood from the Scriptures that He had to rise from
the dead**.*

The Greek is **dei** — it was necessary.

They had heard Him say it three times. It had not connected to anything they had read.

## What happens next

*And **the disciples went back to where they were staying**.*

They go home.

The most extraordinary thing in the history of the world has just been established in an empty
tomb, and the two witnesses walk back to the house.

## Who stays

*But **Mary stood outside by the tomb weeping**.*

The men leave. She stays, and she is the one He speaks to. See the note on
John 20:11–16.

## The three verbs for seeing

John uses three different Greek words in this paragraph: **blepei** (glances at), **theorei**
(observes carefully), and **eiden** (perceived).

The last one is the one attached to believing.

> **What convinced him.** Not an appearance. The wrappings lying where the body had been, and the
> face cloth folded by itself in its own place.
