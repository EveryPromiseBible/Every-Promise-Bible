Four sentences at the supper table, and the middle one is the reason Peter has a rest
of his life.

## The name, twice

*"**Simon, Simon**."*

Doubling a name in Scripture is how the serious things start — *Abraham, Abraham* at the
knife; *Moses, Moses* at the bush; *Saul, Saul* on the Damascus road.

And He uses the old name. Not Peter, the rock, which He gave him. Simon, which he was
born with.

## The request

*"**Satan has asked to have all of you**, to sift you like wheat."*

The Greek is **exaiteomai** — to demand, to ask for the surrender of something, as a
prosecutor demands a prisoner.

He asked. Somebody had to answer.

This is Job's courtroom again: *Have you considered My servant Job?* See the note on
Job 1:6–12.

## "All of you"

The Greek pronoun in *have all of you* is plural. The whole table is asked for.

The next sentence goes singular.

## Sifting

*"To **sift you like wheat**."*

A sieve is shaken hard. Everything in it is thrown about, and what falls through is
thrown away.

The picture is violent, and it is also agricultural: sifting is what you do to keep the
grain.

## The one prayer

*"**But I have prayed for you, Simon**."*

Singular now. Satan asked for all eleven; Jesus names one.

The Greek for *prayed* is **deomai** — to beg, to make petition out of need.

And the tense is aorist: it is already done, before the courtyard, before the fire,
before the girl asks the question.

## What He prayed for

*"That **your faith would not give out**."*

Not that he would not fall. He does fall, three times, with an oath.

The prayer is for the thing underneath — that the collapse would not go all the way
down.

## The other side of it

*"And **when you have turned back**, strengthen your brothers."*

**When**, not if. He is given his assignment after the failure while the failure is
still in the future.

The Greek for *strengthen* is **sterizo** — to fix firmly, to set in place. Peter uses it
in his own letter about God doing it to them. See the note on 1 Peter 5:10.

## Peter's answer

*"Lord, **I am ready to go with You anywhere — to prison, to death**."*

He means it. He draws a sword in the garden a few hours later and takes a swing at an
armed party.

## The reply

*"I tell you, Peter, **the rooster will not crow today before you have said three times
that you do not know Me**."*

Here the name changes back — **Peter**, the rock, in the sentence predicting the
denial.

## What happened

The rooster crowed, *and the Lord turned and looked at Peter*, and he went out and wept
bitterly.

Then a beach, a charcoal fire, and three questions. And at Pentecost the man standing up
to preach to Jerusalem is Simon Peter.

> **What was decided before the denial.** The prayer, the outcome and the assignment.
> All three are stated at the table, while he is still insisting it will not happen.
