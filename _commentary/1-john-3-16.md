John gives the definition of a word everyone thought they already knew.

## The claim

*This is **how we have come to know the love of God**.*

The Greek is **egnokamen** — perfect tense, we have come to know and know still.

He is not describing a feeling arrived at. He is pointing at an event and saying that is
where the knowledge comes from.

## The definition

*He **gave His own life away for us**.*

The Greek is *He laid down His life* — **tithemi**, to put down, to place.

It is the verb Jesus uses of Himself in John's Gospel: *no one takes it from Me. **I lay
it down of My own accord**.* See the note on John 10:16–18.

The same writer, the same word, decades apart.

## What John refuses to do

Define love by how it feels. His letters use the word more than any other book in the New
Testament and he never once describes an emotion.

Every time, he points at the cross or at an action.

## The consequence

*And **we ought to give our own lives away for the brothers**.*

The Greek is **opheilomen** — we are indebted, we owe it. It is a financial word.

## What comes next

*But whoever has this world's goods, and **sees his brother in need**, and **shuts up his
heart from him**, how does the love of God abide in him?*

John immediately scales it down from dying to a coat and a meal, which is the harder
test.

## The verse it turns on

*My little children, **let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth**.*

The Greek is *not in word, neither in tongue*.

## The verse number

3:16 in both — John 3:16 and 1 John 3:16, the same author, the same subject, from the two
sides:

*God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son.*

*He gave His own life away for us; and we ought to give our own lives away for the
brothers.*

## The other definition in the letter

*In this is love, **not that we loved God, but that He loved us**, and sent His Son to be
the propitiation for our sins.*

John insists on the direction every time he defines it. See the note on 1 John 4:8.

## What it produces

*And by this **we know that we are of the truth**, and shall assure our hearts before
Him.*

*For if our heart condemns us, **God is greater than our heart**, and knows all things.*

That last sentence comes two verses later, and it is the letter's answer to a heart that
will not settle.

## Who is writing

The disciple who leaned on His chest at the supper and stood at the cross, and who was
given a mother from it.

He saw the laying down.

> **What he does with the word.** Refuses to let it float. John says *this is how we know
> what love is* and then points at one afternoon — and the very next sentence is about a
> coat.
