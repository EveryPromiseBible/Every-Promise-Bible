title: "I was no prophet"

A priest tells a prophet to go home, and the prophet's reply begins by agreeing with him.

## What Amaziah said

*"Amos is plotting against you in the very heart of Israel. The land cannot bear all his words."*

And then, to Amos directly: *"Go, you seer! Flee to the land of Judah. **There eat bread, and there
prophesy.** But never again prophesy at Bethel, for it is the king's sanctuary and the royal
residence."*

Two accusations in one: sedition, and doing it for money.

## The reply

*"**I was no prophet, and no son of a prophet.**"*

The Hebrew is **lo navi anokhi velo ven-navi anokhi**.

*Son of a prophet* is a technical term — the prophetic guilds, the schools Elisha oversaw. Amos
says he has neither the office nor the training.

He does not defend his credentials. He denies having any.

## What he was

*"**I was a herdsman, and I tended sycamore figs.**"*

**Boqer** — a cattleman. And **boles shiqmim** — a piercer of sycamore figs. The fruit had to be
punctured a few days before harvest to ripen it properly, and it was poor men's food.

Two jobs, both agricultural, neither of them prestigious.

## The turn

*"**But the LORD took me from following the flock, and the LORD said to me, 'Go, prophesy to My
people Israel.'**"*

The Hebrew is **vayyiqqacheni** — and He took me.

The only qualification he offers is that he was collected.

*For you see your calling, brethren, that **not many wise according to the flesh, not many mighty,
not many noble, are called**. But **God has chosen the foolish things of the world** to put to shame
the wise… **that no flesh should glory in His presence**.*

**Paul** in **1 Corinthians** 1, and the last clause is the reason.

*Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were **uneducated and
untrained men**, they marvelled. And they realised that they had been with **Jesus**.*

**Acts** 4, and it is the same scene: an official body, two men with no standing, and an
explanation that is not about their training.

## Who else was taken from a flock

*He chose David His servant, and **took him from the sheepfolds**; from following the ewes that had
young He brought him.* See the note on Psalms 78:70–72.

Moses was keeping Jethro's sheep at the burning bush. **Jesus** called four fishermen off a beach
and a tax collector out of a booth.

## The king's sanctuary

*"For it is **the king's sanctuary and the royal residence**."*

Amaziah's own description of Bethel gives the case away. He is not defending a place of worship.
He is defending a government building.

*"**We ought to obey God rather than men.**"*

## What Amos says to him

The sentence that follows is the harshest personal word in the book, and it is delivered by a
fig-farmer to the chief priest of the national shrine.

> **The credential.** He has none, says so, and names the two agricultural jobs he did instead —
> and then puts one verb between them and the pulpit: *the LORD took me*.
