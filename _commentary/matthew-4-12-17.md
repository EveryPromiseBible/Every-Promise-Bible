Matthew records a change of address and treats it as a fulfilled prophecy.

## The move

*He left **Nazareth** and went to live in **Capernaum**, a town beside the sea in
the territory of **Zebulun and Naphtali**.*

Matthew names both tribes, which is not how anyone in the first century gave
directions. The tribal boundaries had not mattered for centuries.

He names them because Isaiah did.

## What Isaiah had said

*"Land of Zebulun and land of Naphtali, along the road to the sea, beyond the
Jordan, **Galilee of the nations** — the people sitting in darkness have seen a
great light."*

Isaiah 9, seven hundred years earlier. The chapter that goes on to say *for unto us
a Child is born… and His name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, the Mighty
God, the Everlasting Father, the Prince of Peace.*

## Why those two tribes

They were the first to go. When Assyria came, 2 Kings records that Tiglath-Pileser
took *Gilead and Galilee, all the land of Naphtali*, and carried them away.

The first territory to fall into the dark is named as the first to see the light.

## "Galilee of the nations"

The Greek is *Galilee of the Gentiles*. It had been mixed country for centuries,
and the Judeans looked down on it — *can anything good come out of Nazareth?* and
*search and look, for no prophet has arisen out of Galilee.*

That is where He moved.

## Zebulun and Naphtali

Their blessings in Genesis 49 are one line each:

- **Zebulun** — *he will be a harbour for ships.* His name means dwelling, and this
  is where He dwelt.
- **Naphtali** — *a doe set free.* This is where the woman bound for eighteen years
  was loosed.

See the notes on Genesis 49:13 and Genesis 49:21.

## "Sitting in darkness"

Not walking. Sitting — settled in it, no longer trying to get out.

And *the land where death casts its shadow*. Isaiah's word is the same one in Psalm
23: *the valley of the shadow of death.*

## What He began to say

*"**Change your minds and turn to God**, because the kingdom of heaven has come
near."*

The Greek is **metanoeo** — to change the mind, to think differently afterwards.
And the reason given is not a threat. It is proximity: because the kingdom has come
near.

> **What is on the other side of Isaiah's sentence.** *On those living in the land
> where death casts its shadow, **light has dawned**.* The verb is a sunrise. Nobody
> in the dark did anything to produce it; the morning simply came up over them.
