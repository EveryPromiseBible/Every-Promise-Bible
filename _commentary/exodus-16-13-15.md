A nation wakes up to find breakfast on the ground and has no word for it.

## The dew first

*In the morning **a layer of dew lay all around** the camp. **When the dew lifted**,
there on the surface of the desert lay something fine and flaky.*

It comes wrapped. The dew is underneath and above it — Numbers adds *when the dew fell
on the camp in the night, the manna fell on it.*

It was never in contact with the ground.

## What it looked like

*Something **fine and flaky, delicate as frost** on the ground.*

Numbers 11 adds the rest: *like coriander seed, pale like resin*, and it *tasted like
something baked with oil.*

## The name

*The Israelites looked at it and said to one another, "**What is it?**" — for they had
**no idea what it was**.*

The Hebrew is **man hu**. The question became the noun.

For forty years they ate something whose name was a question, because nothing in
their experience had a word for it.

## Moses' answer

*"**This is the bread the LORD has given you to eat.**"*

He does not explain what it is either. He says where it came from.

## The rules attached to it

The rest of the chapter is the arrangement, and every part of it is unusual:

- **enough for one day** — gather a day's portion each day
- **it bred worms** if kept overnight, and stank
- **except on the sixth day**, when a double portion kept perfectly for the Sabbath
- *he who gathered much had nothing left over, and he who gathered little had no
  lack*

That last line is the strangest. Whatever a man collected, it came out to an omer
when it was measured.

Paul quotes it in 2 Corinthians 8, about the collection for Jerusalem.

## Why it had to be daily

The one thing you could not do with manna was store it.

A nation of former slaves, who had spent four hundred years with nothing secured, was
fed by something that would not keep — every day, for forty years, and it stopped the
day they ate the produce of Canaan.

## Where it was kept

One omer of it was put in a golden pot and placed in the ark, beside Aaron's rod
that budded and the tablets of the covenant.

The only manna that did not rot is the jar that went into the holiest place.

## What Jesus said about it

*"**Moses did not give you the bread from heaven**, but My Father gives you the true
bread from heaven."*

And: *"Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and are dead."* See the note on
John 6:47–51.

> **What Revelation promises.** *To him who overcomes I will give some of the **hidden
> manna** to eat.* The jar in the ark, offered to the reader.
