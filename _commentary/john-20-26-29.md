He comes back for one man, and repeats the man's own conditions to him.

## The week

*Eight days later His disciples were inside again, and **this time Thomas was with them**.*

He had refused their testimony flatly — *unless I see… I will not believe*. See the note on
John 20:24–25.

And he stayed with them anyway, for a week.

## The doors

*The doors were shut, and **Jesus came and stood among them**.*

The Greek is **ton thuron kekleismenon** — the doors having been shut. Perfect participle.

Same detail as the first appearance. John records it twice.

## The greeting

*"**Peace be with you**."*

**Eirene humin** — the ordinary Hebrew greeting, **shalom aleichem**.

The same words He used the previous Sunday, when Thomas was out.

## The turn

*Then He said **to Thomas**.*

No rebuke first. No comment on the absence.

## The offer

*"**Put your finger here — look at My hands. Reach out your hand and put it into My side**."*

The three conditions Thomas had named, in his order, offered back to him.

## What that means

Jesus was not in the room when Thomas said it.

The offer quotes a sentence He was not there to hear.

## The instruction

*"**Do not go on doubting; believe**."*

The Greek is **me ginou apistos alla pistos** — do not be unbelieving but believing.

Literally *stop becoming faithless*. Present imperative, addressing a direction of travel rather
than a fixed state.

## What Thomas does not do

Touch anything.

The Gospel records the offer and then records his answer. It does not say he reached out.

## The confession

*"**My Lord and my God**."*

The Greek is **ho Kurios mou kai ho Theos mou**.

Both nouns with the definite article and the possessive.

## What it is

The highest christological statement in the Gospel, and the last thing said by a disciple in it.

John opened with *the Word was God*. The last confession before the epilogue closes the frame.

## Whose words they are

The Roman emperor Domitian required the title **dominus et deus noster** — our lord and our god.

John's readers in Asia knew the phrase and where it was normally directed.

## The verdict

*"Thomas, **you have believed because you have seen Me**."*

Statement or question — the Greek allows both.

## The beatitude

*"**How happy are the ones who have not seen and have believed anyway**."*

The Greek is **makarioi hoi me idontes kai pisteusantes**.

One of only two beatitudes in John. The other is *you are blessed if you do them*, after the foot
washing. See the note on John 13:12–17.

## Who it is for

Everybody reading.

The last people in the Gospel to be called blessed are the ones who were not there.

## What John says next

*And truly Jesus did many other signs in the presence of His disciples, which are not written in
this book.*

*But **these are written that you may believe** that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that
believing you may have life in His name.*

The purpose statement of the whole Gospel, placed immediately after the beatitude on people who
have only the book.

> **The offer.** He repeats Thomas's own three conditions back to him — a sentence He was not in
> the room to hear — and Thomas does not take Him up on any of them.
