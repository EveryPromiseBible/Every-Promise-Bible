The third option for a peace offering, and then a rule that outlives the tabernacle.

## The three grades

The chapter allows a bull, a sheep, or a goat. This is the third.

The procedure is identical in all three, which is the point of repeating it: the animal
changes and nothing else does.

## The hand

*Lay your hand on its head.*

**Samakh** — pressing down. See the note on Leviticus 3:1–5.

## The portions

*The **fat covering the inner parts**, both **kidneys** with the fat around them, and the
**covering of the liver**.*

The same list, third time. Leviticus is a manual and does not vary its wording.

## The word for it

*The priest burns these on the altar **as food**.*

The Hebrew is **lechem** — bread, food.

The offering is called God's food. Nothing in the Old Testament suggests He needs it — Psalm
50 is blunt about that: *if I were hungry, I would not tell you; for the world is Mine, and
all its fullness*.

The term describes the table, not an appetite.

## The claim

*All the fat belongs to the LORD.*

The Hebrew is **kol-chelev laYHWH**.

**Chelev** — fat, and by extension the best of anything. *The fat of the land*, and Genesis
uses it of the best of Egypt.

## The rule

*This is a **lasting rule for every generation, wherever you live**: you are **not to eat any
fat or any blood**.*

The Hebrew is *a perpetual statute throughout your generations in all your dwellings*.

Not only at the sanctuary. Wherever they lived, including centuries later in Babylon, and
including now.

## Why blood

*For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it to you upon the altar to make
atonement for your souls; for it is the blood that makes atonement for the soul.*

Leviticus 17 explains that one at length. The blood is reserved because of what it does.

## Why fat

The text does not explain, and the association with **chelev** as *the best* is the usual
reading: the choicest part is not eaten by the worshipper.

Whatever else the peace offering shares out — and it shares more than any other — the best of
it is not on the human side of the table.

## What is left over

The peace offering is the only sacrifice the worshipper eats. God takes the fat, the priest
takes the breast and the right thigh, and the man takes the rest home. See the note on
Leviticus 7:28–34.

The rule about fat is what marks the boundary of his portion.

## The time limit

*If the sacrifice of his offering is a vow or a voluntary offering, it shall be eaten the
same day… but on the third day the remainder shall be burned with fire.* See the note on
Leviticus 7:11–18.

A thanksgiving peace offering had to be finished the same day, which meant a whole animal and
one evening — so the family invited people.

## Where the phrase goes

*And they shall eat the fat of the land*, said of Egypt. And the prodigal's father: *bring the
fatted calf here and kill it*. See the note on Luke 15:21–24.

The Hebrew idiom for the best of something is the word this chapter reserves.

> **The clause with the widest reach.** *Wherever you live.* A rule about the altar, extended
> to every household in every generation — the best of it, unconditionally, is His.
