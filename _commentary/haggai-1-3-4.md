Two buildings compared, and the same sixteen years produced both.

## The messenger

*Then **the word of the LORD came again through Haggai**.*

The Hebrew is **beyad Chaggai** — by the hand of Haggai.

The book uses this phrasing rather than *to* Haggai. He is the conduit, not the subject, and the
book tells us nothing about him — no father, no tribe, no home town.

His name means *festal, my feast*.

## The question

*"**Is it truly the time for you** to be living in your own **paneled houses, warm and finished**,
while **this house lies in ruins**?"*

Their word was **et** — time. *It is not the time.* See the note on Haggai 1:2.

God takes the same noun and puts it in the question.

## The word for panelled

**Sephunim** — covered, wainscoted, roofed with finished timber.

It is the word used of Solomon's temple: *he built the walls of the house within with boards of
cedar… he covered them on the inside with wood*.

The wood treatment that had been reserved for the house of God is now on their own walls.

## The word for ruins

**Chareb** — desolate, dried up, wasted.

## What the comparison is not

An attack on comfortable housing. Nothing here says the houses are too grand.

The charge is the pairing. One building got finished and the other did not, in the same span of
years, by the same people.

## What their time and money went on

Whatever was in front of them.

Nobody voted to abandon the temple. They just kept doing the next necessary thing, for sixteen
years.

## The verdict on the result

*You have **sown much, and bring in little**; you eat, but do not have enough; you drink, but you
are not filled with drink… and **he who earns wages, earns wages to put into a bag with holes**.*
See the note on Haggai 1:6.

The economy in which the houses got panelled was not working.

## What they are told to do

*"**Give careful thought to your ways**."*

The Hebrew is **simu levavchem al-darkeichem** — set your heart upon your ways. See the note on
Haggai 1:5.

## The instruction

*"**Go up into the hills. Bring down timber and build the house**. Then I will take pleasure in it,
and I will be honored."* See the note on Haggai 1:7–8.

Timber again — the same material that went into the panelling.

## What Jesus asks about buildings

*"Do you see these great buildings? **Not one stone shall be left upon another**."*

Said of the temple this one eventually became, rebuilt magnificently by Herod.

## What the house is now

*Do you not know that **you are the temple of God** and that the Spirit of God dwells in you?* See
the note on 1 Corinthians 3:16–17.

*In whom **you also are being built together for a dwelling place of God** in the Spirit.* See the
note on Ephesians 2:19–22.

## What Haggai promises the second temple

*"**The glory of this latter temple shall be greater than the former**," says the LORD of hosts.*

Said to old men who had wept when the foundation was laid because they remembered Solomon's. See
the note on Haggai 2:6–9.

The building they were embarrassed by is the one a carpenter's son would walk into.

> **The comparison.** Not that the houses were too fine. That both buildings had sixteen years,
> and only one of them got a roof.
