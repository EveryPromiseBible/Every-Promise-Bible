The doxology, and it is the most quoted thing in the letter — twenty-four verses of warning, and
then this.

## Who is able

*"**Now — there is One with the power to hold you on your feet.**"*

**To de dynameno phylaxai hymas aptaistous** — to Him who is able to guard you from stumbling.

**Aptaistos** — not stumbling, sure-footed. It occurs once in the New Testament. It is used in Greek
of a horse that does not trip.

**Jude** has spent the letter describing people who did fall — *wandering stars*, *clouds without
water*, *twice dead, pulled up by the roots* — and he does not end by telling his readers to be
careful.

He tells them who is holding them up.

## The bracket

*"Kept for Jesus Christ."* Verse 1. See the note on Jude 1:1–2.

The letter opens and closes on the same verb and the same subject, and everything frightening is in
between.

*Who are **kept by the power of God through faith** for salvation.*

*"**My Father, who has given them to Me, is greater than all; and no one is able to snatch them out
of My Father's hand.**"*

## What He presents

*"**And to stand you up in front of His own glory with nothing wrong with you and with great joy.**"*

**Katenopion tes doxes autou amomous en agalliasei** — before His glory, blameless, in exultation.

**Amomos** — without blemish. It is the technical word for a sacrificial animal fit for the altar,
and the New Testament uses it of **Christ**: *a lamb without blemish and without spot*.

It is used of Him, and then of us:

*Just as He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world, **that we should be holy and without
blame before Him in love**.*

*That He might present her to Himself a glorious church, not having spot or wrinkle or any such
thing, but **that she should be holy and without blemish**.*

Three times **amomos**, and only once is it something anybody produced.

## The joy

**Agalliasis** — exultation, the shout kind of gladness.

The verse does not say the person presented is relieved. It says there is a great deal of noise.

*"**He will rejoice over you with singing.**"* See the note on Zephaniah 3:16–17.

## Who is being praised

*"**To the only God, our Saviour, through Jesus Christ our Lord** — glory, majesty, power and
authority, before all time, now, and for all the ages to come."*

Four nouns, and three tenses: before all time, now, forever.

*"**Through Jesus Christ our Lord.**"* Some manuscripts omit it and the best include it. The praise
is routed rather than direct, which is the New Testament's ordinary practice.

*For **all the promises of God in Him are Yes, and in Him Amen, to the glory of God through us**.*

## Why the doxology is where it is

Because a letter about people who did not last would otherwise leave its readers checking
themselves.

**Jude** does not finish with an exhortation. He finishes with the name of somebody able to keep
them, and the last word of the letter is **Amen**.

> **The adjective that occurs once.** *Aptaistos* — sure-footed, used of a horse that does not trip.
> Twenty-four verses about people who fell, and the last two are about Somebody else's ability to
> keep the reader standing.
