A sound like an ocean and like thunder, and one word carried on it.

## Where the word occurs

**Hallelouia** appears four times in the New Testament. All four are in this chapter, and there are
none anywhere else.

The church has sung it every week for twenty centuries, and in the Greek Scriptures it is reserved
for one scene. It is a Hebrew word left untranslated — **halelu-Yah**, *praise Yah* — carried
straight across from the Psalms into a Greek book without being converted, the way a word is when
everybody already knows it.

## The sound

*Like a huge crowd, like **the roar of a great deal of water**, like heavy thunder rolling.*

Three attempts at the same noise, and John cannot settle on one. He is a fisherman describing surf,
then a man describing weather, and neither is adequate.

He had heard the voice of the Son of Man described the same way in the first chapter — *His voice as
the sound of many waters*. Now the crowd sounds like Him.

## "Has begun to reign"

*The Lord God, who holds all power, **is ruling**.*

**Ebasileusen** — an aorist. Not *reigns eternally*, which was always true, but *has taken up the
reign*, a moment when something long true became visible.

The same shape as the shout at the seventh trumpet: *the kingdoms of this world **have become** the
kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ.*

## What the shout is announcing

Read what the crowd says next, because the reign and the wedding are the same event: *let us be
glad… **for the marriage of the Lamb has come***.

This is not a coronation followed later by a celebration. The moment God's rule becomes visible is
the moment a bride is brought in, and the Ruler in question is described as a Lamb — the same word
John used the first time he saw Him standing in heaven *as though it had been slain*.

> **The universe is not shouting about power alone.** The One taking the throne is the One who was
> killed for the people cheering. That is why the loudest sound in Scripture is a word of praise
> rather than fear, and why the scene it belongs to is a wedding.
