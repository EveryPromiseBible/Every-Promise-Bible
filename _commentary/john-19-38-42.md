The men who come forward are the two nobody expected.

## Who asks

*Joseph from Arimathea **asked Pilate for permission to take the body**.*

The other Gospels fill him in: a rich man, *a prominent council member*, *a good and just
man* who *had not consented to their decision and deed*.

A member of the Sanhedrin, asking a Roman governor for the body of a man his own council had
just condemned.

## What it cost him

Mark says he *taking courage, went in to Pilate*.

Crucified bodies were normally left, or thrown into a common grave. Asking for one identified
you with it.

## The description

*He was a disciple of Jesus, **though quietly, because he was afraid of the Jewish
leaders**.*

The Greek is **kekrummenos** — having been hidden.

John states the fear and then reports the action. Both are in the same sentence.

## The second man

*Nicodemus came too — **the man who had first come to Jesus at night**.*

John reminds us every time. He has done it three times now: the night visit in chapter 3, the
defence in the council in chapter 7, and here. See the note on John 3:4–8.

The reminder is the point. He is introduced by his caution, and then he does this.

## What he brought

*A mixture of **myrrh and aloes, about a hundred Roman pounds** of it.*

Roughly seventy-five pounds in modern weight. An enormous quantity — royal burial scale.

Herod's funeral procession, according to Josephus, had five hundred servants carrying
spices.

Nicodemus turns up at a public execution with a king's burial.

## The wrapping

*The two of them took the body of Jesus and **wrapped it in linen cloths with the spices**,
which is how the Jewish people prepare a body for burial.*

Two members of the council, handling a corpse on the eve of Passover — which made them
ceremonially unclean and disqualified them from eating it.

## Where the disciples are

Not here. Eleven men are somewhere behind locked doors, and the burial is done by two
councilmen and, watching, some women.

*And Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses observed where He was laid.*

## The garden

*Now there was **a garden in the place where He was crucified**.*

John is the only one who mentions it, and John is the one whose Gospel started *in the
beginning*.

A garden is where the first man was put, and where he lost it.

Three days later Mary Magdalene *supposed Him to be the gardener*.

## The tomb

*And in the garden **a new tomb, one no one had ever been laid in**.*

Matthew says it was Joseph's own, *which he had hewn out of the rock*.

He gave away his grave.

## The reason

*Because it was **the Jewish day of preparation**, and because **that tomb was close by**.*

Sunset was coming and the Sabbath with it. The choice of tomb was made by the clock.

## What Isaiah wrote

*And they made His grave with the wicked, but **with the rich at His death**.*

Executed as a criminal, buried in a wealthy man's rock-cut tomb. Both halves of the sentence
happen on the same afternoon.

## What the two of them had in common

Both were on the council. Both had kept quiet. Both come forward when the movement looks
finished and there is nothing left to gain.

> **When they stopped hiding.** Not after the resurrection. On the Friday, before sunset,
> when the body needed carrying and the eleven were not there.
