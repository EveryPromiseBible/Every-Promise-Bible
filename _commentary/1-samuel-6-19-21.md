The ark comes home after seven months among the Philistines, and the welcome ends
badly.

## What happened

*God struck down some of the men of Beth-shemesh because they looked into the ark
of the LORD.*

They opened it. The lid — the mercy seat, the place God had said He would speak
from — was lifted, and what was under it was the law.

Numbers had laid down that even the Levites who carried the holy things were not
to look at them, on pain of death. These were farmers in a wheat field who took
the covering off.

## The number

The Hebrew reads oddly — seventy men and fifty thousand — and the figure has long
been discussed, since Beth-shemesh was a small town. Some manuscripts and early
witnesses have only the seventy.

The text as it stands records both, and the mourning that followed.

## Their question

*Who can stand before the LORD, this holy God? Where can the ark go from here?*

It is the right question, asked too late. And their answer is to send it
somewhere else — the ark goes on to Kiriath-jearim, where it stays twenty years.

Nobody in the chapter asks how a person might come near safely. They ask who will
take it off their hands.

## What the passage is actually about

The uncovered law kills. That is the plainest reading of it, and it is the same
point the New Testament makes in different words — *the letter kills, but the
Spirit gives life.*

What made the ark approachable on the Day of Atonement was blood on the lid and
smoke over it. Take the covering away and what is left is holiness with nothing
between.

> **Read with Exodus 25:17–22.** God had said He would meet them *above the mercy
> seat*. Not at the tablets — above them, over the covering. Beth-shemesh is what
> happens when the covering comes off, and it is why the arrangement was given in
> the first place.
