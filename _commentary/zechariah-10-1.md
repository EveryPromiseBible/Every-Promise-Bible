One verse, and it is an instruction about timing.

## The season named

*Ask the LORD for rain **in the springtime**.*

The Hebrew word is the one for the **latter** rain — the spring rain that comes
just before harvest and fills out the grain. See the note on Deuteronomy 11:13–15.

So this is not a general encouragement to pray about weather. It is: when the
season for the last rain arrives, ask for it.

## Why ask for something already promised

Deuteronomy had promised both rains to a people who kept the covenant. Zechariah,
centuries later, tells them to ask anyway.

The promise does not remove the asking. It is what makes the asking sensible —
you request what has already been undertaken, in the season it belongs to.

## Who it is that gives it

*For it is the LORD who makes the storm clouds.*

The line matters because of what the verse before it says. Zechariah has just
described people consulting household idols and diviners, who *speak nonsense and
give empty comfort*, which is why the flock wanders like sheep with no shepherd.

Set against that, this is a redirection: not the teraphim, not the diviner. Ask
the One who actually makes the clouds.

## The scope

*He gives showers of rain **to all**, and green plants in the field to everyone.*

Rain falls across a district, not on one field. What is asked for privately
arrives publicly, over everybody's ground.

## Who sends it to everybody

*"He gives showers of rain **to all**, and green plants in the field to everyone."*

*"That you may be sons of your Father in heaven; for **He makes His sun rise on the evil and on the
good, and sends rain on the just and on the unjust**."*

**Matthew** 5, and **Jesus** uses the same fact for the same reason: rain does not fall on one
field. It is His argument for loving enemies, and it rests on the indiscriminateness Zechariah
notices.

*"Nevertheless He did not leave Himself without witness, in that He did good, **gave us rain from
heaven and fruitful seasons**, filling our hearts with food and gladness."*

**Paul** at Lystra, to a crowd who had just tried to sacrifice to him.

## Asking for what is promised

The promise does not remove the asking. It is what makes the asking sensible.

*Elijah was a man with a nature like ours, and **he prayed earnestly that it would not rain**; and
it did not rain on the land for three years and six months. **And he prayed again, and the heaven
gave rain**, and the earth produced its fruit.*

**James** 5, and the point he draws is that the man was ordinary. Elijah had been told by God that
rain was coming — *go, show yourself to Ahab, and I will send rain on the earth* — and then went up
Carmel and prayed seven times for it anyway.

*"**Ask, and it will be given to you**; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to
you."*

*"For **your Father knows the things you have need of before you ask Him**."*

**Matthew** 6 puts the knowing and the asking in the same paragraph and treats neither as making the
other pointless.

## The latter rain

The spring rain that fills out the grain just before harvest.

*Therefore be patient, brethren, until the coming of the Lord. See how the farmer waits for the
precious fruit of the earth, waiting patiently for it **until it receives the early and latter
rain**.*

**James** again, and he uses the two rains as the picture of waiting for **Christ**.

*"And it shall come to pass in the last days, says God, that **I will pour out of My Spirit on all
flesh**."*

**Peter** at Pentecost, quoting Joel — a book whose promise of the latter rain is in the same
paragraph as the outpouring.

## What is being redirected from

The verse before has people consulting household idols and diviners who *speak nonsense and give
empty comfort*.

*"Their idols are silver and gold, made by human hands… **they have mouths but cannot speak**."* See
the note on Psalms 115:4–8.

*And in that day you will ask Me nothing. Most assuredly, I say to you, **whatever you ask the
Father in My name He will give you**.*

> **Where it sits in the book.** Zechariah 9 and 10 are full of restoration —
> a king coming humble on a donkey, prisoners released, the house of Judah
> strengthened. The instruction to ask for rain belongs to that: a people being
> told to expect a season and to say so out loud.
