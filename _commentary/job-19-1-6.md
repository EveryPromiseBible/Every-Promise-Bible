Job counts, and then draws a line his friends have been crossing.

## What words do

*"How long will you keep **tormenting me and grinding me to pieces with words**?"*

The Hebrew for *grinding to pieces* is to crush, to break something small. It is
used of pounding grain.

He is naming the weapon. Not the boils, not the losses — the talking.

## "Ten times"

*"**Ten times now** you have insulted me, and you are not ashamed to wound me
so."*

He has been keeping count. Ten is a round number in Hebrew for a full tally, the
whole of it.

*You are not ashamed.* Not that they are wrong — that they feel nothing about it.

## The line he draws

*"Even if I have gone wrong, **that is mine to carry, not yours**."*

It is a complete answer to all three of them. Suppose he is guilty. It is between
him and God, and none of it is their business to prosecute.

The Hebrew is *my error remains with me*.

## What they were really doing

*"If you really mean to **lift yourselves above me** and **use my disgrace as
proof against me**…"*

Two things he names, and both are about them rather than him.

They are using his condition as evidence, and they are standing on it. A man on
the ground is useful to a theology that says the upright are never on the ground.

## What he concludes

*"Then understand this. **It is God who has bent me down** and drawn His net tight
around me."*

Job's settled conviction, and he holds it for the whole book: God did this.

The reader knows better. Chapter 2 said in plain words that *the adversary went
out from the presence of the LORD and struck Job* — and Job is never told. See the
notes on Job 1:6–8 and Job 2:7–8.

Everyone in the argument is working from the same wrong premise. The three friends
and Job all agree God sent it; they only differ on whether He was right to.

## Where the chapter goes

Thirteen verses further on, out of the middle of all this, comes the most famous
sentence in the book:

*I know that my Redeemer lives, and He shall stand at last on the earth… and in my
flesh I shall see God.*

**Redeemer** is **go'el** — the kinsman-redeemer, the relative whose job was to buy
back what a man had lost.

He is crushed, convinced God has netted him, and he says it anyway.

> **What he asks for two verses before that.** *Oh, that my words were written…
> engraved on a rock forever.* They were.
