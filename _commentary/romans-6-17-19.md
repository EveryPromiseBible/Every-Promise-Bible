Paul thanks God for something and the sentence starts with what they used to be.

## The thanks

*But **thanks be to God** that, **though you were slaves of sin**, you **obeyed from the
heart**.*

The Greek is **charis to theo** — grace to God.

He thanks God for their obedience, which locates the credit.

## From the heart

The Greek is **ek kardias** — out of the heart.

Not compliance. Paul specifies where the obedience came from before he says what it was
directed at.

## The odd phrase

*The **pattern of teaching into whose care you were handed**.*

The Greek is **tupon didaches eis hon paredothete** — the form of teaching to which you were
delivered.

It should be the other way round. Teaching is delivered to people.

Paul reverses it: the people were handed over to the teaching.

## What *tupos* is

A mould, a die, the shape a thing is struck into. The word behind *type*.

Something molten is poured in and comes out the shape of the mould.

Paul uses the same word of Adam — *a type of Him who was to come* — and of the wilderness
events: *these things became our examples*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 10:11–13.

## The result

*Having been **set free from sin**, you became **slaves of righteousness**.*

The Greek is **eleutherothentes** — an aorist passive participle. Having been freed, once,
by someone else.

And then immediately enslaved again, to something else. Paul will not leave anybody
unattached.

## The apology

*I am speaking in **ordinary human terms**, because the old nature in you is weak.*

The Greek is **anthropinon lego** — I speak humanly. And *because of the weakness of your
flesh*.

He is uneasy with the slavery image. It says the ownership is total, which is what he wants,
and it says the service is grudging, which is not.

So he flags it as a concession to their capacity and keeps using it.

## The parallel

*Just as you once **handed over the parts of yourself as slaves to impurity and to
lawlessness**… so now **hand them over as slaves to righteousness**.*

The Greek is **ta mele** — the members, the limbs.

Same verb both halves, same objects. The instruction is to do with the body exactly what
they used to do with it, aimed elsewhere.

## The runaway clause

*Lawlessness **that only bred more lawlessness**.*

The Greek is *lawlessness unto lawlessness* — **anomian eis ten anomian**.

The first road has no destination. It circles back into itself and produces more of the same.

## The other road's destination

*Which leads to **being made holy**.*

The Greek is **eis hagiasmon** — unto sanctification.

One direction arrives somewhere. The other does not.

## The wage and the gift

*For **the wages of sin is death**, but the **gift of God is eternal life** in Christ Jesus
our Lord.*

The Greek is **opsonia** — a soldier's pay, the rations and coin he has earned.

And **charisma** — a free gift.

Paul has run the two slaveries in exact parallel for four verses, and then breaks the
parallel in the last line of the chapter. One pays what is owed. The other gives what is
not.

## What has been established before this

*The old man was crucified with Him… he who has died has been freed from sin.* See the note
on Romans 6:6–7.

And *reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus*. See the
note on Romans 6:8–11.

Every instruction in this chapter comes after those statements and rests on them.

> **The direction the delivery runs.** Not the teaching handed to them. *You were handed over
> to the teaching* — and the word for teaching is the word for a mould.
