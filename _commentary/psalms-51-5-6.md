Two verses in the middle of the most honest psalm in the book.

## The heading

*A Psalm of David when Nathan the prophet went to him, after he had gone in to Bathsheba.*

The adultery, the arranged death of Uriah, a year of silence, and then a prophet with a story
about a poor man's lamb: *you are the man*.

This is what David wrote afterwards.

## What he has already asked

*Have mercy upon me, O God, according to Your lovingkindness… blot out my transgressions. Wash
me thoroughly from my iniquity.*

And: *against You, You only, have I sinned, and done this evil in Your sight*.

Which is remarkable given who else was harmed, and it is a statement about who defines the
offence rather than a denial that anybody else was involved.

## The confession

*See, **I was shaped in wrongdoing**, in sin my mother carried me.*

The Hebrew is **cholalti** — I was brought forth, formed.

He is not blaming his mother, and he is not excusing himself. He is going back further than the
incident.

A man caught in one act says the problem is older than the act.

## The turn

*But **You long for truth in the hidden places**.*

The Hebrew is **battuchot** — a rare word, usually rendered *inward parts* or *the hidden
being*.

**Emet** — truth, faithfulness, reliability.

What is wanted, in the sentence, is not performance but honesty in the part nobody sees.

Which is what the psalm is: a man writing down the worst thing he ever did and putting it in the
national songbook.

## The request

*So **teach me wisdom deep inside**.*

The Hebrew is **besatum** — in the closed-up place, the sealed part.

Two words for the same region in one verse: the hidden, and the sealed.

## What he asks for next

*Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me.* See the note on
Psalms 51:10–12.

The Hebrew is **bara** — create. The verb from Genesis 1, whose subject in the Hebrew Bible is
always God.

Not repair. Make.

## What he does not ask for

His reputation, the child's life, or the situation to be undone.

The requests in the psalm are all interior: wash, blot out, create, renew, restore.

## What he offers

*For **You do not desire sacrifice, or else I would give it**; You do not delight in burnt
offering.*

*The sacrifices of God are **a broken spirit; a broken and a contrite heart** — these, O God, You
will not despise.*

The king who could have brought a herd brings the one thing that cannot be bought.

## What he promises

*Then I will teach transgressors Your ways, and sinners shall be converted to You.*

The psalm is that promise being kept. Three thousand years of transgressors have read it.

## Where the hidden part turns up in the New Testament

*For he is not a Jew who is one outwardly… but he is a Jew who is one **inwardly**; and
circumcision is that of the heart, in the Spirit.*

And Peter: *the hidden person of the heart, with the incorruptible ornament of a gentle and
quiet spirit, which is very precious in the sight of God*.

> **What is asked for in the sealed part.** Not improvement. Truth — and the psalm itself is
> what that looks like: the worst thing a king ever did, written down and handed to the choir.
