James settles the council by quoting a prophet, and the choice of text is the argument.

## Who is speaking

*James answered.*

The Lord's brother, who had not believed during the ministry — *for even His brothers did not
believe in Him* — and is now presiding over the Jerusalem church.

Paul lists a resurrection appearance to him: *after that He was seen by James*.

## The opening

*"**Brothers, listen to me**."*

## The summary

*"**Simeon** has described how God **first visited the Gentiles to take from them a people for
His name**."*

He uses the Hebrew form of Peter's name — **Symeon** — which he does nowhere else in Acts. He is
speaking to a Jewish assembly and marking Peter as one of them.

**Visited** — the Greek is **epeskepsato**, the word for God coming to look after somebody. It is
in Zacharias' song: *the Lord God of Israel has visited and redeemed His people*.

## The test he applies

*"And **the words of the prophets agree with this**."*

Not the other way round. James does not start with the text and deduce the policy.

Something happened at Cornelius' house; he checks it against the prophets and finds it fits.

## The quotation

*"**After this I will return and rebuild the fallen tent of David. I will rebuild its ruins and
set it upright again.**"*

Amos 9:11. See the note on Amos 9:11–12.

The Greek is **skenen** — tent, tabernacle. Not **oikos**, house.

## Why the tent and not the temple

The prophecy does not say God will rebuild the house of David, which would be about a dynasty.

It says the **tent** — and there was a specific one.

David pitched a tent in Jerusalem for the ark, with no veil, singers around it continuously,
while the tabernacle of Moses stood at Gibeon six miles away with the altar and the priests.
See the note on 2 Samuel 6:12–15.

For roughly forty years the ark sat in the open under canvas in the capital, and anyone could
approach.

## The consequence in the text

*"**So that the rest of humanity may seek the Lord — all the nations who are called by My
name**."*

That is the clause James is quoting for.

The rebuilt tent is not for Israel's sake in Amos' sentence. It has a purpose attached, and the
purpose is the nations.

## The Hebrew and the Greek

Amos in Hebrew has *that they may possess the remnant of **Edom**, and all the Gentiles who are
called by My name*.

The Greek Old Testament has *that the rest of **men** may seek the Lord*.

**Edom** and **adam** differ by vowel points, which were not written. And *possess* and *seek*
differ by one consonant.

James quotes the Greek, and the New Testament writers generally do.

## The signature

*"Says the Lord, who does all these things. **Known to God from eternity are all His works**."*

## The judgment

*"Therefore I judge that we should **not trouble those from among the Gentiles who are turning to
God**."*

Four requests are made of them, and circumcision is not among them. See the note on
Acts 15:10–12.

## What the tent had that the temple did not

No veil.

The tabernacle at Gibeon had one, and behind it nothing — the ark was in Jerusalem.

David's tent had the ark, uncovered, with music round it, and no barrier.

## The other torn veil

*And behold, the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom.* See the note on
Matthew 27:45–47.

> **Which building the prophecy names.** Not the house of David. The *tent* — and there had been
> one, in Jerusalem, with the ark in it and nothing in front of it.
