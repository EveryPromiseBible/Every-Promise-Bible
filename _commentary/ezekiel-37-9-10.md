title: "Prophesy to the breath"

The bones have come together and are still dead, and there is a second sermon to preach.

## What has already happened

*There was a noise, and suddenly a rattling; and the bones came together, bone to bone… the
sinews and the flesh came upon them, and the skin covered them over.*

Ezekiel prophesied to the bones and the valley reassembled.

*But **there was no breath in them**.*

A complete army, anatomically finished, lying on the ground. The first sermon produced bodies and
not life.

## The second command

*"**Prophesy to the breath.** Prophesy, son of man, and say to the breath… come from the four
winds, breath, and **breathe upon these slain, that they may live**."*

The Hebrew is **ruach** — breath, wind, spirit. It occurs four times in this one verse and ten
times in the chapter, and the translator has to pick a different English word almost every time.

*Come from **the four winds**.* **Me'arba ruchot** — from the four **ruachs**. The same noun again.

## Where the word comes from

*And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and **breathed into his nostrils the
breath of life**; and man became a living being.*

Genesis 2. A body made from the ground and inert until something is breathed into it, which is
exactly the sequence Ezekiel is watching in a valley.

## Where it goes

*And when He had said this, **He breathed on them, and said to them, "Receive the Holy Spirit."***

**John** 20, in a locked room, to men who had scattered. The Greek is **enephysesen** — and it is
the verb the Greek Old Testament uses in Genesis 2 and here. It appears in the New Testament once.

*And suddenly there came a sound from heaven, as of **a rushing mighty wind**, and it filled the
whole house where they were sitting.*

**Acts** 2, and **Luke**'s word is **pnoes biaias** — a violent breath. Ezekiel calls the four
winds; Pentecost has one arrive with a noise.

## What happens

*And the breath came into them, and they came to life. **They stood up on their feet, a vast army,
too many to number**.*

The Hebrew is **chayil gadol me'od me'od** — an exceedingly, exceedingly great army.

*And you He made alive, **who were dead in trespasses and sins**… **even when we were dead** in
trespasses, made us alive together with Christ.*

**Ephesians** 2, and **Paul** twice states the condition before the verb. The dead do not
cooperate with being raised, which is the whole point of choosing a valley of bones for the
picture.

*God, **who gives life to the dead and calls those things which do not exist as though they did**.*

## What Ezekiel is asked first

*"Son of man, **can these bones live?**" So I answered, "O Lord GOD, You know."*

The one honest answer available, and God does not correct him.

## The graves

*"Behold, O My people, **I will open your graves and cause you to come up from your graves**… Then
you shall know that I am the LORD."*

Eleven verses later, and the picture stops being a metaphor for the nation's return.

*And the graves were opened; and **many bodies of the saints who had fallen asleep were raised**;
and coming out of the graves after His resurrection, they went into the holy city and appeared to
many.*

**Matthew** 27. Graves opened at a death, in the only place Scripture records it happening.

> **The two sermons.** The first assembles the bodies and they stay dead. Ezekiel has to preach a
> second time, to the breath — and John 20 uses the one verb the Greek Old Testament uses in
> Genesis 2 and in this valley.
