Elisha raises the Shunammite's boy, and every step of it is worth counting.

## What it took

- he **shut the door** on the two of them
- he **prayed** to the LORD
- he **climbed onto the bed and lay over the boy** — mouth to mouth, eyes to
  eyes, hands to hands
- the child's body **grew warm**
- he **got up and walked back and forth** across the room
- he **bent over him once more**
- the boy **sneezed seven times** and opened his eyes

Seven movements. A closed door, a prayer, physical contact along the whole length
of the body, a pause, and a second attempt.

The prophet has to give himself to it — literally lie on the boy and cover him,
limb for limb.

## "Take your son"

*Elisha said, "**Take your son**." She came in, fell at his feet, and bowed to the
ground. Then she took her son and went out.*

She has to come in and collect him. And she goes to the floor first.

## Where this happened

Shunem, in the Jezreel valley. The name means double resting place.

A few miles away over the ridge of Mount Moreh — whose name means teacher — is
Nain, which means pleasant.

Two towns in the same valley, either side of one hill, and a boy raised from the
dead in each of them.

## The comparison

At Nain there is no closed door, no prayer, no stretching over the body, no
pacing, no second attempt.

*"Young man, I say to you, get up."*

One sentence, spoken in the open air, in front of two crowds. He does not shut
anybody out and He does not ask God for anything — He addresses the boy.

And at the end: *Jesus **gave him back to his mother**.* Elisha said *take your
son.* Jesus carried him over. See the note on Luke 7:11–17.

## What Elisha had

He had asked for a double portion of Elijah's spirit and had received it. He is
the greatest of the prophets by the count of his miracles.

And this is what the best of them had to do to raise one boy.

> **What the woman had said on the way.** Her son had died on her lap that morning,
> and she saddled a donkey, rode to Carmel, and when Gehazi asked if all was well
> she answered *it is well.* She said it to Gehazi and she said it to her husband
> before she left. She had not told anybody the boy was dead.
