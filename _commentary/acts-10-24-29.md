Peter walks into a Roman officer's house and finds a congregation waiting.

## The preparation

*Cornelius **was expecting them** and **had called together his relatives and close
friends**.*

He had been told to send for a man who *will tell you what you ought to do*, and he
assembled an audience for it.

Nothing in the angel's message said to invite anybody.

## The greeting

*As Peter came in, Cornelius met him, **fell at his feet and bowed low**.*

A Roman centurion on the floor in front of a Galilean fisherman.

## The refusal

*But Peter **lifted him up** and said, "**Stand up. I am only a man myself**."*

The Greek is *I myself am also a man*.

Paul and Barnabas say the same at Lystra when the crowd tries to sacrifice to them: *we
also are men with the same nature as you.*

And the angel in Revelation, twice, when John falls down: *see that you do not do that. I am
your fellow servant.*

## What Peter states first

*"You know that it is **against our law for a Jewish man to associate with a foreigner or to
visit him**."*

The Greek is **athemiton** — unlawful, taboo.

It was custom rather than written Torah — the Old Testament nowhere forbids it, and Jews
did trade and live among Gentiles. But the practice was real and everybody in the room knew
it.

Peter names the wall out loud before crossing it.

## The turn

*"**But God has shown me that I should call no one unholy or unclean**."*

The Greek is **medena** — no man.

The sheet had animals in it. This is Peter's interpretation, and it is about people.

He worked it out on the road from Joppa. See the note on Acts 10:9–16.

## The consequence

*"That is why **I came without objection when I was sent for**."*

The Greek is **anantirretos** — without contradiction, without raising an argument.

## The question

*"So **may I ask why you sent for me?**"*

He has walked thirty miles and still does not know.

## What Cornelius answers

The whole story of the vision, and then: *"**Now therefore, we are all present before God, to
hear all the things commanded you by God**."*

It is one of the best sentences in Acts, and it comes from a Gentile soldier.

## What Peter says next

*"**In truth I perceive that God shows no partiality**."* See the note on
Acts 10:34–43.

The Greek is **prosopolemptes** — a receiver of faces, one who judges by appearance.

## What interrupts him

*While Peter was still speaking these words, **the Holy Spirit fell upon all those who heard
the word**.*

Mid-sermon. Peter reports it that way in Jerusalem: *as I began to speak, the Holy Spirit
fell on them just as He did on us at the beginning.* See the note on Acts 11:15–18.

Nobody had been baptised, circumcised or instructed.

## What Peter asks the six men who came with him

*"**Can anyone forbid water**, that these should not be baptised **who have received the Holy
Spirit just as we have**?"*

## What it cost him

He is put on trial in Jerusalem for it in the next chapter — not for preaching to Gentiles,
but for eating with them.

## Where the wall goes

*For He Himself is our peace, who has made both one, and **has broken down the middle wall
of separation**.*

Paul, in Ephesians, writing about the same barrier.

## What Peter says in the house

*"**In truth I perceive that God shows no partiality. But in every nation whoever fears Him and works
righteousness is accepted by Him.**"*

And then, four verses on, what that acceptance runs through:

*"**To Him all the prophets witness that, through His name, whoever believes in Him will receive
remission of sins.**"*

**Peter** does not tell Cornelius he is already fine. He tells him God is not partial, and then
preaches **Jesus** to him for the rest of the paragraph — and the Spirit interrupts him.

## The worship he will not take

*Cornelius met him and fell down at his feet and worshipped him. But **Peter** lifted him up, saying,
"**Stand up; I myself am also a man.**"*

*"**Worship God!**"*

The angel says the same to **John** in **Revelation**, twice. Everybody in the New Testament who is
offered worship refuses it, with one exception:

*And Thomas answered and said to Him, "**My Lord and my God!**" Jesus said to him, "Because you have
seen Me, you have believed."*

## What he had to be argued out of

*"**You know how unlawful it is for a Jewish man to keep company with or go to one of another
nation.**"*

It took a vision repeated three times and an audible instruction from the Spirit to get **Peter** as
far as a doorway.

*For **He Himself is our peace**, who has made both one, and has broken down the middle wall of
separation.*

> **What Peter says on the doorstep.** Names the custom he is breaking, states what God
> showed him, and then asks why he was invited — because he still does not know.
