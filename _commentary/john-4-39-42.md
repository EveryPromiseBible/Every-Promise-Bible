A Samaritan town asks Him to stay, and He does.

## What started it

*Many of the Samaritans from that town **believed in Him because of what the woman said**.*

The Greek is **dia ton logon tes gunaikos** — because of the word of the woman.

A woman whose personal history was public knowledge in that town, whose testimony would not
have been accepted in a Jewish court, and whose statement to them was a question.

## What she said

*"**He told me everything I ever did**."*

That is the whole content of it.

Not a doctrine. She reports what happened to her, and adds *could this be the Christ?*

## The request

*So when the Samaritans **came out to Him**, they **kept asking Him to stay with them**.*

The Greek is an imperfect — they went on asking.

Samaritans, asking a Jewish rabbi to be their houseguest. The verse at the start of the
chapter explains why that is remarkable: *for Jews have no dealings with Samaritans*.

They had been enemies for four centuries — the Samaritans built a rival temple on Gerizim and
the Jews burned it down.

## The stay

*And **He stayed there two days**.*

Two days in a Samaritan town, eating their food and sleeping in their houses.

Luke has a village of Samaritans refusing to receive Him later, and James and John offering
to call down fire. He rebuked them.

## The second wave

*Many more believed **because of His own words**.*

The Greek is **dia ton logon autou** — because of His word.

John uses the identical construction for both groups. Because of her word, and because of
His.

## What they say to her

*"**It is no longer because of what you told us that we believe. We have heard Him for
ourselves**."*

It reads harshly and is not meant to. It is what any evangelist wants: the people you told
have stopped needing you.

## The title

*"And we know that this really is **the Saviour of the world**."*

The Greek is **ho soter tou kosmou**.

The phrase appears twice in the New Testament, and this is the first. It is said by
Samaritans, about a Jewish teacher, in a town outside Israel proper.

It was also a title used of Caesar.

## The harvest He had pointed at

*"Lift up your eyes and look at the fields, for they are already white for harvest."* See the
note on John 4:31–34.

He said that with these people walking out of the town toward Him across open ground.

## What He had said about sowing and reaping

*"Here the saying holds true: one sows and another reaps. I sent you to reap that for which
you have not laboured; others have laboured, and you have entered into their labours."*

## What Acts does with the town

Philip goes down to Samaria and preaches, *and there was great joy in that city*. Peter and
John come down and lay hands on them.

The commission had named it: *you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and
Samaria, and to the end of the earth*.

Samaria is third on the list, and it starts with a woman at a well.

## What the whole episode rests on

One conversation, at noon, with one person, in the wrong country. See the note on
John 4:25–26.

> **What the town told her.** *We no longer believe because of what you said.* Which is the
> outcome any witness would want, and it took two days.
