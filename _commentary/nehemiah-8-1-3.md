---
key: Nehemiah 8:1–3
title: From early morning until midday
updated: 2026-08-09
---
A city that has just finished a wall asks for a book, and stands in the square to hear it.

## Who asks

*And **they asked Ezra the scribe to bring out the book** of the Law of Moses.*

Nobody summons them. The wall was finished in fifty-two days, and the first thing the people
do with a secure city is send for the Scriptures.

## Where they stand

*In the open square **in front of the Water Gate**.*

Not the temple. A public square, outside the sacred precinct, where anybody could come —
including the people who were not allowed further in.

## Who is there

*Men and women and **everyone old enough to understand**.*

Said twice in three verses. The qualification is not age, rank or sex — it is whether a person
can follow what is being said.

## How long

*From early morning until midday.*

Five or six hours of reading, standing. And the next chapter has them back the following day,
and reading *day by day, from the first day until the last day* for a week.

## The last clause

*And **every ear was turned toward the book** of the Law, listening.*

The Hebrew is *the ears of all the people were toward the book*.

## What happens to them

They weep. And they are told to stop — *this day is holy; do not mourn nor weep… for the joy of
the LORD is your strength*. See the note on Nehemiah 8:9–10.

## What the reading could not do

*For **the law made nothing perfect**, but the bringing in of a better hope did, through which
we draw near to God.*

Five hours of the Law produced a nation in tears, and the correction was administered the same
morning by the men who had read it to them.

## Where the same scene happens again

*And He came to Nazareth… and as His custom was, **He went into the synagogue on the Sabbath
day, and stood up to read**.*

**Jesus** is handed the scroll of Isaiah, reads four lines about good news to the poor and
liberty to the captives, rolls it up, gives it back, sits down — and says *today this Scripture
is fulfilled in your hearing*. See the note on Luke 4:16–21.

Ezra read for five hours and the people wept. He read four lines and said the waiting was
over.

> **What they asked for.** Not a speech and not a celebration. A wall had just gone up in
> fifty-two days, and the people gathered as one and asked for the book.
