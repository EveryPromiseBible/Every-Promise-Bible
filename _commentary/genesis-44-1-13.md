The last test, and Joseph builds it so that the brothers have every reason to walk away.

## The instruction

*"Fill the men's sacks with **as much food as they can carry**, and put **each man's silver
in the mouth of his sack**."*

The same trick as the first trip, and it had terrified them then.

## The addition

*"And put **my cup, the silver one, in the mouth of the youngest one's sack**."*

Benjamin. The one their father would not let out of his sight, and the one Judah has
personally guaranteed.

## The staging

*At dawn the men were sent on their way. **They hadn't gone far from the city** when Joseph
said to his steward, "**Go after those men**."*

Not far. They are close enough to be brought back and far enough to have committed to the
road.

## The accusation

*"**Why have you repaid good with evil?** Isn't this **the cup my master drinks from and
uses for divination**? You've done a wicked thing."*

Divination by cup was a known Egyptian practice — liquid poured and read.

Joseph, who has told Pharaoh outright that interpretations belong to God, has the steward
describe him in Egyptian terms. He is still in character.

## Their answer

*"Your servants **would never do anything like that**. We even **brought back to you from
Canaan the silver we found in our sacks**. Why would we steal silver or gold?"*

The argument is a good one. Men who returned money they could have kept do not steal a
cup.

## What they swear

*"**If any of your servants is found with it, let him die, and the rest of us will become my
lord's slaves**."*

They offer more than is asked. They are certain.

## The steward's terms

*"Very well. But **only the one found with it will be my slave; the rest of you are
free**."*

The condition is deliberately generous. Ten men can go home clean.

That is the whole design of the test: an exit is provided.

## The search

*Each man **quickly lowered his sack to the ground** and opened it.*

They are so confident they unload before being asked.

*The steward searched, **beginning with the oldest and ending with the youngest**.*

The order draws it out. Ten sacks come up clean, one after another, and the tension rises
with each one.

## The find

*And **the cup was found in Benjamin's sack**.*

## What they did

*At this **they tore their clothes in grief. They loaded their donkeys and returned to the
city**.*

Nobody takes the exit.

Twenty-two years earlier these men sold a brother into Egypt and told their father a wild
animal had eaten him. Offered the chance to leave Rachel's other son behind and go home
free, all ten turn round.

That is what Joseph was testing, and it is the answer.

## What Judah says in the next chapter

The longest speech in Genesis — the whole story from their father's side, and then: *let
your servant remain instead of the lad as a slave to my lord, and let the lad go up with
his brothers. **For how shall I go up to my father if the lad is not with me?**"*

The man who proposed the sale offers to take the place.

## What happens immediately after

*Then Joseph could not restrain himself… and he cried out, "**Make everyone go out from
me!**" And he wept aloud.*

*"**I am Joseph; does my father still live?**"*

> **What the test was for.** Not the cup. Joseph gave them a clean way to abandon Rachel's
> remaining son and go home — and every one of them tore his clothes and went back.
