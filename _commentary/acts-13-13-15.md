Three verses of travel notes, and two things in them matter later.

## The change of name

*Paul and his companions set sail from Paphos.*

Luke has said *Barnabas and Saul* through the whole account until Cyprus. From here on it
is **Paul**, and Barnabas comes second.

The switch happens in the verse about the sorcerer — *then Saul, who is also called Paul*
— and the order of the names never goes back. See the note on Acts 13:9–12.

## The departure

*There **John left them and went back to Jerusalem**.*

John Mark. Luke gives no reason.

It becomes the sharpest recorded disagreement between two believers in the New Testament:
at the start of the second journey Barnabas wants to take him and Paul refuses, *because
he had departed from them in Pamphylia and had not gone with them to the work*.

*Then the contention became so sharp that they parted from one another.*

Barnabas takes Mark to Cyprus; Paul takes Silas through Syria.

## How it ends

Decades later, in Paul's last letter: *Get Mark and bring him with you, for **he is useful
to me for ministry**.*

And Mark writes a Gospel.

## The synagogue

*On the Sabbath day they **went into the synagogue and sat down**.*

Paul's method throughout Acts. He goes to the synagogue first in every city, and he says
why in this very chapter: *it was necessary that the word of God be spoken to you
first.* See the note on Acts 13:44–47.

## The order of service

*After **the reading of the law and the prophets**.*

Two readings — a portion from the Torah and one from the Prophets. The pattern is the same
one Luke describes in Nazareth, where Jesus was handed Isaiah. See the note on
Luke 4:16–21.

## The invitation

*The rulers of the synagogue sent word to them: "**Brothers, if you have any word of
encouragement for the people, please speak**."*

The Greek is **logos parakleseos** — a word of exhortation, of comfort. It was the
standard term for the sermon after the readings.

Visiting teachers were routinely asked. Nobody in the room has any idea what they are
about to hear.

## What follows

Paul stands, motions with his hand, and delivers the longest of his recorded sermons —
Israel's history from Egypt to David, then the resurrection, then the two sentences the
whole thing was built toward. See the note on Acts 13:38–39.

## What the invitation cost them

By the next sabbath nearly the whole city was outside the building, the leaders were
contradicting him, and Paul and Barnabas were expelled from the district.

> **How it starts.** A polite formula at the end of a normal service — *if you have any
> word of encouragement* — offered to two travellers who had just sat down at the back.
