The chronicler names fourteen men and then counts the days.

## The rising

*Then **the Levites arose**.*

The response to Hezekiah's speech is one verb, and then a list.

## The names

Fourteen men, and the chronicler groups them by family:

- **Kohathites** — Mahath and Joel
- **Merari** — Kish and Azariah
- **Gershonites** — Joah and Eden
- **Elizaphan** — Shimri and Jeuel
- **Asaph** — Zechariah and Mattaniah
- **Heman** — Jehuel and Shimei
- **Jeduthun** — Shemaiah and Uzziel

The first four groups are the working divisions of Levi. The last three — Asaph, Heman
and Jeduthun — are the musicians, the three families David appointed over the song.

The singers turn out for the clearing.

Kohath was Korah's branch, the one that carried the holy things. See the note on
Numbers 16:1–15.

## What they did first

*They **gathered their brothers, consecrated themselves**, and went in.*

The order Hezekiah gave, carried out in the order he gave it: themselves, then the
building.

## The authority

*As **the king had commanded**, according to **the words of the LORD**.*

Both. The chronicler puts the royal order and the divine word in the same clause.

## The division of labour

*The **priests** went into **the inner part** of the house of the LORD to cleanse it, and
they **brought out to the courtyard** all the unclean things they found.*

*The **Levites** took them and **carried them out to the Kidron Valley**.*

A chain. The priests go where only priests may go, bring things as far as the court, and
the Levites take them the rest of the way.

Nobody does the whole distance.

## Where it went

The Kidron — the ravine between the temple hill and the Mount of Olives.

It is where Asa burned his grandmother's idol, where Josiah burned the Asherah and beat
it to dust, and where the blood from the temple sacrifices drained.

Jesus crossed it on the way to Gethsemane.

## The count

*They began the consecration on **the first day of the first month**, and on **the eighth
day** they came to **the porch** of the LORD.*

*For **eight more days** they consecrated the house, and on **the sixteenth day** of the
first month **they finished**.*

Eight days to get from the inner house out to the porch. Eight days on the rest.
Sixteen in all.

## What the count tells you

The chronicler could have said *they cleansed the temple*. He gives the calendar
instead, twice.

It took over two weeks, and the first eight days produced no visible progress beyond the
porch.

## What it cost them

The Passover had to be moved. Chapter 30 opens with Hezekiah keeping it in the second
month rather than the first — the law's provision for those who were unclean or on a
journey — *for they could not keep it at the regular time, because a sufficient number of
priests had not consecrated themselves.*

The cleaning ran past the feast.

## What was found

The text says *all the unclean things they found in the temple* without listing them.
Ahaz's Damascus altar, the dismantled lavers, whatever had accumulated behind sixteen
years of shut doors.

> **The shape of it.** A speech, a list of names, and a date at each end. The chronicler
> gives more space to the calendar than to the contents, and the number he lands on is
> sixteen.
