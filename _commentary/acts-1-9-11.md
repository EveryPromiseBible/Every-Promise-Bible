They watched Him go, and two men had to tell them to stop looking.

## What they saw

*While they were **looking straight at Him**, He was lifted up, and a cloud took
Him out of their sight.*

Luke is careful about this — they were watching. It happened in daylight, in front
of eleven men, on a hillside outside Jerusalem.

## The cloud

Not weather. The cloud in Scripture is the visible sign of the presence — the
pillar in the wilderness, the cloud that filled the tabernacle and then the
temple, the bright cloud at the transfiguration.

## Standing there

*They were **still standing there staring into the sky** as He went.*

Luke says it twice, and the two men in white say it back to them as a question:
*why are you standing here looking into the sky?*

Nobody rebukes them for grief. They are simply moved along — there is a room in
Jerusalem to go to and ten days to wait.

## The phrase

*"**This same Jesus**, who has been taken from you into heaven, will come back
**in the same way** you have watched Him go."*

Both halves matter.

**This same Jesus.** Not a different one, not an altered one. The Man they had
walked with for three years — the one who wept at a grave, cooked breakfast on a
beach, touched a leper, and would not break a bruised reed.

That is who went up, and that is who is coming back.

## "In the same way"

Bodily, visibly, and to the same place. Zechariah says it: *in that day His feet
will stand on the Mount of Olives* — which is precisely the hill they were standing
on when they were told this.

## What He was doing as He left

Luke's Gospel gives the detail Acts leaves out: *He lifted up His hands and blessed
them. And it came to pass, while He blessed them, He was parted from them and
carried up into heaven.*

His hands were up and He was in the middle of a blessing. The blessing was never
finished — He went up mid-sentence.

## Who He is now

*He has taken His seat at the right hand of God's throne.* Every priest under the
old covenant stood, because the work was never done. He sat down.

And He *always lives to make intercession for them* — the same Man, doing that,
now.

> **The last thing recorded of the disciples in that chapter.** *They returned to
> Jerusalem with great joy.* He had just left, and Luke says joy. They had been
> told He was coming back exactly as He went.
