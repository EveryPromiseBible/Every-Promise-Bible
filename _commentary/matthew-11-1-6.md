The man who identified Him sends to ask whether he got it right.

## Who is asking

*Now **John, sitting in prison**, heard about the things the Messiah was doing.*

This is John the Baptist. He had seen the Spirit descend and remain, and had said
*behold, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world.* He had refused to
baptise Him at first.

He is in Herod's fortress at Machaerus, and he will be beheaded there.

## The question

*"**Are You the One who was coming, or should we keep watching for someone else?**"*

He is not asking a general question about the Messiah. He is asking about the man he
pointed at.

Matthew records it without any embarrassment and without any explanation.

## Why he might ask

He had preached a Messiah with an axe and a winnowing fan: *the axe is laid to the
root of the trees… He will thoroughly clean out His threshing floor, and burn up the
chaff with unquenchable fire.*

What he is hearing about instead is healings and preaching in villages, and no axe
anywhere — and he is in a cell.

## The answer

*"**Go back and tell John what you are hearing and seeing.**"*

Not a rebuke, not a reassurance, and not a direct yes.

Evidence, and John is left to draw the conclusion.

## The list

- *blind people are seeing*
- *crippled people are walking*
- *lepers are being made clean*
- *deaf people are hearing*
- *dead people are being raised*
- *and **the poor are having the good news brought to them***

It is a quotation. Isaiah 35 and Isaiah 61, stitched together — the passages that
describe what happens when God comes.

Jesus answers a question about His identity by listing what is going on and letting
the prophets do the identifying.

## What is not on the list

The axe. The fire. The threshing floor.

And one more thing: Isaiah 61 says *to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the
opening of the prison to those who are bound* — and Jesus had read that scroll aloud
in Nazareth. He leaves that clause out of the message He sends to a man in prison.

John is not getting out.

## The last line

*"And **blessed is the one who does not stumble over Me**."*

The Greek is **skandalizo** — to be tripped up, to take offence.

It is a beatitude, and it is addressed to a man whose expectations were not being
met and who was going to die in a cell.

## What Jesus says about him afterwards

The moment the messengers leave, He turns to the crowd:

*"What did you go out into the wilderness to see? A reed shaken by the wind?… Among
those born of women there has not risen one greater than John the Baptist."*

Not one word of criticism, said behind his back, about a man who had just asked
whether He was real.

> **The word for the doubt.** John does not stop believing in God. He asks a
> question, in the dark, of the person he is asking about — and gets an answer, and a
> character reference he never heard.
