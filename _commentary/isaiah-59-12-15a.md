The nation's own confession, and it is unusually specific.

## Who is speaking

Israel, in the first person plural.

The chapter opened with God's answer to a complaint: *behold, the LORD's hand is not shortened,
that it cannot save; nor His ear heavy, that it cannot hear. **But your iniquities have
separated you from your God**.*

Verses 3 to 8 are His indictment. From verse 9 the people take over the sentence and finish it
themselves.

## The admission

*Our sins are **piled high before You**. Our wrongs **testify against us**.*

The Hebrew is *our transgressions are multiplied before You, and our sins testify against us*.

Legal language. The sins are witnesses for the prosecution.

## The knowledge

*We **carry our failures with us, and we know them well**.*

The Hebrew is *as for our iniquities, we know them*.

Not ignorance. They can name the charges.

## The list

*Turning our backs on the LORD.*

*Lying about our God.*

*Refusing to follow Him.*

*Speaking oppression and revolt.*

*Brewing false words in our hearts and letting them spill out.*

Five items, and four of the five are about speech.

The Hebrew for *brewing* is **hago** — to mutter, to conceive. The picture is of words being
worked up inside before they come out.

## The consequence

*So **justice is pushed back**, and **righteousness stands far off**.*

Two personified figures, backing away.

## The square

*Truth **stumbles in the public square**, and **honesty cannot get in**.*

The Hebrew is **rechov** — the open place inside the gate, where courts sat and business was
done.

Truth is pictured falling over in the one place it was supposed to stand up.

And **nekhochah**, uprightness, *cannot enter* — barred at the door.

## The verdict

*Truth is **nowhere to be found**, and **whoever turns from evil is treated like prey**.*

The Hebrew is *he who departs from evil makes himself a prey*.

The man who stops doing wrong becomes a target. In a society running the way this one is, that
is the cost of stepping out.

## What comes next

*The LORD saw it, and it grieved Him… **and He was astonished that no one stepped in**. So His
own arm brought salvation.* See the note on Isaiah 59:12–15ab–17.

The confession ends and God's response begins in the middle of verse 15.

Nothing in the passage records the people asking for help.

## The chapter's own diagnosis

*Your iniquities have separated you from your God; and your sins have hidden His face from you,
so that He will not hear.*

Which is the answer to the complaint the chapter opened with.

## Where Paul quotes this chapter

Romans 3, in his chain of quotations: *their feet are swift to shed blood; destruction and
misery are in their ways; and the way of peace they have not known*.

That is Isaiah 59:7–8, in the middle of the passage where Paul is proving *there is none
righteous, no, not one*.

## What is not said

Any request. The people give an accurate account of the situation and stop.

The rescue in the next verse is not answering a prayer.

> **What the list is mostly about.** Speech. Four of the five items they confess are things
> said — and the picture that follows is truth falling over in the marketplace.
