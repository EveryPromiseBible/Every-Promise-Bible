Paul explains his own motive, and the verb is a physical one.

## The grip

*For **the love of Christ has us in its grip**.*

The Greek is **sunechei** — holds together, presses in on both sides, confines.

It is used of a crowd pressing round, of a city under siege, and by Luke of a woman with a
fever: *Simon's wife's mother was **taken with** a great fever*.

The older versions have *constrains*. The picture is of something narrowing the options.

## Whose love

**He agape tou Christou** — and the Greek can mean His love for us or ours for Him.

The context settles it. What follows is *One died on behalf of all*, which is His.

## The condition

*Once we have **settled this**.*

The Greek is **krinantas** — having judged, having reached a verdict.

A conclusion has been drawn, and the grip follows from it.

## The verdict

*That **One died on behalf of all, and therefore all of them died**.*

The Greek is **huper panton** — on behalf of all.

And then the deduction: **ara hoi pantes apethanon** — therefore all died.

Not *therefore all should die*. Something happened to them when it happened to Him.

Paul says the same in Romans: *do you not know that as many of us as were baptised into Christ
Jesus were baptised into His death?* See the note on Romans 6:6–7.

## The purpose

*And He died on behalf of all so that **those who go on living should live no longer for
themselves**.*

The Greek is **hoi zontes** — the living.

## The alternative

*But **for the One who died for them and was raised**.*

Two things about Him, and the second is the one that makes the first liveable.

## Why Paul is explaining himself

The Corinthians had been questioning his motives. Chapters 10 to 13 are a defence.

The verse before this one: *for if we are beside ourselves, it is for God; or if we are of sound
mind, it is for you*.

Somebody had said he was unbalanced. He answers by naming what has hold of him.

## What comes next

*Therefore, from now on, **we regard no one according to the flesh**. Even though we have known
Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know Him thus no longer.*

*Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, **he is a new creation**; old things have passed away.* See
the note on 2 Corinthians 5:16–17.

The grip changes how he looks at people. That is the first thing it produces.

## And then

*Now all things are of God, who has reconciled us to Himself through Jesus Christ, and **has
given us the ministry of reconciliation**.* See the note on 2 Corinthians 5:18–19.

*Now then, **we are ambassadors for Christ**, as though God were pleading through us.* See the
note on 2 Corinthians 5:20–21.

The whole passage runs: a love with a grip, a verdict about a death, a changed way of seeing
people, and then a job.

## The other place he names his motive

*I have become all things to all men, that I might by all means save some. Now this I do for the
gospel's sake.*

And: *woe is me if I do not preach the gospel!*

## What John says about the direction

*We love Him because **He first loved us**.* See the note on 1 John 4:19.

> **The verb.** *Sunechei* — presses in from both sides, the word for a siege or a crowd. Paul
> is explaining why he does what he does, and his answer is that the options have narrowed.
