Five years after Solomon dies, everything he made in gold walks out of the country.

## The date

*In **the fifth year** of King Rehoboam.*

Solomon reigned forty years. His son loses the treasury in five.

## What Shishak took

*He **stripped the treasures** from the temple of the LORD and from the royal palace,
taking **everything** — even **all the gold shields** Solomon had made.*

The chapter before records what those shields were: two hundred large shields of
beaten gold, six hundred shekels each, and three hundred smaller ones — hung in the
House of the Forest of Lebanon.

They were ceremonial. Nobody fought with them.

## The replacement

*So King Rehoboam **made bronze shields to replace them**.*

Same number, same shape, same purpose, same place in the procession.

Bronze looks like gold at a distance, especially polished, especially in a corridor.

## The detail that gives it away

*Whenever the king went to the temple of the LORD, **the guards carried the shields,
and afterward they returned them to the guardroom**.*

The gold ones hung in the House of the Forest. Nobody had to lock those away.

The bronze ones go back into a locked room after every use.

You do not put a guard on a thing that is worth what it appears to be worth. The
security arrangements are the confession.

## Why it happened

The chapter says it: *Judah did evil in the sight of the LORD… they also built for
themselves high places, sacred pillars, and wooden images.*

And 2 Chronicles adds the reason Shishak got as far as he did: *they had transgressed
against the LORD* — and, when the king and princes humbled themselves, *they shall be
his servants, that they may distinguish My service from the service of the kingdoms
of the nations.*

## The other shield in Scripture

Saul's, on Gilboa: *the shield of Saul, **no longer anointed with oil**.* See the note
on 2 Samuel 1:17–27.

A shield had to be oiled to keep the leather supple and turn a blade. An unoiled
shield is a shield in name.

## And the one Paul names

*Above all of it, **take up the shield, which is faith**, and with it you will be able
to put out all the burning arrows of the evil one.* See the note on
Ephesians 6:14–17.

Paul's is the full-body **thureos**, the door-shaped shield — and it is the only piece
of the armour that is described as putting fires out.

## What was never replaced

The gold did not come back. Every later reference in Kings and Chronicles to the
temple treasury is to what somebody added, or what somebody else carried off.

> **What Solomon had said would happen.** *If you or your sons at all turn from
> following Me… then I will cut off Israel from the land which I have given them.* God
> said it to him at the dedication, and the reign after his is where the gold starts
> moving.
