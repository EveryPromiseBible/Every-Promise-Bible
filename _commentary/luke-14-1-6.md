He is invited to dinner and the dinner turns out to be a test.

## The setting

*One Sabbath Jesus came to eat at the house of **one of the leading Pharisees**, and **every
eye in the room was on Him, waiting to see what He would do**.*

The Greek is *they were watching Him closely* — **paratereo**, to observe from the side,
to keep under surveillance.

He accepts the invitation knowing what it is. Luke has Him at three Pharisees' tables and
He goes every time.

## Who is there

*Right there in front of Him was a man whose body was **badly swollen with fluid**.*

The Greek is **hudropikos** — dropsical. It is a medical term, and Luke is the only New
Testament writer who uses it.

The man is not said to have asked for anything, and he is not described as a guest. He is
positioned.

## The question

*Jesus turned to the experts in the law and to the Pharisees and asked them plainly, "**Is
it allowed to heal someone on the Sabbath, or not?**"*

He asks first. The trap is sprung before they can spring it.

## The silence

*Not one of them answered.*

The Greek is *they kept silent*.

They could not say no — nothing in the law forbids it. They would not say yes.

## What He does

*So He **took hold of the man, healed him, and sent him on his way**.*

The Greek for *took hold* is **epilabomenos** — he takes the man by the arm.

Three verbs, and the last one removes him from the room. The man does not have to sit
through what follows.

## The argument

*"If your donkey, or your ox, **fell into a well on the Sabbath**, which one of you would
**leave him down there**? **You would have him out that very moment**."*

Their own practice. Rabbinic discussion of exactly this case survives, and the general
ruling permitted rescue.

He argues from what they would do for an animal, on the day in question, without
hesitating.

## The response

*And **they had nothing at all to say to that**.*

Luke says it twice in six verses.

## The other Sabbath healing

The chapter before: a woman bent double for eighteen years, and the synagogue ruler
objecting. Jesus answered with an ox and a donkey there too — *does not each one of you on
the Sabbath loose his ox or donkey from the stall, and lead it away to water?* See the
note on Luke 13:10–17.

Same argument, same animals, and the same result: *all His adversaries were put to
shame.*

## What He says next at the table

He watches the guests choose the best seats and tells them to take the lowest, and then
tells the host to invite *the poor, the maimed, the lame, the blind* — people who cannot
return the invitation.

And then the parable of the great supper, where the invited guests all make excuses and
the servants are sent to the highways and hedges.

## What is not asked of the man

Nothing. No question about his life, no instruction, no condition.

He is taken by the arm, healed, and sent home.

> **The order of the scene.** He asks the question first, gets no answer, heals the man,
> sends him out, and only then makes the argument — to a room that had come to watch.
