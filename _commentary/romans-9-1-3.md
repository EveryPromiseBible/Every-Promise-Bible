---
key: Romans 9:1–3
title: "An ache that never lets up"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
**Paul** opens the hardest three chapters in the letter with an oath and a wish.

## The oath

*"**I am telling you the truth in Christ. I am not lying, and my own conscience, bearing witness with
me in the Holy Spirit, says the same thing.**"*

Three assurances stacked before he has said anything. He knows what is coming will read as cold, and
he goes to unusual lengths to establish that it is not.

## The ache

*"**There is a great sorrow in me and an unceasing pain in my heart.**"*

**Lype megale kai adialeiptos odyne** — great grief and unremitting anguish.

**Adialeiptos** is the word he uses of prayer — *pray without ceasing*. Here it qualifies pain.

## The wish

*"**I could wish that I myself were accursed from Christ for my brethren, my kinsmen according to the
flesh.**"*

**Anathema** — devoted to destruction, cut off.

The man who has just written that nothing can separate us from the love of God in **Christ Jesus**
says he would take the separation himself if it would buy theirs.

*"**Yet now, if You will forgive their sin — but if not, I pray, blot me out of Your book which You
have written.**"*

Moses, after the golden calf. It is the only other place in Scripture anybody offers it, and God
declines both times.

## Why He declines

Because it has already been done by somebody it worked for.

*Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, **having become a curse for us**.*

*For **He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us**, that we might become the righteousness of God
in Him.*

*"**My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?**"*

**Paul** wishes he could be **anathema** for his people. **Galatians** 3 says one Man became **katara**
— a curse — for his, and the wish is refused because the office is filled.

## What follows the wish

Eight privileges: the adoption, the glory, the covenants, the law, the service, the promises, the
fathers, and *from whom, according to the flesh, **Christ came, who is over all, the eternally
blessed God**.*

The list of what Israel has ends with the Person the three chapters are about.

> **The offer.** To be cut off from Christ in their place. Moses made it after the calf and God said
> no; Paul makes it here and gets no answer — because Galatians 3 records somebody for whom the
> exchange was accepted.
