The end of the first half of the upper room discourse, and it closes with Him standing
up.

## What He asks them to feel

*"If you loved Me **you would be glad that I am going to the Father**."*

They are grieving because He is leaving. He tells them the leaving is good news from
His side of it, and asks them to think about where He is going rather than what they
are losing.

## "The Father is greater than I"

Said in the same discourse as *I and My Father are one* and *he who has seen Me has
seen the Father*.

He is speaking as the Son in the middle of the errand — sent, obeying, about to die.
The greater is about position in the sending, not about nature; John has already
settled the nature question in his first sentence.

## Why He is telling them early

*"I have told you now, **before it happens**, so that **when it does happen you will
believe**."*

He does this repeatedly in these chapters. The advance notice is deliberate, and it is
aimed at the moment afterward.

## The ruler of this world

*"The **ruler of this world** is coming. **He has nothing in Me**."*

The Greek is *he has nothing in Me* — no hold, no claim, no matching part.

Everything that happens over the next eighteen hours happens to a man who owes nothing
and can be charged with nothing.

## Why He goes

*"But the world has to know that **I love the Father**, and that **I do exactly as the
Father has commanded Me**."*

He gives His own reason for the cross, and states it as love running upward.

John's Gospel says both directions in the same book: *God so loved the world*, and
this. See the note on John 10:16–18, where He says the Father loves Him because He
lays His life down.

## The last words of the chapter

*"**Get up now; let us go from here**."*

The Greek is *arise, let us go hence.* He stands, and tells them to.

## The oddity

Chapter 15 opens *I am the true vine*, and the discourse runs three more chapters
before the prayer of chapter 17 and the crossing of the Kidron in chapter 18.

He says *let us go* and then keeps talking. The likeliest reading is that they left the
room here and the rest was said walking — through the city, past the temple with its
great golden vine over the doors, down toward the valley.

## What is at the end of the walk

*He went out with His disciples over the Brook Kidron, where there was a garden.*

Passover blood from the temple drained into that brook.

> **The words He uses again.** In Gethsemane, when the torches appear: *rise, let us be
> going. See, My betrayer is at hand.* The same instruction, at the other end of the
> walk, with the arrest party in view.
