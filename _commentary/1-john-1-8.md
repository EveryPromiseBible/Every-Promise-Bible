The claim under attack is not that sins were forgiven. It is that there was never anything there.

## The sentence

*If we say **we have no sin in us**, we are **lying to ourselves**, and **there is no truth in us**.*

The Greek is **hamartian ouk echomen** — we do not have sin.

Singular, with the verb *have*. Not *we have not sinned* — that comes two verses later, in a
different construction.

## The two different claims

Verse 8: *we have no sin* — no sinful principle in us at all.

Verse 10: *we have not sinned* — no acts on the record.

John answers both, and they are not the same denial.

## Who was saying it

The letter is written against teachers who had left the congregation — *they went out from us, but
they were not of us*.

The early Gnostic line was that the spirit is untouched by whatever the body does. On that view, a
man genuinely has no sin, whatever he is up to.

## What John calls it

*We are **lying to ourselves**.*

The Greek is **heautous planōmen** — we are leading ourselves astray.

**Planaō** — to wander, to deceive. The word behind *planet*, the wandering star.

Not primarily a lie told to God. A self-navigation error.

## The second clause

*And **there is no truth in us**.*

The letter's usual formula. Truth in John is something you can be *in*, and something that can be
*in* you.

## What the paragraph has just said

*If we **walk in the light** as He is in the light, we **have fellowship with one another**, and **the
blood of Jesus Christ His Son cleanses us from all sin**.* See the note on 1 John 1:5.

Walking in the light is not the absence of sin — the same sentence has blood cleansing in it.

The light is where the cleansing happens, not where it becomes unnecessary.

## What follows

*If we **own up to our sins**, He keeps His word and He does the just thing.* See the note on
1 John 1:9.

The Greek is **homologōmen** — to say the same thing. To agree with the assessment already made.

## Who the *we* is

The letter's opening addresses people being invited into fellowship: *that you also may have
fellowship with us*.

The proposition being refuted — *we have no sin* — is the entry claim of the teachers John is
writing against.

## What he says to the church

*My little children, I am writing this to you **so that you will not sin**. And **if anyone does sin —
we have someone speaking for us** in front of the Father.* See the note on 1 John 2:1.

And *I am writing to you, little children, **because for the sake of His name your sins are forgiven
you**.* See the note on 1 John 2:12.

To the church, the verb is in the perfect: **apheōntai** — they stand forgiven.

## The other man who said it

*"God, I thank You that I am not like other men."* And the one who *would not so much as raise his
eyes to heaven*. See the note on Luke 18:9–12.

One of them went home justified.

> **The two denials.** *We have no sin* and *we have not sinned* are different claims, two verses
> apart. John refuses both, and the first one is the deeper.
