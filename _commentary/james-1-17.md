James reaches for astronomy to say something about God's character, and the
metaphor is doing precise work.

## The claim about gifts

*Anything good that ever came into your hands, anything that reached you whole
and unspoiled, came down from up there.*

Two words in the Greek — every good *giving* and every perfect *gift*. The act
and the thing given. Both are traced to one source.

The direction is stated: **down from above**, from the Father of lights.

## Why lights

*From the One who made every light burning in the sky.*

The heavenly bodies were the ancient world's picture of reliability — and also
of variation. The sun moves through the year, the moon waxes and wanes,
constellations rise and set. Everything in the sky changes by turning.

James names God as the maker of all of them and then says the thing that
separates Him from what He made.

## The two words

*He never dims, never shifts, never swings around to throw a shadow He was not
throwing before.*

- **no variation** — the astronomical word for change or alteration
- **no shadow cast by turning** — the word is *trope*, the root of *tropic*, used
  of the solstice, the point where the sun turns back

Everything that gives light in the sky produces shadow as it moves. God does not
turn, so there is no side of Him that is dark.

## Why James says it here

Look at the verse before: *do not be deceived, my beloved brothers.* He has just
been saying that God tempts nobody, and that people are drawn away by their own
desire.

This is the correction to a suspicion — that God might be behind the trouble, or
might have a colder side that shows sometimes. James says the light is
unvarying, and everything good that ever reached you came from it.
