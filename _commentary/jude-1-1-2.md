A one-chapter letter, and the last word of its first sentence is the one everything else rests on.

## Who is writing

*"From **Jude**. I belong to **Jesus Christ**, and **James** is my brother."*

**Ioudas Iesou Christou doulos, adelphos de Iakobou.**

**James** is the leader of the Jerusalem church and the writer of the letter that bears his name.
Both men are, on the plain reading of the gospels, **Jesus**' half-brothers — *is this not the
carpenter's son?… and His brothers, James, Joses, Simon and Judas?*

Neither of them ever says so. **Jude** identifies himself by the wrong brother and calls the other
one his master.

*For **even His brothers did not believe in Him**.*

**John** 7. They did not believe during the ministry, and **Paul** records that the risen **Christ**
appeared to **James** personally — after which both brothers are in the upper room at Pentecost.

## The three participles

*"**People God the Father has set apart and made His own** — people who are **kept for Jesus
Christ**, and **called**."*

Called, loved, kept. All three are passives and God is the agent of all three.

## The middle one

**Egapemenois** — having been loved. A perfect participle: loved, and still loved.

## The last one

*"**Kept for Jesus Christ.**"*

**Iesou Christo teteremenois** — having been kept for Jesus Christ.

**Tereo** — to guard, to watch over, to preserve. The perfect tense again: kept, and still kept.

The whole letter is a warning about people who did not last — *wandering stars for whom is reserved
the blackness of darkness forever*, and **Jude** uses the same verb of them, in the same tense, in
the opposite direction.

He opens by telling his readers who is doing the keeping, and closes with it:

*"Now — **there is One with the power to hold you on your feet**, and to stand you up in front of His
own glory with nothing wrong with you and with great joy."* See the note on Jude 1:24–25.

*Who are **kept by the power of God through faith** for salvation ready to be revealed in the last
time.*

## The order

Called, loved, kept — and the letter's readers have not been asked to do anything yet. The
imperatives start in verse 3.

*"**And I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish; neither shall anyone snatch them out
of My hand.**"*

## The greeting

*"**Mercy, peace and love be yours in abundance.**"*

**Eleos hymin kai eirene kai agape plethyntheie** — may they be multiplied.

**Paul** writes *grace and peace*; **Peter** writes *grace and peace be multiplied*; **Jude** writes
mercy, peace and love. It is the only greeting in the New Testament that begins with mercy.

> **The brackets.** *Kept for Jesus Christ* in verse 1, and *able to keep you from falling* in verse
> 24. Between them is the harshest letter in the New Testament about people who did not last — and
> the man writing it is the Lord's brother, who signs himself His slave.
