---
key: Daniel 2:27–28
title: "But there is a God in heaven"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Four professions listed and dismissed in one clause, and then a *but*.

## The four

*"No **wise men**, no **enchanters**, no **magicians**, no **astrologers** can show the king."*

The Aramaic names the whole apparatus of Babylonian divination — **chakkimin**, **ashafin**,
**chartummin**, **gazerin**. The last are the ones who cut the sky into sections to read it.

Daniel does not argue that they are frauds. He says the thing the king wants is outside the range
of the discipline.

## What they had said themselves

*"There is not a man on earth who can tell the king's matter… **and there is no other who can tell
it to the king except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh**."*

The wise men are right, and their sentence is the setup for the whole book. The one thing needed
is a god who lives with flesh.

*And **the Word became flesh and dwelt among us**, and we beheld His glory.*

*"**No one has seen God at any time. The only begotten Son**, who is in the bosom of the Father, He
has declared Him."*

**John**'s verb is **exegesato** — He has explained Him, told Him out. The Babylonian astrologers
name the impossibility; the first chapter of **John** reports it happening.

## The turn

*"**But there is a God in heaven who uncovers mysteries.**"*

The Aramaic is **beram itai elah bishmayya gale razin**.

Three words of denial and then one clause that reopens everything. The construction is the shape
of the gospel's own sentences — *but God, who is rich in mercy*; *but now the righteousness of God
apart from the law is revealed*.

## What Paul does with the same argument

*Where is the wise? Where is the scribe? Where is the disputer of this age? **Has not God made
foolish the wisdom of this world?** For since, in the wisdom of God, the world through wisdom did
not know God, **it pleased God through the foolishness of the message preached to save those who
believe**.*

**1 Corinthians** 1 lists the professions and dismisses them exactly as Daniel does, and then
supplies the same *but*.

*In whom are hidden **all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge**.*

## Who is credited

*"He has made known **to King Nebuchadnezzar** what will happen in the days to come."*

Not to Daniel. The revelation is described as given to the pagan king, and Daniel is the one who
can read it back to him.

*"This mystery was not opened to me **because I have more wisdom than anyone living**."*

He says so explicitly eight verses later, before he interprets a word.

## The days to come

The Aramaic is **be'acharit yomayya** — in the latter days.

*God… **has in these last days spoken to us by His Son**, whom He has appointed heir of all
things.*

> **The wise men's own sentence.** *No one can tell the king except the gods, whose dwelling is not
> with flesh.* They named the exact obstacle, and John 1 reports it removed.
