Paul puts the suffering on a scale, and says the other side is not merely heavier — it is not the
same measure.

## The verdict

*I have weighed it, and I am convinced.*

The Greek is **logizomai** — an accounting word. I reckon, I add it up.

Not a mood. A calculation he says he has done.

## The comparison

*What we suffer in **this present stretch of time** does not even belong on the same scale as **the
glory that is going to be revealed in us**.*

The Greek is **ouk axia pros** — not worthy in comparison with.

## The preposition

*Revealed **in** us.*

The Greek is **eis hēmas** — into us.

Not shown to us as spectators. The glory arrives at our address.

## The next subject

*Creation itself is **craning its neck, watching the road**.*

The Greek is **apokaradokia** — from **kara**, head, and **dokeō**, to watch. Stretching the head
forward.

The noun appears twice in the New Testament, both times in Paul.

## What it is waiting for

*Waiting for **the sons of God to be revealed**.*

The Greek is **tēn apokalupsin tōn huiōn tou Theou**.

The rocks and the weather are described as waiting on a disclosure about people.

## The condition

*For creation was **subjected to futility**.*

The Greek is **mataiotēs** — emptiness, purposelessness. The Septuagint's word for Ecclesiastes'
*vanity of vanities*.

## Whose fault

*Not by its own choice, but **by the will of the One who subjected it**.*

The Greek is **ouch hekousa** — not willingly.

The subjecting is attributed to God, not to Adam and not to the devil.

*Cursed is the ground for your sake.* See the note on Genesis 3:17–19.

## The clause that changes it

*And subjected it **in hope**.*

The Greek is **eph' helpidi**.

The sentence has a comma in it. The futility was imposed with a term attached.

## What is promised

*That creation itself will be **set free from its slavery to decay** and brought into **that freedom,
shining with glory, which belongs to the children of God**.*

The Greek is **douleias tēs phthoras** — slavery of corruption.

**Phthora** — decay, dissolution, the running-down of things.

## The link

Creation's release is tied to the children's. Not parallel — consequent.

## The groaning

*We know that the whole of creation has been **groaning together, laboring together in pain**, right
up to this present moment.*

The Greek is **sustenazei kai sunōdinei** — groans together and travails together.

Both verbs carry **sun-**, together.

## The image

**Ōdinō** — birth pangs.

Not the groan of a dying thing. The noise made at the point where something is about to be born.

## The three groans of the chapter

Creation groans. *We ourselves groan within ourselves.* And *the Spirit makes intercession for us
with groanings which cannot be uttered*. See the note on Romans 8:23–25.

Three, in the same passage, with the Spirit's at the bottom of it.

## Where it goes

*And we know that **all things work together for good** to those who love God.* See the note on
Romans 8:28–30.

And *if God is for us, who can be against us?* See the note on Romans 8:31–34.

## The word for glory

**Doxa** — weight, in the Hebrew behind it. **Kavod**, heaviness.

Paul's scale image is exact: he has put weight on one pan and says the other side does not
register.

## The argument the groaning is inside

*He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all, **how shall He not with Him also
freely give us all things**?*

Fourteen verses later, and it is the chapter's method: the largest thing has already been done, so
the smaller things are not in doubt.

*And we know that **all things work together for good** to those who love God… For whom He foreknew,
He also predestined **to be conformed to the image of His Son**.*

## What creation is waiting for

*The earnest expectation of the creation eagerly waits for **the revealing of the sons of God**.*

**Apokaradokia** — craning the neck. The whole created order is described as waiting for the
disclosure of a group of people, and their disclosure is defined in verse 29 as being made to look
like Him.

*Beloved, now we are children of God; and **it has not yet been revealed what we shall be, but we know
that when He is revealed, we shall be like Him**, for we shall see Him as He is.*

## Who subjected it

*Not willingly, but because of Him who subjected it **in hope**.*

**Eph' helpidi** — the subjection came with hope attached to it, in the same act.

*"Cursed is the ground for your sake."* And a crown of thorns, later, on a head. See the note on
Isaiah 55:12–13.

*"Behold, **I make all things new**."*

**Revelation** 21, and the groaning creation of Romans 8 is the same one **Christ** is said in
**Colossians** 1 to have reconciled: *by Him to reconcile all things to Himself, whether things on
earth or things in heaven, having made peace through the blood of His blood covenant.*

> **The obstetrics.** *Groaning together, labouring together* — the verbs are birth-pang verbs. Paul
> describes the noise as coming from a delivery room, not a deathbed.
