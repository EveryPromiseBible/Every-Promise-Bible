Job's answer to Bildad, and in the middle of it he says something no one else in
the ancient world said.

## The line

*He stretches the northern skies over **empty space** and **hangs the earth on
nothing at all**.*

The Hebrew is *upon nothing* — **beli-mah**, without anything.

Job is probably the oldest book in the Bible. Every culture around it had the
earth resting on something: on a turtle, on elephants, on pillars, on the shoulders
of a giant, on the back of a great fish.

He says it is hanging on nothing, and nobody could confirm it for three and a half
thousand years.

## The circle

*He **drew a circle on the surface of the waters**, a boundary line where light
meets darkness.*

The same Hebrew word Isaiah uses — **chug**, a circle drawn with a fixed radius.

And the second clause is exact. The boundary where light meets darkness on the
surface of a sphere is a circle, and it moves. It is called the terminator, and it
is what you are looking at in every photograph of the earth from space.

## The clouds

*He **wraps up the waters in His thick clouds**, and the clouds do not burst
beneath their weight.*

The Hebrew for *wraps up* is to bind in a bundle.

An average cumulus cloud carries hundreds of tonnes of water and does not fall. Job
noticed that it does not, and said the not-falling was the remarkable part.

## Where he is in the argument

This is a man on an ash heap, covered in boils, whose three friends have been
telling him for twenty chapters that he brought it on himself.

His reply is a description of the universe. Bildad had just said *how then can man
be righteous before God?* — and Job's answer is four verses of cosmology.

## The last line of the chapter

*"And these are but the outer fringes of His ways. How faint the whisper we hear
of Him! Who then can understand the thunder of His power?"*

The Hebrew for *outer fringes* is the ends, the edges. He has just described empty
space, a hanging earth, bundled oceans and a drawn circle, and calls it the hem of
the garment.

> **Who says the same thing later.** *In Him all things hold together* — Colossians
> 1. Job says the earth hangs on nothing; Paul names what nothing is.
