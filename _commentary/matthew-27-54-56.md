The first confession of faith after the death comes from the officer in charge of the
execution.

## Who is speaking

*The **centurion and the soldiers keeping watch with him**.*

The detail assigned to the crucifixion. They had done the nailing, cast lots for the
clothes, and offered Him the sour wine.

Professionals. They had seen men die before.

## What they experienced

*Felt **the earth shake** and **saw everything that happened**.*

Matthew has just recorded it: the darkness from the sixth hour, the cry, the veil torn from
top to bottom, and an earthquake. See the note on Matthew 27:51–53.

## The reaction

*And they were **filled with fear**.*

The Greek is *they feared greatly*.

## The confession

*"**This man really was the Son of God**," they said.*

Mark gives it to the centurion alone. Luke has a different sentence: *certainly this was a
righteous Man.*

The Greek has no article — *a son of God* is a possible rendering, and Roman ears would
have heard it that way.

Matthew records what they said, in their vocabulary.

## The irony Matthew builds

The title has been thrown at Him all day as a taunt.

The passers-by: *if You are the Son of God, come down from the cross.*

The chief priests: *He trusted in God; let Him deliver Him now if He will have Him; for He
said, "I am the Son of God."*

The one man who says it and means it is the officer who killed Him, and he says it because
He did not come down.

## The other centurions

Every centurion in the New Testament comes off well. The one at Capernaum whose faith
Jesus called greater than any in Israel; this one; Cornelius; and Julius, who kept Paul
alive on the ship. See the notes on Matthew 8:10–13 and Acts 10:1–8.

## The women

*A number of women were there as well, **watching from a distance** — women who had
**followed Jesus all the way from Galilee and cared for Him**.*

The Greek for *cared for* is **diakonousai** — served, ministered. The root of *deacon*.

Luke names some of them earlier and says they *provided for Him from their substance*.
They had been funding the ministry.

## Who is named

*Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James and Joses, and the mother of Zebedee's sons.*

The last one had asked, not long before, for her two boys to sit at His right and left in
the kingdom. See the note on Matthew 20:24–28.

She is at the cross, and two men are at His right and left.

## Who is not there

The eleven. Matthew has already recorded it: *then all the disciples forsook Him and
fled.*

John's Gospel puts John himself at the cross with Mary. Matthew's list is women.

## What the women do next

Follow Joseph of Arimathea to the tomb and sit opposite it. Then come back after the
sabbath, first light, and find it open.

They are the last at the cross and the first at the tomb, and Matthew keeps them in every
scene.

> **Who says it first.** The officer commanding the execution squad, after an earthquake,
> about a man he had just watched die — and the reason it lands is that the taunt all day
> had been *come down*.
