The third servant explains himself, and his first sentence is about the master
rather than the money.

## What he says

*"**I knew you to be a hard man**, harvesting where you did not plant and gathering
where you scattered no seed."*

That is his opening line, and it is the whole problem.

The Greek for *hard* is **skleros** — harsh, stiff, rough. It is the root of
*sclerosis*.

## What follows from it

*"**So I was afraid**, and I went and hid your talent in the ground."*

*So.* He states the cause himself, and it is a straight line: this is what I thought
you were like, therefore I was afraid, therefore I buried it.

Nothing about the amount. The man with two talents had a small share too, and
doubled it.

## What he was actually given

Read the first half of the parable. *To one he gave five talents, to another two,
and to another one, **each according to what he was able to handle**.*

A talent was an enormous sum — years of a labourer's wages. He was not slighted; he
was given what he could carry, by a master who had assessed him.

And the master handed over *his property* and left. It is an act of trust from a man
about to be absent for a long time.

## "Here is what belongs to you"

He returns it intact. Nothing lost, nothing stolen, nothing wasted.

By his own accounting he has done nothing wrong. That is what fear produces —
carefulness that looks like integrity.

## The master's answer

*"**So you knew** that I harvest where I have not planted?"*

He does not concede the description. He argues from inside it — *if that is what you
believed, then at the very least the bank.*

The rebuke is that the man did not even act consistently with his own fear.

## What it cost

*"Take the talent away from him."*

The other two are told *well done, good and faithful servant… enter into the joy of
your lord.*

The difference between them was not ability, opportunity or amount. It was what each
believed about the man who gave it.

## The line at the end

*"For everyone who has will be given more… but from the one who has nothing, even
what he has will be taken away."*

The same saying Mark attaches to how you listen. See the note on Mark 4:24–25.

## What Jesus never says

He does not correct the servant's picture of God by softening the master. He lets
the parable stand and puts the burden where the servant put it — on what he thought
he was dealing with.

> **What the other two did first.** *Went **at once** and put them to work.* No
> deliberation is recorded. They knew the man, and knowing him is what made them
> quick.
