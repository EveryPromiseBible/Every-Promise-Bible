The only destructive miracle in the Gospels, performed on a tree.

## When

*Early in the morning, as He was **heading back to the city**.*

Monday of the last week. He had entered Jerusalem on the Sunday, looked round the temple, and gone
back to Bethany for the night.

## The hunger

*He was hungry.*

Matthew states it plainly. A man walking two miles into the city before breakfast.

## The tree

*Seeing a fig tree beside the road, He went over to it and **found nothing on it but leaves**.*

Mark adds the detail: *for it was not the season for figs*.

## What that means

A fig tree in that region puts out small early fruit — **paggim** — before or with the leaves.

Leaves without them advertise a crop that is not there.

The tree is not being punished for having no figs in the wrong season. It is in leaf, which is a
claim.

## The word

*"**May you never bear fruit again**."*

The Greek is **meketi ek sou karpos genetai eis ton aiona** — no longer let fruit come from you
unto the age.

## What happens

*And the tree **withered on the spot**.*

The Greek is **parachrema** — immediately.

Mark, who arranges it over two days, has them notice it the next morning: *dried up from the
roots*.

## What is on either side of it

Matthew puts the temple cleansing between the entry and this. Mark braids them: the tree is cursed,
then the temple is cleared, then the tree is found withered.

Mark's arrangement makes the reading explicit. The tree is the temple.

## What was in the temple

*And Jesus went into the temple of God and drove out all those who bought and sold… "**My house
shall be called a house of prayer, but you have made it a den of thieves**."*

Full of activity, and not producing what it advertised.

## The disciples' question

*"**How did the fig tree wither so quickly?**"*

They ask about the speed, which is the least important thing in the scene.

## The answer

*"If you have **faith and do not waver**, you will not only do what was done to this fig tree, but
you will say to **this mountain**, 'Go, throw yourself into the sea,' and it will happen."*

The Greek is **me diakrithete** — do not be divided, do not hesitate.

**Diakrino** — to separate, and in the middle voice, to be at odds with oneself.

## Which mountain

They are standing on the road between Bethany and Jerusalem, on the Mount of Olives.

*This mountain* is the one under their feet, and the sea is fifteen miles east and four thousand
feet down.

## The promise

*"And **whatever you ask for in prayer, believing, you will receive**."*

Mark's version adds: *and whenever you stand praying, **if you have anything against anyone, forgive
him***.

## What comes next in Matthew

*"**By what authority are You doing these things?**"*

The chief priests and elders, in the temple, the following day.

And then the parable of the two sons, and the vineyard, and *the stone which the builders
rejected*.

## The other fig tree

*"Now learn this parable from the fig tree: **when its branch has already become tender and puts
forth leaves, you know that summer is near**."*

Two chapters later, on the same mountain, the leaves are a sign to read rather than a claim to
check.

> **What the tree was doing.** Leaves before the season advertise fruit. Mark braids the cursing
> around the temple cleansing and leaves the reading to the reader.
