A soldier's psalm, and the request in the middle of it is not for what you would expect.

## What surrounds it

*Though **an army may encamp against me**, my heart shall not fear; though **war may rise
against me**, in this I will be confident.*

That is the verse immediately before.

And the psalm opens: *the LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?*

## The request

*One thing I ask of the LORD, **this is what my whole heart longs for**.*

The Hebrew is *one thing I have desired; that will I seek*.

A man surrounded by an army asks for one thing, and it is not the army's removal.

## The three clauses

*To **live in the house of the LORD all the days of my life**.*

*To **gaze on the beauty of the LORD**.*

*And to **seek Him in His temple**.*

The Hebrew for *gaze* is **chazah** — to behold, to see in vision. The same root as
**chazon**. See the note on Proverbs 29:14–18.

**Beauty** is **noam** — pleasantness, delightfulness. The word is rare and it is the only
time it is used of God in this way.

## What David could not do

Live in the house of the LORD. He was not a priest, and only priests dwelt at the
sanctuary.

He says it anyway, and the psalm does not resolve the impossibility.

## The shelter

*For He will **hide me in His shelter on the day of trouble**.*

The Hebrew is **sukkah** — a booth, a temporary shelter. The kind built for the feast.

*He will **conceal me in His tent** and **set me high upon a rock**.*

Hidden, and then raised. Two opposite movements — out of sight, and up where everything can
see.

## The turn

*Now **my head is lifted above the enemies around me**.*

They are still around him. The psalm does not say they left.

## The response

*And I will **offer in His tent sacrifices with shouts of joy**. **I will sing, I will make
music** to the LORD.*

The Hebrew is *sacrifices of shouting* — **teruah**, the blast, the war-shout.

He offers a war-cry as worship.

## The one thing elsewhere

*"**Only one thing is really needed**. Mary has chosen the good portion."* See the note on
Luke 10:38–42.

And Paul: *one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind and reaching forward to
those which are ahead.*

Three men, three lists of one.

## How the psalm ends

*I would have lost heart, unless I had believed that I would see the goodness of the LORD in
the land of the living.*

*Wait on the LORD; be of good courage, and He shall strengthen your heart. **Wait, I say, on
the LORD**.*

The last word of the psalm is *wait*, said twice.

## What it does not do

Report an outcome. The army is there at the start and nothing in the psalm says it left.

> **What he asks for with an army camped around him.** Not deliverance, in this verse. One
> thing — to live in a house he had no right to live in, and to look at something.
