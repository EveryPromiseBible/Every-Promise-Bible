A man climbs a tree to get a look at Jesus and ends up hosting Him for dinner.

## Who he was

*A **chief tax collector**, and he was **rich**.*

The Greek is **architelones** — a word that appears nowhere else in Greek
literature. Not a tax collector but the head of a district's collectors, taking a cut
of everything they took.

Jericho sat on a major trade route. It was a lucrative posting, and it made him a
collaborator with Rome and a thief in his neighbours' eyes.

## The tree

*He could not get a look at Him over the crowd, **because he was a short man**. So he
**ran on ahead and climbed up into a sycamore tree**.*

A wealthy official in that culture did not run, and did not climb.

The sycamore-fig has a low, broad trunk and wide branches — easy to get into, and
easy to be seen in.

## What Jesus does

*He **looked up** and said, "**Zacchaeus, hurry down. I am staying at your house
today.**"*

He looks up, uses the man's name, and invites Himself.

There is no record of anyone telling Him who it was.

And nothing is asked first. No repentance is required as a condition of the visit,
and no conversation about his profession precedes it.

## The crowd

*Everyone who saw it started grumbling. "**He has gone in to be the guest of a
sinner**."*

Luke uses the word for muttering — the same verb used of Israel in the wilderness.

## What Zacchaeus does

*"Lord, here and now I am **giving half of everything I own to the poor**, and if I
have cheated anyone, **I will pay it back four times over**."*

Nobody asked him for either.

The law required a fifth added to restitution. Fourfold was the penalty for stolen
livestock — the maximum in the law, and the figure David pronounced on the rich man
in Nathan's parable.

He volunteers the harshest rate available, on himself, having already given half
away.

## The order

The dinner invitation comes first. The generosity comes after.

Compare the rich young ruler two chapters earlier, who was told to sell what he had
and *went away sorrowful, for he had great possessions.* Same subject, opposite
sequence, opposite outcome.

## What Jesus says

*"**Today salvation has come to this house** — for this man **too is a son of
Abraham**."*

*Too.* The crowd had written him out of the family. Jesus writes him back in.

## The last line

*"**The Son of Man came to seek and to save what was lost.**"*

Luke puts the mission statement of the Gospel in the mouth of Jesus, at the end of
this story, about a man who climbed a tree.

> **Who else was in Jericho that week.** A blind beggar on the roadside who would not
> stop shouting, and got his sight. Luke puts Bartimaeus in chapter 18 and Zacchaeus
> in chapter 19 — one man nobody would let speak, and one nobody would let near. See
> the note on Mark 10:46–52.
