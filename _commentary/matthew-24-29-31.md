The most public event ever described, and the whole sky is involved.

## The timing

*"**Right after the distress of those days**."*

The Greek is **eutheos meta ten thlipsin ton hemeron ekeinon** — immediately after the tribulation
of those days.

The chapter has just described that distress: *for then there will be great tribulation, such as
has not been since the beginning of the world*.

## The four signs

*"The **sun will be darkened** and the **moon will not give its light**. The **stars will fall from the
sky**, and the **powers of the heavens will be shaken**."*

Every one of them is quoted from the prophets.

Isaiah 13: *the sun will be darkened in its going forth, and the moon will not cause its light to
shine*.

Joel 2: *the sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood*.

Haggai: *once more I shake not only the earth, but also heaven*.

## What the imagery meant

In prophetic language, the collapse of the heavenly bodies signals the fall of powers.

Isaiah uses it of Babylon, Ezekiel of Egypt. Matthew's readers would hear both the cosmic reading
and the political one.

## The sign

*"Then **the sign of the Son of Man will appear in the sky**."*

The Greek is **to semeion tou huiou tou anthropou**.

Unspecified. The text does not say what the sign is, and speculation about it is ancient.

## The reaction

*"And **all the peoples of the earth will mourn**."*

The Greek is **kopsontai** — will beat their breasts, will lament.

Zechariah: *they will look on Me whom they pierced. Yes, they will mourn for Him as one mourns for
his only son*.

## The arrival

*"They will **see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with power and great glory**."*

Daniel 7: *behold, One like the Son of Man, coming with the clouds of heaven!*

The title Jesus uses of Himself more than eighty times comes from that passage, and He quotes it
back to the high priest at His trial.

## What distinguishes this

Visibility. *They will see.*

The Greek is **opsontai** — they shall see.

Nothing here is private or disputable. The whole earth watches.

## The gathering

*"And He will **send out His angels with a loud trumpet call**, and they will **gather His chosen ones
from the four winds**."*

The Greek is **episunaxousin tous eklektous autou** — will gather together His elect.

## The extent

*"From **one end of heaven to the other**."*

Nobody is missed.

## The trumpet

Paul uses the same instrument: *the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised
incorruptible*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:50–52.

And *with the trumpet of God… and the dead in Christ will rise first*. See the note on
1 Thessalonians 4:16–18.

## What follows immediately

*"Now learn this parable from the fig tree: **when its branch has already become tender and puts
forth leaves, you know that summer is near**."*

*"But of that day and hour **no one knows**, not even the angels of heaven, but My Father only."* See
the note on Matthew 24:36–41.

Signs to read, and a date nobody has.

## What the two angels said

*"This same Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, **will so come in like manner as you saw
Him go into heaven**."*

> **The change of register.** Everything before this is described in signs to be interpreted. This
> is described in one verb: *they will see*.
