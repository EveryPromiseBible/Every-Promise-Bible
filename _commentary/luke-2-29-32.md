An old man holds a six-week-old baby and asks to be dismissed.

## Who he is

*A man in Jerusalem whose name was Simeon, and this man was just and devout, **waiting for
the Consolation of Israel**.*

The Greek is **paraklesin** — the comfort, the consoling. Related to **parakletos**, the
Helper.

*And the Holy Spirit was upon him.* See the note on Luke 2:25–28.

## The promise he had

*It had been revealed to him by the Holy Spirit that **he would not see death before he had
seen the Lord's Christ**.*

## What he does

*So he came by the Spirit into the temple… and **took Him up in his arms** and blessed God.*

The song is sung with the child in his hands.

## The opening

*"**Now, Master, You can let Your servant go in peace**."*

The Greek is **despota** — master, owner of a slave. It is where *despot* comes from, and
Luke uses it here and nowhere else of God in his Gospel.

And **apolueis** — release, dismiss, discharge. The word for freeing a slave or standing down
a sentry.

Simeon speaks as a man who has been on duty.

## The warrant

*"**Just as You promised me**."*

The Greek is **kata to rhema sou** — according to Your word.

He is not asking for a favour. He is reporting that a condition has been met.

## The reason

*"For **my own eyes have seen Your salvation**."*

The Greek is **to soterion sou** — Your salvation, neuter. Not a doctrine, and in his arms.

He is holding a baby and calls it salvation.

## The audience

*"The salvation You have made ready **in full view of every people**."*

The Greek is **kata prosopon panton ton laon** — before the face of all peoples.

Prepared publicly, in the plural.

## The two clauses

*"A **light to open the eyes of the nations**, and the **glory of Your people Israel**."*

The Greek is **eis apokalupsin ethnon** — for revelation to the Gentiles.

Isaiah, twice: *I will also give You as a light to the Gentiles, that You should be My
salvation to the ends of the earth*.

Gentiles first in the sentence, in the temple, at the presentation of a Jewish firstborn.

## What the family was doing there

The purification, and the redemption of the firstborn. Luke notes the offering they brought:
*a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons*.

Leviticus allows the birds only *if she cannot afford a lamb*.

## What he says to Mary

*"Behold, this Child is destined for the **fall and rising of many in Israel**, and for a
**sign which will be spoken against** — yes, **a sword will pierce through your own soul
also**."*

The first prophecy about Jesus in Luke after the birth includes a sword through His mother.

## Where the song went

It is the *Nunc Dimittis*, from the first words of the Latin. Sung at Compline, the last
office of the day, and at evensong — a night prayer for centuries, because it is about a man
being allowed to sleep.

## Who arrives next

*And coming in at that instant she gave thanks to the Lord.* See the note on Luke 2:36–38.

Two old people in one paragraph, and both had been waiting.

> **The word he uses of himself.** *Dismiss Your servant* — the term for standing down a
> sentry. He had been given a watch to keep, and he is reporting that it is over.
