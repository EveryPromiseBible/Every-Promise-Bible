Two verses closing a chapter that has had a killing, an arrest and a death in it.

## What the chapter contained

*Now about that time Herod the king stretched out his hand to harass some from the church. Then
**he killed James the brother of John with the sword**.*

One of the three who had been on the mountain. The first apostle to die, and Luke gives him
half a verse.

Then Peter arrested, four squads of soldiers, chains, and an angel — *and his chains fell off
his hands*.

Then Rhoda, who left him standing at the door because she was too pleased to open it.

Then the guards executed, and Herod on his throne in Caesarea being hailed as a god and struck
down.

## The sentence

*But **the word of God grew and spread**.*

The Greek is **euxanen kai eplethuneto** — grew and was multiplied.

Both are agricultural. The verbs for a crop.

## Where the *but* is pointing

At everything in the chapter.

An apostle killed. The leading apostle in prison. A king persecuting on purpose.

And Luke sets one word against all of it.

## The pattern

He does this repeatedly. The phrase is a structural marker in Acts.

*And the word of God spread, and the number of the disciples multiplied greatly in Jerusalem* —
after the complaint about the widows.

*So the word of the Lord grew mightily and prevailed* — after the riot in Ephesus.

Every time, it follows trouble.

## What Herod's end was

*And immediately an angel of the Lord struck him, because he did not give glory to God. And he
was eaten by worms and died.*

Josephus records the same death independently, with the same setting — Caesarea, the games, the
silver garment catching the sun, the crowd shouting that his was the voice of a god — and five
days of abdominal agony.

Luke's account and the historian's agree.

## The contrast the two verses make

Herod is dead in verse 23. The word is growing in verse 24.

Luke puts them next to each other without comment.

## The return

*And **Barnabas and Saul returned from Jerusalem** when they had completed their service.*

They had come up with famine relief from Antioch: *then the disciples, each according to his
ability, determined to send relief to the brethren dwelling in Judea*.

The chapter opens with a king killing believers and closes with two men delivering money to
them.

## The third name

*Taking with them **John, also called Mark**.*

His mother's house is where the prayer meeting was, earlier in the chapter — the one Rhoda
answered the door of.

He goes with them to Cyprus, leaves them at Perga, and becomes the reason Paul and Barnabas
split up. See the note on Acts 13:4–8.

And ends up with Paul at the very end: *get Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful to me
for ministry*.

And the Gospel that bears his name.

## What the chapter says about prayer

*Peter was therefore kept in prison, but **constant prayer was offered to God for him by the
church**.*

They prayed all night, and when the answer knocked on the door they told the girl she was out
of her mind.

## What the two verses do

Change the subject. From chapter 13 the book leaves Jerusalem and follows Paul.

The last thing Luke says about the old centre is that the word grew.

## What the word is

*"**But the word of God grew and multiplied.**"*

**Luke** uses the sentence three times as a hinge, and the content of *the word* is never in doubt in
this book.

*Therefore those who were scattered went everywhere **preaching the word**… Then Philip went down to
the city of Samaria and **preached Christ to them**.*

Two verses apart, in chapter 8. **Luke** treats *the word* and *Christ* as the same object.

*And with great power the apostles gave witness to **the resurrection of the Lord Jesus**.*

## What the two verbs are

**Euxanen kai eplethyneto** — grew and was multiplied. They are the words used of a living thing, and
of Israel in Egypt: *the children of Israel were fruitful and increased abundantly, multiplied and
grew exceedingly mighty.*

*"So is the kingdom of God, as if a man should scatter seed on the ground… **the earth yields crops
by itself**."*

## What the *but* is set against

The chapter has James executed, **Peter** arrested, and Herod acclaimed as a god and struck down. And
then one sentence about the word growing.

*"**So shall My word be that goes forth from My mouth; it shall not return to Me void.**"*

*Remember Jesus Christ, of the seed of David, was raised from the dead according to my gospel, for
which I suffer trouble as an evildoer, even to the point of chains; **but the word of God is not
chained**.*

**Paul** writes it from a Roman prison, and it is Acts 12:24 in one clause.

> **What the sentence is set against.** An apostle executed, the chief apostle in chains, and a
> king killing on purpose. Luke's verdict on the chapter is five words: *but the word grew*.
