They swore an oath by walking between the halves of a calf, and the sentence is the oath taken
literally.

## What they had done

Under siege, Zedekiah made a covenant to release every Hebrew slave, as the law of Moses required
every seventh year.

They did it. And then the Babylonians withdrew temporarily, and they took the people back.

## The charge

*You have not obeyed Me. **You did not proclaim real freedom**, each man to his brother and his
neighbor.*

The Hebrew is **deror** — release, liberty. The word used of the Jubilee.

## The sentence

*So now **I proclaim freedom for you**, says the LORD — **freedom to fall by the sword, by plague, and
by famine**.*

The Hebrew is the same word, **deror**, used against them.

They withheld release; release is granted — to three things they would rather not be released to.

## The covenant they broke

*As for the men who broke My covenant and did not keep the terms of the covenant they made before
Me.*

The ceremony is then described.

## The ceremony

*I will treat them like **the calf they cut in two to walk between its pieces**.*

The Hebrew is **ha'egel asher karetu lishnayim vaya'avru bein betarav**.

## What that tells us

This is the clearest description in Scripture of how a covenant was cut.

An animal halved. The halves laid apart. The parties walking down the corridor between them.

## What the walking says

The oath is self-maledictory. *May I be made like this animal if I break my word.*

The text does not explain the symbolism. It simply applies the penalty and expects the reader to
follow.

## Who walked

*The **officials of Judah and Jerusalem**, the **court officials**, the **priests**, and **all the people
of the land**.*

Four groups. The whole establishment, from the palace to the street.

## The verdict

*I will **hand them over to their enemies who want them dead**. Their bodies will become **food for the
birds of the sky and the beasts of the earth**.*

The halves of the calf, left in the field, would have gone the same way.

The sentence is the ceremony completed.

## What this illuminates

*A smoking firepot and a blazing torch appeared and **passed between the pieces**.* See the note on
Genesis 15:17–21.

Same ritual, six centuries earlier, and Abram is asleep on the ground.

## The difference

In Jeremiah, the men who walked bear the penalty.

In Genesis, only one party walked — and it was not the man.

## What that costs

If the Abrahamic covenant fails, the penalty falls where the oath was sworn.

## What was at stake in the law they broke

*And if your brother… is sold to you and serves you six years, then **in the seventh year you shall
let him go free from you**.* See the note on Deuteronomy 15:12–18.

*You shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt.*

The reason given for the release law is that they had been released.

## What Jeremiah says elsewhere

*"Behold, the days are coming… when **I will make a new covenant** with the house of Israel — **not
according to the covenant that I made with their fathers**… **My covenant which they broke**."* See the
note on Jeremiah 31:31–34.

The chapter here is a case study of that broken covenant, and the terms of the new one are that God
does the keeping.

## What the one-party walk cost

In Genesis only God passed between the pieces, and Abram was asleep. If the covenant failed, the
penalty fell where the oath was sworn.

*For **where there is a testament, there must also of necessity be the death of the testator**.
For a testament is in force after men are dead.*

**Hebrews** 9 says the arrangement plainly. A covenant of that shape is not completed until
somebody dies, and only one party had undertaken to.

*Likewise He also took the cup after supper, saying, "**This cup is the new covenant in My
blood**, which is shed for you."*

**Luke** 22. The self-maledictory oath sworn at Genesis 15 is paid, and the one paying it is the
one who walked.

## The curse the calf stood for

*May I be made like this animal if I break my word.*

*Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, **having become a curse for us**.*

*And He, bearing His cross, went out to a place called the Place of a Skull… **outside the
gate**.*

The halves of the calf were left in the field. **Hebrews** 13 notes where the bodies of the sin
offerings were burned and where **Jesus** was taken — *therefore Jesus also, that He might
sanctify the people with His own blood, **suffered outside the gate**.*

## The freedom they would not proclaim

**Deror** — release, the Jubilee word.

*"The Spirit of the LORD is upon Me… He has sent Me to heal the brokenhearted, **to proclaim
liberty to the captives**… to proclaim **the acceptable year of the LORD**."*

**Luke** 4, and the Greek Old Testament's word in the Isaiah passage **Jesus** reads is
**aphesin** — the same noun the New Testament uses for the forgiveness of sins.

Zedekiah's officials proclaimed **deror** and took it back. The One who read the Jubilee out loud
in Nazareth said *today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing*, and did not.

## And the new covenant's terms

*"Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers… **My covenant which they broke**…
**I will put My law in their minds, and write it on their hearts**."*

The difference is who does the keeping. This chapter is the case study of the covenant they broke;
chapter 31 is the one where God stops asking them to keep it.

> **The literal sentence.** They walked between the halves of a calf and swore. The punishment named
> is that they be treated like the calf.
