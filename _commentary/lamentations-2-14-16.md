The poet names why the disaster was not averted, and it is not the army.

## The charge

*Your prophets saw for you visions that were **false and empty**. **They never exposed your guilt
to turn your captivity around**.*

The Hebrew is **velo gillu al-avonekh** — they did not uncover your iniquity.

**Galah** — to uncover, to strip, to reveal. The verb for taking the lid off a thing.

## What that would have done

*To **turn your captivity around**.*

The Hebrew is **lehashiv shevutekh** — to bring back your captivity, to reverse the turning away.

The poet's claim is precise and it is medical: a wound that is not uncovered is not treated. The
prophets who would not name the sickness cost the city the cure.

*They have also **healed the hurt of My people slightly, saying, "Peace, peace!" when there is no
peace**.*

Jeremiah says it twice, of the same men.

## Who did uncover it

*"Go, call your husband, and come here." The woman answered and said, "I have no husband." Jesus
said to her, "**You have well said, 'I have no husband,' for you have had five husbands, and the
one whom you now have is not your husband**."*

*"Come, see a Man who told me all things that I ever did. **Could this be the Christ?**"*

**John** 4. She runs into the town advertising the exposure as the reason to come and look.

*But **if we walk in the light** as He is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and
the blood of Jesus Christ His Son cleanses us from all sin.*

**John**'s letter puts the uncovering and the cleansing in one sentence, in that order, and does
not treat the first as a threat.

## The passers-by again

*All who pass along the road clap their hands at you. They **hiss and shake their heads** at
Daughter Jerusalem.*

*And those who passed by blasphemed Him, **wagging their heads**.*

The second time in two poems that Lamentations' onlookers turn up at Golgotha doing the same
thing. See the note on Lamentations 1:12.

## What they said

*"**Is this the city they called the perfect beauty, the joy of all the earth?**"*

*"**He saved others; Himself He cannot save.** If He is the King of Israel, let Him now come down
from the cross, and we will believe Him."*

Both are taunts built on a title the speaker does not believe any more, and both are accurate
about the appearance and wrong about the case.

> **The accusation.** Not that the false prophets lied about the future — that they would not
> name the wound. The poet says the uncovering was the thing that could have turned it, and it is
> a woman at a well who works out that being told everything she ever did is good news.
