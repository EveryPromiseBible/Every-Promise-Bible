The exile is explained by an accounting nobody had been keeping.

## What has happened

Jerusalem is taken. The temple burned, the walls broken down, the vessels carried off.

*And they burned the house of God, broke down the wall of Jerusalem, burned all its palaces with
fire, and destroyed all its precious possessions.*

## The survivors

*Those who **escaped the sword** he carried away to Babylon, where they became **servants to him and
his sons** until the kingdom of Persia came to power.*

The Hebrew is **avadim** — slaves, servants.

## The explanation

*So **the word of the LORD spoken through Jeremiah was fulfilled**.*

Jeremiah 25: *this whole land shall be a desolation and an astonishment, and these nations shall
serve the king of Babylon **seventy years***.

## What was fulfilled

*The **land enjoyed its sabbath rests**.*

The Hebrew is **ratzetah ha'aretz et-shabbetoteha** — the land paid off, or made up, its
sabbaths.

**Ratzah** — to be pleased with, or to satisfy a debt.

## The law behind it

*Six years you shall sow your field… but **in the seventh year there shall be a sabbath of solemn
rest for the land**, a sabbath to the LORD. You shall neither sow your field nor prune your
vineyard.* See the note on Leviticus 25:1–7.

Every seventh year the land lay fallow.

## The threat attached

*Then the land shall enjoy its sabbaths as long as it lies desolate and you are in your enemies'
land… **as long as it lies desolate it shall rest for the time it did not rest on your sabbaths
when you dwelt in it**.*

Leviticus 26 states the consequence in advance, using the same verb.

## The arithmetic

Seventy sabbath years, one every seven, is four hundred and ninety years.

Roughly the span from the settlement in the land to the exile.

The Chronicler is saying the account had been running the whole time and nobody had noticed.

## The clause

*All the days it lay desolate **it kept sabbath**.*

The Hebrew is **kol-yemei hoshammah shavatah**.

The land is the subject. It rests, whether or not anyone gives it permission.

## What is being said

The rest was going to happen.

It could be taken in seven-year instalments, with the people on the land, or all at once with
them gone. The Chronicler says which way it went.

## Who the tally belonged to

Nobody in Israel was counting. The debt accrued silently for centuries.

## What Daniel does with it

*In the first year of his reign I, Daniel, understood by the books the number of the years
specified by the word of the LORD through Jeremiah the prophet, that **He would accomplish seventy
years in the desolations of Jerusalem**.*

*Then I set my face toward the Lord God to make request by prayer.* See the note on
Daniel 9:1–2.

He read the number, worked out where he was in it, and prayed.

## How the book ends

*Now in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia… the LORD stirred up the spirit of Cyrus, so that he
made a proclamation: "**All the kingdoms of the earth the LORD God of heaven has given me. And He
has commanded me to build Him a house at Jerusalem**."*

*"Who is among you of all His people? **May the LORD his God be with him, and let him go up**!"*

The last verse of Chronicles, and the Hebrew Bible ends on **veya'al** — let him go up.

See the note on Isaiah 45:1–4.

> **The accounting.** Seventy sabbath years, unkept over four hundred and ninety, taken in one
> instalment. The land was going to rest either way.
