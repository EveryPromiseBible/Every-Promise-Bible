The prologue breaks off from eternity to introduce a man with a name.

## The interruption

*There came **a man, sent from God, whose name was John**.*

Five verses about the Word who was in the beginning, and then a person with a name and a
job.

The Greek shifts too: the Word **was** — imperfect, continuous — and John **came into
being**.

## His job

*He came **as a witness, to testify about the Light**, so that through him **everyone
might come to believe**.*

The Greek is **martyria** — testimony, evidence. It is a legal word, and John's Gospel
runs on it: the Baptist testifies, the works testify, the Father testifies, the Scriptures
testify.

## What he was not

*He was **not the Light himself**; he came **to point people to it**.*

John the Evangelist says it twice about John the Baptist — here and again a few verses
on.

The Baptist says it himself: *"I am not the Christ"*, and *"He must increase, but I must
decrease."*

## The Light

*The true Light, **the Light that gives light to every person**, was coming into the
world.*

**True** is **alethinos** — real, genuine, as against a copy.

## The three rejections

The prologue states it three times, each closer in:

- *He was **in the world**, and the world came into being through Him, and **the world
  never recognized Him**.*
- *He came **to what belonged to Him**.*
- *And **His own people did not take Him in**.*

The Greek moves from *ta idia* (his own things, neuter) to *hoi idioi* (his own people).

He made it, He owned it, and it did not let Him in the door.

## The exception

*But **to everyone who did receive Him**, to all who **put their trust in His name**.*

The Greek for *receive* is **elabon** — took, accepted.

## What was given

*He gave them **the right to become the children of God**.*

The Greek is **exousia** — authority, the right to act. Not merely permission and not
merely power.

**To become** — the Greek is a verb of coming into being, the same one used of John
appearing in verse 6.

## The three negatives

*Born **not of bloodline**, not out of **any human wanting**, not out of **a husband's
decision** — but **born of God**.*

Three ways a person normally comes into a family, and all three are excluded:

- **Bloodline** — the Greek is plural, *bloods*: descent, ancestry, the thing the crowd
  in chapter 8 insists on when they say *we are Abraham's descendants*.
- **Human wanting** — the will of the flesh.
- **A husband's decision** — the will of a man.

Nothing on the list is sinful. They are the ordinary means, and John rules all three out
before naming the actual one.

## Where the prologue goes next

*And **the Word became flesh and dwelt among us**, and we beheld His glory.*

The Greek for *dwelt* is **eskenosen** — pitched His tent, tabernacled.

> **What is given and what it is given for.** Not a status conferred, but the right to
> become — and the three usual routes into a family are named and excluded first.
