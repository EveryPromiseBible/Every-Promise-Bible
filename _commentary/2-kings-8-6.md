She asked for a house and a field, and the ruling covers seven years she was not there.

## The hearing

*The king asked the woman about it, and **she told him**.*

The Hebrew is **vattesapper-lo** — and she recounted to him.

The same verb used two verses earlier when the king asked Gehazi to *recount to me all the great
things Elisha has done*. See the note on 2 Kings 8:4–5.

Gehazi was telling the story. Now the woman tells her own.

## The officer

*Then he **assigned an official to her case**.*

The Hebrew is **saris** — a court official, or eunuch.

Not a promise. An administrator, appointed to see it done.

## The ruling

*"**Give back everything that belonged to her**."*

The Hebrew is **hashev et-kol-asher-lah** — restore all that is hers.

The verb **shuv** — to return, to restore.

## The addition

*"Including **all the produce of her land from the day she left the country until now**."*

The Hebrew is **ve'et kol-tevu'ot hassadeh** — and all the increase of the field.

## What that means

Seven harvests, off land she did not sow, in a country under famine.

Whoever occupied it kept it working. The yield is awarded to her.

## What she asked for

*Her house and her land.*

The produce is not in her petition. The king adds it.

## The famine

The seven years were a famine — *the LORD has decreed a famine in the land that will last seven
years*. See the note on 2 Kings 8:1.

Which raises the question of how much produce there was, and the ruling covers whatever there
was.

## The pattern in the law

*He shall make restitution for it in full, **and shall add one-fifth more to it**.* See the note on
Leviticus 6:1–7.

The guilt offering required the original plus twenty per cent. Restoration in the law is
never merely the amount taken.

## The other restitution

*If a man steals an ox or a sheep, and slaughters it or sells it, he shall restore **five oxen for
an ox and four sheep for a sheep**.*

## Where she started

A woman who had said *I dwell among my own people* when offered a word to the king on her behalf.

She wanted nothing from the palace, and left the country on a prophet's instruction, and came
back to nothing.

## What she has at the end

Her house, her field, seven years of yield, and a court officer assigned to enforce it.

## What was said over her at the beginning

*"About this time next year you shall embrace a son."*

*And she said, "No, my lord. Man of God, **do not lie to your maidservant**!"*

The whole story runs from a promise she did not want to believe.

## What she said with the boy dead upstairs

*"**It is well**."*

Twice — to her husband, and to Gehazi on the road — while riding to Carmel with the body on the
prophet's bed. See the note on 2 Kings 4:18–25.

## What she asked for and what the ruling gave

She petitioned for the house and the land. The king restored both, *and all the proceeds of
the field from the day that she left the land until now*.

Seven years of harvests off a field she had not worked, paid to a woman who had been in
Philistine country.

Jesus: *"**give, and it will be given to you: good measure, pressed down, shaken together, and
running over**, will be put into your bosom."*

## Where it began

Building a small room on a roof — a bed, a table, a chair and a lampstand — for a man who
walked past her house, and refusing to let him speak for her to the king. See the note on
2 Kings 4:8–10.

She would not be spoken for then. Years later the king rules in her favour on the strength of
a story she was not even in the room to tell.

*"**And your Father who sees in secret will Himself reward you openly.**"*

## The word she used that day

*It is well.* See the note on 2 Kings 4:18–25.

## What the pattern is called

Restitution with increase. It is the law's own shape — *he shall restore what he took… and
shall add one-fifth to it* — and here a king applies it to a famine nobody caused.

*And God is able to make **all grace abound toward you**, that you, always having all
sufficiency in all things, may have an abundance for every good work.*

> **What the king adds.** She petitioned for the house and the field. The ruling covers seven
> harvests off land she was not on.
