The end of the darkest chapter in Isaiah, and it closes with a coronation.

## Where this sits

Chapter 24 is the opening of what is usually called Isaiah's apocalypse — chapters 24 to
27, the whole earth rather than one nation.

It has already said *the earth is violently broken, the earth is split open, the earth is
shaken exceedingly*, and *the earth shall reel to and fro like a drunkard*.

## The two ranks

*The LORD will bring judgment on **the powers in the heights above** and **the kings of the
earth below**.*

The Hebrew is *the host of exalted ones on high, and the kings of the earth on the earth*.

Two tiers, dealt with in the same sentence. Paul's list is the same shape: *principalities,
powers, rulers of the darkness of this age, spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly
places*. See the note on Ephesians 6:10–13.

## The holding

*They will be **gathered together like prisoners in a pit**. They will be **shut up in the
prison**.*

The Hebrew for *pit* is **bor** — a cistern. Joseph was put in one, and so was Jeremiah.

## The delay

*And **after many days** they will be **called to account**.*

The Hebrew is *after many days they shall be visited*.

Confinement first, sentencing later. Revelation has the same two-stage arrangement — bound
for a thousand years, and then the judgment.

Peter writes it too: *God did not spare the angels who sinned, but cast them down… and
delivered them into chains of darkness, to be **reserved for judgment***.

## The shaming

*Then **the moon will be ashamed** and **the sun will be put to shame**.*

The Hebrew is *the moon shall be confounded and the sun ashamed* — **chapherah** and
**boshah**.

The two greater lights of Genesis 1, embarrassed. Not extinguished, and not eclipsed.

Something brighter walks in and they have nothing to contribute.

## The reason

*For the **LORD of hosts will reign on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem**, and **before His
elders, in glory**.*

The Hebrew for *before His elders* is *before His ancients* — and the last phrase, **kavod**,
glory.

Revelation's throne room has twenty-four elders around it, and a city with *no need of the
sun or of the moon to shine in it, for the glory of God illuminated it*.

## What follows immediately

Chapter 25, verse 1: *O LORD, You are my God. I will exalt You, I will praise Your name, for
You have done wonderful things.*

And then the feast on the same mountain, with the veil swallowed and death swallowed with
it. See the note on Isaiah 25:6–9.

The chapter break is a printer's decision. The judgment scene and the banquet scene are
one continuous passage, and the address is the same: Mount Zion.

## The two mountains

*You have not come to the mountain that may be touched and that burned with fire… but you
have come to **Mount Zion**.* See the note on Hebrews 12:18–24.

Isaiah's judgment sits on the mountain Hebrews names as the one believers have arrived at.

> **What happens to the lights.** Ashamed — the word is about embarrassment, not
> destruction. Genesis appointed them to rule the day and the night, and they are still
> shining when something makes them redundant.
