Stephen is on trial, and this is the part of his defence where the pattern becomes the argument.

## The setting

Accused before the Sanhedrin of speaking against the temple and the law.

He answers with a history of Israel, running from Abraham forward, and the council listens for
fifty verses.

## The phrase

*"**This Moses, the one they refused**."*

The Greek is **touton ton Mousen hon ernesanto** — this Moses, whom they denied.

**Arneomai** — to deny, to disown. The verb used of Peter in the courtyard.

## What they said

*"**Who made you a ruler and a judge?**"*

Two Hebrews fighting, and Moses intervened, and one of them said it. He fled to Midian the next
day and stayed forty years.

## The reversal

*"**This is the very one God sent as ruler and deliverer**."*

The Greek is **archonta kai lutroten** — ruler and redeemer.

The exact title they threw at him as a sneer is the one God gave him.

## The pattern Stephen is building

Joseph: *the patriarchs, becoming envious, sold Joseph into Egypt. But God was with him.*

Moses: refused, and sent back as ruler.

The rejected deliverer is the sent one. Twice, before Stephen gets anywhere near his conclusion.

## The forty years

*"He brought them out, doing wonders and signs **in the land of Egypt, and at the Red Sea, and in the
wilderness for forty years**."*

## The prophet

*"This is that Moses who said, '**The Lord your God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from among
your brothers. Listen to Him.**'"*

Deuteronomy 18, quoted with its last two words: **autou akousesthe** — listen to Him.

## The living words

*"The one who received **living words to hand on to us**."*

The Greek is **logia zonta** — living oracles.

Stephen is on trial for speaking against the law, and he calls it living.

## The turn

*"**Our fathers were unwilling to obey him. They pushed him away, and in their hearts they turned
back toward Egypt**."*

The Greek is **aposanto** — thrust away.

**Estraphesan en tais kardiais auton eis Aigupton** — they turned in their hearts to Egypt.

Physically out, and inwardly heading back.

## The calf

*"They said to Aaron, '**Make us gods to go ahead of us**, because as for **this Moses**… we have no
idea what has become of him.'"*

Quoted from Exodus 32. See the note on Exodus 32:1–4.

**This Moses** — the same dismissive phrase Stephen has just used ironically at the start of the
paragraph.

## The celebration

*"In those days they made a calf, and they brought a sacrifice to the idol and **celebrated the work
of their own hands**."*

The Greek is **euphrainonto en tois ergois ton cheiron auton** — they rejoiced in the works of
their hands.

## The consequence

*"So **God turned away and gave them over** to the worship of the host of heaven."*

The Greek is **paredoken** — handed them over.

The same verb Paul uses three times in Romans 1: *therefore God also gave them up*.

The judgment is being let have what they chose.

## The quotation

Amos 5, from the Septuagint: *"House of Israel, **was it to Me that you brought slain animals and
sacrifices for forty years in the wilderness?**"*

*"And I will carry you away beyond Babylon."*

Amos said *beyond Damascus*. Stephen extends it to the exile that actually came.

## Where the speech ends

*"**You stiff-necked and uncircumcised in heart and ears!** You always resist the Holy Spirit; as your
fathers did, so do you."*

*"Which of the prophets did your fathers not persecute?"* See the note on
Acts 7:51–53.

The pattern he has been laying out for fifty verses is turned on the room.

## What happens

They stone him, and *the witnesses laid down their clothes at the feet of a young man named Saul*.

## The pattern Stephen is building toward

Moses rejected — *who made you a ruler and a judge over us?* — and then sent back by God as exactly
that.

*"**This Moses whom they rejected**… God sent to be both a ruler and a deliverer."*

Joseph sold by his brothers and made their saviour. Moses refused and sent back as ruler. Stephen
runs the same shape twice before he applies it.

*"**Which of the prophets did your fathers not persecute? And they killed those who foretold the
coming of the Just One, of whom you now have become the betrayers and murderers.**"*

That is the application, seventeen verses later, and it is the last thing he says before they pick
up stones.

*"**The stone which the builders rejected has become the chief cornerstone.**"*

## The prophet like Moses

*"This is that Moses who said to the children of Israel, '**The LORD your God will raise up for you a
Prophet like me from your brethren. Him you shall hear.**'"*

Stephen quotes it in the middle of the argument. **Peter** had already used the same verse in chapter
3 and named who it meant.

## What he sees at the end

*"**Look! I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God!**"*

The only place in the New Testament outside the gospels where anybody says *Son of Man* — and the
only place He is described standing rather than seated.

*Then he knelt down and cried out with a loud voice, "**Lord, do not charge them with this sin.**"*

He is looking at Saul of Tarsus while he says it, and it is the sentence **Jesus** prayed from the
cross. Stephen's whole speech is that Israel rejects its deliverers and God sends them back — and
the last thing he does is ask that the rejection not be counted.

> **The construction.** *This Moses, the one they refused — this is the very one God sent.* Stephen
> states it of Joseph, then of Moses, and lets the council reach the third case themselves.
