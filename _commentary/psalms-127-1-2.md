A short psalm attributed to Solomon — the man who built the actual house and
watched over the actual city — and it begins by saying so.

## The two examples

*Unless the LORD builds the house, its builders labour in vain. Unless the LORD
watches the city, the watchman keeps awake for nothing.*

Building and guarding: making something, and keeping it. Between them they cover
most of what anyone spends a life doing.

The psalm does not say the builders are idle or the watchmen asleep. It says
their work is *in vain* without something else — the labour is real and the
result is not in their hands.

## "The bread of anxious toil"

*It is no use rising early, sitting up late, eating the bread of anxious toil.*

The Hebrew is literally the bread of *pains* or *sorrows* — the same root used of
the toil laid on the ground in Genesis 3. It is a picture of work eaten as
misery: early starts, late nights, and food taken standing up.

## The line at the end

*For to His beloved He gives sleep.*

The Hebrew here is compressed and can be read two ways: *He gives sleep to His
beloved*, or *He gives to His beloved while they sleep*. Translators have split
on it for centuries.

Either way, sleep is the image, and it is offered as the alternative to the
anxious bread. The psalm's argument is not that work is pointless — it is that
the outcome does not depend on staying awake.

> **What comes next.** The rest of the psalm is about children being a heritage
> from the LORD. The two halves belong together: a house is built and a city is
> kept, and what actually endures out of a life is described as something
> received rather than achieved.
