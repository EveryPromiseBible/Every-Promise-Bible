The first sign in John's Gospel happens at a party, and it starts with an
embarrassment.

## What running out meant

A Jewish wedding feast could last a week, and providing for the guests was the
family's responsibility. Running out of wine was not a catering inconvenience —
it was a public humiliation the village would remember for years.

Nobody was in danger. Nobody was ill. The need was social, and Mary brings it to
Him anyway.

## What she says

*They have no more wine.*

That is the entire request. No instruction, no suggestion, no argument about why
He should act. She states the situation and stops.

## His answer

*Dear woman, what does this have to do with you and Me? My hour has not come
yet.*

It reads sharply in English. The address is not harsh in Greek — it is the same
word He uses to her from the cross. The idiom behind *what does this have to do
with you and Me* is a way of saying the matter is not ours to be involved in.

And *My hour*, in John, always means the cross. He is saying the time for public
disclosure has not arrived.

## What she does with it

*His mother turned to the servants and said, "Whatever He tells you, do it."*

She does not argue with Him and she does not withdraw the request. She hands the
matter over and instructs the staff to obey whatever comes.

They are the last recorded words of Mary in Scripture, and they are about doing
what He says.

> **What He then makes.** Water in six stone jars kept for ceremonial washing —
> the vessels of purification rites — turned into the best wine of the feast. The
> master of the banquet says the good wine has been saved till last, and has no
> idea where it came from. Only the servants who filled the jars knew.
