Two verses, and a private insult becomes a policy of extermination.

## "Too small a thing"

*It seemed **too small a thing** to lay hands on Mordecai alone.*

Killing the man who offended him is beneath the scale of Haman's anger. He wants
proportion, and the proportion he settles on is a nation.

The Hebrew is contemptuous — it despised in his eyes. Murder was not enough.

## What made it possible

*They had told him **who Mordecai's people were**.*

The servants' report in the verses before did this. Mordecai's answer had been *I
am a Jew*, and that single fact converts one man's stubbornness into a target the
size of an empire.

## Amalek doing what Amalek does

Haman is *the Agagite*, and this is Amalek's method exactly as Deuteronomy
describes it: not a declared war on soldiers, but an attack on a whole people
including *young and old, little children and women* — the phrase in the decree
nine verses later.

Amalek attacked the stragglers at the back. Haman writes them into the law.

## The scale

*To wipe out **all the Jews, every one of them**, throughout the whole kingdom of
Ahasuerus.*

A hundred and twenty-seven provinces. There was nowhere in the known world a Jew
could go that this decree would not reach.

Israel had no land, no king, no army and no temple. There was no earthly
protection anywhere in the picture.

## What is not said

God is still not mentioned. Nobody prays in this chapter.

And the reader who knows the end of the book knows what is already true while
Haman is planning: a Jewish girl has been queen of Persia for four years, and a
king's insomnia is coming.

> **The date they cast lots for.** Haman throws the *pur* — the lot — to pick the
> day, and it falls on the thirteenth of Adar, eleven months away. The one thing he
> leaves to chance gives his enemies a year to live. Proverbs says the lot is cast
> into the lap, but its every decision is from the LORD.
