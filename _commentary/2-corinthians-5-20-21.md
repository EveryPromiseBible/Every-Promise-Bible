One sentence, and it is built as a swap with nothing left over on either side.

## The two halves

*He made Him who never knew sin **to be sin** for us, so that in Him we might
become **the righteousness of God**.*

Not *treated as a sinner*, and not *made righteous-ish*. Paul writes both sides
at full strength:

| Him | us |
|---|---|
| never knew sin | had nothing else |
| **made to be sin** | **made the righteousness of God** |

Whatever weight the first half carries, the second carries the same. If He was
genuinely made sin, then we are genuinely made righteousness.

## "Never knew sin"

The verb is about knowing by experience. It is not that He avoided it
successfully — He had no acquaintance with it at all. That is what made the
transfer possible: there was nothing of His own on His side of the ledger.

## "In Him"

The location matters and Paul is exact about it. The righteousness is not
manufactured in us and inspected. It is **in Him** — held in the same place the
blessings of Ephesians 1 are held.

Which means it is as secure as He is.

## What it is for

The sentence before it is the appeal: *we come as ambassadors on behalf of
Christ… come back to God.* The exchange is the reason the appeal can be made at
all. An ambassador carries terms he did not set, and these are the terms.

> **The word "become."** Not *have* righteousness, as a possession that could be
> mislaid. Paul says we **become** it — the noun, not the adjective. It is the
> strongest way the sentence could have been put.
