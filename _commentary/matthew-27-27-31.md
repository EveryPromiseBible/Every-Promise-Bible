A whole garrison assembles to make fun of one prisoner, and every prop they use
means something they do not know.

## The company

*Called **the whole company together** around Him.*

The Greek is a cohort — the tenth part of a legion, several hundred men.

They are not busy. This is entertainment.

## The robe

*They **stripped Him** and put **a scarlet robe** on Him.*

Mark and John call it purple; Matthew calls it scarlet. It is a soldier's cloak,
faded red, used as a stand-in for imperial purple.

They dress Him as a king in order to laugh at Him.

## The crown

*They **twisted thorn branches into a crown** and pressed it onto His head.*

Thorns entered the world in Genesis 3, in the sentence on the ground: *cursed is the
ground for your sake… **both thorns and thistles** it shall bring forth for you.*

They are the visible sign of the curse on the earth, and the soldiers press a
circle of them into His scalp.

## The reed

*And put **a reed in His right hand**.*

A mock sceptre. Then: *they took the reed out of His hand, and **struck Him on the
head with it**.*

The thing they gave Him as a joke is used to drive the thorns further in.

## The kneeling

*Then they **knelt down in front of Him** and jeered, "**Hail, king of the Jews!**"*

Every gesture is correct. The robe, the crown, the sceptre, the bent knee, the
acclamation — the full ceremony of homage to a monarch.

They are performing, with total accuracy, something that is true.

Philippians says every knee will bow eventually. A cohort of Roman soldiers got
there first, and did it as a joke.

## The spitting

*They **spat on Him**.*

Isaiah had it: *I gave My back to those who struck Me, and My cheeks to those who
plucked out the beard; **I did not hide My face from shame and spitting**.*

## And then they dressed Him again

*When they were finished mocking Him **they took the robe off, put His own clothes
back on Him**, and led Him away.*

The mock robe comes off and His own clothes go back on — the ones that will be
divided among four soldiers within hours, with lots cast for the seamless tunic.

## What Pilate had already said

*I find no fault in Him at all* — three times in John's account.

The examination had passed. What follows is not judicial; it is a barracks with
nothing to do.

> **What the thorns were made of.** The curse on the ground, worn on His head — and
> Paul: *Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for
> us.* See the note on Galatians 3:10–14.
