One verse, and it is quoted or alluded to more often in the New Testament than any other
line in the Old.

## The two Lords

*The **LORD** says to **my Lord**.*

The Hebrew has two different words. The first is **YHWH**, the covenant name. The second
is **Adoni** — my lord, my master.

David wrote it, and the second Lord is somebody David calls his superior.

## The question Jesus asked about it

*"**David himself calls Him Lord — so how can He be David's son?**"*

He put it to the scribes in the temple during passion week, and nobody answered. See the
note on Mark 12:35–37.

## The instruction

*"**Sit here at My right hand**."*

The right hand of a throne is the seat of the co-regent — the place of delegated
authority.

Nothing is asked for. He is told to sit.

## What sitting means

Hebrews builds a chapter on the posture: every priest *stands ministering daily,
offering repeatedly the same sacrifices*, and this one *sat down*. See the note on
Hebrews 10:11–13.

You sit when the work is finished.

## "Until"

*"**Until** I make your enemies a footstool under your feet."*

The seating and the subduing overlap. Both are happening in the same window, and the one
seated is not the one doing the subduing.

Paul makes the same point: *He must reign **until** He has put all enemies under His
feet.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:23–28.

## The footstool

A footstool is what a seated man rests on. The picture is not a battle in progress; it
is furniture.

Joshua's commanders were told to put their feet on the necks of five kings, and told
*this is what the LORD will do to every enemy you fight.* See the note on
Joshua 10:22–27.

## Who does the making

*"**I** make your enemies a footstool."*

The subject is the first Lord. The one sitting is not described as fighting.

## Where the New Testament uses it

Peter, at Pentecost, quotes it to close his sermon: *"For David did not ascend into the
heavens, but he says himself: 'The LORD said to my Lord…'"* See the note on
Acts 2:14–16.

Hebrews 1 uses it as the closing argument about angels: *to which of the angels has He
ever said, "Sit at My right hand"?*

Stephen sees it: *"I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man **standing** at the right
hand of God"* — and the change of posture in that one sentence has been noticed ever
since.

## The rest of the psalm

*The LORD has sworn and will not relent: **You are a priest forever according to the
order of Melchizedek**.*

A king on David's throne who is also a priest of a line older than Aaron's. Hebrews takes
two chapters over it. See the notes on Genesis 14:17–20 and Hebrews 7:20–22.

> **What is unusual about the verse.** It is a report of a conversation David overheard —
> one Lord speaking to another — and the whole New Testament reads it as the seating
> after the cross.
