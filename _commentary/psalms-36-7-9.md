Three verses in the middle of a psalm that opens with a description of wickedness.
David turns from that to this without transition, and the change of temperature
is the point.

## The shadow of the wings

*People take shelter under the shadow of Your wings.*

The Hebrew for shelter is the word for taking refuge — running to a rock or a
stronghold.

The wings are the cherubim wings over the mercy seat, and also a bird's over its
young. Both pictures are in the Psalms, and both are about a small thing being
covered by something larger.

## Feasting

*They **feast on** the rich abundance of Your house, You give them drink from Your
river of delights.*

The verb is the one for being saturated, drinking to the full. The noun behind
*rich abundance* is *deshen* — the fat, the richest portion, the part of the
offering that was burned as the choice part.

**Delights** is **adanim** — the plural of the word Eden is built from.

## The two "withs"

*For with You is the fountain of life, and in Your light we see light.*

Not near You. **With** You. The source is not somewhere He points to; it is where
He is.

**Fountain** is *maqor* — a spring, the origin of running water, as against a
cistern that only holds what was poured in.

## "In Your light we see light"

The sentence is circular on purpose. You do not bring a light to God in order to
find Him. You see by His.

It is the same logic as the lamp in Psalm 119 and the lamp in 2 Peter 1 — the
seeing is done in borrowed light, and the borrowing is not a deficiency.

> **What David was looking at.** The verses immediately before this describe a man
> who has *no fear of God before his eyes*, who plots on his bed. David does not
> argue with him. He looks up instead, and the psalm changes register in a single
> line: *Your unfailing love, O LORD, reaches to the heavens.*
