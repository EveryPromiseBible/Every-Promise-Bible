The town comes out to look, sees a man in his right mind, and asks Jesus to go.

## What has happened

A man who lived among the tombs, who broke chains and could not be tamed, who cut himself with
stones night and day. See the note on Mark 5:1–5.

The legion, the request to be sent into the pigs, and two thousand animals down the bank into the
lake.

## Who spreads it

*The men who had been **tending the pigs** ran off and spread the story **through the town and out
across the farms**.*

The Greek is **hoi boskontes** — the herdsmen.

They have just lost their livelihood, and they are the ones who carry the news.

## The crowd

*And people came **to see for themselves** what had happened.*

The Greek is **elthon idein** — came to see.

## What they find

*And there was the man who had been held by the legion — **sitting down, dressed, in his right
mind**.*

The Greek is **kathemenon himatismenon kai sophronounta**.

## The three participles

**Sitting** — the man who never stopped moving, out among the tombs and in the hills night and
day.

**Clothed** — Luke adds that he *wore no clothes for a long time*.

**In his right mind** — **sophroneo**, to be of sound mind, self-controlled. The opposite of the
word for madness.

Three words, and each of them names the reversal of something in the description of him.

## The reaction

*And **it frightened them**.*

The Greek is **ephobethesan** — they were afraid.

Not of the man. They had lived with the man. He is the least frightening thing in the field.

## The report

*The ones who had seen it told the rest **exactly what had happened to the man, and what had
happened to the pigs**.*

Both facts, in that order.

## The request

*And they began to **beg Jesus to leave their district**.*

The Greek is **parakalein auton apelthein** — to urge Him to go away.

## What they weighed

A man restored, and two thousand pigs.

Mark records the calculation without comment, and does not say which side of it decided them —
the loss, or the power that caused it.

## What the man asks

*And when He got into the boat, **he who had been demon-possessed begged Him that he might be with
Him**.*

The same verb — **parekalei**. The town begs Him to go; the man begs to come.

Both requests are refused.

## The answer

*"**Go home to your friends, and tell them what great things the Lord has done for you**, and how He
has had compassion on you."*

The Greek is **apangeilon autois hosa ho Kurios soi pepoieken**.

## Why this is unusual

Mark's Gospel is full of the opposite instruction. *See that you say nothing to anyone.* *He
commanded them that no one should know it.*

This man is the exception, and he is a Gentile in Gentile territory.

## What he does

*And he departed and began to **proclaim in the Decapolis** all that Jesus had done for him, and all
marveled.*

The Decapolis — ten Greek cities east of the lake.

One man, sent home, becomes the first preacher in Gentile country.

## What happens when Jesus returns there

*Then they brought to Him one who was deaf and had an impediment in his speech.*

And in chapter 8, the feeding of four thousand in the same region — with seven baskets left. See
the note on Mark 6:42–44.

The district that asked Him to leave has a crowd waiting the next time.

> **The three participles.** Sitting, clothed, in his right mind — each one the reversal of a
> detail in the description of him. And the town's response to it is fear.
