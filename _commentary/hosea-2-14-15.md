The chapter before this is a divorce proceeding. Then one word changes everything.

## What has come before

Hosea 2 opens with God laying out a case against an unfaithful wife — strip her,
hedge her way with thorns, take back the grain and the wine and the wool, end the
feasts.

The charge is that she went after other lovers and *did not know that I gave her
grain, new wine and oil.*

## The turn

*"**So now, listen. I will draw her back to Myself.**"*

The Hebrew opens with *therefore* — and it is the strangest *therefore* in the
prophets. Everything before it argues for judgement, and the conclusion drawn is
courtship.

The verb is to allure, to entice, to persuade. It is the word for winning somebody
over.

## Into the wilderness

*"I will **lead her out into the wilderness** and **speak softly to her heart**."*

The Hebrew for the speaking is *speak upon her heart* — the idiom for wooing, used
of a man courting a woman.

And the destination is a desert. He takes her to the emptiest place available in
order to talk to her.

The wilderness in Israel's memory is the honeymoon: *I remember you, the kindness of
your youth, the love of your betrothal, when you went after Me in the wilderness.*

## The vineyards

*"**There I will give her back her vineyards.**"*

In the desert. He returns the fruitfulness in the place where nothing grows, and
before she is out of it.

## The valley

*"And **turn the Valley of Achor, that valley of trouble, into a door of hope**."*

**Achor** means trouble, and it is a place with a history.

Joshua 7 — Achan took the devoted things from Jericho, Israel was defeated at Ai, and
the valley where he was dealt with was named Achor because of it. *Why have you
troubled us? The LORD will trouble you this day.*

It was the site of Israel's first defeat in the land, and the name preserved the
shame.

God says He will make a **door** of it.

Not remove it, not rename it, not compensate for it. Turn the place itself into an
entrance.

## The last line

*"There she will answer Me **as she did when she was young**, as on the day she first
came up out of Egypt."*

The Hebrew is that she will sing there — the same verb used of Miriam at the sea with
a tambourine.

Not a chastened response. The first one.

## Who Hosea was

The prophet told to marry a woman who would be unfaithful, and to name his children
*Not-Pitied* and *Not-My-People*. See the note on Hosea 1:8–9.

And then, in chapter 3, told to go and love her again — and he buys his own wife back
for the price of a slave.

The book's argument is made with his household before it is made with words.

> **Where the door leads.** *And it shall be, in that day, that you will call Me "My
> Husband," and no longer call Me "My Master."* Two Hebrew words, *ishi* and *baali*.
> The valley of trouble opens onto a change in what she calls Him.
