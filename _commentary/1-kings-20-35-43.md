A prophet stages a scene so that a king will sentence himself.

## What has happened

Ahab has just won two victories over Ben-Hadad of Syria, both announced in advance by a prophet.

And then he lets Ben-Hadad go — *he is my brother* — and makes a treaty with him.

The man God had *determined should die*.

## The strange opening

*By the word of the LORD one of the sons of the prophets said to his companion, "**Strike me**."*

*But the man refused.*

## The consequence

*"**Because you have not obeyed the voice of the LORD**, the moment you leave me a lion will kill
you."*

*And when the man walked away, a lion found him and killed him.*

The instruction was odd and the disobedience was fatal. The narrative reports it without softening
it, and it sets the terms for what follows: this prophet's words happen.

## The second man

*The prophet found another man and said, "Strike me." That man **struck him and wounded him**.*

## The disguise

*Then the prophet went and **waited for the king along the road, disguising himself with a bandage
over his eyes**.*

He needs to look like a soldier back from the fighting.

## The story

*"Your servant went into the thick of battle, and a man brought me a captive and said, '**Guard this
man. If he goes missing, it will be your life for his**, or you'll pay a talent of silver.'"*

The stake is named up front: a life, or an impossible sum.

## The excuse

*"But **while I was busy here and there, he slipped away**."*

The Hebrew is **osseh hennah vahennah** — doing this way and that way.

Not asleep. Not negligent in any way he could name. Occupied.

## What the excuse is

The best possible one, and it is no defence at all.

He had one job, and he was doing other things.

## The verdict

*"**That is your sentence**," the king of Israel said. "**You have pronounced it yourself**."*

The Hebrew is **ken mishpatecha attah charatzta** — so is your judgment; you have decided it.

## The reveal

*Then the prophet quickly **pulled the bandage from his eyes**, and the king recognized him.*

## The application

*"You have **set free the man I had determined should die**. So it will be **your life for his life,
your people for his people**."*

The exact terms of the parable, transferred.

## The technique

The same one Nathan used with David: *there were two men in one city, one rich and one poor* — and
then *you are the man*.

A story told to somebody who cannot see it is about them, until they have committed to the
verdict.

## The reaction

*Sullen and angry, the king of Israel went home to Samaria.*

The Hebrew is **sar vezaeph** — resentful and sullen.

The same two words used of him in the next chapter, when Naboth will not sell his vineyard — and
he goes home, lies on his bed, turns his face to the wall, and will not eat.

## What happens to Ahab

*So the king of Israel died, and was brought to Samaria.*

Three years later, in a chariot at Ramoth Gilead, struck by an arrow shot at random.

## The line the passage is known for

*While I was busy here and there, he slipped away.*

> **The excuse.** Not negligence he could name — *busy here and there*. He had one thing to hold
> and was occupied with other things, and the king sentenced him for it before knowing who he was.
