The middle story of the three, and the shortest — but it changes one thing about
the picture.

## The coin could not stray

A sheep wanders off. A son leaves on purpose. A coin does neither.

It was lost in the house, by no fault and no decision of its own, and it could
not have found its way anywhere. That is a third kind of lostness, and Jesus puts
it deliberately between the other two.

## What she does

*Does she not light a lamp and sweep out the whole house, going over every corner
of it until she finds it?*

Three actions: light, sweep, search. Houses of that period had small high windows
and packed earth floors — a coin could sit in the dust for a long time. The lamp
is lit because you cannot find anything in that room without one.

And again: **until**.

## What it was worth

A drachma, roughly a day's wage. Not a fortune — one tenth of what she had.

She turns the entire house over for it, which is the proportion the story wants
you to notice.

## The joy is somewhere specific

*It is like that among the angels of God. They are glad over one sinner whose
mind is turned around.*

The first story said *in heaven*; this one says *among the angels*. Jesus is
locating the celebration a little more precisely each time.

> **Why a woman.** Jesus tells three stories in a row and gives the middle one to
> a woman searching her own house — set beside a shepherd and a father. In a
> chapter arguing about who God is like, He uses all three without comment.
