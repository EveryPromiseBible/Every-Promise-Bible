Six verses of thanksgiving, written to the church that gave him the most trouble.

## The thanks

*I **thank my God for you always**.*

Paul opens almost every letter this way. Galatians is the exception — there he goes straight
from the greeting to *I marvel that you are turning away so soon*. See the note on
Galatians 1:6–7.

Corinth had more disorder in it than Galatia. It still gets the thanksgiving.

## What he thanks God for

*Thinking of **the grace of God that was given you in Christ Jesus**.*

Not their progress, and not their conduct. What was given.

## The enrichment

*How **in Him you were made rich in every way**, in all your **speaking** and in all your
**understanding**.*

The Greek is **eploutisthete** — you were made wealthy. Passive, aorist.

**Logos** and **gnosis** — word and knowledge. The two things Corinth was proudest of, and
the two things it was misusing. Paul names them as gifts before he corrects their use of
them.

## The confirming

*Just as **the testimony about Christ was confirmed and made firm among you**.*

The Greek is **ebebaiothe** — a legal word. Ratified, guaranteed, made binding.

## The consequence

*So that you are **not behind in a single gift**.*

The Greek is **me hustereisthai** — not lacking, not falling short.

Written to people who were fighting about which gifts mattered, in a letter that spends
three chapters sorting it out.

## What they are doing meanwhile

*While you **wait for our Lord Jesus Christ to be unveiled**.*

The Greek is **apekdechomenous** — waiting eagerly, with the head up.

And **apokalupsin** — the unveiling.

## The promise

*And **He will hold you steady to the very end**.*

The Greek is **bebaiosei** — the same root as *confirmed* two verses earlier.

The testimony was confirmed among them; He will confirm them. Paul uses the word twice on
purpose.

## The verdict at the end

*So that **no charge can be laid against you** on the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.*

The Greek is **anegkletous** — unaccusable. Not *forgiven of the charges*; no charge is
brought.

He writes this to the church he is about to accuse of six or seven things.

The standing on that day and the state at the moment of writing are different questions, and
Paul is not confused about which he is addressing.

## The ground

*God is **faithful**, and it was **He who called you** into the shared life of His Son.*

The Greek is **koinonian** — fellowship, partnership, having in common.

The guarantee is not their reliability, and Paul does not pretend otherwise. It is His, and
the sentence puts the calling in God's hands too.

## The same construction elsewhere

*He who has begun a good work in you **will complete it** until the day of Jesus Christ.*
See the note on Philippians 1:3–6.

*The One who calls you is faithful. **He will do it**.* See the note on
1 Thessalonians 5:23–24.

Three letters, three closings of the same shape, and every one puts the finishing on the
caller.

## What comes next

*Now I plead with you, brethren, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that you all speak
the same thing, and that **there be no divisions among you**.*

Verse 10. The thanksgiving ends and the letter's first problem starts in the same breath.

> **Who does the confirming.** The testimony was confirmed among them, and He will confirm
> them to the end — same verb both times, and neither instance has the Corinthians as its
> subject.
