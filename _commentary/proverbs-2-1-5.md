One sentence, five verses long, and the whole weight is on the last two words.

## The shape

*If… if… yes, if… if… **then**.*

Four conditions and one result. Hebrew poetry piling up clauses before the verb arrives.

Nothing in the passage can be taken alone; it is built as a single sentence.

## The address

*My son.*

The father's voice, which runs through the first nine chapters. Not the aphorisms of the
later collection — these are a parent talking.

## The first pair

*If you **take my words to heart** and **treasure my commands within you**.*

The Hebrew is *if you receive my words and hide my commandments with you* — **tsaphan**, to
store up, to hoard, to conceal for safekeeping.

The same verb the psalmist uses: *your word I have hidden in my heart, that I might not sin
against you*.

Received, and then stored.

## The second pair

*Tuning your ear to wisdom, **turning your heart toward understanding**.*

The Hebrew for *tuning* is *inclining* — **qashab**, to prick up the ears, the way an animal
turns its head toward a sound.

Ear and heart, in that order.

## The third pair

*Yes, if you **call out for insight** and **lift your voice for understanding**.*

Now it is audible. The first four verbs were internal; this pair involves the mouth.

## The fourth pair

*If you **search for her like silver**, if you **dig for her like buried treasure**.*

The Hebrew is **chaphas** — to search, to rummage. And *if you seek her as hidden treasures*
— **matmonim**, things buried.

Mining, in the ancient world, meant hacking galleries into a hillside by hand.

Job has the description: *man puts an end to darkness, and searches every recess for ore in
the darkness and the shadow of death… he cuts out channels in the rocks*.

And then Job asks the real question: *but where can wisdom be found?*

## The wording

*Like silver.* Not *more than silver*.

The comparison is about method, not value. Look for it the way people look for money.

## The result

*Then you will **understand the fear of the LORD**, and you will **find the knowledge of
God**.*

Two things, and the first one is odd. Not *fear the LORD* — *understand* the fear of the
LORD.

The book has already said *the fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge*. Here the fear
is itself the thing to be understood.

## Who is doing the giving

The verse straight after the *then*:

*For **the LORD gives wisdom**; from His mouth come knowledge and understanding.*

Four verses of digging, and then the treasure turns out to be handed over.

The searching is real and so is the giving. The passage puts them in consecutive verses
without reconciling them.

## The other place they sit together

*Ask, and it will be given to you; **seek, and you will find**; knock, and it will be opened.*

And James: *if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all liberally and
without reproach*.

## What wisdom does next in the chapter

*He stores up sound wisdom for the upright; He is a shield to those who walk uprightly… then
you will understand righteousness and justice, equity and every good path.*

And *discretion will preserve you*, and *wisdom will deliver you from the way of evil*.

The chapter is about protection, and this passage is how it starts.

## What Proverbs says elsewhere about the search

*Happy is the man who finds wisdom… **for her proceeds are better than the profits of
silver**.*

The comparison to silver again, and this time it is about value.

> **What the comparison is about.** *Search for her like silver.* Not worth more than silver
> — looked for the way people look for money, which is to say by digging.
