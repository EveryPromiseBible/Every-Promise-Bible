The first move after Sinai, and the box goes in front.

## The departure

*So they **left the mountain of the LORD** and traveled three days.*

Almost a year at Sinai. The law given, the golden calf, the tabernacle built and raised, the
priesthood consecrated, the census taken.

And then they walk away from the mountain and the book of Numbers begins in earnest.

## The position

*And the **ark of the LORD's covenant went ahead of them the whole way**.*

The Hebrew is *went before them a three days' journey*.

Ahead. Not in the middle of the column, which is where the arrangement in chapter 2 puts the
tabernacle — three tribes on each side, the Levites in the centre.

For this march it goes to the front.

## What it was doing

*Searching out **a resting place** for them.*

The Hebrew is **menuchah** — rest, a settled place. The noun behind the psalm's *He leads me
beside the still waters* and *He makes me lie down*.

The ark is described as looking for somewhere for them to stop.

## The cloud

*And the **cloud of the LORD was over them by day** as they moved out of camp.*

Over them while the ark went before them. Two signs at once.

## The words at setting out

*Whenever the ark set out, Moses would say, "**Rise up, O LORD! Let your enemies scatter; let
those who hate you flee before you**."*

The Hebrew is **qumah YHWH**.

Note whose enemies. Not Israel's — *Your* enemies, *those who hate You*.

Psalm 68 opens with the same sentence: *let God arise, let His enemies be scattered*.

## The words at stopping

*And whenever it came to rest, he would say, "**Return, O LORD, to the countless thousands of
Israel**."*

The Hebrew is **shuvah** — return, come back.

Out at the front while they march; back in the middle when they stop.

## What these two lines are

In a synagogue, the first is said when the ark is opened and the Torah scroll is carried out,
and the second when it is put back.

Two sentences from a wilderness march, still spoken over a cabinet in a wall.

## What follows immediately

Chapter 11: *now when the people complained, it displeased the LORD… and the fire of the LORD
burned among them*.

Then the craving for meat, the quails, Miriam and Aaron against Moses, and then the twelve
spies.

The ark goes ahead searching out a resting place, and the next forty years are spent not
entering one.

## What Hebrews makes of that

*They could not enter in because of unbelief.* See the note on Hebrews 3:16–19.

And *there remains therefore a rest for the people of God*. The Greek is
**sabbatismos** — a Sabbath-keeping.

## The same arrangement at the end

*When you see the ark of the covenant… then leave your places and follow it.* See the note on
Joshua 3:1–4.

Forty years later, a different generation, the same box in front.

Between the two the ark is in the middle of the camp. It goes first at the two boundaries —
leaving the mountain, and entering the land.

## Where the resting place ends up

*Arise, O LORD, to **Your resting place**, You and the ark of Your strength.*

Psalm 132, quoted by Solomon when the ark is finally carried into the temple.

The searching that starts in Numbers 10 has an address by 2 Chronicles 6.

> **What the ark was doing out in front.** The Hebrew says searching out a resting place for
> them — and it says so at the start of forty years in which nobody rested.
