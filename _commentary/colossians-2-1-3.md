Paul describes what he is doing for strangers, and the word he uses is athletic.

## The word

*I want you to know **how great a struggle I am having** for you.*

The Greek is **agona** — a contest, a fight in the arena. The root of *agony*.

He has just used the verb: *to this end I also labour, **striving** according to His working
which works in me mightily*.

Praying is described as an event in a stadium.

## Who it is for

*For you, and for those in **Laodicea**, and for **everyone who has never met me face to
face**.*

Paul had not founded Colossae. Epaphras did — *as you also learned from Epaphras, our dear
fellow servant*.

He is writing to a church he has never visited, in a town he has never been to, from prison.

And the letter's last chapter names his condition: *remember my chains*.

## Laodicea

Ten miles from Colossae, and the letter is to be swapped: *when this epistle is read among you,
see that it is read also in the church of the Laodiceans, and that you likewise read the epistle
from Laodicea*.

The Laodicean letter is lost.

That is also the church Christ addresses last in Revelation: *because you say, "I am rich, have
become wealthy, and have need of nothing"*. See the note on Revelation 3:14–15.

## What he is asking for

*That their **hearts may be given courage**.*

The Greek is **paraklethosin** — encouraged, comforted, called alongside. Same root as
**parakletos**, the Helper.

*Bound together in love.*

The Greek is **sumbibasthentes** — knit together, joined.

## The destination

*And brought into **all the wealth of an understanding that is full and sure**.*

The Greek is **plerophorias** — full assurance, complete conviction. The word Paul uses of
Abraham being *fully convinced*.

He asks for riches, and the riches are certainty.

## The object

*Into **the knowledge of the mystery of God, and of the Father, and of Christ**.*

The Greek is **epignosin** — full knowledge, thorough knowing.

**Mysterion** — not a puzzle. Something hidden that has now been disclosed.

## The clause

*In whom **every treasure of wisdom and knowledge lies hidden**.*

The Greek is **apokruphoi** — hidden away, stored up. The root of *apocryphal*.

Two words: **sophia** and **gnosis**.

## Why it matters in this letter

Colossae was being sold something extra.

The next verses name it: *beware lest anyone cheat you through philosophy and empty deceit,
according to the tradition of men*. And *let no one cheat you of your reward, taking delight in
false humility and worship of angels*.

And the food rules: *do not touch, do not taste, do not handle*.

Somebody was offering a higher knowledge, available to initiates.

Paul's answer is that the treasures are all in one place already, and the place is a person.

## What follows immediately

*For in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily; and **you are complete in Him**.* See
the note on Colossians 2:8–10.

The Greek is **pepleromenoi** — having been filled full.

Every word in the argument is about fullness: the fullness dwells in Him, and you have been
filled in Him.

Nothing is missing, so nothing can be added.

## The letter's summary

*Christ in you, the hope of glory.*

> **What he wrestles for.** Not their behaviour. Certainty — and the reason given is that
> everything on offer elsewhere is already stored up in one person.
