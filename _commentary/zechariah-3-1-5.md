A courtroom scene, and the defence never says a word.

## The three figures

*Joshua the high priest, standing before the angel of the LORD, and **Satan standing
at his right hand to accuse him**.*

The Hebrew is **ha-satan** — the accuser, with the article. The right hand was the
prosecutor's position in a court of that period.

Joshua is the high priest of the returned exiles, standing in filthy clothes.

## What the accuser had

Everything. The Hebrew for the clothes is the strongest word available — garments
covered in excrement. It is not a poetic way of saying stained.

The high priest of Israel, in the presence of God, in that condition. There is no
suggestion anywhere in the passage that the accusation was false.

## The answer

*"**The LORD rebuke you, Satan. The LORD who has chosen Jerusalem rebuke you.**"*

No argument about the evidence. No defence of Joshua's record.

The rebuke rests on one thing: *the LORD **who has chosen** Jerusalem.* The choosing
is the whole case.

## The brand

*"**Is this not a burning stick snatched from the fire?**"*

A charred piece of wood pulled out of the flames — half burnt, blackened, and out.

That is God's own description of the man being accused. He does not deny the
scorching.

## The order of what happens

*"**Take off his filthy clothes.**" Then he said to Joshua, "**See, I have taken away
your sin, and I will clothe you in fine garments.**"*

The clothes come off, and only then is the sin declared taken away, and only then
does the new clothing go on.

Joshua does nothing in the entire scene. He does not repent, protest, promise, or
speak. Others undress him and others dress him.

The Hebrew for *fine garments* is a rare word for rich festal robes.

## The turban

*"Put **a clean turban** on his head." So they placed a clean turban on his head.*

Zechariah himself interrupts to ask for this — *and I said, put a clean turban on his
head.* The prophet joins in.

The high priest's turban carried a gold plate engraved *HOLINESS TO THE LORD*. It is
the item that marked him as fit to enter.

## Who is standing there through all of it

*While **the angel of the LORD stood by**.*

The one who rebuked the accuser stays present for the whole re-clothing.

## Where the chapter goes next

*"Hear now, O Joshua… I am bringing forth **My Servant, the BRANCH**… and **I will
remove the iniquity of that land in one day**."*

The vision ends with a promise of one day on which iniquity is removed.

> **What Jude does with the same phrase.** *Michael the archangel, contending with
> the devil… dared not bring against him a reviling accusation, but said, "**The Lord
> rebuke you.**"* The same sentence, quoted centuries later, and still the whole
> answer.
