The last three verses before four hundred years of silence.

## The backward look

*"**Remember the law of My servant Moses**, the decrees and rulings I gave him **at Horeb for all
Israel**."*

The Hebrew has **torat Mosheh avdi** — the law of Moses My servant.

Horeb is Sinai. The Old Testament closes by pointing back to the mountain where it started.

## The forward look

*"See, **I will send you the prophet Elijah** before that **great and dreadful day of the LORD**
comes."*

Elijah, who did not die — *a chariot of fire appeared, and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into
heaven*.

The one prophet with no grave is the one promised to return.

## The two adjectives

*Great and dreadful.*

The Hebrew is **hagadol vehanora** — great and terrible.

The same day is both. Malachi has already described it: *the day is coming, burning like an
oven*, and in the same chapter *the Sun of Righteousness shall arise with healing in His wings*.

## What Elijah does

*"And **he will turn the hearts of the parents to their children, and the hearts of the children
to their parents**."*

The Hebrew verb is **heshiv** — to turn, to bring back.

Not a national programme, a political settlement, or a religious reform. Households.

## The consequence if he does not

*"**So that I will not come and strike the land with total destruction**."*

The Hebrew word is **cherem** — the ban, devotion to destruction. The word used of Jericho.

The last word of the Hebrew Bible's prophets is that one.

## What the whole book has been about

Malachi is a series of arguments in which the people answer back.

*I have loved you — yet you say, "In what way have You loved us?"*

*A son honours his father… **if then I am the Father, where is My honour?***

The book opens with a father-and-son question, and it closes with fathers and children.

## Who this turned out to be

*He will also go before Him **in the spirit and power of Elijah**, "to turn the hearts of the
fathers to the children"… to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.* See the note on
Luke 1:16–17.

Gabriel quotes this verse to Zacharias, in the temple, about the son he is about to have.

And Jesus: *if you are willing to receive it, **he is Elijah who is to come***.

## Where the silence ends

Four hundred years, and then an angel standing on the right side of the altar of incense — and
the first thing announced is a father getting his heart back to his child.

## What the New Testament does with the fatherhood

*I have made Your name known to the people You gave Me out of the world.* See the note on
John 17:6–8.

*You received the Spirit of adoption by whom we cry out, "Abba, Father."* See the note on
Romans 8:15–17.

## How the New Testament ends

*The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.* See the note on
Revelation 22:20–21.

One canon ends with **cherem** and the other ends with **charis**.

> **The last word.** The Old Testament closes on a threatened curse, held off by hearts turning
> between parents and children — and the New Testament's last sentence is grace.
