A teacher asks how, and is told he has not yet got past the elementary part.

## The question

*"**How can any of this happen**?"*

The Greek is **pōs dunatai tauta genesthai**.

His third question of the night, and each one is a *how*.

## What he was told before

*"Unless one is **born again**, he cannot see the kingdom of God."* And *"the wind blows where it
wishes… **so is everyone who is born of the Spirit**."* See the notes on John 3:1–4 and John 3:5–8.

## The reply

*"**You are Israel's teacher**, and you do not understand these things?"*

The Greek is **ho didaskalos tou Israēl** — *the* teacher of Israel, with the article.

Not a teacher. A recognised authority, a member of the ruling council.

## What he should have known

The new birth is not a novelty. Ezekiel: *I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within
you*. See the note on Ezekiel 36:26–27.

The rebuke is that the material was in the books he taught from.

## The plural

*"**We** speak about what **We** know, and **We** testify to what **We** have seen."*

The Greek switches to first person plural mid-speech and does not explain who is included.

## The refusal

*"And still **you people will not accept Our testimony**."*

**Ou lambanete** — you do not receive. Plural.

He came at night alone, but the answer addresses a group.

## The two categories

*"If I have told you **earthly things** and you do not believe, how will you ever believe if I tell
you **heavenly things**?"*

The Greek is **ta epigeia** and **ta epourania**.

## Which is which

The earthly things are what has already been said: birth, wind, water.

If the elementary matter is not landing, the argument runs, the higher one will not either.

## The claim

*"**No one has gone up into heaven** except **the One who came down out of heaven**."*

The Greek is **oudeis anabebēken eis ton ouranon ei mē ho ek tou ouranou katabas**.

## The direction

Every religious system is an ascent. The sentence denies that anybody has completed one.

The only person with heaven's information is the one who came the other way.

## The last clause

*"The Son of Man, **who is in heaven**."*

The Greek is **ho ōn en tō ouranō** — the one being in heaven.

Present tense, spoken while standing in a room in Jerusalem.

## The textual note

Some manuscripts omit the clause; most translations that keep it mark it.

Where it stands, it says the speaker is in both places at once.

## What He says next

*"And as **Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness**, even so must the Son of Man be lifted
up."* See the note on John 3:14–15.

The heavenly thing He goes on to tell him is a bronze snake on a pole.

## And then

*"**For God so loved the world** that He gave His only begotten Son."* See the note on John 3:16–17.

## Where Nicodemus turns up again

Objecting on procedure in the council: *"Does our law judge a man before it hears him?"*

And at the end, with about a hundred pounds of myrrh and aloes. See the note on John 19:38–40.

He brings enough spices for a king's burial, in daylight, in public.

> **The direction of travel.** Every system is a climb. The sentence says nobody has completed one —
> the only man with heaven's information came down.
