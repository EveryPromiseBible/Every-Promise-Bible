The setup, and two things in it are decided before any servant does anything.

## Whose property it is

*"A man going away on a journey, who called his servants and **put his property into their
hands**."*

The Greek is **ta hyparchonta autou** — the things belonging to him.

Nothing in the parable is the servants'. They are given custody of somebody else's estate, and the
accounting at the end is about what was done with what was handed over.

*For **what do you have that you did not receive?** Now if you did indeed receive it, why do you
boast as if you had not received it?*

## The amounts

*"To one he gave **five talents**, to another **two**, and to another **one**."*

A talent was an enormous sum — roughly twenty years of a labourer's wages. The man with one is not
given a token; he is given a fortune.

## The clause

*"**Each according to what he was able to handle.**"*

The Greek is **hekasto kata ten idian dynamin** — each according to his own capacity.

The distribution is unequal and the inequality is deliberate, calibrated, and the master's
decision. Nobody is given more than they can carry, and nobody is asked afterwards why they were
not somebody else.

*But **the manifestation of the Spirit is given to each one for the profit of all**… distributing to
each one individually **as He wills**.*

*Having then gifts differing according to the grace that is given to us, let us use them.*

**Romans** 12, and **Paul**'s word for the differing gifts is **charin** — grace.

## What the first two do

*"**Went at once** and put them to work."*

The Greek is **eutheos** — immediately.

## What the third does

*"But the man who had received one talent went off, **dug a hole in the ground, and hid his
master's money**."*

Burying money was, in Jewish law, the recognised way to be safe from liability for a deposit. He is
not a thief and he is not careless. He takes the legally prudent option.

## What he says later

*"**I was afraid**, and went and hid your talent in the ground."*

And before that: *I knew you to be a hard man, reaping where you have not sown.*

The parable gives his reason and it is a misdescription of his master. He acts on a picture of
somebody who does not exist in the story, and the fear follows from the picture.

*There is **no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear**, because fear involves torment.*

*For you did not receive **the spirit of bondage again to fear**, but you received the Spirit of
adoption by whom we cry out, "Abba, Father."*

## What is not the problem

Yield. The two-talent man does not match the five-talent man and hears the identical sentence. See
the note on Matthew 25:19–23.

The one-talent man is not condemned for earning less. He is condemned for having a master he was
afraid of.

> **What the parable settles before anybody acts.** Whose money it is, and how much each could
> carry. Both are the master's decisions, and the servant who fails does it holding a view of him
> the story does not support.
