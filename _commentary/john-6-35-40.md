The first of the seven great *I am* sayings in John, and what follows it is a
run of promises with nothing conditional in them.

## The saying

*I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to Me will never be hungry, and whoever
believes in Me will never be thirsty again.*

Bread and thirst in one sentence — He answers a need He was not asked about. The
crowd had come looking for another meal after the five thousand.

## The double negative

*The one who comes to Me I will never turn away.*

The Greek stacks two negatives, which in that language intensifies rather than
cancels: I will certainly not, under any circumstances, cast out.

And the verb is *ekballo* — to throw out, put outside. It is the word used of
driving out demons and of expelling someone from a synagogue.

Whatever else is uncertain, the person who comes is not going to be shown the
door.

## Twice, the same will

Jesus states the Father's will twice in three verses, and both times it is about
keeping:

- *that of everything He has given Me I should **lose none of it**, but raise it
  up on the last day*
- *that everyone who looks to the Son and believes in Him should have eternal
  life, and I will raise him up*

The verb for *lose* is the one used of a lost sheep and a lost coin. He describes
His own commission as not losing anything.

## The order

*Everyone the Father gives Me will come to Me, and the one who comes to Me I will
never turn away.*

Both halves are in the sentence and John does not resolve the tension. The giving
comes first; the coming is real; and the welcome is unconditional.

> **The repeated line.** *I will raise him up on the last day* appears four times
> in this chapter. Whatever the bread of life turns out to mean, Jesus keeps
> attaching it to resurrection rather than to sustenance.
