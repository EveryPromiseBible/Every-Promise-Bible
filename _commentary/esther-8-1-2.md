The transfer happens in a single day, and it is total.

## "That same day"

Not a process. The morning Haman was still second in the empire; by evening his
estate belongs to the woman he sentenced to death.

## The house

*King Ahasuerus gave Queen Esther **the household of Haman, the enemy of the
Jews**.*

The writer will not let his name stand alone. He is *the enemy of the Jews* here
and in five other places — the title follows him to the end of the book.

Everything he boasted about to his friends — the glorious riches, the great house
— is signed over to a Jewish orphan.

## "For Esther had told him what he was to her"

The secret comes out at last. Mordecai had told her at the beginning not to
reveal her people or her family, and she had kept it for years.

She tells the king now, and the man at the gate walks into the royal presence.

## The ring

*The king took off his signet ring, **the one he had taken back from Haman**, and
gave it to Mordecai.*

The same ring. Not a new one — the writer says where it came from.

That ring sealed the decree ordering the death of every Jew in the empire. It is
now on the hand of a Jew, and the next thing it will seal is the decree that lets
them live.

The instrument of the sentence becomes the instrument of the rescue.

## Esther sets him over it

*And Esther set Mordecai over the household of Haman.*

She receives it and immediately puts him in charge of it. Two verses, and the
whole apparatus of the enemy is in the hands of the two people it was built
against.

> **What has not changed.** The first decree still stands. Persian law cannot be
> revoked, and the thirteenth of Adar is still in the calendar — Haman is dead and
> the sentence he wrote is not. That is the problem chapter 8 has to solve, and it
> is not solved by cancelling anything. See the note on Esther 8:9–14.
