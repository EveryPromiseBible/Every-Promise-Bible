An offering for the sins nobody noticed committing.

## The category

*If someone **acts unfaithfully and sins unintentionally** against any of the **LORD's holy
things**.*

The Hebrew is **ma'al** — treachery — combined with **bishgagah**, in error, by straying.

Both words in one clause. Treachery done by accident.

## What the holy things were

Anything set apart for the sanctuary: tithes, firstfruits, dedicated property, the offerings
themselves.

Eating what belonged to the priests, or using a dedicated animal for ordinary work. Things a
man could do in a week without a thought.

## The offering

*He must bring to the LORD a **ram without flaw** from the flock, **of the proper value in
silver, by the sanctuary weight**, as a **guilt offering**.*

The Hebrew is **asham** — the guilt or trespass offering.

It is the only sacrifice with a price attached. The animal had to be assessed in shekels
before it could be accepted.

## The repayment

*He **makes good for the wrong** he did against the holy thing, **adding a fifth of its
value**, and gives it to the priest.*

The same rate as the property law: full value plus twenty per cent. See the note on
Leviticus 6:1–7.

Chapter 5 is for what was taken from God. Chapter 6 is for what was taken from a neighbour.
The arithmetic is identical.

## The second case

*If someone **sins by doing what the LORD forbids, even without knowing it, he is still
guilty and bears his responsibility**.*

The Hebrew is *though he does not know it, yet he is guilty and shall bear his iniquity*.

Ignorance is stated and does not remove liability. Leviticus does not argue the point; it
just says it.

## What is unusual about the second case

No restitution is required, because nobody can price what was not identified.

The man brings the ram anyway. He is not told what he did.

## The last sentence

*It is a guilt offering, for **he was truly guilty before the LORD**.*

The Hebrew doubles the root: *guilting he was guilty*.

## What David prayed about it

*Who can understand his errors? **Cleanse me from secret faults**.*

The Hebrew for *secret* is **nistarot** — hidden things, and it can mean hidden from the man
himself.

## Which offering Isaiah names

*When You make His soul **an offering for sin**.*

The Hebrew there is **asham** — this offering, from this chapter. See the note on
Isaiah 53:10–12.

Five kinds of sacrifice in Leviticus, and the one Isaiah picks for the Servant is the one
that comes with a valuation and a restitution clause.

## What the New Testament does with the unwitting sin

*Father, forgive them, **for they do not know what they do**.*

And Paul: *I obtained mercy because **I did it ignorantly** in unbelief*.

And Peter to Jerusalem: *I know that you did it in ignorance, as did also your rulers.*

Three times, the ignorance is stated — and every time it is a reason given for mercy rather
than an argument that nothing happened.

## The sentence repeated

*And **he is forgiven**.*

Twice in six verses, both times passive, both times at the end of the paragraph.

Nothing in either case depends on the man working out what he did.

> **The hardest clause in the chapter.** *Though he does not know it, yet he is guilty* —
> and the remedy given is an animal with a price on it, brought by a man who could not name
> the offence.
