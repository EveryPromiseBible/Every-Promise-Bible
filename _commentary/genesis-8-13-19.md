The ground has been dry for nearly two months and Noah is still inside.

## What he does when he sees dry land

*Noah **removed the covering** from the ark and looked out — and the surface of the
ground was dry.*

He looks. He does not leave.

That is the first day of the first month. The next date given is the twenty-seventh
day of the second month — fifty-seven days later — and only then does God speak.

He had been shut in by the LORD's own hand and he waited to be let out the same
way. See the note on Genesis 7:11–16.

## The word

*Then God said to Noah, "**Come out** of the ark."*

He went in when God said *come in*, and he comes out when God says *come out*.

The entire year is bracketed by two instructions, and Noah supplies neither of
them.

## The list

*"You, your wife, your sons, and their wives."*

Note the order. Going in, it was *you and your sons, your wife, and your sons'
wives* — the men and then the women.

Coming out: *you, your wife, your sons, and their wives* — the couples together.
A year of confinement and the households are paired.

## "So they can multiply and fill the earth"

The same commission given to Adam in Genesis 1 — *be fruitful and multiply, and
fill the earth.*

The world is being restarted with the original sentence.

## Every one that went in

*Every animal, every creeping thing, and every bird **came out of the ark, kind by
kind**.*

The Hebrew is *by their families*.

Nothing that went in is missing. The account has already recorded that the animals
came to Noah rather than being hunted, and it now records that all of them came out
again.

The count going in matched the count coming out, across a year, in a boat, with no
port of call.

## What is under his feet

A world that had been scoured. Everything he had known was gone — every place, every
person outside the eight, every landmark.

He steps out onto ground that is completely dry and completely emptied.

## What he does first

The next verse: *Noah built an altar to the LORD, and took of every clean animal
and every clean bird, and offered burnt offerings.*

He had been given the clean animals by sevens rather than twos — the extra ones,
which had been fed and carried for a year, were for this.

*And the LORD smelled a soothing aroma*, and said in His heart *I will never again
curse the ground for man's sake.*

The promise not to do it again follows an altar.

> **How long it lasted.** He entered on the seventeenth day of the second month and
> came out on the twenty-seventh day of the second month the following year — a year
> and ten days, shut in.
