Two kings go into a battle they were warned about. One of them has a plan, and it
involves the other one.

## Ahab's arrangement

*"I will disguise myself and go into battle, but you wear your royal robes."*

He asks his ally to stay identifiable while he becomes invisible. And the reader
already knows what Ahab does not: the king of Aram has ordered his chariot
commanders to fight nobody *except the king of Israel*.

So the disguise sends the entire enemy strike force at the man in the robes.

## What happened to the man in the robes

*When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they thought, "This is the king of
Israel," and they turned to attack him.*

He is surrounded, in the wrong battle, wearing the wrong clothes, because he
agreed to an alliance at a banquet.

## And then

*But Jehoshaphat cried out, and the LORD helped him. God drew them away from
him.*

Three short clauses. He was in a mess entirely of his own making, in a war he had
been told not to enter, and when he shouted, God moved the army off him.

No rebuke recorded at that moment. No conditions. He cried out and was helped.

## The arrow

*But someone drew his bow at random and struck the king of Israel between the
sections of his armour.*

A shot fired at nobody in particular, finding the one gap in a disguised man's
armour. Everything Ahab had arranged to make himself safe was undone by an arrow
with no aim.

Chronicles reports it without comment: *at random*, and then a king propped up in
his chariot until sunset, dying.

> **Two men, one battle.** Both were where they should not have been. One called
> out and was pulled clear; the other had a plan and did not survive the
> afternoon. The chapter puts the two outcomes side by side and leaves them there.
