Nine words in English, and three New Testament books are built on them.

## The sentence

*And Abram **believed** God, and God **counted it to him as righteousness**.*

The Hebrew for *believed* is **aman** — to lean on, to trust, to find something
firm enough to put weight on. It is the root of **amen**.

## What he was believing

The verses before it: God takes him outside at night and says *look now toward
heaven, and count the stars if you are able to number them. So shall your
descendants be.*

Abram is seventy-five, childless, with a barren wife. There is nothing to inspect
except the sky and the promise.

## "Counted"

**Chashab** — to reckon, to impute, to enter into an account. Paul's Greek for it is
**logizomai**, the bookkeeping word.

Righteousness was **credited** to him. Not produced by him, not earned, not
demonstrated. Entered on his account by the One keeping the books.

## What Abram had not done

There was no law. Sinai is four hundred and thirty years away — no commandments, no
tabernacle, no priesthood, no sacrifice system, no circumcision yet either. Paul
makes a great deal of that last one: the declaration came *while he was still
uncircumcised*, so that he could be the father of everyone who believes.

He had not built anything, kept anything, or performed anything.

He believed, and the entry was made.

## What Abram did afterwards

Went down to Egypt and told a king his wife was his sister. Took Hagar because the
promise seemed slow. Laughed on his face when God said Sarah would have a son.

The entry stood.

## The three books that quote it

Paul in Romans 4, to prove righteousness is credited rather than earned. Paul again
in Galatians 3, to prove the blessing of Abraham comes by faith. And James 2, from
the other side, on what a living faith looks like.

Three arguments, one verse, and all three treat it as the founding precedent.

## What God calls him

*The offspring of **Abraham My friend**.* Isaiah says it, and 2 Chronicles and
James say it too.

The only man in Scripture God calls His friend, and the friendship is dated from a
night when he had nothing but a sky full of stars and a sentence.
