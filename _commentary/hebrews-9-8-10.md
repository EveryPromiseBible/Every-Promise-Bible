The first room was not an entrance. It was a closed door with furniture in it.

## Who is speaking

*The **Holy Spirit was saying something** by all of this.*

The Greek is **touto delountos tou Pneumatos tou Hagiou** — the Holy Spirit signifying this.

The architecture is treated as speech. The floor plan is the sentence.

## What He was saying

*As long as **that front room was still standing**, **the road into the holiest place had not yet been
opened**.*

The Greek is **mēpō pephanerōsthai tēn tōn hagiōn hodon** — the way of the holies not yet having
been made manifest.

## The layout

Two rooms, one curtain between them. Priests went into the first daily. One man went into the
second once a year, with blood, and not without it. See the note on Hebrews 9:6–7.

## The point about the wall

A curtain is a statement. As long as it hangs, the room behind it is shut.

The daily work in the outer room was, by its continuing, evidence that the inner one was closed.

## The word for picture

*The whole arrangement was **a picture of the time they were living in**.*

The Greek is **parabolē eis ton kairon ton enestēkota** — a parable for the present time.

## What the offerings could not do

*Gifts were brought and sacrifices were made, and **not one of them could reach the conscience of
the worshiper and leave him finished**.*

The Greek is **kata suneidēsin teleiōsai** — to perfect as to conscience.

**Teleioō** — to bring to completion, to finish.

## Where they did reach

The body. The outside.

*It was all a matter of **food and drink and one washing after another** — **rules about the body**.*

The Greek is **dikaiōmata sarkos** — ordinances of flesh. And **baptismois** — washings.

## What the letter says the problem was

Not that the sacrifices were fraudulent. That they landed on the wrong surface.

They cleaned what could be seen and stopped at the skin.

## The evidence given for it

*For then would they not have ceased to be offered? For the worshipers, **once purified, would have
had no more consciousness of sins**.* See the note on Hebrews 10:1–2.

The argument is from the calendar: a sacrifice that worked would not need repeating next year.

## The time limit

*Laid on the people **until the time came for everything to be put right**.*

The Greek is **mechri kairou diorthōseōs** — until a time of setting straight.

**Diorthōsis** — a reformation, a putting right. A medical word for setting a broken bone.

The system is described as temporary in its own terms.

## What happens to the curtain

*Then, behold, **the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom**.* See the note on
Matthew 27:50–51.

From the top. Nobody reached that high.

## The conclusion the letter draws

*Therefore, brethren, having **boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus**, **by a new and
living way which He consecrated for us, through the veil, that is, His flesh**.* See the note on
Hebrews 10:19–21.

The Greek is **prosphatos** — freshly slaughtered. A way that does not go stale.

## The word for boldness

**Parrēsia** — the right of a free citizen to speak in the assembly. Frankness, unrestricted
speech.

The opposite of what the two-room building was saying.

> **The architecture as argument.** While the outer room stood, the inner one was shut. The Spirit's
> message, according to this letter, was the floor plan itself.
