A house full of professional mourners, and Jesus clears them all out.

## The noise

*Jesus heard the noise of it — the weeping and the wailing and all the
commotion.*

Funeral wailing was hired. Even a poor family was expected to provide mourners
and flute players, and the volume was the point.

## What He says, and what they do

*"The child is not dead. She is asleep." And they laughed in His face.*

Mark keeps the laughter in. The people making the noise switch to mockery
instantly, which tells you what the grief was worth.

Jesus is not denying the death — He uses *asleep* the way the New Testament uses
it everywhere, of a state that ends. See the note on 1 Thessalonians 4:13–15.

## Who is allowed in

*He put them all outside.* Then five people: the father, the mother, and Peter,
James and John.

The parents are in the room. Whatever happens next, they see it.

## The words

*"Talitha koum" — which means, "Little girl, I am speaking to you. Get up."*

Mark preserves the Aramaic, which he does only a handful of times. This is what
He actually said, in the language He said it in — a household phrase, the sort of
thing a parent says in the morning.

No formula, no gesture. He takes her hand and speaks to her.

## The last instruction

*He said they should give her something to eat.*

Mark ends the raising of a dead child with a note about breakfast. The
practicality is the tell — the girl is not a vision or a spirit. She is a
twelve-year-old who has not eaten.

> **The detail Mark saves for the end.** *She was twelve years old.* And the woman
> healed on the way to this house had been bleeding twelve years. Mark
> deliberately interleaves the two stories, and gives the same number to both.
> See the note on Mark 5:30–34.
