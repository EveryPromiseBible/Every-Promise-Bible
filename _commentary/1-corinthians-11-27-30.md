Paul gives a reason for weakness and sickness in the Corinthian church, and the
reason he gives is not sin in general. It is one specific failure: *not
discerning the Lord's body.*

## What the word means

**Diakrino** — to distinguish, to tell one thing apart from another. Not
*deserve*. Not *feel worthy of*. To recognise what is in front of you and see
what it is.

So the failure is a failure of recognition. Something was being handled without
being seen for what it is.

## What there was to recognise

Paul has just quoted the words of institution, four verses earlier: *this is My
body, which is for you.* The bread is named as the body given on their behalf.

That is what was going unrecognised at Corinth — people eating in a hurry, some
drunk, the wealthy shaming the poor, and the bread on the table treated as
nothing in particular. Paul's whole complaint is that they were not seeing what
they were holding.

## "In an unworthy way"

The Greek is an adverb. It describes **how** they ate, not who they were.

That distinction is the difference between a table and a tribunal. Paul is not
saying only worthy people may come — that would empty the room, and it would
contradict what the bread means. He is saying there is a way of coming to it that
misses the point of it entirely.

## The remedy he gives

Not staying away. Two verses later: *wait for one another.* And in the same
paragraph, *let a man examine himself, and so let him eat* — the examining leads
to eating, not away from it.

The answer to eating badly is eating properly, with the body recognised.

> **The last line, and what it implies.** *For this reason many among you are weak
> and sick.* Paul states a link between failing to discern the body and physical
> weakness — which means the reverse is worth taking seriously too. This is a
> table where the body broken *for you* is set in front of you, and Paul plainly
> thought that recognising it mattered for more than the soul.
