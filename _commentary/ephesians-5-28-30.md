Paul is writing about marriage and says something about the church that goes
further than the illustration needed.

## The argument

*No one ever hated his own flesh; **he feeds it and tends it warmly**.*

The Greek for *tends it warmly* is **thalpo** — to keep warm, and it is used of a
bird brooding over her young in the nest.

Paul's case to husbands is not built on duty. It is built on what a man already
does for his own body without being asked.

## And then the turn

*— exactly as **the Lord does the church**.*

The same two verbs, applied to Him. He feeds it and He broods over it.

## The clause that goes furthest

*For **we are parts of His body, of His flesh and of His bones**.*

Paul could have stopped at *His body*. He does not.

The phrase is Adam's, word for word, when he first saw Eve: *this is now **bone of
my bones and flesh of my flesh***.

Adam said it about a woman taken out of his side while he slept. Paul says it about
the church, and two verses later he quotes the rest of that sentence — *for this
reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the
two shall become one flesh* — and adds: *this is a great mystery, but I speak
concerning Christ and the church.*

## What the union is not

Not an arrangement, an alliance, or a membership. Paul uses the language of one
body.

*He who is joined to the Lord is one spirit with Him*, he says in the other letter.
And in this one: *God raised us up together, and made us sit together in the
heavenly places in Christ Jesus* — the tense is past.

## What that means about the body

The head is in one place and the body is not sick in a way the head is not.

Whatever is true of Him is what the passage says is true of the parts. *As He is,
so are we in this world* — 1 John says it about now, not later. See the note on
1 John 4:17.

## Where the instruction lands

*Husbands ought to love their own wives as their own bodies.*

Everything addressed to the husband in this passage is about what he gives:
love her, give yourself for her, feed her, keep her warm.

> **What was taken from His side.** Adam's bride came out of an opened side while
> he slept. On the cross, a soldier *pierced His side with a spear, and immediately
> blood and water came out.* John records it and then insists on it: *he who has
> seen it has borne witness, and his testimony is true.*
