key: Psalms 103:8–10
title: He does not deal with us as our sins deserve
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Three verses, and the last of them is one of the plainest statements of grace in the Old
Testament.

## Where the first line comes from

*The LORD is **merciful and gracious, slow to anger, overflowing with love**.*

That is a quotation. It is what God said about Himself to Moses in the cleft of the rock, after
the golden calf, when Moses asked to see His glory. See the note on Exodus 34:5–7.

David is not composing a description. He is repeating the one God gave.

## The two negatives

*He will not always accuse, **He will not hold His anger forever**.*

The Hebrew for *accuse* is **riv** — to bring a legal case, to contend at law.

*Who shall bring a charge against God's elect? **It is God who justifies. Who is he who
condemns?***

**Paul** asks the same two questions in Romans 8 and leaves both unanswered, because in his
argument nobody is left standing to make the charge.

## The sentence

*He **does not deal with us as our sins deserve**, He **does not pay us back for our wrongs**.*

Said twice, two ways. Not according to our sins, and not according to our iniquities.

*For the wages of sin is death, **but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our
Lord**.*

Wages are what you are paid according to what you did. The psalm says He does not pay that way,
and **Paul** names what is given instead of the wage.

## Why He can do it without injustice

The psalm does not say. It states the fact and moves on to the east and the west.

*Whom God set forth as **a propitiation by His blood**, through faith… **that He might be just
and the justifier** of the one who has faith in **Jesus**.*

That is the missing clause, supplied a thousand years later. The psalm knows what God does; the
gospel says how it was possible.

> **The verse that carries it.** *He has not dealt with us according to our sins.* No condition
> is attached to it anywhere in the psalm, and no explanation is offered — only the fact, said
> twice.
