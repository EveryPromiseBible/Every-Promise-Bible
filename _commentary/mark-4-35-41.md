Mark keeps a detail the other Gospels leave out, and it is the kind of thing an
eyewitness remembers.

## The cushion

*Jesus was in the stern, asleep on a cushion.*

The Greek is *the* cushion — the definite article. It is the steersman's seat
cushion, a fixed part of a fishing boat.

Only Mark records it, and Mark's Gospel is the one the early church associated
with Peter's preaching. Peter owned a boat.

## What He had said

*Let us cross over to the other side.*

The sentence before the storm. Everything that happens next takes place inside
that instruction — they are going where He said they were going.

## The question they ask

*Teacher, do You not care that we are going down?*

Not *save us*. It is an accusation, and it is about His caring rather than His
power. Fishermen who have been on that lake all their lives are certain they are
sinking.

## What He says to the sea

*"Peace. Be still."*

The Greek behind *be still* is the word used for muzzling — put a muzzle on it.
It is the same word Mark used a chapter earlier when Jesus silenced an unclean
spirit.

He speaks to the storm the way He speaks to a demon.

## The two fears

*"Why are you so frightened? Do you still not believe?" And a deep awe came
over them.*

Mark is precise: they were afraid of the storm, and after it stopped they were
more afraid. The word for their reaction afterwards is the great fear reserved
for the presence of God.

They ask *who is this?* — and the answer, for anyone who knew the Psalms, is that
stilling the sea is something God does.

> **What the disciples had already been told.** He had just spent the afternoon
> teaching in parables from that boat. The storm follows immediately, and Mark
> places it there deliberately: the men who had heard the word all day are the
> ones asked whether they still do not believe.
