The psalm quoted at the cross more than any other except the twenty-second.

## The opening

*Save me, O God. **The waters have come up to my neck**.*

The Hebrew is *the waters have come in unto my soul* — **nephesh**, which can mean the
throat.

Water at the throat. Not drowning yet, and past the point of standing.

## The mud

*I sink in **deep mud where there is no place to stand**.*

The Hebrew is *the mire of the deep*, and *there is no standing*.

Jeremiah was lowered into a cistern with no water in it, *and Jeremiah sank in the mire* —
the same word.

## The flood

*I have come into **deep waters**, and the **flood sweeps over me**.*

Three water images in three lines: at the neck, under the feet, over the head.

## The exhaustion

*I am **worn out with my crying**, my **throat is dry**, my eyes **grow dim from looking,
looking for my God**.*

The Hebrew is *my throat is parched* — he has cried until his voice has gone.

And the eyes fail *while I wait for my God*. The doubling in the translation catches the
Hebrew participle: the looking is continuous and it is what is wearing him out.

## The number

*More than **the hairs on my head** are those who **hate me for no reason**.*

The Hebrew is **sinah chinnam** — hating without cause. Free hatred, gratuitous.

Jesus quotes this line in the upper room and says it is about Him: *but this happened that
the word might be fulfilled which is written in their law, "They hated Me without a cause."*
See the note on John 15:22–25.

## The last line

*They make me **pay back what I never stole**.*

The Hebrew is *that which I took not away, then I restored it*.

The verb is the restitution word. This is the legal language of Exodus 22 and Leviticus 6 —
a man paying the multiple for goods he never took. See the note on Exodus 22:1–4.

The psalmist is describing a courtroom outcome, not a mugging.

## What else the psalm supplies

*Zeal for Your house has eaten Me up* — quoted by the disciples when He cleared the temple.

*They also gave me **gall** for my food, and for my thirst they gave me **vinegar** to
drink* — all four Gospels at the crucifixion.

*Let their dwelling place be desolate; let no one live in their tents* — Peter, in Acts 1,
applying it to Judas.

Four separate quotations from one psalm, in four different contexts.

## Whose psalm it is

David's, by the heading. And it contains a line no reader has ever comfortably applied to
Christ: *O God, You know my foolishness, and my sins are not hidden from You.*

The New Testament quotes the psalm freely and never quotes that verse.

## The turn

*But as for me, my prayer is to You, O LORD, **in the acceptable time**.*

And at the end, before anything has changed: *I will praise the name of God with a song, and
will magnify Him with thanksgiving.*

The praise arrives while he is still in the water.

## The last two verses

*For God will save Zion and build the cities of Judah… the descendants of His servants shall
inherit it, and **those who love His name shall dwell in it**.*

The psalm that begins with a man up to his neck ends with people living in a city.

> **The legal word in verse 4.** *Restored what I did not take* — the Hebrew is the
> restitution verb from the theft statutes. He is paying a multiple on goods that were never
> in his hands.
