The two examples given of demonic doctrine are celibacy and dieting.

## The source

*The Spirit **says it in so many words**.*

The Greek is **rhētōs legei** — expressly, in stated terms.

The adverb appears once in the New Testament. Not an inference Paul is drawing; a message he says
was given plainly.

## The timing

*Later on there will come a time when **some will let go of the faith**.*

The Greek is **apostēsontai** — they will stand away from. The root of **apostasia**.

## Where they go

*And give their attention instead to **spirits that lead people astray** and to **teachings that come up
out of demons**.*

The Greek is **didaskaliais daimoniōn**.

The genitive is one of source. Doctrines originating from them, not doctrines about them.

## What the doctrines are

The passage names two, and both are abstinences.

## The mechanism

*It will happen through **the pretending of men who tell lies**.*

The Greek is **en hupokrisei pseudologōn** — in the hypocrisy of liars.

Not demons speaking audibly. Men teaching.

## The condition

*Men whose own consciences have been **burned over until they feel nothing at all**.*

The Greek is **kekaustēriasmenōn tēn idian suneidēsin**.

**Kautēriazō** — to brand with a hot iron. The root of *cautery*.

## What the image is

Two readings have been offered.

Branded, as a slave or an animal is marked as somebody's property.

Or cauterised — burnt over until the nerve is dead and the surface registers nothing.

The Illumination takes the second.

## Why it matters

The teachers imposing the strictest rules are described as the ones who feel least.

Paul does not say their consciences are oversensitive. He says they are numb.

## The first prohibition

*They **forbid people to marry**.*

The Greek is **kōluontōn gamein**.

## The second

*They **order people to keep away from certain foods**.*

The Greek is **apechesthai brōmatōn**.

## The rebuttal

*Foods **God Himself made to be taken with thanks** by those who believe and who have come to know the
truth.*

The Greek is **eis metalēmpsin meta eucharistias** — for sharing with thanksgiving.

## The argument

Not that the rules are excessive. That they contradict the doctrine of creation.

*And God saw everything that He had made, and indeed it was very good.*

Marriage and food are both instituted before Genesis 3.

## What follows

*For **every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be refused if it is received with
thanksgiving**; for it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer.* See the note on
1 Timothy 4:4–5.

The Greek is **hagiazetai** — it is made holy.

## Where else Paul does this

*These things have an **appearance of wisdom** in self-imposed religion, false humility, and neglect
of the body, but are of **no value against the indulgence of the flesh**.* See the note on
Colossians 2:20–23.

*"Do not touch, do not taste, do not handle."*

## The instruction to Timothy

*If you instruct the brethren in these things, you will be a good minister of Jesus Christ… but
**reject profane and old wives' fables, and exercise yourself toward godliness**.* See the note on
1 Timothy 4:6–8.

And a few verses later, on the same subject: *no longer drink only water, but use a little wine for
your stomach's sake*.

> **The numbness.** The men laying down the hardest rules are described as the ones whose
> consciences have been burnt past feeling. Paul makes the severity the symptom.
