key: Revelation 7:9–10
title: "Too many to count"
updated: 2026-08-09

Immediately after a chapter of careful arithmetic, John looks again and gives up counting.

## The contrast with the verses before

The previous paragraph numbers the sealed: twelve thousand from each tribe, listed by name, a
hundred and forty-four thousand exactly.

Then: *there was a crowd — **so many people that no one could have counted them***.

The counted and the countless, side by side. And the promise God made to Abraham was in the second
category: *look now toward heaven, and count the stars if you are able to number them… **so shall
your descendants be**.*

## Four words for everybody

*Out of **every nation and every family line and every people and every language**.*

John stacks four terms where one would do, and he does it repeatedly through the book. The effect is
deliberate exhaustiveness: there is no category of human being that is not represented in that
crowd.

The promise to Abraham had that in it too — *in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed* —
and Paul says the promise was a gospel preached in advance.

## Palm branches

*Every one of them was **holding up a palm branch**.*

Only one feast in Israel's calendar required branches: Tabernacles, the seventh and last, the harvest
festival where the nation lived in shelters for a week to remember the wilderness.

It is also what the crowd in Jerusalem grabbed when He came in on a donkey.

And it is a harvest image at the end of a book about a harvest — a countless crowd, brought in, at
the last feast of the year.

## What they shout

*Our God, the One on the throne, and the Lamb — **the saving belongs to them**.*

**He soteria** — *the* salvation, with the article. Not *salvation is a good thing* but *the saving
— this, what happened to us — belongs to Him.*

It is a statement of ownership. Every person in that crowd is saying that the rescue was not theirs
and that they know exactly whose it was.

## Where they came from

The elder asks who they are, and answers his own question: *these are the ones who came out of the
great tribulation, and **washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb**.*

The whiteness is not their own and the shout says so.

> **Nobody could count them.** That is the fact to sit with. Whatever else this book contains, its
> picture of the outcome is a number too large to take, drawn from every language on earth, standing
> — the answer to *who is able to stand?* — and shouting that the saving belonged to God and to the
> Lamb.
