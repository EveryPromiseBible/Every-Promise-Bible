The only place in Luke where He calls them friends, and it is in a paragraph about being killed.

## The address

*"**My friends**, I tell you."*

The Greek is **tois philois mou**.

Luke uses it once. John records *I have called you friends* at the last supper.

## The setting

An innumerable crowd — *so many that they trampled one another* — and He turns to speak to the
disciples first.

## The first instruction

*"**Do not be afraid of those who can kill the body and after that can do nothing more**."*

The Greek is **meta tauta me echonton perissoteron ti poiesai** — after these things having nothing
further to do.

He grants the killing and names the limit.

## The second

*"But **I will tell you who to fear**: fear the One who, after the body is dead, **has authority to
throw a person into hell**."*

The Greek is **exousian echonta** — having authority.

**Geenna** — the valley outside Jerusalem where refuse burned.

## The repetition

*"Yes — **He is the one to hold in awe**."*

He says it twice, which he rarely does.

## What the sentence does

Displaces one fear with a larger one.

The logic is not that the second fear is pleasant. It is that a person who holds it has nothing
left over for the first.

## The turn

*"Are five sparrows not sold for **two small coins**?"*

The Greek is **assarion duo** — two assaria, the smallest copper coin.

Matthew has two for one coin. Luke has five for two, which means the fifth is thrown in.

## The clause

*"And **not one of them is forgotten in God's sight**."*

The Greek is **ouk estin epilelesmenon enopion tou Theou**.

**Epilanthanomai** — to forget, to overlook.

Matthew's version says *not one falls to the ground without your Father*. Luke's says not one is
forgotten. See the note on Matthew 10:28–31.

## Which sparrow

The free one.

Luke's arithmetic makes it unavoidable: the fifth bird is not part of the price, and the sentence
says it is not forgotten either.

## The hairs

*"Why, **even the hairs on your head are all numbered**."*

The Greek is **erithmentai** — perfect passive.

## The conclusion

*"So **do not be afraid**. You are worth more than **a whole sky full of sparrows**."*

The Greek is **pollon strouthion diapherete**.

Three times *do not be afraid* in the paragraph.

## What follows immediately

*"Also I say to you, whoever confesses Me before men, him the Son of Man also will confess before
the angels of God."*

And then a man in the crowd interrupting about an inheritance, and the parable of the barns. See
the note on Luke 12:16–21.

And then the ravens and the wildflowers. See the note on Luke 12:22–31.

## The last of the chapter's sparrows

*"**Do not be afraid, little flock. It is your Father's delight to give you the kingdom**."* See the
note on Luke 12:32–34.

## What the value cost

Five sparrows for two coins, and the fifth thrown in.

*Knowing that you were **not redeemed with corruptible things, like silver or gold**… but with **the
precious blood of Christ**, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot.*

*For **you were bought at a price**.*

**Jesus** argues the worth from a market rate. The epistles state what was actually paid, and it is
deliberately not coinage.

*He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all, **how shall He not with Him also
freely give us all things**?*

## Fear, and what displaces it

The paragraph says *do not fear* twice and names one thing worth fearing in between.

*There is **no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear**, because fear involves torment. But he
who fears has not been made perfect in love.*

**John**'s verb is **ballei exo** — throws it out. And the ground given in the sentence before is
*because as He is, so are we in this world.*

*For God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power and of love and of a sound mind.*

*For you did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received **the Spirit of
adoption by whom we cry out, "Abba, Father."***

## The address He opens with

*"**My friends.**"*

**Luke** is the only evangelist who records **Jesus** using it here, and it is the same word He uses
in the upper room: *no longer do I call you servants… but I have called you friends.*

He tells them not to be afraid, and starts by naming what they are to Him.

> **The arithmetic.** Two coins buy five sparrows, which means the fifth is free. And the sentence
> says not one of them is forgotten.
