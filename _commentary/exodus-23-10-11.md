The shortest statement of the sabbath year, and the reason attached to it is about
somebody else's stomach.

## Where it sits

In the middle of the book of the covenant — the block of case law in Exodus 21 to 23,
given straight after the ten commandments.

The laws around it are about oxen, wells, borrowed property, bribes and lawsuits.

## The six

*For six years **sow your land and gather its harvest**.*

## The seventh

*But in the seventh year **let it rest and lie fallow**.*

The Hebrew has two verbs — **shamat**, to release, to let drop, and **natash**, to
abandon, to leave alone.

**Shamat** is the root of **shemittah**, the name the sabbath year still carries. It is
also the word in Deuteronomy 15 for the release of debts in the same year.

Letting the field go and letting the debt go are the same word.

## The reason given

*So **the poor among your people may eat from it**.*

Leviticus gives the theological account — the land keeps a Sabbath to the LORD. See the
note on Leviticus 25:1–7.

Exodus gives the practical one, and it is about the people with no field.

## What comes after them

*And **whatever they leave, the wild animals may have**.*

The order is: the owner does not touch it, the poor eat, and what is left goes to the
animals.

The owner is the only party in the sentence who gets nothing that year.

## The extension

*Do the same with **your vineyard and your olive grove**.*

Vines and olives, not just grain. Both are long-term crops and both need pruning; leaving
them a year costs more than leaving a field.

## What is in the next verse

*Six days you shall do your work, and **on the seventh day you shall rest, that your ox
and your donkey may rest, and the son of your female servant and the stranger may be
refreshed**.*

The same shape at the scale of a week — and again the reason given is other people and
animals.

The land gets one year in seven; the household gets one day in seven.

## What is unusual in the ancient world

Fallowing itself was known — leaving ground to recover is ordinary agriculture. What is
unusual is fixing it to a calendar, releasing the produce to the public, and grounding it
in a command rather than in soil science.

## What happened to it

2 Chronicles counts the exile as the sabbaths the land was owed: *as long as she lay
desolate she kept Sabbath, to fulfil seventy years.*

Seventy years of exile, one for each sabbath year not kept — which puts the neglect at
four hundred and ninety years.

## Where the release goes

Deuteronomy 15, the same seventh year: *every creditor who has lent anything to his
neighbour **shall release it**… **there shall be no poor among you**.*

> **The two clauses.** Let it rest, and the poor eat. Exodus puts the fallow year and the
> food bank in one sentence and does not separate them.
