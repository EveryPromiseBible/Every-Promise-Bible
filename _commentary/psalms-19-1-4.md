The sky is described as talking, and then as having nothing to say it with.

## The verbs

*The heavens are **telling** the glory of God. The skies **pour out** the work of His
hands.*

The Hebrew for *telling* is **saphar** — to count, to recount, to narrate. It is the
same verb God used when He took Abram outside: *count the stars, if you can even
number them.* See the note on Genesis 15:4–5.

Numbering and narrating are the same word.

## Day and night

*Day after day they keep **speaking**. Night after night they keep **teaching**.*

The Hebrew verbs are continuous — pouring forth, and declaring.

Two shifts, and neither one stops. Whatever is being said is being said around the
clock.

## The contradiction

*There is **no speech**, there are **no words**. Their voice is **never heard**.*

Three negatives in a row, immediately after four verbs of speaking.

It says nothing and it will not stop saying it.

## And then

*Yet their sound goes out **through all the earth**, their words **to the end of the
world**.*

No language, and universal reach. The one message on earth that needs no
translation.

## Where Paul puts this verse

Romans 10, in the middle of his argument about preaching:

*But I say, have they not heard? Yes indeed: **"Their sound has gone out to all the
earth, and their words to the ends of the world."***

He quotes this psalm as evidence that the message has been available.

## The tent

*In the heavens **He pitched a tent for the sun**.*

The Hebrew is that He set a tent for it — the sun has an address.

And the verses following describe it *like a bridegroom coming out of his chamber,
and rejoicing like a strong man to run its race.*

## What the psalm does next

Halfway through, without warning, it changes subject entirely:

*The law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul; the testimony of the LORD is
sure, making wise the simple.*

Six statements about Scripture, matching the six about the sky.

Two books, in one psalm — the heavens, and the word — and David sets them side by
side without explaining the join.

## How it ends

*Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in Your
sight, O LORD, my strength and my Redeemer.*

Sky, Scripture, and then a man asking that his own speech be acceptable.

> **What Job is asked about the same sky.** *Can you bind the beautiful cords of the
> Pleiades, or loosen the belt of Orion? Can you **lead out the constellations in
> their seasons**?* God asks about specific star groups by name, and asks whether Job
> can steer them. See the note on Job 38:31–33.
