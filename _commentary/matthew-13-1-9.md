The first parable Matthew records at length, and the one Jesus says the others
depend on.

## Where He sat

*Jesus went out of the house and **sat down by the sea**… He **stepped into a boat**
and sat there, while all the people stood on the shore.*

A boat as a pulpit, and the water carrying the voice up the slope of the shore. It is
an acoustic arrangement as much as a practical one.

Luke records the same use of Simon's boat in chapter 5, and it ends with a net
tearing. See the note on Luke 5:1–3.

## What varies, and what does not

One sower. One kind of seed. Four soils.

Nothing in the parable suggests the seed differed, that some was thrown carelessly,
or that the sower ran out. The variable is entirely the ground.

## The four

- **the path** — the seed never gets in; birds take it
- **rocky ground** — it springs up *quickly* and dies *quickly*, having no root
- **among thorns** — it grows and is choked
- **good soil** — a harvest, *a hundred times what was sown, or sixty, or thirty*

## The second soil is the fast one

*It **sprang up quickly**, because the soil was shallow.*

The shallowness is why it was fast. Rock a few inches down warms early and the shoot
comes up before anything else in the field.

The quickest response in the parable is the one with no root.

## What killed it

*When **the sun climbed high** it was scorched.*

The sun is not the villain — it is the same sun the good soil needed. Heat exposes
what the root system is, and does not create the problem.

## The thorns

They *grew up and choked it out.* Jesus explains them later as *the cares of this
world and the deceitfulness of riches.*

Nothing dramatic. Nothing hostile. The plant is not attacked; it is crowded.

## The three numbers

*A hundred times what was sown, or sixty, or thirty.*

Even the good soil varies, and thirtyfold is still an extraordinary return by ancient
farming standards — sevenfold to tenfold was a decent harvest.

## Why this parable matters most

Mark records what He said about it: *do you not understand this parable? **How then
will you understand all the parables?***

It is the key one.

## The closing line

*"**Whoever has ears, let them listen.**"*

And a few verses on, the instruction about how: *be careful how you listen. The
measure you use will be the measure used for you.* See the note on Mark 4:24–25.

> **What He was doing that day.** Matthew says *that same day* — the day the Pharisees
> had accused Him of casting out demons by Beelzebub, and His mother and brothers came
> asking for Him. He walks out of that house, sits in a boat, and starts talking about
> soil.
