God answers an objection by asking about an object.

## The objection

*"**But what if they don't believe me? What if they won't listen and say, 'The LORD never
appeared to you'?**"*

Moses' third objection. He has already asked *who am I* and *what is Your name*.

The bush is still burning while this conversation happens. See the note on Exodus 3:1–3.

## The question

*"**What is that in your hand?**"*

God does not answer the objection. He asks about equipment.

## The answer

*"A staff."*

The Hebrew is **matteh** — a shepherd's stick.

Forty years of tending Jethro's sheep, and it is the only thing he owns that the story
mentions. A piece of wood, worn to his grip.

## The instruction

*"**Throw it on the ground**."*

One verb. Let go of the only thing you have.

## What it became

*Moses threw it down, and **it became a snake, and he ran from it**.*

The Hebrew is **nachash** — the word in Genesis 3. See the note on Genesis 3:1–5.

He does not stay to watch. The text says he fled.

## The second instruction

*"**Reach out and take it by the tail**."*

Every instinct in a man who handles snakes says the head. Take it by the tail and it can
turn on your arm.

There is no promise attached. He is told to grab the dangerous end of the wrong end.

## What happened

*So he **reached out and grabbed it**, and **in his hand it turned back into a staff**.*

The transformation happens on contact.

## The stated purpose

*"**This is so they may believe**," God said, "that the LORD, **the God of their fathers**
— the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob — has appeared to you."*

The sign is for the audience, not for Moses. His objection was *they will not believe me*,
and the answer is a demonstration they can watch.

## What Egypt made of snakes

The cobra — the uraeus — sat on the front of Pharaoh's crown. It was the symbol of the
sovereignty of Lower Egypt and of the goddess Wadjet.

A Hebrew shepherd is shown holding one by the tail.

## What the staff does after this

It becomes *the rod of God*. It turns the Nile to blood, brings the plagues, splits the sea,
and strikes the rock at Horeb.

Aaron's version swallows the magicians' snakes in Pharaoh's court.

And Moses holds it up on a hill while Joshua fights Amalek, until his arms have to be
propped.

## The one time it goes wrong

At Meribah, told to *speak to the rock*, he strikes it twice with the same rod — and it
costs him the land.

## What is in the hand

The question is asked once and never repeated in Scripture, but the shape of it recurs: a
boy's five loaves and two fish, a widow's handful of meal, a jar with a little oil in it.
See the note on 2 Kings 4:1–7.

Elisha asks the widow the same kind of question — *what do you have in the house?*

> **What was asked.** Not *do you believe*, and not *what do you need*. *What is that in
> your hand* — and the answer was a stick he had been leaning on for forty years.
