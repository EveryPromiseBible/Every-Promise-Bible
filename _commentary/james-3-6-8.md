James has just used two pictures — a bit in a horse's mouth, a rudder on a ship —
about small things steering large ones. Then he stops being gentle.

## The fire

*And the tongue is that fire… touching off the whole running wheel of your life.*

The phrase behind *running wheel* is odd in Greek and has been discussed for
centuries — something like the wheel of existence, the whole course of a life
turning.

The picture is of a spark at the axle. Not damage in one place; damage that
travels with the wheel wherever it goes.

## The claim about taming

*People have broken in every sort of creature there is… but nobody has ever
broken in the tongue.*

James surveys the whole animal kingdom — the four categories from Genesis — and
says humanity has managed all of them and not this.

*It will not be held still.* That is a statement about human capacity, and it is
absolute. Nobody. Ever.

## Why he says something so bleak

Because the chapter opens by warning that not many should become teachers, since
they will be judged more strictly. James is writing to people who talk for a
living, and he is refusing to pretend it can be mastered by effort.

The honest reading is that this is a diagnosis with no cure offered in the
paragraph — which is deliberate. If nobody can tame it, self-improvement is not
the answer.

## Where the letter goes for the answer

*The wisdom that comes down from above is clean, first of all. Then: it makes
peace, it goes easy on people, it will hear you out.*

Eight verses later, and the solution is something that comes **down from above**
rather than something worked up. See the note on James 3:17–18.

> **What Paul says about the same problem.** *If by the Spirit you go on putting
> the body's doings to death, you will live* — continuous, and by the Spirit, not
> by resolve. The two writers agree about the diagnosis and about where the
> remedy comes from. See the note on Romans 8:12–14.
