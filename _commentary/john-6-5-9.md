A question is asked by someone who is not asking for information.

## The look

*When Jesus **looked up and saw the great crowd coming toward Him**, He said to Philip, "**Where are
we going to buy bread for these people to eat?**"*

John alone records the question, and alone records who it was put to.

## Why Philip

He was from Bethsaida, the nearest town. If anyone in the group knew where bread could be bought
in that district, it was him.

The question is aimed at the man best equipped to answer it.

## The narrator's note

*He said it **to test him**, because **He already knew what He was about to do**.*

The Greek is **peirazon** — testing, proving.

John tells the reader the outcome before the scene plays. There is no suspense about whether the
crowd will be fed.

The test is not to find out what Philip will do. It is for Philip.

## Philip's answer

*"**Two hundred silver coins** — the better part of a year's pay — **would not buy enough bread for
every one of them to have a little**."*

The Greek is **denarion diakosion** — two hundred denarii. A denarius was a day's wage for a
labourer.

His arithmetic is correct. He has costed it, and even the sum he names would fall short of *a
little* each.

## What kind of answer it is

Accurate, and entirely about money.

He was asked *where*, and he answered *how much*, which is the natural translation for a
practical man.

## Andrew

*One of His disciples, **Andrew, the brother of Simon Peter**, spoke up.*

John identifies him by his brother every time he appears — the man who brought Peter to Jesus in
chapter 1, and is still described by the relationship.

## What Andrew found

*"**There is a boy here with five barley loaves and two small fish**."*

The Greek is **paidarion** — a little boy. And **opsaria** — small fish, the salted or pickled
sort eaten as a relish with bread.

**Barley** was the poor man's grain, half the price of wheat.

Andrew has been looking. He is the only one who went and found something.

## What he adds

*"**But what is that among so many?**"*

He brings the supply and dismisses it in the same breath.

Philip did the sum and concluded nothing could be done. Andrew found the little that existed and
concluded the same thing.

## Whose lunch it was

A child's, and nobody asked him for it before Andrew found him.

The boy is not mentioned again.

## What Jesus does with it

*Then Jesus took the loaves, and when He had given thanks He distributed them to the disciples,
and the disciples to those sitting down.*

Luke's account has the imperfect: *He kept handing the pieces to the disciples*. See the note on
Luke 9:10–17.

## The leftovers

*"**Gather up the fragments that remain, so that nothing is lost**."*

Twelve baskets, from five loaves, gathered on the instruction of a man who had just made bread
out of nothing.

## Where the chapter goes

The crowd tries to make Him king by force, He withdraws to a mountain alone, and the next day
they find Him and He tells them *you seek Me not because you saw the signs, but because you ate
of the loaves and were filled*.

Then the bread discourse, and the departure. See the note on John 6:66–71.

The chapter that begins with a boy's lunch ends with almost everybody gone.

> **The note John inserts.** *He already knew what He was about to do.* The question was never
> about locating bread — it was put to Philip for Philip.
