Four verbs on the bread, and then a sentence about the cup that names a covenant.

## The four things He does

*He **picked up** the bread, and **gave thanks** over it, and **broke** it, and **handed
it** to them.*

Took, blessed, broke, gave. Luke uses the same four in the feeding of the five
thousand and again at Emmaus, where the two disciples recognise Him at the exact
moment He does them.

## The thanks

The Greek is **eucharisteo** — where *Eucharist* comes from.

He gives thanks holding bread He has just said is His body, on the night He is
betrayed.

## "Given for you"

*"This is My body," He said, "**given for you**."*

Not taken from Him. **Given.**

He says the same about His life in John: *no one takes it from Me. I lay it down of My
own accord.* See the note on John 10:16–18.

## "Do this to remember Me"

The Greek for *remembrance* is **anamnesis**. Luke and Paul both record the
instruction; Matthew and Mark do not.

It is the only ongoing practice He tells them to keep by saying *do this*.

## The timing of the cup

*And in the same way, **after the supper**, He took the cup.*

Luke marks it. The bread was in the meal; the cup came after it.

At a Passover seder four cups are drunk. The one after the meal is the third — the cup
of redemption.

## The covenant

*"This cup is **the new covenant in My blood**," He said, "**poured out for you**."*

The phrase *new covenant* comes from Jeremiah 31, written six centuries earlier, where
it ends: *I will forgive their iniquity, and **their sin I will remember no more**.*

See the note on Hebrews 8:10–12.

## The two rememberings

He says *remember Me*. The covenant He is inaugurating says *I will remember their
sins no more*.

The same verb, running in opposite directions, in the same cup.

## The old covenant's ratification

Moses took blood, threw it on the people and said *this is the blood of the covenant
which the LORD has made with you*. Exodus 24, on the mountain, with the elders eating
and drinking in God's presence.

This is the same form of words, at a table, with the blood not yet shed.

> **Who was still at the table.** Luke puts the next sentence like this: *but behold,
> the hand of My betrayer is with Me on the table.* The order in Luke's account has
> Judas present for the bread and the cup.
