The one day He accepts the acclamation instead of stopping it.

## The road

*As He rode along, people kept **spreading their cloaks out on the road** in front of Him.*

The Greek is an imperfect — they kept doing it.

Cloaks on the road is a coronation gesture. When Jehu was declared king, *each man took his
garment and put it under him on the top of the steps, and blew trumpets, saying, "Jehu is
king!"*

## The place

*When He came to the place where **the road starts down from the Mount of Olives**.*

The point where the path comes over the ridge and the whole temple platform appears below,
across the Kidron valley.

Luke marks the spot exactly, because of what happens at it a few verses later.

## The shout

*The whole crowd of His disciples **broke out in joy, praising God at the top of their
voices**.*

The Greek is *rejoicing to praise God with a loud voice*.

## What they were praising Him for

*For **all the wonderful things they had seen Him do**.*

The Greek is **dunameon** — acts of power.

Luke gives the reason, and it is a list of things already witnessed. Bartimaeus is a few
verses back, and Lazarus is in John's account of the same week.

## The words

*"**Blessed is the King who comes in the name of the Lord!**"*

Psalm 118, and every pilgrim knew it — it was sung going up to the feasts.

But the psalm says *blessed is he who comes*. Luke's crowd adds a word: **the King**.

## The second line

*"**Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest!**"*

An echo of what the angels said over Bethlehem — *glory to God in the highest, and on earth
peace*.

The angels said peace on earth. The crowd says peace in heaven.

Luke sets the two sentences at the two ends of the Gospel, and they do not match.

## The objection

*Some of the Pharisees in the crowd said to Him, "**Teacher, tell Your disciples to stop**."*

The Greek is **epitimeson** — rebuke them.

They are asking Him to do what He has done all through Luke: silence people who say who He
is. He has told demons to be quiet, and healed men not to tell anyone, and the disciples not
to say He was the Christ.

## The refusal

*"I tell you, **if these people were silent, the very stones would start shouting**."*

The Greek is **kraxousin** — cry out, scream.

The one day He will not have it stopped.

Habakkuk has the image: *the stone will cry out from the wall, and the beam from the timbers
will answer it* — said of a house built by injustice.

## The date

The tenth of Nisan. The day the Passover lamb was selected and brought into the house. See
the note on Joshua 4:19–24.

Four days later He is killed, and in between the authorities examine Him in public and find
nothing.

## What He does at that exact spot

*Now as He drew near, **He saw the city and wept over it**.*

Two verses later, at the place where the road comes over the ridge. See the note on
Luke 19:41–44.

The crowd is still shouting while He is crying.

## The word He uses there

*"If you had known, even you, especially in this your day, **the things that make for your
peace**."*

Peace, for the third time in six verses. The disciples shout it, the crowd's psalm implies
it, and He says the city cannot see it.

## What He does next

Goes into the temple and drives out the traders: *My house is a house of prayer, but you have
made it a den of thieves.*

The triumphal entry ends at an overturned table.

> **The one thing He would not silence.** He had spent the whole Gospel telling people to say
> nothing. Asked to stop it here, He says the stones would take over.
