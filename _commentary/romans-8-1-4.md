The sentence that follows the worst chapter in Paul's letters.

## What comes immediately before

Romans 7 ends *O wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death?*
— and then *I thank God — through Jesus Christ our Lord!*

Chapter 8 opens on the next line.

## The word

*So then, **there is no condemnation now — none at all** — for those who are in Christ
Jesus.*

The Greek is **katakrima** — the sentence passed after a verdict. Not the accusation and
not the trial. The punishment handed down.

## "Now"

The Greek is **nun** — at this present moment.

Not eventually, and not at the judgment. Now, while the struggle described in chapter 7
is still going on.

## Who it is for

*For those who are **in Christ Jesus**.*

The location is the qualification. Paul's whole letter turns on the phrase, and it does
not describe a standard of behaviour.

## The two laws

*For in Christ Jesus **another law is at work** — **the law of the Spirit, the law of
life** — and that law has **set me free from the law of sin and death**.*

Not the law repealed. A second one operating, and the stronger of the two winning.

The Greek for *set free* is aorist — it happened.

## What the law could not do

*What **the law could not accomplish**, **weakened as it was by the old nature**.*

Paul locates the failure precisely, and it is not in the law. He has already said *the
law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good.*

The weakness is in the material it was working on.

## What God did instead

*God accomplished Himself: **He sent His own Son in the likeness of that sin-claimed
humanity**.*

The Greek is *in the likeness of sinful flesh* — **homoioma**. Paul chooses the word
carefully: real humanity, and not sinful.

## The sentencing

*And there, **dealing with sin, He passed sentence on sin right where sin had made its
home**.*

The Greek is *He condemned sin in the flesh* — the same root as the **condemnation** in
verse 1.

The condemnation that is not on the believer went somewhere. Paul says where.

## The purpose

*He did this so that **everything the law rightly asks might be fully met in us**.*

The Greek is *the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled **in** us* —
passive. Not *by* us.

## The walk

*Who **walk not according to the old nature but according to the Spirit**.*

It appears twice, at the end of verse 1 and again at the end of verse 4, framing the
paragraph.

## Where the chapter ends

*Neither death nor life, neither angels nor rulers nor powers… has the strength to cut us
off from the love of God.* See the note on Romans 8:38–39.

No condemnation at the top, no separation at the bottom, and the whole chapter in
between.

> **What has changed between the chapters.** Nothing about the man. Chapter 7's *what I
> hate, that I do* is not resolved by improvement — Paul answers it with a location,
> and then says there is no sentence there.
