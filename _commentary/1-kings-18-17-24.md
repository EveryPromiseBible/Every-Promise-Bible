Three and a half years of drought end with a confrontation, and it opens with an
accusation and a correction.

## Who troubled Israel

*"So it's you — the man who's brought all this trouble on Israel."*

*"I haven't troubled Israel. You have."*

Ahab blames the prophet who announced the drought. Elijah puts it back on the man
whose house abandoned the commands and went after the Baals.

The word Ahab uses is a strong one — the same root used of Achan, whose theft
brought disaster on the whole camp. Elijah does not soften it; he redirects it.

## The question to the people

*How long will you go limping between two opinions?*

The Hebrew verb is the one used a few verses later of the prophets of Baal
**dancing** around their altar. Israel is doing the same hop from foot to foot,
and the word is chosen deliberately.

They had not rejected the LORD outright. They were keeping both, and Elijah's
charge is that this is not a position at all — it is a limp.

*The people said nothing — not a word.* Nobody answers him.

## The terms of the test

Two bulls, two altars, no fire lit by anyone. *The God who answers by fire — He
is God.*

It is a fair test and the crowd says so. Baal was the god of storm and lightning;
fire from the sky was precisely his advertised speciality. Elijah lets them
choose their bull first and gives them the whole morning.

> **"I am the only prophet of the LORD left."** It is how he sees it, and it is
> not quite true — Obadiah has a hundred hidden in caves, and the same chapter has
> just said so. God corrects the same thought a chapter later with the seven
> thousand who never bowed. Elijah's courage is real, and so is his sense of
> being alone in it.
