The upper-room discourse starts the moment the door closes behind Judas.

## The timing

*When he had gone out.*

Judas has just left with the piece of bread. See the note on John 13:21–26.

John has already told us it was night. Everything Jesus says from here to chapter 17 is said
to eleven men.

## The word

*"**Now the Son of Man is glorified**."*

The Greek is **edoxasthe** — an aorist. It has happened.

Nothing has happened yet, in the visible sense. A man has walked out of a room.

The arrest, the trial and the cross are all still ahead, and He speaks of the glorifying in
the past tense.

## What glory means in this Gospel

John uses the word of the cross throughout. *The hour has come that the Son of Man should be
glorified* — said when the Greeks asked to see Him, and immediately followed by *unless a
grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies*. See the note on John 12:23–26.

The lifting up He keeps referring to is a lifting onto a beam.

## The five glories

*"Now the Son of Man **is glorified**, and God **is glorified** in Him. If God **is
glorified** in Him, God will also **glorify** Him in Himself, and He will **glorify** Him at
once."*

Five times in two sentences, moving back and forth between the two of them.

## The address

*"**My children**."*

The Greek is **teknia** — little children. A diminutive, and the only time Jesus uses it in
the Gospels.

John picks it up and uses it seven times in his letter. It stayed with him.

## The notice

*"I am with you **only a little longer**."*

## The exclusion

*"You will look for Me, and **what I said to the Jewish leaders I now say to you: where I am
going, you cannot come**."*

He had said it to His opponents in chapter 7 and again in chapter 8 — and it landed as a
threat there.

Here it is said to His friends, and it is a statement of timing rather than of judgment. Two
chapters later: *I go to prepare a place for you… that where I am, there you may be also*.

Peter asks immediately: *Lord, where are You going?* And gets the prediction about the
rooster.

## The command

*"I am giving you **a new command: love one another**."*

The Greek is **entolen kainen** — a new commandment.

The command itself is not new. Leviticus has *you shall love your neighbour as yourself*, and
Jesus has already named it as the second great commandment.

## What is new

*"**As I have loved you**, so you are to love one another."*

The standard is new. Not *as yourself* — *as I have loved you*.

And He has just washed their feet, including the feet of the man who left. The measure was
demonstrated an hour before it was commanded.

## The badge

*"**This is how everyone will know that you are My disciples** — if you have love for one
another."*

The Greek is *by this all will know*.

Not doctrine, not miracles, not conduct in general. One identifying mark, and it is
horizontal.

## What Tertullian recorded

Writing around AD 200, he quotes what outsiders said about Christians: *see how they love one
another*.

## What John does with it later

*A new commandment I write to you… **because the darkness is passing away, and the true light
is already shining**.*

And *he who says he is in the light, and hates his brother, is in darkness until now*.

And *by this we know love, because He laid down His life for us*. See the note on
1 John 4:10.

The old man is still working on the sentence he heard that night.

> **What was new about it.** Not the loving. *As I have loved you* — and He had washed
> twenty-four feet, including two that were about to walk out and sell Him.
