Paul announces peace twice, to two groups who had nothing in common.

## Both

*He came and announced the good news of peace to **you who were far away**, and peace
**to those who were near**.*

Far away is the Gentiles. Near is Israel — who had the covenants, the law, the
promises and the temple.

The same message is preached to both, and the same peace is announced. Neither group
gets a different arrangement.

Paul is quoting Isaiah 57: *peace, peace to him who is far off and to him who is
near.*

## The access

*For through Him **we both have our way in to the Father, by one Spirit**.*

The Greek for *way in* is **prosagoge** — access, introduction, the word for being
presented at court by somebody with standing.

One access, one Spirit, one Father, and *we both*.

## The three denials

*So now you are **no longer strangers**, **no longer guests in someone else's
country**.*

**Xenoi** — foreigners, outsiders.

**Paroikoi** — resident aliens. People living somewhere legally and permanently who
will never be citizens of it.

That was the exact status of a God-fearing Gentile in relation to Israel: welcome as
far as the outer court, and no further.

## The two affirmations

*You hold **the same citizenship as all God's holy people**, and **you belong to the
household of God**.*

Citizenship, and family. The first is a legal standing; the second is not.

You can be naturalised into a country. Nobody is naturalised into a household.

## What the wall was

Earlier in the chapter: *He… has broken down the middle wall of separation.*

In the temple there was a literal one — a barrier in the outer court with notices in
Greek and Latin warning that any foreigner passing it was responsible for his own
death. Two of those inscriptions have been dug up.

Paul, who had been accused of taking a Gentile past it and was in prison partly
because of the riot that followed, writes that it has been demolished.

## Where the paragraph goes

The very next verses:

*Built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, **Jesus Christ Himself being
the chief cornerstone**, in whom the whole building, being fitted together, grows
into a **holy temple** in the Lord, in whom you also are **being built together for a
dwelling place of God** in the Spirit.*

Citizens, then family, then masonry.

And the stones in Solomon's temple were cut and finished in the quarry so that the
site was silent. See the notes on 1 Kings 6:6–8 and 1 Peter 2:4–5.

> **What they had been eleven verses earlier.** *Without Christ, aliens from the
> commonwealth of Israel, strangers from the covenants of promise, **having no hope
> and without God in the world**.* Five negatives in one sentence, and then *but now
> in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of
> Christ.*
