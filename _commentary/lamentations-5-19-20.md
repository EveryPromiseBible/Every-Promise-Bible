key: Lamentations 5:19–20
title: "But You, LORD, reign forever"
updated: 2026-08-09

Two lines that put the throne and the complaint in the same breath.

## The turn

*But You, LORD, **reign forever**. Your throne endures **from age to age**.*

The Hebrew is **attah YHWH le'olam teshev** — You, LORD, sit forever. **Kisakha ledor vador** —
Your throne to generation and generation.

The whole fifth poem before this is an inventory of what has gone: the inheritance to strangers,
water bought, firewood bought, women violated, young men at the grinding stones, the old men gone
from the gate, the crown fallen from the head.

And then one thing that has not moved.

## The complaint immediately after

*Why do You **forget us so long**? Why **forsake us** for so many days?*

The poet does not use the throne to close the question. He uses it to ask one.

*"**My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?**"*

**Matthew** and **Mark** both record it, and it is a quotation of a psalm that also has a throne
in it — *You are holy, enthroned in the praises of Israel.* The complaint and the throne together
is the shape of the Old Testament's honest prayer, and it is the shape of the loudest sentence
from the cross.

## The throne that did not move

*Immediately I was in the Spirit; and behold, **a throne set in heaven, and One sat on the
throne**.*

**Revelation** 4, written to churches under pressure, and the first thing **John** is shown is
furniture.

*But **this Man, after He had offered one sacrifice for sins forever, sat down** at the right hand
of God.*

**Hebrews** 10, and the sitting is the argument: every priest before Him stood, because the work
was never finished.

## And the throne you can approach

*Let us therefore **come boldly to the throne of grace**, that we may obtain mercy and find grace
to help in time of need.*

Lamentations has a throne that endures and a people who cannot get near it. **Hebrews** 4 gives
the same throne a second noun and an instruction — and the reason given, in the verse before, is
that the one on it *was in all points tempted as we are*.

## What comes next

*Restore us to Yourself, LORD, that we may return.* See the note on Lamentations 5:21–22.

> **The two sentences together.** *Your throne endures from age to age* and *why do You forget us
> so long?* The poet does not use the first to silence the second — and the New Testament gives
> the throne a name it did not have here: the throne of grace.
