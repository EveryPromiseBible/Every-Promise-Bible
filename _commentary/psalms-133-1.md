Three verses in the whole psalm, and this is the first. The other two are the
pictures.

## The two words

*Look how **good**, look how **pleasant**.*

Both, and they are not the same thing. Some good things are not pleasant and some
pleasant things are not good. The psalm says unity is both at once.

## "Dwell together"

The Hebrew is about settling, staying, living in one place — not visiting. It is
the word used of a family sharing an inheritance rather than dividing it and
moving apart.

## What it is compared to

The psalm goes on to two images, and both are about something running downward:

- **oil poured on Aaron's head**, running down his beard, down onto the collar of
  his robes. The anointing oil of a priest, and the psalm follows it all the way
  down.
- **the dew of Hermon** falling on the mountains of Zion. Hermon is the great
  snow peak in the far north; Zion is two hundred kilometres south. The image is
  of moisture from the highest place arriving somewhere else entirely.

Both are liquid, both come from above, and both go down.

## Where the psalm ends

*For there the LORD commanded the blessing — life for evermore.*

*There* — in the place where brothers dwell together. The blessing is located
rather than merely described.

## Where the oil goes

*It is like **the precious oil upon the head**, running down on the beard, the beard of Aaron,
running down on the edge of his garments.*

Poured in one place only — the head — and everything else that gets any is downstream of that.
Nobody's beard is anointed directly. See the note on Exodus 30:22–33.

*But **you have an anointing from the Holy One**… the anointing which you have received from
Him abides in you.*

**John** writes it to ordinary people, and the reason they have it is that it was poured on the
Head and ran down.

*And He is **the head of the body, the church**.*

## What the unity is not

Agreement, or effort. The psalm gives two pictures — oil running down and dew falling on
mountains — and both are things that arrive from above and travel by gravity.

*Endeavouring to **keep the unity of the Spirit** in the bond of peace.*

**Paul**'s verb is keep, not make. There is nothing in his sentence about constructing it.

## What is commanded there

*For there the LORD **commanded the blessing** — life forevermore.*

The last line, and the blessing is not the unity's reward. It is commanded at the place where
brothers are together.

> **What kind of psalm it is.** One of the Songs of Ascents, sung by pilgrims
> walking up to Jerusalem for the festivals — people from scattered towns
> arriving in one place three times a year. The unity being celebrated is not
> theoretical. It is a road full of families converging.
