The one thing the disciples ask to be taught, and they ask after watching Him do it.

## The request

*"He was praying in a certain place, and **when He had finished**, one of His disciples said to Him,
'**Lord, teach us to pray.**'"*

They do not interrupt. They wait, and then ask.

**Luke** never records them asking to be taught to preach, to heal, or to cast out demons — and they
did all three. This is the only curriculum they request.

## The first word

*"**Father.**"*

**Pater.** **Luke**'s version is shorter than **Matthew**'s and begins with the same noun.

Jewish prayer addressed God in many ways and rarely like this. **Mark** preserves the Aramaic **Jesus**
Himself used in Gethsemane — **Abba** — and **Paul** twice reports it as the word the Spirit produces
in Christians.

*For you did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption
by whom we cry out, "**Abba, Father**."*

*And because you are sons, **God has sent forth the Spirit of His Son into your hearts, crying out,
"Abba, Father!"***

The address is not a technique. **Galatians** says it is evidence of a relationship somebody else
established.

## The daily bread

*"**Give us day by day our daily bread.**"*

**Epiousion** — a word that occurs almost nowhere else in Greek, usually taken as *for the coming
day*. One day's ration, which is the manna arrangement.

*"**I am the bread of life.**"*

## The forgiveness

*"**And forgive us our sins, for we ourselves also forgive everyone who is indebted to us.**"*

**Luke** has *sins* in the first clause and *indebted* in the second, which keeps the two categories
visible: what we need forgiven is sin, and what we forgive is debt.

The **for** is not a rate of exchange. The parable of the unforgiving servant settles the order —
ten thousand talents cancelled first, a hundred denarii outstanding second.

*"**Should you not also have had compassion on your fellow servant, just as I had pity on you?**"*

## What follows immediately

The friend at midnight, and then *ask, and it will be given you*. See the note on
Luke 11:5–8.

The prayer is taught and then defended, and the defence ends with the Father giving the Holy Spirit
to those who ask.

> **The one thing they asked to be taught.** Not preaching or healing. And the first word of the
> answer is a noun that Paul twice says the Spirit puts in a Christian's mouth.
