Peter goes up to pray and comes down with the whole Gentile mission.

## The timing

*The next day, **as they were on their way and coming near the city**.*

The three men from Caesarea are a mile off. Luke synchronises the two scenes so the reader
can see the arrangement.

## The place

*Peter went up on **the flat roof to pray**. It was about **noon**.*

Roofs were living space. Noon was one of the three Jewish prayer hours.

## The hunger

*He **grew hungry and wanted something to eat**, and **while the meal was being prepared** he
fell into a trance.*

Luke gives the physical detail. The vision arrives in a hungry man waiting for lunch, and
it is about food.

## The sheet

*Something like **a great sheet coming down, let down to the earth by its four corners**.*

Four corners — the four quarters of the earth.

## The contents

*All kinds of **four-footed animals of the earth, and wild beasts, and creeping things, and
birds of the air**.*

The categories of Leviticus 11, clean and unclean together.

## The command

*"**Get up, Peter. Kill and eat**."*

## The refusal

*"**Never, Lord. I have never eaten anything unholy or unclean**."*

The Greek is **medamos** — by no means.

He addresses Him as Lord and refuses in the same breath. He had done it before — *depart
from me, for I am a sinful man*, and *You shall never wash my feet*.

His record is clean and he says so. Peter is not being disobedient; he is being observant.

## The correction

*"**What God has cleansed, you must not call unholy**."*

The Greek is **ekatharisen** — has purified.

The issue is not what the animals are. It is who has ruled on them.

## Three times

*This happened **three times**, and then the sheet was taken up again into heaven.*

The man who denied three times and was asked three times on the beach.

## What it is not about

Food. Peter says so himself when he gets to Caesarea: *God has shown me that **I should call
no one unholy or unclean**.* See the note on Acts 10:24–29.

The sheet had animals in it and the application is people. Peter works that out on the
journey.

## What he does with it

*While Peter was **still turning the vision over in his mind**.* See the note on
Acts 10:17–20.

He does not understand it immediately. The interpretation arrives in the form of three men
at the gate.

## Where the argument goes

Peter tells the story twice more — to the church in Jerusalem when he is put on trial for
eating with Gentiles, and again in substance at the council. See the notes on
Acts 11:4–14 and Acts 15:6–9.

Luke tells the Cornelius episode three times in his book. Nothing else gets that.

## What Mark's Gospel says

*Thus He declared all foods clean.*

Mark adds that clause himself, in a parenthesis, in the passage about what defiles a man —
and Mark is traditionally held to have written down Peter's preaching.

Peter had been in the room when it was said, and had not drawn the conclusion.

> **What is corrected.** Not his obedience. Peter's answer is accurate about the law and
> accurate about his own record — and what he is told is that a ruling has been made
> somewhere else.
