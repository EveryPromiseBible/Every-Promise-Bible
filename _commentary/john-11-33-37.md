The shortest verse in the Bible, and the sentence before it is the one that
carries the weight.

## "Something moved deep in His spirit, and He was shaken"

Two Greek verbs, and neither is gentle.

The first, **embrimaomai**, is used in classical Greek of a horse snorting. Where
it appears elsewhere in the Gospels it describes a stern, forceful reaction. It
is not sorrow — it is closer to indignation.

The second, **tarasso**, means to stir up, agitate, throw into turmoil. It is
what John uses of water being churned, and of Jesus' own soul later in the book.

So before the tears there is anger, and it is anger at something. He is standing
in front of death with the man's sisters weeping beside Him, and He is about to
undo it in ten minutes' time. The reaction is not grief at a loss He cannot fix.
It is fury at the thing itself.

## Then the tears

*Jesus wept.* The verb is different from the one used of the crowd — theirs is
loud, ritual wailing; His is quiet weeping.

He cries at a funeral He is about to interrupt. Nothing about the outcome
required it. The tears are simply what He does in the presence of what death does
to people He loves.

> **The two reactions in the crowd.** *Look how He loved him* — and, from others,
> *could He not have kept this man from dying?* John reports both without picking
> one. The second is the sharper question and it is left standing in the text, as
> it usually is in life. The answer, when it comes four verses later, is not an
> explanation. It is Lazarus walking out.
