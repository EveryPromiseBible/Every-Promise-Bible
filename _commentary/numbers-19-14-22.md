The procedure for a person who has touched a corpse, and it is the only remedy in the law for it.

## The case

*"When someone **dies in a tent**, all who are inside and **every open vessel** become unclean for
seven days."*

Everybody present, and every container without a lid.

## The other cases

*"Whoever touches **a body in the open field**, or **a bone**, or **a grave**, is unclean."*

Four situations, and one of them is unavoidable in a wilderness where people die.

## Why it matters

Corpse-uncleanness was the most serious in the law. Seven days, not one, and the person could not
approach the sanctuary during it.

## The remedy

*"To cleanse them, take some of **the ashes of the burned heifer** and add **fresh, living water** in a
vessel."*

The Hebrew is **mayim chayyim** — living water. Running water, from a spring or stream, not
standing.

## The heifer

A red heifer without blemish, never yoked, taken outside the camp and burned entirely — hide,
flesh, blood and dung — with cedar, hyssop and scarlet thrown into the fire. See the note on
Numbers 19:1–10.

The ashes were kept.

## The paradox

Everyone involved in preparing the ashes becomes unclean by doing it.

The priest, the man who burns it, and the man who gathers the ashes all wash and remain unclean
until evening.

The thing that cleanses defiles everybody who handles it.

## The application

*"**A clean person dips hyssop in it** and sprinkles the tent, the vessels, and the people who were
there."*

**Ezov** — hyssop. The same plant used on the doorposts at Passover, and offered to Jesus at the
cross.

## The two days

*"And sprinkles the one who touched death, **on the third day and the seventh**."*

Two applications, four days apart. Not one.

## The completion

*"On the seventh day **he washes his clothes and bathes**, and by evening he is clean."*

## The penalty

*"But anyone who has become unclean and **does not purify himself must be cut off** from the
assembly, for he has defiled the LORD's sanctuary."*

The provision exists. Refusing it is the offence.

## The summary

*"From **ashes and running water**, from **a single sacrifice made once outside the camp**, God gave His
people **a way back**."*

## The economy of it

One heifer. Its ashes stored and mixed with water as needed, for the whole nation, over
generations.

The sacrifice happens once; the application is repeated.

## What Hebrews does with it

*For if the blood of bulls and goats, and **the ashes of a heifer** sprinkling the unclean, sanctifies
for the purifying of the flesh, **how much more shall the blood of Christ**… cleanse your conscience
from dead works?* See the note on Hebrews 9:13–14.

**Dead works** — and the ceremony being compared is the one for touching the dead.

## Where the sacrifice happened

*Outside the camp.*

*Therefore Jesus also, that He might sanctify the people with His own blood, **suffered outside the
gate**.* See the note on Hebrews 13:11–13.

## What the last clause names

*A way for **the one who has stood near death to return to the living**, clean and welcomed home.*

> **The paradox.** Everybody who prepares the ashes becomes unclean by preparing them. The thing
> that cleanses defiles whoever handles it.
