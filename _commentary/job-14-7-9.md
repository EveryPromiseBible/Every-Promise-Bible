Job compares himself unfavourably to a stump.

## The setting

He is answering Zophar, and the chapter opens with the shortest summary of a human life in the
book:

*Man who is born of woman is of few days and full of trouble. He comes forth like a flower and
fades away; he flees like a shadow and does not continue.*

## The claim

*"For **there is hope for a tree**."*

The Hebrew is **ki yesh la'etz tiqvah**.

**Tiqvah** — hope, and literally a cord or a line. The word Rahab's scarlet thread is called.

## The first stage

*"If it is cut down, **it will sprout again**. Its new shoots will not fail."*

The Hebrew is **yachaliph** — will change, will renew.

Olive, oak and terebinth all coppice: cut them and the stump throws up new growth.

## The second

*"Even if its **roots grow old in the ground** and **its stump dies in the soil**."*

Both parts named as dead. Not dormant — old, and dying.

## The turn

*"**At the scent of water** it will bud and put out branches like a young plant."*

The Hebrew is **mereach mayim** — from the smell of water.

**Reach** — scent, fragrance. The word used of the sacrifices: *a sweet aroma to the LORD*.

## What the image says

Not that water is applied.

The smell of it is enough. Something in the dead stump registers the presence of water before the
water arrives.

## The comparison

*"But man dies and is laid away; indeed he breathes his last and where is he?"*

*"As water disappears from the sea, and a river becomes parched and dries up, so man lies down and
does not rise."*

Job's point is that a tree has something a man does not.

## What he asks in the same chapter

*"**If a man dies, shall he live again?** All the days of my hard service I will wait, till my change
comes."*

The Hebrew is **halipati** — my change, and it is the same root as the tree's *sprouting again* in
verse 7.

He uses the tree's word of himself, as a question.

## What he says six chapters earlier

*"For **I know that my Redeemer lives**, and He shall stand at last on the earth… and **after my skin
is destroyed, this I know, that in my flesh I shall see God**."* See the note on
Job 19:25–27.

## Where the stump image goes

*There shall come forth **a Rod from the stem of Jesse**, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots.*
See the note on Isaiah 11:1–3.

The Hebrew is **geza** — a stump.

Written when David's line had been reduced to one, and using the same picture Job used of a
hopeless case.

## What the New Testament says about the question

*"**If a man dies, shall he live again?**"*

*Now Christ is risen from the dead, and has become the firstfruits of those who have fallen
asleep.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 15:20–22.

## Where Job ends up

*"I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, **but now my eye sees You**."* See the note on
Job 42:1–6.

And four generations of descendants, and an end richer than the beginning. See the note on
Job 42:16–17.

> **The image.** Not water applied — *the scent of water*. Something in a stump that is described
> as dead registers it before it arrives.
