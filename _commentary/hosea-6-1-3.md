Three verses that sound like a revival hymn, and the chapter turns on them a line later.

## The call

*Come, **let us return to the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **shuv** — turn back. The word that runs through the whole book, and gives
Israel's repentance and God's turning the same verb.

## The diagnosis

*He has **torn** us, and He will **heal** us. He has **struck us down**, and He will **bind
up** our wounds.*

The Hebrew for *torn* is **taraph** — what a lion does to prey. Hosea has just used it of God
in the previous chapter: *I will be like a lion to Ephraim… I will tear them and go away*.

They quote His own image back at Him.

## The two days

*After **two days He will revive us**.*

The Hebrew is **yechayyenu** — He will make us live.

## The third

*On the **third day He will raise us up**, and we will live before Him.*

The Hebrew is **yeqimenu** — He will raise us up, cause us to stand.

Two days, then the third. Hosea uses the pattern to mean *shortly* — a Hebrew idiom for
something imminent, the way *once or twice* means *a few times*.

## What the New Testament does with it

*He was buried, and **He rose again the third day according to the Scriptures**.*

Paul writes *according to the Scriptures* without naming which. Jonah's fish is one candidate;
this is the other and the language matches more closely.

Nothing in Hosea's context is about a Messiah. The sentence is about a nation.

## The resolve

*Let us know Him. **Let us press on to know the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **nirdephah** — let us pursue, chase. The verb for hunting, or for an army in
pursuit.

The same intensity as Proverbs' *if you dig for her like buried treasure*. See the note on
Proverbs 2:1–5.

## The certainty

*As surely as **the sun rises**, He will come to us.*

The Hebrew is *His going forth is established as the morning*.

## The rains

*Like the **winter rains**, like the **spring rains that water the earth**.*

The Hebrew names both: the **yoreh**, the early rain that softens the ground for ploughing in
October, and the **malqosh**, the latter rain in March and April that swells the grain before
harvest.

James uses the same pair: *the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, waiting
patiently for it until it receives the early and latter rain*.

## What God says to it

The next verse: *"O Ephraim, what shall I do to you? O Judah, what shall I do to you? For
**your faithfulness is like a morning cloud, and like the early dew it goes away**."*

The people compare His coming to the reliable rain. He compares their loyalty to the dew that
is gone by nine o'clock.

The beautiful poem in verses 1 to 3 is answered by a complaint that it does not last.

## The line He quotes twice

*"For **I desire mercy and not sacrifice**, and the knowledge of God more than burnt
offerings."*

Six verses later. Jesus quotes it twice in Matthew — once over dinner with tax collectors,
and once over grain picked on a Sabbath.

Both times, to people who were objecting to something.

## What the book is

Hosea is told to marry a woman who will be unfaithful, and does. The marriage is the sermon.

And the last chapter opens with the same word as this one: *O Israel, return to the LORD your
God*.

> **What is said and what is answered.** The people's poem is three of the best verses in the
> prophets. God's reply is that their loyalty burns off like dew.
