The last two verses of the longest chapter in Genesis.

## The chapter

Sixty-seven verses, all about one errand: a servant sent four hundred miles to find a wife
for Isaac.

Genesis gives more space to this than to the creation. And it tells the story twice — once
as it happens, and once as the servant repeats it to Rebekah's family.

## The report

*Then the servant **told Isaac everything he had done**.*

The servant is never named in the chapter. He is *the eldest servant of his house, who
ruled over all that he had*, and every step of it is his.

He had prayed at the well for a specific sign — a woman who would offer to water ten
camels — and *before he had finished speaking*, Rebekah came out.

## What Isaac was doing

Three verses earlier: *and Isaac **went out to meditate in the field in the evening**; and he
lifted his eyes and looked, and there, the camels were coming.*

The Hebrew for *meditate* is a rare word and translations divide over it.

## The tent

*And Isaac **brought her into the tent of his mother Sarah**.*

Sarah had died in chapter 23 at a hundred and twenty-seven. Abraham bought the cave of
Machpelah to bury her — the only piece of Canaan the family ever owned.

The tent has been standing empty.

## The order

*And **he married Rebekah. She became his wife, and he loved her**.*

The Hebrew runs: he took her, she became his wife, and he loved her.

Love is third in the sentence. It is the second occurrence of **ahav** in Genesis; the
first is Abraham's for Isaac on the mountain. See the note on Genesis 22:1–2.

## The last clause

*And Isaac **was comforted after the loss of his mother**.*

The Hebrew is **vayinnachem** — was comforted, consoled.

Three years have passed since chapter 23. Genesis says nothing about the intervening
grief and closes the chapter by saying it ended.

## Isaac in this chapter

He does almost nothing. He is not consulted, does not travel, and does not choose.

A father sends, a servant prays, a woman agrees to go, and the son walks out into a field
in the evening and looks up.

## Rebekah's decision

*And they called Rebekah and said to her, "**Will you go with this man?**" And she said,
"**I will go**."*

Two words in the Hebrew. She is asked directly and answers for herself.

## Where the chapter began

*The LORD had blessed Abraham in all things.* And the oath: *put your hand under my thigh…
that you will not take a wife for my son from the daughters of the Canaanites.*

The same gesture Jacob later asks of Joseph about his burial. See the note on
Genesis 47:27–31.

## What the servant says he was given

*"**I being on the way, the LORD led me** to the house of my master's brethren."*

He says it twice, and bows and worships in the middle of a stranger's courtyard.

## How the chapter closes the picture

The Father sent. The servant went, never named, speaking only of the son and spending only
the father's wealth. The bride was asked one question and said *I will go*. And the son came
out into the field at evening and lifted his eyes, and the camels were coming. See the note
on Genesis 24:1–4.

She had never seen him. She travelled four hundred miles on the strength of what the servant
told her about him, and everything she was wearing had come out of his father's house.

*Whom having not seen you love. Though now you do not see Him, yet believing, **you rejoice
with joy inexpressible and full of glory**.* See the note on 1 Peter 1:8–9.

## The last thing said about the son

*And he loved her.*

Isaac does nothing in this chapter except go out to meet her and love her. He is not
consulted about the choice, and there is no test set for her when she arrives. She is brought
in, and she is loved.

> **How the chapter ends.** Not with a wedding scene. With a man taking a woman into his
> dead mother's tent, one word about grief finally lifting — and the last clause of the
> longest chapter in Genesis being *and he loved her*.
