The sentence the whole book is built on, stated here for the second time — it
opened the collection back in chapter 1.

## "Begins"

*The fear of the LORD is where wisdom begins.*

The Hebrew word can mean the starting point or the chief part, and both senses
are usually intended. It is the first thing and the principal thing.

What the verse rules out is treating wisdom as a technique that could be acquired
by someone starting anywhere. There is a door, and this is it.

## The second line defines the first

*And knowing the Holy One is understanding.*

Hebrew poetry works by pairing lines that illuminate each other. So *fear of the
LORD* is set beside *knowing the Holy One* — which keeps the fear from collapsing
into terror.

It is the awe of someone who knows who they are dealing with. You cannot be in
awe of someone you do not know at all.

## Who carries the result

*If you are wise, your wisdom rewards you. If you mock, you carry the weight
alone.*

An unusually stark note to end on. Wisdom is described as paying its owner — and
the mocker is not threatened with punishment so much as told he will bear it by
himself.

Both outcomes land on the person who chose them. Nobody else absorbs either.

> **Where it sits in the chapter.** Proverbs 9 stages two dinner invitations —
> Wisdom has built her house and set her table, and Folly sits at her door calling
> to passers-by. Both call the same people. This verse is at the hinge, and the
> chapter is asking which house you eat in.
