Two companies of fifty men are sent to fetch a prophet, and neither comes back.

## What has happened

Ahaziah, Ahab's son, has fallen through a lattice in his upper room and is injured.

He sends messengers to enquire of **Baal-Zebub, the god of Ekron** — a Philistine deity — whether
he will recover.

Elijah intercepts them: **1:3** *is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to enquire of
Baal-Zebub?*

**1:4** *"Therefore you shall not come down from the bed to which you have gone up, but you shall surely
die."*

## The identification

The messengers describe him to the king: **1:8** *a hairy man wearing a leather belt around his waist*.

**1:8** again: *And he said, "**It is Elijah the Tishbite**."*

## The first company

**1:9** *Then the king sent **a captain with fifty men** to bring Elijah in.*

Fifty armed men to arrest one prophet.

## Where he is

**1:9** again: *The captain found him **sitting on the top of a hill**.*

Not hiding. Sitting, visible, on high ground.

## The summons

**1:9** again: *And called up, "**Man of God, the king says: Come down**."*

The title is used, and then the king's authority is set against it in the same sentence.

**Man of God** — a formal designation. The captain knows exactly who he is addressing.

## The answer

**1:10** *Elijah answered, "**If I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and consume you and your
fifty**."*

The Hebrew turns on the captain's own word.

He said *man of God*. Elijah says: if that is what I am, then this follows.

## What happens

**1:10** again: *And fire came down and consumed the captain and his fifty men.*

## The second company

**1:11** *The king sent another captain with another fifty. This one called out, "**Man of God, the king
says: Come down at once**."*

Worse. **Meherah** — quickly, hurry.

Fifty men have just died and the second captain adds urgency to the same demand.

**1:12** *Again Elijah answered… and once more the fire came down and consumed them.*

## The third

The chapter continues. A third captain comes up the hill **1:13** *and fell on his knees before Elijah, and
pleaded with him*.

**1:13** again: *"Man of God, please let my life and the life of these fifty servants of yours be precious in your
sight."*

He does not deliver the king's message. He asks.

## What the angel says

**1:15** *And the angel of the LORD said to Elijah, "**Go down with him; do not be afraid of him**."*

Elijah goes, and delivers the same word to the king's face: **1:16** *you shall surely die*.

The third captain lives, and the prophet walks down the hill with him.

## What the two companies were doing

Carrying an arrest order from a king who had just sent to a Philistine god.

The confrontation is not about Elijah's dignity. It is a continuation of Carmel — *if the LORD is
God, follow Him; but if Baal, follow him*. See the note on 1 Kings 18:17–24.

## Where the disciples quote it

Samaritans refuse Jesus passage, and **Luke 9:54** *when His disciples James and John saw this, they said,
"Lord, do You want us to command fire to come down from heaven and consume them, **just as Elijah
did**?"*

*But He turned and rebuked them.* See the note on Luke 9:51–56.

## What they had missed

The precedent existed. They cited it correctly.

The village had done nothing but decline hospitality, which is not the same as a king enquiring
of Baal-Zebub through fifty armed men.

## The two on the mountain

**Luke 9:30** *And behold, two men talked with Him, who were Moses and **Elijah**.*

Standing next to Him, six days after Peter's confession, and the disciples who wanted the fire
were there.
