The day after the greatest day of his life, he asks to die — and what he is given is
sleep and a hot meal.

## The report

*Ahab told Jezebel **everything Elijah had done**.*

The fire, the water, the four hundred and fifty, the rain. Ahab saw all of it and
reports it to his wife as news.

## The threat

*"**May the gods deal with me, and worse, if by this time tomorrow I haven't made your
life like the life of one of them**."*

A messenger, not an arrest party. She had the power to send soldiers and sent words.

And she gives him a day's notice.

## What he had just done

Outrun a chariot from Carmel to Jezreel with the hand of the LORD on him — about
seventeen miles, on foot, after a full day on the mountain.

## The turn

*Elijah **was afraid, and he ran for his life**.*

The Hebrew can be read *he saw* or *he was afraid*; the older versions have *when he
saw*.

The man who stood in front of a nation and four hundred and fifty prophets runs from
one message.

## How far

*He came to **Beersheba in Judah**, **left his servant there**, and went **a day's
journey into the wilderness alone**.*

Beersheba is at the far southern end of Judah — about a hundred miles, and out of
Jezebel's jurisdiction entirely. He was safe when he got there.

Then he leaves the last person with him and walks another day into the desert.

## The tree

*He came to **a solitary broom tree**, sat down under it.*

The Hebrew is **rothem** — a desert shrub with thin foliage. It gives some shade and not
much.

It is the only shelter for miles and it is barely shelter.

## The prayer

*And **prayed that he might die**. "**I've had enough, LORD**," he said. "**Take my
life. I'm no better than my ancestors**."*

The Hebrew is *it is enough now*.

He asks God to kill him, a day's walk from a woman who had offered to do it for free.

*I'm no better than my ancestors* — his complaint is that he has not turned out to be
more than the men before him.

## What he is given

Not a rebuke, not a correction, not a reminder of yesterday.

*Then he **lay down under the tree and fell asleep**.*

## The first touch

*And suddenly **an angel touched him** and said, "**Get up and eat**."*

*He looked, and there by his head was **a warm loaf of bread, baked over hot coals, and a
jug of water**.*

Bread and water at his head while he slept. The same two things Obadiah carried to a
hundred prophets in caves. See the note on 1 Kings 18:1–6.

Baked on coals, so somebody had made a fire.

## And then

*He ate and drank and **lay back down**.*

He goes back to sleep, and nothing is said about it.

## The second touch

*The angel of the LORD came **a second time**, touched him, and said, "**Get up and eat.
The journey is too much for you**."*

The Hebrew is *the way is too great for you*.

The reason given for the second meal is the distance ahead, which Elijah has not been
told about yet.

## The result

*Strengthened by that food, he traveled **forty days and forty nights** until he reached
**Horeb, the mountain of God**.*

Forty days on two meals.

Horeb is Sinai — where Moses saw the bush, and where the law was given. Elijah walks to
the place the covenant started.

## What he is not given

A rebuke.

He has run from a threat the day after the greatest public victory in his ministry, abandoned
his servant, sat down under a bush and asked to die. He tells God *I am no better than my
fathers*.

There is no correction anywhere in the passage. Not one word about his fear, his flight, or
his self-assessment.

*For **He knows our frame; He remembers that we are dust**.*

*A bruised reed He will not break, and smoking flax He will not quench.*

## What he is given instead

*Then as he lay and slept under a broom tree, suddenly **an angel touched him**, and said to
him, "**Arise and eat**."*

Sleep. Then food and water. Then sleep again. Then food again.

Two meals and two naps before a single question is asked, and the question when it comes is
gentle: *what are you doing here, Elijah?*

## The reason given

*"**Arise and eat, because the journey is too great for you.**"*

Not *because you are being feeble*. Because the road ahead is more than he can manage — which
is a statement about the road, not about him.

*"**Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden**, and I will give you rest."*

## The touch

*An angel **touched** him.* And again: *the angel of the LORD came back the second time, and
**touched him**.*

Twice. Before any instruction, and before any theology.

*Then Jesus **put out His hand and touched him**, saying, "I am willing; be cleansed."*

## Where he ends up

Forty days at Horeb — the mountain where the law was given — and there, no wind, no
earthquake, no fire.

*And after the fire **a still small voice**.*

The Hebrew is **qol demamah daqqah** — a sound of thin silence. The God who had just answered
by fire in front of a nation speaks to one exhausted man in a whisper.

> **The order of what he was given.** Sleep, food, sleep, food. Then, and only then, a
> journey and a conversation. Nothing was said to him about Jezebel, Carmel, or the
> prayer under the tree.
