The last of seven quotations, and it closes a case about seating.

## The argument of the chapter

Hebrews opens with the Son — heir of all things, through whom the worlds were made, the brightness
of God's glory — and then compares Him to angels.

Seven Old Testament quotations follow, each asked as *to which of the angels did He ever say?*

## The question

*And did He ever say to an angel, "**Sit at My right hand until I make Your enemies a stool for Your
feet**"?*

Psalm 110:1, the most quoted Old Testament verse in the New Testament. See the note on
Psalms 110:1.

## The expected answer

No.

Every quotation in the chapter is framed to produce it.

## What angels do instead of sit

*Are not they every one of them **spirits in God's service, sent out on errands**?*

The Greek is **leitourgika pneumata eis diakonian apostellomena**.

**Leitourgikos** — of public service. **Diakonia** — service. **Apostellomena** — being sent out.

Three service words in one clause.

## The contrast

One sits. The others are sent.

The whole chapter turns on posture: the Son sat down, and the angels are dispatched.

## Who they are sent for

*For the sake of **the people who are going to inherit salvation**.*

The Greek is **dia tous mellontas kleronomein soterian**.

Not sent to God. Sent because of the heirs.

## The future participle

**Mellontas** — about to, going to.

The inheritance is described as not yet received, by people who already have the angels working on
their behalf.

## What the letter has already said

*When He had by Himself purged our sins, **sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high**.* See
the note on Hebrews 1:1–3.

The sitting is what the purging made possible.

## Why sitting matters in this letter

*And **every priest stands ministering daily** and offering repeatedly the same sacrifices, which can
never take away sins.*

*But this Man, after He had offered one sacrifice for sins forever, **sat down at the right hand of
God**.* See the note on Hebrews 10:11–13.

No seat is recorded in the tabernacle. The priests stood, because the work never finished.

## Where the angels appear in the Gospels

Ministering to Him after the wilderness. Strengthening Him in Gethsemane. At the tomb.

And *do you think that I cannot now pray to My Father, and He will provide Me with more than twelve
legions of angels?*

## Where they appear in Acts

Opening prison doors for Peter. Standing by Paul on a sinking ship: *there stood by me this night
an angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I serve*.

## What the next verse says

*Therefore **we must give the more earnest heed to the things we have heard**, lest we drift away.*

The Greek is **pararuomen** — lest we drift, as a boat slips its mooring.

The first of the letter's five warnings, and it follows immediately from the seating.

## What Elisha's servant saw

*And behold, **the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire** all around Elisha.*

He had not been able to see them until the prophet prayed *LORD, I pray, open his eyes*.

> **The posture.** One sits; the others are sent — and the errands are run *for the sake of* people
> who have not received the inheritance yet.
