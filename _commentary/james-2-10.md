One sentence about how the law works as a unit.

## The sentence

*Keep **every last piece of the law** and **trip over one**, and you are **answerable for
the whole of it**.*

The Greek is *whoever keeps the whole law and stumbles in one point is guilty of all*.

## The verb

**Ptaio** — to stumble, to trip. Not to abandon or to defy.

James picks the mildest available word. A stumble is what happens to somebody who is
walking in the right direction.

## The claim

**Enochos** — liable, answerable, under charge. It is a courtroom word.

Not *has broken all of them*. Answerable for all of it.

## Why it works that way

The verse after gives the reason: *for **He who said**, "Do not commit adultery," **also
said**, "Do not murder."*

The law is one lawgiver's word, not a list of separate requirements. Offending at one
point is not failing a subsection; it is contradicting the person who spoke all of it.

James makes the argument about the speaker rather than the statute.

## What he is arguing against

The context is favouritism. The chapter opens with a rich man in fine clothes and a poor
man in filthy ones walking into the same meeting, and the rich man being given the good
seat.

James calls that a breach: *if you show partiality, you commit sin, and are convicted by
the law as transgressors.*

Somebody was evidently treating it as a minor lapse, and this verse is the answer.

## The royal law

*If you really fulfil **the royal law** according to the Scripture, "You shall love your
neighbour as yourself," you do well.*

He calls it royal — **basilikon**, the king's law.

## Where Paul says the same thing

*For as many as are of the works of the law are under the curse; for it is written,
"Cursed is everyone who does not continue in **all things** which are written in the book
of the law, to do them."* See the note on Galatians 3:10–14.

Both writers land on the same word. Paul quotes Deuteronomy's *all things*; James says
*guilty of all*.

They are usually set against each other on faith and works, and on this point they are
identical.

## What James says next

*So speak and so do **as those who will be judged by the law of liberty**. For **judgment
is without mercy to the one who has shown no mercy**. **Mercy triumphs over judgment**.*

The paragraph that contains the strictest statement of the law's unity ends on that
sentence.

The Greek for *triumphs over* is **katakauchatai** — boasts against, exults over.

## The chain image

The old illustration is a chain over a pit: it does not matter which link goes.

James's own argument is not about links. It is about one voice.

> **How light the offence is in the sentence.** A stumble, at one point, by somebody
> keeping the rest. James chooses the smallest possible failure and attaches the largest
> possible liability to it.
