Two people walking to Emmaus get a seven-mile exposition of the Old Testament
from its subject, and Luke does not record a word of the content.

## What He rebukes them for

*How slow you are, and how heavy of heart, when it comes to believing everything
the prophets said.*

Not for failing to recognise Him — Luke says their eyes were kept from doing
that. For not believing what was already written.

The material to work it out had been available the whole time.

## The word "had to"

*Did the Messiah not have to suffer all of this and then come into His glory?*

The Greek carries necessity — it was *necessary*. Luke uses this word repeatedly
of Jesus' path.

And the order is fixed: suffering, then glory. That is the sequence the two on
the road could not make fit, because a defeated Messiah made no sense to them.
Isaiah 52 has the same two elements in the same relationship.

## "Beginning with Moses"

*And beginning with Moses and going on through all the Prophets, He opened up for
them everything in the Scriptures that was about Himself.*

The whole Hebrew Bible, in order, with Himself as the thread. Later in the same
chapter He names the third division too — the Psalms.

Every reader since has wanted the transcript. Luke gives the method and not the
notes, which may be deliberate: the exercise is left for us to do.

## Where they recognised Him

Not during the exposition. *When He was at the table with them, He took bread,
gave thanks, broke it and began to give it to them. Then their eyes were opened.*

The Scriptures burned in them on the road. The recognition came at the breaking
of bread.

> **What they said afterwards.** *Were not our hearts burning within us while He
> talked with us on the road and opened the Scriptures to us?* They knew
> something had happened before they knew who had done it.
