Paul empties the vocabulary of everything he can think of, in pairs.

## The confidence

*For **I am fully persuaded**.*

The Greek is **pepeismai** — perfect passive. He has been convinced, and stands
convinced.

Not *I hope* and not *I feel*. A settled state.

## The pairs

Every item comes with its opposite, so that the space between them is covered:

- **death** and **life** — the two conditions a person can be in
- **angels** and **rulers** — beings above the human order
- **things present** and **things to come** — time, both directions
- **height** and **depth** — space, both directions

And then the catch-all: *nor **anything else anywhere in the whole of creation**.*

## What is missing from the list

The reader.

Every item is external. Paul does not include failure, doubt, sin, or the person's own
performance — and he has just spent seven chapters on exactly those.

## "Powers"

The Greek is **dunamis** — the word for power, ability, force. Paul includes them
alongside angels and rulers.

Colossians has the same categories, disarmed and led in a procession. See the note on
Colossians 2:11–15.

## The verb

*Has **the strength to cut us off**.*

The Greek is **chorisai** — to separate, to put a space between. It is the word used of
divorce: *what God has joined together, let not man **separate**.*

Paul is denying that anything has the strength to do it.

## What it is separation from

*From **the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord**.*

Not from salvation, not from heaven, not from blessing. From the love.

And it is located: **in Christ Jesus**. The same phrase that opens the chapter — *no
condemnation for those who are **in Christ Jesus**.* See the note on Romans 8:1–4.

## The questions before it

The paragraph is built out of five questions, each answered by silence:

*If God is for us, who can be against us? He who did not spare His own Son… how shall He
not with Him also freely give us all things? **Who shall bring a charge** against God's
elect? **Who is he who condemns? Who shall separate us** from the love of Christ?*

Paul does not answer the first four. He answers the last one, and this is the answer.

## The list in between

*Tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or
sword?*

Seven, and Paul had personally had six of them.

Then he quotes a psalm: *for Your sake we are killed all day long; we are accounted as
sheep for the slaughter.*

He does not soften it. He includes the psalm and then says *yet in all these things we
are more than conquerors.*

## "More than conquerors"

One Greek word — **hupernikao**. Over-conquering. Paul appears to have made it up.

> **What the sentence is not.** A statement that nothing bad happens. The list of things
> that do happen is in the paragraph, and the psalm about sheep for the slaughter is
> quoted in full. What Paul denies is that any of it can get between.
