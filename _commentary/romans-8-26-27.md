Paul admits an inability and then says who covers it.

## "In the same way"

The Greek is **hosautos** — likewise, in the same manner.

It connects to the verses before, about creation groaning and about waiting *with
perseverance* for what we do not see.

Three groans in the paragraph: creation groans, we groan, and the Spirit groans.

## What He does

*The Spirit also **comes alongside our weaknesses and takes hold with us**.*

The Greek is **sunantilambanetai** — a triple compound: *sun* (with) + *anti* (opposite)
+ *lambano* (take hold).

To take hold of a load together with somebody, from the other end.

The word occurs twice in the New Testament. The other is Martha's complaint: *tell her to
**help** me.* See the note on Luke 10:38–42.

## The weakness

*Our **weaknesses**.*

The Greek is **astheneia** — the general word for infirmity, used of illness and of any
lack of strength.

Paul says it about the readers and includes himself.

## The specific failure

*We **do not know what we ought to pray for**.*

Not that we do not know how. That we do not know **what** — the Greek is *what we should
pray as we ought*.

Paul, who wrote the prayers in Ephesians and Colossians, says this about himself.

## The intervention

*But **the Spirit Himself steps in and pleads for us**.*

The Greek is **huperentunchanei** — to intercede on behalf of. Another compound, and
another word that appears once in the New Testament.

## The manner

*With **groans too deep to be put into words**.*

The Greek is **stenagmois alaletois** — unspoken, unutterable groanings.

## Who hears it

*And **the One who searches our hearts knows the Spirit's mind**.*

The Father searches the heart, and what He finds there is the Spirit's own mind.

The Greek for *searches* is **eraunon** — to examine, to investigate. The same word John
uses of the risen Christ in Revelation: *I am He who searches the minds and hearts.*

## Why it lands

*Because the Spirit **intercedes for God's holy people according to the will of God**.*

The Greek is *according to God* — in line with Him.

Two of the three persons are involved on the reader's behalf, and Paul says the third is
listening.

## The other intercession

Eight verses on, in the same chapter: *it is Christ who died, and furthermore is also
risen, who is even at the right hand of God, **who also makes intercession for us**.*

One inside, one at the right hand.

Hebrews puts it the same way: *He always lives to make intercession for them.*

## What comes immediately after

*And we know that **all things work together for good** to those who love God.* See the
note on Romans 8:28–30.

The famous verse follows directly from the two about not knowing what to pray for. Paul
puts the ignorance first and the confidence second.

> **What is conceded.** That we do not know what to ask for. Paul does not treat it as a
> failure to be corrected — he names it and then says who takes the other end of the
> load.
