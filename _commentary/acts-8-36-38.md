A man in a desert finds water and asks the only question left.

## Who spots it

*As they went on down the road **they came to some water, and the official said,
"Look, here is water."***

Not Philip. The Ethiopian.

Luke had already told us the road was a desert one. The man notices water in a place
that does not have much, and knows immediately what it is for.

## The question

*"**What is there to keep me from being baptized?**"*

Every honest answer available in the first century would have been *a great deal*.

He is a Gentile, from a foreign court, and — as an official of that kind — a eunuch.
Deuteronomy 23 barred a eunuch from the assembly of the LORD outright.

He had travelled fifteen hundred miles to a temple that would not let him past the
outer court.

He asks what is in the way, and Philip does not list anything.

## The answer

*"**If you believe with all your heart, you may.**"*

And: *"I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God."*

That is the entire examination.

## What Isaiah says about him

He had been reading Isaiah 53. Three chapters further on, in the same scroll, is
this:

*Do not let the eunuch say, "Here I am, a dry tree." For thus says the LORD: **to the
eunuchs who keep My Sabbaths… I will give them a place and a name better than that
of sons and daughters. I will give them an everlasting name that shall not be cut
off.***

Isaiah 56, addressed to exactly his condition, written centuries earlier.

## The stopping

*He **gave the order to stop the chariot**, and the two of them went down into the
water.*

A senior official halts his own convoy in a desert to be baptised by a man who ran
up alongside an hour earlier.

## What happens after

*When they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught Philip away, and
the official saw him no more, **and went on his way rejoicing**.*

Philip vanishes. The man is left standing in a desert with wet clothes, no teacher,
and a scroll — and Luke's note is that he went on rejoicing.

## Where the gospel had reached

Acts 1:8 set the programme: *Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end
of the earth.*

Philip had just done Samaria. This man is going home to the southern edge of the
known world.

> **The verse that keeps some manuscripts apart.** Verse 37 — *if you believe with all
> your heart, you may* — is absent from the oldest Greek manuscripts and present in
> many later ones. Whatever its history, the confession it records is the only
> requirement anyone in the passage names.
