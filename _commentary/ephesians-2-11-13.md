Paul tells Gentile readers to remember what they were, and lists five deficits.

## The instruction

*So **remember** that you, as far as the flesh goes, were once **the outsider nations**.*

The Greek is **mnemoneuete** — keep remembering. Present imperative.

**Ta ethne en sarki** — the nations in flesh.

## The nickname

*Called "**the uncircumcised**" by those who call themselves "**the circumcised**."*

The Greek is **akrobustia** — foreskin. A crude term, and Paul reports it as it was used.

## The qualifier

*A mark **cut into the flesh by human hands**.*

The Greek is **cheiropoietou** — made by hands.

He does not deny the distinction; he relativises it. Both parties are being described by something
done in the flesh.

## The five things

*Remember that at that time you were:*

**Without Christ** — **choris Christou**.

**Shut out of the citizenship of Israel** — **apellotriomenoi tes politeias**.

**Strangers to the covenants that carried the promise** — **xenoi ton diathekon tes epangelias**.

**With no hope** — **elpida me echontes**.

**And no God in the world** — **atheoi en to kosmo**.

## The last word

**Atheoi** — godless, without God. The word English took as *atheist*.

Paul applies it to people who had a great many gods. Ephesus had the temple of Artemis, one of the
seven wonders.

Full of religion, and the word he uses is *without God*.

## The order of the list

It runs from Christ outward: no Messiah, no citizenship, no covenants, no hope, no God.

The first item is the reason for all the others.

## The turn

*But **now, in Christ Jesus**.*

The Greek is **nuni de en Christo Iesou**.

Two words carry the whole reversal, and the whole letter turns on them.

## The change

*You who were once **far away** have been **brought near**.*

The Greek is **hoi pote ontes makran egenethete engus**.

Isaiah's phrase: *peace, peace to him who is far off and to him who is near*.

The rabbis used *far* and *near* as technical terms for Gentiles and Jews.

## The means

*By **the blood of Christ**.*

Not by circumcision, not by conversion to Judaism, not by keeping the law.

The letter's whole argument about the Gentile mission runs through those four words.

## What follows

*For He Himself is our peace, who has made both one, and has **broken down the middle wall of
separation**.* See the note on Ephesians 2:14–16.

The Greek is **to mesotoichon tou phragmou** — the dividing wall of the fence.

## What that wall was

A literal barrier stood in the temple, between the court of the Gentiles and the inner courts,
with inscriptions in Greek and Latin warning that any foreigner passing it had only himself to
blame for his death.

Two of those stones have been found.

## Why Paul is in prison

He was arrested on the accusation that he had *brought Greeks into the temple and defiled this
holy place*. See the note on Ephesians 3:1–4.

The letter is written by a man in chains for the doctrine in this paragraph.

## What they become

*Now, therefore, you are **no longer strangers and foreigners, but fellow citizens with the saints
and members of the household of God**.* See the note on Ephesians 2:17–19.

Every item on the deficit list is answered.

> **The word.** **Atheoi** — without God. Applied to a city with one of the seven wonders of the
> world standing in it as a temple.
