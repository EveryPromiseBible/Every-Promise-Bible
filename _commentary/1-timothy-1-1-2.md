Two verses of address, and both halves are unusual.

## Not by his own choosing

*From Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ — **not by any choosing of mine, but under orders from
God our Savior** and from the Lord Jesus Christ.*

The Greek is **kat' epitagen** — by command, by injunction.

He uses this construction only here and in Titus. Elsewhere it is *by the will of God*. **Under
orders** is stronger, and it is aimed at a situation in Ephesus where Paul's authority is going
to be exercised hard.

## The title given to the Father

*God **our Savior**.*

Paul normally reserves *Savior* for Christ. In the letters to Timothy and Titus he applies it to
the Father repeatedly — six times across the three letters.

## The title given to Christ

*The Lord Jesus Christ, **who is our hope**.*

Not *who gives us hope*. The hope is a person, in apposition.

The same construction as *Christ in you, the hope of glory*. See the note on
Colossians 1:26–27.

## The address

*To Timothy, **my own true son in the faith**.*

The Greek is **gnesio tekno** — legitimate child, born in wedlock. The word is the opposite of
*nothos*, illegitimate.

Paul uses it of Timothy here and of Titus in the same phrasing. It is a legal word about
parentage, borrowed for a man he led to Christ.

## Who Timothy was

*The son of a certain Jewish woman who believed, but his father was Greek.*

Mixed parentage, which in that setting made his standing ambiguous — and Paul opens with the
word for a child whose standing is not ambiguous at all.

*The genuine faith that is in you, which dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother
Eunice.* See the note on 2 Timothy 1:3–5.

The same adjective, **anupokritos**, applied to the faith.

## The three-word greeting

*May **grace and mercy and peace** be yours.*

Paul's standing greeting is two words: *grace and peace*. To Timothy — both letters — he adds
**mercy** in the middle.

He does it for no other church and no other man except in the same personal letters.

## Why mercy might be there

Paul's own account, a few verses later: *I obtained mercy because I did it ignorantly in
unbelief*, and *I obtained mercy, that in me first Jesus Christ might show all longsuffering*.

The word he inserts into the greeting is the word he uses of himself twice in the first chapter.
See the note on 1 Timothy 1:12–14.

## What the letter is for

*As I urged you when I went into Macedonia — **remain in Ephesus that you may charge some that
they teach no other doctrine**.*

A young man left in charge of a difficult church, addressed as a legitimate son by a man
writing under orders.

## The second letter

*To Timothy, **a son I love dearly**.* See the note on 2 Timothy 1:1–2.

The legal word becomes an affectionate one, and by then Paul is in prison writing his last
letter.

> **The word for son.** **Gnesios** — legitimate, born in wedlock. A legal term about parentage,
> used of a man whose actual parentage was mixed and whose standing was not.
