He asks the wrong question and gets an answer to a different one.

## Where he is

At a bush that burns and is not consumed, on the far side of the desert, forty years into
shepherding somebody else's sheep.

*"I have surely seen the oppression of My people… **Come now, therefore, and I will send you to
Pharaoh**."* See the note on Exodus 3:7–10.

## The objection

*"**Who am I**, that I should go to Pharaoh — that I should bring the Israelites out of Egypt?"*

The Hebrew is **mi anochi**.

## Why it is a fair question

He is eighty. He left Egypt forty years ago as a fugitive, having killed a man and buried him in
the sand.

The Israelites had already rejected him once: *who made you a prince and a judge over us?*

He has no standing in Egypt and no standing with the people.

## The answer

*And God said, "**I will be with you**."*

The Hebrew is **ki-ehyeh immach** — for I will be with you.

## What is not answered

The question.

Moses asked about himself and God does not describe him, correct him, or reassure him about his
qualifications.

The subject is changed from *who am I* to *I will be*.

## The verb

**Ehyeh** — I will be.

Three verses later, when Moses asks what name to give, the answer uses the same word: *I AM WHO I
AM* — **Ehyeh asher Ehyeh**.

The answer to *who am I* and the answer to *what is Your name* are the same Hebrew verb. See the
note on Exodus 3:13–15.

## The sign

*"And here is the sign that it is I who sent you: **when you have brought the people out of Egypt,
you will worship God on this very mountain**."*

## The problem with the sign

It comes afterwards.

A sign is normally something given to confirm a commission before you act on it. Gideon asked for
a fleece and got it that night. Hezekiah got the shadow moving backwards while he was still in
bed.

This one can only be verified once the whole thing is done.

## What that means

Moses is being asked to go to Pharaoh on the strength of *I will be with you*, and the
confirmation is a worship service on this mountain at some point in the future.

## What happens on that mountain

Exodus 19. The same Horeb, with the whole nation at the foot of it, thunder and lightning and a
thick cloud, and Moses going up.

The sign is fulfilled, and the book records it happening.

## The rest of the objections

Moses raises four more.

*Suppose they will not believe me.* — the staff, the hand, the water.

*I am not eloquent… I am slow of speech and slow of tongue.*

*O my Lord, please send by the hand of whomever else You may send.*

*So the anger of the LORD was kindled against Moses*, and Aaron is appointed.

Five objections in one conversation, and only the last one draws anger.

## What the objections are about

Every one of them is about Moses. God's answers are almost never about Moses.

*"**I will be with you**." "**I AM WHO I AM**." "**Who has made man's mouth?**"*

## What Hebrews says

*By faith Moses, when he became of age, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter…
esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures in Egypt.* See the note on
Hebrews 11:24–26.

> **The question and the answer.** He asks *who am I*, and gets *I will be*. The subject of the
> sentence is changed, and the same verb turns up three verses later as the name.
