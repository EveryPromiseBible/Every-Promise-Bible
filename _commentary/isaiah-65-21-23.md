Part of the picture Isaiah gives of the world made new, and every clause in it is about
keeping what you made.

## What the chapter is

*For behold, **I create new heavens and a new earth**; and the former shall not be
remembered or come to mind.*

John quotes the phrase at the end of Revelation.

## The building

*They will **build houses and dwell in them**. They will **plant vineyards and eat their
fruit**.*

Two ordinary activities, each with its outcome attached.

## The negative version

*No longer will they **build for another to live in**, or **plant for another to eat**.*

That is what the promise is set against.

Deuteronomy's curses had named it: *you shall build a house and not dwell in it; you shall
plant a vineyard and not gather its grapes*. See the note on
Deuteronomy 28:58–68.

And Israel's history had run that way — invasions, deportations, and other people
occupying the houses.

## The comparison

*For **as the days of a tree, so will be the days of My people**.*

Olives and oaks live for centuries. Some of the olives on the Mount of Olives are held to
be a thousand years old.

Isaiah picks the longest-lived thing that grows.

## The enjoyment

*My chosen ones will **long enjoy the work of their hands**.*

The Hebrew is *shall long enjoy* — literally *wear out*, in the sense of using something to
the full.

## The labour

*They will **not labor in vain**.*

The Hebrew is **riq** — emptiness, for nothing.

Ecclesiastes' complaint answered: *what profit has a man from all his labour in which he
toils under the sun?*

## The children

*Or **bear children doomed to misfortune**.*

The Hebrew is *bring forth for trouble*.

Infant mortality in the ancient world ran near half. The clause is not abstract.

## The reason

*For they will be **a people blessed by the LORD**, **they and their descendants with
them**.*

Two generations named, as in the covenant language.

## What is in the same chapter

*The wolf and the lamb shall feed together, the lion shall eat straw like the ox… **they
shall not hurt nor destroy in all My holy mountain**.*

And: *it shall come to pass that **before they call, I will answer**; and while they are
still speaking, I will hear.*

## What the chapter opens with

*"I was sought by those who did not ask for Me; I was found by those who did not seek Me. I
said, '**Here I am, here I am**,' to a nation that was not called by My name."*

Paul quotes it in Romans 10 of the Gentiles.

The chapter that ends with houses and vineyards begins with God saying *here I am* to
people who were not looking.

## What the tree image does elsewhere

*He is like **a tree planted by the water**… **it never stops bearing fruit**.* See the note
on Jeremiah 17:7–8.

And Psalm 92: *those who are planted in the house of the LORD… **shall still bear fruit in
old age**.*

> **What the promise consists of.** Not spectacle. Living in the house you built, eating
> off the vine you planted, and lasting as long as a tree.
