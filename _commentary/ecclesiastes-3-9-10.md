Two verses of anticlimax, set immediately after the most quoted passage in the book.

## Where it sits

Directly after *a time to be born and a time to die* — fourteen pairs, the seasons of a whole
human life laid out in poetry.

And then: *so **what does the worker gain from all his toil?***

The Preacher writes the beautiful thing and then asks what any of it was worth. That is the
book's method throughout.

## The question

*Mah-yitron ha'oseh* — what profit has the doer?

**Yitron** is Ecclesiastes' own word. It is an accounting term — the surplus, what is left over
when the books are balanced — and it appears ten times in this book and nowhere else in
Scripture.

He is asking whether anything shows in the bottom line.

## The task

*I have seen **the task God has given us all to keep us busy**.*

The Hebrew is **ha'inyan** — the business, the occupation, the thing given to be busied with.
And **la'anot bo** — to be occupied with it, from a root that also carries *to be afflicted*.

He does not say the task is meaningless, and he does not say God did not give it. He says it is
given, and it keeps everybody busy, and he cannot find the surplus.

## The verse that answers him

*"**Come to Me, all you who labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.** Take My yoke
upon you and learn from Me… for My yoke is easy and My burden is light."*

**Jesus** addresses the exact category — **kopiontes**, the ones worn out by toil — and offers
the one thing the Preacher's ledger has no column for.

He does not abolish the yoke. He swaps it.

## And the rest

*There remains therefore **a rest for the people of God**. For **he who has entered His rest has
himself also ceased from his works**, as God did from His.*

**Hebrews** 4, and its argument is the answer to *what does the worker gain*. The surplus is not
produced by the labour. It is entered.

*For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of
God, **not of works**, lest anyone should boast.*

Ecclesiastes audits human effort and finds no **yitron**. **Ephesians** agrees, in the same
terms, and says salvation was never on that ledger.

## What the Preacher does with it

Six verses later: *I know that nothing is better for them than to rejoice, and to do good in
their lives, and also that **every man should eat and drink and enjoy the good of all his
labour — it is the gift of God**.* See the note on Ecclesiastes 2:24–26.

Having found no profit in the work, he goes back to the work, and calls the enjoyment of it a
gift rather than a wage.

> **The accountant's word.** *Yitron* — the surplus on a balanced set of books. The Preacher
> uses it ten times and never finds any, and Hebrews says the rest he was looking for is
> entered rather than earned.
