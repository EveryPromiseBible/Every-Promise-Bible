The garden, and Matthew does not hide any of it.

## The place

*They came to a place called **Gethsemane**.*

The name means **oil press** — **gat shemanim**. An olive grove on the slope of the Mount
of Olives, with a press in it.

The fruit is crushed to get what is in it.

## The arrangement

*"**Sit down here** while **I go over there and pray**."*

The eight are left at the entrance. Three go further.

## The three

*He took **Peter and the two sons of Zebedee**.*

The same three from Jairus's house and the mountain. See the note on
Matthew 17:1–8.

They had seen Him transfigured. Now they see this.

## What came over Him

*And **grief came over Him, and a heaviness He did not hide**.*

The Greek is **lupeisthai** and **ademonein** — the second is a rare word for distress,
anguish, being overwhelmed.

Mark has an even stronger word: **ekthambeisthai**, appalled.

## What He tells them

*"**My soul is crushed with sorrow**," He told them. "**It is almost more than I can
bear**."*

The Greek is **perilupos estin he psuche mou heos thanatou** — sorrowful to the point of
death.

He tells them. He does not manage it privately and does not perform composure.

## The request

*"**Stay here, and keep watch with Me**."*

The Greek is **gregoreite** — stay awake. The same word Peter later uses in his letter.
See the note on 1 Peter 5:8.

They fall asleep three times, and He comes back three times.

## The distance

*Then **He went on a little farther**.*

Luke says *about a stone's throw*.

Near enough to be seen and far enough to be alone.

## The posture

*And **fell with His face to the ground**.*

Not kneeling. Face down.

## The address

*"**My Father**."*

Mark preserves the Aramaic: **Abba**. It is the word Paul says the Spirit puts in the
believer's mouth. See the note on Galatians 4:4–7.

## The request

*"**If there is any way, let this cup pass from Me**."*

The Greek is *if it is possible*.

The cup is the Old Testament image for what a person is given to drink — and in the
prophets it is usually the cup of wrath. Isaiah: *you have drunk the cup of His fury*.

## The clause after it

*"**Yet not what I want — what You want**."*

Matthew records three prayers. The first has *if it is possible*. The second changes: *if
this cup cannot pass away from Me unless I drink it, Your will be done.*

The request shifts once and the last clause never does.

## What Hebrews says about the same night

*Who, in the days of His flesh, when He had offered up **prayers and supplications, with
vehement cries and tears** to Him who was able to save Him from death, **and was heard**
because of His godly fear.* See the note on Hebrews 5:9–10.

**And was heard.** The writer says the prayer was answered — and the cup was not taken.

## What Luke adds

*Then an angel appeared to Him from heaven, strengthening Him. And being in agony, He
prayed more earnestly. Then His sweat became like great drops of blood falling down to the
ground.*

## What He says when He comes back the last time

*"**Rise, let us be going. See, My betrayer is at hand.**"*

The same words He used in the upper room. See the note on John 14:28–31.

## What was in the cup

*"O My Father, if it is possible, **let this cup pass from Me**."*

The cup is not death. The Old Testament has a fixed image for it:

*For in the hand of the LORD there is **a cup**, and the wine is red; it is fully mixed, and He
pours it out.*

*Awake, awake! Stand up, O Jerusalem, you who have drunk **at the hand of the LORD the cup of His
fury**; you have drunk the dregs of the cup of trembling, and drained it out.*

*He himself shall also drink of **the wine of the wrath of God**, which is poured out full strength
into the cup of His indignation.*

Every occurrence is the same cup, and **Revelation** 14 says who normally drinks it.

*For **He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us**, that we might become the righteousness of God
in Him.*

*Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, **having become a curse for us**.*

## What He said the second time

*"O My Father, **if this cup cannot pass away from Me unless I drink it, Your will be done**."*

The first prayer has an *if it is possible*. The second has an *if it cannot*, and the grammar has
moved.

*"**The cup which My Father has given Me, shall I not drink it?**"*

**John** 18, said in the same garden a few minutes later, to Peter with a sword.

## What He drank instead

*Then they gave Him **sour wine mingled with gall** to drink. But when He had tasted it, He would
not drink.*

He refuses the sedative and takes the other cup.

*Likewise He also took **the cup** after supper, saying, "This cup is **the new covenant in My
blood**, which is shed for you."*

Hours earlier He had handed a cup round. In the garden He asks to be spared one. **Paul** says the
first is offered to everybody because He did not get out of the second.

> **What He does not do.** Hide it. He tells three men He is crushed, asks them to stay
> awake, goes face down, and asks — twice with an *if* and every time with the same last
> clause.
