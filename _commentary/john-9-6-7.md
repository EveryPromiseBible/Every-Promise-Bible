The strangest method in the Gospels, and the pool's name is the explanation.

## What He had just said

*"Neither this man nor his parents sinned, but **that the works of God should be revealed in
him**."*

And *as long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world*. See the note on
John 9:1–5.

The disciples had asked whose fault the blindness was. He refused the question and then made
mud.

## The action

*He **spat on the ground** and worked the dirt and the spittle into a **little clay**.*

The Greek is **pelon** — clay, mud.

Nothing about it is hygienic and nothing about it is necessary. He heals with a word
elsewhere, and at a distance.

## What is being made

Dust and moisture, worked by hand, applied to a part of a man that does not work.

*And the LORD God **formed man of the dust of the ground**, and breathed into his nostrils
the breath of life.*

The chapter has already said the man was blind **from birth**, so there is nothing to
restore. Something has to be made.

## The application

*And He **spread the clay over the man's eyes**.*

The Greek is **epechrisen** — anointed, smeared on.

A blind man now has mud on his face and has been given no explanation.

## The instruction

*"**Go**," He told him, "**and wash in the pool of Siloam**."*

One command, and a walk across Jerusalem with clay on his eyes, in public.

The pool is at the south end of the city, at the bottom of the Tyropoeon valley. From the
temple area it is downhill all the way and a fair distance.

## The name

*The name means **Sent**.*

John stops the narrative to translate it. He does this a handful of times, and never
idly.

**Shiloach**, from **shalach**, to send. The pool is named for the channel that sent water
into it from the Gihon spring — Hezekiah's tunnel, cut through solid rock before the Assyrian
siege.

## Why John translates it

Because *sent* is his word for Jesus. He uses it more than thirty times: *Him whom the
Father sanctified and sent into the world*, *the works which the Father has given Me to
finish… bear witness of Me, that the Father has sent Me*.

The man is washed in a pool called Sent by a man who calls Himself the Sent One.

## What Isaiah said about that water

*Because this people refused **the waters of Shiloah that flow softly**… now therefore the
Lord brings up over them the waters of the River, strong and mighty — the king of Assyria.*

Isaiah contrasts a quiet local channel with an imperial flood, and says they preferred the
flood.

## The result

*So the man **went off and washed, and he came back seeing**.*

Three verbs, and the whole miracle is in them. John spends one sentence on the cure and
thirty-four more verses on the argument about it.

## What follows

The neighbours cannot agree whether it is the same man. He keeps saying *I am he*.

Two interrogations by the Pharisees, his parents questioned and refusing to commit because
*they feared the Jews*, and then the sentence that ends it: *do you also want to become His
disciples?*

They throw him out.

## What he ends up saying

*"Whether He is a sinner or not I do not know. **One thing I know: that though I was blind,
now I see**."*

And later, when Jesus finds him: *"Lord, I believe!" And he worshipped Him.*

## Where it lands

*"For judgment I have come into this world, **that those who do not see may see, and that
those who see may be made blind**."*

> **Why John stops to translate.** *Siloam* means *Sent* — and *sent* is the word he has been
> using of Jesus since the first chapter.
