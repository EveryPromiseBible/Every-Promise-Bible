Four verses after being told the Father revealed the truth to him, Peter is
called Satan. Matthew puts the two scenes back to back on purpose.

## "From that time on"

*From that time on Jesus began to make it plain.*

The confession changes what He tells them. Once they know who He is, He starts
explaining what that means — and what it means is Jerusalem, suffering, death,
and the third day.

Matthew notes it as a turning point in the Gospel.

## What Peter does

*Peter took Him aside and began to correct Him.*

The verb is the one used for rebuking — the same word Jesus uses on demons and on
a storm. Peter is not raising a question. He is telling Him off, privately, to
spare Him the embarrassment.

*Never, Lord! This will never happen to You.* In Greek it is emphatic: by no
means, not ever.

## The reply

*Get behind Me, Satan. You are a stone in My path.*

The word is **skandalon** — the trigger of a trap, the thing that makes you
stumble. Peter has just been called a rock to build on; now he is a rock to trip
over.

## The diagnosis

*Because you are not thinking the thoughts of God but the thoughts of men.*

That is the charge, and it is worth being precise about. Peter is not accused of
malice. He is accused of ordinary human reasoning — a friend who does not want
the man he loves to be killed.

It is the most natural sentiment in the passage, and Jesus identifies its source
as the same one that offered Him the kingdoms of the world without a cross.

> **The pattern.** Both temptations come from someone who cares. The devil in the
> wilderness offered a route to the crown with no suffering in it; Peter offers
> the same route in the voice of a friend. Jesus answers both with the same
> refusal. See the note on Matthew 4:1–4.
