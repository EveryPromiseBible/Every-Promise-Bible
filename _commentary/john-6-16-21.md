John tells the storm in six verses and adds an ending the other Gospels do not
have.

## Where they were going

*Started across **toward Capernaum**. It was dark by then, and **Jesus had not come to
them**.*

John notes the absence. They set out expecting Him.

## The conditions

*A strong wind was blowing, and **the water was rising rough** around them.*

## The distance

*When they had rowed about **twenty-five or thirty stadia — three or four miles
out**.*

John gives the figure. The lake is roughly seven miles across at that point, so they
are about halfway and have been at it for hours.

He is the only one who measures it.

## What they saw

*They saw Jesus **walking on the sea and coming near the boat**, and **they were
afraid**.*

John does not mention the ghost. Just that they were frightened.

## What He says

*"**It is I. Do not be afraid.**"*

The Greek is **ego eimi** — two words, and John has been building his whole Gospel on
that phrase.

In John's hands it is never just *it's me*. He records seven sayings with a predicate
— bread, light, door, shepherd, resurrection, way, vine — and several without one,
including *before Abraham was, I am*, which nearly got Him stoned. See the note on
John 8:57–59.

Here it is spoken over water in the dark.

## The reception

*Then they were **glad to take Him into the boat**.*

The Greek is that they were willing to receive Him.

Mark says He was *about to go right past them* and stopped for their screaming. John
records the moment they wanted Him aboard. See the note on Mark 6:45–52.

## The ending nobody else has

*And **immediately the boat was at the shore they had been heading for**.*

Three or four miles out, at night, into a headwind — and then at the destination.

John states it flatly, in one clause, with no explanation and no reaction from
anybody.

Matthew and Mark both record the wind dropping. Only John records the distance
disappearing.

## What comes next

The crowd finds Him on the other side the following day, and He tells them *you seek
Me, not because you saw the signs, but because you ate of the loaves and were
filled.*

And then the bread of life discourse, ending with many disciples walking away. See the
note on John 6:52–59.

> **The two problems in the chapter.** Five thousand people and not enough bread, and
> then a lake and not enough time. John puts them on consecutive pages, and the second
> is solved in half a sentence.
