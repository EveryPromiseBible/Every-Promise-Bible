The closing prayer of the argument, after eleven chapters of theology and four of practice.

## Where it sits

Paul has just finished quoting four Old Testament passages about Gentiles praising God, ending
with Isaiah: *there shall be a root of Jesse; and He who shall rise to reign over the Gentiles, in
Him the Gentiles shall hope*.

The prayer follows the quotation, and takes its words from it.

## The title

*Now may **the God of hope**.*

The Greek is **ho Theos tes elpidos**.

He picks the title out of the verse he has just quoted. Isaiah said the Gentiles would hope in
Him; Paul calls Him the God of that hope.

## The request

*Fill you **full**.*

The Greek is **plerosai** — fill, make complete.

## With what

*With **all joy and all peace in believing**.*

The Greek is **pases charas kai eirenes en to pisteuein**.

Two nouns, and *all* attached to both.

## The condition

**En to pisteuein** — in the believing.

A present infinitive: in the ongoing act of trusting.

Not a state achieved. Something that happens while trusting is happening.

## The purpose

*So that you **overflow with hope**.*

The Greek is **eis to perisseuein humas en te elpidi** — that you may abound in the hope.

**Perisseuo** — to exceed, to run over. The verb used of the twelve baskets left after the five
thousand.

## The order

Filled with joy and peace, in order to overflow with hope.

Hope is the outflow, not the input. Paul does not ask for hope directly.

## The agent

*By **the power of the Holy Spirit**.*

The Greek is **en dunamei Pneumatos Hagiou**.

The filling and the overflowing are both attributed to Him.

## What the letter has said about hope

*And **hope does not disappoint**, because the love of God has been poured out in our hearts by the
Holy Spirit.* See the note on Romans 5:3–5.

*For we were saved in this hope, but **hope that is seen is not hope**; for why does one still hope
for what he sees?* See the note on Romans 8:23–25.

*But if we hope for what we do not see, **we eagerly wait for it with perseverance**.*

## Who the letter is written to

A congregation Paul has never visited, divided between Jewish and Gentile believers, arguing about
food and days.

*Receive one another, just as Christ also received us, to the glory of God.*

The prayer comes at the end of that argument, and asks that both sides be filled with the same
thing.

## What follows

Paul's travel plans — Jerusalem with the collection, then Rome, then Spain.

*Now I beg you, brethren… **that you strive together with me in prayers to God for me**, that I may be
delivered from those in Judea who do not believe.*

He got to Rome. In chains, after a shipwreck, as a prisoner.

## The other benediction

*Now to Him who is able to establish you according to my gospel… **to God, alone wise, be glory
through Jesus Christ forever**. Amen.* See the note on Romans 16:25–27.

The letter has two closing prayers, and this is the first.

> **The order.** Joy and peace poured in, *so that* hope runs over. Paul does not ask for hope —
> he asks for the two things whose overflow it is.
