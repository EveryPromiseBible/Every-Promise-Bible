The psalm ends by telling the most powerful men on earth what to do, and it is not surrender.

## Who is addressed

*So now, **you kings, be wise**. Take the warning, you judges of the earth.*

The rulers who opened the psalm plotting — *why do the nations rage… against the LORD and
against His Anointed* — are addressed directly in the last three verses and offered terms.

## The two commands together

*Serve the LORD with reverence, **and rejoice, though your knees be trembling**.*

The Hebrew pairs **gilu bir'adah** — rejoice with trembling. The psalm will not separate them.

*Work out your own salvation **with fear and trembling**, for it is God who works in you.*

## The instruction

*"**Kiss the Son**, or He will grow angry."*

**Nashqu bar** — the kiss of homage, given to a king by a subject. Samuel kisses Saul at his
anointing.

*Kiss the Son*, said to kings, in a psalm the New Testament reads as a coronation of the
Messiah. See the note on Psalms 2:7–9.

## Where the kiss goes wrong

*"**Judas, are you betraying the Son of Man with a kiss?**"*

The gesture the psalm commands, used in a garden at night to identify Him for arrest.

## And where it goes right

*Then she stood at His feet behind Him weeping… and she **kissed His feet** and anointed them
with the fragrant oil.*

A woman with no standing, in a Pharisee's house, doing what the psalm tells kings to do — and
**Jesus** defends her against the host: *you gave Me no kiss, but this woman has not ceased to
kiss My feet since the time I came in.*

## The last line

*"**How happy are all who take shelter in Him.**"*

The psalm about raging nations and an iron sceptre ends on the word **ashrei** — blessed,
happy — and the same word opens Psalm 1.

The two psalms are bracketed by it. Whatever else Psalm 2 is, its final sentence is an
invitation.

> **What the last verse does.** Three verses of warning to kings, and then the psalm stops and
> holds the door open: *blessed are all those who put their trust in Him*.
