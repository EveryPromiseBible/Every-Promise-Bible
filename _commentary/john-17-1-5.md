In the middle of the longest prayer of His recorded, Jesus stops to define a
term, and the definition is not what most people would guess.

## The definition

*And this is eternal life: that they know You, the only true God, and Jesus
Christ, whom You sent.*

Not endless duration. Not a destination. **Knowing a person** — and the Greek
verb is the ordinary one for knowing by acquaintance and experience, not by
information.

Duration is real and John says so elsewhere. But when Jesus supplies the
definition Himself, He puts the emphasis on relationship rather than length.

## "The hour has come"

John has been counting down to this since chapter 2, where Jesus tells His
mother His hour has *not* come. It is said again in chapter 7, and again in
chapter 8.

Here it arrives, and what it means is the cross. The prayer that follows is
spoken by a man who knows what the next eighteen hours contain.

## "I have finished the work"

Said before the arrest. The verb is the same root as *it is finished* on the
cross — completed, brought to its end.

He speaks of the work as done while the hardest part is still ahead, which is
consistent with everything else in John: the crucifixion is described throughout
as a lifting up and a glorifying, not as a defeat that gets reversed later.

> **The request.** *Glorify Me in Your own presence with the glory I had with You
> before the world began.* He does not ask for something new. He asks for the
> return of something He had and set down — which is what Paul describes in
> Philippians 2 as emptying Himself. The prayer assumes a life before Bethlehem.
