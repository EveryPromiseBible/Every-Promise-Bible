Three of the shortest sentences Paul ever wrote, and one of them is the shortest verse in the Greek New Testament.

## The first

*Be joyful **always**.*

The Greek is **pantote chairete** — two words.

The shortest verse in the Greek New Testament. English translations make *Jesus wept* shorter,
but in Greek this one wins.

## The second

*Never stop praying.*

The Greek is **adialeiptos proseuchesthe** — pray unceasingly.

**Adialeiptos** — without interval. The word used of a persistent cough, and of continuous pain.

Paul uses it of himself twice: *without ceasing I make mention of you in my prayers*.

## The third

*Whatever the circumstances, **give thanks**.*

The Greek is **en panti eucharisteite** — in everything give thanks.

## The preposition

**En panti** — *in* everything, not *for* everything.

Paul is not asking anyone to be grateful that a thing happened. He says to give thanks inside
whatever is happening.

Ephesians has the other construction — *giving thanks always for all things* — and the two verses
are usually read together.

## The reason

*For **this is what God wants of you in Christ Jesus**.*

The Greek is **touto gar thelema Theou en Christo Iesou eis humas**.

## What the singular refers to

*This* — **touto**, singular, after three imperatives.

The three are one thing. Joy, prayer and thanks are not a list of separate duties.

## What the letter has just said

*But concerning the times and the seasons, brethren, you have no need that I should write to you.
For you yourselves know perfectly that **the day of the Lord so comes as a thief in the night**.*

*Therefore let us not sleep, as others do, but let us watch and be sober.* See the note on
1 Thessalonians 5:4–8.

## Who is being written to

A congregation a few months old, in Macedonia, under pressure from the start.

*For you, brethren, became imitators of the churches of God which are in Judea… **for you also
suffered the same things from your own countrymen**.*

Paul had been run out of the city by a mob and had not been able to get back.

## What follows immediately

*Do not **quench the Spirit**. Do not **despise prophecies**. **Test all things; hold fast what is
good**. Abstain from every form of evil.*

The Greek is **me sbennute** — do not extinguish, as of a fire.

## The prayer at the end

*Now may the God of peace Himself sanctify you completely; and may your **whole spirit, soul, and
body** be preserved blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.*

*He who calls you is faithful, who also will do it.*

The Greek is **pistos ho kalon humas, hos kai poiesei** — faithful is the one calling you, who
also will do it.

Fifteen imperatives in the closing paragraphs, and then a sentence saying who does it.

## What Paul says from a cell

*Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice!*

*Do not be anxious about anything, but in every single thing, **by prayer and by asking, with thanks
alongside**.* See the note on Philippians 4:6–7.

The same three, in the same order, in a different letter.

> **The singular.** *This is what God wants* — **touto**, one thing, after three imperatives. Joy,
> prayer and thanks are named as a single item.
