Four short arguments for company, from the book that spends most of its time on futility.

## Where it sits

The chapter opens on oppression — *I looked again at all the acts of oppression under the
sun, and I saw the tears of the oppressed, and they had no comforter*.

And then a man Solomon describes a few verses earlier: *there is one alone, without
companion; he has neither son nor brother. Yet there is no end to all his labours… and he
never asks, "For whom do I toil?"*

This paragraph is the answer to that man.

## The claim

*Two are better than one, because they have **a good reward for their labor**.*

## The first case

*If one falls down, **his friend can lift him up**. But **pity the one who falls and has no
one to help him rise**.*

The Hebrew is *woe to him who is alone when he falls*.

Falling is assumed. The variable is who is nearby.

## The second

*And if two lie down together, **they keep each other warm**, but **how can one be warm
alone?**"*

Nights in the Judean hills get cold, and a traveller's cloak was also his blanket. Two men
sharing them survive.

## The third

*Though one may be overpowered, **two can stand their ground**.*

The Hebrew is *if one prevails against him, two shall withstand him*.

## The fourth

*And **a cord of three strands is not quickly broken**.*

The Hebrew is *the threefold cord*.

The argument has been about pairs for three verses, and the last line adds one.

Rope is stronger than the sum of its strands because of how it is laid — three twisted
together bear more than three side by side.

## The line's afterlife

It is read at weddings constantly, and the chapter is not about marriage. Ecclesiastes is
talking about work, cold nights, muggings and the man with no brother.

The application is wider than the usual use of it, not narrower.

## Where the book has come from

*Vanity of vanities, all is vanity.* Thirty-eight uses of **hevel** — vapour, breath.

And the conclusions Solomon keeps reaching: nothing new under the sun, the wise man dies
like the fool, and *I hated life*.

Into that, this.

## What else the book grants

*Go. **Eat your bread with joy**, and drink your wine with a glad heart, for **God has already
approved what you do**.* See the note on Ecclesiastes 9:7–9.

The concessions in Ecclesiastes are always concrete — bread, wine, a companion, work
you can see.

## What the book ends on

*Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: **Fear God and keep His commandments**, for
this is man's all.*

## What Jesus does with the principle

*He sent them out **two by two**.*

And: *where two or three are gathered together in My name, I am there in the midst of
them.*

> **The one the paragraph is written against.** A man with no son and no brother, working
> without end and never asking who for. Four arguments follow, and the last one adds a
> third strand to a book that had been counting to one.
