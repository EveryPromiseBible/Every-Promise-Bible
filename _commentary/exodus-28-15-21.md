The most detailed garment specification in Scripture, and every stone has a name on it.

## The name

*Now **the breastpiece of judgment**.*

The Hebrew is **choshen mishpat** — the breastpiece of judgment, or of decision.

**Mishpat** — judgment, verdict. The name comes from what was inside it: the Urim and Thummim, by
which decisions were sought. See the note on Exodus 28:30.

## The materials

*Skillfully worked like the ephod — **gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, and finely twisted
linen**.*

The same five materials as the tabernacle's inner curtains and the veil.

## The shape

*Make it **square, folded double** — a span long and a span wide.*

The Hebrew is **kaphul** — doubled.

A span is roughly nine inches. Folded double, it forms a pouch — which is what the Urim and
Thummim went into.

## The stones

Four rows of three.

**Carnelian, chrysolite, beryl.**

**Turquoise, sapphire, emerald.**

**Jacinth, agate, amethyst.**

**Topaz, onyx, jasper.**

The Hebrew names are old and several are uncertain; translations differ on almost every one.

## The setting

*Mount each in **gold filigree**.*

The Hebrew is **mishbetzot zahav** — settings of gold.

Each stone individually mounted. None of them shares a setting.

## The engraving

*Twelve stones in all, **one for each of the sons of Israel**, each **engraved like a seal with the
name of one of the twelve tribes**.*

The Hebrew is **pittuchei chotam** — engravings of a signet.

The technique for a personal seal — the deepest, most permanent cut a craftsman could make in
stone.

## What that means

Twelve different stones, twelve different names, each cut as a seal, each in its own gold mount.

No two alike, and none merged.

## Where else the names are

On the shoulder pieces: two onyx stones, six names on each, *according to their birth*.

*So Aaron shall bear their names before the LORD on his two shoulders **as a memorial**.*

Shoulders and heart. The place of strength and the place of affection.

## What it is for

*So whenever Aaron walks into the Holy Place, he **carries the names of the sons of Israel over his
heart**… **a constant remembrance before the LORD**.* See the note on Exodus 28:22–29.

The Hebrew is **lezikkaron liphnei-YHWH tamid** — for a memorial before the LORD continually.

## Who does the carrying

The priest. The tribes are not present; their names are.

They go in on somebody else's chest.

## Where the stones turn up again

*The foundations of the wall of the city were adorned with **all kinds of precious stones**: the
first foundation was jasper, the second sapphire, the third chalcedony, the fourth emerald…*

Twelve foundations, twelve stones, and *on them were the names of the twelve apostles of the
Lamb*. See the note on Revelation 21:18–21.

The same idea at the other end of the Bible: named stones, built in.

## What Malachi says

*"**They shall be Mine**," says the LORD of hosts, "**on the day that I make them My jewels**."*

The Hebrew is **segullah** — treasured possession.

> **The engraving.** **Pittuchei chotam** — cut like a signet, the deepest mark a craftsman could
> make. Twelve stones, no two alike, each in its own mount, each with a name.
