Three verses for a woman nobody else in the Bible mentions.

## The title

*There was also **a prophetess** there.*

The Greek is **prophetis**. It occurs twice in the New Testament.

Luke gives her the title before he gives her name.

## The name

**Anna** — the Greek form of **Hannah**, which means *grace* or *favour*.

The other Hannah stood in a sanctuary praying so hard the priest thought she was drunk, and
was given a son. See the note on 1 Samuel 1:9–18.

## The lineage

*The daughter of **Phanuel**, from the **tribe of Asher**.*

**Phanuel** is the Greek form of **Peniel** — *the face of God*. The place where Jacob
wrestled and said *I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved*.

**Asher** is one of the ten northern tribes, scattered by Assyria seven centuries earlier.
Luke records her tribe anyway, which means she knew it.

## The arithmetic

*She had lived with her husband **seven years** after her marriage, and then she had been **a
widow until she was eighty-four**.*

The Greek can be read two ways: a widow of eighty-four years, or a widow now aged
eighty-four.

If the first, and she married at the usual age, she is past a hundred.

## The habit

*She **never left the temple**, worshiping God **night and day with fasting and prayer**.*

The Greek is *she did not depart from the temple*.

Night first. Luke writes it in the Jewish order, in which the day begins at sunset.

Decades of it, and nothing to show anyone.

## The timing

*She came up **at that very moment**.*

The Greek is **aute te hora epistasa** — standing by at that same hour.

Simeon has just finished the song and the words to Mary. She walks in on the end of it. See
the note on Luke 2:29–32.

## What she does

*And she began to **thank God and to speak about the child**.*

Two verbs. Vertical, then horizontal.

The Greek for *speak about* is **elalei** — an imperfect. She kept on talking about Him.

## Who she talked to

*To everyone who was **still waiting for Jerusalem to be set free**.*

The Greek is **lutrosin** — redemption, ransom.

There was a group of them, and she knew who they were. Simeon was *waiting for the
Consolation of Israel*; these people were waiting for the redemption of the city.

Luke's opening chapters are full of people who have been waiting a long time: Zacharias and
Elizabeth, Simeon, Anna, and the shepherds who were simply on shift.

## What she is not given

A song. Luke records Mary's, Zacharias', the angels', and Simeon's — four canticles in two
chapters — and gives Anna none.

He records that she spoke and not what she said.

## The pairing

Luke does this repeatedly: a man and a woman, side by side, in matched scenes.

Zacharias and Mary both get an angel. Simeon and Anna both get the child. And later, a
shepherd looking for a sheep and a woman looking for a coin.

## What she had been doing for sixty years

Fasting and praying in a building, as a widow, in a society where a childless widow had
nothing.

And she was in the right room on the right morning.

> **What Luke records her doing.** Not a song. She thanked God, and then went and told the
> people who were still waiting — and he notes there was a group of them.
