Two hundred and seventy-six people come ashore, and the first thing that happens is a
bonfire.

## The island

*Once we were safe, we learned that the island was called **Melita**.*

Malta. The name may come from a Phoenician root for refuge.

**We** — Luke is there. This is one of the eyewitness sections.

## The welcome

*The people there showed us **unusual kindness**.*

The Greek is **philanthropian** — the root of *philanthropy*, love of humankind.

Luke calls them **barbaroi** — the standard Greek word for anyone who did not speak Greek.
It carried no moral weight; it meant *foreign*.

## The fire

*They **built a fire and welcomed us all**, because **it had begun to rain and it was cold**.*

Late autumn, after fourteen days at sea and a swim ashore.

## What Paul did

*Paul had **gathered an armful of sticks**, and as he laid them on the fire.*

An apostle picking up firewood, with a Roman guard, after a shipwreck.

Luke notes it because of what comes next, and it is also just what Paul did.

## The snake

*A **viper driven out by the heat fastened itself on his hand**.*

The Greek is **kathepsen** — fastened, took hold.

## What the islanders concluded

*"**This man must be a murderer. He escaped the sea, but justice will not let him live**."*

The Greek names her: **he Dike** — Justice, a goddess in Greek religion, daughter of Zeus.

Their theology is coherent. A man survives a shipwreck and is bitten within the hour;
something is clearly pursuing him.

## What Paul did

*Paul **shook the creature off into the fire and suffered no harm**.*

The Greek is **apotinaxas** — shook off.

No comment, no sermon, and no reaction recorded.

## The wait

*They kept **waiting for him to swell up or suddenly fall down dead**.*

Luke gives the two expected symptoms, and he is a doctor.

## The reversal

*But after **waiting a long time** and seeing that **nothing out of the ordinary happened to
him**, they **changed their minds and said he was a god**.*

The Greek is **metabalomenoi** — turned round.

From murderer to deity in one paragraph, on the same evidence.

## What happened before at Lystra

The crowd called Paul and Barnabas Zeus and Hermes and tried to sacrifice to them — and
minutes later stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city for dead.

Luke records both swings without comment.

## What follows

The chief man of the island, Publius, whose father was *sick of a fever and dysentery* —
Luke's terms — and Paul prays and lays hands on him and he is healed.

*So when this was done, the rest of those on the island who had diseases also came and were
healed.*

Three months on Malta.

## What Mark's ending says

*"They will take up serpents; and if they drink anything deadly, it will by no means hurt
them."* See the note on Mark 16:15–18.

Luke does not connect them. He reports what happened at a bonfire.

## Where Paul ends up

Rome, in his own rented house, *preaching the kingdom of God and teaching the things which
concern the Lord Jesus Christ with all confidence, **no one forbidding him**.*

That is the last sentence of Acts.

> **What the islanders got right and wrong.** Both times they reasoned from the same
> event. A man bitten must be guilty; a man unharmed must be divine — and in between, Paul
> shook it into the fire and went on stacking wood.
