The answer to an objection, and it is about a funeral.

## The objection

*What shall we say then? **Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound?**"*

Somebody has heard *where sin abounded, grace abounded much more* and drawn the obvious
conclusion.

*Certainly not!* — **me genoito**, the strongest negative in Greek.

## The counter-question

*How shall we who **died to sin** live any longer in it?*

The Greek is **apethanomen** — we died. Aorist.

Paul does not answer the objection with a rule. He answers it with a fact about what happened.

## The reminder

*Or **have you forgotten** that every one of us who was baptized into Christ Jesus was **baptized into
His death**?*

The Greek is **e agnoeite** — or are you ignorant?

He assumes they know it and are not reasoning from it.

## The burial

*We were **buried with Him**, then, through baptism into death.*

The Greek is **sunetaphemen oun auto** — we were buried together with Him.

The first of the letter's **sun-** compounds: buried with, crucified with, raised with, glorified
with.

## Why burial

Burial is what confirms a death.

Nobody buries someone who might be coming back. The image is chosen because it makes the death
irreversible.

## The purpose

*So that just as **the Father in all His glory raised Christ from the dead**, we too might **take up a
wholly new life and walk in it**.*

The Greek is **en kainoteti zoes peripatesomen** — in newness of life we might walk.

**Kainotes** — freshness, a new kind rather than a further instance.

## The agent

*The Father in all His glory.*

The Greek is **dia tes doxes tou Patros** — through the glory of the Father.

The resurrection is attributed to the Father's glory, and the same power is named as the source of
the new walk.

## The condition

*For **if we have been joined with Him in the likeness of His death**.*

The Greek is **sumphutoi gegonamen** — we have become grown together with.

**Sumphutos** — planted together, grafted, fused. A horticultural word for two things that have
knit into one.

## The conclusion

*We shall **certainly also be joined with Him in the likeness of His resurrection**.*

The Greek is **alla kai tes anastaseos esometha** — but also of the resurrection we shall be.

The certainty is in the grammar. The two halves cannot be separated.

## What follows

*Knowing this, that **our old man was crucified with Him**, that the body of sin might be done away
with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin.* See the note on Romans 6:6–7.

*For he who has died has been freed from sin.*

## The instruction

*Likewise you also, **reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God** in Christ
Jesus.* See the note on Romans 6:8–11.

The Greek is **logizesthe** — an accounting word. Count it as true.

## The order

Facts first, then the reckoning, then the imperative.

*Do not let sin reign in your mortal body* comes in verse 12, after three paragraphs of
indicative.

## What Paul says about his own case

*I have been crucified with Christ; **it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me**.* See the
note on Galatians 2:19–20.

> **The word.** **Sumphutos** — grown together, fused. Not a comparison between two events but a
> claim that they are one, and the resurrection half follows by grammar.
