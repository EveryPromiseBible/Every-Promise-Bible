Fourteen verses on one garment, and most of them are about how it was fastened.

## Where it sits in the book

Exodus 39 is the report of the work. Chapter 28 gave the instructions; this chapter records
that they were carried out, phrase for phrase.

*Just as the LORD had commanded Moses* appears seven times in this chapter alone.

## The materials

*Of **gold**, and of **blue, purple, and scarlet yarn**, and **finely twisted linen**.*

The same five as the tabernacle curtains and the veil. The breastpiece is made of the building's
own fabric.

## The shape

*It was **square and folded double**, **a span long and a span wide**.*

A span is roughly nine inches. Folded double makes it a pouch — which is where the Urim and
Thummim go. See the note on Exodus 28:30.

## The stones

*Four rows of precious stones.*

Twelve, named individually, three to a row. The identifications are uncertain — ancient stone
names do not map cleanly onto modern mineralogy — and translations differ.

## The engraving

*Twelve stones there were, **one for each of the names of the sons of Israel**, each **engraved
like a seal** with the name of one of the twelve tribes.*

The Hebrew is **pittuchei chotam** — engravings of a signet.

Every tribe, named. Not a symbol or a colour code. The names, cut into stone the way a personal
seal is cut.

## The fastening

Seven verses on how it stays on.

Two braided gold cords. Two filigree settings. Two rings at the top corners, two at the lower
corners, and two more low on the shoulder pieces.

*And they bound the breastpiece by its rings to the rings of the ephod **with a cord of blue**,
so it rested snug above the waistband.*

## The clause

*And **would never come loose from the ephod**.*

The Hebrew is *that it might not be loosed from the ephod*.

Chapter 28 gives the same instruction in the same words. It is stated twice, which in this
material means it matters.

The garment carrying the twelve names is engineered so that it cannot come off.

## What is on the shoulders

*Two onyx stones… **six of their names on one stone, and six names on the other stone**.*

And the reason given in chapter 28: *so Aaron shall bear their names before the LORD on his two
shoulders **as a memorial***.

Twelve names on the shoulders and twelve on the chest.

## What the two positions carry

The shoulders are where weight goes. The chest is where affection goes.

The same twelve names on both, and the priest cannot go in without them.

## Where the names turn up next

*And the wall of the city had twelve foundations, and on them were the names of the twelve
apostles of the Lamb.*

*And she had a great and high wall with twelve gates… and names written on them, which are the
names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel.*

Twelve stones on a chest, and twelve foundations in a wall.

## The stones in Revelation

*The foundations of the wall of the city were adorned with all kinds of precious stones: jasper,
sapphire, chalcedony, emerald, sardonyx, sardius, chrysolite, beryl, topaz, chrysoprase,
jacinth, amethyst.*

Twelve, listed, and the overlap with this passage is substantial.

## The last verse of the chapter

*Then Moses looked over all the work, and indeed they had done it; as the LORD had commanded, so
they had done. **And Moses blessed them**.*

> **The clause stated twice.** *That it might not be loosed.* Seven verses of rings and cords,
> to make sure the piece with the twelve names on it cannot come off the man going in.
