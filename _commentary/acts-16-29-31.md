A prison officer at midnight, with a sword already in his hand.

## What he had done that day

He was the man who took Paul and Silas after the beating, *put them into the inner
prison, and fastened their feet in the stocks.*

The inner prison is the deepest cell. The stocks were a punishment device as much
as a restraint — the legs forced apart and held.

He did that to two men whose backs had just been laid open with rods, and then went
to bed.

## What woke him

*At midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the
prisoners were listening to them.*

Then the earthquake, the doors open, everyone's chains loose.

He drew his sword to kill himself, because under Roman practice a jailer answered
with his own life for escaped prisoners. Paul stopped him: *do yourself no harm,
for we are all here.*

Nobody ran. That is the detail that brings him in.

## The question

*Trembling, he **fell down before Paul and Silas**… "Sirs, **what must I do to be
saved?**"*

He is on the floor in front of two men he had put in the stocks a few hours
earlier.

## The answer

*"**Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved** — you and your
household."*

Eight words to the question. No conditions, no probation, no list.

Not *make restitution*. Not *release us first*. Not *stop being a jailer*.

## "And your household"

The same phrase used over Lydia at the start of the chapter. See the note on
Acts 16:14–15.

The next verse says they *spoke the word of the Lord to him and to all who were in
his house* — the household hears it too, and then *he and all his family were
baptised immediately*.

## What he did before the night was over

*He took them the same hour of the night and **washed their stripes**… and he set
food before them, and **rejoiced, believing in God with all his household**.*

He washed the wounds he had caused, in the dark, and then fed them at his own
table.

Luke puts the washing and the baptism in the same sentence: *he washed their
stripes, and immediately he and all his family were baptised.* Water both ways, in
the same hour.

## Two households, one chapter

A wealthy purple merchant at a riverside prayer meeting, and a Roman jailer at
midnight. The top and the bottom of that city, in one chapter, and both times Luke
says *and her household*, *and his household*.

> **What Philippi cost them.** Beaten with rods, illegally — they were Roman
> citizens, and Paul says so the next morning. The magistrates who ordered it come
> down in person to ask them to leave quietly. Paul goes back to Lydia's house
> first.
