One sentence, and the first thing he does with the answer is stop and sing.

## What is at stake

Nebuchadnezzar has dreamed something and demanded that his wise men tell him both the dream
and its meaning.

They protest that no king has ever asked such a thing, *and there is no other who can tell
it to the king except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh*.

The king orders every wise man in Babylon killed. Daniel and his three friends are on the
list.

## What Daniel did first

*Then Daniel went in and asked the king **to give him time**, that he might tell the king
the interpretation.*

He asks for an extension on a death sentence, from a king who has just executed his own
advisers for stalling.

## Who he told

*Then Daniel went to his house, and **made the decision known to Hananiah, Mishael, and
Azariah, his companions**, that they might **seek mercies from the God of heaven concerning
this secret**.*

He does not pray alone. He goes and gets three men.

Their Babylonian names are the famous ones — Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego — and here
they are used as their own.

## The word

*And **in the night, in a vision, the mystery was opened to Daniel**.*

The Aramaic is **raz** — a secret, a mystery. It is a Persian loan-word, and it is the term
the whole chapter turns on.

**Opened** — the Aramaic is *revealed*, uncovered.

## The timing

*In the night.*

They had prayed in the evening and he was asleep.

## What he did next

*Then Daniel **blessed the God of heaven**.*

Before he goes to Arioch, before he goes to the king, before he tells his three friends,
and before anyone is saved from execution.

The soonest possible action would have been to run.

## What the blessing says

Verses 20 to 23, and it is one of the finest prayers in the Old Testament:

*"Blessed be the name of God forever and ever, for **wisdom and might are His**. And **He
changes the times and the seasons; He removes kings and raises up kings**. He gives wisdom
to the wise and knowledge to those who have understanding."*

*"**He reveals deep and secret things; He knows what is in the darkness, and light dwells
with Him**."*

## What he says about himself in it

*"I thank You and praise You, O God of my fathers; **You have given me wisdom and might, and
have now made known to me what we asked of You**."*

**What we asked** — plural. He credits the three men who prayed with him.

## What he says to Arioch

*"**Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon**; take me before the king."*

The first use he makes of it is to stop the execution of the astrologers who could not do
it.

## What he says to the king

*"**This mystery was not opened to me because I have more wisdom than anyone living**."* See
the note on Daniel 2:29–30.

## What the dream was

A statue of gold, silver, bronze, iron and clay, struck by a stone *cut out without hands*
which became a mountain and filled the whole earth.

> **The order of the paragraph.** The mystery is revealed in the night, and the next
> recorded act is a blessing — before the king is told, before anyone is saved, and before
> the man is out of danger himself.
