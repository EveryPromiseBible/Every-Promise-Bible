The elders' pay, and it is deferred.

## The title

*When **the Shepherd over all the shepherds** shows up.*

The Greek is **archipoimenos** — chief shepherd. It occurs once in the New Testament, and
Peter coined it or close to it; it turns up in a papyrus as a job title for the head herdsman
of an estate.

The elders are shepherds. Above them is one more.

## The three shepherd titles

*I am **the good shepherd**. The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep.* See the note on
John 10:11–15.

*The **great Shepherd** of the sheep*, in Hebrews, *brought up from the dead*.

*The **Chief Shepherd***, here, *when He appears*.

Good, at the cross. Great, at the resurrection. Chief, at the return.

## The appearing

The Greek is **phanerothentos** — when He is manifested, brought into the open.

The same verb John uses twice in one sentence: *when He does come out into the open, we will
be like Him*. See the note on 1 John 3:2–3.

## The word for crown

*You will be handed **the wreath of glory**.*

The Greek is **stephanon** — the victor's wreath at the games, or the garland at a feast. Not
**diadema**, the royal crown.

It is the winner's wreath, made of wild olive or laurel, and it was worth nothing except as
proof.

## The adjective

*The one that **never wilts**.*

The Greek is **amarantinon** — unfading. From the flower **amaranth**, which was proverbial in
the ancient world for not withering.

An athlete's wreath was dead within days. Peter picks the one flower that does not do that.

He uses the same root earlier in the letter, of the inheritance: *incorruptible and undefiled
and that **does not fade away**, reserved in heaven for you*. See the note on
1 Peter 1:23.

## What Paul says about the same wreath

*And everyone who competes for the prize is temperate in all things. Now they do it to obtain
**a perishable crown**, but we for an imperishable crown.*

And at the end: *there is laid up for me the **crown of righteousness**, which the Lord, the
righteous Judge, will give to me on that Day*. See the note on 2 Timothy 4:6–8.

## What the timing does

Peter has just told elders not to serve for money. See the note on 1 Peter 5:2.

The payment named in verse 4 is one nobody collects in this life, which is the point of naming
it immediately after the warning about shameful gain.

## What the elders were facing

The letter is written to churches under pressure. *Beloved, do not think it strange concerning
the fiery trial which is to try you.*

And the paragraph ends: *may the God of all grace, who called us to His eternal glory by Christ
Jesus, **after you have suffered a while, perfect, establish, strengthen, and settle you**.*

Four verbs, all of them things done to a person.

## Where the wreaths end up

*The twenty-four elders fall down before Him who sits on the throne… and **cast their crowns
before the throne**, saying, "You are worthy, O Lord."*

The same word — **stephanous**.

Elders, and crowns, and they put them on the floor.

> **The word Peter picked.** *Amarantinos*, from the flower that does not wither — said to men
> who had just been told not to do the job for money.
