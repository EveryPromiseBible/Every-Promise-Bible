The law has just been spoken, and the first recorded response of the nation is to back
away.

## What they experienced

*When the people saw **the thunder and lightning**, heard **the trumpet**, and saw **the
mountain wrapped in smoke**.*

The Hebrew is *they saw the voices* — the same word for thunder and for voice. They saw
the sound.

## The reaction

*They **trembled and stood far off**.*

The Hebrew is *they moved and stood at a distance*.

Two chapters earlier they had been told to wash their clothes and be ready on the third
day. They came, and then went backward.

## The request

*"**You speak to us, and we will listen. But don't let God speak to us, or we will
die**."*

They ask for a middleman.

This is the origin of the office of the prophet — Moses recalls it in Deuteronomy, and
God answers it there: *I will raise up for them a Prophet like you from among their
brethren, and will put My words in His mouth.*

## What they had just heard

The ten commandments, spoken aloud by God to the whole nation. It is the only time in
Scripture He addresses an entire people directly.

They asked Him not to do it again.

## Moses' answer

*"**Don't be afraid**. God has come **to test you**, so that **the fear of him will keep
you from sinning**."*

Two kinds of fear in one sentence, and Moses distinguishes them: do not be afraid, so
that the fear of Him would stay with you.

The Hebrew uses the same root twice — *do not fear*, *that His fear may be before your
faces*.

## The two directions

*So **the people stayed at a distance**, while **Moses drew near** to the thick darkness
where God was.*

One sentence, two movements, opposite ways.

The Hebrew for *thick darkness* is **araphel** — heavy cloud, gloom. It is the word used
of the cloud on Sinai and of the darkness God is said to dwell in: *He made darkness His
secret place.*

## What Moses walked into

The thing the nation was certain would kill them.

## Where Hebrews takes it

*You have not come to the mountain that may be touched and that burned with fire, and to
blackness and darkness and tempest… **For they could not endure what was commanded**.*

*But you have come to Mount Zion… to God the Judge of all, and to Jesus the Mediator of
the new covenant.*

Two mountains, and the letter says which one its readers are standing at. See the note on
Hebrews 12:18–24.

## What the veil later did

The tabernacle formalised the distance the people asked for here — a curtain, a court, a
priest, one man once a year.

And Matthew records the curtain torn from top to bottom.

> **What was on offer and what was asked for.** God spoke to the whole nation once, and
> the nation asked Him to stop and send somebody instead. Moses went in.
