The only book in the Bible that opens as a complaint to God and never stops being a
conversation with Him.

## The heading

*This is **the burden Habakkuk the prophet carried, the vision he saw**.*

The Hebrew is **massa** — a burden, a load, and the standard term for a prophetic oracle.

**The vision he saw** — **chazon**, the word Proverbs uses: *where there is no vision the
people cast off restraint*. See the note on Proverbs 29:14–18.

The book calls its own contents a vision, and the vision starts with a question.

## Who he is speaking to

Not the nation. Most prophets deliver a message from God to Israel; Habakkuk opens by
addressing God about Israel.

## The first question

*"**How long, LORD, must I cry for help before You hear me?**"*

The Hebrew is *how long shall I cry, and You will not hear*.

## The second

*"**How long must I call to You over the violence, and still You do not save?**"*

The Hebrew for *violence* is **chamas** — brutality, wrong done to a person.

## The accusation

*"**Why do You make me stare at wrong? Why do You let me watch such trouble?**"*

The Hebrew is *why do You show me iniquity and cause me to see trouble*.

He is not asking why it exists. He is asking why he has to look at it.

## What he sees

*"**Ruin and cruelty are in front of me**. Everywhere there is fighting, and **the quarreling
never stops**."*

## The legal collapse

*"**The law goes limp. Justice never wins**."*

The Hebrew for *goes limp* is **pug** — to be numbed, chilled, ineffective.

*"The wicked crowd around the good and **hem them in**, so that when a verdict comes, **it
comes out crooked**."*

The courts are running and the outcomes are wrong.

## What is unusual about the book

The complaint is preserved. Nobody edits it out.

Habakkuk is the only prophet who argues with God through a whole book and is answered
twice.

## What God answers

*"Look among the nations and watch — be utterly astounded! For I will work a work in your
days which you would not believe, though it were told you. **For indeed I am raising up the
Chaldeans**."*

The answer makes the problem worse. Judah's corruption will be dealt with by Babylon — a
nation Habakkuk regards as far worse.

## The second complaint

*"You are of purer eyes than to behold evil… **why do You look on those who deal
treacherously, and hold Your tongue when the wicked devours a person more righteous than
he?**"*

He goes back with a harder question.

## What he does next

*I will take my stand at my watchpost.* See the note on Habakkuk 2:1.

He stops arguing and waits.

## The answer that comes

*Write the vision… **though it tarries, wait for it; because it will surely come**.* See the
note on Habakkuk 2:2–3.

And in the middle of it: *the just shall live by his faith* — the sentence Paul quotes
twice and Hebrews once.

## How the book ends

*Though the fig tree may not blossom, nor fruit be on the vines; though the labour of the
olive may fail, and the fields yield no food; though the flock be cut off from the fold, and
there be no herd in the stalls — **yet I will rejoice in the LORD**.*

Six failures listed, and then that.

He never gets the answer he asked for.

> **What the book preserves.** The complaint, unedited. Habakkuk asks how long twice, why
> twice, and accuses God of making him watch — and it is filed under *the vision he saw*.
