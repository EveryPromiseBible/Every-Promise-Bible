Paul ends a chapter about factions by giving the factions' leaders to the church.

## The warning

*Let **no one fool himself**.*

The Greek is **medeis heauton exapatato** — let nobody thoroughly deceive himself.

## The instruction

*If anyone among you thinks he is wise **in the way this age counts wisdom**, let him **become a fool,
so that he may become truly wise**.*

The Greek is **moros genestho hina genetai sophos**.

The same paradox as chapter 1: *God has chosen the foolish things of the world to put to shame the
wise*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 1:26–31.

## The verdict

*For **the wisdom of this world is foolishness in God's sight**.*

## The first quotation

*"**He catches the wise in their own craftiness**."*

Job 5:13 — from Eliphaz, one of the friends God later rebukes.

Paul quotes him anyway. The line is true even in the mouth of a man who applied it badly.

## The second

*"**The Lord knows the thoughts of the wise, that they are empty**."*

Psalm 94:11, with *the wise* substituted for *man*.

## The conclusion

*So then, **let no one boast in men**.*

The Greek is **meden kauchastho en anthropois**.

This is the answer to the whole problem of the letter's first chapters: *I am of Paul, I am of
Apollos, I am of Cephas*. See the note on 1 Corinthians 1:10–13.

## The reversal

*For **all things are yours**.*

The Greek is **panta gar humon estin**.

## The list

*Whether **Paul or Apollos or Cephas**.*

The three names the factions had formed around.

They belong to the church, not the reverse. The Corinthians had been dividing over which teacher
they owned; Paul says they own all three.

## The extension

*Whether **the world or life or death**, whether **things present or things to come**.*

Six more items, in three pairs.

The list starts with three men and expands to include death.

## The repetition

*All of them are yours.*

Said twice in two verses.

## The chain

*And **you are Christ's, and Christ is God's**.*

The Greek is **humeis de Christou, Christos de Theou**.

Three links, and the direction reverses at the first one.

Everything belongs to them; they belong to Christ; Christ belongs to God.

## Why the chain matters

The ownership only works because of the last two clauses.

A church that owns everything and belongs to nobody is the problem the chapter started with.

## What has come before in the chapter

*I planted, Apollos watered, **but God gave the increase**.* See the note on
1 Corinthians 3:5–9.

*Each one's work will become clear… **If anyone's work is burned, he will suffer loss; but he
himself will be saved**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 3:10–15.

*Do you not know that **you are the temple of God**?* See the note on
1 Corinthians 3:16–17.

## What Paul says elsewhere in the same shape

*He who did not spare His own Son… **how shall He not with Him also freely give us all things?***
See the note on Romans 8:31–34.

> **The reversal.** The Corinthians were arguing about which teacher they belonged to. Paul tells
> them the teachers belong to them — and then names who they belong to.
