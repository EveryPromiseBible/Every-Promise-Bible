The most consequential meeting in church history, and Peter settles it with two sentences.

## What is being argued

*Certain men came down from Judea and taught the brethren, "**Unless you are circumcised
according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved**."*

The question is whether Gentile believers have to become Jews.

Paul and Barnabas had *no small dissension and dispute with them*, and the matter went to
Jerusalem.

## The debate

*And when there had been much dispute, Peter rose up.*

He reminds them of Cornelius' house: *God, who knows the heart, acknowledged them by giving them
the Holy Spirit, just as He did to us, and made no distinction between us and them, **purifying
their hearts by faith***. See the note on Acts 10:44–48.

He is arguing from something that happened to him and that he had not planned.

## The question

*"So why are you now **testing God** by laying a yoke on the neck of the disciples."*

The Greek is **peirazete** — putting God to the test.

## The yoke

*"That **neither our fathers nor we have been able to carry**."*

The Greek is **ischusamen bastasai** — were strong enough to bear.

Peter says it about his own nation and his own generation, in Jerusalem, to the Jerusalem
church.

Not that the law is bad. That nobody has ever carried it.

Paul says the same to the Galatians: *stand fast therefore in the liberty by which Christ has
made us free, and do not be entangled again with a yoke of bondage*.

And the alternative: *take My yoke upon you… for My yoke is easy and My burden is light*. See
the note on Matthew 11:28–30.

## The sentence

*"No — we believe that **we shall be saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, even as
they**."*

The Greek is **kath' hon tropon kakeinoi** — in the same manner as they also.

The direction is the striking part. The argument in the room was whether Gentiles could be
saved like Jews. Peter reverses it: we are saved the way they are.

## The silence

*Then **the whole assembly fell silent**.*

The Greek is **esigesen** — became silent.

An argumentative meeting, and it stops.

## The reports

*And they listened to **Barnabas and Paul telling all the signs and wonders God had done through
them among the Gentiles**.*

Evidence, not argument. Two men describing what had happened on the first missionary journey.

Luke names Barnabas first here, which he does when they are in Jerusalem.

## What James does with it

Quotes Amos. *After this I will return and rebuild the fallen tent of David… so that the rest of
humanity may seek the Lord.* See the note on Acts 15:13–18.

## The decision

*"Therefore I judge that **we should not trouble those from among the Gentiles who are turning
to God**."*

Four things are asked of them — abstain from things offered to idols, from blood, from things
strangled, and from sexual immorality — and circumcision is not one of them.

## The letter

*"It seemed good to the Holy Spirit, and to us, **to lay upon you no greater burden than these
necessary things**."*

And the response in Antioch: *when they had read it, **they rejoiced over its encouragement**.*

## What was at stake

If the meeting had gone the other way, the church would have remained a sect within Judaism and
every Gentile convert would have had to become a proselyte first.

Peter's contribution is the sentence about the yoke, and it is an argument from experience: our
fathers could not carry it either.

> **Which way round he puts it.** The room was asking whether Gentiles could be saved like Jews.
> Peter says *we shall be saved… even as they*.
