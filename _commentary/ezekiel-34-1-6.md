The most sustained attack on bad leadership in the Bible, and it is delivered as a job
description.

## Who is addressed

*"Son of man, **speak against the shepherds of Israel**."*

Shepherd is the standard ancient title for a king, used across the whole Near East. Ezekiel
means the rulers, and the priests with them.

He is writing from Babylon, after the exile has happened. The men he is indicting have
already lost the country.

## The charge

*"How terrible for the shepherds of Israel who **only feed themselves**. **Should not
shepherds feed the flock?**"*

The Hebrew is *woe to the shepherds who are feeding themselves*.

The question in the second line is rhetorical and it is the whole indictment.

## The three products

*"You eat **the curds**, you clothe yourselves with **the wool**, you slaughter **the choice
animals**, but you do not feed the flock."*

Milk, wool, meat. Everything a flock produces, going one direction.

They are not accused of stealing the sheep. They are accused of taking what the sheep make
and giving nothing back.

## The five failures

*"You have **not strengthened the weak** or **healed the sick** or **bound up the injured**.
You have **not brought back the strays** or **searched for the lost**."*

Five, and every one is a negative. The list is what a shepherd is for.

**Healed the sick** — the Hebrew is *the sick you have not healed*. It is put in the middle
of a list of things God expected of them, which means it was in their remit.

## The manner

*"You have **ruled them harshly and without gentleness**."*

The Hebrew is *with force and with cruelty* — **chozqah** and **perek**.

**Perek** is a rare word, and its other main use is Exodus: the Egyptians made the
Israelites serve *with rigour*. Ezekiel puts Israel's own leaders in the Egyptians' role.

## The result

*"**So My sheep were scattered, for they had no shepherd**."*

The Hebrew makes it a consequence. Not persecution, not invasion — the scattering is caused
by the men who were supposed to prevent it.

*"And scattered, they became **food for every wild beast**."*

## The extent

*"My sheep **wandered over all the mountains and every high hill**. They were scattered
over the whole face of the earth, and **no one searched for them. No one went looking**."*

The last two sentences are the same statement twice. Ezekiel will not let it go.

## What God says He will do

*"**I Myself will search for My sheep and seek them out**… I will deliver them from all the
places where they were scattered on a cloudy and dark day."*

*"**I will feed My flock, and I will make them lie down**," says the Lord GOD. "**I will
seek what was lost and bring back what was driven away, bind up the broken and strengthen
what was sick**."*

Point by point, He takes over the list they failed.

## The shepherd He appoints

*"I will establish **one shepherd** over them, and he shall feed them — My servant David.
He shall feed them and be their shepherd."*

David has been dead four hundred years when Ezekiel writes this.

## What Jesus does with it

*"**I am the good shepherd**. The good shepherd gives His life for the sheep. But a hireling
— he sees the wolf coming and leaves the sheep and flees."* See the note on
John 10:11–15.

He takes Ezekiel's contrast and stands on the right side of it.

And the parable: *what man of you, having a hundred sheep, if he loses one, does not leave
the ninety-nine and **go after the one which is lost until he finds it**?* See the note on
Luke 15:3–7.

*No one went looking*, in Ezekiel. In Luke, someone goes.

## What Matthew saw

*When He saw the multitudes, He was moved with compassion for them, because they were
**weary and scattered, like sheep having no shepherd**.* See the note on Matthew 9:35–38.

Ezekiel's exact diagnosis, six centuries later, on the same ground.

> **What the list assumes.** *You have not healed the sick.* It is filed among the charges,
> which means He had expected it of them.
