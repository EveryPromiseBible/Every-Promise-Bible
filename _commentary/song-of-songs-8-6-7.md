The high point of the Song, and the only place in the book where the name of God
appears.

## The seal

*Set me as a seal upon your heart, as a seal upon your arm.*

A seal was a personal signet — pressed into wax to mark what belonged to you, and
worn on a cord at the chest or as a ring on the hand. It was identity and
ownership in one object.

Two placements: the **heart**, where affection is, and the **arm**, where
strength acts. She is asking to be stamped on both.

## "The very flame of the LORD"

*Its flashes are flashes of fire, the very flame of the LORD.*

The Hebrew is *shalhevetyah* — a flame with the shortened divine name attached to
the end. It is the only occurrence of the name in the book, and it turns up here,
in a description of what love is like.

The claim being made is not that love is intense. It is that this fire comes from
God's own.

## The two things that cannot touch it

- *Many waters cannot quench love, neither can the floods drown it* — the deep
  waters are the Bible's standing image for chaos and death. They cannot put this
  out.
- *If a man offered all the wealth of his house for love, it would be utterly
  scorned* — and it cannot be bought either.

Strong as death, unquenchable by the flood, not for sale. Three of the largest
forces anyone knows, and love outlasts all three.

> **How the church has read it.** The Song is a love poem and it is not
> embarrassed about that. It has also been read from the earliest times as
> describing God's love for His people — and this passage is why: language this
> absolute, with His own name burning at the centre of it, is doing more than
> describing an engagement.
