A man with a withered hand, a room full of men watching to see what Jesus will
do, and an instruction to do the one thing he cannot.

## What they were there for

*Looking for something to accuse Him of, they asked Him, "Is it permitted to heal
on the Sabbath?"*

Matthew tells us the motive before he tells us the question. Nobody in the room
is asking about the man.

## The sheep

*"Suppose one of you has a single sheep, and it falls into a pit on the Sabbath.
Will you not take hold of it and lift it out?"*

He answers with their own practice. Not a theory about the Sabbath — what they
would in fact do, that same day, for one animal.

*"And a human being is worth so much more than a sheep."*

The argument is from the lesser to the greater, and the whole weight of it sits
on the value of the man standing in front of them.

## The command

*"Stretch out your hand."*

The hand is withered. Stretching it out is exactly what it cannot do — that is
the definition of the condition.

Nothing is offered first. No touch is recorded, no word over the hand. He is told
to do the impossible thing, and Matthew's next clause is *He stretched it out.*

## "As sound as the other one"

The restoration is measured against something. There was a good hand on the other
arm the whole time, and the healed one comes back matching it.

## What the healing produced

*But the Pharisees went out and began making plans against Him, to find a way to
destroy Him.*

Matthew puts the plotting immediately after the healing, without a gap. The first
recorded plan to kill Jesus in this Gospel follows a restored hand.

> **What He said before the healing.** *So yes, it is permitted to do good on the
> Sabbath.* He does not argue that the Sabbath is suspended. He rules on what it
> was for.
