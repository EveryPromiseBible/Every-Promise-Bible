The throne room, described by a man who has been told to write down what he sees.

## What is happening

*After these things I looked, and behold, **a door standing open in heaven**… "Come up here, and
I will show you things which must take place after this."*

*Immediately I was in the Spirit; and behold, **a throne set in heaven, and One sat on the
throne**.*

The whole chapter is furniture, and everything in it is arranged around one seat.

## The noise

*The throne itself was **throwing off lightning, and thunder rolling out of it, and voices**.*

The same three at Sinai: *there were thunderings and lightnings, and a thick cloud on the
mountain*. See the note on Exodus 19:16–19.

Revelation repeats the trio at intervals through the book, each time something significant
opens.

## The seven

*Seven fires were burning in front of it, seven lamps, and those are **the seven Spirits of
God**.*

The Greek is **hepta lampades puros**.

The lampstand in the tabernacle had seven lamps and burned continuously. See the note on
Exodus 25:31–40.

Zechariah saw one too, and asked what it meant: *not by might nor by power, but by My Spirit*.

John identifies them outright rather than leaving them symbolic.

## The sea

*Spread out before the throne was something like **a sea made of glass, clear all the way
through**, the way crystal is.*

The Greek is **thalassa hualine** — a glassy sea.

In the tabernacle there was a laver of bronze between the altar and the tent, for the priests
to wash. In the temple it was enlarged into *the Sea* — a cast basin holding thousands of
gallons on twelve bronze oxen.

Here it is not water. It is glass, and it is still.

Nobody is washing in it.

## What the calm means

The sea in Hebrew imagery is chaos. The deep, the monsters in it, the storm the disciples were
in.

Isaiah: *the wicked are like the troubled sea, when it cannot rest*.

A sea of glass is that, motionless.

## The creatures

*And at the center of the throne, and all around it, were **four creatures**, and there were
**eyes all over them**, on the front of them and on the back.*

The Greek is **zoa** — living ones. Not *beasts*, which the old versions have; **therion** is
the word for a beast, and Revelation uses that one for something else entirely.

## What they look like

The next verse: *the first living creature was like **a lion**, the second like **a calf**, the
third had a face like **a man**, and the fourth was like **a flying eagle***.

The same four faces as Ezekiel's creatures by the river Chebar. See the note on
Ezekiel 1:10–11.

And the same four as the standards of the camp in Numbers, by the ancient tradition — Judah,
Ephraim, Reuben and Dan, three tribes to a side, all facing inward. See the note on
Numbers 2:1–2.

## The eyes

Front and back, and covered.

Whatever else they are, they are described as things that see in every direction and do not
stop.

## What they say

*And they **do not rest day or night**, saying: "**Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, who was
and is and is to come!**"*

Isaiah heard the same three words in the temple, from seraphim, in the year King Uzziah died.

## Who calls the riders

One creature per seal, for the first four. See the note on Revelation 6:1–2.

The four faces round the throne are the four voices that summon the four horses.

## The other sea, in Solomon's temple

There was already a sea in front of God's house, and it was not made of glass.

*And he made **the Sea** of cast bronze, ten cubits from one brim to the other… **the priests were to
wash in it**.*

An enormous bronze basin standing in the temple court, holding thousands of gallons, and its whole
purpose was that nobody serving there could stay clean. It had to be there because the washing never
stopped.

In the throne room John sees a sea that is solid. Nothing is moving in it and nobody is washing.

That is a picture of a finished cleansing rather than a suspended one: *not by works of
righteousness which we have done, but according to His mercy He saved us, through **the washing of
regeneration**.* And the church is presented *having cleansed her with the washing of water by the
word*.

The basin is glass because the work it existed for was finished by Jesus Christ.

> **What the water is doing.** Nothing. In the tabernacle it was a basin for washing; here it is
> glass, motionless, and the sea in Hebrew imagery is what will not be still.
