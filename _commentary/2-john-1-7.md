title: "Came in a body of flesh"

The reason for the letter, and the test it applies is about a body.

## The charge

*"**Plenty of frauds have gone out into the world, people who will not admit that Jesus Christ came
in a body of flesh.**"*

The Greek is **erchomenon en sarki** — coming in flesh, and the participle is present, not past.

The people **John** is writing against were not denying that a man called **Jesus** existed. They
were denying that God had actually got into a body — that the divine could be that involved with
matter, blood, digestion and death.

## Why it is the test

*"**By this you know the Spirit of God: every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in
the flesh is of God.**"*

**1 John** 4, and **John** makes it the single diagnostic. Not sincerity, not power, not results.

*And **the Word became flesh** and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory.*

**John** 1:14, and **sarx** is the word — the crude one, the meat word. He could have written
**soma**, body, and did not.

## What depends on it

Everything the New Testament says about the cross.

*Inasmuch then as the children have partaken of flesh and blood, **He Himself likewise shared in the
same**, that through death He might destroy him who had the power of death.*

**Hebrews** 2, and the logic is a chain: to die He had to be mortal, and to be mortal He had to be
flesh. A God who only appeared to be human only appeared to save anybody.

*For **there is one God and one Mediator between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus**.*

*Who Himself bore our sins **in His own body** on the tree.*

*For we do not have a High Priest who cannot sympathise with our weaknesses, but One who **was in
all points tempted as we are**, yet without sin.*

## What John keeps saying he touched

*That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, **which we have seen with our eyes**, which
we have looked upon, and **our hands have handled**, concerning the Word of life.*

Four verbs of sense in one sentence, at the top of his first letter. He is not being poetic. He is
filing testimony.

*"**Behold My hands and My feet, that it is I Myself. Handle Me and see**, for a spirit does not have
flesh and bones as you see I have."*

**Luke** 24, and the risen **Christ** makes the same argument with the same word.

## What John calls them

*"**Anyone like that is the deceiver and the antichrist.**"*

The Greek is **ho planos kai ho antichristos** — the deceiver and the antichrist, with the article
both times.

**John** is the only writer in the New Testament to use the word **antichristos**, and he uses it four
times in these two short letters. He never attaches it to a political figure. Every occurrence is
about this doctrine.

> **The single diagnostic.** Not sincerity, not power — whether the confession says He came in
> **sarx**. And John's grounds for insisting are in his first sentence: *our hands have handled*.
