The famous giving verse has three sentences in front of it, and they are all about
something else.

## What comes first

*"**Do not sit in judgment** on people, and you will not be judged. **Do not hand
down sentences**, and no sentence will be handed down on you. **Forgive**, and you
will be forgiven."*

Three clauses, and only then: *give, and it will be given back to you.*

The passage is a run of four, and the first three are about how you treat people.
Giving is the fourth item on a list about generosity of judgement.

## The measure

*"**A full measure, pressed down, shaken together, spilling over the top**, poured
right into the fold of your coat."*

Four stages, and they describe a grain merchant filling a container properly:

- **a full measure** — filled to the brim
- **pressed down** — compacted so the air is out
- **shaken together** — settled so it packs tighter
- **spilling over the top** — and then still more, until it runs over

An honest dealer did the first. A generous one did all four.

## Where it goes

*Into **the fold of your coat**.*

The outer garment was belted, and the fabric above the belt formed a deep pouch —
the way a man carried grain home when he had no sack.

It is a picture of somebody being given more than they came prepared to receive.

## The rule underneath it

*"**Whatever measure you use for others is the measure that will be used for
you.**"*

That is the sentence the whole passage runs on, and it applies to all four items —
judgement, condemnation, forgiveness and giving alike.

The measure is not set by God's generosity, in this saying. It is set by yours, and
then matched.

## Who "they" are

*"It will be given back to you"* — the Greek is *they will give*, an impersonal
plural. Luke does not name the giver.

It is the same construction as *they will pour into your lap*. Something comes back,
and the source is left unstated.

## Where it sits in the sermon

This is Luke's Sermon on the Plain, and the paragraph before it is *love your
enemies, do good, and lend, hoping for nothing in return… for He is kind to the
unthankful and evil.*

*Be merciful, just as your Father also is merciful.*

That is the model given, and then these four instructions.

> **What the passage is not.** A transaction. The three clauses in front of it —
> judging, condemning, forgiving — cannot be done for a return without becoming
> something else entirely, and the giving is fourth in the same list.
