One of two churches in the seven that gets no rebuke.

## The city

**Philadelphia** — in Lydia, founded as a deliberate outpost to spread Greek culture eastward.
A gateway city on the road inland.

It sat on a fault line and had been flattened by earthquakes more than once, so that people
lived outside the walls, expecting the next one.

## The titles

*The One who is **holy**, the One who is **true**.*

**Hagios** and **alethinos** — holy, and genuine as opposed to counterfeit.

Both are Old Testament titles for God, used here of Christ without comment.

## The key

*The One holding **David's key** — when He **opens a thing, nobody shuts it**, and when He
**shuts a thing, nobody opens it**.*

Isaiah 22, about Eliakim replacing Shebna as steward of the palace:

*The key of the house of David I will lay on his shoulder; so he shall open, and no one shall
shut; and he shall shut, and no one shall open.*

A steward's key. The man who controlled access to the king.

## What He knows

*"**I know what you have been doing**."*

The formula for all seven letters — *I know your works*. To Ephesus, to Smyrna, to Sardis, to
Laodicea.

Praise and rebuke both start from the same clause.

## The door

*"Look — **I have put a door in front of you and it stands open**, and **there is no one who
can shut it**."*

The Greek is a perfect participle — **eneogmenen**, having been opened and standing open.

Paul uses the same image of opportunity: *a great and effective door has opened to me, and
there are many adversaries*.

## The admission

*"**There is not much strength in you**."*

The Greek is **mikran echeis dunamin** — you have little power.

Said as a fact, not a criticism. The letter has no rebuke in it anywhere.

The open door and the small strength are in consecutive clauses, and the door does not depend
on the strength.

## What they did

*"And still you **kept My word and never disowned My name**."*

Two things, both of them about holding rather than achieving.

## What follows

*"Indeed I will make those of the synagogue of Satan… come and worship before your feet, and
**to know that I have loved you**."*

*"Because you have kept My command to persevere, **I also will keep you from the hour of trial
which shall come upon the whole world**."*

## The promise about the pillar

*"He who overcomes, I will make him **a pillar in the temple of My God, and he shall go out no
more**."*

Said to a congregation in an earthquake city where people slept outside because buildings
fell down.

*And I will write on him the name of My God, and the name of the city of My God, the New
Jerusalem… and My new name.*

Philadelphia had been renamed twice in its history to flatter Roman emperors.

## The other church without rebuke

Smyrna — poor, slandered, and about to be imprisoned. *I know your works, tribulation, and
poverty (but you are rich).*

The two churches Christ finds nothing against are the weak one and the poor one.

## The seventh letter

Laodicea, forty miles away: *I am rich, have become wealthy, and have need of nothing* — and
He is outside the door knocking. See the note on Revelation 3:14–15.

Two doors in the same chapter. One is open in front of a church with little strength, and one
has Christ on the wrong side of it.

> **What the door does not depend on.** He names the open door and their small strength in
> consecutive sentences, and puts *nobody can shut it* between them.
