The last instruction in Mark, and it has no limits on it anywhere.

## The scope

*"Go out into **the whole world**, and tell the good news to **every person
alive**."*

The Greek is *to all creation*. Matthew's version has *all the nations*; Mark's is
wider still.

Nobody is excluded by geography, nation, or condition.

## Who He said it to

Eleven men who had run. The chapter has just recorded Him rebuking them for
unbelief and hardness of heart because they would not believe the ones who had
seen Him.

He rebukes them, and in the next sentence hands them the world.

## The message

*"Whoever **believes and is baptised** will be saved; whoever **refuses to
believe** stands condemned."*

The two halves are not symmetrical. Believing and baptism on one side; on the other
He does not say *whoever is not baptised* — only *whoever refuses to believe*.

The dividing line in the sentence is belief.

## The signs

*"And these signs will **go along with** the ones who believe."*

The Greek is to follow after, to accompany. The signs follow the believers; the
believers do not chase the signs.

Five are listed:

- *in My name they will **drive out demons***
- *they will **speak in new tongues***
- *if they **pick up snakes** or **drink anything deadly**, it will do them no
  harm*
- *they will **lay their hands on the sick**, and the sick will get well*

## The two that are not commands

Nothing here tells anyone to handle a snake or drink poison. Both are described as
things that will not harm, not as things to attempt.

Paul is bitten by a viper on Malta, shakes it off into the fire, and the islanders
wait for him to swell up and die. He had not gone looking for it — he was picking
up firewood.

## "In My name"

The authority is stated once and covers the list. Not in their own name, and not by
any technique.

## What the last verse of Mark says happened

*And they went out and preached everywhere, **the Lord working with them** and
confirming the word through the accompanying signs.*

Mark ends with the two parties named: they preached, and He worked with them.

## What is actually commanded

*"Go into all the world and **preach the gospel** to every creature."*

**Kerussate to euangelion** — herald the good news.

The one imperative in the sentence is about announcing something that has already happened. A
herald does not negotiate terms; he reads out what the king has done.

*Moreover, brethren, I declare to you **the gospel** which I preached to you… that **Christ died for
our sins according to the Scriptures, and that He was buried, and that He rose again the third day
according to the Scriptures**.*

**Paul** in **1 Corinthians** 15 gives the content, and every clause of it is a past-tense report.

## The dividing line

*"**He who believes and is baptised will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned.**"*

Two clauses, and note what is not repeated. The condemnation clause has one condition in it, not
two: it is *he who does not believe*, and baptism is not named on the negative side.

*"For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that **whoever believes in Him**
should not perish but have everlasting life."*

*That if you confess with your mouth the Lord **Jesus** and believe in your heart that God has
raised Him from the dead, **you will be saved**… For "whoever calls on the name of the LORD shall be
saved."*

## Who the signs belong to

*"And these signs will follow those who believe."*

**Parakolouthesei** — will accompany, follow along after. They are described as consequences rather
than credentials, and they follow the believing rather than producing it.

*"**In My name** they will cast out demons."*

The phrase governs the list, and it is the same phrase the men in **Matthew** 7 use — *did we not
cast out demons in Your name?* — to whom He says *I never knew you*. See the note on
Matthew 7:21–23.

The signs are real and they settle nothing about the person doing them, which is why the sentence
before them is about belief.

## The last verse

*And they went out and preached everywhere, **the Lord working with them** and confirming the word
through the accompanying signs.*

**Synergountos** — working together with. The commission ends with the one who gave it doing the
work alongside the people sent.

*"And lo, **I am with you always**, even to the end of the age."*

> **Where He is while they are doing it.** *So then, after the Lord had spoken to
> them, He was received up into heaven, and sat down at the right hand of God.* Mark
> puts the ascension and the seat in one line, and then says He was working with
> them. Seated, and working.
