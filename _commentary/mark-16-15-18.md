The last instruction in Mark, and it has no limits on it anywhere.

## The scope

*"Go out into **the whole world**, and tell the good news to **every person
alive**."*

The Greek is *to all creation*. Matthew's version has *all the nations*; Mark's is
wider still.

Nobody is excluded by geography, nation, or condition.

## Who He said it to

Eleven men who had run. The chapter has just recorded Him rebuking them for
unbelief and hardness of heart because they would not believe the ones who had
seen Him.

He rebukes them, and in the next sentence hands them the world.

## The message

*"Whoever **believes and is baptised** will be saved; whoever **refuses to
believe** stands condemned."*

The two halves are not symmetrical. Believing and baptism on one side; on the other
He does not say *whoever is not baptised* — only *whoever refuses to believe*.

The dividing line in the sentence is belief.

## The signs

*"And these signs will **go along with** the ones who believe."*

The Greek is to follow after, to accompany. The signs follow the believers; the
believers do not chase the signs.

Five are listed:

- *in My name they will **drive out demons***
- *they will **speak in new tongues***
- *if they **pick up snakes** or **drink anything deadly**, it will do them no
  harm*
- *they will **lay their hands on the sick**, and the sick will get well*

## The two that are not commands

Nothing here tells anyone to handle a snake or drink poison. Both are described as
things that will not harm, not as things to attempt.

Paul is bitten by a viper on Malta, shakes it off into the fire, and the islanders
wait for him to swell up and die. He had not gone looking for it — he was picking
up firewood.

## "In My name"

The authority is stated once and covers the list. Not in their own name, and not by
any technique.

## What the last verse of Mark says happened

*And they went out and preached everywhere, **the Lord working with them** and
confirming the word through the accompanying signs.*

Mark ends with the two parties named: they preached, and He worked with them.

> **Where He is while they are doing it.** *So then, after the Lord had spoken to
> them, He was received up into heaven, and sat down at the right hand of God.* Mark
> puts the ascension and the seat in one line, and then says He was working with
> them. Seated, and working.
