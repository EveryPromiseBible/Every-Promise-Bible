Two verses near the end of an angry letter, and they are about not stopping.

## What comes just before

*Do not be deceived, God is not mocked; for **whatever a man sows, that he will also reap**.*

*For he who sows to his flesh will of the flesh reap corruption, but he who sows to the Spirit
will of the Spirit reap everlasting life.* See the note on Galatians 6:7–8.

The image is agricultural, and these two verses continue it.

## The instruction

*And let us **not grow tired of doing good**.*

The Greek is **me enkakomen** — let us not lose heart, not become cowardly, not give in to
weariness.

**To kalon poiountes** — doing the beautiful thing, the noble thing.

## What is assumed

That doing good is tiring.

Paul does not say it should not be. He addresses what happens when it is.

## The promise

*For **at the right season we will reap**.*

The Greek is **kairo gar idio** — for in its own **kairos**.

**Kairos** — the appointed moment, the right time, as against **chronos**, elapsed duration.

A harvest has a season, and the season belongs to the crop rather than the farmer.

## The condition

*If we do not give up.*

The Greek is **me ekluomenoi** — not becoming slack, not fainting. The word for a body going
limp.

The harvest is promised. The only variable is whether the sower is still standing.

## The second instruction

*So then, **whenever we have opportunity**, let us do good to everyone.*

The Greek is **hos kairon echomen** — as we have **kairos**.

The same word. The season for reaping is God's; the opportunity for doing is ours, and Paul uses
one noun for both.

## The scope

*To **everyone**.*

**Pros pantas** — toward all.

Not the household of faith only. Everyone.

## The priority

*And **most of all to those who belong to the household of faith**.*

The Greek is **malista de pros tous oikeious tes pisteos** — especially to the family members of
the faith.

**Oikeios** — of the household, domestic. The word for people who live under one roof.

## The order of the two

Everyone first, then especially the family.

Paul does not narrow the circle. He states the wide obligation and then names where the pressure
falls hardest.

## What the letter has been about

Circumcision, the law, and men who *want to make a good showing in the flesh* pressing Gentile
believers to be circumcised.

Paul has called the Galatians foolish, described Peter's face-to-face rebuke, and said *I could
wish to be present with you now and to change my tone*.

## What he says with his own hand

*See with what large letters I have written to you **with my own hand**!*

The last paragraph is written by Paul personally rather than dictated, and he says so.

## The last thing he says

*But God forbid that I should boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ… **for neither
circumcision nor uncircumcision avails anything, but a new creation**.* See the note on
Galatians 6:14–16.

*From now on let no one trouble me, for **I bear in my body the marks of the Lord Jesus**.*

The Greek is **stigmata** — brands, the marks burned on a slave or an animal to show ownership.

> **The two uses of one word.** *In its own **kairos** we will reap* — the harvest's timing is not
> ours. *As we have **kairos**, let us do good* — the opportunity is.
