key: Job 19:25–27
title: I know that my Redeemer lives
updated: 2026-08-07
---

The most confident sentence in the book is spoken from its lowest point.

## Where it comes

The chapter before it is Bildad's second speech, on what happens to the wicked.

The chapter it sits in is Job's answer, and it is the bitterest thing he says: *He has removed my
brothers far from me… my relatives have failed, and my close friends have forgotten me*.

*My breath is offensive to my wife.* And *even young children despise me*.

## The verse before

*Oh, that my words were written! Oh, that they were **inscribed in a book**! That they were **engraved
on a rock with an iron pen and lead, forever**!*

He asks for a permanent record, and then says this.

## The claim

*"**But I know that my Redeemer lives**."*

The Hebrew is **va'ani yadati go'ali chai**.

## The word

**Go'el** — the kinsman-redeemer.

A specific legal office. The nearest relative, obliged to buy back sold family land, to redeem a
relative sold into slavery, to marry a childless widow, and to avenge a killing.

## What Job wants from him

Someone with a family obligation to take his side.

He has just said his brothers and friends have all gone.

## What he has been asking for through the book

*"For He is not a man, as I am, that I may answer Him… **nor is there any mediator between us**, who
may lay his hand on us both."* See the note on Job 9:32–33.

The office he says does not exist is the one this verse claims does.

## The standing

*"And **in the end He will stand upon the earth**."*

The Hebrew is **acharon al-afar yaqum** — the last, upon the dust, He will arise.

**Afar** — dust. The word for the grave, and for what Job says he will return to.

## The witness

*"And after **my skin has been destroyed**, even so, **in my flesh I will see God**."*

The Hebrew of the verse is among the most difficult in the book. The phrase can be read *from my
flesh* or *apart from my flesh* — with or without a body.

Translations divide. The confidence in the sentence does not depend on which.

## The repetition

*"**I myself will see Him, with my own eyes I will look on Him**, and not as a stranger."*

The Hebrew is **ani echezeh-li** — I, for myself, shall see. And **velo-zar** — and not a stranger.

Three times: I will see, I myself, my own eyes.

## What he wants

Not an explanation.

The whole passage asks for a face, and specifies that it be his own eyes that see it.

## The last line

*"**How my heart aches with longing inside me** for that day."*

The Hebrew is **kalu kilyotai bechekki** — my kidneys are consumed within me.

The kidneys, in Hebrew idiom, are the seat of longing.

## What he gets

*"I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, **but now my eye sees You**."* See the note on
Job 42:1–5.

The request is granted, in those terms, at the end of the book — after four chapters of questions
about wild goats and ostriches and the morning stars.

## What is never given

An answer to why.

Job never learns about the scene in the first chapter. See the note on Job 1:6–8.

## Where the office is filled

*In whom we have **redemption through His blood**.* See the note on Ephesians 1:7–10.

The Greek is **apolutrōsis** — a buying back out of.

And *He is not ashamed to call them brethren*. See the note on Hebrews 2:11–13.

The **go'el** had to be a relative. That is the condition the letter to the Hebrews spends a chapter
establishing.

> **The office.** A **go'el** is a near relative with a legal duty to buy his kinsman back. Job's
> own relatives have just left, and he says he has one anyway.
