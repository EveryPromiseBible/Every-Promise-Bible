Peter opens his last letter by levelling the ground.

## The name

*Simon Peter here.*

Both names. The one he was born with and the one he was given.

He uses the old form **Symeon** in the Greek, which is how James addresses him at the
council in Acts 15.

## The description

*I **belong to Jesus Christ**, and He is the one who **sent me out**.*

The Greek is **doulos** — a slave — and **apostolos**, one sent.

Slave first, apostle second. He puts the ownership before the office.

## Who he is writing to

*You who were **handed the same trust we were**.*

The Greek is **lachousin** — to obtain by lot. It carries the sense of something
allotted, not achieved.

## The claim

*And **in your hands it is worth every bit as much as it is in ours**.*

The Greek is **isotimon** — of equal value, of the same honour. It occurs once in the New
Testament.

It was a commercial and civic word, used of foreigners granted the same rights as
citizens.

Peter — an eyewitness, one of the three on the mountain, the man who preached at
Pentecost — tells readers he has never met that their faith is worth exactly what his is.

## Why

*You have it **because God is righteous, and because Jesus Christ our Savior is**.*

The Greek is *by the righteousness of our God and Saviour Jesus Christ*.

The equality is grounded in God's fairness, not in their attainment. It would be unjust
for the standing to differ, because it does not come from the holder in either case.

## The greeting

*May **grace and peace keep arriving, more of it each time**.*

The Greek is **plethuntheie** — be multiplied.

Peter uses the same greeting in his first letter. Grace and peace, in that order, which
is the order every New Testament letter uses.

## How

*As you **come to know God, and Jesus our Lord**.*

The Greek is **epignosis** — full knowledge. It is the theme word of the whole letter;
Peter uses it four times in the first chapter.

The multiplying is attached to the knowing.

## What comes immediately after

*His divine power has given to us **all things that pertain to life and godliness**,
through the knowledge of Him who called us by glory and virtue.*

And then the list of seven things to add. See the note on 2 Peter 1:5–7.

The supply is stated before anything is asked.

## Why he is writing

*Knowing that shortly **I must put off my tent**, just as our Lord Jesus Christ showed
me.*

Jesus had told him at the lakeside how he would die.

This is a man setting down what he wants remembered.

> **What the first sentence establishes.** That the reader's faith is not a lesser
> edition of the writer's. Peter says it in the opening line, before any instruction, and
> grounds it in God's righteousness rather than theirs.
