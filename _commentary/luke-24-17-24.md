Two men explain the resurrection to the risen Christ and get every fact right.

## The question

*"**What is this you are discussing** with each other as you walk along?"*

He asks. He knows, and He asks.

*And they stopped, **their faces heavy**.*

Luke notes the stopping. The question halts them in the road.

## The reply

*"Are You **the only visitor in Jerusalem** who does not know what has happened there
this week?"*

They are addressing the one person in Jerusalem who knows more about it than anybody.

## "What things?"

Two words, and He lets them tell Him.

## What they knew

Run through their account. It is accurate on every point:

- Jesus of Nazareth, *a prophet, powerful in what He did and what He said*
- handed over by the chief priests and rulers
- crucified
- *this is now **the third day** since it all happened*
- the women went early, found no body, and *came back saying they had seen a vision
  of angels who told them **He is alive***
- some of the men went and *found it exactly as the women had said*

They have the timeline, the third day, the empty tomb, the angelic message, and
independent corroboration.

## The sentence in the middle

*"**But we had hoped** that He was the one who was going to redeem Israel."*

Past tense. The saddest three words in the chapter.

The hope is spoken of as something finished — and the man it was about is walking
next to them.

## "But they did not see Him"

Their last line, and it is the complaint. They had the report and not the sight.

## What was wrong

Not their information. Their conclusion.

*"**Foolish ones, and slow of heart to believe in all that the prophets have
spoken.**"*

That is His response, and note what He faults. Not slow to believe the women, or the
empty tomb, or the angels. Slow to believe **what was already written down**.

## What He does about it

*Beginning at Moses and all the Prophets, He **expounded to them in all the
Scriptures** the things concerning Himself.*

Seven miles of it, still unrecognised.

And their own verdict afterwards is about the road, not the moment of recognition:
*were our hearts not burning inside us while He talked with us on the road, while He
was **opening the Scriptures** to us?* See the notes on Luke 24:13–16 and
Luke 24:25–27.

> **What "redeem Israel" meant to them.** A throne, an army, and the Romans out. He
> had done something larger and they could not see it, standing on the third day with
> all the evidence in hand.
