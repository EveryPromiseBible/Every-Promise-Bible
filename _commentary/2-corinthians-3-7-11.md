Paul concedes everything about the first arrangement and then makes the concession his
argument.

## The names he gives the two

He never calls them law and grace here. He calls them ministries:

- **The ministry that dealt in death**
- **The ministry that handed down condemnation**

against

- **The ministry of the Spirit**
- **The ministry that hands us righteousness**

Two administrations, each with a job description.

## The medium

*Chiseled **letter by letter into stone**.*

Written by the finger of God, on the hardest available surface. See the note on
Exodus 31:18.

## What Paul concedes

*Arrived **in such glory** that the people of Israel **could not hold their eyes on the
face of Moses**.*

He does not diminish it. Exodus 34 records that Moses came down not knowing his face
shone, and *they were afraid to come near him*.

## The clause

*A glory that was **already fading**.*

The Greek is a present participle — being done away with, in the process of being
abolished.

Paul says the shine on Moses' face was on its way out while it was still bright. He
returns to it in the next paragraph: Moses put a veil on *so that the children of Israel
could not look steadily at the end of what was passing away*.

## The two comparisons

*If **the ministry that dealt in death** had that glory, **will the ministry of the Spirit
not come with far more?**"*

*If **the ministry that handed down condemnation** had glory, **how much more does the
ministry that hands us righteousness overflow**.*

Both are arguments from lesser to greater, and both start by granting the lesser its
glory.

## The blunt line

*In fact, **what once shone with glory has no glory left at all, set beside a glory so far
beyond it**.*

The Greek is *has not been glorified in this respect*.

Not that it was never glorious. That next to the other one there is nothing left of it.

The comparison Paul is reaching for is a lamp in daylight.

## The two words

*If what was **on its way out** came with glory, much more glorious is **what stays**.*

The Greek is **to katargoumenon** (being abolished) against **to menon** (the remaining).

Temporary and permanent, and the permanent one is the second.

## What was written on stone

The ten commandments, and Paul calls their delivery a ministry of death.

He is not attacking the law. Romans has him calling it *holy, and just, and good*.

He is describing what it was appointed to do.

## Where the chapter goes

*Therefore, since we have such hope, **we use great boldness of speech**.*

And then: *we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord,
**are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory**.*

Moses' face faded. Paul says the readers' is going the other way.

> **What Paul does with the argument.** He grants the old one everything — glory,
> divine authorship, a face too bright to look at — and then says that glory was
> already going out while the crowd was covering their eyes.
