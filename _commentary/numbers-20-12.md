One verse ends the careers of both men who led Israel out of Egypt.

## Who it is said to

*The LORD said **to Moses and Aaron**.*

Both. Aaron was standing there and is included in the ruling; he dies on Mount Hor before
the chapter is over.

## The charge

*"**Because you did not trust Me enough to honor Me as holy in the sight of the people of
Israel**."*

The Hebrew is *because you did not believe Me, to sanctify Me in the eyes of the children
of Israel*.

Two clauses, and the second is the one that names the setting: **in the sight of the
people**.

## What was at stake

Not water — that came. See the note on Numbers 20:10–11.

The nation had watched. What they saw was a man calling them rebels and hitting a rock
twice, and what they were meant to see was a word spoken and water arriving.

The charge is about the picture given.

## The sentence

*"**You will not bring this assembly into the land I am giving them**."*

Forty years of leading, and the last stretch is withheld.

He gets to the border. Deuteronomy 34 has him on Nebo, shown the whole land from Gilead to
the Western Sea, and he dies there.

## The closing line

*It was **a heavy word, and it was final**.*

## What Moses did about it

He asked. Deuteronomy records the prayer: *"I pleaded with the LORD at that time, saying,
'O Lord GOD… **let me cross over and see the good land beyond the Jordan**.'"*

And the answer: *"**Enough of that! Speak no more to Me of this matter**. Go up to the top
of Pisgah, lift your eyes westward, northward, southward and eastward, and see it with
your eyes, **for you shall not cross over this Jordan**."*

Asked once, refused, and told not to raise it again.

## What he says about the reason

Three times in Deuteronomy he attaches it to the people: *the LORD was angry with me **for
your sakes***.

He never disputes the ruling and never mentions the rock in those passages.

## What was not taken away

He writes Deuteronomy after this. He commissions Joshua, blesses the tribes, sees the
land from the mountain, and God buries him.

And the man who was told he would not enter the land is standing in it in Matthew 17, on
a mountain, talking with Jesus. See the note on Matthew 17:1–8.

## The epitaph

*But since then **there has not arisen in Israel a prophet like Moses, whom the LORD knew
face to face**.*

Written after the ruling, in the same book, as the last word on him.

> **What the charge is.** Not temper and not disobedience in the abstract. The verse says
> *in the sight of the people of Israel* — the nation was watching, and what they were
> shown was wrong.
