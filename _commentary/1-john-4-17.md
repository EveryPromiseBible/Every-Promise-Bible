One of the boldest sentences in the New Testament, and it is easy to read past
because of how quietly John drops it.

## The claim

*Because what He is, that is what we are, **in this world**.*

Not what we will be. Not what we are working toward. The tense is present and the
location is named — here, now, in this world.

John does not say we are like Him in some respects. He says *as He is, so are we*.
The comparison is unqualified.

## What it is given as the reason for

*So that we can stand bold on the day of judgment.*

The word is **parresia** — freedom of speech, the confidence of someone who has
the right to be in the room. John uses it four times in this letter.

And the ground of that boldness is not the reader's record. It is that their
standing and Christ's are the same standing. If He is accepted, so are they —
because it is the same acceptance.

## Where the sentence starts

*This is how love comes to full growth with us.*

The whole thing is about love being brought to completion. And the very next
verse says what that maturity does: *there is no fear in love; perfect love
throws fear outside.*

So the sequence runs — love matured, boldness at judgment, fear thrown out. Not
by working on the fear, but by knowing what you are in Christ.

> **Why it is such a strong statement.** John has just spent the chapter saying
> God is love, that He sent His Son, and that we love because He first loved us.
> This verse is the conclusion of all of it: the love that came down did not stop
> at rescuing us. It made us, in this world, what He is.
