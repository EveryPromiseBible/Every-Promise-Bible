Two cuts of meat, and the instruction is specific about which two.

## What the offerer brings

*With **his own hands** he brings the offerings made by fire — the fat, together with
the breast.*

The Hebrew stresses it: his own hands. Nobody carries it in for him.

## The wave

*And **waves the breast before the LORD as a wave offering**.*

The Hebrew **tenuphah** — a waving, a moving to and fro. The priest and the offerer
held it and moved it horizontally before the altar.

## The two portions

*The breast belongs to **Aaron and his sons**… You are to give **the right thigh** to
the priest as a contribution.*

**The breast** — the front of the animal, over the heart.

**The right thigh** — the shoulder and haunch, the strongest part of the beast.

The heart-part and the strength-part, and both go to the priest.

## Which offering this is

The **peace offering** — Hebrew **shelamim**, from **shalom**. It is the only
sacrifice in the system that the person bringing it got to eat.

The burnt offering went up entirely. The sin offering went to the priest or outside
the camp. This one is a meal: God receives the fat, the priest receives the breast
and thigh, and the offerer eats the rest with his family.

Three parties at one table, over one animal.

## The name

Peace offering, and it is not brought to make peace. Leviticus places it after the
burnt and grain offerings, and it is the sacrifice of somebody at peace already —
brought for thanksgiving, or a vow, or freely.

## The time limit

*The meat of **the thanksgiving** peace offering must be eaten **the same day** it is
offered; **none may be left until morning**.*

A vow or freewill offering could be eaten on the second day. The thanksgiving one
had to be eaten fresh.

And on the third day, anything left *counts for nothing to the one who brought it. It
has become unfit.* See the note on Leviticus 7:11–18.

Yesterday's thanksgiving does not keep.

## What it is called

*Their **lasting share** from the people.*

The Hebrew is a perpetual statute — a permanent arrangement for the priests, who had
no land. See the note on Ezekiel 44:28–31.

> **What comes with the meat.** *Loaves of bread without yeast mixed with oil, thin
> wafers spread with oil, and loaves of fine flour well-mixed with oil* — and then,
> unusually, *loaves of bread made with yeast.* Leaven is banned from almost every
> offering in Leviticus. In the peace offering it is commanded.
