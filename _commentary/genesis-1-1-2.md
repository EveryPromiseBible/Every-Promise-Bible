Two verses, and everything the Bible says afterwards assumes them.

## The first sentence

*In the beginning, **God made it all** — the heavens above and the earth below.*

The Hebrew is seven words: **bereshit bara Elohim et hashamayim ve'et ha'arets**.

No argument for God's existence is offered here or anywhere else in Scripture. The book opens
with Him already at work.

## The verb

**Bara** — to create. In the Hebrew Bible its subject is always God. No human being ever
**bara**s anything.

It appears three times in the creation account: the heavens and earth, the sea creatures, and
man.

## The name

**Elohim** — plural in form, singular in the verbs that follow it.

And the Hebrew has a small word that does not translate: **et**, the marker of a direct
object, used twice. The first and last letters of the alphabet, aleph and tav.

*I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End.*

## The state

*At first the earth was **formless and empty**.*

The Hebrew is **tohu va-vohu** — an assonant pair, untranslatable neatly. Waste and void.

Isaiah uses **tohu** of the desert, and says of the earth: *He did not create it in vain, He
formed it to be inhabited*.

## What the six days do

Formless, and empty — two problems.

Days one to three deal with the formlessness: light and dark, sky and sea, land and
vegetation. Three environments.

Days four to six fill them: sun and moon, birds and fish, animals and man.

Form, then fill. The structure is stated in verse 2 and worked through in order.

## The darkness

*And **darkness lay over the deep waters**.*

The Hebrew is **tehom** — the deep. The word for the primeval ocean, and for what bursts open
in the flood.

## The Spirit

*But God was not far off: the **Spirit of God was already there, hovering** over the waters.*

The Hebrew is **merachefet** — a participle. Hovering, fluttering, brooding.

The verb occurs three times in the whole Hebrew Bible. One of the others is in Moses' song:
*as an eagle **stirs up its nest, hovers over its young**, spreading out its wings, taking
them up, carrying them on its wings*.

A bird over a nest.

## The three

The Spirit is here in verse 2. The word is in verse 3 — *and God said*.

*In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God… **all things
were made through Him**, and without Him nothing was made that was made.*

John opens his Gospel with Genesis' first three words and puts Christ inside them.

## What is not said

How long. Nothing in the Hebrew fixes an interval between verse 1 and verse 2, and the
chapter is not written to answer that question.

## The other beginnings

*In the beginning was the Word.* John.

*The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God.* Mark.

*In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.* And then, at the far end of the
book: *now I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had
passed away*.

## The New Testament's use

*By faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that **the things
which are seen were not made of things which are visible**.*

And Paul: *God, who commanded light to shine out of darkness, has shone in our hearts to give
the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ*. See the note on
2 Corinthians 4:6–9.

He treats a man's conversion as day one happening again.

> **The word for what the Spirit was doing.** *Merachefet* — used three times in the Hebrew
> Bible, and one of the others is an eagle over a nest of chicks.
