The last sentence of Romans, and it is one long clause held open until the final word.

## The claim

*Now to Him who is **able to hold you steady**.*

The Greek is **sterixai** — to fix firmly, to establish, to set in place.

It is the word Jesus used to Peter — *when you have turned back, **strengthen** your
brothers* — and the word Peter uses of what God does after suffering. See the notes on
Luke 22:31–34 and 1 Peter 5:10.

Paul opened the letter wanting to do it himself: *I long to see you, that I may impart to
you some spiritual gift, **so that you may be established**.*

Sixteen chapters later he attributes the establishing to God.

## According to what

*In keeping with **the good news I carry** and **the preaching of Jesus Christ**.*

The steadying is measured by a message. Not by effort, and not by conditions.

**My gospel** — Paul calls it his twice elsewhere. He means the one he was given to
carry, not one of his own.

## The mystery

*According to the revelation of **the mystery that was held in silence through ages beyond
counting**.*

The Greek is **musterion** — not a riddle, but something that could not be worked out and
had to be disclosed.

**Held in silence** — the Greek is *kept secret*. Paul uses the same idea in Ephesians and
Colossians: *the mystery which has been hidden from ages and from generations, but now has
been revealed*.

## The disclosure

*But is **now brought out into the open**.*

The Greek is **phanerothentos** — made manifest, brought into the light.

## By what means

*And, **through the writings of the prophets**, according to the commandment of the
everlasting God, **made known to all nations for the obedience of faith**.*

The odd clause is *through the writings of the prophets*. A secret kept silent through the
ages is now published — and the publishing runs through documents that had been available
the whole time.

Paul has spent the letter doing exactly that: Habakkuk, Genesis, the Psalms, Isaiah,
Hosea, Deuteronomy, quoted at every turn.

## The phrase that bookends the letter

*For **the obedience of faith**.*

He used it in the opening paragraph: *through whom we have received grace and apostleship
for **obedience to the faith among all nations**.*

Chapter 1 and chapter 16, the same phrase, closing the circle.

## To whom

*To **God, the only wise One**, be glory **through Jesus Christ** forever. Amen.*

## What the letter has been

Sin, law, faith, Abraham, no condemnation, the Spirit, Israel, the olive tree, living
sacrifices, the weak and the strong, and a chapter of greetings naming twenty-six people.

And it ends on a doxology about being held steady.

## Where the doxology sits in the manuscripts

Some early copies place it at the end of chapter 14 or chapter 15 rather than here. The
variation is old and has been noted since antiquity.

Wherever it is placed, it is the last word Paul gives the letter.

> **What is being celebrated at the end.** Not the argument. Paul closes sixteen chapters
> of the tightest reasoning in the New Testament by pointing at the One able to hold the
> reader steady, and attributing the ability to the message rather than to the reader.
