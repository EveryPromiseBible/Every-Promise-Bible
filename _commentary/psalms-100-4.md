Four imperatives in one verse, and two of them describe an approach.

## The psalm

*A Psalm of Thanksgiving.*

The Hebrew is **mizmor letodah** — a psalm for the thank offering.

The only psalm with this heading. It was sung when the **todah** — the thanksgiving sacrifice —
was brought.

## What comes before

*Make a joyful shout to the LORD, all you lands! **Serve the LORD with gladness**; come before His
presence with singing.*

*Know that the LORD, He is God; **it is He who has made us, and not we ourselves**.* See the note on
Psalms 100:1–3.

## The gates

*Enter **His gates with thanksgiving**.*

The Hebrew is **bo'u she'arav betodah**.

The outer gates of the temple courts. The first boundary a worshipper crossed.

## The courts

*Step into **His courts with praise**.*

The Hebrew is **chatzerotav bithillah**.

**Chatzer** — the courtyard, inside the gates. Further in.

## The two stages

Gates, then courts. Outside, then inside.

The psalm marks the approach in two steps and names something to carry at each.

## The two words

**Todah** — thanksgiving. From the same root as **yadah**, to acknowledge, to confess. The word
for the thank offering.

**Tehillah** — praise. The root behind **Tehillim**, the Hebrew name for the Psalter.

## What they are not

Feelings to arrive with.

The Hebrew treats them as what you bring — the way you would bring an animal or a measure of
flour.

## The third imperative

*Give thanks to Him.*

The Hebrew is **hodu-lo** — from **yadah** again.

## The fourth

*Bless His name.*

The Hebrew is **barachu shemo**.

**Barach** — to kneel, and then to bless. The physical posture is inside the word.

## The reason given

*For **the LORD is good; His mercy is everlasting, and His truth endures to all generations**.*

The last verse.

**Chesed** and **emunah** — steadfast love and faithfulness.

## Where the temple gates were

Two courts and then a barrier: *no foreigner may pass beyond*, on pain of death.

The psalm calls *all you lands* to make a joyful shout, and the architecture stopped most of them
at a wall.

## What Paul says about that wall

*For He Himself is our peace, who has made both one, and **has broken down the middle wall of
separation**.* See the note on Ephesians 2:14–16.

## What Hebrews says about the entering

*Therefore, brethren, having **boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus**, by a new and
living way which He consecrated for us.* See the note on Hebrews 10:19–21.

Not the gates or the courts. All the way in.

## The other thank offering

*By Him therefore let us **continually offer the sacrifice of praise to God**, that is, the fruit of
our lips, giving thanks to His name.*

The Greek is **thusian aineseos** — a sacrifice of praise. The Septuagint's rendering of
**todah**.

## Where the gates end up

*Its gates shall not be shut at all by day (**there shall be no night there**).* See the note on
Revelation 21:24–27.

> **The two stages.** Gates with thanksgiving, courts with praise — an approach marked in steps,
> and something to be carried at each one.
