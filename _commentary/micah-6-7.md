title: "Shall I give my firstborn?"

Four escalating offers, and the last one is the question the gospel answers.

## Who is speaking

A worshipper, in the middle of God's lawsuit against the nation. God has said *my people, what have
I done to you? How have I wearied you? Answer Me* — and this is the answer.

It is a sincere question, and the escalation in it is the point.

## The ladder

*"Shall I come to Him with **burnt offerings**, with **calves a year old**?"*

The ordinary offering, and then a costlier version of it.

*"Will the LORD be pleased with **a thousand rams**, with **ten thousand rivers of oil**?"*

Now the numbers are royal. Solomon offered a thousand burnt offerings at Gibeon; nobody else in
Scripture is in that range. Rivers of oil is beyond any quantity that exists.

## The last rung

*"Shall I give **my firstborn** for my wrongdoing, **the child of my own body** for the sin of my
soul?"*

The ladder ends where the surrounding nations' religion ended. Child sacrifice was practised in the
valley outside Jerusalem, and two kings of Judah did it.

*Something I never commanded or spoke of, **something that never entered My mind**.* See the note on
Jeremiah 19:1–5.

## What the question assumes

That the sin is real, that something must be given, and that the price rises with the offence.

Every rung is right about the first two. What the passage exposes is that the ladder has no top —
the worshipper reaches the most valuable thing he owns and it is still a question.

*For **it is not possible that the blood of bulls and goats could take away sins**.*

## The answer given

*"**He has shown you, mortal, what is good.** And what does the LORD ask of you? Only to do justice,
to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God."* See the note on Micah 6:8.

## The answer not given

The worshipper's last question — *shall I give my firstborn?* — is not answered in Micah at all. It
is simply not taken up.

*"For God so loved the world that **He gave His only begotten Son**, that whoever believes in Him
should not perish but have everlasting life."*

*He who did not spare His own Son, but **delivered Him up for us all**, how shall He not with Him
also freely give us all things?*

The question is asked in Micah 6 by a man wondering what it would cost. The answer, when it comes,
is that somebody did give a firstborn, and it was not the worshipper.

*"Take now your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love… **God will provide for Himself the lamb**."*
See the note on Genesis 22:6–8.

Abraham is stopped on the mountain. Micah's worshipper is not answered. **John** 3 says who
finished the sentence.

## What Hebrews does with the ladder

*Then I said, "Behold, I have come — in the volume of the book it is written of Me — **to do Your
will, O God**."… By that will **we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus
Christ once for all**.*

**Hebrews** 10 quotes Psalm 40 and reaches the top of Micah's ladder: a body, given, once.

> **Where the ladder ends.** With a man offering his own child and still asking. Micah never answers
> that rung — and the sentence that finally does is *God so loved the world that He gave His only
> begotten Son*.
