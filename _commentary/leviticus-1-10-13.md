The burnt offering from the flock, and the instructions include a compass point.

## "A male without flaw"

*Let it be **a male without flaw**.*

The Hebrew is **tamim** — whole, sound, unblemished. The same word used of Noah, and
the word Peter reaches for: *a lamb without blemish and without spot.*

## Where it dies

*It is put to death **on the north side of the altar** before the LORD.*

The direction is specified, and Leviticus does not usually waste words.

Golgotha lay north of the temple mount. The gospels place the crucifixion *outside
the gate*, and Hebrews makes a point of it: *Jesus also, that He might sanctify the
people with His own blood, suffered outside the gate.*

## What the offerer does

Read verse 4, from the paragraph before: *he shall put his hand on the head of the
burnt offering, and it will be accepted on his behalf to make atonement for him.*

One gesture. He leans on the animal's head, and the transfer happens.

The examination in this system falls on the animal, never on the man. See the note
on John 1:29–34.

## What is burned

*The priest arranges them **with the head and the fat** on the wood above the fire…
Then the priest **offers it all**, burning it on the altar.*

The whole animal. The burnt offering is the one sacrifice in which nothing is kept
back — no portion for the priest, no portion for the giver.

The Hebrew name for it is **olah**, from the verb to go up. It all goes up.

## What is washed

*While **the inner parts and the legs are washed with water**.*

The two things nobody sees: what is inside, and what it walked on.

## What it is called

*A gift by fire, **a fragrance that pleases the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is a soothing, restful aroma. The same phrase used after Noah's altar,
when God said in His heart that He would not curse the ground again.

Paul takes it into Ephesians: *Christ also has loved us and given Himself for us, an
offering and a sacrifice to God for **a sweet-smelling aroma**.*

## Why it is first in the book

Leviticus opens with this one, before the grain offering, the peace offering, the
sin offering or the trespass offering.

The burnt offering is not about a particular sin. It is about a whole life given up,
entirely consumed, and the first thing the book describes.

> **The graded provision.** A bull, or a sheep or goat, or — in the paragraph after
> this — *turtledoves or young pigeons*, for those who could afford nothing else.
> Mary and Joseph brought the birds. The system was written so that no one was
> excluded by price.
