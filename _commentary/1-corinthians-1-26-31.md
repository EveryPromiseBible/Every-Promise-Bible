Paul makes his case by telling the Corinthians to look around the room.

## The evidence he points at

*Not many of you were wise by the reckoning of the flesh, not many were powerful,
not many were nobly born.*

He is not insulting them. He is presenting them as data. If the message spread by
being intellectually fashionable or socially connected, this congregation would
look different than it does.

Note the precision: *not many* — not *none*. Corinth had some people of standing,
and Paul does not overstate his case.

## The four things God chose

- what the world calls **foolish**
- what the world calls **weak**
- the **low and the despised**
- **the things that count for nothing at all**

The last one escalates past the others — literally *the things that are not*,
brought in to nullify *the things that are*.

And the stated purpose is entirely negative: *so that no human being might boast
in His presence*. The selection criteria were designed to rule something out.

## What Christ is said to have become

*Wisdom from God — and everything that puts us right with God, and everything
that makes us holy, and everything that sets us free.*

Four things, and every one of them is the sort of thing a person would otherwise
be trying to achieve. Paul does not say Christ helps with them. He says Christ
**became** them, for us.

> **The quotation at the end.** *Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord* — from
> Jeremiah, where the context is a warning against boasting in wisdom, strength
> or riches. The three things Jeremiah names line up almost exactly with the three
> Paul has just said the Corinthians did not have much of. He is not improvising
> the argument; he is landing on a text.
