Daniel had been reading Jeremiah, counting the seventy years of the exile. The
answer he gets back is about seventy of something else.

## The unit

*Seventy **sevens** are decreed for your people and your holy city.*

The Hebrew is *shabuim* — sevens, weeks. Seventy sevens is four hundred and ninety.

Daniel had been counting years; the answer comes back in the same currency, and
Israel already reckoned in sevens — a sabbath year every seventh, a jubilee after
seven sevens.

## Whose clock it is

*Decreed for **your people** and **your holy city**.*

Israel and Jerusalem. The measure is not running on the world in general.

## The six things it is for

1. *to **finish the transgression***
2. *to **put an end to sin***
3. *to **make atonement** for wickedness*
4. *to **bring in everlasting righteousness***
5. *to **seal up vision and prophecy***
6. *to **anoint the Most Holy***

Not one of them is something Israel does. All six are things done for and to them,
and the third one names the mechanism: atonement — the Hebrew is **kaphar**, to
cover, the word behind the Day of Atonement.

*Everlasting* righteousness. Not a righteousness that has to be maintained.

## The division

The verses that follow split the four hundred and ninety: seven sevens, then
sixty-two sevens, then one seven left over.

*From the going forth of the command to restore and build Jerusalem until Messiah
the Prince* — sixty-nine sevens. Four hundred and eighty-three years.

Then, in verse 26, something happens that is not in the arithmetic: *the Anointed
One will be cut off and will have nothing.* See the note on Daniel 9:26.

## The gap

Sixty-nine sevens run out at the cutting off. The seventieth is described in verse
27 and has not been described as beginning.

Between the sixty-ninth and the seventieth there is a space, and the church age is
in it. The clock in this prophecy is Israel's, and it stopped.

## Why Daniel was praying

The chapter's first half is one of the great prayers of Scripture, and it is
confession — *we have sinned, we have done wickedly* — for a nation, by a man with
nothing personally to confess.

Gabriel arrives *at the time of the evening sacrifice*, in a city with no temple
and no sacrifice for seventy years, and tells him he is *greatly beloved*.

> **What the answer is bigger than the question.** Daniel asked about seventy
> years. He was given seventy sevens, and the six things listed are not about
> getting a people home from Babylon. They are about finishing sin and bringing in
> a righteousness that does not end.
