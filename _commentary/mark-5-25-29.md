She is in a crowd on the way to somewhere else, and she interrupts the story.

## Where it happens

Jesus is on his way to Jairus's house. A synagogue ruler's daughter is dying and the
whole crowd is moving.

Mark puts this inside that journey — one story cut open and another set in the middle
of it. He does this deliberately, and the two are connected by a number.

## Twelve years

*A woman in that crowd who had been **bleeding for twelve years**.*

The girl he is walking toward is twelve years old.

For as long as one family has had a daughter, this woman has been bleeding.

## What her condition meant

Under Leviticus 15 a woman with a discharge is unclean, and so is anything she sits on
and anyone who touches her.

Twelve years of that means twelve years without the temple, without a synagogue, and
without being touched by anybody.

## What she had tried

*She had been through **a great deal at the hands of many doctors**, and she had **spent
every last coin she had**, and **none of it had done any good** — she had **only grown
worse**.*

Four clauses, and each one is worse than the last. Mark takes his time over it.

Luke — a physician — records this scene and leaves out the line about the doctors.

## What she had heard

*She had **heard the reports about Jesus**.*

That is all she has. No introduction, no invitation, and no conversation with Him.

## How she came

*She came up **behind Him** in **the crush of people** and reached out for **His
clothes**.*

Behind Him, and at the level of the hem. She is legally unclean in a packed street, and
everyone she brushes past becomes unclean too.

She does not ask permission because asking would have ended it.

## What she kept saying

*"**If I can only touch what He is wearing**," she kept telling herself, "**I will be
well**."*

The Greek is imperfect — she **was saying** it, over and over, while pushing through.

The word she uses is **sozo**: saved, made whole, rescued.

## The edge of the garment

Numbers commands a tassel on the four corners of the garment with a blue thread —
**tzitzit**. Malachi's promise reads *the Sun of Righteousness shall arise with healing
in His wings*, and *wings* is the same word used for those corners.

She reaches for the corner. See the note on Mark 6:53–56, where whole villages do the
same.

## What happened

*And she touched Him, and **at that very moment** she **felt in her own body** that the
bleeding had stopped and **she was healed**.*

Immediately, and she knew it internally before anyone said anything.

## What she expected next

Nothing good. When He asks who touched Him she comes *fearing and trembling* and falls
down and tells Him the whole truth.

What she gets is a word she had not heard in twelve years: *Daughter, your faith has
made you well. Go in peace, and be healed of your affliction.* See the note on
Mark 5:30–34.

> **The law she broke.** Under Leviticus her touch made others unclean. In this case
> the traffic ran the other way — she touched Him, and it was the uncleanness that
> stopped.
