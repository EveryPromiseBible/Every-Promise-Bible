The section of Leviticus that deals with wages.

## Whose instructions these are

Chapters 1 to 5 tell the worshipper what to bring. From chapter 6 the manual turns to the
priests. See the note on Leviticus 6:24–30.

This paragraph is about what they live on.

## The grade

*It is **most holy**.*

The Hebrew is **qodesh qodashim** — holy of holies.

The guilt offering gets the highest classification, and so does the sin offering.

## The place

*The animal is slaughtered **in the place where the burnt offering is slain**.*

North side of the altar. The same ground for all of them, and no lesser corner for the
offerings about failure.

## What burns

*The **fat tail**, the fat covering the inner parts, both **kidneys** with the fat around
them, and the **covering of the liver**.*

The fat tail is specific to the broad-tailed sheep of the region, which stored fat in the
tail — a delicacy, and the best part of the animal.

The same list as the peace offering. See the note on Leviticus 3:1–5.

## The rule

*Every male among the priests may eat of it, but it must be **eaten in a holy place**.*

Not taken home.

## The principle

*It **belongs to the priest who makes things right with it**.*

The Hebrew is *the priest who makes atonement with it shall have it*.

The man who does the work is the man who eats.

## The hide

*The priest who offers a burnt offering for anyone **keeps the hide** of that offering for
himself.*

In the burnt offering everything goes on the fire — except the skin.

The one part not consumed goes to the priest. Hides were valuable; leather was money.

Which means the priest's income from a burnt offering was a skin.

## Where the first hides came from

*Also for Adam and his wife the LORD God **made tunics of skin, and clothed them**.*

They had sewn fig leaves. God covered them with something that had died.

## The baked and the raw

*Every grain offering **baked in an oven or prepared in a pan or on a griddle belongs to the
priest who offers it**, while every grain offering, whether **mixed with oil or dry, belongs
equally to all of Aaron's sons**.*

Two different arrangements. Cooked portions go to the officiating priest; uncooked flour is
divided among all of them.

Nothing in it is casual. The book specifies who gets what down to whether the offering had
been near a fire.

## Why any of this is in Scripture

Because the priests had no land. *The LORD said to Aaron: "You shall have no inheritance in
their land, nor shall you have any portion among them; **I am your portion and your
inheritance**."*

Eleven tribes got territory. The twelfth got the altar.

Paul cites the arrangement: *do you not know that those who minister the holy things eat of
the things of the temple… even so the Lord has commanded that those who preach the gospel
should live from the gospel.*

## Which offering Isaiah names

The guilt offering — **asham**. See the note on Leviticus 5:14–19.

*When You make His soul an offering for sin.* See the note on Isaiah 53:10–12.

> **What the priest got from a whole burnt offering.** The one thing that did not burn. A
> hide — which is what God used in Genesis to clothe two people who had tried leaves.
