Paul stops arguing and asks a question he says he only needs one answer to.

## The address

*Galatians, **what has come over you?**"*

The Greek is *O foolish Galatians* — **anoetoi**, without understanding, not thinking.

He names them by their region and calls them senseless, twice in five verses.

## The spell

*Who has **put you under a spell**?*

The Greek is **ebaskanen** — bewitched, from the word for the evil eye.

Paul is not saying it literally. He is saying nothing else explains it.

## What they had seen

*You, **before whose very eyes Jesus Christ was set out plainly**, right there among you,
**as the crucified One**.*

The Greek is **proegraphe** — placarded, posted up publicly, the way a public notice was
displayed.

The tense of *crucified* is perfect: crucified and standing crucified.

None of them were in Jerusalem. What was placarded in front of them was the preaching.

## The one question

*There is **only one thing I want to learn from you**.*

He sets aside the whole argument and reduces it to a single test.

## The test

*Did you **receive the Spirit by doing the works of the law, or by hearing and
believing**?*

The Greek is *by works of law or by the hearing of faith*.

It is a question about their own experience, and they know the answer. Something
happened when they believed, and it did not happen because they had kept anything.

Paul does not argue from Scripture here. He argues from what they remember.

## The direction

*You **began in the Spirit** — are you now **to be brought to completion by the flesh**?*

The Greek for *brought to completion* is **epiteleisthe** — to finish off, to perfect.

The question is about the second half of the process, not the first. Nobody in Galatia
was denying how it started.

## The suffering

*Did you go through **so much suffering for nothing** — **if it really was for nothing**?*

Acts 13 and 14 record what happened when Paul was in that region: driven out of Antioch,
threatened at Iconium, and stoned and left for dead at Lystra.

The converts had paid for this.

**If it really was for nothing** — Paul adds the clause and then leaves it. He does not
finish the thought.

## The question again

*So then, the One who **supplies the Spirit to you and works miracles among you** — does He
do it **because you keep the works of the law, or because you heard and believed**?*

He asks it a second time, in the present tense.

Not only how it started — how it is still running. He says miracles are currently
happening among them, and asks what the supply is connected to.

The Greek for *supplies* is **epichoregeo**, the word for the wealthy citizen who paid
for the whole chorus out of his own pocket. Peter uses it of God and then of the reader.
See the note on 2 Peter 1:5–7.

## What comes next

Abraham, believing God, counted as righteousness — four hundred and thirty years before
the law. See the note on Galatians 3:6–9.

Paul answers his own question with a case older than the thing in dispute.

> **The evidence he calls.** Not a text. Their own memory of what happened when they
> heard, and what is still happening among them now.
