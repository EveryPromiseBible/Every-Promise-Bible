David explains what went wrong the first time, and the sentence is unusually direct.

## Where this comes

Three months after Uzzah. The ark has been at Obed-edom's and the household has visibly
prospered. See the note on 2 Samuel 6:9–11.

Samuel records the second attempt as a triumph. Chronicles records the preparation.

## Who he summons

*Zadok and Abiathar the priests, and the Levites **Uriel, Asaiah, Joel, Shemaiah, Eliel, and
Amminadab**.*

Six Levite family heads, named, with the number of their brethren given in the verses before —
a hundred and twenty here, two hundred and twenty there.

The whole thing is organised.

## The instruction

*"**Consecrate yourselves**, you and your relatives, so that you may bring up the ark."*

The Hebrew is **hitqaddeshu** — sanctify yourselves.

Nothing about consecration came into the first attempt. Thirty thousand men and a new cart.

## The admission

*"It was because **you Levites did not carry it the first time** that the LORD our God broke out
against us, for **we did not seek Him about the proper way**."*

The Hebrew is **kammishpat** — according to the ordinance, the prescribed manner.

Two failures named: the wrong carriers, and no enquiry.

The second is the one David puts last, and it is the root of the first. Nobody looked it up.

## Who says it

The king, to the priests, about his own decision.

He does not blame Uzzah, and he does not blame the oxen. *We did not seek Him.*

## What the instruction had been

*The sons of Kohath shall come to carry them, **but they shall not touch any holy thing, lest
they die**.*

And: *the poles shall be in the rings of the ark; they shall not be taken from it*.

The poles were permanently installed for exactly this. The equipment was designed so that
nobody would ever need to touch it.

## The obedience

*So the priests and Levites **consecrated themselves**.*

*And the Levites **carried the ark of God on their shoulders with the poles, just as Moses had
commanded** according to the word of the LORD.*

**On their shoulders.** The Hebrew is **bikthephām** — on their shoulder.

The ark's weight has been estimated at well over a hundred pounds, and the poles distributed it
across four men walking six miles uphill.

## The word

The verb for carrying is **nasa** — to lift, bear, carry.

It is the verb of Isaiah 53:4 — *surely He has **borne** our griefs*. See the note on
Isaiah 53:4–6.

The Levites bear the ark; the Servant bears the sicknesses. Same word.

## The help

*Because **God had helped the Levites who carried the ark**, they sacrificed seven bulls and
seven rams.* See the note on 1 Chronicles 15:25–28.

Chronicles notes that carrying it needed help, and that the help was given.

## What the cart had been

Faster, easier, and borrowed from the Philistines — who had no instructions and got it right by
accident with two milk cows. See the note on 1 Samuel 6:10–12.

Israel had the instructions and used the Philistine method.

## What David also arranged

Singers. Chapter 15 lists them by name and by instrument, and chapter 16 has the psalm they
sang: *oh, give thanks to the LORD! Call upon His name; make known His deeds among the
peoples!*

## What the correction was not

More sincerity.

The first attempt had thirty thousand men, harps, tambourines, cymbals and genuine joy. What
it did not have was shoulders.

*"Because you did not do it the first time, the LORD our God broke out against us, for **we
did not consult Him about the proper order**." And **the Levites bore the ark of God on their
shoulders**, by its poles, as Moses had commanded.*

## What was wrong with the cart

Nothing, as farm equipment. It was borrowed from the Philistines, for whom it had worked
because they did not have the instructions. See the note on 2 Samuel 6:1–5.

*For they being ignorant of God's righteousness, and **seeking to establish their own
righteousness**, have not submitted to the righteousness of God.*

**Paul** is describing a zeal that is real and misdirected, which is exactly what chapter 13
records.

## What David does say

*"**We did not seek Him about the proper order.**"*

He puts it in the first person plural and does not blame Uzzah, the oxen or the road.

*If we confess our sins, **He is faithful and just to forgive us** our sins and to cleanse us
from all unrighteousness.*

## Who carries it in the end

Men, on shoulders, by poles that were never meant to come out of the rings. See the note on
Exodus 25:10–16.

The ark was built to be carried by people and it never stopped being.

> **The clause David puts last.** *We did not seek Him about the proper way.* Not the cart, not
> the oxen, not the man who reached out — nobody had looked it up.
