Two verses for the crucifixion itself. John spends more words on the sign over it.

## The carrying

*Carrying **His own cross**.*

The Greek is **bastazon heauto ton stauron**.

The condemned man carried the crossbeam — the **patibulum** — to the site, where the upright
already stood.

The other three Gospels have Simon of Cyrene pressed into carrying it. John does not mention
him.

Both are true of one walk. He started with it and could not finish.

## Where He goes

*He **went out** to the place called the Place of a Skull.*

**Went out** — outside the wall.

Hebrews takes that geography and makes an argument from it: *the bodies of those animals whose
blood is brought into the sanctuary are burned outside the camp. Therefore Jesus also… suffered
outside the gate*. See the note on Hebrews 13:7–14.

## The name

*Which in Hebrew is called **Golgotha**.*

**Gulgolta** — skull. The Latin is **calvaria**, which gives *Calvary*.

Why it was called that is not stated. A rock formation, a place of execution, or both.

John gives the local name, as he does with Gabbatha and Siloam. He is writing for people who
could walk there.

## The sentence

*There **they crucified Him**.*

Two words in Greek.

No description of the nails, the beam, the raising, or the pain. All four Gospels do this.

The Romans crucified thousands; every reader knew exactly what the word covered. The writers
say it once and move on.

## The others

*And **two others with Him, one on each side**.*

Matthew and Mark call them **lestai** — robbers, or insurrectionists. Luke records the
conversation between them.

## The position

*With **Jesus in the middle**.*

The Greek is **meson de ton Iesoun**.

John records where He was standing in the row.

The centre position was the place of the principal offender — the one the execution was
arranged for.

## What it fulfils

*And He was numbered with the transgressors.*

Isaiah 53, and Luke has Him quote it that evening: *for I say to you that this which is written
must still be accomplished in Me: "And He was numbered with the transgressors."*

## What happens between the two crosses

*"Lord, remember me when You come into Your kingdom."*

*"Assuredly, I say to you, today you will be with Me in Paradise."*

One of the two changes his mind in the last hours of his life, and gets a promise with a
deadline of *today*.

## What John spends his verses on

Not the crucifixion. The sign.

*Now Pilate wrote a title and put it on the cross. And the writing was: **JESUS OF NAZARETH,
THE KING OF THE JEWS**.*

*Then many of the Jews read this title… and it was written in **Hebrew, Greek, and Latin**.*

The chief priests object to the wording and Pilate refuses: *what I have written, I have
written*. See the note on John 19:12–16.

Four verses on the sign and two on the execution.

## And then the garments

*They divided My garments among them, and for My clothing they cast lots.*

Psalm 22, quoted by John, with the seamless tunic they would not tear.

> **What John records about the position.** The middle. Two others, one on each side, and He is
> in the place reserved for the man the execution was arranged for.
