The visitors leave for Sodom and one man does not move.

## The outcry

*"**The outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah is so great**, and their sin so grievous."*

The Hebrew is **za'aqah** — a scream, the noise made by somebody being wronged.

It is the same word used of Abel's blood, of Israel under the whips in Egypt, and of the poor
man whose cloak is kept overnight. Scripture consistently describes cruelty as making a sound
that reaches God.

Ezekiel names what the outcry was about: *pride, fullness of food, and abundance of idleness…
neither did she strengthen the hand of the poor and needy*.

## "I will go down and see"

*"**I will go down and see** whether what they have done is as bad as the outcry that has
reached Me. If not, I will know."*

He does not need to. The going down is a courtesy the passage records twice — He said the same
before Babel: *the LORD came down to see the city and the tower*.

Judgement in Genesis is never remote. It is described as arriving in person and looking.

## Who leaves and who stays

*The men turned and went toward Sodom, **but Abraham remained standing before the LORD**.*

Two of the three go on. One stays, and Abraham stays with Him.

The scribes of Israel left a note on this verse — an old tradition holds that the original
read the other way round, *the LORD remained standing before Abraham*, and that it was
reversed out of reverence. Either way the picture is a man and God left alone on a hillside
with a city below them.

## What standing means

*Then Abraham came near and said, "**Would You also destroy the righteous with the wicked?**"*

Fifty, forty-five, forty, thirty, twenty, ten. Six times he lowers the number and six times
the answer comes back yes. See the note on Genesis 18:27–33.

God never once tells him to stop.

## Why he could pray it at all

Because he had been told. *Should I hide from Abraham what I am about to do?* See the note on
Genesis 18:16–19.

The disclosure came first and the intercession followed. He is not interrupting; he is
responding to something he was deliberately let in on.

## Who is standing now

*Therefore He is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him, since
**He always lives to make intercession for them**.*

Abraham stood on that hill once, for one city, and got God down to ten.

There is Someone standing permanently, and He is not negotiating a number — He is holding up
people He has already bought. See the note on Hebrews 7:20–22.

## What came of the ten

There were not ten. But *God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the
overthrow*. See the note on Genesis 19:27–29.

The number was not met and the nephew came out anyway, and Genesis says the reason was
Abraham.

> **The line to notice.** *Abraham remained standing before the LORD.* Everyone else in the
> passage is going somewhere. The intercession begins with a man who does not leave.
