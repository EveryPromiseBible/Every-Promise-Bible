Two piles of stones, and only one of them is visible afterwards.

## The obedience

*So the Israelites **did just as Joshua commanded**.*

The Hebrew adds *as the LORD had told Joshua*. The chain is stated: God to Joshua, Joshua to
the twelve, and the twelve did it.

## The first set

*They took up **twelve stones out of the middle of the Jordan**, one for each tribe… and
**carried them to the campsite and set them down there**.*

Out of the river, onto dry land, where people camp and children ask questions. See the note
on Joshua 4:4–7.

## The second set

*And **Joshua also set up twelve stones in the middle of the Jordan**, on the spot **where
the priests carrying the ark had stood**.*

Nobody told him to.

The instruction in the chapter is about stones coming out. This pile is Joshua's own
addition, and he puts it in the riverbed.

## Where exactly

*On the spot where the priests carrying the ark had stood.*

The precise square yards of ground where the feet of the men holding the ark had been while
two million people walked past.

## What happens to it

The chapter ends with the priests coming up out of the river: *and it came to pass, when the
priests who bore the ark of the covenant of the LORD had come from the midst of the Jordan…
that the waters of the Jordan returned to their place and overflowed all its banks as
before*.

The second pile is under water within the hour, and stays under.

## The phrase

*And **they are there to this day**.*

Written by somebody who could not see them and knew where they were.

The Hebrew phrase **ad hayyom hazzeh** runs all through Joshua — of Rahab's family living in
Israel, of the heap of stones over Achan, of the cave at Makkedah.

## The two monuments

One at Gilgal, above ground, for the questions.

One in the Jordan, below the water, for nothing anyone can point at.

Both are twelve stones and both mark the same event. The visible one says *this happened*;
the drowned one marks the place it happened from.

## Where the priests were

They stood in the middle of the river while everyone crossed, and did not move until the last
person was over.

Chapter 3: *and the priests who bore the ark of the covenant of the LORD **stood firm on dry
ground in the midst of the Jordan**, and all Israel crossed over on dry ground, until all the
people had crossed completely over*.

The stones mark where they stood, and the water came back over the mark.

## The other buried stone

Jacob at Bethel took the stone he had slept on, set it up as a pillar, poured oil on it and
named the place.

And at Gilgal, the next thing that happens is circumcision — *and the LORD said to Joshua,
"This day I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you"*.

## What is on both banks

Behind them, a pile under a river. In front of them, Jericho.

The first thing after the memorial is the Passover, kept in the plains of Jericho on the
fourteenth day. And the manna stops the day after they eat the produce of the land.

> **The pile that cannot be visited.** Joshua set it up on the ground the priests' feet had
> been on, and the river closed over it — and the account says *they are there to this day*.
