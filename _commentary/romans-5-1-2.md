The verse turns on its first word.

## "Therefore"

Four chapters of argument stand behind it — all have sinned, none is righteous, the
law shuts every mouth, and then righteousness credited to Abraham and to everybody
who believes.

Everything in this sentence is the conclusion of that, not a fresh claim.

## "Having been put right"

*Since we **have been put right with God** through faith.*

The Greek is aorist passive — done, in the past, to us. Not being put right, and not
hoping to be.

Paul has spent chapter 4 on the ledger entry: *it was credited to him as
righteousness*, and *not written for his sake alone, but for ours also.* See the note
on Romans 4:23–25.

## Peace with God

*We are **at peace with God**.*

Not peace *from* God, or the feeling of peace. **Peace with** — the ending of a
hostility.

Paul uses war language about the previous state without embarrassment: *when we were
enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son.*

The treaty was signed at the cross and this verse reports its terms.

## "Through our Lord Jesus Christ"

The peace is not a mood that can be lost on a bad morning. It runs through a Person,
and Paul names Him as the channel in the same breath.

## Brought in

*Through Him we have also been **brought in**, by faith, **to this grace in which we
now stand**.*

The Greek for *brought in* is **prosagoge** — access, introduction. It is the word
for being presented at a royal court by somebody with the standing to do it.

Nobody introduces himself to a king. The word assumes an escort.

## "In which we now stand"

Perfect tense — we have stood and are standing.

Grace is described as a place you are on your feet in, not a favour you keep
applying for.

## The overflow

*And **our joy overflows in the hope of the glory of God**.*

The Greek is to boast, to exult.

And the object is *the glory of God* — the very thing chapter 3 said everybody had
fallen short of. Paul turns the deficit into the expectation, and the distance
between the two is what the first four chapters were for.

## What is not in the sentence

Any condition, any maintenance, any qualification. Justified, at peace, brought in,
standing, and exulting — five settled things in two verses, and every one of them
arrives through the same Person.

> **What comes immediately after.** *And not only that, but our joy holds even in our
> sufferings.* Paul establishes the ground before he mentions the pressure, and the
> pressure never gets a vote on any of the five. See the note on Romans 5:3–5.
