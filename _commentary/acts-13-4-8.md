The first missionary journey begins, and the opposition is waiting at the far end of the
island.

## Who sent them

*Sent out, then, **by the Holy Spirit**.*

Luke says it twice in four verses. The church at Antioch had fasted and laid hands on them
and *sent them away* — and then Luke corrects the record: it was the Spirit who did the
sending.

The instruction had been *separate to Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have
called them*.

## The route

*The two of them went down to **Seleucia**, and from there **sailed to Cyprus**.*

Seleucia is Antioch's port, sixteen miles downriver.

Cyprus is Barnabas' home. Luke has already told us: *Joses, who was also named Barnabas by
the apostles… a Levite of the country of Cyprus*.

The first stop on the first journey is the senior partner's own island.

## The method

*When they reached Salamis they announced the word of God **in the Jewish synagogues**.*

The pattern for the rest of Acts. Paul goes to the synagogue first in every city, and
usually gets thrown out of it.

## The third man

*And they had **John with them as their helper**.*

John Mark. The Greek is **huperetes** — an assistant, an under-rower.

He leaves them at Perga a few verses later, and the argument about whether to take him again
splits Paul and Barnabas so badly that they part company.

He is with Paul at the end, though: *get Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful to me
for ministry*.

## The crossing

*They traveled **the whole length of the island** as far as **Paphos**.*

A hundred miles, east to west. Paphos is the Roman capital and the seat of the governor.

## Who is there

*A certain **sorcerer, a false prophet, a Jewish man named Bar-Jesus**.*

The Greek is **magos** — a magician, the same word as the wise men in Matthew.

**Bar-Jesus** — son of Joshua, son of salvation. A good name on a bad man.

## Where he was employed

*He was **attached to the proconsul, Sergius Paulus**.*

A Roman governor with a court astrologer. Not unusual; Tiberius kept one.

## The governor

*A man of **real intelligence**, who **sent for Barnabas and Saul because he wanted to hear
the word of God**.*

The Greek is **sunetos** — intelligent, discerning.

Luke records his rank and his character, and the invitation comes from him.

## The interference

*But **Elymas the sorcerer — that is what his name means — kept working against them**.*

An imperfect: he kept on doing it. And the Greek is **anthistato** — stood against, resisted.

*Trying to **turn the proconsul away from the faith**.*

The one man with access to the governor is spending it on keeping him from a hearing.

## What happens next

Saul — *who is also called Paul* — looks at him and calls him *son of the devil, enemy of
all righteousness*, and he goes blind *for a time*. See the note on Acts 13:9–12.

Luke switches from *Saul* to *Paul* in the middle of this scene and never goes back.

And Barnabas and Saul becomes Paul and his party for the rest of the book.

## The result

*Then the proconsul believed, when he saw what had been done, being astonished at the
teaching of the Lord.*

The first named convert of the first missionary journey is a Roman governor.

## The name in the middle

*Bar-Jesus.* Luke puts *son of Jesus* and *enemy of all righteousness* in the same
paragraph, and does not comment.

> **What was standing between the governor and the sermon.** His own adviser — a man whose
> name meant son of salvation, working full time to prevent one.
