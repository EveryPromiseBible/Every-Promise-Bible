Matthew's account adds one phrase the others do not have, and it is the phrase
about what the blood is for.

## What Matthew adds

*"This is My blood — the blood of the new covenant, poured out for many **for the
forgiveness of sins**."*

Mark and Luke stop at *for many*. Matthew names the purpose.

This is the Gospel that opened with an angel saying *you shall call His name
Jesus, for He shall save His people from their sins*. Matthew closes the circle
at the table.

## "Every one of you"

*"**Every one of you**, drink from it."*

In Matthew it is a command, not just a report. Mark records that they all drank;
Matthew records that they were all told to.

Nobody at that table had earned it. The betrayer is present and the man who will
deny Him three times before morning is holding a cup.

## Two elements, two things given

The cup is given a stated purpose — forgiveness. The bread is given without one:
*take this and eat it. This is My body.*

Matthew has already told us what the body did. In chapter 8 he quotes Isaiah over
a night of healings: *He took our infirmities and bore our diseases.* The same
writer, the same book.

Whoever eats the bread and drinks the cup is receiving two things, not one. See
the note on 1 Corinthians 11:27–30, where Paul's warning is about failing to
distinguish them.

## Thanks, twice

*He **gave thanks** over it… and when He had **given thanks** He passed it to
them.*

The Greek is **eucharisteō** — the word the meal has been named after ever since.

He gave thanks over bread He was about to call His broken body, hours before the
whipping post. The thanks came first.

## The unfinished cup

*"I will not drink of the fruit of the vine again until the day I drink it new
with you in My Father's kingdom."*

**With you.** Matthew keeps the pronoun. The next cup has a guest list.

> **The hymn.** *Then they sang a hymn together and went out to the Mount of
> Olives.* The Passover ended with the second half of the Hallel — Psalm 118
> among them, which says *the LORD is on my side, I will not fear; what can man do
> to me?* He walked from that hymn into the garden.
