The end of the fourth poem, and it uses a title.

## The hunt

*They stalked our every step, so we could not walk our own streets… **those who chased us moved
faster than eagles** in the sky.*

The Hebrew is **qallim hayu rodefenu minnishrei shamayim** — swifter than the eagles of heaven.
The comparison Jeremiah's contemporaries reached for when describing Babylonian cavalry.

## The title

*The breath of our life, **the LORD's anointed**, was taken in their pits.*

The Hebrew is **meshiach YHWH** — the anointed of the LORD. **Mashiach**.

It is used here of Zedekiah, the last king of Judah, who tried to escape by night, was caught in
the plains of Jericho, watched his sons killed, and was blinded and taken to Babylon in chains.

## The breath

*The breath of our life.*

**Ruach appenu** — the breath of our nostrils.

The phrase is Genesis 2's: *the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and **breathed into
his nostrils the breath of life**.* The nation is saying the king was to them what that breath was
to Adam.

*"**I am the resurrection and the life.** He who believes in Me, though he may die, he shall
live."*

*In Him was life, and the life was the light of men.*

## The shadow

*The one under whose shadow we said, "**Here we will live among the nations**."*

That was the hope for Zedekiah, and it failed in a field outside Jericho.

*He who dwells in the secret place of the Most High shall abide **under the shadow of the
Almighty**.*

*"How often I wanted to gather your children together, **as a hen gathers her chicks under her
wings**, but you were not willing!"*

**Matthew** 23, and the sentence is spoken over the same city about six centuries later.

## The pit again

*Was **taken in their pits**.*

**Nilkad bishchitotam** — caught in their pits. The word for a trap dug and covered.

The third pit in the book. Jeremiah is in a cistern in chapter 3; the last king is caught in a pit
here.

*Him, being delivered by the determined purpose and foreknowledge of God, **you have taken by
lawless hands, have crucified, and put to death**.*

**Peter** at Pentecost. There is one other **mashiach** taken by men who had planned it, and
**Acts** 2 says the taking was inside a purpose rather than outside one — *whom God raised up,
having loosed the pains of death, because it was not possible that He should be held by it.*

## The two anointed ones

Lamentations 4 ends with the anointed one caught, and the nation's hope of living among the
nations ending in a pit.

*"**And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all peoples to Myself.**"*

> **The word.** *Mashiach.* Lamentations uses it of a king caught in a trap on the road out of a
> burning city, and says the nation's whole shelter went with him. It is the same noun the New
> Testament renders **Christos**.
