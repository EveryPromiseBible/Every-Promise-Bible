The last healing Mark records before the triumphal entry, and the man is named.

## The name

*A blind beggar named **Bartimaeus, the son of Timaeus**.*

Mark gives the name and then translates it — *bar Timaeus* means son of Timaeus, so
he says the same thing twice.

Most people Jesus heals in the Gospels are anonymous. This one is not, which readers
have long taken to mean he was known in the early church afterwards.

## What he shouts

*"**Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!**"*

**Son of David** is a messianic title, and this is the first time in Mark anybody
uses it out loud.

A blind beggar on a roadside has worked out what the disciples are still arguing
about — two paragraphs earlier James and John were asking for the best seats.

## The crowd

*Plenty of people told him **sharply to be quiet**, but he **only shouted louder**.*

The Greek is that they rebuked him — the same verb used of Jesus rebuking demons and
the storm.

## What stops Jesus

*Jesus **stopped** and said, "Call him over."*

One word. The whole procession into Jerusalem for the last week of His life halts on
the shout of a man nobody wanted to hear.

## What the crowd says next

*"**Take heart. Get up. He is calling you.**"*

The same people who were telling him to shut up are now helping him to his feet.

## The cloak

*He **threw off his cloak**, jumped to his feet, and came to Jesus.*

A beggar's cloak was his licence and his livelihood — spread on the ground in front
of him to catch coins, and his blanket at night. For a blind man it was also the one
thing he could find again.

He throws it away before he can see, on the strength of a summons.

## The question

*"**What do you want Me to do for you?**"*

The identical question He had asked James and John a few verses earlier. They wanted
thrones.

*"Rabbi, **I want to see**."*

## What He says

*"Go. **Your believing has made you well**."*

The Greek is *your believing has saved you* — **sozo**, the wide word covering healing,
rescue and salvation.

## What he does with the *go*

*And immediately he could see, and **he followed Him down the road**.*

He is told to go, and follows instead — down the road that leads to Jerusalem, and to
the blood covenant, six days away.

> **Where this sits in Mark.** The disciples have just failed to understand the third
> passion prediction and asked for positions. Then a blind man calls Him the Son of
> David, asks for one thing, and follows Him to Jerusalem. Mark puts the sighted
> disciples and the blind beggar side by side on purpose.
