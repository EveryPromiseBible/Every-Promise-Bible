He goes up mid-conversation.

## What they were doing

*As they walked on, **talking together**.*

No ceremony, no farewell speech, no last instruction. They are walking and talking.

## The separation

*Suddenly **a chariot of fire and horses of fire swept between them**.*

The Hebrew is *separated the two of them*.

The chariot's job in the sentence is to divide them. It is not recorded that Elijah rode
in it.

## How he went

*And **Elijah went up to heaven in a whirlwind**.*

The whirlwind, not the chariot. The chapter says so at the top as well: *when the LORD
was about to take up Elijah into heaven **by a whirlwind**.*

The Hebrew is **se'arah** — a storm wind, a tempest. It is the word for what God answers
Job out of.

## The shout

*Elisha saw it and cried out, "**My father! My father! The chariots and horsemen of
Israel!**"*

Two things in one breath.

**My father** — he had asked for a double portion, the inheritance of a firstborn son.
The word he shouts at the moment it is granted is the word for the relation it depends
on. See the note on 2 Kings 2:9–10.

**The chariots and horsemen of Israel** — a military title. He is calling the departing
prophet the nation's actual defence, in a kingdom that had a standing army and
chariotry.

## Where the words come back

Elisha is on his own deathbed, decades later, and King Joash comes and weeps over him
and says the identical sentence: *"My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and their
horsemen!"*

The words Elisha shouted are shouted over Elisha.

## The condition met

*And then **he saw him no more**.*

He saw, which was the condition. And then it was over.

## The tearing

*He **caught hold of his own clothes and tore them in two**.*

The mourning gesture — and the last thing he does before picking up the mantle that
fell.

He tears his own garment and puts on his father's.

## What he had asked for and what he got

He asked to inherit. He gets a shout, a torn coat, and a cloak on the ground.

Then he walks to the Jordan alone and finds the water still parts.

## The other man who did not die

Enoch. *And Enoch walked with God; and he was not, for God took him.* See the note on
Hebrews 11:4–7.

Two men in the whole Old Testament, and Elijah turns up again on a mountain with Moses,
talking with Jesus about His departure.

## What Elijah left

A cloak. That is the whole estate.

> **What the chariot did.** It came between them, and the man it came for went up in
> something else. Elisha shouts about the chariots and horsemen while watching the one
> he calls father go into a storm.
