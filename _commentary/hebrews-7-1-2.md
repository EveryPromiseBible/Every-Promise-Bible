A man who appears in three verses of Genesis and one line of a psalm, and Hebrews builds a
chapter on him.

## Who he is

*Melchizedek **ruled Salem** and was **priest of God Most High**.*

Both offices in one person, which was not how Israel worked. Kings came from Judah and
priests from Levi, and the one king who tried to burn incense came out a leper.

Melchizedek holds both before either tribe exists.

## Where he comes from

Genesis 14, in the middle of a war story. Abraham has just rescued Lot and defeated four
kings.

*Then Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine; he was the priest of God Most
High.* See the note on Genesis 14:17–20.

He arrives, blesses, and leaves. Genesis says nothing else about him.

## The meeting

*Abraham was on his way back from **cutting down the kings** when Melchizedek met him and
**blessed him**, and Abraham **gave him a tenth of everything**.*

The tithe is a hundred and fifty years before the law that requires it.

And Hebrews makes the point about the blessing: *beyond all contradiction, the lesser is
blessed by the better*.

## The first name

*Start with his name: it means **king of righteousness**.*

The Hebrew is **malki-tsedeq** — my king is righteousness, or king of righteousness.

**Tsedeq**, the same root as **tsedaqah**, righteousness.

## The second

*Then take the place he ruled, **Salem, which means peace** — so, king of peace.*

**Shalem**, from **shalom**. And Salem is Jerusalem — the psalm says so: *in Salem also is
His tabernacle, and His dwelling place in Zion*.

## The order

Righteousness first, then peace. Hebrews takes them in the order the names come.

Isaiah has the same sequence: *the work of righteousness will be peace, and the effect of
righteousness, quietness and assurance forever*. See the note on Isaiah 32:15–17.

And Paul: *having been justified by faith, we have peace with God*. See the note on
Romans 5:1–2.

And the psalm where the two meet: *righteousness and peace have kissed*. See the note on
Psalms 85:10–13.

Every time, in that order.

## What Hebrews says next

*Without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end
of life, but made like the Son of God, **remains a priest continually**.*

Not a claim that he had no parents. An argument from the silence of the record: Genesis gives
his lineage nowhere, which for a priest is remarkable, since the whole Levitical system ran
on documented descent.

Ezra records priests excluded from service because *their genealogy was not found*.

## The psalm

*The LORD has sworn and will not relent, "**You are a priest forever according to the order
of Melchizedek**."*

Psalm 110, verse 4. See the note on Psalms 110:1.

Written by David, about somebody he calls *my Lord*, a thousand years after Genesis 14 and a
thousand before Hebrews.

## Why the argument matters

Jesus is from Judah. *For it is evident that our Lord arose from Judah, of which tribe Moses
spoke nothing concerning priesthood.*

Under Levitical rules He could not be a priest at all. Hebrews answers by pointing at a
priest who predates Levi and outranks Abraham.

## The change

*For the priesthood being changed, **of necessity there is also a change of the law**.* See
the note on Hebrews 7:15–17.

> **The order the names come in.** King of righteousness, and then king of peace — and
> Hebrews stops to point it out before saying anything else about him.
