A psalm about a thunderstorm crossing the country from north to south.

## The refrain

*The voice of the LORD.*

The Hebrew is **qol YHWH**, and it occurs seven times in the psalm.

**Qol** is both *voice* and *thunder*, and the psalm uses the ambiguity throughout.

## Where the storm starts

The verses before this one: *the voice of the LORD is over the waters… the voice of the LORD
breaks the cedars, yes, the LORD splinters the cedars of Lebanon*.

Lebanon is the north. The storm comes in off the Mediterranean.

## The mountains

*He makes **Lebanon leap like a calf**, **Sirion like a young wild ox**.*

**Sirion** is the Sidonian name for Mount Hermon — nine thousand feet, snow-capped, the highest
thing in the region.

The psalm has it skipping like livestock.

## The fire

*The voice of the LORD **strikes with flashing fire**.*

The Hebrew is *hews out flames of fire*.

Lightning, described as something cut.

## The wilderness

*The voice of the LORD **shakes the wilderness**. The LORD shakes **the desert of Kadesh**.*

Kadesh is the south, in the Negev.

The storm has crossed the entire country in four verses — sea, Lebanon, Hermon, and now the
southern desert.

## The trees

*The voice of the LORD **twists the oaks and strips the forests bare**.*

The Hebrew is ambiguous and can be read *makes the deer calve*, which some translations follow.
The consonants allow both.

Either way something is being brought forcibly to an end or a beginning.

## The temple

*And **in His temple, all of it cries, Glory!**"*

The Hebrew is **kullo omer kavod** — all of it says, Glory.

Outside: mountains skipping, forests stripped, the desert shaking.

Inside: one word.

## The structure of the psalm

It opens in heaven — *give unto the LORD, O you mighty ones, give unto the LORD glory and
strength*.

It moves through the storm, north to south.

And it ends sitting down: *the LORD sat enthroned at the Flood, and the LORD sits as King
forever*.

## The last verse

*The LORD will **give strength to His people**; the LORD will **bless His people with peace**.*

Eleven verses of a storm tearing across a country, and the last line is peace.

The Hebrew is **shalom**.

## What is under all of it

*The LORD sat enthroned at the Flood.*

The Hebrew is **mabbul** — the word used only of Noah's flood, and here.

The one storm that covered the world, and the psalm's claim is that He was seated during it.

## Where the storm imagery goes

*And behold, there arose a great tempest on the sea, so that the boat was covered with the
waves. **But He was asleep**.*

*Then He arose and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, "Peace, be still!" And the wind
ceased, and there was a great calm.*

See the note on Mark 4:35–41.

The men in the boat ask *who can this be, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?* — and this
psalm is the answer they had been singing all their lives.

> **What is inside while the outside is being flattened.** One word. Mountains skipping,
> forests stripped, the desert shaking — and in the temple, *Glory*.
