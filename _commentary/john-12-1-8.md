A dinner in Bethany, and one person in the room knows what week it is.

## The date

*Six days before the Passover.*

John counts. The next paragraph is the entry into Jerusalem on the following day.

## The place

*Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was — **the one who had died, and whom He had raised from the
dead**.*

Two miles from Jerusalem. The chief priests have already decided *from that day on they plotted to
put Him to death*, and by the end of this chapter they are planning to kill Lazarus too.

## The three

*There they made Him a supper. **Martha served**, and **Lazarus was one of those at the table** with
Him.*

Martha is serving, which is what she was doing in Luke's account. See the note on
Luke 10:38–42.

Lazarus is at the table, alive, in the room.

## Mary

*Then Mary took **a pound of perfume, pure nard, very costly**.*

The Greek is **litran murou nardou pistikes polutimou**.

**Litra** — a Roman pound, about eleven ounces.

**Nardos pistike** — nard, genuine or unadulterated. Imported from the Himalayas, and one of the
most expensive substances traded.

## The value

Judas names it: **three hundred denarii**.

A denarius was a day's wage. Three hundred is close to a year's earnings for a labourer.

## What she does

*And **poured it over the feet of Jesus, and wiped His feet with her hair**.*

Mark and Matthew record the head. John records the feet.

Anointing a head was an honour. Anointing feet was a servant's job, and unbinding her hair in
public was not something a respectable woman did.

## The house

*And **the whole house was filled with the fragrance of it**.*

The Greek is **eplerothe ek tes osmes**.

A detail only somebody in the room would supply.

## The objection

*"**Why was this perfume not sold for three hundred silver coins and the money given to the poor?**"*

Which is a reasonable question, and the arithmetic is right.

## Who asks it

*Judas Iscariot, the son of Simon, **the one who was going to hand Him over**.*

## What John adds

*He said this **not because he cared about the poor, but because he was a thief. He kept the money
bag, and he used to help himself to what was put into it**.*

The Greek is **eklepten** — imperfect. He was stealing, habitually.

John is the only evangelist who says this. He is writing decades later and no longer sees any
reason to be discreet.

## The defence

*"**Leave her alone. She has kept this for the day of My burial**."*

The Greek is **aphes auten** — let her be.

## What that means

Bodies were anointed for burial. Mary has done in advance what the women will come to the tomb on
Sunday morning to do — and find they cannot.

Everyone at that table had heard Him predict the death. She is the only one who acts as though it
is going to happen.

## The last line

*"**The poor you will always have with you, but you will not always have Me**."*

He is quoting Deuteronomy 15, and the half he quotes is followed in the original by *therefore I
command you, saying, "You shall open your hand wide to your brother"*. See the note on
Deuteronomy 15:7–11.

It is not a dismissal of the poor. It is a statement about a window that is closing.

## What Mark records Him saying

*"Wherever this gospel is preached in the whole world, **what this woman has done will also be told
as a memorial to her**."*

## Where Judas goes next

Matthew puts it immediately after: *then one of the twelve, called Judas Iscariot, went to the
chief priests and said, "What are you willing to give me?"*

Thirty pieces of silver, a tenth of what was in the jar.

> **What she knew.** Bodies were anointed for burial. Everybody at the table had heard Him predict
> His death, and she is the only one who acted as though it was going to happen.
