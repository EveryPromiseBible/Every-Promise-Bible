Everything about this healing is done privately, slowly, and by hand.

## The route

*Then He left the region of **Tyre and Sidon** and came down to the Sea of Galilee, **through the
country of the Decapolis**.*

Tyre is north-west. Galilee is south-east. The Decapolis is east.

The itinerary is a long loop through Gentile territory, and Mark records it without explaining why.

## Who lives there

The Decapolis is where the man full of demons was sent home: *tell them what great things the Lord
has done for you*. See the note on Mark 5:18–20.

He went and told it through the Decapolis. This is Jesus arriving in the region afterwards.

## The man

*A man who was **deaf and could barely speak**.*

The Greek is **kōphon kai mogilalon**.

**Mogilalos** — hard of speech. The word appears once in the New Testament and once in the Greek
Old Testament, in Isaiah 35: *the tongue of the dumb shall sing*.

## The request

*They **begged Jesus to lay His hand on him**.*

They ask for a touch. What follows is more than they asked.

## The removal

*So He **took the man away from the crowd, off by themselves**.*

The Greek is **kat' idian apo tou ochlou** — privately, away from the crowd.

## Why it matters here

The man cannot hear. Everything the crowd is saying is noise he cannot use.

The first act of the healing is to take him somewhere he is not being talked about.

## The touching

*He **put His fingers into the man's ears**. He **touched the man's tongue with His own spittle**.*

Two gestures, aimed at the two broken things.

A man who cannot hear is told what is about to happen in the only language available: hands on the
places concerned.

## The look up

*He **looked up into the sky**.*

The Greek is **anablepsas eis ton ouranon**.

Also visible. The man watches Him address someone.

## The sigh

*And He **sighed from somewhere deep inside**.*

The Greek is **estenaxen** — He groaned.

The same root as Paul's word for creation: *the whole creation groans*. See the note on
Romans 8:18–22.

## What it is not

Not effort. Nothing in Mark suggests the healing was difficult.

The groan is put in the record between the touching and the word, and Mark leaves it unexplained.

## The word

*And He said **one word: "Ephphatha"** — which means, "**Be opened**."*

Aramaic. Mark keeps the sound and then translates it, as he does with **Talitha koum** and
**Abba**.

## The passive

*Be opened.* Not *open*.

## The result

*And **the man's ears were opened**, and **whatever had bound his tongue came loose**, and he spoke
clearly.*

The Greek is **elysē ho desmos tēs glōssēs** — the bond of his tongue was loosed.

A binding is named and released. The same verb Jesus used of the woman bent for eighteen years:
*ought she not to be loosed from this bond?*

## The instruction

*Jesus told them **not to tell anyone**. But **the more He told them, the more they went on spreading
the news**.*

Mark's recurring pattern. The silence is commanded and never kept.

## What they said

*"**Everything He does, He does well**," they kept saying.*

The Greek is **kalōs panta pepoiēken**.

The Septuagint of Genesis 1: *and God saw everything that He had made, and indeed it was very
good*.

## The last line

*"He even **makes the deaf hear and gives the speechless their voice**."*

Isaiah 35, almost exactly: *then the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped… and the tongue of the
dumb shall sing*. See the note on Isaiah 35:5–6.

A Gentile crowd, without meaning to, quotes the messianic prophecy at Him.

> **The privacy.** He takes a deaf man out of the crowd first. The first thing done for him is to
> get him away from the conversation he cannot hear.
