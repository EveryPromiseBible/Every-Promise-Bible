David asks for his words to be treated as temple furniture.

## The urgency

*LORD, **I call to You. Come quickly**.*

The Hebrew is *hasten to me*. It is how a lot of David's psalms open, and he never
apologises for it.

## What he asks first

*Listen to my voice **when I cry**.*

Not when he composes. When he cries out.

## The request

*Let my prayer **stand before You like incense**.*

The Hebrew is *let my prayer be set forth* — **kun**, to be established, fixed, made
ready. It is the word used for setting a thing in its proper place.

He is asking for his words to be positioned where the incense was positioned: in front
of the veil, on the golden altar, directly outside the room where God said He would
meet them.

## Why that is a large request

David was not a priest. He was of Judah, not Levi, and the incense altar was off limits
to him entirely — Uzziah, a king of his own line, tried it and came out a leper.

He asks for his prayer to go where his body could not.

## The second half

*Let **my lifted hands** be **the evening offering**.*

The evening sacrifice was at the ninth hour — three in the afternoon — a lamb on the
bronze altar, every day without fail.

It is the hour Elijah prayed on Carmel, the hour Gabriel reached Daniel, and the hour
Jesus died.

## The two altars

The verse names both. Incense for the golden altar inside, and the offering for the
bronze altar in the court.

David asks for one prayer to count as both.

## The lifted hands

The standard posture for prayer in the Old Testament, and the standard posture of the
priest lifting the offering.

Paul keeps it: *I desire that the men pray everywhere, lifting up holy hands.*

## What he prays next

*Set a guard, O LORD, over my mouth; keep watch over the door of my lips.*

Having asked for his words to be accepted as incense, the next thing he asks is a
sentry on his mouth.

## The answer given later

*Every one of them had a harp, and a gold bowl full of burning incense — **and the
incense is the prayers of God's people**.* See the note on Revelation 5:7–8.

David asked for it. John saw the bowls.

## What the incense was made of

Four spices, equal parts, salted, compounded by a perfumer, and unlawful to make for
private use. See the note on Exodus 30:34–38.

> **What he is asking to be counted.** A voice, and a pair of raised hands. He puts them
> in the place of the two things the priesthood did every day and asks God to take them
> as such.
