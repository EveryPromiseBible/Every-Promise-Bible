The acquisition list, and the man writing it had genuinely acquired all of it.

## What is on it

Servants bought and servants born in the house. Herds and flocks *more than anyone who came
before me in Jerusalem*. Silver and gold, *the treasure of kings and provinces*. Singers, men
and women. And then a clause that gives up on the inventory: *every delight the human heart
could want, and more*.

## The pronoun

*I bought… I owned… **I gathered silver and gold for myself**.*

The first person runs through the whole chapter. Thirty-eight occurrences of *I* in
twenty-three verses, and *for myself* attached to most of the verbs.

## What it was for

*I said in my heart, "Come now, I will test you with mirth; therefore enjoy pleasure"; but
surely, this also was vanity.*

It is not greed being described. It is an experiment, run by a man with the resources to run it
properly, and reported honestly when it failed.

## Who looked at the same wardrobe

*"Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow: they neither toil nor spin; and yet I say to
you that **even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these**."*

**Jesus** picks the richest man in the nation's memory as His comparison and says a weed in a
field beats him — and the reason He gives is not that the clothes were shabby. It is that the
lily did not have to get them.

*"Therefore do not worry… for **your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things**."*

## And who stood in the same city

*"The queen of the South will rise up in the judgment with this generation… for she came from
the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and indeed **a greater than Solomon is
here**."*

**Matthew** puts the two side by side deliberately. The man who wrote the acquisition list is
the standard, and the standard is exceeded by somebody with nowhere to lay His head.

## What was said of that Man's wealth

*For you know the grace of our Lord **Jesus Christ**, that though **He was rich, yet for your
sakes He became poor**, that you through His poverty might become rich.*

**Paul** runs Ecclesiastes 2 backwards. Solomon acquired everything and found it vapour;
**2 Corinthians** has somebody who had everything, released it, and made other people rich by
doing it.

> **The two words attached to nearly every verb.** *For myself.* Solomon reports the experiment
> honestly, and Jesus later picks him as the example of the best a man can do on his own
> account — and compares him unfavourably to a flower.
