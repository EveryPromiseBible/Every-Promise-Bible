Paul traces everything back to one man and then names who the man was a picture of.

## The chain

*Sin made its way into the world **through one man**, and **death came in on sin's
heels**, and so death came through to everyone.*

Three steps: one man, sin enters, death follows, and it reaches everybody.

The Greek for *made its way in* is to enter alongside — sin came in as a
gate-crasher.

## The proof he offers

*Sin was in the world **before the law was given**, but **sin is not charged to
anyone's account where there is no law**. **Yet death reigned from Adam to Moses**.*

This is Paul's argument and it is worth following slowly.

Between Adam and Sinai there was no written law. On his own accounting, nothing could
be formally charged to anybody's account in that period.

And people died anyway, for two and a half thousand years.

So death was not being handed out for individual breaches of a code. Something else
was in operation, and Paul says what it was: one man's act, reaching everybody.

## "Even over those who had not sinned in the same way Adam disobeyed"

Adam broke a stated command with a stated penalty. Nobody between him and Moses was
in that position, and they died too.

## The turn

*And **Adam is a pattern of the One who was coming**.*

The Greek is **typos** — a stamp, an impression, a die.

Adam is described as the mould, and Christ as what it was made for.

## Where the comparison goes

The rest of the chapter runs it out: *as by one man's disobedience many were made
sinners, so also **by one Man's obedience many will be made righteous**.*

The mechanism is the same in both directions. Adam's act reached people who were not
consulted; Paul says Christ's works the same way.

That is the whole argument, and it is why he needed the Adam-to-Moses point first —
to establish that an act of one man can be credited to people who did not perform it.

## "Much more"

Five times in this chapter.

Paul never argues that grace merely balances the damage. Every comparison is
weighted: *much more the grace of God and the gift by the grace of the one Man, Jesus
Christ, abounded to many.* See the note on Romans 5:15–17.

## What comes after

*Where sin increased, grace overflowed all the more.* See the note on
Romans 5:20–21.

And then the objection Paul knows is coming: *shall we keep on sinning, so that there
will be all the more grace?*

> **The one word the chapter turns on.** *Reigned.* Death reigned from Adam to Moses;
> sin reigned in death; and then *those who receive abundance of grace and the gift of
> righteousness **will reign in life** through the One, Jesus Christ.*
