The gifts listed here are people, and what they are for is stated in a purpose clause.

## What has come before

*But to each one of us grace was given according to the measure of Christ's gift.*

*Therefore He says: "When He ascended on high, **He led captivity captive, and gave gifts to men**."*

Psalm 68, quoted. The image is a returning conqueror distributing spoil.

## What the gifts are

*And He is the One who gave **some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, some pastors and
teachers**.*

The Greek is **edoken** — He gave.

Not abilities. People.

Elsewhere the gifts are functions — prophecy, service, teaching. Here the gift is the person
holding the office.

## The list

**Apostoloi** — sent ones.

**Prophetai** — prophets.

**Euangelistai** — evangelists. The word appears three times in the New Testament.

**Poimenas kai didaskalous** — pastors and teachers. One article covers both, which is why they
are often read as a single office: shepherd-teachers.

**Poimen** — shepherd. Its only use as a church office is here.

## What they are for

*Given to **get God's holy people ready and equipped for the work of serving**.*

The Greek is **pros ton katartismon ton hagion eis ergon diakonias**.

**Katartismos** — the setting of a broken bone, the mending of nets. Putting something into
working order.

## The grammar that matters

The Greek can be read two ways, depending on whether a comma is placed after *saints*.

With the comma: the leaders do the equipping, the ministering and the building.

Without it: the leaders equip the saints, and the saints do the ministering.

The second reading has become standard, and the sentence structure supports it.

## The result

*So that **the body of Christ is built up**.*

The Greek is **eis oikodomen tou somatos tou Christou** — for the building up of the body.

## The destination

*Until all of us **arrive together at one faith and one knowing of the Son of God**.*

The Greek is **mechri katantesomen hoi pantes** — until we all arrive.

**Katantao** — to reach a destination, used of a ship making port.

## The two markers

**Henoteta tes pisteos** — the unity of the faith.

**Epignoseos tou huiou tou Theou** — the full knowledge of the Son of God.

## The measure

*At **full-grown manhood**, at **the whole measure and height of everything Christ is**.*

The Greek is **eis andra teleion** — to a mature man.

**Eis metron helikias tou pleromatos tou Christou** — to the measure of the stature of the fullness
of Christ.

Not a standard anybody sets for themselves.

## What it protects against

*That we should **no longer be children, tossed to and fro and carried about with every wind of
doctrine**.*

The Greek is **kludonizomenoi** — storm-tossed, like a boat in a swell.

## The alternative

*But, **speaking the truth in love**, may grow up in all things into Him who is the head — Christ.*

The Greek is **aletheuontes en agape** — truthing in love. A verb, not a noun.

## The whole body

*From whom the whole body, joined and knit together by what every joint supplies, **according to the
effective working by which every part does its share**, causes growth of the body for the edifying of
itself in love.*

**Every part does its share** — which is why the equipping goes to the saints.

> **The gift.** Not abilities distributed — people given. And the purpose clause says what they are
> for: getting everybody else ready to do the work.
