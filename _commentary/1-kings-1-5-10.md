A prince crowns himself, and the narrator stops mid-story to explain why.

## The line

**1:6** *His father had **never once crossed him**, never asked, "**Why do you do
this?**"*

The Hebrew is *never grieved him at any time by saying*. Not once, in his whole
life, had David asked him a question about his conduct.

The writer puts it in as the explanation. It is not an aside.

## What it produced

**1:5** *Adonijah began to **strut and swell** with ambition. "**I will be king**," he told
himself.*

He does not consult David, who is still alive. He does not consult the prophet. He
says it to himself and then acts on it.

**1:5** again: *He got himself **chariots and horsemen, and fifty men to run ahead of him**.*

The trappings first. He arranges the appearance of a king before there is anything
behind it.

## Where he learned it

**1:6** again: *He was a handsome man, **born just after Absalom**.*

Absalom did exactly the same thing, **2 Samuel 15:1**: *Absalom prepared chariots and horses, and
fifty men to run before him* — and it ended with him hanging in an oak tree.

David watched one son do it and did not ask the next one a single question.

Absalom's rebellion also had a history. Amnon violated Tamar, and, **2 Samuel 13:21**: *when King David
heard of all these things, he was very angry* — and did nothing. Two years later
Absalom killed Amnon.

Three sons, one pattern, and the same silence behind each of them.

## Who joined him

**1:7** *Joab, the commander of the army, and **Abiathar the priest***.

The army and the priesthood. Adonijah had the two institutions that mattered.

## Who did not

**1:8** *Zadok the priest, Benaiah, **Nathan the prophet**, Shimei, Rei, and David's mighty
men.*

Nathan is the man who came to David after Bathsheba with, **2 Samuel 12:7**: *you are the man.* He is
on the other side, and it is Nathan who goes to Bathsheba and gets Solomon crowned
before the day is out.

## The guest list

**1:9–10** *He invited all his royal brothers and all the king's men of Judah. **But he did
not invite Nathan the prophet, or Benaiah, or the mighty men, or his brother
Solomon.**"*

He knew exactly who would say no. The omissions are a complete list of everyone
with the standing to stop him.

## What it is not

Nothing in the passage suggests David should have been harsh with him. David was
never harsh with anybody in his family, and that is the problem the verse names.

He never asked. *Why do you do this?* — one question, and it was never put.

> **How it ends.** Solomon lets him live, **1:52**: *if he proves himself a worthy man, not
> one hair of his shall fall to the earth.* Adonijah then asks for Abishag, the
> girl who had nursed David — a claim on the throne by another route — and Solomon
> has him executed. The strutting outlives the reprieve.
