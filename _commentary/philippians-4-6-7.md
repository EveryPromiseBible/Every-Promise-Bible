An instruction not to worry, written from a prison cell.

## Where Paul is

In custody in Rome, chained to a guard, with his case unheard.

*I want you to know that the things which happened to me have actually turned out for the
furtherance of the gospel*, and *my chains are in Christ… in the whole palace guard*.

## What comes before

*Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice!*

*Let your gentleness be known to all men. **The Lord is at hand**.* See the note on
Philippians 4:4–5.

The Greek is **ho Kurios engus** — the Lord is near.

## The command

*Do not be anxious about **anything**.*

The Greek is **meden merimnate** — nothing be anxious about.

**Merimnao** — to be pulled apart, divided. The verb Jesus uses of Martha: *you are worried and
troubled about many things*. See the note on Luke 10:38–42.

## The replacement

*But in **every single thing**.*

**En panti** — in everything.

Nothing, and everything. Paul sets the two words against each other in consecutive clauses.

## The three ways

*By **prayer** and by **asking**, with **thanks** alongside.*

**Proseuche** — prayer generally, the word for approaching God.

**Deesis** — petition, the specific request arising out of need.

**Eucharistia** — thanksgiving.

## Where the thanksgiving goes

*Alongside.*

**Meta eucharistias** — with thanksgiving.

Not afterwards, when it works out. Simultaneously, in the same sentence as the request.

## The instruction

*Let your requests **be laid out before God**.*

The Greek is **gnorizestho pros ton Theon** — let them be made known toward God.

Which He already knows. *Your Father knows exactly what you need long before you ask Him.* See
the note on Matthew 6:5–8.

The laying out is not for His information.

## What is promised

Not the request granted.

*And **the peace of God**.*

The Greek is **he eirene tou Theou**.

## The description

*Which **reaches far beyond anything our minds can take in**.*

The Greek is **he huperechousa panta noun** — surpassing all understanding.

**Huperecho** — to hold above, to rise over.

It can mean the peace exceeds what the mind can grasp, or that it exceeds what the mind can
manage on its own. Both readings fit a man in a cell.

## The verb

*Will **stand guard over** your hearts and your thoughts.*

The Greek is **phrouresei** — will garrison, will keep watch as a military post.

**Phroureo** — the word for a garrison holding a city. Paul uses it of being kept under guard in
Damascus.

He is chained to a soldier while he writes it.

## What is guarded

*Your **hearts** and your **thoughts**.*

**Kardias kai ta noemata** — hearts and mental faculties.

The same **nous** that could not comprehend the peace in the previous clause is what the peace
stands over.

## Where

*In Christ Jesus.*

## What follows

*Finally, brethren, whatever things are true, whatever things are noble, whatever things are just…
**meditate on these things**.* See the note on Philippians 4:8–9.

Six categories, and then *the God of peace will be with you*.

Verse 7 gives the peace of God; verse 9 gives the God of peace.

## What he says about his own state

*Not that I speak in regard to need, for **I have learned in whatever state I am, to be content**.*

The Greek is **autarkes** — self-sufficient, the Stoics' word.

*I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me.* See the note on
Philippians 4:11–13.

> **The verb.** **Phroureo** — to garrison, to hold a post. Written by a man under guard, about
> what stands over the mind that could not take it in.
