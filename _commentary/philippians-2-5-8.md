title: "Seven steps down"

The highest thing ever written about Christ, and Paul's reason for writing it is that two women in
the church could not get on.

## What he is actually settling

*Let **the mind** that was in Christ Jesus be the mind in you as well.*

**Phroneite** — think this among yourselves. Two chapters later the same verb turns up with two
names attached: *Euodia and Syntyche — **think the same way** together in the Lord.*

So the most exalted paragraph in the New Testament was written into a squabble. Paul does not
correct the Philippians with a rule. He shows them a Person going downstairs, and lets that do the
work.

## Not a prize to be clutched

*He who, being **in the form of God**, did not treat equality with God as a prize to be grasped.*

**Morphe** is the essential form — what a thing truly is, not how it looks. And **harpagmos** is
plunder, something snatched and held on to.

He did not need to hold it. A man clutches what he is afraid of losing; nobody at the quarrel in
Philippi was secure enough to let anything go, and He was secure enough to let go of everything.

## Emptied — by adding

*But **emptied Himself**, taking the form of a servant.*

**Ekenosen** — He poured Himself out. And Paul tells you in the same breath what He poured Himself
into: **morphen doulou labon**, *having taken the form of a slave*.

The emptying happens by addition. He did not put down deity; He picked up humanity, and the two
words are the same — **morphe** of God, **morphe** of a slave. Whatever the first one means, the
second one means as truly.

Then the word changes: *being found in the **shape** of a man* — **schema**, the outward figure. He
was truly a slave and He was recognisably a man.

And the word for servant is not **diakonos**, the waiter. It is **doulos**. Property.

## The stairs

Count them as Paul sets them down. Equality with God — not held. Emptied. The form of a slave. The
likeness of men. Found as a man. Humbled Himself. Obedient as far as death.

And then Paul adds one more step, as though the bottom had not been reached: *and that, **a death on
a cross**.*

Crucifixion was the execution Rome kept for slaves. He took the form of a slave at the top of the
stairs and died the death of one at the bottom. Nothing in the descent was done to Him that He had
not first accepted; the verbs are all active until the last one.

## Which way the sentence runs

*Therefore **God also has highly exalted Him**.*

Every step down was taken by Him. Every step up is done to Him. Paul will not let the humility be
something Christ arranged in order to be raised — the raising is the Father's answer, given after
the obedience was complete and unrewarded.

> **The argument under the hymn.** Paul is not asking the Philippians to try harder at getting on.
> He is telling them whose mind they are already in — *in Christ Jesus* — and that the mind found
> there is the mind of Someone who had every right in the universe and did not stand on one of
> them. You do not manufacture that. You look at Him until it starts to look reasonable.
