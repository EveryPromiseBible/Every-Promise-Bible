The third temptation, and it is the only one that does not begin *if You are the Son of God*.

## The setting

*Once more the devil took Him, this time to **a very high mountain**.*

The first two were stones into bread and the pinnacle of the temple. See the notes on
Matthew 4:1–4 and Matthew 4:5–7.

## The view

*And showed Him **every kingdom in the world and all their splendor**.*

No mountain has that view. Luke adds *in a moment of time*.

## The offer

*"**All of this I will give You**."*

Luke has the fuller version: *all this authority I will give You, and their glory; **for this
has been delivered to me, and I give it to whomever I wish***.

The claim is not disputed. Jesus calls him *the ruler of this world* three times in John, and
John's letter says *the whole world lies under the sway of the wicked one*.

## What is being offered

The kingdoms. Which He is going to get anyway.

*Ask of Me, and I will give You the nations for Your inheritance.*

*The kingdoms of this world have become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ.*

The offer is not of something false. It is the real inheritance, on different terms and without
a cross.

## The price

*"**If You will fall down and worship me**."*

One act.

## The answer

*"**Go away, Satan**."*

The Greek is **hupage** — be gone, get out.

The first two temptations got a quotation. This one gets a dismissal first.

He uses the same word to Peter, later, when Peter tells Him He must not go to Jerusalem: *get
behind Me, Satan! You are an offence to Me, for you are not mindful of the things of God*.

Same offer, from a friend: a Messiah without a cross.

## The quotation

*"For it is written: '**Worship the Lord your God, and serve Him only**.'"*

Deuteronomy 6:13. See the note on Deuteronomy 6:13–15.

All three answers come from Deuteronomy, and all three from the same few chapters — the ones
about the wilderness.

He is forty days into a wilderness, quoting the manual of a nation that spent forty years in
one and failed the same tests.

They wanted bread; He refuses to make it. They tested God at Massah; He refuses to jump. They
made a calf; He refuses the kingdoms.

## The result

*So **the devil left Him**.*

Luke adds the chilling clause: *he departed from Him **until an opportune time**.*

## What arrives

*And **angels came and cared for Him**.*

The Greek is **diekonoun** — they were serving, ministering. An imperfect: they kept at it.

He had refused to command stones into bread, and he is fed.

The same word is used of the women who followed and *provided for Him from their substance*.

## Where the mountain turns up again

*Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "**All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on
earth**."*

On a mountain in Galilee, after the resurrection.

All authority, given — the thing that had been offered on a mountain three years earlier, and
refused.

> **What was on offer.** Not a lie. The kingdoms were genuinely his to hand over, and they were
> genuinely the inheritance — the only variable was the price.
