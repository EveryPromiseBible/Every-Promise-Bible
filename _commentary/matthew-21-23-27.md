The examination begins, and it runs for three days.

## What is happening this week

The lamb was brought into the house on the tenth of Nisan and kept until the
fourteenth — *you shall keep it up until the fourteenth day* — and in those days it
was watched for any blemish.

Jesus rode in on the tenth. From this point until the Passover, one delegation
after another comes at Him with questions.

- **the chief priests and elders** — by what authority?
- **the Pharisees and Herodians** — is it lawful to pay taxes to Caesar?
- **the Sadducees** — the woman with seven husbands, whose wife is she?
- **a lawyer** — which is the great commandment?

Four inspections in two chapters, from four groups who agreed on almost nothing
else, and every one of them goes away with nothing.

Pilate's verdict at the end of the week is the standard formula: *I find no fault
in Him.* Herod finds none. Pilate's wife, Judas, and the thief on the cross all say
the same thing in their own words.

The lamb was examined and passed.

## The question they ask

*"**By what authority** are You doing these things? And **who gave You** this
authority?"*

They are asking about the tables. Nobody licensed Him to clear the court.

## What He does with it

*"I will ask you one question. If you answer it for Me, I will tell you."*

He does not refuse to answer. He puts a condition on it, and the condition is a
question they cannot afford to answer.

## The question He asks

*"**Where did John's baptism come from? From heaven, or from men?**"*

It is exactly parallel to theirs. They asked where His authority came from; He asks
where John's did.

If John was from heaven, then John's testimony about Him settles their question
already — *behold, the Lamb of God.*

## Their working

Matthew records their deliberation, which is the most damning thing in the passage:

*"If we say, 'From heaven,' He will ask us why we did not believe him. But if we
say, 'From men,' **we have the crowd to fear**."*

Not one clause about what is true. Both options are weighed entirely by
consequence.

## The answer they give

*"We do not know."*

Men whose office was to rule on exactly this kind of question — that was the
Sanhedrin's job — say in public that they cannot tell.

## And His

*"**Then I will not tell you** by what authority I do these things."*

He keeps the terms of the deal precisely.

> **What He tells them instead.** Three parables, straight afterwards, all aimed at
> them: the two sons, the wicked tenants, and the wedding feast. He gives them no
> answer about authority and then describes them to their faces for the rest of the
> chapter.
