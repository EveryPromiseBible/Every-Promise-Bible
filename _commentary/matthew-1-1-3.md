The New Testament opens with a list, and the fourth name in it is a woman with a scandal attached.

## The first sentence

*This is **the family line of Jesus the Messiah, the son of David, the son of Abraham**.*

The Greek is **biblos geneseos** — the book of the genealogy, or the book of the origin.

The same phrase the Septuagint uses at Genesis 5: *this is the book of the generations of Adam*.

Matthew opens the New Testament with the words that open a section of the first book of the Old.

## The two names

**Son of David.** **Son of Abraham.**

David gives the throne — the covenant that a descendant would reign forever.

Abraham gives the promise that *in your seed all the nations of the earth shall be blessed*.

Kingship and universality, in the first line, in that order.

## The order they are named in

David before Abraham, which is backwards chronologically.

Matthew leads with the throne, because the argument of his Gospel is that this is the king.

## The form

*Abraham was the father of Isaac. Isaac was the father of Jacob.*

The Greek **egennesen** — begot — repeated forty times.

It is the standard Hebrew genealogical formula, and its rhythm is the point. A list of fathers,
each handing on to the next.

## The break

*Judah was the father of Perez and Zerah, **and their mother was Tamar**.*

The first woman in the list, and Matthew has gone out of his way to include her. Genealogies did
not name mothers.

## Who Tamar was

Judah's daughter-in-law. Twice widowed, denied the third son she was owed, she dressed as a
prostitute, sat at the roadside, and conceived twins by her father-in-law.

*She has been more righteous than I*, Judah says, when the evidence is produced. See the note on
Genesis 38:24–26.

## The other three

**Rahab** — the prostitute of Jericho who hid the spies.

**Ruth** — a Moabitess, from a nation barred from the assembly to the tenth generation.

*Solomon by **her who had been the wife of Uriah***. Matthew does not write Bathsheba's name. He
writes the name of the man David had killed.

Four women in a royal genealogy, and all four have irregularity attached.

## Why they might be there

Every one of them is either a foreigner or the centre of a scandal, or both.

Matthew is introducing a king whose line runs through Tamar at the roadside and Rahab on the
wall.

## The structure

*So all the generations from Abraham to David are **fourteen** generations, from David until the
captivity in Babylon are **fourteen**, and from the captivity to the Christ are **fourteen**.*

Matthew says so himself. He has arranged it, and he tells the reader he has — several names are
omitted to make the count work.

The numerical value of the Hebrew letters in **David** is fourteen.

## The last link

*Jacob begot Joseph **the husband of Mary**, of whom was born Jesus.*

The formula breaks at the end. Not *Joseph begot Jesus*.

The Greek relative pronoun is feminine singular — **ex hes**, of whom — pointing to Mary alone.

Forty repetitions of *begot*, and the chain does not close the way it has closed forty times.

## The name

*"And you shall call His name **JESUS**, for He will save His people from their sins."* See the
note on Matthew 1:20–21.

**Yeshua** — the LORD saves.

## What Luke does instead

A different genealogy, running the other direction, back past Abraham to *Adam, the son of God*.
See the note on Luke 3:23–27.

Matthew stops at Abraham. Luke does not stop.

> **The break in the formula.** Forty times *begot*, and then not. The relative pronoun at the end
> is feminine, and it points at one person.
