Paul's argument here runs on a single comparison, and everything depends on the
two sides being the same shape.

## The parallel

| through one man | through the One |
|---|---|
| one trespass | one act of righteousness |
| condemnation to everyone | the setting-right that is life |
| the many were **made sinners** | the many will be **made righteous** |

The same verb sits on both sides — *kathistemi*, to appoint, constitute, place
into a category. Not treated as. **Made.**

## Why the symmetry matters

Nobody chose to be made a sinner by Adam's act. It arrived by connection, not by
decision, and the effects were real long before anyone understood them.

Paul insists the second half works identically. The righteousness comes by the
obedience of the One, into a group described in exactly the same terms. If the
first transfer was real, so is the second — that is the whole force of the
argument.

## "One act of righteousness"

The Greek is singular and concrete: one righteous deed. Paul does not point at
Christ's whole life of obedience in general but at a single act, and in this
letter that is the cross.

Against one trespass, one obedience. The books are balanced by matching pieces.

## Where it lands

*So also through the obedience of the One the many will be made righteous.*

The righteousness on offer is not a reward for the reader's obedience. It comes
by *His* obedience, credited across the same way the trespass was.

> **The verse that follows.** Paul says the law came in so that the trespass would
> increase — *but where sin increased, grace increased all the more.* He is not
> trying to make sin look small. He builds it as high as it goes and then says
> grace went higher.
