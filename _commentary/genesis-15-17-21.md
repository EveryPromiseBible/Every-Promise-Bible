The oath is sworn by one party, and the other one is asleep.

## The hour

*When **the sun had fully set and darkness came**.*

Abram laid the pieces out in daylight and drove birds off them all afternoon. See the note on
Genesis 15:9–11.

Nothing happened until dark.

## What appears

*A **smoking firepot and a blazing torch** appeared.*

The Hebrew is **tannur ashan** — a smoking oven. And **lappid esh** — a torch of fire.

**Tannur** is a domestic bread oven, a clay cylinder.

## The movement

*And **passed between the pieces** — God Himself moving through, sealing the covenant.*

The Hebrew is **avar bein hagezarim** — passed between the pieces.

## What that act means

The self-maledictory oath. Walking the aisle between the halves says: *let this be done to me if I
fail to keep this*.

## Who does not walk

Abram.

*A deep sleep fell upon Abram*, four verses earlier. He is unconscious on the ground while it
happens.

## What kind of covenant that makes it

Both parties should walk. Only one does.

The covenant is therefore unilateral in its guarantee. Its keeping does not depend on the man it is
made with, because he never swore to anything.

## The consequence

If it fails, the penalty falls on the party who walked.

## The grant

*"**To your offspring I give this land**, from **the river of Egypt** to **the great river, the
Euphrates**."*

The Hebrew is **natatti** — I have given. Perfect tense, for land not entered for four hundred more
years.

## The borders

Two rivers, the widest boundaries ever stated for the land.

Reached, briefly, under Solomon.

## The list

*The land of the **Kenites, Kenizzites, Kadmonites, Hittites, Perizzites, Rephaites, Amorites,
Canaanites, Girgashites, and Jebusites**.*

Ten nations. The lists elsewhere usually give six or seven.

## What is not said

No condition. No *if*. Nothing Abram is required to do.

The chapter is a grant, not a treaty.

## What he was told during the darkness

*"Know certainly that **your descendants will be strangers in a land that is not theirs**… four
hundred years."* And: *"**in the fourth generation they shall return here**, for the iniquity of the
Amorites is not yet complete."* See the note on Genesis 15:12–16.

The delay is explained as patience toward the people currently living there.

## The other covenant, and its condition

Sinai. *"**All that the LORD has spoken we will do**."* See the note on Exodus 19:7–8.

That one is bilateral, and both parties speak.

## What Paul makes of the order

*The law, which was **four hundred and thirty years later**, cannot annul the covenant that was
confirmed before by God in Christ, that it should make the promise of no effect.* See the note on
Galatians 3:15–18.

A later agreement does not cancel an earlier grant.

## Where the fire and smoke go

*The LORD went before them by day in a pillar of cloud… and **by night in a pillar of fire**.* See the
note on Exodus 13:21–22.

The same two elements, leading the descendants out of the land the sleeping man was told about.

> **The single signature.** Both parties should walk between the pieces. God put Abram to sleep and
> walked it alone — which puts the whole penalty on one side.
