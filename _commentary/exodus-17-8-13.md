Israel's first battle after the exodus, and it is won on a hilltop rather than in
the valley.

## Where it happened

*The Amalekites came and attacked Israel **at Rephidim**.*

The name means *resting places*, or places of refreshment.

They had just come through the sea and been given water out of a rock, and the attack
lands at the place named for rest.

## Who Amalek was

Esau's grandson, and Deuteronomy describes the method: they *met you on the road and
cut down all the stragglers at the rear, when you were weary and worn out.* See the
note on Deuteronomy 25:17–19.

They attack from behind, at the point of exhaustion.

## The two assignments

*"**Choose some men and go out to fight** Amalek. **Tomorrow I will stand on top of the
hill** with the staff of God in my hand."*

Joshua in the valley with a sword. Moses on the hill with a staff.

Both are given, and the passage is about the relationship between them.

## The correlation

*As long as Moses **held his hands up, Israel prevailed**; but whenever he **let his
hands down, Amalek prevailed**.*

Joshua's fighting is not what varies. The men, the swords and the tactics stay the
same all day.

The only variable in the account is a pair of arms on a hill a mile away.

## What happens to Moses

*When Moses' arms **grew tired**.*

The Hebrew is heavy. He is eighty, and it is an all-day battle.

The man holding up the staff of God cannot hold up his own arms.

## The stone and the two men

*They took a stone and put it under him, and **he sat on it**, while **Aaron and Hur
held up his hands — one on each side** — so his hands **stayed steady until the sun went
down**.*

Three details, and each one matters.

He sits. The posture changes from standing to seated, and the account does not treat
that as a failure.

Two men hold the hands up. Not his own strength, and not one helper — one on each
side.

And *stayed steady* — the Hebrew is **emunah**, the word for faithfulness, steadiness.
It is the root of *amen*, and it is used here of a pair of hands.

## The result

*And Joshua **overcame the Amalekite army** with the sword.*

Both halves are true. The sword did the work in the valley, and the hands on the hill
decided it.

## What Moses builds

*Moses built an altar and called its name **The-LORD-Is-My-Banner**.*

Hebrew **Yahweh-Nissi**. A banner is what an army rallies to and fights under.

And then: *the LORD will have war with Amalek from generation to generation.*

## The thing that cannot be won by effort

Amalek is the one enemy the Old Testament says will be fought in every generation. It
is not defeated once.

Saul was told to finish it and did not, and an Amalekite is standing over him on
Gilboa. Haman the Agagite is signing a decree centuries later. See the notes on
2 Samuel 1:5–10 and Esther 3:1–4.

The one enemy that is never beaten by effort is beaten here under raised hands
somebody else was holding.

> **What the day required.** A young man fighting, an old man seated on a stone, and
> two friends who stood there from morning until sundown holding his arms. Exodus
> names all four.
