The psalm has already covered God's knowledge and God's presence. This is the part about
manufacture.

## The claim

*For **You formed my inmost parts**.*

The Hebrew is **kilyotai** — literally the kidneys. In Hebrew idiom the kidneys are the seat
of the innermost self, the way the heart is in English.

*You created my inward parts* is the older rendering, and the verb is **qanah** — acquired,
got, formed. The verb Eve uses at Cain's birth.

## The weaving

*You **knit me together** in my mother's womb.*

The Hebrew is **sakak** — to weave, to screen, to hedge in. The same root as the covering
cherubim over the ark.

## The response

*I praise You, for **I am fearfully and wonderfully made**.*

The Hebrew is *I am fearfully distinguished* — **nora'ot niphleti**. Awesomely set apart.

The reaction to the anatomy is worship, not analysis.

## The confirmation

*Wonderful are Your works, and **my soul knows it well**.*

The Hebrew is *my soul knows it very well* — **yoda'at me'od**.

The one thing the psalmist claims to know thoroughly, in a psalm mostly about what he cannot
know.

Four verses earlier: *such knowledge is too wonderful for me; it is high, I cannot attain
it*.

## What was not hidden

*My **frame was not hidden from You** when I was **made in secret**.*

The Hebrew is **otsmi** — my bone, my substance.

Secret from everyone else. Not from Him.

## The location

*Woven together in **the depths of the earth**.*

The Hebrew is *in the lowest parts of the earth* — **tachtiyyot arets**.

An odd phrase for a womb. It is the language used elsewhere of the grave, or of deep places
generally.

The image is of something made underground, out of sight, the way a seed germinates or ore
sits in rock. And of the man formed *of the dust of the ground*. See the note on
Genesis 2:4–7.

## The unformed thing

*Your eyes **saw me unformed**.*

The Hebrew is **golmi** — my unformed substance, an embryo. The word occurs once in the whole
Bible.

It is the root behind the later Hebrew *golem*: unshaped matter.

## The book

*And in Your book were written **all my days, every one of them shaped before a single one
had come**.*

The Hebrew is *the days were fashioned for me, when as yet there were none of them*.

Written in a book before day one.

## What the psalm has already said

*You know my sitting down and my rising up; You understand my thought afar off… **there is
not a word on my tongue, but behold, O LORD, You know it altogether**.*

And *where can I go from Your Spirit?… if I make my bed in hell, behold, You are there. If I
take the wings of the morning and dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea, even there Your
hand shall lead me.*

Knowledge, then presence, then this. The three sections build.

## How it ends

*Search me, O God, and know my heart; try me, and know my anxieties; and see if there is any
wicked way in me, and **lead me in the way everlasting**.*

A psalm that opens *O LORD, You have searched me and known me* closes by asking Him to do it.

## The same idea in Jeremiah

*Before I formed you in the womb I knew you; before you were born I sanctified you; I
ordained you a prophet to the nations.*

And Paul: *God, who separated me from my mother's womb and called me through His grace*. See
the note on Galatians 1:15–16.

> **The word that occurs once.** *Golem* — unformed substance. And the sentence around it says
> the days were already written down while it was still that.
