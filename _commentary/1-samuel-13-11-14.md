Saul loses the kingdom over a matter of minutes, and every reason he gives is true.

## The situation

Thirty thousand chariots at Michmash, the army hiding in caves and cisterns, and men slipping
across the Jordan. See the note on 1 Samuel 13:5–7.

Samuel had said wait seven days.

*He waited seven days, according to the time set by Samuel. But Samuel did not come to Gilgal,
and the people were scattered from him.*

He waits the full seven, and then offers the burnt offering himself. And *as soon as he had
finished presenting the burnt offering, Samuel came*.

## The question

*"**What have you done?**"*

The same question God asked Eve, and Cain.

## The three reasons

*"I saw the **men scattering**."*

*"And **you didn't come when you said you would**."*

*"And **the Philistines were massing at Michmash**."*

Every one is accurate. The army was deserting, Samuel was late by Saul's reckoning, and the
enemy was three miles away.

## The reasoning

*"I thought, 'The Philistines will come down on me here at Gilgal, and **I haven't sought the
LORD's favor**.'"*

He frames it as piety. The thing he is worried about not having done is the religious
observance.

## The admission

*"So **I forced myself to do it** — I offered the burnt offering."*

The Hebrew is **va'et'appaq** — I compelled myself, I restrained myself and then broke.

It is the most honest sentence in the passage. He knew, and did it anyway, and says so.

## The verdict

*"**You've acted foolishly**," Samuel said. "**You have not kept the command** the LORD your God
gave you."*

The Hebrew is **niskalta** — you have been foolish.

## What it cost

*"**If you had**, He would have **established your kingdom over Israel for all time**. But now
**your kingdom will not endure**."*

A dynasty, lost over seven days and a few minutes.

## The successor

*"The LORD has **sought out a man after His own heart** and appointed him ruler over His
people."*

David is not named. He is a boy with sheep at this point, and Samuel will not anoint him for
some chapters yet.

Paul quotes this line in his sermon at Antioch. See the note on Acts 13:20–22.

## The reason restated

*"**Because you did not keep what the LORD commanded you**."*

Samuel says it twice, at the start and the finish of the verdict.

## What was actually wrong

Not that a king offered a sacrifice — David does, and Solomon does.

The command was specific and to him: *wait seven days, till I come to you and show you what you
should do*.

He was told to wait for instructions, and he acted before they arrived.

## The pattern

It happens again in chapter 15, over Amalek, and the excuse is the same shape: *the people
spared the best of the sheep and the oxen, to sacrifice to the LORD your God*.

And Samuel's answer: *has the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices as in
obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, **to obey is better than sacrifice***.

Both times the disobedience is dressed as worship.

## What Saul says at the end

*"I have sinned; yet **honour me now, please, before the elders of my people** and before
Israel."*

The concern is the audience.

David, confronted over something far worse: *against You, You only, have I sinned*.

> **The most honest thing he says.** *I forced myself.* He knew what he was doing, waited the
> seven days, and moved a few minutes before the prophet walked in.
