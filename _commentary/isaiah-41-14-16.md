The address is an insult and the promise is a farm implement.

## The name

*Do not be afraid, **you worm Jacob**, you **little people** of Israel.*

The Hebrew is **tolaath** — a worm, a grub. It is the word used of the maggot in the
manna that was kept overnight, and of the crimson dye made from a crushed insect.

It is also the word in Psalm 22: *but I am a worm, and no man.*

## Why the address is there

The chapter is God against the idol-makers — *let them bring forth and show us what will
happen*, *behold, you are nothing, and your work is nothing.*

Into that argument He turns to Israel and calls them a grub.

He is not flattering them into confidence. He states what they are and then says what He
is going to do with them.

## The help

*"**I Myself will help you**," says the LORD, "**your Redeemer, the Holy One of
Israel**."*

Two titles. **Redeemer** is **goel** — the kinsman-redeemer, the relative obliged to buy
back what a family member had lost. It is Boaz's role in Ruth.

The nearest relative and the Holy One, in one line.

## The transformation

*"See, **I will make you into a threshing sledge, new and sharp, with many teeth**."*

A **morag** — a heavy wooden sled dragged over cut grain, its underside set with stone or
iron teeth, to separate the wheat from the straw.

The Hebrew for *many teeth* is **piphiyoth** — literally *possessors of mouths*, from the
word for mouth. The same construction is used of a sword: *a sword with two mouths* is
the phrase behind *two-edged*.

The teeth of the sledge and the edge of a sword are described in Hebrew as mouths.

## What it threshes

*"**You will thresh the mountains and crush them**, and **reduce the hills to chaff**."*

A sledge is for grain. This one is put over mountains.

The scale is deliberately wrong, and the results are the same either way: what was solid
becomes chaff.

## The wind

*"You will **winnow them, and the wind will carry them off**, and a gale will scatter
them."*

Winnowing is the step after threshing. You throw the crushed material into the air and
the breeze takes the light part away.

Nothing is left to sweep up.

## Whose the outcome is

*"**But you will rejoice in the LORD, and glory in the Holy One of Israel**."*

The worm ends the passage rejoicing, and not in what it did.

## What is around it in the chapter

*Fear not, for I am with you; be not dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you,
yes, I will help you, I will uphold you with My righteous right hand.*

That is verse 10, four verses earlier.

And after this passage: *I will open rivers in desolate heights, and fountains in the
midst of the valleys… I will plant in the wilderness the cedar and the acacia tree.*

> **The distance in three verses.** From a grub to a threshing sledge new and sharp,
> going over mountains — and the only thing between the two is *I Myself will help you*.
