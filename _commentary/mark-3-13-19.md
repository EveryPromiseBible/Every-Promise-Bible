The appointment of the twelve, and Mark gives the job description in three parts with an
unexpected first item.

## Where

*Then He went up **on the mountainside**.*

Luke adds what happened first: *He went out to the mountain to pray, and continued all
night in prayer to God. And when it was day, He called His disciples to Him, and from them
He chose twelve.*

A night of praying, and then the list.

## The calling

*And **called to Himself the ones He wanted**, and they came to Him.*

The Greek is *whom He Himself willed*.

Nobody applies. He says it later in John: *you did not choose Me, but I chose you*.

## The first purpose

*He appointed twelve of them, **so that they would be with Him**.*

That is the first item, and it is not a task.

The Greek is **hina osin met' autou** — that they might be with Him.

## The second and third

*And so that He could **send them out to preach**, with **authority to heal the sick and to
drive out demons**.*

The sending comes second, and the power comes attached to the sending.

Mark puts them in that order and does not reverse it.

## What "apostle" means

The Greek **apostolos** — one sent out. The name describes the second purpose.

## The list

Twelve names, and Mark notes what was distinctive about several:

- **Simon, to whom He gave the name Peter** — **Petros**, a stone
- **James and John, whom He called Boanerges, Sons of Thunder** — the two who wanted to
  call fire down on a Samaritan village. See the note on Luke 9:51–56.
- **Simon the Zealot** — from the party committed to armed resistance against Rome
- **Matthew** — who collected taxes for Rome

A zealot and a tax collector in the same group of twelve.

- **Judas Iscariot, the one who would hand Him over**

## How Mark names the last one

Every list of the twelve in the New Testament ends with Judas, and every one attaches the
same clause.

He was on the mountain, called by name, and included in the sending and the authority.

## The number

Twelve. Nobody in that world would have missed it — twelve tribes, twelve sons of Jacob.

## What Mark records around it

Immediately before: crowds pressing so hard He had to have a boat kept ready, and unclean
spirits crying out that He was the Son of God.

Immediately after: *then the multitude came together again, so that they could not so much
as eat bread.* And His family coming to take Him home, *for they said, "He is out of His
mind."*

## The first thing

Mark's ordering has been noticed for a long time. The men are appointed to be with Him
first, and everything else in the sentence follows from that clause.

They are with Him for roughly three years before Acts opens.

> **What comes first in the job description.** Not preaching and not power. That they
> would be with Him — and Mark puts it at the head of the sentence with the other two
> hanging off it.
