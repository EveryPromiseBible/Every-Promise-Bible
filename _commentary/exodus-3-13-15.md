Moses asks for a name and gets one that is a sentence.

## The question

*"They ask me, 'What is his name?' **What shall I tell them?**"*

A reasonable question in that world. Every nation had gods with names, and a name
was what you had to know in order to call on one.

## The answer

*"**I AM WHO I AM.**"*

The Hebrew is **ehyeh asher ehyeh** — the verb *to be*, first person, twice, with
*who* between them.

It is not a description of anything. It is not tied to a place, a function, a
mountain, a season or an element, which is what every other name in the ancient
world was tied to.

## Then the short form

*"Say to the Israelites: **'I AM has sent me to you.'**"*

He takes His own sentence and gives Moses one word out of it to carry.

## And then the third form

*"Say to the Israelites, '**the LORD**, the God of your fathers — the God of
Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob — has sent me to you.'"*

**The LORD**, in capitals, is **YHWH**, and it is built from the same verb. The
name and the sentence are the same root.

He attaches it immediately to three men: Abraham, Isaac, Jacob. The name that is
pure existence is given to Moses fastened to a family.

## "This is My name forever"

*The name you are to remember Me by **from generation to generation**.*

Hebrew for *remember* here is the memorial word — the name is given as something
to be kept in mind, generation after generation.

## Where the name turns up hidden

In the Hebrew of Esther 7:5 — the verse where the king demands *who is he, where
is the man who has dared in his heart to do such a thing?* — the letters of
**EHYEH**, *I AM*, stand at the start of four consecutive words.

The name given at a burning bush is written into a Persian banquet hall, in the
sentence asking who is behind all this, in a book that never says God once.

And **YHWH** is formed the same way four times elsewhere in that book, twice
forwards and twice backwards.

He said it was His name forever. It is in the one book where nobody speaks it.

> **What Jesus does with it.** *Before Abraham was, I AM.* They picked up stones,
> because they understood exactly which sentence He had just used. And in the
> garden, when the soldiers say they are looking for Jesus of Nazareth and He
> answers *I am* — they drew back and fell to the ground. See the note on
> Esther 7:5–6.
