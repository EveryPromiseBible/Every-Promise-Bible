A command that is explicitly not a new one, in a letter of thirteen verses.

## What he asks for

*"So here is what I am asking of you, lady, and **it is nothing new — you have had it from the
start**: let us love each other."*

The Greek is **ouch hos entolen kainen graphon soi alla hen eichomen ap' arches** — not as writing a
new commandment, but one we have had from the beginning.

**John** says the same thing in his first letter and then immediately says the opposite: *again, a
new commandment I write to you.* He is not confused. It is old because they have had it from the
start, and new because of what happened in between.

*"**A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another; as I have loved you**, that you also
love one another."*

**John** 13, in the upper room, and the new part is the clause in the middle. The command to love is
Leviticus 19. The standard attached to it is not.

## The definition

*"**And love means this: living the way He has asked us to live.**"*

The letter defines love by obedience and then, in the same breath, defines the commandment by love —
*this is the commandment, that as you have heard from the beginning, you should walk in it.*

The circle is deliberate. **John** will not let either term be held without the other.

*"**If you love Me, keep My commandments.**"*

## Where it starts

*"You have had it **from the start**."*

**Ap' arches** — from the beginning. The phrase runs through **John**'s letters seven times, and in
the first one it is not a date in the readers' lives:

*That which was **from the beginning**, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which
we have looked upon, and **our hands have handled**, concerning the Word of life.*

## And the direction love runs in

*In this is love, **not that we loved God, but that He loved us** and sent His Son to be the
propitiation for our sins.*

*We love Him **because He first loved us**.*

**John** puts the order beyond argument twice in one chapter of the first letter. The command in
**2 John** is not the source of anything; it is the second move.

*Therefore be imitators of God as dear children. **And walk in love, as Christ also has loved us and
given Himself for us**, an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweet-smelling aroma.*

**Ephesians** 5, and the *as* carries the whole weight.

## Who the lady is

**Eklekte kyria** — the chosen lady. Read by some as a woman's name and by most as a congregation,
with *her children* as its members and *your chosen sister* at the end as another church.

Either way the letter is addressed to somebody chosen, and the word is the first one in it.

> **Why it is both old and new.** Old because they had it from the start. New because of the four
> words John 13 adds to it: *as I have loved you.*
