One sentence, and it is the first half of the exchange the letter is built on.

## The condition

*"**So if you and I are in this together.**"*

The Greek is **ei oun me echeis koinonon** — if then you have me as a partner.

**Koinonos** — a sharer, a partner, from **koinonia**. It is a commercial word before it is a
religious one: a business partner, somebody whose accounts are joined to yours.

**Paul** has already used the root in verse 6, praying that *the sharing of your faith may become
effective*.

## The request

*"**Open your door to him the way you would open it to me.**"*

**Proslabou auton hos eme** — receive him as me.

Two words in Greek carry it: **hos eme**. Not *as you would a brother*, not *as one of us*. As me.

## What is being asked

Philemon has an account with Onesimus, and it is in deficit. He has an account with **Paul**, and it
is not.

**Paul** asks him to treat the first man on the terms of the second — to let the standing of the one
be applied to the other.

*Now to him who works, the wages are not counted as grace but as debt. But to him who does not work
but believes on Him who justifies the ungodly, **his faith is accounted for righteousness**.*

## The other half

*"And if he has done you wrong, or owes you anything, **put it down to my account**."* See the note on
Philemon 1:18–19.

The two verses are one transaction. The credit goes one way and the debt goes the other, and
**Paul** is standing between them holding both.

*For **He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God
in Him**.*

**2 Corinthians** 5:21 has exactly this shape, and it is the only other place in the New Testament
where the double transfer is stated in one sentence.

## What Philemon is receiving

A man who robbed him, presented on the credit of somebody Philemon owes his own conversion to —
*not to mention that you owe me your very self*, **Paul** adds two verses later.

*Therefore **receive one another, just as Christ also received us**, to the glory of God.*

**Romans** 15, and the verb is **proslambanesthe** — the same one. **Paul** gives the church the
instruction he gives Philemon, and names the precedent.

*"**The one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out.**"*

> **The two words.** *Hos eme* — as me. Philemon is asked to open the door to a thief on the standing
> of the man who led him to Christ, and the next sentence transfers the debt in the other direction.
