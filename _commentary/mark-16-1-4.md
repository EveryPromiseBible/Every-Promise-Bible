They set out to finish a burial and arrive at an open door.

## Who goes

*Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of James and Salome.*

Three named women. Mark named the first two at the crucifixion and at the burial as well:
*and Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses observed where He was laid.*

They knew which tomb because they had watched.

## What they bought

*Spices so that they could **go and anoint Him**.*

The Greek is **aromata**.

Bought after the Sabbath ended at sunset, which means the shops opened Saturday evening and
they went straight there.

Nicodemus had already brought a hundred pounds of myrrh and aloes. They are going anyway.

## The timing

*Very early on the first day of the week, **just as the sun was coming up**.*

Mark says *very early* and then *when the sun had risen*, which reads like a contradiction
and is the ordinary way of describing first light.

## The conversation

*On the way they were saying to each other, "**Who is going to roll the stone back from the
entrance for us?**"*

The Greek is an imperfect. They kept asking it.

Three women walking through a dark garden with jars, discussing a problem they cannot solve
and going anyway.

Nobody suggests turning round.

## What they expected

A sealed tomb, a stone they could not move, and a body to prepare.

They are not going to a resurrection. They are going to finish a funeral.

## The discovery

*Then they **looked up, and the stone had already been rolled aside**.*

The Greek is **anablepsasai** — looking up. And **apokekulistai**, a perfect passive: it has
been rolled away and stays rolled away.

## The note

*And it was **an enormous stone**.*

Mark adds it after the fact, which is the order the women would have thought it.

Matthew adds the guard and the seal, and an earthquake, and an angel sitting on the stone.

## What the stone was not for

Not to keep Him in. Luke and John both have Him appearing in a locked room afterwards.

It was moved so that people could get in and look.

*Come, see the place where the Lord lay*, says Matthew's angel.

## The other stone

At Lazarus' tomb the instruction is *take the stone away*, and human hands do it. See the
note on John 11:38–40.

Here nobody is asked and nobody helps. It is done before the first witness arrives.

## What they find inside

*A young man clothed in a long white robe sitting on the right side.*

*"Do not be alarmed. **You seek Jesus of Nazareth, who was crucified. He is risen! He is not
here**. See the place where they laid Him."*

## The message

*"But go, tell His disciples — **and Peter** — that He is going before you into Galilee."*

Peter, named separately. Mark's Gospel is traditionally Peter's account, which makes the two
extra words worth noticing.

## What they did

*And they went out quickly and fled from the tomb, for they trembled and were amazed. And
**they said nothing to anyone, for they were afraid**.*

Mark's oldest manuscripts end there, mid-fear.

## Who was first

Women, at every stage. Last at the blood covenant, present at the burial, first at the tomb, and first
told.

In a legal system where their testimony did not count.

> **The question they asked on the way.** *Who will roll away the stone?* They had no answer
> and went anyway — and it had been moved before they got there.
