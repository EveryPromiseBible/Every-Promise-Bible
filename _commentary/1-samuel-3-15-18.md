---
key: 1 Samuel 3:15–18
title: He told him everything
updated: 2026-08-09
---
A boy has to deliver the worst message in the book to the man who raised him.

## What he does first

*Samuel lay there until morning, then **opened the doors of the house of the LORD**.*

He gets up and does his job. The doors are opened at the usual hour by a boy carrying a
sentence against the household he serves in.

## What he is afraid of

*He was **afraid to tell Eli the vision**.*

He is a child. Eli is the high priest, the judge of Israel, and the only father he has known
since his mother handed him over at the tent door.

## Who makes him say it

*But Eli called him: "**Samuel, my son.**"*

The man under judgement uses the word *son*, and then insists on hearing it: *do not hide it
from me. **May God deal with you, and worse, if you hide a single word**.*

He puts Samuel under oath to tell him the thing that condemns him.

## What Samuel does

*So Samuel told him everything — **held nothing back**.*

## What Eli says

*"**He is the LORD. Let Him do what seems good to Him.**"*

No argument, no plea, no attempt to negotiate the terms. It is the same submission Aaron made
on the day his sons died. See the note on Leviticus 10:16–20.

And it is not repentance. Eli says this and changes nothing; his sons stay in office and the
chapter's sentence stands.

## What it cost to say it

*And **the LORD was with him, and let none of his words fall to the ground**.*

Two verses later. The first thing recorded of Samuel's ministry is that nothing he said fell —
and the first thing he ever said was the hardest.

*Therefore, putting away lying, **let each one of you speak truth with his neighbor**.*

## The word for what he was given

*The word of the LORD was rare in those days; **there was no widespread revelation**.* See the
note on 1 Samuel 3:1–3.

The drought ends with a message nobody wanted, delivered by a child, to a blind old man, at
dawn.

*"For **there is nothing covered that will not be revealed**, and hidden that will not be
known."*

> **What Eli asks for.** Not mercy, and not a second opinion. He puts the boy under an oath to
> tell him the whole of it, and then says *He is the LORD*.
