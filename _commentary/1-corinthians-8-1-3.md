The question was about meat from the temple butchers. Paul answers it in three chapters, and he
begins by moving the whole discussion off the ground the Corinthians had chosen.

## Two things that make something bigger

*Knowledge **swells us up**; love **builds us up**.*

**Physioo** is to inflate — the word comes from **physa**, a bellows. It occurs six times in the New
Testament and five of them are in this letter; Corinth had a chronic case.

**Oikodomeo** is to build a house: **oikos** and **domeo**, stone on stone.

Both make a thing larger. One adds volume and nothing else; the other adds substance. An inflated
church looks exactly like a growing one from outside, until something leans on it.

## The knowledge that disqualifies itself

*If anyone thinks that he knows something, **he does not yet know it as he ought to know**.*

A sentence built to close a trap. The moment a man is confident he has the matter mastered, that
confidence is itself the evidence he has not — because anything genuinely known about God produces
worship, and worship is not a swelling.

Paul is not against knowledge. He is the most theologically precise writer in the Bible. He is
against knowledge held as rank.

## The turn nobody sees coming

*But if anyone loves God, **that one is known by Him**.*

Read what he was about to say. The sentence has been running on knowing for three verses; the
symmetry demands *knows Him*. Paul will not write it.

He does the same thing five chapters later — *then I shall know fully, even as I **have been fully
known*** — and again to the Galatians, where he starts the sentence and then corrects himself in
mid-air: *now that you have come to know God, **or rather to be known by God***.

Three times, and every time he takes the security out of our grasp of Him and puts it in His grasp
of us. Knowledge that swells is knowledge with me as the subject of the verb. The moment God is the
subject, there is nothing left to inflate.

> **Known first.** This is the whole grace of the New Testament sitting in a chapter about
> restaurant food. What holds you is not the firmness of your grip on Jesus but the firmness of His
> on you — *no one is able to snatch them out of My Father's hand*. Love God as much as you can
> today; Paul's point is that the fact worth resting on is the other one, and it was settled before
> you loved anybody.
