The end of the first thing Job says after seven days of silence.

## The silence

*So they sat down with him on the ground **seven days and seven nights**, and no one spoke a word to
him, **for they saw that his grief was very great**.*

The friends do the right thing for a week. It is the last thing they get right.

## Who breaks it

*After this Job opened his mouth and cursed the day of his birth.*

Not God. The day.

*"May the day perish on which I was born… **may that day be darkness**."*

## What he asks

*"Why did I not die at birth?"*

*"For now I would have lain still and been quiet, I would have slept; then I would have been at
rest."*

Three chapters after being called *blameless and upright*, and one chapter after *in all this Job
did not sin with his lips*.

## The eating

*For **my sighing comes before I even eat**, and **my groans pour out of me like water**.*

The Hebrew is **liphnei lachmi anchati** — before my bread comes my sighing.

The groaning arrives ahead of the meal.

## The verse

*The thing I dreaded **has come over me**. What I feared **has found me**.*

The Hebrew is **pachad pachadti vayye'etayeni** — a dread I dreaded, and it came upon me.

**Vaasher yagorti yavo li** — and what I was afraid of has come to me.

## What it says about him

He was afraid of this before it happened.

Chapter 1 records what he did about it: *for Job said, "It may be that my sons have sinned and
cursed God in their hearts." Thus Job did regularly.*

He rose early and offered burnt offerings for children who had done nothing wrong, in case.

## What the verse does not say

That the fear caused it.

Job is describing what happened, not diagnosing it. The book's own account of the cause is in
chapter 1, and it is not fear.

The reader has been told. Job never is.

## The three denials

*I have **no peace**, **no quiet**, **no rest**.*

The Hebrew is **lo shalavti velo shaqateti velo nachti**.

Three verbs of stillness, each negated.

## The last clause

*Only trouble comes.*

The Hebrew is **vayyavo rogez** — and turmoil came.

**Rogez** — agitation, trembling, raging. The word he uses of the wicked in chapter 3: *there the
wicked cease from troubling*.

He asked for the place where **rogez** stops, and the chapter ends with it arriving.

## What happens next

Eliphaz answers, and the first cycle of speeches begins. Three friends, three rounds, and
increasingly little sympathy.

*"Remember now, who ever perished being innocent?"* See the note on Job 4:6–9.

## What Job says later in the book

*"Though He slay me, yet will I trust Him."*

*"For **I know that my Redeemer lives**, and He shall stand at last on the earth."* See the note on
Job 19:25–27.

## What he says at the end

*"I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear, **but now my eye sees You**."* See the note on
Job 42:1–6.

## Where the book leaves the question

Unanswered, in the terms Job asked it.

God's reply is four chapters of questions about the natural world, and the reason for the suffering
is never disclosed to the man it happened to.

> **What the verse does and does not say.** *What I feared has found me* — a description, from a
> man who had been offering sacrifices in case. The book's account of the cause is elsewhere, and
> he never hears it.
