The one offer in the temptation that is not disputed.

## The claim

*"**I will hand all of this authority over to You, and all their glory with it, because it has been
handed over to me, and I give it to anyone I please.**"*

**Jesus** does not contradict him. He argues with the terms and not with the title deed.

*"**Now is the judgment of this world; now the ruler of this world will be cast out.**"*

*"**The ruler of this world is coming, and he has nothing in Me.**"*

*We know that we are of God, and **the whole world lies under the sway of the wicked one**.*

The New Testament grants the claim three or four times over. The offer was real, which is what makes
it a temptation rather than a trick.

## What it was an offer of

The kingdoms — without the cross.

*"**Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and all things that are written by the prophets concerning
the Son of Man will be accomplished.**"*

*"Get behind Me, Satan! You are an offence to Me, for you are not mindful of the things of God but
the things of men."*

**Matthew** 16, said to **Peter**, for making the same offer in gentler language: *this shall not
happen to You.*

## The price

*"**If You will worship before me, all will be Yours.**"*

One act. And what He answers with is a quotation, as with the other two:

*"**You shall worship the LORD your God, and Him only you shall serve.**"*

## What He got instead, and how

*"**All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.**"*

**Matthew** 28. He is given the thing He was offered, and the route is the one the offer was designed
to avoid.

*He humbled Himself and became obedient to the point of death, even the death of the cross.
**Therefore God also has highly exalted Him** and given Him the name which is above every name.*

*Then the seventh angel sounded: and there were loud voices in heaven, saying, "**The kingdoms of
this world have become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ**."*

> **What is not disputed.** The devil's right to make the offer. Jesus argues with the price, not the
> deed — and receives the same kingdoms at the end of Matthew by the route the offer was meant to
> avoid.
