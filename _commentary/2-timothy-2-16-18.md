key: 2 Timothy 2:16–18
title: "Gangrene"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
Paul reaches for a surgeon's word, names two men, and then — in the very next sentence — refuses to
be worried.

## Empty talk that goes somewhere

*Steer well clear of **godless, empty talk**. The people who give themselves to it **only drift
further and further from God**.*

**Kenophonias** — empty-soundings. Noise with nothing in it.

And the verb Paul attaches to it is a progress word: *they will advance to more ungodliness*. Empty
talk does not stay level. It is a direction of travel dressed up as a conversation.

## The word he chose

*The talk itself **spreads the way gangrene eats its way through healthy flesh**.*

**Gangraina** — a medical term, and this is its only appearance in the Bible. Whether Paul had it
from the physician who was sitting with him in that cell, we are not told.

The point of the image is precise. Gangrene does not attack from outside; it spreads through tissue
that is alive, and it advances by killing what it touches so it can go on touching more. That is
why Paul says *steer clear* rather than *argue back*. You do not debate necrosis.

## The error, named

*Hymenaeus and Philetus… **saying the resurrection has already come and gone**.*

A spiritualised resurrection — it has happened, inwardly, and there is nothing bodily still to come.

It sounds advanced, and it guts everything. Paul had already spent a whole chapter on why: *if
Christ is not risen, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins.* Our future is fastened to
His body. Cut the bodily resurrection out of the gospel and there is no firstfruits, no sheaf, no
harvest — just an idea about how to feel.

*And **the faith of some people is coming apart in their hands**.* **Anatrepousin** — overturning,
capsizing.

## And then the next sentence

Paul writes *nevertheless* — and what follows is the answer to every alarm in the paragraph: *the
foundation God laid stands there unmoved.*

He names the danger honestly, names the men, and then puts the weight somewhere the danger cannot
reach.

> **Two things Paul does at once.** He does not pretend the damage is not real: some people's faith
> is being capsized, and he says so. And he does not conclude that anything is at risk which God
> laid. The security of the church has never been the soundness of the church. *The Lord knows those
> who are His.*
