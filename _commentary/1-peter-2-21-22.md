Peter is writing to household slaves, and this is what he tells them.

## Who is being addressed

The paragraph before: *servants, be submissive to your masters with all fear, not only to the
good and gentle, but also to the harsh*.

The Greek is **oiketai** — domestic slaves. And **skoliois**, crooked, twisted — the harsh
masters are described with the word behind *scoliosis*.

*For what credit is it if, when you are beaten for your faults, you take it patiently? But
when you do good and suffer, if you take it patiently, this is commendable before God.*

## The sentence

*This is **the very thing you were called into**.*

The Greek is **eis touto eklethete** — to this you were called.

Not *this may happen to you*. The suffering is named as the content of the calling.

## The reason

*Christ **suffered for us** and **left us a track to walk in**.*

The Greek is **hupogrammon** — a writing-copy. The line of letters a schoolmaster wrote at the
top of a wax tablet for a child to trace underneath.

The word occurs once in the New Testament.

## The instruction

*Put your feet where His went.*

The Greek is **epakolouthesete tois ichnesin autou** — follow closely in His footprints.

**Ichnos** — a track, a footprint. The same image as the answer given to the bride when she
asked where he was: *follow the tracks of the flock*. See the note on Song of Songs 1:8.

## The first credential

*He **never sinned once**.*

Peter is quoting Isaiah 53: *He had done no violence*.

## The second

*Nobody ever **caught a lie coming out of His mouth**.*

Isaiah again: *nor was any deceit in His mouth*.

The Greek is **dolos** — bait, a lure, a trick.

Peter picks the line about the mouth, and he is writing to people whose suffering came from
being spoken to and about.

## What follows

*Who, when He was reviled, **did not revile in return**; when He suffered, **He did not
threaten**, but **committed Himself to Him who judges righteously**.*

The Greek for *committed* is **paredidou** — an imperfect. He kept handing it over.

The one thing He did with the abuse was pass it to somebody else to deal with.

## What Peter watched

He was in the courtyard. See the note on Matthew 26:69–75.

He saw the trial, the silence, and the beating, and forty years later he writes about the
mouth that had nothing in it.

He also drew a sword in the garden and had to be told to put it away, which is presumably why
he stresses that Christ did not threaten.

## What comes next

*Who Himself **bore our sins in His own body on the tree**, that we, having died to sins,
might live for righteousness — **by whose stripes you were healed**.* See the note on
1 Peter 2:24.

The example turns into a substitution in the space of two verses. Peter starts with a copybook
line to trace and ends with something nobody can imitate.

*For you were like sheep going astray, but **have now returned** to the Shepherd and Overseer
of your souls.* See the note on 1 Peter 2:25.

> **The word for the example.** A schoolboy's copy-line — the letters written at the top of
> the tablet for a child to trace. Written to household slaves with harsh masters.
