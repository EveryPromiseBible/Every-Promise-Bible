The vision has been measuring the water. Now it looks at what the water does.

## What has happened

Ezekiel is shown water coming out from under the threshold of the temple, on the south side of
the altar. See the note on Ezekiel 47:1–2.

The man with the measuring line takes him out a thousand cubits at a time: ankle deep, knee
deep, waist deep, and then *water in which one must swim, a river that could not be crossed*.

Four measurements, and no tributary joins it. It deepens on its own.

## The trees

*When I got there, I saw **a great many trees on both banks**.*

The Hebrew is *very many trees on the one side and on the other*.

He had walked out along the river and had not noticed them until he was brought back.

## Where it goes

*"This water flows out toward **the eastern region** and goes down into **the Arabah**, and it
**enters the sea**."*

The Arabah is the rift valley. Down from Jerusalem to the Dead Sea is a drop of about four
thousand feet in under twenty miles.

*The sea* is the Dead Sea — a lake with no outlet, roughly ten times as salty as the ocean.
Nothing lives in it.

## What happens to it

*"When it flows into the sea, **the salt water is made fresh**."*

The Hebrew is **nirpa'u** — are healed.

The verb for healing, used of water.

## The consequence

*"**Wherever the river flows, every living thing that swarms will live**, and there will be a
great many fish."*

The Hebrew is *every living creature that swarms* — the phrase from Genesis 1, of the fifth
day.

*"So **wherever the river flows, everything will live**."*

Said twice in three verses. The condition is entirely about location, and it is repeated.

## The fishermen

*"**Fishermen will stand along the shore**, from **En-gedi to En-eglaim** there will be places
for spreading nets."*

Two real places, on the western shore of the Dead Sea.

**En-gedi** is the oasis where David hid from Saul, and where he cut the corner off Saul's
robe in a cave.

Nobody has ever fished the Dead Sea. The vision has men drying nets on it.

## The variety

*"The fish will be of **many kinds, like the fish of the Great Sea**, a great many of them."*

The Great Sea is the Mediterranean.

## The exception

*"But **its swamps and marshes will not be made fresh. They are left for salt**."* See the note
on Ezekiel 47:11–12.

The vision is not uniform. Some of it stays salty, and Ezekiel is told why: salt was needed —
every grain offering was seasoned with it, and it was the covenant word.

## Who the fishermen are

*"Come, follow Me, and **I will make you fishers of people**."* See the note on
Matthew 4:18–22.

Said to two men throwing a net into a lake.

## Where the river reappears

*And he showed me **a pure river of water of life**, clear as crystal, proceeding from the
throne of God and of the Lamb. In the middle of its street, and on either side of the river,
was **the tree of life**… and **the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations**.*

Revelation's last chapter, with Ezekiel's trees and Ezekiel's healing leaves.

## Where the water starts

*Out from under the threshold of the temple.*

It has one source and no tributaries, and everything in the chapter depends on where it has
reached.

> **The clause said twice.** *Wherever the river flows.* Not how deep, not how wide — the only
> variable in the passage is whether the water has arrived.
