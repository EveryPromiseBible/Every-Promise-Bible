A man arrives with a compliment and gets an answer to a question he did not ask.

## Who he is

*A man among the Pharisees named **Nicodemus**, one of **the ruling council of the Jews**.*

The Greek is **archon** — a ruler. He is a member of the Sanhedrin, the seventy-one who
governed Jewish religious life.

Jesus calls him *the teacher of Israel* a few verses later, with the definite article — which
may mean he held a recognised position.

The name is Greek: *conqueror of the people*.

## The hour

*He came to Jesus **at night**.*

John records it, and mentions it every time Nicodemus appears — *the man who had first come to
Jesus at night*.

Whether the reason was fear, or the desire for an unhurried conversation, John does not say.

## The opening

*"**Rabbi, we know** You are a teacher who has come from God."*

The Greek is **oidamen** — we know.

Plural. He is speaking for a group, and the sentence is a considered verdict rather than a
personal opinion.

## The evidence

*"Because **no one could do the signs You are doing unless God were with him**."*

Sound reasoning, and it is the conclusion of a careful man.

It is also as far as it goes. *A teacher come from God* is a large compliment and a small
category.

## The answer

*"I tell you the truth: **unless a person is born all over again, he cannot see the kingdom of
God**."*

Which answers nothing Nicodemus said.

He came with an assessment; he gets a statement about eyesight.

## The word

**Anothen** — and it carries two meanings at once: *again*, and *from above*.

Nicodemus takes the first: *how can a man be born when he is old?* See the note on
John 3:4–8.

John chose a word that carries both, and the conversation runs on the gap.

## What is being said about knowing

Nicodemus opens with *we know*.

The reply is about a faculty rather than information: *he cannot **see** the kingdom of God*.

Not that he needs more data. That he needs different eyes.

## The contrast John sets up

The next chapter is a Samaritan woman at a well at noon. See the note on
John 4:7–9.

A ruler and an outsider. Night and midday. A man who opens with *we know* and a woman who says
*I have no husband*.

He is told he must be born again. She is told *I am He, the very one speaking to you now*. See
the note on John 4:25–26.

The two conversations are placed back to back, and the ordering is John's.

## Where the conversation ends up

*"For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son."* See the note on
John 3:14–18.

The most quoted sentence in the Bible is the end of a private conversation held after dark
with a member of the council.

## What Nicodemus does later

Objects in the Sanhedrin: *does our law judge a man before it hears him?*

And then, on the Friday, arrives at a public execution with a hundred pounds of myrrh and
aloes. See the note on John 19:38–42.

> **What he came with.** A verdict, arrived at properly, on the evidence: *a teacher come from
> God*. The answer he gets is not about the verdict but about whether he can see.
