Paul says he is confident, twice, and both times it is about dying.

## The confidence

*So we are **always full of courage**.*

The Greek is **tharrountes** — of good cheer, bold, confident.

He says it in verse 6 and repeats it in verse 8. It brackets the paragraph.

## What comes before it

*For we know that if our earthly house, this tent, is destroyed, we have a building from God,
a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens.*

The Greek for *tent* is **skenous** — a temporary structure. Paul made them for a living.

And: *we do not want to be unclothed, but **further clothed**, that mortality may be
swallowed up by life.*

## The two homes

*As long as we are **at home in this body** we are **living away from the Lord**.*

The Greek is a pair Paul builds for this passage: **endemountes** and **ekdemountes** — being
among your own people, and being abroad.

At home here means abroad there. The two states are mutually exclusive, and he says so
plainly.

## The line in the middle

*For we make our way by **trusting, not by seeing**.*

The Greek is **dia pisteos peripatoumen, ou dia eidous** — through faith we walk, not through
appearance.

**Eidos** is the visible form of a thing. Not *sight* as the faculty; the look of it.

The sentence is dropped into the middle of an argument about death and the body, and it is
the reason the argument works: the building in the heavens has not been seen by anybody
writing or reading.

## The preference

*And we are full of courage, and would **gladly prefer to be away from the body and at home
with the Lord**.*

The Greek is **eudokoumen** — we are well pleased, we think it good.

He states a preference for dying, calmly, in a letter about his sufferings.

## The other place he says it

*For to me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain… **having a desire to depart and be with
Christ, which is far better**. Nevertheless to remain in the flesh is more needful for you.*
See the note on Philippians 1:21–24.

Same preference, same reason, and there he adds that he expects to stay.

## What the chapter has already said

*For we who are in this tent **groan, being burdened** — not because we want to be
unclothed, but further clothed.*

The groaning is real and Paul does not deny it. The confidence and the groaning are in the
same paragraph.

## The deposit

*Now He who has prepared us for this very thing is God, who also **has given us the Spirit
as a guarantee**.*

The Greek is **arrabona** — a down payment, earnest money. In modern Greek the word is used
for an engagement ring.

Part of the sum, handed over now, on a transaction that will be completed.

## What follows immediately

*Therefore we make it our aim, whether present or absent, **to be well pleasing to Him**.
For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ.*

The Greek is **bema** — the raised platform where a magistrate sat, and where prizes were
awarded at the games.

The preference for departing does not remove the aim. Paul puts the two in consecutive
sentences.

## Where the chapter ends

*If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation.*

And *He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of
God in Him*. See the note on 2 Corinthians 5:20–21.

The paragraph about being at home or abroad sits between the deposit and the exchange.

> **What the confidence rests on.** Nothing visible. He says so in the middle of the
> paragraph: *we make our way by trusting, not by the look of it.*
