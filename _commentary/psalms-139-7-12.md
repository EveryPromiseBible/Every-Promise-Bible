Two questions with no answer, and the psalm spends five verses failing to find one.

## The questions

*Where could I go **from Your Spirit**? Where could I flee **from Your face**?*

The Hebrew is **ana elek meruchekha** and **ana mippaneikha evrach**.

## What they are not

Not a boast. **Barach** — to flee, to run away. It is the verb used of a slave escaping and of
Jonah going to Tarshish.

The psalm asks where a man could go to get away, and then works through the options.

## The first pair

*If I **climb up to heaven**, You are there. If I **make my bed in the depths**, You are there.*

The Hebrew is **she'ol** — the grave, the place of the dead.

Highest and lowest. The two ends of the vertical axis.

## The word for the second

**Atzi'ah** — if I spread out my bed.

Not *if I am thrown there*. He pictures himself making it up deliberately, like a man settling in
for the night.

## The second pair

*If I take **the wings of the morning** and settle at **the far edge of the sea**.*

**Kanfei-shachar** — the wings of the dawn. Light travelling west across the Mediterranean at the
speed light travels.

East to west, at the fastest thing he can name.

## What is waiting there

*Even there **Your hand would lead me**, **Your right hand would hold me fast**.*

The Hebrew is **tancheni** — will lead, guide. And **tochazeni** — will grasp, take hold of.

## The turn in it

He set out to describe escape and describes being led.

The hand at the far edge of the sea is not blocking him. It is guiding, and holding on.

## The third attempt

*If I say, "**Surely the darkness will cover me**, and the light around me become night."*

The Hebrew is **yeshufeni** — will bruise me, or will cover me. The verb is uncertain; the ancient
versions divide.

## Why darkness

The first two attempts were about distance. This one is about concealment — not going somewhere
else but being unseen where he is.

## The answer

*Even **the darkness is not dark to You**. The night **shines like the day**.*

The Hebrew is **kachashekhah ka'orah** — as the darkness, so the light.

## The last line

*Darkness and light are **the same to You**.*

Not that God sees through the dark. That the category does not apply.

## What the psalm has just said

*You know my sitting down and my rising up; You understand my thought afar off… **there is not a
word on my tongue, but behold, O LORD, You know it altogether**.* See the note on Psalms 139:1–6.

And *such knowledge is too wonderful for me; it is high, I cannot attain it*.

## What it says next

*For **You formed my inward parts**; You covered me in my mother's womb… **I am fearfully and
wonderfully made**.* See the note on Psalms 139:13–16.

The Hebrew is **sakakni** — You wove me, You screened me over.

## How it ends

*Search me, O God, and know my heart… and **lead me in the way everlasting**.* See the note on
Psalms 139:23–24.

The psalm that opens by saying it cannot escape being known ends by asking to be searched.

## Where the same hand appears

*Neither shall anyone snatch them out of My hand.* See the note on John 10:27–30.

And *if we are faithless, He remains faithful; He cannot deny Himself*. See the note on
2 Timothy 2:11–13.

> **The reversal.** He is listing places to escape to, and at the furthest one the hand that meets
> him is not catching him — it is leading him, and holding on.
