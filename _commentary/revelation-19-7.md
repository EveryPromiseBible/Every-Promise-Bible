---
key: Revelation 19:7
title: ""His bride has made herself ready" — and what she was given to wear"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
One clause in this verse sounds like the bride's achievement, and the next verse takes it back.

## The three verbs

*Let us **be glad**, and let us **celebrate**, and let us **give Him the honor**.*

**Chairomen kai agalliomen** — rejoice and leap for joy. The second is a word for exuberance, not
composure.

And the third is not a fourth emotion: **dosomen ten doxan auto**, let us give the glory to Him. The
crowd's own instinct at a wedding is to hand the credit to the Bridegroom.

## The clause people stop at

*His bride **has made herself ready**.*

**Hetoimasen heauten** — she prepared herself. Read alone it sounds like a summary of a long
self-improvement project, and a great deal of anxious teaching has been built on it.

## And the clause that follows

*And **to her it was granted** to be arrayed in fine linen, clean and bright.*

**Edothe aute** — it was given to her. A divine passive: somebody handed it over, and the somebody
is God.

There is the whole thing. Her readiness consists of wearing what she was given. The one preparation
she made was to put on a garment she did not weave, could not afford, and had no part in producing.

The Old Testament said it first, in the same wedding language: *He has clothed me with the garments
of salvation, **He has covered me with the robe of righteousness**, as a bridegroom decks himself.*

## The fine linen

*The fine linen is **the righteous acts of the saints**.*

**Dikaiomata** — righteous deeds. That does not overturn the gift; it describes what the gift
produces. Paul puts both in one breath: *not of works, lest anyone should boast — for we are His
workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, **which God prepared beforehand***.

Even the deeds were laid out in advance, like clothes on a bed.

## And who did the washing

Two chapters earlier the crowd in white is explained: *they **washed their robes and made them
white in the blood of the Lamb***.

Nothing whitens in blood. It is the one image in Scripture that could not have been drawn from
experience, and it is exact: the whiteness comes from somewhere else entirely.

> **She is ready because He got her ready.** A bride at any wedding is not admired for having
> produced her own dress. The Lamb who is being married is the one who *loved the church and gave
> Himself for her… that He might present her to Himself a glorious church* — and the presenting, the
> cleansing and the clothing are all in His sentence, not hers.
