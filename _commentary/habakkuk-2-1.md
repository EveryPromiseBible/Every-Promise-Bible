One verse, and it is a decision about where to stand.

## What comes before

Two rounds of complaint. *How long, LORD, must I cry for help before You hear me?* — and
then an answer about Babylon that made it worse, and a second complaint harder than the
first. See the note on Habakkuk 1:1–4.

Then this. He stops arguing.

## The post

*I will **take my stand at my watchpost**.*

The Hebrew is **mishmarti** — my watch, my guard-post. The place a sentry is assigned.

## The wall

*I will **plant my feet on the wall** and keep watch.*

The Hebrew is *I will station myself on the rampart*.

A watchman on a city wall in that world had one job: to see something coming before anybody
else did, and to say so.

Isaiah: *I have set watchmen on your walls, O Jerusalem, who shall never hold their peace
day or night.*

Ezekiel is appointed one: *son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel;
therefore hear a word from My mouth, and give them warning from Me.*

## What he is watching for

*I will **look to see what He will say to me**.*

The Hebrew is *to see what He will speak in me*.

Look, to see what He will say. Habakkuk mixes the senses deliberately — the thing he is
watching for is a word.

## The last clause

*And **what I will answer when I am corrected**.*

The Hebrew is *what I shall answer when I am reproved*.

He expects to be corrected. He has just accused God twice of watching and doing nothing,
and he assumes an answer will include a rebuke.

He plans what to say when it comes.

## What actually happens

*Then the LORD answered me. **Write the vision**.* See the note on Habakkuk 2:2–3.

There is no rebuke.

## The posture

Nothing in the verse is about effort. He goes to a post, plants his feet, and waits for
something to arrive from outside.

## Who else stands and waits

Elijah at Horeb, after wind, earthquake and fire, coming out to the cave mouth for a
whisper. See the note on 1 Kings 19:9–18.

Daniel, three weeks in mourning, with the answer already dispatched on day one. See the
note on Daniel 10:12–13.

## What the watchman's answer turns out to be

*Behold, the proud, his soul is not upright in him; **but the just shall live by his
faith**.*

Two verses later, and it is the sentence Paul quotes in Romans and Galatians and Hebrews
quotes in chapter 10.

The most consequential line in the Reformation comes out of a man standing on a wall
waiting to be told off.

## The end of the same chapter

*But **the LORD is in His holy temple. Let all the earth keep silence before Him**.*

The prophet who opened by demanding an answer closes the chapter by calling for silence.

> **What he does after two rounds of arguing.** Stops. Goes to a post, stands on a wall,
> and watches to see what will be said — and prepares an answer for the correction that
> never comes.
