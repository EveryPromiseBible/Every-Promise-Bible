The psalm gives an announcement to make and then hands the singing over to
creation.

## The announcement

*Say among the nations, "**The LORD reigns.**"*

Two words in Hebrew. It is not a wish or a prayer — it is a statement of what is
currently the case, to be said out loud among people who do not know it.

## What follows from it

*The world **stands firm, it cannot be shaken**.*

The Hebrew is established, fixed, made to stand. The stability of everything is
placed downstream of who is reigning.

## Who does the rejoicing

*Let the **heavens** be glad and the **earth** rejoice; let the **sea** and all it
holds resound. Let the **fields** be joyful, and all that is in them; then all the
**trees of the forest** will sing for joy.*

Five subjects, and not one of them is a person.

Sky, ground, sea, field, forest. The psalm moves outward and downward and the
whole of creation is put in the choir.

## Why they are glad

*Before the LORD, for **He is coming, He is coming to judge the earth**.*

Said twice. And the reason creation rejoices is the judgement — which is not how
the word usually lands on us.

The Hebrew for *judge* is the governing word: to put right, to set in order, to
rule a matter. Creation is described elsewhere as *groaning*, waiting; here it is
told the One who will set it right is on His way.

## The terms

*He will judge the world with **righteousness**, and the peoples with **His
faithfulness**.*

Righteousness and faithfulness — **emunah**, the steady, dependable word, the root
of *amen*.

## What the psalm is for

The first line of it is *sing to the LORD a new song, sing to the LORD, all the
earth.* Then *declare His glory among the nations, His marvellous works among all
the peoples.*

It is a psalm about telling. The whole thing is instruction on what to say and to
whom.

## The sentence to say

*Say among the nations, "**The LORD reigns**."*

An instruction to speak, given to people whose nation was small and repeatedly overrun.

*And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb and **by the word of their testimony**.*

## What the creation does

*Let the heavens rejoice, and let the earth be glad… **then all the trees of the woods will
rejoice before the LORD**.*

The psalm has fields, sea and forest joining in — and **Paul** says why they would want to:
*the creation itself also will be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious
liberty of the children of God. For we know that the whole creation groans and labours with
birth pangs together until now.*

## The reason the trees sing

*For **He is coming to judge the earth**.*

Judgement is the reason given for the trees singing. In the psalm it is not a threat — it is
the arrival of somebody who will finally put things right.

*He has appointed a day on which He will judge the world in righteousness **by the Man whom He
has ordained**. He has given assurance of this to all by raising Him from the dead.*

**Paul** names who the Judge is, in Athens, and gives the resurrection as the receipt.

*"He shall judge the world with righteousness, and the peoples with His truth."*

> **Where it was first sung.** 1 Chronicles 16 records almost this entire psalm as
> the song David gave Asaph on the day the ark was brought into Jerusalem. It was
> written for the day the presence came home.
