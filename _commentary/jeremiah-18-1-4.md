key: Jeremiah 18:1–4
title: "The same clay"
updated: 2026-08-09
---
A prophet is sent to watch a man work, and the message is in what the potter does when it goes
wrong.

## The instruction

*"Go down to the potter's house, and **there I will give you My message**."*

He is not given the word and then the illustration. He is sent to the workshop first and told the
message will be waiting there.

*So I went down to the potter's house, and **there he was, working at his wheel**.*

The Hebrew is **al-ha'ovnayim** — on the two stones. A kick-wheel: a lower stone turned by the
foot and an upper one carrying the clay.

## What went wrong

*But the vessel he was shaping from the clay **was spoiled in his hands**.*

**Nishchat beyad hayotzer** — ruined in the hand of the potter.

The failure happens while he is holding it. Jeremiah does not say the clay fell off the wheel or
that somebody knocked it.

## What he does about it

*So the potter **gathered up the same clay** and shaped it into another vessel, **one that seemed
good to him to make**.*

The Hebrew is **vayashov vaya'asehu** — he returned and made it.

Two things are worth stopping on. He does not reach for fresh clay: it is **the same clay**. And
the standard he works to is not the original design — it is *as it seemed good to the potter*.

## What is not in the passage

Any suggestion the clay cooperated, improved, or asked to be reworked.

*Being confident of this very thing, that **He who has begun a good work in you will complete it**
until the day of Jesus Christ.*

**Paul** to the **Philippians**, and the confidence is placed entirely in the one doing the work.

*For **we are His workmanship**, created in Christ Jesus for good works.*

The Greek is **poiema** — a thing made.

## Where Paul takes the image

*Does not the potter have power over the clay, from the same lump to make one vessel for honour
and another for dishonour?*

And then, three verses later, what the vessels are for: *that He might make known **the riches of
His glory on the vessels of mercy**.* See the note on Isaiah 45:9–10.

**Skeue eleous** — vessels of mercy. The potter's answer in **Romans** is not a display of
authority. It is an exhibition of kindness, and the pots are the exhibit.

## And what gets put in them

*But **we have this treasure in earthen vessels**, that the excellence of the power may be of God
and not of us.*

**Ostrakinois** — cheap baked clay. **Paul** stays in Jeremiah's workshop and moves the subject
from the potter's rights to the contents.

## What the chapter goes on to say

*"O house of Israel, **can I not do with you as this potter?**"… "If that nation against whom I
have spoken turns from its evil, **I will relent** of the disaster I thought to bring upon it."*

The lesson drawn is not fatalism. It is that the outcome is not fixed, because the hands are still
on the clay.

## The chapter after this one

The same prophet is sent to buy a jar that has already been fired, take the elders to the valley,
and smash it. See the note on Jeremiah 19:1–5.

Soft clay is reworked. Fired pottery is broken. The two chapters are the same image at two stages
and they are deliberately consecutive.

> **The three words.** *The same clay.* The potter does not fetch a new lump — he gathers up what
> spoiled in his hands and starts again, working to what seems good to him rather than to the
> shape it failed to become.
