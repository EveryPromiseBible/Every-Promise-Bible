A processional for a marching army stops in its third verse to name the people with nobody.

## Who the psalm is about so far

God riding on the clouds, enemies scattering like smoke, the wicked perishing before Him. It
opens with the sentence Moses said whenever the ark set out.

And then, without transition:

*Father of the fatherless and protector of widows is God **in His holy dwelling**.*

## Where He is when He does it

*In His holy dwelling.* The psalm locates the title in the sanctuary — the same place the
chariots and the scattering come from.

*Pure and undefiled religion before God and the Father is this: **to visit orphans and widows in
their trouble**, and to keep oneself unspotted from the world.*

**James** takes the divine job description and hands it to the church as its definition.

## The three sentences

*God **sets the lonely in families**. He **leads out the prisoners** into freedom. But the
rebellious dwell in a scorched land.*

The Hebrew of the first is **moshiv yechidim baytah** — He causes the solitary to dwell in a
house. **Yachid** is the word for an only child, and for Isaac on the mountain.

*He predestined us to **adoption as sons** by **Jesus Christ** to Himself, according to the good
pleasure of His will.*

**Paul**'s word is **huiothesia** — the legal placing of somebody as a son. The psalm's picture
is a house; his is a family court.

## The prisoners

*He leads out the prisoners **into freedom**.*

The Hebrew is *into prosperity* — **bakosharot**, a rare word, possibly *with singing*.

*"**He has sent Me to heal the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives** and recovery
of sight to the blind, to set at liberty those who are oppressed."*

**Jesus** reads that in Nazareth and says it is fulfilled that day. See the note on
Luke 4:16–21.

## Where the psalm goes from here

Up the mountain, and then the ascension verse **Paul** quotes in Ephesians. See the note on
Psalms 68:15–18.

The psalm that stops to name orphans and widows is the same psalm that ends with a King going
up on high and giving gifts to men.

> **Who the procession pauses for.** Three categories with nobody — the fatherless, the widow,
> the solitary. A war psalm interrupts itself to say what God is to them, and gives His address
> while it does.
