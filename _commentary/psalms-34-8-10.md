The most physical invitation in the Psalter.

## Where it comes from

The heading: *a psalm of David when he pretended madness before Abimelech, who drove him away,
and he departed*.

The same episode as Psalm 56 — Gath, the Philistine city, scrabbling at the gate with saliva
in his beard. See the note on Psalms 56:8–9.

He got out alive and wrote two psalms about it, and this is the cheerful one.

## The form

An acrostic. Each verse begins with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet.

Which means the psalm was built to be memorised, and that a man wrote a teaching poem about
the day he escaped by pretending to be insane.

## The invitation

*Taste and see for yourself **how good the LORD is**.*

The Hebrew is **ta'amu ur'u** — taste and see.

Two senses, and the first one requires putting something in your mouth. You can see a thing
from across a room. Tasting means it is already inside.

## The beatitude

*Oh, the happiness of the one who **runs to Him for shelter**.*

The Hebrew is **ashrei** — the word Psalm 1 opens with. And **chasah**, to take refuge, to
flee to for protection.

The picture is a person running to something, not resting in it. It is the verb for getting
under cover.

## The fear

*Stand in awe of the LORD, **you who are His**.*

The Hebrew is *fear the LORD, you His saints* — **qedoshav**, His holy ones.

Not the outsiders. The instruction is addressed to people who already belong.

## The promise

*For those who love Him **never go without**.*

The Hebrew is *there is no lack to those who fear Him* — **ein machsor**.

## The comparison

*Even **young lions grow hungry and weak**, but those who seek the LORD **lack nothing good**.*

The Hebrew is **kephirim** — young lions, at their strongest.

The strongest hunters in the region, and the psalm says they go short.

The contrast is between what strength secures and what seeking secures, and the strength loses.

## What the psalm has already said

*I sought the LORD, and He heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.*

*This poor man cried out, and the LORD heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles.*

*The angel of the LORD encamps all around those who fear Him, and delivers them.*

## What comes after

*Come, you children, listen to me; I will teach you the fear of the LORD.*

*Who is the man who desires life, and loves many days, that he may see good? **Keep your tongue
from evil**, and your lips from speaking deceit. Depart from evil and do good; **seek peace and
pursue it**.*

Peter quotes that paragraph at length in his first letter.

## The line about the bones

*He guards all his bones; **not one of them is broken**.*

John quotes it at the crucifixion: *for these things were done that the Scripture should be
fulfilled, "Not one of His bones shall be broken."*

The soldiers broke the legs of the two beside Him and found Him already dead.

The same clause is in the Passover instructions: *nor shall you break one of its bones*.

## What Peter does with verse 8

*If indeed you have **tasted that the Lord is gracious**.*

He quotes it as the reason for laying aside malice and desiring the pure milk of the word.

> **The verb that makes it different.** *Taste.* Seeing works at a distance. The psalm's first
> instruction requires the thing to already be in your mouth.
