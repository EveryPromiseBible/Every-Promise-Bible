Two verses of family description, and everything that goes wrong for the next twenty
years is in them.

## The two men

*Esau became **a skilled hunter, a man of the open country**, while Jacob was **a quiet
man, staying among the tents**.*

The Hebrew for *quiet* is **tam** — complete, sound, whole. It is the same word used of
Job: *that man was blameless and upright*.

It is not the word for timid. It carries the sense of settled, of a man without a divided
purpose.

## The tents

Staying among the tents is what a herdsman does. Jacob is with the flocks and the
household; Esau is out.

## The parents

*Isaac loved Esau, **because he had a taste for wild game**, but Rebekah loved Jacob.*

Moses gives the reason for one and not the other.

Isaac's reason is his stomach. It is stated as flatly as that, and it will be the exact
mechanism of the deception in chapter 27 — a bowl of savoury food and a father who could
no longer see.

Rebekah's reason is not given. What she had been told is: *two nations are in your womb…
and **the older shall serve the younger**.*

## What was already said

Before either boy was born, at the oracle. Paul quotes it in Romans and adds *for the
children not yet being born, nor having done any good or evil, that the purpose of God
according to election might stand.*

The younger was named as the heir before either of them had a personality.

## What happens in the same chapter

Six verses later, Esau comes in from the field exhausted and sells the birthright for a
bowl of red stew.

*Thus Esau **despised** his birthright* — the Hebrew is to hold in contempt, to treat as
worthless.

Hebrews calls him *a profane person, who for one morsel of food sold his birthright.*

## The parenting

Each parent has a favourite and they do not have the same one.

It produces a wife coaching one son to deceive a blind husband, a father blessing the
wrong boy, a brother planning a murder, and a mother sending her favourite four hundred
miles away — where he is deceived by his uncle over a wife, and then works twenty years
for it.

Moses reports the whole thing and never once says it was a good idea.

## The names

**Esau** — *hairy*. **Jacob** — **Yaaqov**, from the heel he came out holding; it becomes
the word for supplanter, one who takes another's place.

Esau uses it as an accusation in chapter 27: *is he not rightly named Jacob?* See the
note on Genesis 27:34–37.

## Where it ends

Twenty years later, at the Jabbok, wrestling until daybreak, and the name is changed:
*your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but Israel.*

> **What the two verses set up.** A father who loves by appetite and a mother who loves
> by the promise. Everything in chapter 27 runs down the lines drawn here.
