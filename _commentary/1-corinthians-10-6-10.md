Five wilderness incidents, listed as warnings, to a church that thought it was doing well.

## What has just been said

*All our fathers were **under the cloud**, all passed through the sea… all ate the same spiritual
food, and all drank the same spiritual drink.*

*But with **most of them God was not well pleased**, for their bodies were scattered in the
wilderness.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 10:1–4.

Five *alls*, and then the reversal.

## The statement

*Now these things happened **as examples for us**.*

The Greek is **tupoi hemon** — types of us, patterns for us.

**Tupos** — the mark left by a blow, a stamp, a die. The word English took as *type*.

## The purpose

*So that we would **not crave what is evil the way they craved it**.*

The Greek is **epithumetas kakon** — cravers of evil things.

Which points at Numbers 11: *the mixed multitude who were among them yielded to intense craving*,
and the quail.

## The first

*Do not become **idolaters**, as some of them were; as it is written, "**The people sat down to eat
and to drink, and got up to play**."*

Exodus 32, quoted exactly. The golden calf. See the note on Exodus 32:1–4.

## The second

*Nor let us commit **sexual immorality**… and **twenty-three thousand of them fell in a single day**.*

Numbers 25, at Baal of Peor, with the women of Moab.

Numbers says twenty-four thousand. Paul says twenty-three. Either he is counting the day rather
than the plague's total, or citing from memory; the New Testament does not smooth it over.

## The third

*Nor let us **put Christ to the test**, as some of them tested Him, and were **destroyed by the
serpents**.*

Numbers 21, on the long way round Edom. See the note on Numbers 21:5.

Paul writes **ton Christon** — Christ. He has already said the rock that followed them was Christ.

## The fourth

*Nor let us **grumble**, as some of them grumbled, and were **destroyed by the destroyer**.*

The Greek is **gonguzete** — an onomatopoeic word. Muttering.

**Ho olothreutes** — the destroyer. The definite article.

## What grumbling is doing in this list

Idolatry, immorality, testing God — and complaining.

Paul lists it fourth, with the same construction and the same consequence. The letter does not
grade it as a lesser item.

## Why he is saying it

Corinth had spiritual gifts in abundance, and Paul has just spent a chapter on his own
discipline: *lest, when I have preached to others, I myself should become disqualified*.

The wilderness generation had every advantage — cloud, sea, manna, water from the rock — and did
not arrive.

## The conclusion

*Now all these things happened to them as examples, and **they were written for our admonition, upon
whom the ends of the ages have come**.* See the note on 1 Corinthians 10:11–13.

## The warning

*Therefore **let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall**.*

## And immediately

*No temptation has overtaken you except such as is common to man; but **God is faithful, who will
not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able**, but with the temptation will also make the
way of escape.*

The Greek is **ekbasin** — a way out, an exit.

Five warnings and then a promise, in the same paragraph.

> **The fourth item.** Grumbling, listed with idolatry and immorality, in the same construction
> and with the same outcome. Paul does not grade it lower.
