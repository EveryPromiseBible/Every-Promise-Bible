Nine months of silence end on a writing tablet.

## The day

*On **the eighth day** they came to circumcise the child.*

The day set in Genesis 17 and in Leviticus. Naming happened at the same ceremony.

## The assumption

*And they were going to **name him Zechariah, after his father**.*

The family assumes. Nobody asks.

## The mother

*But **his mother spoke up: "No. He is to be called John."**"*

Elizabeth has not been told by anyone in the room. Zechariah has been deaf and mute since
the temple.

Whatever passed between them, she knows the name.

## The objection

*"But **there is no one in your family with that name**."*

Which is the whole argument they have.

## The appeal

*So they **made signs to his father**.*

They gesture. He is deaf as well as silent — Luke shows it here rather than stating it.

## The tablet

*He asked for **a writing tablet**, and he wrote: **His name is John**.*

The Greek is *John is his name* — a statement rather than a choice.

He is not selecting. He is reporting what he was told nine months earlier.

**John** — **Yochanan**, *the LORD is gracious*.

## The astonishment

*And **everyone was astonished**.*

Two people who could not have consulted each other produced the same name.

## The release

*Right then **his mouth was opened and his tongue was set loose**, and he began to speak.*

The moment he agrees in writing, the speech comes back.

Gabriel had said *until the day all of this happens*.

## The first thing out

*And **the first thing that came out of him was praise to God**.*

Nine months of stored-up speech, and Luke says what it was spent on.

## The effect

*A **hush of awe settled over all the neighbors**, and **the story traveled through the whole
hill country of Judea**.*

## The question

*And people kept **laying it up in their hearts** and asking, "**What is this child going to
be?**"*

The Greek for *laying it up* is the same verb Luke uses of Mary twice: *Mary kept all these
things and pondered them in her heart*.

Luke uses it of ordinary neighbours here.

## The clause

*For **the hand of the Lord was plainly on him**.*

## What follows

*And his father Zechariah was **filled with the Holy Spirit**, and he began to prophesy.*

The Benedictus:

*Blessed is the Lord God of Israel, for **He has visited and redeemed His people**… to give
light to those who sit in darkness and the shadow of death, **to guide our feet into the way
of peace**.*

And to the child: *and you, child, will be called the prophet of the Highest; for **you will
go before the face of the Lord to prepare His ways**.*

## What John becomes

The man in the wilderness in camel's hair, saying *behold the Lamb of God*, and *He must
increase, but I must decrease*. See the note on John 3:25–30.

And executed in a prison at a birthday party, on a girl's request.

Jesus on him: *among those born of women there has not risen one greater than John the
Baptist*. See the note on Matthew 11:12–15.

> **What broke the silence.** Not the birth. Zechariah wrote down the name he had been
> given and had not believed — and the sentence about it opens with *right then*.
