The man who has to knock on the door, and he argues.

## Who he is

*There was **a disciple in Damascus named Ananias**.*

That is all Luke gives. He appears in this chapter, is mentioned in Paul's own retelling as
*a devout man according to the law, having a good testimony with all the Jews who dwelt
there*, and is never heard of again.

## The call

*And the Lord spoke to him **in a vision**. "**Ananias**," He said. "**Here I am, Lord**," he
answered.*

The Hebrew equivalent is **hinneni** — Abraham's word at the mountain, Moses' at the bush,
Samuel's in the night. See the note on Genesis 22:1–2.

## The address

*"Get up and go to **the street called Straight**, and ask at **the house of Judas** for a man
**from Tarsus named Saul**."*

A street, a householder, a hometown and a name.

The Street Called Straight ran east to west through Damascus and still does.

Luke gives the address with the precision of a man who checked it.

## What Saul is doing

*"**He is praying at this moment**."*

Three days blind, eating and drinking nothing.

## The other vision

*"And **he has seen in a vision a man named Ananias coming in and laying hands on him so
that he might see again**."*

Two visions again, as with Cornelius and Peter — and again neither man knows about the
other's until told. See the note on Acts 10:17–20.

Saul has already seen Ananias arrive. Ananias is being asked to complete a picture that
has been shown to somebody else.

## The objection

*"**Lord, I have heard from many people about this man** and **all the harm he has done to
Your holy ones in Jerusalem**."*

The Greek is **kaka** — evils.

He is not exaggerating. Luke has recorded it: *Saul made havoc of the church, entering every
house, and dragging off men and women, committing them to prison.*

## The second half

*"And **he is here with authority from the chief priests to arrest everyone who calls on Your
name**."*

The warrants are in the house on Straight Street.

Ananias would be one of the names on them. *Everyone who calls on Your name* is a
description of himself.

He is being sent to lay hands on the man who came to arrest him.

## What he is told

*"**Go, for he is a chosen vessel of Mine** to bear My name before Gentiles, kings, and the
children of Israel. **For I will show him how many things he must suffer for My name's
sake**."*

Not a reassurance about safety. An assignment for the other man, and a note about what it
will cost him.

## What Ananias does

*And Ananias went his way and entered the house; and **laying his hands on him he said,
"Brother Saul…"**"*

**Brother.** The first word out of his mouth.

*"…the Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on the road as you came, has sent me that you may
receive your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit."*

*Immediately there fell from his eyes something like scales, and he received his sight at
once.*

## What Paul remembers him saying

In his own account years later, Ananias adds: *the God of our fathers has chosen you that
you should know His will… **and now why are you waiting? Arise and be baptised**.*

## What Ananias is

The man who let Paul into the church. Everything Paul wrote came through a door somebody
had to be argued into opening.

> **What he is not given.** Any assurance for himself. He raises the objection — the man
> has warrants and my name is on them — and the answer is about what the other man is for.
