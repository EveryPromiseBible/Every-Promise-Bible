The end of a psalm about being nearly dead, and it turns into an accounting of what is owed.

## What the psalm has been about

*I love the LORD, because He has heard my voice… **the pains of death surrounded me**, and the
pangs of Sheol laid hold of me; I found trouble and sorrow.*

*Then I called upon the name of the LORD: "O LORD, I implore You, deliver my soul!"*

And the rescue: *You have delivered my soul from death, my eyes from tears, and my feet from
falling*.

The man writing had been close enough to know it.

## The line

*Precious in the sight of the LORD is **the death of those He loves**.*

The Hebrew is **yaqar** — costly, valuable, rare. The word used of precious stones.

The usual reading takes it as: their deaths are not cheap to Him. He does not spend them
carelessly.

Which is why it sits in a psalm by a man who did not die — the sentence is his explanation for
why he is still here.

The verse is read at funerals, and it was written by a survivor.

## The self-description

*O LORD, **I am truly Your servant**, Your servant, **born to Your servant girl**.*

He says *servant* twice and then adds his mother.

The Hebrew is **ben-amatekha** — son of Your handmaid. A slave born in the household rather than
bought, and therefore belonging by birth.

## The release

*You have **loosened my chains**.*

The Hebrew is **pittachta lemoserai** — You have loosed my bonds.

A servant, in chains, whose chains have been undone by the master he still calls himself the
servant of.

## The offering

*I will bring You **a thank offering** and **call on the name of the LORD**.*

The Hebrew is **zevach todah** — the sacrifice of thanksgiving.

That is a specific offering. It is the peace offering brought for thanksgiving, and Leviticus
requires it to be eaten the same day. See the note on Leviticus 7:11–18.

Which meant a whole animal, one evening, and a family that could not finish it alone. People
were invited.

## The vows

*I will **keep my promises to the LORD in front of all His people**.*

The Hebrew is **nedarai** — my vows.

Made in the crisis, and paid in public.

## The location

*In the courts of the house of the LORD, **in your very heart, Jerusalem**.*

## What the psalm is

One of the Hallel psalms — 113 to 118 — sung at Passover.

Which means this is among the last things Jesus and the disciples sang before Gethsemane: *and
when they had sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives*.

*Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of His saints*, sung on that night.

## The verse two lines earlier

*What shall I render to the LORD for all His benefits toward me? **I will take up the cup of
salvation** and call upon the name of the LORD.*

The cup, in a Passover psalm, sung at a Passover table.

## The last words

*Praise the LORD.*

**Hallelu-Yah.**

> **Who wrote it.** A man who did not die. The line about the death of the saints being costly
> is his own explanation for still being alive to sing it.
