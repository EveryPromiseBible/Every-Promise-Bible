title: "The word "that" is doing all the work"
updated: 2026-08-09

The most argued-over sentence in the letter, and most of the argument disappears once you notice
which faith James is asking about.

## He does not say a man has faith

*When a man **says** he has faith and has no works with it.*

**Ean pistin lege tis echein** — if someone *says* he has faith. James introduces the case as a
claim, not as a fact, and everything that follows is about the claim.

He is not examining a believer who is having a bad year. He is examining a sentence somebody said
about himself.

## The article nobody translates

*Can **a faith like that** save him?*

The Greek is **me he pistis sosai auton** — *can THE faith save him*, with the definite article
pointing back at what was just described.

Not *can faith save*. Can **that** faith — the one made entirely of saying — save him. And the
question is framed in Greek with a particle that expects the answer *no*.

Every writer in the New Testament would answer it the same way, Paul included. A faith that is only
a sentence has never saved anyone, because it was never faith in the first place.

## What the chapter's own opening names

James is not vague about what real faith is attached to. He begins the chapter: *my brethren, do not
hold **the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory**, with partiality.*

That is the faith in question. Its object is a Person, and the rest of the chapter is about whether
what somebody claims to have is actually that.

## Where the works come in

Paul puts the two in order in a single breath, and there is no tension in it at all:

*For **by grace you have been saved through faith**, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of
God, **not of works**, lest anyone should boast. For we are His workmanship, **created in Christ
Jesus for good works**, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them.*

Not of works — then created for works. The works are downstream of the saving, and God had them
ready before we arrived. Nobody is paying for anything with them.

> **The question James asks and the question he does not.** He never asks whether faith saves. He
> asks whether a faith that is only talk is the kind of faith he named in verse one — the faith of
> our Lord Jesus Christ. Paul says the same thing from the other side: *faith working through love.*
> One writer is defending the gate, the other is describing the road, and it is the same house.
