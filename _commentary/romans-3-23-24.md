The most quoted diagnosis in the Bible, and it is only half of the sentence.

## Where it usually stops

*Everyone has sinned. Everyone falls short of the glory of God.*

Quoted alone, that is a verdict. But in Greek it is not the end of the sentence —
the participle that follows carries straight on into the next clause without a
full stop.

*And everyone is put right with Him as a gift, freely, by His grace.*

The same *everyone* governs both halves. Paul builds the universal charge and
then applies the universal remedy in the same breath.

## "Falls short"

The verb is about coming up behind, lacking, being left wanting. It is the word
used in Hebrews for someone missing out on God's grace.

Not *broke a rule*. Failed to reach — which is a description of distance rather
than of crimes committed.

## Three words for how it comes

Paul stacks them because one is not enough:

- **as a gift** — the word means gratuitously, without cause in the receiver. It
  is the same word Jesus uses in John for being hated *without reason*.
- **freely**
- **by His grace**

He is closing every door through which a contribution might be smuggled back in.

## The ransom

*Through the ransom paid in Christ Jesus that bought us out of slavery and set us
loose.*

**Apolutrosis** again — the release accomplished by paying a price. See the note
on Ephesians 1:7–10.

The gift is free to the person receiving it. It was not free.

> **What comes next.** Paul says God set Christ forth publicly as the mercy seat
> — the same word the Greek Old Testament uses for the lid of the ark. What was
> hidden behind a curtain and approached once a year with blood, Paul says was
> displayed in the open. See the note on Exodus 25:17–22.
