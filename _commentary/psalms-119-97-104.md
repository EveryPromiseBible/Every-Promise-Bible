The **Mem** stanza — eight verses, every one beginning with the same Hebrew letter.

## What the psalm is

Psalm 119 is the longest chapter in the Bible: twenty-two stanzas of eight verses, one for
each letter of the Hebrew alphabet.

Every verse in a stanza begins with that letter, and almost every verse in the psalm names
God's word by one of eight synonyms — law, testimonies, precepts, statutes, commandments,
judgments, word, promise.

It is a work of enormous craft, and this stanza is usually reckoned the finest of them.

## The opening

*Oh, **how I love the teaching that You gave!**"*

The Hebrew is *how I love Your law*.

The psalm says this repeatedly, and it is startling every time. Nobody expects affection
for a legal code.

## The practice

*All day long **I turn it over and over**.*

The Hebrew is **sichah** — musing, meditation, going over something in the mind.

The related verb in Joshua 1:8 is **hagah** — to mutter, to murmur under the breath. See
the note on Joshua 1:6–9.

Ancient reading was aloud. Meditation in the Old Testament is a sound.

## The three comparisons

*Your good commands have made me **wiser than my enemies**, for they are always mine.*

*I understand **far more than all my teachers**, because Your steady words are my long
study.*

*I see with **clearer eyes than aged men**, because I've kept the precepts You have given.*

Three groups, each with more claim to know than the last: opponents, teachers, and the
old.

The reasons given are different each time — possession, study, and keeping.

## The restraint

*I've **held my feet back from each evil road**, so I might **keep the word** that You have
spoken.*

The order is deliberate. He stays off the road in order to keep the word, not the reverse.

## The teacher

*I have not turned aside from Your fair judgments, for **You Yourself have been the one who
taught me**.*

The three comparisons are all undercut by this line. He outstrips his teachers because of
who taught him.

## The taste

*How **sweet Your words taste when I let them linger**, sweeter than honey dripping in my
mouth!*

The Hebrew is *how sweet to my palate*.

The manna tasted *like wafers made with honey*. See the note on Exodus 16:31–34.

And Ezekiel is given a scroll to eat: *it was in my mouth like honey in sweetness*.

## The last verse

*Out of Your precepts **I have gathered wisdom**; so now **I hate each false and crooked
road**.*

The love and the hatred are in the same stanza and joined by a *so*.

## Who wrote it

Unknown. The psalm gives no author and no occasion, and the voice in it is of somebody
under pressure — princes persecuting him, the proud digging pits, and *my soul melts from
heaviness*.

## The verse everyone knows

From the **Nun** stanza: *Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.*

And from **Beth**: *Your word I have hidden in my heart, that I might not sin against
You.*

## What the meditation is on

*Oh, how I love Your law! **It is my meditation all the day**.*

The Hebrew for meditate, **siach**, is to mutter, to go over something under your breath. It is
not silent.

## What the Word turned out to be

*In the beginning was **the Word**… And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us.*

*You search the Scriptures, for in them you think you have eternal life; and **these are they
which testify of Me**.*

**Jesus** says it to men who had this psalm by heart, and the complaint in His sentence is not
that they read too much.

## The three he outstrips

Wiser than my enemies. More understanding than all my teachers. More than the ancients.

Each is about a source of authority, and each is set below the same thing.

*But God has revealed them to us through His Spirit… **now we have received the Spirit who is
from God**, that we might know the things that have been freely given to us by God.*

## Sweeter than honey

*How sweet are Your words to my taste, **sweeter than honey to my mouth**!*

*As newborn babes, **desire the pure milk of the word**, that you may grow thereby.*

> **The claim in the middle.** More understanding than his teachers and clearer sight than
> the elders — and the reason given at the end of the stanza is that God taught him
> Himself.
