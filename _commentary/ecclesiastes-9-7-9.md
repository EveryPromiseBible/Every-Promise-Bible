The bleakest book in the Bible stops to tell somebody to enjoy their dinner.

## The instruction

*So **go**. **Eat your bread with joy**, and **drink your wine with a glad heart**.*

Three imperatives, and none of them is religious.

## The reason

*For **God has already approved what you do**.*

The Hebrew is *God has already accepted your works*.

The approval is in the past tense and stated before anything is asked. The eating follows
it rather than earning it.

## The clothes

*Let your clothes be **white, always**.*

White linen in that climate was what you wore to a feast. Ordinary work clothes were
undyed and dark.

The instruction is to dress for a celebration as the normal condition.

## The oil

*And **never let the oil run dry on your head**.*

Oil on the head was for guests and for festivity. A host anointed a visitor's head — which
is the courtesy Simon the Pharisee omitted. See the note on Luke 7:44–50.

Both images are things you do on a good day, and both are commanded as a standing state.

## The marriage

*Enjoy life with **the one you love**, through **all the fleeting days God has given you**
under the sun, **all your fleeting days**.*

The Hebrew is **hevel** — vapour, breath, mist. It is the book's key word, used
thirty-eight times, and usually translated *vanity*.

Solomon repeats it twice in one verse here, and both times attached to the instruction to
enjoy.

## What the sentence is doing

The book's argument is that life is short, unpredictable, and cannot be mastered. He does
not resolve that.

He draws a different conclusion from it: since you cannot secure the days, receive them.

## The verse before

*For the living know that they will die… **also their love, their hatred, and their envy
have now perished**; nevermore will they have a share in anything done under the sun.*

That is what the *so go* follows.

## The verse after

*Whatever your hand finds to do, **do it with your might**; for there is no work or device
or knowledge or wisdom in the grave where you are going.*

## What the book calls it

*This is **your portion** in life, and in the labour you pour out under the sun.*

The Hebrew is **cheleq** — a share, an allotted piece. The surveying word, the one the
Levites were given about God Himself. See the note on Numbers 18:8–20.

## How often the book says this

Seven times, in almost the same words: *there is nothing better for a man than that he
should eat and drink, and that his soul should enjoy good in his labour.*

And each time it adds: *this also, I saw, was from the hand of God.*

## Where the book ends

*Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: **Fear God and keep His commandments**, for
this is man's all.*

> **What licenses the bread and the wine.** Not that the days are secure — the book says
> repeatedly that they are vapour. One clause: *God has already approved*, in the past
> tense, before the eating starts.
