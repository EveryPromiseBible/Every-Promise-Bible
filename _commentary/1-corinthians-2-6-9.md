Paul has just said he came in weakness and fear, and now says he does speak wisdom after all.

## What he said a paragraph earlier

*I determined not to know anything among you except Jesus Christ and Him crucified. I was
with you in weakness, in fear, and in much trembling.*

*And my speech and my preaching were not with persuasive words of human wisdom, but in
demonstration of the Spirit and of power.*

## The qualification

*Yet **we do speak wisdom among those who are mature**.*

The Greek is **teleiois** — the complete, the full-grown.

He has not abandoned wisdom. He has abandoned one kind of it.

## Whose wisdom it is not

*Not the wisdom of **this present age**, nor of **the rulers of this age**, who are **all
coming to nothing**.*

The Greek is **katargoumenon** — being brought to nothing, a present passive participle. The
process is already running.

Corinth was full of professional rhetoricians. The city paid to hear men speak well.

## What it is

*The wisdom of God, **a wisdom kept hidden in mystery**.*

The Greek is **en musterio** — in a mystery. Not a riddle; something that was concealed and
has now been disclosed.

## When it was decided

*Which **God appointed before the ages for our glory**.*

The Greek is **pro ton aionon** — before the ages.

Not a response to the fall. Settled beforehand.

Peter says the same: *He indeed was foreordained before the foundation of the world, but was
manifest in these last times for you*.

## The sentence

*Which **none of the rulers of this age understood; for if they had understood it, they would
never have crucified the Lord of glory**.*

The most striking claim in the passage.

The crucifixion is described as an act of ignorance. Not a miscalculation about a man — a
failure to see what was in front of them.

## Who the rulers were

Read plainly: Pilate, Herod, Caiaphas and the council.

Peter says it to the crowd in Jerusalem: *I know that you did it in ignorance, as did also
your rulers*.

And from the blood covenant: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*.

## The title

*The **Lord of glory**.*

James uses the same phrase. It is the highest title Paul gives Him anywhere, and he uses it
in the sentence about the execution.

## The quotation

*What **no eye has seen and no ear has heard**, what has **never risen into the heart of
anyone**, is what **God has prepared for those who love Him**.*

Closest to Isaiah 64: *since the beginning of the world men have not heard nor perceived by
the ear, nor has the eye seen any God besides You, who acts for the one who waits for Him*.

## Three channels, all closed

Eye, ear, and the heart's own imagining.

Nothing gets in by observation, by report, or by thinking it up.

## What comes next

The verse the quotation is usually severed from:

*But **God has revealed them to us through His Spirit**. For the Spirit searches all things,
yes, the deep things of God.*

The passage is not about how unknowable it all is. It is about how the knowing arrives — and
Paul says it in the very next sentence.

## What the Spirit is compared to

*For what man knows the things of a man except the spirit of the man which is in him? Even so
**no one knows the things of God except the Spirit of God**.*

The same argument as *no one knows the Son except the Father*. See the note on
Matthew 11:25–27.

> **What the crucifixion is called.** A failure of recognition. Paul says the rulers would not
> have done it if they had known — and then says the knowing comes by the Spirit, in the next
> line.
