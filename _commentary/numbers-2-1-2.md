Two verses of camp layout, and the arrangement is the whole point.

## The instruction

*"Let the Israelites camp **under their own banners**, each family **beneath the standard of its
ancestors**."*

The Hebrew is **degel** — a standard, a military banner. And **ot**, a sign or ensign.

Tribal identity is not erased. Everybody keeps their own flag.

## The orientation

*"They are to pitch their tents **facing the Tent of Meeting on every side**."*

The Hebrew is **minneged saviv** — over against, round about.

Every tent door points inward.

## The centre

*"Gathered around **the place where I dwell**."*

The tabernacle is not at one end of the camp or on a hill above it. It is in the middle, with
the Levites camped immediately round it and the twelve tribes in four blocks beyond them.

## The four sides

The rest of the chapter gives the arrangement.

**East** — Judah, Issachar, Zebulun. Judah leads, and the east is the entrance side.

**South** — Reuben, Simeon, Gad.

**West** — Ephraim, Manasseh, Benjamin.

**North** — Dan, Asher, Naphtali.

Three tribes to a side, with the leading tribe of each block named.

## The numbers

Each block is counted. Judah's side: 186,400. Reuben's: 151,450. Ephraim's: 108,100. Dan's:
157,600.

Over six hundred thousand fighting men, plus families.

## The four standards

Scripture does not say what was on them.

Jewish tradition, in the Targums and the rabbinic sources, assigns a lion to Judah, a man to
Reuben, an ox to Ephraim, and an eagle to Dan.

Those are the four faces of Ezekiel's creatures. See the note on
Ezekiel 1:10–11.

And the four creatures round the throne in Revelation. See the note on
Revelation 4:5–6.

The tradition is late and cannot be verified from the text. The correspondence is why it is
repeated.

## What the shape does

A camp arranged this way has its most valuable thing furthest from every edge.

And every family, waking up and stepping out of a tent, is looking at the same place.

## The marching order

Chapter 10 gives it. Judah first, then Issachar and Zebulun. Then the tabernacle comes down and
travels in the middle of the column.

*So they started out for the first time according to the command of the LORD by the hand of
Moses.*

And the ark going ahead: *the ark of the covenant of the LORD went before them for three days'
journey, to search out a resting place for them*. See the note on
Numbers 10:33–36.

## What Balaam saw from the hill

*And Balaam raised his eyes, and **saw Israel encamped according to their tribes**… and he took
up his oracle and said: "**How lovely are your tents, O Jacob!**"* See the note on
Numbers 24:3–9.

He was hired to curse, and what he was looking at when he blessed them instead was this
arrangement, laid out below him.

## Where the shape goes

*And the city had twelve gates… **three gates on the east, three gates on the north, three gates
on the south, and three gates on the west**.*

The New Jerusalem, four sides, three per side, and the names of the twelve tribes on them.

> **What the layout guarantees.** Everybody's door faces the same direction. Six hundred
> thousand men, twelve tribes, four sides, and one thing in the middle of it that nobody is
> camped with their back to.
