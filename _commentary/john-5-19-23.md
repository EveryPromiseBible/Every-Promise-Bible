He has just healed a man on the sabbath and told him to carry his mat, and this is the
answer to the objection.

## What provoked it

*Therefore the Jews sought all the more to kill Him, because He not only broke the Sabbath,
but also **said that God was His Father, making Himself equal with God**.*

They understood the claim correctly. John records their reading and does not correct it.

## The denial

*"The Son **can do nothing on His own**."*

Which sounds like a retreat and is not.

## The claim

*"He does **only what He sees the Father doing**, and **whatever the Father does, the Son
does in the same way**."*

The Greek is **homoios** — likewise, in like manner.

The first clause limits Him to what He sees. The second says the range is everything the
Father does.

Together they are a larger claim than the one being denied.

## What the Father does about it

*"For **the Father loves the Son and shows Him everything He Himself is doing**."*

The love and the showing are one clause.

John says the same pairing repeatedly: *the Father loves the Son and has given all things
into His hand*. See the note on John 3:31–36.

## The promise of more

*"And **He will show Him works greater than these, until you are left astonished**."*

## What the greater works are

*"Just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, **so the Son also gives life to
whomever He chooses**."*

The Greek is *whom He wills*.

Raising the dead was God's prerogative alone in Jewish thought. He assigns it to Himself
in the same sentence.

## The judgment

*"And **the Father judges no one. He has handed the whole of judgment over to the Son**."*

The Greek is *has given all judgment to the Son*.

## The reason

*"**So that everyone will honor the Son just as they honor the Father**."*

The Greek is **kathos** — in the same way, to the same degree.

## The consequence

*"**Anyone who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent Him**."*

There is no third position in the sentence.

## The pattern

The passage is built on seeing. He does what He sees; the Father shows Him; and the works
are the evidence.

Later in the same Gospel: *the words that I speak to you I do not speak on My own authority;
but the Father who dwells in Me does the works.*

## What He says about the man at the pool

The healing that started the argument had one odd feature. The pool at Bethesda was full of
sick people — *a great multitude of sick people, blind, lame, paralysed*. See the note on
John 5:1–5.

He healed one man and walked away.

Nothing in the account explains the selection, and this passage is the nearest thing to
one: *what He sees the Father doing*.

## The two resurrections

The verses after: *the hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the
Son of God; and those who hear will live.*

And: *the hour is coming in which all who are in the graves will hear His voice and come
forth.*

Present and future, in consecutive paragraphs.

> **The shape of the answer.** A denial that sounds like modesty — *the Son can do nothing
> on His own* — attached to a claim that He does everything the Father does, and that
> judgment has been handed over entirely.
