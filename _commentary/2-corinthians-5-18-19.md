Paul states what God did, and then what He has put into our hands, and the two
are the same word.

## Who did the reconciling

*All of it comes from God, who has brought us back to Himself through Jesus
Christ.*

The direction is fixed and it is worth noticing. In ordinary usage the offended
party is the one who needs to be appeased — you reconcile the person you have
wronged.

Paul never says God was reconciled to us. He says God reconciled **us** to
Himself. The move came from the side that had been sinned against.

## "God was in Christ"

Not God sending an agent at a distance. Paul puts Him in the person doing the
work — which is why the next clause can be so absolute.

## The bookkeeping term

*Refusing to hold their sins against them.*

The verb is **logizomai** — to count, to reckon, to enter in a ledger. It is the
same word Paul uses in Romans 4 for righteousness being credited to Abraham.

So the claim is accounting language: the sins are not being entered. Not
overlooked in a vague sense — not posted to the account.

## And the scope

*Bringing **the world** back to Himself.*

Not a narrow group. Paul then says the message of that homecoming has been placed
in us, and two verses later he is pleading with people to be reconciled — so the
reconciling accomplished and the appeal to receive it are both in the passage,
without contradiction.

> **What "the ministry of reconciliation" actually is.** Paul defines it in the
> same breath: God has *placed in us the message of that homecoming.* The work
> handed over is not to reconcile anybody — that is done. It is to carry the news
> that it has been.
