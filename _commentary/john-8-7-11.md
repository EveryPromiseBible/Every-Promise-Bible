A trap set with a woman in it, and He answers by writing on the floor.

## The setup

They had brought her *caught in adultery, in the very act* — which requires witnesses, and
raises the question of where the man is.

Their question was the trap: *Moses commanded us that such should be stoned. But what do You
say?* Say stone her and He contradicts everything He has taught; say release her and He
contradicts Moses.

John states the motive: *this they said, testing Him, that they might have something of
which to accuse Him.*

## The first writing

*But Jesus stooped down and wrote on the ground with His finger.*

The only recorded instance of Jesus writing anything, and John does not say what.

The finger of God wrote the law on stone twice. And Jeremiah has a line about the same
posture: *those who depart from Me shall be written in the earth.*

## The pressing

*And when they **kept pressing Him for an answer**.*

The Greek is an imperfect — they went on asking.

## The sentence

*He straightened up and said to them, "**Let the one among you who is without sin be the
first to throw a stone at her**."*

The Greek is **anamartetos** — without sin, and it occurs once in the New Testament.

He does not overturn the law. He grants the stoning and assigns who goes first.

Deuteronomy required the witnesses to throw first: *the hands of the witnesses shall be the
first against him to put him to death*.

## The second writing

*Then He **bent down again and went on writing** on the ground.*

He does not watch them leave. He hands them the sentence and goes back to the dust.

## The departure

*And those who heard it, **convicted by their own conscience**, went out **one by one,
beginning with the oldest**, until the last of them was gone.*

The oldest first. Whatever the test was, the men with the most years behind them failed it
soonest.

## What is left

*Jesus was left alone, with **the woman still standing there in front of Him**.*

The Greek is *in the midst* — she is still standing in the position they put her in, in the
middle of the temple court, and the circle around her has gone.

She could have left with them, and did not.

## The question

*"**Woman, where are your accusers? Has no one condemned you?**"*

The Greek for *condemned* is **katekrinen** — passed sentence.

## Her answer

*"**No one, Lord**."*

Two words, and the second one is a title.

## The verdict

*"**Neither do I condemn you**."*

The only man in the courtyard qualified to throw the first stone declines to.

He is not saying nothing happened. He is saying no sentence is passed by Him — and He is the
one who will carry the sentence Himself.

## The instruction

*"**Go, and from now on leave your life of sin**."*

The order matters. Not *sin no more and then I will not condemn you*.

The acquittal comes first and the instruction follows it, and He gives her nothing to earn
in between.

He says the same to the man at Bethesda, and there too the healing came first. See the note
on John 5:14–18.

## What He says immediately after

*"**I am the light of the world**. He who follows Me shall not walk in darkness, but have
the light of life."*

The next sentence in the chapter, said in the temple court she is standing in.

## Where the words go in Paul

*There is therefore now **no condemnation** to those who are in Christ Jesus.* See the note
on Romans 8:1–4.

The Greek is **katakrima**, the noun from the verb John uses here.

> **The order of the two sentences.** *Neither do I condemn you* comes first, and *go and
> sin no more* second. Nothing in the exchange asks her to change anything before He
> speaks.
