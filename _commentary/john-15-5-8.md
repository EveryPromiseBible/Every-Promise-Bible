The last of the seven *I am* sayings in John, spoken on the way to Gethsemane.

## What a branch does

*"I am the vine; **you are the branches**."*

A branch does not manufacture fruit. It does not decide to produce grapes, work at
producing grapes, or know how a grape is made.

It is attached, and the sap does the rest. Everything the fruit is made of comes up
through the vine.

## The one instruction

*"**Whoever remains in Me**, and I in him, is the one who bears much fruit."*

The Greek is **meno** — to remain, to stay, to abide. It occurs eleven times in
this chapter.

Not work, not strive, not produce. Stay where you are.

## The clause at the end

*"Because **apart from Me you can do nothing**."*

Not *very little*. Nothing.

It is an absolute, and He states it as the reason the previous sentence is true.

## Which vine

Israel had been called a vine before. Isaiah 5: God planted a vineyard, cleared the
stones, put in the choicest vine — *and He looked that it should bring forth
grapes, and it brought forth wild grapes.*

Psalm 80: *You brought a vine out of Egypt.* Jeremiah: *I planted you a noble
vine… how then have you turned into the degenerate plant of an alien vine?*

Every time, the vine fails.

Then, in an upper room: *I am the **true** vine.* The Greek is genuine, real, the
authentic one — the vine the others were pictures of.

## What He says about the asking

*"If you remain in Me, and **My words remain in you**, you may ask whatever you
want, and it will be done for you."*

The condition is not a formula. It is remaining, and His words remaining in you —
and out of that, the asking.

## Jacob's line

*"He will tie his donkey to the vine, his colt to the choicest branch. He will wash
his garments in wine."*

Jacob said it over Judah on a deathbed in Egypt. Sixteen hundred years later a Man
of Judah's tribe sat in an upper room in Jerusalem, on the night of a cup, and said
*I am the vine.* See the note on Genesis 49:8–12.

> **What He says the fruit is for.** *This is how **My Father** is glorified: that
> you bear much fruit.* The branch is not producing to prove anything about itself.
> The whole crop goes to the vinedresser.
